# Are the two effects isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural reading, `general` on the bond distinction. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The union claim in `01` F1 says accountability gain and control excess are two conditions that can hold at once. Two questions are owed: (1) are they *isomers* under Fig 12's definition, that is, the same atoms arranged differently, or are they different atoms altogether; and (2) in what dependency order must the habitat be read so that neither condition is mistaken for the other?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms available in the firm frame: a stake (quantity), voting or control rights (quantity), information about management (state), the manager's action (state), minority holders' claim (state), the market's payment (state). Bonds available: Fig 12's seven plus negation. No new primitive is introduced.

- Monitoring is `information → voice or exit → changed management action`, an **ordered** bond (Fig 12 :70 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* for →). It produces a state that did not exist before, so ⇒ production is also present.
- Diversion is `control rights − minority claim`, a **subtraction** bond (Fig 12 :78 *"A with B removed or absent"*). The second operand's absence is the point.
- The threshold where one condition overtakes the other is ⋛ comparison, Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*: cash-flow rights against control rights.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Isomers or different substances?

Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Written narrowly, the two effects do **not** share all atoms: monitoring needs information about management, diversion needs a minority claim. By Fig 12 :117 they are not isomers on that narrow writing; they are two different molecules that happen to share the controlling stake.

The stronger reading: write both over the full shared atom set `{stake, control rights, cash-flow rights, information, manager action, minority claim, market payment}` and let only the bonds differ. Monitoring arranges `information → voice → manager action`, diversion arranges `control rights − minority claim`, and their union is a compound whose behaviour differs from either alone. On that writing they **are** isomers: same atoms, different bonds, different substance. Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* and Fig 12 :95-96 *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* This is the claim worth testing, because a governance score that records only the ingredient list (ownership percentage, board size, rating) is the flat formula Fig 12 names as a collapse.

Fig 12 :99-101 *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."* and *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."* A single "concentration" scalar is a flat case: it charges one bond where at least an ordered production and a subtraction are required, so it cannot carry the distinction between the two conditions.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either effect can be written without an atom the other requires, under the same bond set, the isomer claim fails and the narrow reading (two different substances) stands. If both require exactly the same atoms and the same bonds, the isomer claim also fails, because they are then one molecule spelled twice and Fig 12's symmetric-bond rule would make them one term.

### Q2 — What is the order?

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

The dependency chain for this question:

1. **Perception:** who beneficially owns and controls; what cash-flow rights and what voting rights; what is disclosed. Without this link the other links operate on a false object.
2. **Vocabulary:** separate terms for monitoring (voice, exit) and for private benefits (tunneling, entrenchment); a comparison term for the wedge between control and cash-flow rights. This is the link that usually breaks: no instrument, so nobody looks at it (Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).
3. **Objective:** what counts as accountability here, in the answerer's own terms; what counts as excess. Fig 3 :9-10 *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*.
4. **Planning:** which governance mechanism reaches the objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** verify against an unmoved ground, name what worked, carry the method.

Effort spent on governance mechanisms (step 4) before the vocabulary link (step 2) works is effort above the break. This is why the standard policy question "should ownership be more or less concentrated?" lands above the break: it assumes a vocabulary that merges two conditions into one scalar.

### Q3 — Functional group, not a single lever

Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Both effects share a functional group: an owner with both incentive and power acts on the firm. The qualifier is the bond. A lever that changes only the size of the stake changes the functional group's magnitude and leaves the bond arrangement standing. Fig 9 :56 *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

### Q4 — Recursion and level

Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*. Each condition, once settled against a ground, becomes an atom at the next level: a settled monitoring arrangement becomes a governable firm; a settled diversion arrangement becomes a legal case. Their comparison is second-order. This is recursion, not import; no new primitive is needed.

### Q5 — Analogues from low-overlap fields

- **Engineering (NIST SP 800-160v1r1):** *"Loss control should not rely on a single defensive element."* and *"Redundancy employs multiples of the same system elements, data and control flows, or paths to avoid single points of failure."* Concentrated control is a single decision point: it concentrates the capacity to act and the capacity to fail. The engineering source also warns that *"Redundant solutions are susceptible to common mode failure"*, which is Fig 2 :40's independence rule in another field. Corroborates D3; not a firm-level measurement.
- **Institutional ecology (Ostrom 1990):** *"A regulatory agency, on the other hand, always needs to hire its own monitors."* Ostrom also notes that the parties to a contract monitor each other at low cost. This is the D1 capability claim with the anchor named differently: monitors accountable to the principals are cheaper and better informed. Corroborates D1 and Fig 11 :40 from a genuinely different problem domain, though the framework essay references Ostrom, so the overlap is declared rather than denied.
- **Human cognition (Ross and Anderson 1982):** *"the tendency for attributers to underestimate the impact of situational factors and to overestimate the role of dispositional factors in controlling behavior."* Corroborates Fig 11 :49-51: do not read the owner's sign as a disposition; read the residual and the habitat. A 1981 critique disputes whether this is an error at all; recorded in `04`, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

Under the narrow writing the two effects are different molecules sharing the controlling stake; under the full shared atom set they are isomers, and the difference is carried entirely by the bonds. Either way a single concentration scalar is the flat formula and cannot carry the distinction. The dependency chain breaks at vocabulary unless cash-flow rights, control rights, monitoring and private benefits are named separately. The isomer claim and the chain order both have written falsifiers above. No new primitive was introduced, and the recursion to the second-order comparison needs no import.
