## 0. The claim under audit

> Does concentrated ownership make companies more accountable, or give powerful owners too much control?
>
> Stated as an effect: concentrating ownership moves the residual claim and the control rights into fewer hands. Does the resulting change reduce the gap between what the firm's controllers predict and what its ground returns (more accountability), or does it increase the power of one actor over the firm's assets and its other stakeholders (too much control)? Both options are offered as if one must win.

The shape claim bound before the run, so that it can accrue a residual like any other term (Formula step "Direction"): **concentrated ownership is a union of two conditions, accountability gain and control excess, which can hold at once in the same firm; the anchor that would decide each is external to the party being measured; no figure line yields a single ownership percentage.** This is an ordinary bound term, not a preference smuggled in after the fact. §3 states what refuses it.

## 1. Residual and domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** A multi-place relation among a large shareholder or controller, management, the board, minority shareholders, the firm's assets, and the market or regulator as ground. Ownership concentration is a threshold quantity (a stake above a disclosure or control line), which is the comparison bond's domain: Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (also Operators table :48).
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric. One controller faces many dispersed holders; the monitoring relation (owner acts on manager) and the diversion relation (owner acts on minority) are different bonds, not inverses. Fig 12's → and − are ordered, not symmetric. So the question's "or" cannot be treated as a symmetric trade on one axis.
- **Scale.** Single firm loop, blockholder stratum, population of firms, legal and regulatory habitat, and time (entrenchment). The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30), so an effect at the owner stratum does not copy unchanged to the firm or the market.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Monitoring and accountability** | blockholder, manager, board | monitoring effort, information, voice or exit threat, reduction of R on management actions | realized firm value and the market's payment (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| **D2 Private benefits and control excess** | controlling shareholder, minority holders, firm assets | private benefits of control, diverted assets, control rights exceeding cash-flow rights | minority returns, courts, disclosure, market valuation (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :93) | admitted |
| **D3 Verification and governance frame** | directors, regulator, courts, auditors, market for control | agreement versus residual, convention versus ground | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, Fig 10 :18) | admitted |
| **D4 Entrenchment channel over time** | incumbent controller, pyramids, dual-class stock, disclosure channel | continuity of control, decay of challenge | the ground, which does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 "The owner is good" or "the owner is bad" as a fixed property** | the owner alone | supposed disposition | none nameable independent of a stated residual | **not a domain yet** |
| **X2 "Concentration" as a single lever** | the percentage stake | one scalar | absorbed into D1 and D2 | absorbed |

### 1.3 Partition or union, and dependency order

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D2 overlap in the same firm. Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56 *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Raising or lowering concentration is that single lever. Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* So the two effects must be kept separate and never averaged. The "or" in the question is the error the figure names.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (who beneficially owns and controls, what is disclosed) → vocabulary (cash-flow rights versus control rights, voice versus exit, private benefits) → objective (what counts as accountability, what counts as excess) → planning (which governance mechanism) → learn and carry (verify against an unmoved ground, carry the method). By Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The vocabulary link is where this question usually breaks: without separate terms for cash-flow rights and control rights, the two conditions are read as one scalar and the question becomes a false dichotomy.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The question is a union, not an either/or (decisive)

Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56 *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The two effects are not points on one line. A controlling block can monitor management (D1) and divert value to itself (D2) in the same quarter, through the same voting power. "More accountable" and "too much control" are conditions, not ends of a scale. The correct unit is the firm-slot with both conditions read separately.

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single "net effect of concentration" score is the named error: it averages away the slot where control excess lives.

**Mode `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 9 union rule, Fig 1 per-slot rule, low overlap between the search procedure and the agent's bookkeeping). **Refuter:** a ground-visible measurement showing that one lever changes both conditions in the same direction in every firm, with no categorically different intersection. That would collapse the union to one condition and falsify F1.

### F2 — Accountability is a property of owner and habitat, not of the owner (D1)

Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A large stake is not itself accountability. It becomes monitoring only when the habitat supplies disclosure, an enforceable duty, a board that can act, and a market that pays. This is the figure-grounded reason the question cannot be answered by the size of the stake alone. It also gives the D1 anchor: Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

**Mode `general` for the capability claim, `specified` for the firm application, triangulated** (Fig 11 habitat, Fig 2 anchor, Fig 4 scale, three distinct figures). **Refuter:** a replicated case in which the same stake produces the same measured monitoring improvement after disclosure, enforcement and market payment are all removed, so that contact did not change. That would break Fig 11 :40.

### F3 — "Too much control" is a relation to a residual, not a kind of owner (D2, decisive against the question's framing)

Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The question's phrase "powerful owners" treats power as a property with a fixed sign. The figures say the sign is set by which residual is in view. For the residual "management shirking", a concentrated owner is a benefactor (D1). For the residual "minority expropriation", the same owner is a malfactor (D2). X1, the free-floating good or bad owner, has no nameable anchor and is therefore not a domain yet. It is refused, not averaged.

Fig 11 :52-54 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A related-party transaction is a first-order transfer; its effect on minority returns and on the market's payment is second and third order. Enumeration must go outward (Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*).

**Mode `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 valence, Fig 11 cascade, Fig 9 union). **Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which the same controller, under the same disclosure and enforcement regime and measured against the same anchor, remains a net benefactor for both residuals. That would give the owner a fixed valence and break Fig 11 :49-51.

### F4 — A movable ground is not an anchor (D2 and D3)

Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30 *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :25 *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"*.

Where the controller also writes the disclosure, chooses the auditor, or controls the board that reports the result, the reported number is no longer independent of the actor being measured. Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Fig 10 :86 *"Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."*

**Re-census note, not a retraction.** An earlier reading of this section treated a managed disclosure channel as a moved anchor. That strains Fig 2 :33-34, which says the anchor becomes another frame only if the anchor itself updates. Re-census: the market's payment is the anchor; the controller-managed disclosure is a degraded channel (Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*). The anchor did not move; access to it did. The verdict is unchanged and the strain is dissolved by naming the correct object. This is the README rule that a reading making figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

**Mode `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor, Fig 10 ground maintainer). **Refuter:** a case in which the market's payment itself changes when only the controller's opinion changes, with information and enforcement held fixed, and no independent frame can range against an unmoved ground. That would break Fig 2 :29-30.

### F5 — Conventions cannot settle what they are meant to measure (D3)

Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*. Fig 10 :19 *"install what can be violated"*. Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 2 :43 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.

Governance ratings, board-compliance checklists and "independent director" labels are conventions: they make a bug statable, they do not fix it. A governance score that is used as the verdict has become a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority. This is why the question is not settled by counting board structures or ownership percentages, and why the remedy must be installable and violable.

Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A chorus of governance committees agreeing is panel 1 or panel 2 of Fig 2, not triangulation. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Mode `general` for the convention rule, `specified` for the governance application, triangulated** (Fig 10, Fig 2, distinct panels). **Refuter:** a convention whose declaration changes the ground in the same way an instrument does, replicated across independent frames. That would collapse the distinction Fig 10 :46 draws between a convention and a verdict.

### F6 — Entrenchment is a channel failure over time, not a different owner (D4)

Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.

Control-enhancing mechanisms (pyramids, dual-class shares, cross-holdings) keep the same controller in place across time. The ground keeps paying or not paying; what decays is the channel by which a challenge reaches it.Entrenchment is therefore D4, a distinct domain with its own loop, not a second name for D2. It needs its own instrument: whether a challenge can still reach a ground that has not moved.

**Mode `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 10 channel, Fig 7 room chain, Fig 11 cascade). **Refuter:** entrenchment measured as a decline in the ground's payment while the challenge channel tests high and non-declining on independent instruments. That would locate the failure in the ground, not the channel, and break Fig 10 :24.

### F7 — Selecting checkers for agreement disqualifies their agreement (outreach)

Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*. Fig 10 :60 *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

A list of recipients chosen because they already favour concentrated ownership, or favour dispersed ownership, is a list selected to agree. Its agreement is a convention, not corroboration. The first contact must carry the residual and its refuter, and the verdict must be recorded before the framework is shown (Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*). This is a contact rule, never an evidence weight.

**Mode `general`, triangulated** (Fig 2, Fig 10, seat rules). **Refuter:** a demonstration that two frames with declared, structurally different stakes and no shared evidence pool still produce the same failure to refute a false shape claim. That would weaken the independence requirement; it would not flip the union verdict.

### F8 — No figure line yields the ownership percentage (`gap`)

Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.

After a genuine census, no figure line states a number, a range, or a functional form linking stake size to net accountability. The figures license a threshold shape (there is a comparison; there are two quantities; the relation is not monotone by construction) but no value. World sources do report non-monotonicity and a control-versus-cash-flow wedge (see `09_watson_answer.md`), but they are secondary and they describe the world, not the figure set. So the ownership percentage is declared `gap`, not filled with a borrowed number. Note that 5 percent is a legal disclosure convention, not a figure-derived threshold; using it as an answer would repeat F5.

**Mode `general` for the shape, `gap` for the value. Refuter:** a figure re-census that finds a line directly governing a stake percentage, or a ground reading that settles the percentage; either would move this item from `gap` to `specified` or `grounded`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before outreach; a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The union shape claim and its per-domain verdicts are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The same controller is shown, under one stated residual, one anchor and one measurement regime, to be a net benefactor for both management shirking and minority expropriation (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).
2. A ground-visible measurement shows that one concentration lever moves accountability and control excess in the same direction in every firm, with no categorically different intersection (breaks Fig 9 :55-57 and Fig 1 :19).
3. The market's payment is shown to move when only the controller's opinion moves, with information and enforcement held fixed and no independent frame able to range against an unmoved ground (breaks Fig 2 :29-30).
4. A challenge channel tests high and non-declining on independent instruments while the ground's payment declines (breaks Fig 10 :24).

A fifth item would refute the `gap`, not the union: a figure line that directly governs a stake percentage. Until one of these is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement; send the refutation, not agreement.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union finding (F1) and the refused category (F3). Ask what would refute them, not whether the reader agrees.
2. Keep D1, D2, D3 and D4 separate in any write-up. Never report a "net effect of concentration".
3. Describe the residual before naming a frame or a percentage (Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*; Fig 9 :30-31 *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*).
4. Put the remedy as an anchor that cannot be moved by the party being measured (Fig 2 :25, Fig 10 :86, :93), not as a call for more or less concentration.
5. Declare the threshold as `gap` (F8). Use filters (tier, field, venue) for budget only; Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
6. Figure 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator: the census, the refuter and the per-domain verdicts, not the conclusion.
