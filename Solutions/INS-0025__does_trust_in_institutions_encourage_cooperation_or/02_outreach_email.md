# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide which reading is true

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker has confirmed the shape claim in §1 survives. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on the trust/cooperation question, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in institutional research: does trust in institutions encourage cooperation, or do institutions work well mainly where trust already exists? I have one output I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with either reading. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain claims; not a ground reading):**
>
> 1. The forced choice is chain-shaped, and the thing being asked about may be a cycle. The figure set makes the two different structures: a sequence, and a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes. A chain cannot close by construction. So "A or B" is a union of at least four conditions, not one question: trust enabling cooperation, institutions producing trust, a shared third factor, and the possibility of no relation in a given setting.
> 2. Covariance cannot order the direction. Two ways to get a correlation without a causal verdict are two frames updating on each other, and two frames never talking but drawing on one evidence pool. Trust and cooperation can produce each other, or both be driven by conditions such as local inequality or enforcement history.
> 3. Institutional capability is a property of institution-and-habitat, never of the institution alone. Moving an institution into a low-trust setting is not removing a habitat; it is substituting one, and you inherit actors you did not choose.
> 4. The residual has to be read per slot. A global average can be near zero while one agency or one community carries a live signal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have settled whether A or B is true. I have not. My derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on. I am also not claiming that self-reported trust is the ground, or that a laboratory public-goods game demonstrates field capability. Both are instruments, and instruments can be wrong on the axes they do not measure.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A randomized intervention on trust, holding institution and habitat fixed, produces a monotone per-slot change in measured cooperation in two independent frames ranging against the same unmoved anchor, with no reverse path. That kills the union/shape claim.
> 2. An institution transplanted into a low-trust setting produces sustained cooperation with no reproduction of the trust habitat and no imported actors. That kills the habitat claim.
> 3. A re-census shows the trust/cooperation relation is a simple chain whose direction is derivable without an anchor. That kills the figure reading.
>
> If any of those holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract, cited as Fig N :line): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The loop and its mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, because the sought-for shape is a preference, not a term it holds. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## Two-stage rule

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape, the per-domain verdicts and the three refuters. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three local framework paths above. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").

A Stage 1 reply is a conversation, not a witness. If it contains a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it in the same way as any other source.
