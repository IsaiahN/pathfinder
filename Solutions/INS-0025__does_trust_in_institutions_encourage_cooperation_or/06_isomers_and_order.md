# Bond type, cycle sign and level order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The forced choice assumes a single directed bond between trust and cooperation. Three questions are owed: (1) what kind of bond is actually being claimed, (2) is the relation a chain or a cycle, and (3) at which level does each claim hold.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held in this frame: Trust (T), Institution (I), Cooperation (C), plus habitat conditions (H). Bonds available: the seven (Fig 12), plus negation, which is not a bond. No new primitive is assumed. The question's own vocabulary already contains the three atoms; what is missing is the bond and its sign, not the nouns.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bond is "encourage"?

"Encourage" reads as production or sequence. Fig 12 defines them differently:

- sequence, →: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Operators table :31; Fig 12 :72).
- production, ⇒: "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Operators table :37).
- comparison, ⋛: "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :49).

Cooperation exists before any given trust intervention, so ⇒ is mistyped here. "Institutions work well where trust already exists" reads as a threshold or gating relation, closer to ⋛, but the operands are not two quantities in one frame; they are a condition and a behaviour in a habitat. Both readings are bond claims, and the bond "is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording" (Fig 12 :5). **Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."** Naming T, I and C settles nothing about which molecule they form.

**Falsifier (Q1):** show that the relation is recoverable from the three atoms alone, that is, that the same three atoms under two different bonds give the same per-slot prediction. Then the bond is free and Fig 12 is misapplied here.

### Q2: Chain or cycle?

Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

The candidate structures:

| structure | spelling | what it predicts |
|---|---|---|
| A: T → C | one directed sequence | raising trust raises cooperation; no reverse path |
| B: H → T, H → I → C | habitat as common cause | both trust and cooperation track the same conditions; I works only with H |
| cycle: T → C → I_performance → T | output re-enters as input | each pass changes the next; sign decides whether trust accumulates or depletes |
| none: T ∥ C (disjunction) | either suffices, not both required | cooperation can arise from enforcement alone |

Fig 12 :121 rules out treating a closing loop as a chain. It does not tell us which of the four holds in any slot. The world sources occupy different rows: Kim et al. 2022 and Kuipers 2022 speak to row 1; Martinangeli et al. 2023, Murtin et al. 2026 and Goist & Kern 2018 to rows 2 and 3; Bauer et al. 2019 to row 4. Dinesen et al. 2020 supplies a habitat condition. All four rows are live. Mode `specified`.

**Falsifier (Q2):** a within-slot time series in which cooperation changes the next trust reading and the next institutional-performance reading, with a measured sign, in two low-overlap frames. That would name the cycle and close the sub-gap; if no such path is found after the intervention described in R1, the chain rows win and F1 of the audit must be withdrawn.

### Q3: Are trust and cooperation isomers, or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two institutions with the same formal design, one in a high-trust habitat and one in a low-trust habitat, share the atom I and differ in the bond to T and H. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same formula, different substance, decisive behavioural difference. This is the precise figure-grounded content of "similar institutional designs produce different outcomes in different societies" (a neighbouring question in the same source file, used here only as an analogue, not as a citation of evidence).

If instead we compare "trust-based cooperation" and "enforcement-based compliance" across many settings, they share only the outcome C and differ in the whole mediating structure. Under Fig 12 :124 they are members of a **functional group**, not isomers: the group says cooperation, the qualifier says which route. The distinction is testable, not rhetorical.

**Falsifier (Q3):** two institutional designs that share every atom and bond yet produce the same per-slot outcome after habitat is varied. That collapses isomer to identity and removes the figure's leverage.

### Q4: Order by level, not by prestige

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

| level | what is measured | which claim is assessable there |
|---|---|---|
| micro (individual) | a person's expectation of another's cooperation and their own contribution | row 1, the trust-to-cooperation path in a single situation |
| meso (agency, community) | compliance rates, contribution rates, perceived fairness of enforcement | rows 2 and 3, the institution-and-habitat claims |
| macro (society) | cross-national trust items, institutional quality indices | common-cause and trend claims only; no direction |

A macro correlation cannot validate a micro mechanism; that is the aggregation error of Fig 1 :19. A lab result at micro level cannot validate meso capability; that is Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." The dependency order is micro measurement, then meso habitat, then macro pattern. Effort spent at macro level before the micro ground is named arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20).

**Falsifier (Q4):** a macro-level index that predicts a specific micro-level intervention outcome better than the micro-level reading itself, across two low-overlap frames. Then the dependency order is reversed here and must be rewritten.

### Q5: Trust as catalyst or as spent stock

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." The amendment in the same figure says spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses and nothing records which a term is. Applied here: a trust stock that is spent by cooperation predicts decline after repeated use; a catalytic trust predicts persistence or regeneration. The figure supplies both possibilities and no selector. Kuipers 2022 describes the regenerative side; the encyclopedic source records an asymmetry between building and destroying trust. Neither is a ground reading.

**Verdict:** open, `specified`, single-source for the figure definition. **Falsifier:** a monotonic per-slot depletion or regeneration series in two frames, which would name the sign.

## CONCLUSION

The atoms are already held; what is missing is the bond, the loop sign and the level order. A-or-B mistakes a union compound for a single sequence. Fig 12 :121 makes a closing loop a different structure from a chain, Fig 12 :117 and :124 give a testable isomer versus functional-group distinction for cross-setting comparisons, and Fig 3 :20 plus Fig 11 :70 order the levels so that a lab result cannot be promoted to field capability. None of this orders trust against cooperation; it constrains the shape of any answer that claims to.
