# The Tether's answer: trust in institutions and cooperation

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain claims, `gap` on the direction. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Trust in institutions and cooperative behaviour covary. Reading A says the trust causes the cooperation. Reading B says the institution works only where trust already exists. What each leads to is a different lever and a different ground: A changes trust and watches cooperation; B enumerates the habitat and watches whether a transplanted institution still works. The residual is the *structure* of that covariance, read per slot (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated").

## 2. The shape, by domain

**D1, figure-internal structure.** The either/or is a chain-shaped framing of a union compound. Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." At least four conditions are in play: trust enabling cooperation, institutions producing trust, a shared third factor, and no relation in some slots. A chain cannot represent a closing loop. Verdict: refuse the forced choice. Triangulated.

**D2, institutional capability and habitat.** Capability is institution-and-habitat. Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable" Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Moving an institution into a low-trust setting substitutes a habitat and inherits actors you did not choose (Fig 11 :66). Verdict: B-like transfer warning holds as a constraint, not as a direction verdict. Triangulated.

**D3, intervention and measurement.** Ordering A against B needs an anchor that does not update. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A randomized reading in one frame, or agreement between frames sharing an evidence pool, does not supply one. Verdict: ordering is reachable in principle but not reached here. Single-source for the structural requirement (Fig 2 plus Fig 8 :29), with world sources showing mixed results.

**D4, instruments.** Survey trust and laboratory cooperation are instruments, not the ground. Fig 2 :37 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Verdict: readings must be ranged against behaviour that does not move with opinion. Single-source for the figure rule, corroborated by the measurement disagreements in the world literature.

**Direction:** `gap`. Not a silence. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The gap says what is owed: a source-discriminating instrument plus two low-overlap frames.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that A is true. Raising reported trust is not shown to raise cooperation.
- Not that B is true. "Institutions work where trust exists" is a habitat constraint, not a demonstrated causal direction.
- Not that there is no relation. Bauer et al. 2019 found none in one real situation; that is a per-slot result (Fig 1 :19), not a universal null.
- Not that the figures settle an empirical question. They constrain the shape; the ground settles the direction.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this derivation ranks A against B.
- Not a claim that this simulation validates itself. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." The corresponding readings are:

1. **Direction test:** a randomized intervention on trust with institution and habitat held fixed, producing a monotone per-slot change in measured cooperation, repeated in two low-overlap frames against the same unmoved anchor, with no reverse path. If it fires, A is ordered in those slots and the union reading for them is withdrawn.
2. **Transplant test:** an institution moved into a low-trust habitat producing sustained per-slot cooperation with no reproduction of the trust habitat and no imported actors. If it fires, the habitat constraint is wrong.
3. **Sign test:** a within-slot time series showing whether cooperation depletes or regenerates the next trust reading (Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use.").
4. **Standing refuters from the audit:** a figure re-census that admits a simple chain whose direction is derivable without an anchor; a demonstration that global aggregation predicts per-slot outcomes better than per-slot readings.

Until one of these fires, the verdict is `candidate`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "trust encourages cooperation" or "institutions only work where trust already exists" as a single choice. It says the choice is chain-shaped while the thing being asked about may be a cycle: trust, institutions and cooperation form a union of conditions whose direction cannot be ordered by their covariance. It says institutional capability belongs to institution-and-habitat, so transplanting a design into a low-trust setting substitutes a habitat and brings actors you did not choose. It says the residual must be read per slot, that a survey reading is an instrument and not the ground, and that the anchor used to order the direction must not move when a frame's opinion changes. What it offers is that shape, three refuters written in advance, and the readings that would close it. The direction stays open until the ground settles it.
