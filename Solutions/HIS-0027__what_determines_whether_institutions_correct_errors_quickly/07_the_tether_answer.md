# The Tether's answer: what determines institutional error-correction speed

**Mode: `general` on the structural conditions, `specified` on the latency prediction, not
`grounded`.** A shape and a dependency order, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked
for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An institution holds a library Γ of claims, standards and categories. An error is a held claim the
ground does not pay. Correction is the event in which that claim loses its standing to be composed
over. The question is what sets the lag between arrival and correction. The residual is indexed per
case, not as an institutional average: Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not
aggregated."* An error is the fifth kind of gap, the one that says something held is false, not that
something is missing (Fig 5 :53). That is why a purely additive review process cannot reach it.

## 2. The shape: five conditions in dependency order

1. **A non-updating anchor.** Correction is possible only if the thing the institution ranges against
   does not change its answer when members push on it. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the
   question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor
   must not update."* If it does, Fig 2 :34 says *"everything drifts together, and the drift is
   coherent, which is why nobody notices."* This is the root condition: it sits beneath the chain.
2. **Per-case measurement.** Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating
   across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single accuracy KPI can be near
   zero while one live case carries a large residual.
3. **An operator for refusal.** Fig 12 :139-140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*
   The operator is unary negation, not a bond: Operators table :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which
   is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A retraction, correction or named failure
   class removes a claim's standing to be composed over while adding a term to the record (Fig 5 :55
   *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."*).
4. **A committed failure condition, written before review.** Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go
   looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 5 :65 says make the sought-for shape *"an
   ORDINARY TERM"* so it can accrue a residual and be refused. This is the middle link of Fig 3's
   chain, the one with no obvious instrument, and Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link
   that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
5. **Independent frames and a maintained channel.** Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room.
   The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 8 :45 *"Two
   frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* And Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay,
   the channel does"*, with :25-26 naming the decay modes: *"stale percepts, dropped transients,
   saturated metrics, pooled readings"*.

The order matters. It is a sequence of products, not a checklist (Fig 3 :20; Fig 5 :33 *"The three
guards are a product, not a checklist."*). Counting how many conditions an institution appears to
satisfy reproduces the aggregation error in F5.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named institution is fast or slow. No institution was measured.
- Not that prestige, publication volume, or the number of reviewers determines correction. Fig 9 :60
  *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
  Prestige can fund a review; it cannot settle what the review found.
- Not that agreement between reviewers is evidence of correction. Fig 2 :40 agrees with this: *"agreement
  about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Legitimate
  agreement is convergent derivation from separate experience, which is a different provenance.
- Not that any particular instrument, registered report, red team, court or retraction policy is *the*
  cure. The framework returns a shape, not a name; binding an instrument is provenance work that
  needs a ground.
- Not that correction is the same as compliance or as the absence of published errors. A low
  retraction count has several possible sources (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §4).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A measurement of correction latency per case, against an anchor that did not move during the study,
with the five conditions varied and the frame-to-anchor overlap recorded. Concretely:

1. **Anchor test.** Did the standard of correctness change in response to member opinion during the
   window? If yes, there is no anchor for that case and the case is out of scope.
2. **Channel test.** Were the readings fresh, or were they stale, dropped, saturated or pooled? The
   decoder essay's instrument here is *"watch the trend in correction size, not the count of lessons
   given."*
3. **Refusal test.** Did a held claim lose its standing to be composed over, with the refusal
   retained in the record, or did the institution only rephrase its descriptions?
4. **Commitment test.** Was a failure condition written before the review? Popper's formulation:
   *"criteria of refutation have to be laid down beforehand"* (SEP entry, quoting *Conjectures and
   Refutations*, read 2026-11-23).
5. **Independence test.** Did the checking frames draw on different evidence, and was the room refreshed
   when the question moved? Fig 10 :71 *"When the question changes, the room should change."*

Until such a study exists, the verdict is `candidate`, not `grounded`. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that
finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 5. The honest stop

Where the anchor no longer exists, for example a historical case whose records did not survive, no
determinant can be verified. That is reported as a **gap**, not as a silence: Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable
is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming
it would mean already holding it."* Two strains remain named, not hidden: historical grounds are
often remote, and institutional maintainers usually have standing that survives the outcome they
earned it on, which the seat map says must be declared rather than removed. The answer stops where
the instruments stop. Unreached, not unreachable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that some institutions are simply better at self-correction. It says that
> correction speed is governed by five conditions in dependency order: an anchor that does not move
> when members push on it; a residual measured per case rather than as one average; an operator for
> saying "this held claim is false"; a failure condition written down before the review; and
> checking frames that differ from one another and keep a live channel to the anchor. Agreement,
> prestige and review volume are filters for what gets tried, never witnesses for whether a
> correction happened. If the anchor updates on opinion, the institution drifts coherently and nobody
> notices; if it cannot write a refusal, it can only accumulate. What this offers is a shape, the
> order of its conditions, the instruments that would test each one, and refuters written in advance.
> No institution was measured here, so nothing in it is claimed as settled.
