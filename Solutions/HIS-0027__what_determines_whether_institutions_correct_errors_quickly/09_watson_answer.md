## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what sets the lag between an error entering an institution's library of
held claims and the institution's library being corrected. Option A: the lag is set by the quality
of the people, by prestige, or by review volume. Option B: the lag is set by a dependency order of
five conditions, a non-updating anchor, per-case residuals, a refusal operator, a pre-committed
failure condition, and independent frames with a maintained channel; agreement, prestige and volume
are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict. Option A ends with a score the institution
produces about itself. Option B ends with a shape, refuters, and instruments that a person with a
real ground could test.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition
or union; order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R.** The lag between an error arriving and a library being corrected. Arity: multi-slot, many
  claims at once; the global average is not the signal (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: an error is the fifth
  kind of gap, a held claim that is false, which requires unary negation and is not a symmetric join
  of two frames (Fig 5 :53). Scale: an inner perceive-to-settle loop inside one institution and an
  outer frame-refresh loop across scales; a lag can enter at either.
- **D1, anchor and frames.** Actors: the ground and the member frames. Currency: residuals; the anchor
  does not update on frames. Anchor: a measurement, replication, counter or outcome that is
  constitutively about the question (Fig 2 :29, :36). Type: triangulation, not negotiation.
- **D2, chamber of frames.** Actors: peers, reviewers, committees, red teams. Currency: agreement
  versus residual; independence is how little evidence is shared. Anchor: the same ground, ranged
  against separately by each frame (Fig 2 :46). Type: union when an error sits in two conditions,
  otherwise partition.
- **D3, channel and maintainer seat.** Actors: editors, registries, ombuds, auditors. Currency:
  contact with the ground, conventions versus verdicts. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay
  while the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Type: a seat office, not a loop step.
- **D4, vocabulary of refusal.** Actors: the library and the negation operator. Currency: whether an
  institution can write that a held claim is false. Anchor: settlement of a minted refusal by the
  ground (Fig 5 :70). Type: partition, refuse or accumulate.
- **D5, measurement.** Actors: cases, KPIs, dashboards. Currency: per-case residual versus global
  average. Anchor: the live case's residual (Fig 1 :19). Type: partition, per slot or averaged.
- **D6, verification of this answer.** Actors: expert readers and this simulated derivation.
  Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: **absent in this run**, since no correction latency was
  measured. Type: same evidence pool, so independence is zero. Recorded as a gap, not a domain with
  a verdict.

Order by dependency: anchor beneath the chain, then per-case measurement, refusal, committed
failure condition, independent frames, maintained channel, settlement with provenance. Strain test:
Fig 2 and Fig 10 both speak of the ground, but one governs triangulation between frames and the other
the seat that keeps the channel reachable. Different offices, so no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures decide the verdict, not the world sources. Correction latency is governed by five
conditions in dependency order: a non-updating anchor, per-case residuals, a refusal operator, a
failure condition committed before review, and independent frames with a maintained channel.
Agreement, prestige and volume are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. Where
the anchor updates on opinion, the system drifts coherently and nobody notices; where no refusal
operator exists, it can only accumulate. The result is a `specified` prediction with a `general`
structural spine, not a `grounded` finding. D1, D2 and D4 are triangulated; D3 and D5 are
single-source in the figures and world-corroborated; D6 is a gap. The strongest single world source
is Ioannidis (2005) on prestige suppressing refuting findings, which is independent of the framework
and points at the same channel failure as Fig 10.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and
  refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is
  sending a recording instead of a generator and thereby spending the independence of outside
  readers, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That
  cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The five conditions and their order are removable without touching the figure
  text, and no claim about a named institution was made. A superseding packet costs only the work of
  rewriting it. No primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be corrected, and the record of
  the correction is what lets a later reader locate the error.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measurement of correction latency per case,
  against an anchor that did not move during the study, with the five conditions varied and
  frame-to-anchor overlap recorded, would settle it. So would a demonstrated case of durable
  correction with a moving anchor, with no refusal operator, or with two frames sharing one evidence
  pool producing a surplus neither had. A figure re-census that shows the dependency order commutes
  would reverse C3. Prestige, agreement and publication volume are not such instruments.

**Stake declaration.** The framework author benefits from the framework being found useful, and this
derived answer is favourable to it. That interest cannot be removed, only declared (Fig 10 :60, seat
map :171). It is declared here, and it is why the world sources from other authors are weighted more
heavily in the corroboration record, while the verdict remains with the figures.
