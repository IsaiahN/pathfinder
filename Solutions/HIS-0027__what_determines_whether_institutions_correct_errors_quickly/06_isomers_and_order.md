# Order, isomers and analogues for the five conditions

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading
(see `05_what_shape_means.md` §8).

## RESIDUAL

`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` returns five conditions. Three further questions are owed: in what
order do they stand; are two institutions with the same visible parts **isomers** under Fig 12; and
which of Fig 12's four structures (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group) actually applies?
This file answers those without adding a sixth condition.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the order of the five conditions?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one
above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Apply the test "does this condition consume what the previous one produces?":

1. **Anchor.** Consumes nothing from the other four; it is the ground underneath the chain. Without
   it there is nothing to range against. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and
   does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
2. **Per-slot measurement.** Consumes the anchor, because a residual is a gap between prediction
   and what the anchor returns. Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
3. **Refusal operator.** Consumes the measured residual, because a term is refused only when its
   residual *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* (Fig 5 :59). A refusal without a residual is a
   preference, not a correction.
4. **Independent frames.** Consume the anchor and each other's difference. Fig 7 :36 *"The next room
   must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* The residual from step 3 selects
   the next frame.
5. **Maintained channel.** Consumes all four, because it is the seat's office to keep each of them
   reachable over time. Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*
6. **Settlement and carry.** Consumes a minted refusal, with provenance. Fig 10 :65-66 *"Provenance
   shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that."*

The order is a **sequence**, bond `→`: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Operators table :30).
It is not a conjunction, because the conditions do not merely coexist; the later ones consume the
earlier ones' output. But the *guards* within each condition are a product: Fig 5 :33 *"The three
guards are a product, not a checklist."* So the structure is a sequence of products, not five
independent checkboxes. A reader who scores institutions by counting which boxes are ticked has
made Fig 1 :22's aggregation error.

**Falsifier for Q1:** exhibit a durable correction in which the channel was restored before any
non-updating anchor existed, or in which frames were selected before any residual was described,
across multiple cases. Either would break the sequence and reduce the order to a set.

### Q2: Are two institutions with the same visible parts isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Take two institutions with the same listed atoms: a journal, a registry, an editorial board, a
retraction policy. Institution X ranges each claim against an external measurement that the board
cannot move; disagreement between reviewers is used as information. Institution Y uses the same
parts but treats the senior editor's judgement as the final standard and the registry as a record of
decisions. Same atoms, different bond: X uses `⋛` triangulation, Y uses a negotiation bond that is
not one of the seven (a claim that has not been made, per Fig 12 :90-91). Under Fig 12 they are
**isomers**, and no list of their parts will tell you which is which. The bond is the rest of the
information, and it is the half nothing was recording (Fig 12 :5).

**Falsifier for Q2:** show that the two institutions produce the same correction behaviour, measured
per slot against a non-updating anchor, across multiple cases. Then the bond carries nothing and
the isomer claim fails.

### Q3: Which of Fig 12's four structures apply?

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- **Catalyst applies, and it is the sharpest analogue.** A checking body loses independence each
  time it is used: *"Every correction raises correlation between the corrector and the corrected"*
  (seat map shelf life). A permanent red team, ombuds, or rotating audit is a part that survives its
  own use only if it is regenerated by rotation or separate experience (Fig 10 :80-81). A one-off
  review committee is a spent part. Nothing in the organigram records which one it is, which is
  exactly Fig 12's warning.
- **Cycle applies conditionally.** A correction closes the loop only if the refusal re-enters as a
  term in the library: Fig 5 :55 *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* and Fig 6
  :42-46 on the monotone record and non-recurring refusal. An institution that issues a correction
  and then deletes the record has a chain, not a cycle; a chain cannot close by construction.
- **Functional group applies to the pair anchor plus refusal.** Those two fix the kind of thing a
  correction is; the qualifier, replication, court ruling, audit, or retraction, says which member.
  This is why the audit's findings name the condition and leave the instrument open.
- **Isomer applies across institutions** (Q2), not within the five conditions. The conditions do not
  share one atom set and differ only by bond, so they are not isomers of one another.

### Q4: What bond holds between independence and the anchor?

Not `+` (both hold at once) alone, and not negotiation. Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground.
They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :46 *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes
the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."* Operators table :50 gives `⋛` as *"a
threshold relation between two quantities"* with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than
both being present?"* The relation here is of the second kind: each frame's prediction is compared
against what the anchor returns, and the comparison decides. So `⋛` is the correct glyph for the
frame-to-anchor relation, with the type caveat that the operands are a frame output and an anchor
reading at the same level. Negation `¬` remains outside the bonds: it acts on one held claim, joins
nothing, and is what makes a refusal possible at all (Operators table :70-74).

**Falsifier for Q4:** show that the frame-to-anchor relation is symmetric, that is, that the anchor's
answer changes when a frame pushes on it while still settling the question legitimately. That would
put the relation in Fig 2's mutual-update panel and break the `⋛` reading.

## CONCLUSION

The five conditions form a **sequence of products**, ordered by dependency, with the anchor beneath
the chain and `¬` operating on a held claim at step 3. Two institutions with the same visible parts
can be **isomers** whose difference is carried entirely by the bond between frames and anchor. Of
Fig 12's four structures, **catalyst** and **cycle** apply with stated conditions, **functional
group** applies to the anchor-plus-refusal pair, and **isomer** applies across institutions. This
rescues the parts of an institutional comparison that can be checked structurally: the order, the
bond type, and whether a checking part is spent or regenerating, without naming the one correct
reform for any institution.

## FALSIFIER (one paragraph)

The whole supporting derivation is refuted if any of the following holds across multiple measured
cases: the dependency sequence commutes; two institutions with identical atoms and different bonds
show identical per-slot correction behaviour; a checking body's independence does not decay with use
or is restored by effort alone; or a frame-to-anchor relation is shown to be symmetric while still
settling the question. Any one fires the re-census, not a softer reading.
