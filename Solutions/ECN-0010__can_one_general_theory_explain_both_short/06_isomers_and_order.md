# Are a short recession and a depression isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `gap` on the discriminator. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).**

## RESIDUAL

The audit left three questions open. (1) If both classes are downturns over a shared atom set, are they isomers under Fig 12, or something weaker? (2) What is the dependency order of the pieces, so that a claim does not price a composition before its subject exists? (3) What analogues from genuinely different fields bear on the shape of the missing discriminator, and what weight do they carry?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition? (decides whether there is one thing to explain)

- Fig 9 :4: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- The world sources support the union reading: the propagation literatures fit ordinary recessions, the persistence literatures fit deep and long ones, and the 2025 JEL review holds the negative-natural-rate condition and the regime switch together in one framework rather than reducing one to the other.
- ⇒ The two classes are not a partition of downturn kinds. They are a **union of conditions over one trajectory**, with an overlap where both propagation and persistence bind. One lever clears the short-only region and leaves the overlap standing. Triangulated (Fig 9 :4/:55 with the world literature, low overlap for the complexity sources).

### Q2: Functional group or isomer?

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Both classes share the substructure `state -> structure -> state`: output and employment (states) composed through a propagation or persistence structure into a recovery or a further decline. That shared substructure is the functional group. It fixes the kind, "downturn with propagation". The qualifier, "which bond closes", fixes the member.
- If the atom set is held fixed and the two episodes differ only in arrangement (which structure mediates which state pair, and whether a regime refusal fires), they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in the aggregate GDP path and decisive in which policy pays.
- If one episode requires an atom absent from the other (a depression with no counterpart to ordinary propagation), the isomer claim fails and only the functional group remains.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit an episode pair in which one class requires an atom absent from the other's closure, so the fixed-atom condition breaks. Then withdraw the isomer claim and keep the functional-group claim. Single-source for the figure definition; the fixed-atom condition stays live.

### Q3: What is the order of the pieces?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Dependency order: (1) per-slot residual and a vocabulary for what an episode affords; (2) an objective, meaning the declared duration/depth comparator; (3) the propagation loop; (4) the persistence loop and the regime refusal; (5) the cross-episode unifying claim.
- The break is between (1) and (3). There is no instrument that assigns a live episode to a region before the duration is known, and the output-gap source notes that potential output "is not an observable variable". Work spent refining (4) before (1) arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- The regime refusal must come before the persistence mint: Formula step 2 sorts a REFUTED residual by what is wrong, and no arrangement of the four "what is missing" bins reaches it. Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- **Order strain, named:** the world sources report regime changes as historical events recovered after the fact. The framework's order requires the refusal to be available as a term before the prediction. That is a claim about model structure, not about the historian's reconstruction; the instrument that would settle it is the same missing selector.

### Q4: Cycle sign and the threshold cut

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 13 :31-32: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise. Debt deflation is the positive-feedback case; a damped inventory cycle is the negative case.
- Operators :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." The two-year or ten-percent cut is a `⋛` threshold on duration and depth. It is a filter for the budget and a convention for the label; it is not a witness for the mechanism (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- **Falsifier:** show a depression-class episode whose persistence is fully accounted for by an open chain, or a damped loop with depression-class depth and duration. Then F4 of the audit is fired.

### Q5: Analogues from low-overlap fields, with their weight

These are analogues for the **shape** of the missing instrument, not economic evidence.

| analogue | source | what it predicts about the selector | weight |
|---|---|---|---|

No analogue supplies a name for the selector. They constrain what it must look like: per-slot, directional, regime-invariant, and built from a series already returning something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :34: "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there.").

### Q6: The second-order comparison and its operand type

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Each episode settled against the data becomes an atom at the cross-episode level. A general theory of the two classes composes over settled episodes; it needs no seventh primitive.
- The comparison of two regions at that level uses `⋛`, which the Operators table defines as "a threshold relation between two quantities". At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Fig 12 :104 licenses the demotion to atom, but no figure settles whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level.
- **Strain, live:** the operand type of `⋛` at second order. It is named here and in `08`, `09` and `11`, not hidden. An instrument, not an argument, would settle it.

## CONCLUSION

Short recession and prolonged depression are best read as **functional-group variants** of one downturn substructure, and as **isomers** only under a fixed atom set with a stated falsifier. Their dependency order puts the per-slot residual and the region discriminator first, the propagation loop second, the signed persistence cycle and regime refusal third, and the cross-episode unifying claim last; the chain currently breaks at the discriminator. Low-overlap analogues constrain the shape of the owed instrument without naming it. The second-order comparison is legal under Fig 12 :104 but carries a live operand-type strain. This derivation rescues what is defensible in "one general theory" (one generator over a fixed atom set, with a written refusal and a declared gap) without the part that fails (a final covering law with one parameter set and no region selector).
