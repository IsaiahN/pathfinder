# Outreach draft: validate the shape claim first, then decide about any theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked against the verbatim figure text. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** A split I would like you to check before we argue about theories of recessions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know about recession and depression data. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** We observe short recessions and prolonged depressions. My claim is that "short" and "prolonged" are threshold cuts on one measured trajectory, not two natural kinds, and that the honest object is a union of overlapping conditions with a per-episode and per-slot residual. A national average of recession durations throws away the signal that says where to look. The figure I am relying on says: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." (Fig 9 :4)
>
> **The shape claim.** A theory can be general in the sense of a generator that composes per episode, provided its closure contains three things: a threshold comparison that defines the duration and depth cut; a persistence loop whose sign decides whether it closes; and a refusal operation for a policy-regime change, because a vocabulary that can only add mechanisms cannot record a reversal. What the framework cannot supply is the real-time selector that tells you, before the outcome, which region a live episode is entering. That selector is a `gap`. I am reporting it as a verdict, not as a silence, and I am not going to invent a name for it.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that this answers whether Keynes, Friedman, Minsky or any modern unified review is right. I am not claiming that a depression is just a bigger recession, or that a single parameter set can fit both classes. I am not claiming to have measured anything. This is a `specified` structural claim from a simulated derivation, not a ground reading.
>
> **The category warning.** The boundary at "two years" or "a ten percent fall" is a declared convention. I will not let the label decide the mechanism. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before writing any of the above:
> 1. Show that short and long episodes are one condition observed at two horizons, and that one lever clears both the short-only and the overlap regions with a per-slot residual that trends down across independent data vintages.
> 2. Show a depression-class episode whose persistence is fully accounted for by an open chain with no re-entrant output, or whose loop is damped while depth and duration remain depression-class.
> 3. Show a depth-to-recovery transition explained entirely by adding a new propagation term, with no refusal of any held policy convention, and with the residual trending down.
> 4. Produce a real-time selector with a non-updating anchor and low-overlap instruments that assigns episodes to regions before the duration is known and survives a data revision.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The 2025 JEL review that attempts a unified framework, for comparison with my shape claim: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20241306
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the loop is in the local formula file. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a committed prediction is the only discipline available here. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

**Two-stage discipline.** Stage 1 is the shape claim and its refuter. Record the recipient's verdict on stage 1 *before* any theory is shown. Stage 2 is opt in: only if they are curious, offer the links. Stage 3 offers the loop as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user declares a ground and writes a refuter before running it. A stage-2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once the recipient has absorbed the interior they have joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).
