**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Are tipping cascades physically plausible at the global scale, and which links in proposed chains are well supported? Restated as an effect: does a threshold crossing in element A compose through physical contact into a global cascade, and do the individual A-to-B links survive contact with the ground? Option A asserts a global physical fact; option B keeps the cascade shape and refuses to name a supported link until an instrument pays for it. Option A delivers a global probability that aggregates away the per-link signal and spends the independence of the people checking it. Option B delivers a per-link list with refuters and an open slot. R is indexed per element and per link, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: a chain is a set of pairwise links plus element states, so it must be enumerated outward, not composed. Symmetry: links are ordered (`→` sequence, `⇒` production, `⋛` threshold) and may close as a cycle; they are not symmetric bonds. Scale: first order at an element, second order at a coupling, and a lossy transform to a global aggregate.
- **D1 Physical contact habitat.** Actors: ice sheets, AMOC, Amazon, permafrost, coral and what acts on them. Currency: physical fluxes, forcing, freshwater and moisture transport. Anchor: observations, palaeo records, process instruments. Admitted.
- **D2 Proposed chain as notation.** Actors: elements as terms, links as typed bonds. Currency: composition inside `closure(Γ)`; bits to state a link, actions to settle it. Anchor: the ground settles each link separately. Admitted.
- **D3 Evidence and verification frame.** Actors: research groups, Earth system models, expert elicitation. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Admitted.
- **D4 Scale bridge.** Actors: element-level descriptions and the global aggregate. Currency: the lossy transform and its round-trip gap `R_T`. Anchor: the round trip, measured. Admitted.
- **X1 Planetary threshold as one global variable.** Actors: one aggregate number. Currency: averaged risk. Anchor: none nameable that is not itself a model output. Not a domain yet; absorbed into D4 as an aggregate.

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D2 form the physical compound (the chain is a notation over a habitat). D3 and D4 form the evidence/scale compound. The whole is a union because link status depends on both a physical contact path and the instrument that reads it; a single lever clearing one condition leaves the intersection standing.

**Order by dependency.** Enumerate contact → give each link a status → settle each link against the ground → bracket the cross-scale transform and measure `R_T` → triangulate across low-overlap frames. Effort above a broken link cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19).

Strain test: chain versus cycle strains Fig 12 against the physical habitat in Fig 11. Re-census resolves it: Fig 12 governs the notation, Fig 11 :43 governs the habitat. Different objects, so no strain after re-census except the notation limit itself, which is carried forward.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The cascade shape survives and the specific bindings do not. A cascade is contact enumeration with a compounding residual; each proposed link needs its own instrument, bond type and ground status, ordered by dependency; a chain diagram with a return path is missing a cycle bond; the element-to-global move carries a measured round-trip gap; and agreement inside one evidence pool is not corroboration. Which link is well supported is a `gap`, not a hidden answer, and the planetary-threshold aggregate is not a domain. The verdict is kept per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error has an address. The real cost is spending an outside checker's independence by sending a recording (a global risk number) instead of a generator (the link list and its refuter), which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a refuter fires; a refusal is added, and the reach falls because something was added rather than deleted. `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 records every refuter and §6 records every retraction, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An observation separating transmitted forcing from a shared driver on a named link, a quantified coupling with provenance, a demonstrated signed return path, or a measured element-to-global `R_T` would move the relevant finding. Two genuinely low-overlap instruments agreeing after each ranging against the ground would move D3 from one-pool to triangulated. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
