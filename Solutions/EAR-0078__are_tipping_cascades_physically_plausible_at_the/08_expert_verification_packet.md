## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are tipping cascades physically plausible at global scale, and which links in proposed chains are well supported" is two compounds and must be split. A cascade is a contact effect: the habitat is enumerated outward until the effect stops mattering, and the residual that matters can be several steps out. A proposed chain has only the support of its weakest link, so each link needs a named contact path, a bond type, an instrument and a ground status; a link with no instrument is unexamined, not weak. A chain diagram cannot close a feedback loop, so a chain with a return path is missing a bond. The move to a global aggregate is a lossy transform that must carry a measured round-trip gap `R_T`. Agreement among models sharing thresholds is one evidence pool, not corroboration. The framework does not name a supported link; which link is supported stays `gap` until an instrument pays for it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 1

- **Statement:** a cascade is enumerated contact, and the residual is indexed per element and per link, never averaged into one global number.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Check:** do these lines say what the claim says? Is the DDT example a case of third-order contact, as the figure states?
- **Refuter:** a proposed cascade link with no nameable contact path, actor or currency; or a global aggregate that predicts every element-level residual without loss.

### C2 — framework-internal and mechanism-facing; checkable by reading Figure 3 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** link status is per-link and dependency-ordered; a chain is one bond type and cannot represent a feedback return.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 12 :48 "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object." Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **Check:** does the proposed chain contain a return path? If so, is it drawn as a cycle with a sign, or as a one-way arrow? Is any link asserted as supported without an instrument?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated cascade among the claimed elements with no feedback return path where the diagram asserts one; or a link whose status is measured at the ground while it has no instrument; or a chain shown to represent a two-way coupling without loss.

### C3 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the element-to-global move is a lossy transform requiring a measured round-trip gap; agreement inside one evidence pool is not corroboration; the link-support question is a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
- **Check:** is a global cascade probability quoted without an element-to-global gap? Do the agreeing sources share thresholds, expert elicitation or coupling signs? Is any specific link labelled supported without a ground reading?
- **Refuter:** two low-overlap instruments, each ranging separately against the ground, agreeing on the same link; or a ground measurement quantifying a specific link's coupling; or a lossless element-to-global transform demonstrated at the ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that global tipping cascades are physically established, or that a planetary threshold exists.
- Not that any named link is well supported. Naming one is a ground result, not a figure result.
- Not that multiple models agreeing makes a coupling real.
- Not that a chain diagram is the mechanism. It is a notation over a habitat.
- Not that the framework can settle whether a specific cascade occurs. It returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. The derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument on a named link, not another ensemble. Concretely: an observation that separates transmitted forcing from a shared driver; a quantified coupling with provenance; a demonstrated return path with a sign; or a measurement of the element-to-global round-trip gap.
