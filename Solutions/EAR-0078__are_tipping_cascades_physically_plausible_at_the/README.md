# Tether outreach: figure-grounded package for EAR-0078

**Question:** Are tipping cascades physically plausible at the global scale, and which links in proposed chains are well supported?

**Source:** `earth systems.txt` line 103 (question 78). **Section:** 8. Tipping points, thresholds, and early warning.

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. No measurement was taken, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done. **Registry status:** pending.

**Field for public contact points:** earth system cascades research.

## The residual as an effect

The question asks two things at once. First, does a threshold crossing in one Earth-system element compose through physical contact into a cascade that reaches global scale? Option A says yes as a physical fact; option B says the cascade shape is composable but its physical occurrence is a claim for the ground. Second, which individual links in a proposed chain survive contact with the ground? That is a per-link status question, not a single global verdict. R is indexed by element and by link, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22). What an answer would look like: a dependency-ordered list of links, each marked `reached, so its state can be read` or `not reached, so not assessable` (Fig 3 :16-17), plus a measured round-trip gap between element-level and global-level descriptions (Fig 4 :30).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that cascades are plausible.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."; Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions.
