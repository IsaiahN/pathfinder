# Chain, cycle, and common cause: structure and order in cascade claims

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

A proposed tipping cascade is drawn as a chain of links. Three questions are owed: (1) is a chain the right structure for the mechanism, (2) what is the dependency order of the links, and (3) can apparent cascades arise without any link at all? The third is the common-cause problem, and if it is ignored, a correlation is read as a propagation.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: tipping elements as states (ice, ocean circulation, forest, permafrost), forcings and responses as gradients, the network of physical contact. Bonds held: `→` sequence (one-way), `⇒` production (B absent until A fires), `⋛` threshold (a comparison between two quantities), and the cycle (output re-enters as input). No new primitive is needed; the question is which bond is present at each link.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a cascade chain a molecule or a flat formula?

Fig 12 :48: "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."

Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

A diagram that lists the elements and draws arrows has recorded the atoms and one bond type. It has not recorded whether the link is a sequence (A then B), a production (A creates B where B did not exist), a threshold (A compares against a quantity), or a feedback return. Those are different substances under Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

**Reading:** two cascade diagrams with the same elements but different bond types are isomers in the figure's sense. They are not two spellings of one claim. Mode `general` for the bond distinction, `specified` for cascade diagrams. **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the link type cannot be recovered by any instrument even in principle, so that two diagrams with different bonds are observationally identical for every possible ground. This is the union-horizon case, and it would move the claim to `gap`, not make the bonds the same.

### Q2 — Can a chain close?

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Steffen et al. 2018 describe a return path: ice loss freshens the North Atlantic, weakens the overturning, shifts Southern Ocean heat, and acts back on ice. That is a cycle with a sign. A one-way domino chain cannot represent it. The fix is not to add another arrow but to change the bond: record the loop, state the sign, and keep the lineage of the redraw (Fig 12 :130, "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them").

**Reading:** chain diagrams in this field systematically under-represent feedback. This is a notation limit, not a discovery about ice. Mode `general`. **Falsifier:** a demonstrated cascade among the claimed elements with no return path at any link, where the diagram asserts one.

### Q3 — What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Ordering the links by dependency gives: contact path exists → the forcing is transmitted → the receiving element crosses its threshold → the state change is observed → the global aggregate is updated. A failure at the contact path makes every later reading a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). A global risk number computed above a broken transmission link is effort spent above the break.

**Reading:** the order is dependency, not size or importance. Mode `specified`. **Falsifier:** a cascade whose downstream link is well measured while its upstream link has no ground reading; that would show the order is not dependency.

### Q4 — Is a cascade ever just a shared driver?

Rocha et al. 2018 distinguish three structures: domino effects (one-way dependency), hidden feedbacks (two-way interaction), and driver sharing (correlation at distant places). Driver sharing is the common-cause collapse named in Fig 2 :6-7, "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)". Two elements tipping together because both warmed do not thereby propagate to each other. Fig 2 :18-21: "one evidence pool / they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool talking, / not two frames confirming each other."

Fig 11 :49-51: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Whether a warming trend acts as a shared driver or as a transmitted forcing is relative to the residual being tracked, not a fixed property of the trend.

**Reading:** a cascade claim must discriminate propagation from a shared driver. A common warming trend is the default alternative hypothesis. Mode `specified`. **Falsifier:** a method that discriminates the two without an instrument that can measure the link, or a case where the shared driver and the propagated forcing are indistinguishable at every resolution (then the link claim is `gap` for that case).

### Q5 — Scale order and the round trip

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

Fig 4 :33-35: "Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress." "The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

The element-level loop is the kernel. At the global level the actors change (elements become a network), the currency changes (physical flux becomes aggregate risk), and the anchor changes (instrument becomes model ensemble). The transform is lossy (Fig 4 :27), so the round trip must be measured: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." (Fig 4 :30).

**Reading:** scale order is element → coupling → network → global aggregate, with a measured `R_T` at the last step. Mode `specified`. **Falsifier:** a lossless element-to-global transform demonstrated at the ground for cascade probability. That would set `R_T` to zero and remove the bracket.

## CONCLUSION

The cascade claim is neither a chain nor a cycle as first drawn: it is an isomer question over a set of bond types. The chain notation records one bond and drops the rest, so a redraw with the correct bond type is owed for every link. The dependency order must be respected, a shared driver must be excluded as the default alternative, and the move to global scale must carry a measured round-trip gap. No new primitive is required. The falsifiers are written above and are carried into `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.
