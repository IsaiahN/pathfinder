# Outreach draft: check the link list before you decide about the cascade claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold.

---

**Subject:** Which link in a proposed tipping cascade would fail first?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific question I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree that global tipping cascades are plausible. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "are tipping cascades physically plausible at global scale, and which links are well supported" is two questions, not one. Treated as a single risk number it loses the signal, because the residual is indexed per element and per link, not aggregated. The shape the figures return is this:
>
> 1. A cascade is a contact effect. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Do not scope to direct contact.
> 2. A proposed chain has only the support of its weakest link. Each link needs a named contact path, a bond type (one-way sequence, production, threshold, or feedback cycle), an instrument, and a ground status. A link with no instrument is not a weak link, it is one nobody has looked at.
> 3. A chain diagram cannot close a loop. If the physical mechanism includes a return path, the diagram is missing that bond. That is a notation choice, not a fact about the world.
> 4. "Global scale" is a coarser description of the same loop. Carry the element-level description up, bring it back down, and measure the gap. A global cascade probability quoted without that gap is dead reckoning.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named link, for example Greenland ice loss to AMOC weakening, is well supported by the figures. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which link is supported is a ground question, and this email does not answer it. I also am not claiming that multiple models agreeing on a coupling makes that coupling real; models sharing thresholds are one evidence pool.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this email:**
> 1. a proposed cascade link with no nameable contact path, actor, or currency;
> 2. a link whose cascade status is measured at the ground while it has no instrument;
> 3. a demonstrated cascade with no feedback return path where the diagram asserts one, or a one-way chain shown to represent a two-way coupling without loss;
> 4. a global aggregate that reproduces every element-level residual with zero round-trip gap;
> 5. two genuinely independent instruments, each ranging against the ground, agreeing on the same link (that would move the claim from one evidence pool to triangulation);
> 6. a ground measurement that quantifies coupling on a specific link or establishes a planetary threshold.
>
> The first four break the shape. The last two would improve it. I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The core sources behind the worked example, for comparison with your own: https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/12/601/2021/ and https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-022-01545-9
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a cascade claim you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage discipline

**Stage 1:** recipient checks the shape claim and its refuter against their own ground contact. Record their verdict *before* showing any theory. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the source links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority. A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors (Fig 10 :42).
