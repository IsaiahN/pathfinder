# Outreach draft: check the shape claim first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have read `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 and are prepared to have the claim fail. The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

---

**Subject:** A checkable shape claim about snowpack, seasonal streamflow and water supply

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on a figure-grounded method for stating the shape of an answer before filling in the numbers. I have one specific output about snowpack change and seasonal water supply that I would like you to check against what you already measure. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the winter snowpack is a stored difference that can be spent. The method distinguishes three changes that are usually reported as one: a change in the size of the store, a change in the date it is spent, and a change in phase (snow falling as rain, which removes the store rather than resizing it). It also says that the snow store and the discharge at a gauge cannot be treated as directly bonded: a melt gradient and the channel network sit between them, and either can change independently. Finally, it says the residual lives in basin-by-season slots, so an annual water-supply total throws away the signal that matters.
>
> This is a shape, not a result. It does not say how many weeks earlier the peak flow will be in your basin, and it has no number for summer supply. Those are measurements, and I am not claiming them.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. a multi-basin study in which between-basin and between-season variance in timing response is no larger than within-slot noise, so aggregation loses nothing;
> 2. a proportional link between store magnitude and annual runoff volume across basins, with no decoupling;
> 3. a direct snow-water-equivalent to discharge mapping that predicts timing as well as volume without a melt-gradient or network term;
> 4. a basin that shifts to rain-dominated runoff while keeping the same seasonal hydrograph and storage cycle;
> 5. downstream supply reliability unchanged after a large timing shift with no compensating storage.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names the mechanism, that it produces hydrological numbers, that it replaces a gauge or an isotope source partition, or that a list of people who agree would corroborate any of this. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and agreement among people who share an evidence pool is one witness, not two.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are sources, and the figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a basin you know well and can check against a gauge. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

**Legal and practical note.** Public institutional or opt-in channels only. No scraped personal emails, no bulk send, and an opt-out line in any one-to-one message. GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules apply to unsolicited outreach in many jurisdictions, which is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
