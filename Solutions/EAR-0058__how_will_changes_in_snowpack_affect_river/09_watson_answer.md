## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A change in the cold-season snow store (SWE, melt date, melt rate, rain/snow phase) and its consequences for discharge timing, seasonal water availability, and slot location. Arity: multi-condition, so a union compound, not one hard question. Symmetry: asymmetric, because accumulation precedes release and the seasonal loop has a sign. Scale: first order within a basin-by-season slot, second order across the network and supply system, third order into groundwater and ecology.
- **Domain 1, cryosphere store.** Actors: snowpack and SWE, temperature, humidity, elevation, aspect, precipitation phase, melt energy. Currency: stored water and stored cold, a difference that can be spent. Anchor: SNOTEL SWE and snow courses.
- **Domain 2, routing and discharge.** Actors: channel network, soil, groundwater, reservoirs, vegetation. Currency: discharge and travel time. Anchor: stream gauges and reservoir records.
- **Domain 3, water supply and demand.** Actors: irrigators, cities, hydropower, ecosystems, storage operators. Currency: reliably deliverable water against demand timing. Anchor: delivery records and failures.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: hydrologists, gauges, isotope tracers, models. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run every seat draws on the same evidence pool, so its independence is zero.
- **Partition or union:** union. D1, D2 and D3 overlap; a store change is simultaneously a routing change and a supply change. Seasons are slots within each loop, not sub-questions to be averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perceive SWE -> have terms for what the snow affords and what counts as progress in supply -> plan forecasts and operations -> name what worked and carry it. The usual break is at vocabulary and objective, not at planning.

- **Snow hydrology / water resources.** T. P. Barnett, J. C. Adam and D. P. Lettenmaier, Nature, 2005. Quote: "In a warmer world, less winter precipitation falls as snow and the melting of winter snow occurs earlier in spring. Even without any changes in precipitation intensity, both of these effects lead to a shift in peak river runoff to winter and early spring, away from summer and autumn when demand is highest." Predicts earlier peak flow and reduced summer supply. Corroborates Fig 13 :76. Overlap: high with the other observational studies in this pool.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a single averaged answer does not. The residual is per basin and per season. The snowpack is a spendable gradient whose size, spend date and phase are different terms. Snow store and discharge compose only through a melt gradient and a routing network, so decoupling is expected rather than anomalous. The question is a union compound that must be unbundled, the seasonal loop has a sign, and contact cascades into supply several steps out. The anchor is the measurement; the observing channel can fail. No figure-derived number exists, and none is claimed. Every substantive claim is `specified` or `general`, none `grounded`. Direction and magnitude in the world section are secondary and are cited, not derived.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, five refuters and the provenance of each claim before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reviewer's independence by sending a polished conclusion before the residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. Any of the seven shape claims can be withdrawn without touching the others, because each has its own refuter and its own figure line. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one. No primitive, gauge record or world source has to be retracted.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot variance decomposition showing no between-slot signal would break the slotting claim; a proportional SWE-to-annual-runoff mapping across basins would break the magnitude-versus-timing claim; a direct discharge mapping that predicts timing without mediation would break the cross-kind claim; a rain-dominated basin retaining the old hydrograph and cycle would break the cycle-sign claim; unchanged supply reliability after a large timing shift with no added storage would break the cascade claim. A source-discriminating isotope or tracer study would also change what may be named as the mediating structure. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
