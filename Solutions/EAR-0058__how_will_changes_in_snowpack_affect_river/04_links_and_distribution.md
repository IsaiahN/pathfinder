## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Directional consensus, one evidence pool.** Barnett 2005, Clow 2010, Stewart 2005, IPCC AR6 and Jenicek 2021 all report earlier melt and earlier peak flow with reduced summer flow. They are one witness in the ledger's overlap column, not five independent confirmations. Agreement from a shared observation pool is exactly Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- **Low-overlap witness, process complexity.** Gordon et al. 2022 come from a land-surface and ecohydrology frame with three interacting mechanisms. Their result that prediction is hardest where mechanisms overlap is the world-side statement of the union compound in Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Low-overlap witness, decoupling.** López-Moreno et al. 2020 use a physically based cold-regions model over 44 virtual mountain basins. Their finding that snow and hydrological regimes decouple, and that magnitude of snowpack change does not predict annual runoff, is the strongest empirical check on the gradient-versus-magnitude distinction.
- **Low-overlap witness, live dispute.** Berghuijs and Hale 2025 contest the "less snow equals earlier streamflow" paradigm with a Matters Arising in Nature. This is a disagreement that the figures would classify as a possible union: different snow-fraction regimes behaving differently. It is a reason to split, not to average.
- **Operational ground.** The NRCS SNOTEL program and its provisional-data flags are the channel through which the anchor is read. Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.
- **Source discrimination.** The isotope partitioning used in Himalayan snowmelt studies is the world analogue of the spectrometer in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a gauge reading cannot say whether the water came from snow, rain or groundwater, so a source-discriminating instrument is required.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape claim and its five refuters first. The figures and the framework come later, and only after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 9 :30-31** *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Describe what a reviewer would expect to see in their own basin and season; do not lead with Tether vocabulary.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement, and do not select recipients by likely agreement.
- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The third stage offers a method, not an answer. Each user writes their own ground and refuter.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuters; (2) ask what the reviewer would count as a failure in their own basin; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, if they are curious, the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Residual, shape claim and pre-registered refuter. Ask what would count as a failure. Record the answer before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** Offer the figures and formula. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked, so their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. The architecture essay itself warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 shape survives the reviewer's ground. Nothing is sent by this agent. Hosting and sending are yours to do. Prefer an opt-in download page or a public submission route over an attachment pushed to a list.
