# Expert verification packet: the Tether on snowpack change and seasonal water supply

**What this is:** a claim in a form a snow hydrologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. One-to-one use only.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural constraints, not `grounded`.** No discharge number is asserted. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The winter snowpack is a stored difference that can be spent. The size of the store, the date it is spent, and the phase of precipitation (snow versus rain) are three different changes with different consequences for river flow and water supply. The snow store and the discharge at a gauge do not bond directly: a melt gradient and a routing network mediate, and either can change independently, so snow and river regimes can decouple. The question is a union of overlapping conditions that must be unbundled before it is searched; the residual lives in basin-by-season slots, so an annual total throws away the live signal. The seasonal loop has a sign, and contact cascades into soil moisture, groundwater, ecology and supply. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and does not say which mechanism dominates in a given basin.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : slotting and decomposition (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11)

- **Statement:** report the residual per basin and per season; split the union before searching; do not aggregate into an annual mean.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 11 :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*.
- **What to check:** in the reviewer's own basins, is the between-basin and between-season variance in timing response larger than the within-slot noise? If yes, the slotting claim is supported.
- **Refuter:** between-slot variance is no larger than within-slot noise, so aggregation loses no signal.

### C2 : magnitude, timing, phase and mediation (hydrology-facing, checkable against gauges and process studies)

- **Statement:** store size, spend date and phase are different terms; the snow store and discharge compose through a melt gradient and a channel network rather than directly.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :76 *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* Fig 13 :46 *"a difference that can be spent"*. Fig 13 :75 *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*
- **What to check:** does a change in peak SWE map proportionally to annual runoff across basins, or do timing and routing terms intervene? Does a direct SWE-to-discharge mapping predict timing as well as volume without a melt or network term?
- **Refuter:** a proportional magnitude-to-volume link with no decoupling, or a direct mapping that predicts timing without mediation. Present world evidence (López-Moreno et al. 2020) reports the opposite: no link between the magnitude of snowpack change and annual runoff.

### C3 : cycle sign and cascade (framework-internal with a world check)

- **Statement:** the seasonal loop has a sign; shifting snow to rain can change the regime; contact reaches supply several steps out.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- **What to check:** in a basin that shifted to rain-dominated runoff, did the seasonal hydrograph and storage cycle remain the same? Did downstream supply reliability stay unchanged despite a large timing shift and no added storage?
- **Refuter:** an unchanged hydrograph and cycle after a phase shift, or unchanged supply reliability with no compensating storage.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a number for timing, volume or reliability in any basin.
- Not a named mechanism. Which structure dominates is a measurement, since the framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that annual runoff must decline when snowpack declines. The figures distinguish magnitude from timing, and the world literature reports decoupling.
- Not that a consensus of hydrologists corroborates the claim. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a substitute for SNOTEL, stream gauges, reservoir records or isotope source partitioning.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A per-slot variance decomposition showing whether timing response is basin and season specific.
2. A multi-basin test of whether peak SWE magnitude predicts annual runoff, which is the gradient-versus-magnitude discriminator.
3. A source-discriminating instrument (stable isotopes, tracers, co-located SWE and stream gauges) that separates snowmelt, rain and groundwater contributions to the same hydrograph reading.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :54, Fig 13 :75-76 and Fig 12 :121, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own basins: does SWE magnitude track annual runoff, or do timing and routing dominate?
3. Name one instrument that would separate snowmelt from rain and groundwater in their gauge record, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
