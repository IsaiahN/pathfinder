## What in the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The global budget is not the regional answer.** IPCC AR6 A.4.1 reports net warming mainly from
   greenhouse gases, partly reduced by aerosol cooling, while A.3.3 reports that for South Asia,
   East Asia and West Africa the GHG-driven monsoon increase was *counteracted* by aerosol-driven
   decrease over the twentieth century. One is a global accounting statement; the other is a
   slot-indexed regional statement. This is exactly the distinction F1 makes, and it comes from the
   ground literature, not from the figures.
2. **The operands are not interchangeable.** Monerie et al. find aerosol forcing drives drying via
   circulation shifts and moisture advection, while greenhouse forcing drives precipitation
   increases. Bellouin et al. find aerosol lifetimes of days to weeks and strong spatial
   heterogeneity, in contrast to well-mixed long-lived greenhouse gases. Both corroborate F2:
   swapping the operands changes the outcome.
3. **The coupling, not the sum, is the hard part.** Kalisoras et al. find aerosol-cloud interaction
   dominates both the global mean ERF and the inter-model spread, with strong spatial pattern
   dependence. Rockström et al. find that an interhemispheric AOD difference can shift the ITCZ and
   trip regional hydrological tipping points. These are the bond, not the ingredients, and they
   corroborate F3.
4. **Actor sign depends on the residual.** Sulfate cools a warming residual but shifts monsoon
   rainfall and harms health; black carbon warms while reducing rainfall through stabilisation. The
   ecology source (Pringle) is a genuinely low-overlap witness for the same shape: an actor's effect
   changes sign with context. It corroborates F6 without sharing the climate evidence pool.
5. **The instrument gap is real and named in the literature.** Bellouin et al. state that aerosol
   effects on mixed-phase and ice clouds remain poorly constrained, and IPCC AR6 notes that
   aerosol-cloud interaction is not well represented in models. This is the `gap` of F6 reported by
   the field itself, not by the framework.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Residual, shape and refuter. Ask for their failure criterion. Record it first.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in.** If curious, offer the three source links. Their later agreement is never
   pooled as corroboration (Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes
   their own refuter and states their own mode. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does
   not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 shape survives. The refuters in
`01` §3 and `08` §2 are the test. If one fires, withdraw the affected finding before sending.
