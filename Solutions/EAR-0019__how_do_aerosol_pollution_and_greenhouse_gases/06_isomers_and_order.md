# Supporting derivation: bond type, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`, with the framework-internal steps `general`.** Simulated derivation checked
against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit refuses the flat addition of aerosol and greenhouse forcing. Three supporting questions
are owed: (1) which bond type actually holds between them, (2) whether their order matters, and
(3) whether the figure's isomer and threshold vocabulary says anything checkable about the pair.
A fourth, the numerical magnitude, is a `gap` and is not derived here.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond holds? CORRECTION TO THE FLAT-ADDITION READING

- Fig 12 :59 gives the symmetric test: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Here it
  changes: a days-long patchy aerosol and a centuries-long well-mixed gas are not interchangeable.
- Fig 12 :74 gives the ordered test: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* That is
  the case here. So the bond is ordered, not `+`.
- Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it
  is bonded to is part of what it is."* The state each forcing bonds to is part of what it is.
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is
  the half nothing was recording."* The coupling lives in the bond.
- ⇒ The forcing-budget notation `aerosol + greenhouse` is a flat formula. It records ingredients and
  not the typed relation. This is a **notation finding**, not a claim that either sign is wrong.
- **I correct my own first pass:** I initially treated the comparison bond ⋛ as the natural join
  here. Re-census refuses that. The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two
  quantities"*, and Fig 13 :75 bars same-kind direct joins at first order. ⋛ is reported as a
  single-source observation about the sign-opposed competition, not as the first-order bond.

### Q2 - Does order matter?

- Fig 12 :70-72 defines sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; Fig 12 :74: *"swap
  them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- The physical order is observable: aerosol emissions rise first and cool a region while greenhouse
  warming accumulates; when aerosol emissions fall under clean-air policy, the previously masked
  warming is revealed. The two histories produce different regional trajectories even when the
  final forcing totals are similar. That is the ordered-bond signature.
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The derivation order is:
  perceive the slot residual, name the objectives, then plan. You cannot diagnose the response
  before the objective (region, variable, baseline) is stated.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two forcing histories with the same final totals but
  opposite order produce the same regional slot trajectories at every season, the ordered-bond
  claim fails and the operands may be treated as symmetric after all.

### Q3 - Are the two forcings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
  invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- As written, the two forcings share no species: sulfate and black carbon are not CO₂ or CH₄. They
  are **not isomers** under Fig 12's definition. They are two members of the same **functional
  group**: a signed radiative forcing bonded to a state, with different atoms in the slots.
- The stronger version would write both forcings over a shared atom set, for example the six-term
  substrate of Fig 13 (state, structure, state with a coupling), differing only in which structure
  mediates which state pair. Then they would be isomers. This is a structural claim about how the
  two are modelled, not a claim about atmospheric chemistry, and it needs a figure re-census plus a
  ground check before it is asserted.
- **Falsifier:** if either forcing cannot be written over the shared atom set without importing an
  atom the other does not require, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - What does the threshold analogue say, and where does it come from?

- The Operators table: ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, and the test is *"is it
  about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- A low-overlap world source, Rockström et al. 2023 (*Nature*, sustainability science, not
  atmospheric chemistry), proposes a safe boundary at an interhemispheric aerosol optical depth
  difference below 0.15, because a rising difference *"can trigger regional-scale tipping points
  and cause substantial adverse effects on regional hydrological cycles"*. This is a threshold
  relation between two quantities, the exact shape ⋛ names.
- The figures do **not** give that number. The world source is secondary corroboration for the
  *shape* of a threshold, never for its value. Mode `specified` for the analogy; the numeric
  boundary stays with the ground source and is not imported into the framework.
- **Falsifier:** a figure line that returns the numeric threshold, or a ground result showing the
  regional response is smooth with no threshold in interhemispheric AOD difference. The first would
  be a genuine new governing line; the second would break the threshold analogy.

### Q5 - Recursion and the round trip

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A regional
  response that has been settled against observations becomes an atom for the next level, for
  example for an assessment of multiple regions or a policy boundary. This is recursion, not an
  import that moves the wall.
- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead
  reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* A parameterisation carried from particle
  scale to region and back does not return unchanged. `R_T` must be measured, not assumed zero.

## CONCLUSION

The pair is joined by an **ordered bond**, not a symmetric sum; the order is physically observable
in the masking and unmasking history. The two forcings are not isomers as written, but members of a
**functional group**; the isomer version over a shared atom set is a checkable structural claim with
a stated falsifier. The threshold analogue is corroborated by a genuinely low-overlap field but its
number is not figure-derived. All of this is `specified` or `general`, none of it `grounded`, and
the dominant mechanism remains a declared `gap`.
