## DERIVATION

### Q1 - At what order does the replacement sit?

- Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*
- A first-order molecule is `state - structure - state`, one crossing. GR core: `Space - structure - Time`. QM core: `Energy - structure - Time`. Both first order and legal under the cross-kind rule.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*.
- Both first-order molecules are settled against the ground (decades of experiment for GR and QM). Each can therefore serve as an atom at the next level. The comparison that locates the replacement is **second order**: `(settled GR molecule) ⋛ (settled QM molecule)`. It is a threshold relation at the contact, not a new field and not a seventh primitive.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that a settled molecule cannot serve as an atom, or that the contact does not admit a ⋛ relation between the two molecules. If either holds, the second-order claim fails.

### Q2 - Are the candidate end states new atoms or structures over held atoms?

Fig 12 defines four structures, each naming something a flat formula cannot write:

| structure | verbatim line | candidate end state |
|---|---|---|
| catalyst | Fig 12 :119, *"A part that survives its own use."* | a **remnant**: a product that survives the evaporation that consumes the hole |
| cycle | Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* | a **bounce** or black-hole-to-white-hole transition: the output re-enters as input and the sign decides closure |
| functional group | Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* | the family of **regular (nonsingular) black holes**: same substructure, different member (Hayward, Bardeen, Ayon-Beato to Garcia) |
| isomer | Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* | the stronger claim: fuzzball, Planck star and regular black hole written over the same atom set, arranged differently |

So the candidate menagerie is not evidence of many new primitives. It is evidence that the arrangements over a closed atom set are not closed, exactly as Fig 13 :115 states: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* The import that is owed is the microphysical atom that makes the interior finite; the variety of arrangements is not itself an import.

### Q3 - Is the isomer claim real?

- As the candidates are usually written, they share no settled atom set. Fuzzball is built from strings, Planck star from loop quantum gravity discreteness, regular black holes from modified matter couplings. They are different closures, so they are not Fig 12 isomers as written.
- The stronger, checkable form: write each candidate over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}` and let them differ only in which structure mediates which state pair at the contact. Then they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference *"invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour"* (Fig 12 :117).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any candidate requires an atom the others do not, or if two candidates written over the shared set behave identically under every available instrument, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. The functional-group claim is weaker but still figure-licensed (Fig 12 :123).

### Q4 - Is the question a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*. The candidate end states overlap: a remnant may also be a regular black hole; a bounce may also be a Planck star; a fuzzball may have no interior horizon at all. Treating them as a list of alternatives is a bundled search.

Unbundling separates at least three conditions: (a) no divergent curvature invariant (regularity condition); (b) finite information budget and a unitary evaporation endpoint (information condition); (c) a causal structure that does not terminate the infalling observer at a spacelike boundary (causal condition). Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Each condition has its own ground. A single lever that clears one leaves the intersections standing.

**Falsifier:** if all three conditions turn out to be one condition under a single instrument reading (for example, a single observation simultaneously proves regularity, unitarity and causal extension), the union reading is wrong and the problem is a partition after all.

### Q5 - Does the cycle reading require a new atom?

Fig 12 :121: a cycle closes only if the sign of the loop is right, and *"a chain cannot close by construction."* A bounce is a cycle, so it needs a mechanism that changes the sign of the collapsed branch. That mechanism is exactly the imported microphysical atom. It cannot be manufactured by concatenating first-order molecules: the substrate set is closed and the chain cannot close by construction. This is a second, independent route to the same import verdict as F2 of `01`. Triangulated across Fig 12 :121 and Fig 6 :23; single-source for the physics identification of a bounce as a cycle.

## CONCLUSION

The replacement sits at **second order**, over settled GR and QM molecules. It is not a new primitive; the new atom it requires is an import from a differing closure. Under Figure 12 the candidate end states classify as **catalyst** (remnant), **cycle** (bounce), **functional group** (regular black holes) and, in the stronger form over a shared atom set, **isomers**. The candidate list is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition, and must be unbundled before search. Each claim carries a falsifier written above.

This framing keeps what is checkable without pretending to name the replacement: order, structure and union status are figure-derived; the identity of the atom is not.
