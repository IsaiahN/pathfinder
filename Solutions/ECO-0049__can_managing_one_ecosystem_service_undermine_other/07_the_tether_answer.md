# The Tether's answer: can managing one ecosystem service undermine other functions?

**Mode: `general` on the structural answer, `specified` on the ecological patterns, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. Nothing here settles which service in which ecosystem is being harmed.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

A manager raises service S, which is visible, fast and measured. Other ecological functions F are diffuse, slow and often unmeasured. The residual is the gap between the predicted effect of the intervention (the metric improves) and what the habitat does on all the slots the metric does not carry. Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The answer is a **possibility established structurally**: yes, it can. Not "it always does," and not "this one does."

## 2. The shape

> `S_visible -> (cascade over unmonitored slots) -> F_slow`

The middle term is an unknown cascade, held as an ordinary term with its own residual. The figures do not name which function. They do constrain the shape: contact must be enumerated outward, the residual indexed per slot, and the union unbundled before the lever is chosen.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that every intervention undermines every other function. Synergies exist, and some high-output farms cross no thresholds (Raudsepp-Hearne et al. 2010). The claim is *can*, not *must*.
- Not that a named service in a named ecosystem is currently being damaged. No ground reading was taken here.
- Not that less visible means metaphysically unverifiable. It means no slot, no instrument, no attention, all of which are repairable.
- Not that the framework sets conservation values. A preference for one service over another is a preference, not a term the loop can certify.
- Not `grounded`. Mode is `general` / `specified`, candidate throughout.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A monitoring record that tracks the slow functions at first, second and third order under the intervention, with an anchor that does not move when the manager pushes on the service metric. Concretely:

1. **Cascade test:** does the intervention change any unmonitored function at two or three steps out? A null result at all three orders, replicated, fires refuter 1.
2. **Aggregation test:** is a global service residual sufficient for all slots? If yes, refuter 2 fires.
3. **Union test:** do service and function share conditions? A demonstrated partition fires refuter 3.
4. **Threshold test:** which slow variables crossed a socially accepted threshold, and when (Raudsepp-Hearne et al. 2010 used soil phosphorus retention, soil organic matter and drinking water quality)?

An instrument, not an argument, closes the residual. Agreement among people who already accept trade-offs does not (Fig 2 :40).

## 7. One-paragraph version

> Yes, and the reason is structural rather than anecdotal. The habitat is enumerated, never composed; contact cascades outward past first order; and the residual is indexed per ecological function, so a healthy service metric can sit beside a live residual on a slow, unmeasured one. The service and the other functions are overlapping conditions of one habitat, so a single lever clears only the service-only region and leaves the intersections standing. Valence is relative to the residual, which is why no intervention is good in general, and the manager's power is asymmetric: actors can be introduced, the habitat cannot be subtracted. The Tether therefore returns a shape, *visible service leads to an unmonitored cascade leading to a slow function*, with three refuters written in advance. It does not name which function is at risk in your ecosystem; that requires a ground: a monitoring record of the slow variables at two and three steps out. Until then, the result is `general` in structure and `specified` in ecology, and it is not a measurement.
