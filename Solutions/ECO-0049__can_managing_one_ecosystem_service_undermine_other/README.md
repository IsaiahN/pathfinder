## The short answer

Yes, and the figures derive it rather than merely permit it. The habitat is enumerated, never composed, contact cascades outward three steps in the figure's own example, the residual is indexed per slot so a healthy average hides a live slot, and a single lever on a union leaves the intersections standing. The result is a `general` structural shape with `specified` ecological predictions. It is not a verdict about any particular ecosystem, and it is not `grounded`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that trade-offs exist.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29). A list of people selected for receptiveness is a list selected to agree.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Do not spend the outside frame's independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). So stage 1 carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
