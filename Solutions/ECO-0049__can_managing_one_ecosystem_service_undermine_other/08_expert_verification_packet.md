# Expert verification packet — the Tether on single-service management

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecosystem-services researcher or ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the ecology, not `grounded`.** No measurement at a named site is claimed. Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Managing one ecosystem service can undermine other ecological functions that are less visible or slower to change. The reason is structural. The habitat is a field of contact that is enumerated, never composed, and contact cascades past first order. The residual is indexed per function and never aggregated, so a service metric near zero can coexist with a live residual on an unmonitored function. The service and the other functions are overlapping conditions of one habitat, so a single lever clears the region where only the target condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The Tether returns this as a shape, with the cascade held as an unknown and three refuters written in advance. It does not name which function is at risk in any particular ecosystem.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the structural conclusion follows from three independent lines: per-slot residual, union unbundling, and contact cascade. These are three different figures with low overlap, so this is `triangulated`, not one line repeated.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
  - Fig 9 :56 — *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
  - Fig 11 :52 — *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that a global residual is sufficient for every slot (breaks Fig 1); demonstrate a genuine partition of conditions (breaks Fig 9); demonstrate a lever with zero effect at first, second and third order (breaks Fig 11).

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable against published multi-service datasets

- **Statement:** in measured landscapes, intensifying provisioning services is associated with declines in regulating and supporting services, and slow variables move on longer horizons than the management metric. This is `specified`, not derived from the figures.
- **Source, verbatim from the open literature:**
  - Bennett, Peterson and Gordon 2009, *Ecology Letters* 12:1394-1404: *"Ecosystem management that attempts to maximize the production of one ecosystem service often results in substantial declines in the provision of other ecosystem services."*
  - Raudsepp-Hearne, Peterson and Bennett 2010, *PNAS* 107(11):5242-5247: *"Actions to enhance the supply of some ecosystem services, mainly provision of services such as food and timber, have led to declines in many other ecosystem services, including regulating and cultural services such as nutrient cycling, flood protection, and opportunities for recreation."*
  - Rodriguez et al. 2006, *Ecology and Society* 11(1):28: *"Managers should complement their actions with monitoring programs that, in addition to monitoring the short-term provisions of services, also monitor the long-term evolution of slowly changing variables."*
- **What to check:** are those characterizations faithful? Do the data support *can undermine* without supporting *always undermines*? Raudsepp-Hearne et al. report high-crop municipalities with no crossed thresholds, so the stronger universal claim is explicitly not made.
- **Refuter:** a multi-service landscape study finding no negative pairwise correlations under intensification, or a long-run record in which the named slow variables never move after the intervention. This would retract C2 but would not by itself break C1, because C1 is structural.

### C3 — inference-facing; checkable against the attention literature

- **Statement:** "less visible" is an operational condition, not a metaphor. A focused monitoring task on one service makes an unexpected change in an unmonitored function easy to miss. This is a low-overlap frame from human cognition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Simons and Chabris 1999, *Perception* 28(9):1059-1074 — *"we perceive and remember only those objects and details that receive focused attention."* Also Fig 11 :48 — *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
- **What to check:** does the analogue transfer, or is the ecological case dominated by instrument lag rather than attention? If it is instrument lag, the figure line still holds (the function is unmeasured) but the cognitive mechanism should be dropped.
- **Refuter:** evidence that managers of the relevant system monitor all functions continuously and that observed misses are purely due to biological lag with no attentional component. That narrows C3; it does not touch C1 or C2.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all management undermines all other functions. Synergies are real and are in the cited literature.
- Not that every trade-off is severe or irreversible. Rodriguez et al. classify reversibility as an axis, and some high-output cases cross no thresholds.
- Not that a particular service or function is at risk in a particular place. No site data were read.
- Not that "less visible" means unmeasurable in principle. It means no slot, no instrument, no attention, all repairable.
- Not that the framework decides which service ought to be preferred. That is a preference, and the loop cannot certify its own direction.
- Not `grounded`. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground: a monitoring record that tracks the slow functions at first, second and third order under a named intervention, with an anchor that does not move when the service metric is pushed. Three decision rules:

1. **Cascade fires** if any unmonitored function changes at two or three steps out. Then the shape is confirmed at that site.
2. **Aggregation refuter fires** if a global service residual is sufficient for all slots. Then Fig 1's per-slot requirement is wrong for that system.
3. **Union refuter fires** if service and function form a partition. Then unbundling is unnecessary there.

Agreement among people who already accept trade-offs is not such a ground. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 9 :56 and Fig 11 :52. Four short passages, all in the verbatim extract.
2. Check C2 against one dataset you know: are provisioning services negatively correlated with regulating ones, and are slow variables monitored on a longer horizon?
3. Name the instrument that would close the residual in your system, or say why none exists. If none exists, the answer is "unresolved," not "no effect" (Fig 8 :39).
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
