## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a manager acting on one ecosystem service can undermine other ecological functions that are less visible or slower to change. Option A: single-service management is safe by default because the service metric is a sufficient statistic for habitat health. Option B: the service metric is one slot among many, contact cascades outward, and an intervention on a union of conditions leaves intersections standing. What each leads to: A implies that a healthy measured service certifies the unmeasured functions, which requires aggregating the residual away. B implies enumerating the habitat outward, giving slow functions their own slots and residuals, and permanent monitoring of slow variables.

- **R (the residual).** Gap between the predicted effect of a single-service intervention and what the habitat does on functions absent from the metric. Arity: many slots, not one. Symmetry: asymmetric, because the same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another (Fig 11 :49-50). - **Domain 2, the unmonitored functions.** Actors: soil formation, nutrient cycling, water regulation, pollination, carbon storage and the species that carry them. Currency: the functions, mostly unpriced and slow. Anchor: the ground, meaning measured state of those functions over time. This anchor is missing from Domain 1's frame.
- **Domain 3, the habitat and field of contact.** Actors: everything in contact with R, then what contacts those, outward (Fig 11 :44), with conditions and relations read off the world. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not update (Fig 11 :73).
- **Partition or union.** Union. Service and function are overlapping conditions of one habitat. Fig 9 :54 requires unbundling before searching (see TETHER).
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate the habitat outward (D3), give slow functions their own slots (D2), then choose and evaluate the lever (D1). Fig 3 :19: optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.

Strain test applied: an early reading tried to make the service metric the anchor. Re-census moved it to Domain 1 as an instrument; the anchor is the long-run measurement of the slow function. No strain remains after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Yes, managing one ecosystem service can undermine other ecological functions that are less visible or slower to change. The verdict is `general` in structure and `specified` in ecology, and is a candidate, not `grounded`. The habitat is enumerated, never composed; contact cascades past first order; the residual is indexed per function so a healthy average hides a live slow slot; and a single lever on a union leaves intersections standing. The manager adds to a habitat that cannot be subtracted, so the work is permanent, and valence is relative to the residual, so no lever is good in general. The shape is `S_visible -> (unmonitored cascade) -> F_slow`, with the cascade held unknown. The ecology sources corroborate that provisioning services often trade off against regulating ones, but they also show severe trade-offs are not inevitable. Which function is at risk in a particular ecosystem is not answered here and needs a ground.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because the shape carries three written refuters and states its mode. The real cost is behavioural: treating a service metric as a sufficient statistic and therefore never measuring the slow functions until they fail. That is the aggregation failure Fig 1 :22 names, and the DDT case in Fig 11 is the figure's own record of what it costs. A second cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending agreement instead of a refuter (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. No intervention, species or primitive has to be withdrawn. The management prescription is reversible in method: add the missing slots and instruments, monitor slow variables on a longer horizon, and re-run the census before the next lever. A claim already sent can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measured lever with zero effect at first, second and third order on every other slot fires refuter 1. A demonstration that a global residual is sufficient for all slots fires refuter 2. A demonstrated partition of conditions fires refuter 3. In the ecology frame, a multi-service study finding no negative pairwise correlations under intensification would retract the `specified` pattern. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as a candidate.
