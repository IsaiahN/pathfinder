# Structure, order and analogue: are the service and the function isomers?

**Mode: `general` for the structural result, `specified` for the ecological analogue. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed by `01`:

1. What is the **bond** between a managed service S and an unmonitored function F? If they share atoms but differ only in arrangement, Fig 12 has a name for that: isomers. If not, the right structure is something else.
2. What is the **order** of the cascade, and what analogue licenses reading it as a sequence rather than a simultaneous co-occurrence?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held for this derivation: the habitat register of Fig 11 :46-47 (conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain; relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency) plus the organisms that act. Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12, including the symmetric `+` (both hold at once) and the ordered `→` (sequence) and `⋛` (comparison). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are S and F isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Two habitats with the same species list but a different relation between them (pollination present versus absent; predation released versus intact) are **isomers** under this definition. Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. The inventory cannot tell them apart. That is exactly why an inventory-style service dashboard misses the damage: it records the atoms and not the arrangement.

But S and F themselves are **not** isomers of one another. Crop yield and water quality do not share an ingredient set arranged differently; they are different molecules over a shared habitat. The correct claim is weaker and more useful:

> Management changes the **bond**, and the bond is decisive in the behaviour, while the inventory of atoms can stay constant.

World corroboration, single pool: Raudsepp-Hearne et al. 2010 (opened) write that bundles *"are distinct from inventories of ecosystem services that can be added up to obtain a total quantity of services, because adding the services within a bundle would both double count ecosystem services that interact and ignore varying social values placed on different ecosystem services."* That is Fig 12 :94 in ecology vocabulary. It is corroboration, not a figure line.

**Falsifier for Q1:** if the behaviour of the habitat could be recovered from the actor list alone, with no record of relations, the isomer/bond claim fails and the inventory is sufficient. This is checkable against any before/after dataset that records both species presence and interaction topology.

### Q2 — What is the order?

Fig 12 defines `→` as **sequence**: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :71-73). The cascade of Fig 11 :52 is ordered: *"first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* That is a `→` chain, not a symmetric `+` co-occurrence. The order is also a **dependency** order (Fig 3 :20): *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

Two ordering rules govern the management loop:

1. **Enumerate before lever.** Fig 11 :44 (*"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering"*) and Fig 9 :27-28 (*"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*). Choosing the lever first is the inversion the figures forbid.
2. **Refuse before mint.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: a term held that is false owes a refusal, not an explanation. In this question, the term "the service metric is sufficient for habitat health" is the one to refuse before any new monitoring term is minted.

**Falsifier for Q2:** if the effect on the slow function is simultaneous with the lever at no delay and no intermediate state, the chain is a `+` bond, not a `→` cascade, and the three-step-out reasoning is inapplicable to that pair. The claim would then need restating as a threshold comparison `⋛`, not a sequence.

### Q3 — Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54-56: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

A managed service and a slower ecological function overlap: both depend on water, season, organisms and relations. So they are a union. A lever on S alone clears the S-only region; the intersection stays bound by F's conditions. This is the structural reason the answer to the question is *can undermine*, and also why a well-designed lever that respects the intersection need not undermine (Raudsepp-Hearne et al. 2010 found high-crop municipalities with no crossed thresholds).

**Falsifier for Q3:** demonstrate a partition, that is, disjoint conditions with no shared variable. Then a single lever could be safe by construction and the unbundling requirement would not apply.

### Q4 — The analogue and its limit

The figure's own analogue is DDT (Fig 11 :52-54): first-order scoping, second-order effect, residual three steps out, with the mechanism established over the following decade. It is a **retrodiction**, a case the figure describes, not a forward test produced by this derivation. It is recorded as an analogue, not as evidence that this solution discovered anything.

A second analogue, from the ecology pool: Rodriguez et al. 2006 (opened) classify trade-offs along spatial scale, temporal scale and reversibility, and note that unintentional trade-offs arise *"when we are ignorant of the interactions among ES"* or *"when our knowledge of how they work is incorrect or incomplete."* That is the habitat-enumeration requirement stated in the world vocabulary. Low overlap with the figure text; still a single evidence pool with the other ecology sources.

## CONCLUSION

The right structure is not "two services in trade" as if they were two fixed objects. It is a habitat with a constant or near-constant inventory whose **bonds change**, and a cascade that runs in dependency order three steps out. The managed service and the slow function form a union of overlapping conditions, so a single lever leaves intersections standing. Fig 12 :94 is the compact statement: the arrangement is the substance.

Three falsifiers were written before the conclusion: recoverability of behaviour from the actor list (Q1), simultaneity with no intermediate state (Q2), and a genuine partition of conditions (Q3). Any one firing would narrow or withdraw the structural claim.
