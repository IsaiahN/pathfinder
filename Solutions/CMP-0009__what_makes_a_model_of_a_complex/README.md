## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *accuracy* of a model but what licenses the claim that a model explains. In effect terms: a model Γ predicts an observation o′ for a belief and action (b,a); R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is indexed per object slot s, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). "Reproduces observed patterns" is the case where R is near zero on the slots already observed. "Explanatory" is a different status that the figures must characterize without letting the model score itself with a quantity it also produces (Fig 1 :27).

- Option A, "a model is explanatory when it fits observed patterns well": leads to coverage and compression, which Fig 1 :25-27 names as frame-internal and disqualifies as a verdict. A term that explains one occasion perfectly fails the acceptance bargain (Fig 5 :44).
- Option B, "a model is explanatory when it carries a compositional structure that pays its description length, maintains contact with a ground that can refute it, and survives triangulation between low-overlap frames": leads to a two-condition shape, kept separate per domain and never averaged.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conditions that must both hold for a fitted model to earn the standing "explanatory," plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of internal structure from a failure of ground contact. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it (Fig 5 :62-64).

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `general`/`specified` mode. The model alone is not the seat of explanation; explanation is a property of model-and-ground-and-habitat, as capability is a property of agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the modelling question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of explanation.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
