# Expert verification packet - the Tether on explanatory models

**What this is:** a claim in a form that a modelling-and-simulation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the application, not `grounded`.** No intervention test or habitat substitution is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A model does not become explanatory by reproducing observed patterns. Coverage and compression are quantities the model produces about itself, so they cannot be the verdict, and a term that explains one occasion perfectly fails the acceptance bargain. Explanatory standing requires two conditions kept separate: C1, typed relations between the variables, so the model carries a route a flat co-occurrence cannot write and a re-applicable method crosses to a new scale; and C2, a maintained channel to a ground that can refuse it, with the residual reported per slot, never averaged, and the claim checked by a second low-overlap frame ranged separately against the same ground. Explanation is a property of model and habitat together, not of the model alone. The framework returns this shape, not the name of the right model for a given system, and it states its failure conditions before the fit is inspected.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 5, 12 and 4

- **Statement:** fit is not a verdict, and a bond, not a variable, is what a flat fit lacks.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
  - Fig 5 :44 - *"fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*
  - Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
  - Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a one-occasion fit whose stated term plus leftover residual still pays under the same declared code on a second occasion; or exhibit two composition terms with identical operands and identical bond types that differ in what they predict under intervention. Either fires against this sub-claim.

### C2 - ground-facing; checkable by a modeller against their own practice

- **Statement:** explanatory standing needs a per-slot residual, a ground that cannot be moved by the model, and a low-overlap second frame; agreement inside one evidence pool does not supply it.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
  - Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know well, is the reported error a single aggregate, or is a live slot exposed? Is there an instrument or intervention the model cannot talk out of a verdict? Do the two checks draw on different evidence?
- **Refuter:** a model declared explanatory whose per-slot residual hides a live slot that changes a prediction under intervention; or a standing established by agreement within one evidence pool with no refusing ground.

### C3 - union and habitat; checkable by a modeller against their own workflow

- **Statement:** C1 and C2 are a union, not a partition, and explanation is model-and-habitat, not model alone. Improving fit is effort above the break in the dependency chain.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
  - Fig 3 :19 - *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
  - Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
- **Refuter:** an explanatory standing that survives a habitat substitution removing the ground's payment; or a standing established with no stated failure condition and no bond vocabulary, purely by improving fit.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that prediction is worthless. It is a different goal. Predictive success alone does not certify explanatory standing.
- Not that every explanatory model must be complex, or that a small model cannot explain. What matters is the bond and the contact, not the parameter count.
- Not that agreement among modellers settles the question. Agreement in one evidence pool is one witness.
- Not that the framework certifies its own account of explanation. Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."*
- Not `grounded`. No intervention, substitution or second-frame test has been run in this packet.
- Not a name for the right model. The figures return a shape; naming a specific architecture as *the* explanation is outside what this packet claims.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument or intervention, not a further argument.

1. **Intervention test:** does the bond structure predict an intervention result that a flat-fit twin gets wrong?
2. **Habitat substitution:** does the method generate novel correct predictions in a new habitat, or only replay a past success?
3. **Per-slot exposure:** does the model declare a live slot residual that changes a prediction, or does the aggregate hide it?
4. **Second-frame check:** does a low-overlap frame, ranged separately against the same ground, converge or conflict?
5. Any of the nine pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :25-27, Fig 5 :44, Fig 12 :5 and Fig 4 :22 - four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one model you know: is its error per slot, is there a refusing instrument, and are the two checks independent?
3. Name one intervention that would distinguish a bonded model from its flat-fit twin, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
