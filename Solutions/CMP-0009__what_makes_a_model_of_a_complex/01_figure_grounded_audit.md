## 0. The claim under audit, and the proposal header

> **DECIDES:** A model earns "explanatory" only when two conditions hold and are kept separate: C1, a compositional structure whose bonds carry information the ingredient list does not and whose terms pay the description-length bargain; C2, a maintained channel to a ground that can refute it, with the per-slot residual exposed and the claim triangulated between low-overlap frames. A = B? No. Option A (fit is enough) answers "does the curve match"; Option B answers "what would have to be true for this curve to be a stand-in for the world." They are different questions (Fig 2 :40, Fig 9 :60).
> **HOURS:** This is a conceptual ruling, not a build. A twenty-minute expert check is specified in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. The cost of being wrong is a misdiagnosed model, not a deployed artifact.
> **PRIOR CASE:** `Solutions/QMGR-0037__spacetime_continuum_or_emergent/` and the BUS-0001 pilot, both read this session. Both refused a name where the figures return a shape and both kept verdicts per domain.
> **ANALOGUE:** The Library of Babel searcher: describe the residual in effect terms, bootstrap vocabulary by proximity, steer by the question-as-tether, stop at a shape. The Habitat: explanation is a property of model-and-habitat, never of the model alone (Fig 11 :39).
> **LIMIT:** At scale, agreement among modellers compounds while evidence does not (Fig 7 :13). The verdict cannot be handed to a consensus of fitted models. Only a ground can settle it.

## 1. Residual in effect terms

R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per object slot s (Fig 1 :11, Fig 1 :19). The question's residual is the gap between two statuses a single fitted model can have:

- **Reproduced patterns:** R near zero on the observed slots. This is coverage and compression, which the figure disqualifies: *"coverage, and compression achieved"* (Fig 1 :25) and *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* (Fig 1 :26) are *"frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* (Fig 1 :27).
- **Explanatory:** a status that must be anchored outside the frame, because *"The ground enters as the only metric."* (Fig 1 :23).

**Arity.** The comparison is a threshold relation between two model properties, so it carries the ordered comparison bond ⋛, not a symmetric join: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* (Operators table :51). Explanatory is a standing above fitted, not a second name for the same quantity.

**Symmetry.** Fitting is symmetric under relabelling of observed slots; explanation is not. Change the intervention and a merely-fitted model can be wrong in a way a structured model predicts. This is the seeing/doing asymmetry the causal literature names (Pearl, 2018, world source below), read here as the ordered bond of Fig 12 :87.

**Scale.** Single-model loop (per slot), population of models (selection between rooms, Fig 7 :35), and across the membrane between a fitted recording and a re-applicable method (Fig 4 :22-25). The same question is asked at all three scales; only actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :20).

**Strain test applied early.** A tempting first reading is "explanatory = high compression." That strains Fig 1 :25-27, which puts compression exactly in the disqualified bin. A line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity, so re-census: compression prices the description inside Γ; explanation is a standing of Γ against a ground. Different objects. No strain after re-census.

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

**Partition or union?** Union, not partition. "Explanatory" is the intersection of C1 (right internal structure) and C2 (maintained ground contact). *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the"* intersections standing (Fig 9 :55); *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). More fit clears a region where only C1 holds and leaves C2 untouched. So the question is two loops over one compound, not one harder problem.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (is the per-slot residual measured at all?) to vocabulary (are there terms for interventions and bonds, or only ingredients?) to objective (can the model say what would count as failure?) to planning (is there a route to a term that pays?) to learn and carry (does the method survive a scale crossing?). *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* (Fig 3 :20). *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27). Effort spent improving fit (a link above the break) cannot rescue a missing refuter below it (Fig 3 :19).

## 4. Findings

### F1 - Reproducing patterns is frame-internal and cannot be the verdict (decisive against Option A)

Fig 1 :24-27:

> *"Everything else either produces a residual or consumes the library. These do not count:"*
> *"coverage, and compression achieved"*
> *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*
> *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

And Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

A model that reproduces observed patterns is being scored by coverage and compression, the two quantities the figure explicitly removes from the verdict set. This is not a rhetorical preference; it is the figure's own accounting rule. The explanatory standing must come from something the frame does not itself produce.

**Refuter:** exhibit a criterion, independent of the model's own coverage and compression, on which a high-fit but ungrounded model is explanatory. Until such a criterion is named, Option A is disqualified by Fig 1 :27.

### F2 - A term that explains one occasion perfectly fails the bargain

Fig 5 :43-44:

> *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*

Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."*

Fitting one dataset exactly is the named failure, not the success. The bargain compares |φ| + |R|φ| against |R| (Formula step 3). A curve that memorizes one occasion has a large |φ| and a near-zero left-over residual on that occasion only; it has not paid across occasions. This is the figure-grounded form of the overfitting objection, and it lands without importing any statistical vocabulary.

**Refuter:** show a one-occasion fit whose |φ| + |R|φ| is still less than |R| under a declared code, for that same term on a second occasion. If the term is re-applicable it was not a one-occasion recording (Fig 4 :25).

### F3 - Per-slot residual, never aggregated

Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*.
Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A model can score well in aggregate while one slot it never resolved carries a live residual. That is precisely the difference between "reproduces observed patterns" and "explains": the latter exposes the slot where the prediction would fail under an intervention. Explanatory status requires the residual to be reported per slot, so the live one is visible.

**Refuter:** a model declared explanatory whose per-slot residual hides a live slot that changes a prediction under an intervention, while its aggregate score stays near zero.

### F4 - The bond is half the information, not the ingredient list

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

A fitted model can hold the right variables and the wrong relations among them. The difference is invisible in the fit, because the fit records which patterns occurred and not what produced them. An explanatory model carries the typed relation, so it can say what would happen if the arrangement changed. Two models with the same ingredients under different bonds are isomers, and *"the difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour"* (Fig 12 :117). This is why pattern reproduction cannot certify explanation: it is a reading of the flat formula.

**Refuter:** two models that share every ingredient but differ in a typed bond, shown to be interchangeable under intervention. That would collapse the isomer claim of Fig 12 :117.

### F5 - Explanatory is a method that crosses up, a fit is a recording

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*
Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success"* and cannot produce a new one.

A curve fitted to a dataset is a recording of one occasion. An explanatory model carries a generator that can be reapplied when the habitat changes. The membrane rule decides which one earns the label: only the method crosses up. This is the cleanest single figure-grounded answer to the question, and it is `general` in mode because it says what the label requires at any scale.

**Refuter:** a pure recording, with no re-applicable method, that generates novel correct predictions after a habitat substitution. Under Fig 4 :25 that cannot happen; if it does, the membrane rule is wrong.

### F6 - Explanatory claims are triangulated, not agreed

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

A model that merely reproduces patterns is a frame agreeing with its own data. Explanatory standing needs a second low-overlap frame ranged separately against the same ground. *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* (Fig 2 :31). Agreement among modellers who trained on the same corpus is one witness wearing many names.

**Refuter:** an explanatory standing established by agreement within one evidence pool, with no second closure and no ground that can refuse the claim. Fig 2 :40 forbids it; if it is allowed, F6 falls.

### F7 - Explanatory means refutable; the verifier must be outside the interior

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.
Fig 5 :50-55 names the fifth kind of gap: a term we hold is wrong, and the term owed *"refuses another rather than explaining a gap."*

A model flexible enough to reproduce any pattern cannot be refuted, and a claim that cannot be refuted cannot be checked. It reconstructs whatever the data returns, which is the collapse Fig 10 :42 forbids. Explanatory status therefore requires a stated failure condition written before the fit is inspected. This is the framework's own discipline: *"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."* (Fig 5 :66).

**Refuter:** a model declared explanatory whose verifier holds its full interior and whose failure condition cannot be stated in advance. If the framework admits that as a check, Fig 10 :42 is violated.

### F8 - Explanation is model-and-habitat, not model alone

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

The question "what makes a model explanatory" inherits the same shape as "what makes a system capable." The answer cannot be a property of the model considered alone; it is a property of the model and the habitat it is ranged against. A more elaborate architecture that does not change contact with the ground changes nothing. This is why X1 in the census has no anchor and is not yet a domain.

**Refuter:** an explanatory standing that survives a habitat substitution that removes the ground's payment, with contact unchanged. That would refute the capability claim as applied to models.

### F9 - Fitting searches inside a fixed closure

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* is import.
Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
Fig 13 :118: *"still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."*

More fitting is more search at the same reach. It cannot manufacture the relation the frame lacks. An explanatory model either already holds the bond that the patterns leave unstated, or it imports one from a differing closure with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13). This is a barrier result: no amount of curve fitting adds an atom.

**Refuter:** a model that acquires a genuinely new primitive by internal fitting alone, with no contact with a differing frame. Under Fig 8 :32 that is impossible; a demonstrated case breaks the closure law.

### F10 - Where the figures run out (gap)

Naming *which* bond structure, *which* second frame, or *which* instrument would make a specific complex-system model explanatory is a `gap` after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The figures give the shape of explanation (bonds, per-slot residual, a ground, a method, low-overlap triangulation, a refuter). They do not name the right model for a given system. That name needs a ground reading and a provenanced import, which this derivation does not have.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A criterion independent of a model's own coverage and compression settles explanatory standing in favour of a high-fit, ungrounded model (breaks F1).
2. A one-occasion fit still pays the MDL bargain on a second occasion under the same declared code (breaks F2).
3. A model declared explanatory hides a live per-slot residual that changes a prediction under intervention (breaks F3).
4. Two models sharing every ingredient but differing in a typed bond are interchangeable under intervention (breaks F4, the isomer claim).
5. A pure recording generates novel correct predictions after a habitat substitution with no re-applicable method (breaks F5, the membrane rule).
6. Explanatory standing is established by agreement within one evidence pool, with no second closure and no refusing ground (breaks F6, Fig 2 :40).
7. A model is explanatory while its verifier holds its interior and no failure condition can be written in advance (breaks F7, Fig 10 :42).
8. Explanatory standing survives a habitat substitution that removes the ground's payment (breaks F8).
9. A model acquires a genuinely new primitive by internal fitting alone (breaks F9, Fig 8 :32).

If any fires, the shape is wrong in the named place and the affected finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Refuse Option A in `general` mode: coverage and compression are frame-internal (F1), and a one-occasion fit fails the bargain (F2).
2. Adopt the two-condition shape C1 and C2, kept separate per domain and never averaged (sections 2 and 4).
3. Report residuals per slot, never as a single accuracy figure (F3). A global score is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
4. Describe the model's bonds, not only its variables; the arrangement is the substance (F4).
5. Ask what re-applicable method crosses the membrane, not what curve fits best (F5).
6. Require a second low-overlap frame and a refusing ground; do not pool agreement (F6).
7. Write the failure condition before the fit is inspected and keep the verifier outside the interior (F7).
8. Evaluate model-and-habitat, not model alone (F8).
9. State the `gap` at F10 plainly rather than naming a structure the figures do not return.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is fit explanatory?** No. Fig 1 :25-27 disqualifies the quantities fit uses as self-scores. Status of the criterion, not of the prediction.
2. **Does one-occasion accuracy help?** No. Fig 5 :44 names it as the failure of the bargain.
3. **What does a fitted model lack?** A typed bond and a per-slot residual. Fig 12 :5 and Fig 1 :19. Shape, not a name.
4. **What licenses the standing?** A method crossing the membrane (Fig 4 :22-25), a ground that does not update (Fig 2 :29), and low-overlap triangulation (Fig 2 :35). All three, not one.
5. **Can fit manufacture the missing relation?** No. Closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32); only import moves the wall (Fig 6 :14).
6. **Is this settled?** No. Fig 11 :39 makes capability a property of model-and-habitat; the ground must pay. Status `candidate`.
7. **Is the question one question?** No. It is a union of C1 and C2 (Fig 9 :55-57); unbundle before searching again.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: explanatory standing requires the right bonds and a re-applicable method (C1) together with a maintained, refutable, triangulated channel to a ground (C2), reported per slot and never averaged. Option A fails: coverage and compression are disqualified as self-scores, a one-occasion fit fails the bargain, and a recording cannot cross the membrane. The specific name of the right model is a `gap`, not a silence. Strains after re-census: **none**; the two apparent conflicts were resolved by re-census, not smoothed over. Every claim above is `general` or `specified` and none is `grounded`.
