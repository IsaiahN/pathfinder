## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What does a bond add that the ingredient list does not?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

If a fitted model and an explanatory model contain the same variables, the difference cannot be in the variables. It is in the typed relation: which variable produces which, which is a threshold comparison, which is a sequence, which is a negation of a held claim. A fit records co-occurrence, which is the symmetric conjunction `+` of a flat formula; the Operators table notes that `+` is *"the only bond in a flat formula, which is why a formula records ingredients and not arrangement"* (Operators :16). An explanatory model must carry at least one ordered bond (`→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`) or the unary negation `¬`, because only those can carry a route, a threshold, an absence that is the point, or a refusal.

- ⇒ **Q1 result:** the missing content is a bond, not a variable. This is `general` in mode (a structural claim about compositions) and is triangulated for this question across Fig 12 :5, :94 and the Operators table :16, three low-overlap passages inside the figure set.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two composition terms with identical operand sets and identical bond types that nevertheless differ in what they predict under intervention. If so, the bond is not the rest of the information, and Fig 12 :5 is wrong as applied here.

### Q2 - Are a fitted model and an explanatory model isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 gives the test: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the formula and decisive in behaviour.
- Fig 12 :123 gives the weaker relation: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two cases:

1. **Same variable set, different bonds.** If the two models use the same variables but the fit uses only symmetric co-occurrence and the explanatory model uses an ordered or refusing bond, they satisfy Fig 12 :117. They are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is the strong and testable version.
2. **Different variable sets.** If the explanatory model requires a variable the fit lacks (an instrument, an intervention indicator, a second frame's reading), they are not isomers under Fig 12 :117. They are two members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), the group being "state - structure - state" or "model ranged against a ground", with different qualifiers.

- ⇒ **Q2 result:** the isomer claim is real only in case 1; case 2 collapses it to a functional-group claim. Stating which case holds for a given pair of models is a ground question, not a figure question.
- **Falsifier:** if a model becomes explanatory by adding a variable without changing any bond, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading stands. That is a legitimate outcome and is recorded here in advance.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order of the two conditions?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Formula step 2: refuse before mint; a REFUTED gap *"is a refusal, not an explanation."*
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

The order is not "fit first, then add explanation." It is:

1. **Perception** (Fig 3 :4-5): is R measured per slot at all? Without this, no later link has an input.
2. **Vocabulary** (Fig 3 :6-8): are there bond terms and intervention terms, or only ingredients? This is Fig 12 :111's grammar, not a dictionary.
3. **Objective** (Fig 3 :9-11): can the model state what would count as a failure? This is the pre-registered refuter of `01` section 5.
4. **Refuse before mint** (Formula step 2, Fig 5 :53-55): if a held claim about the model is false, the owed term is a refusal (`¬`), not a more elaborate fit.
5. **Planning and carry** (Fig 3 :12-15, Fig 4 :22): choose a route to a term that pays, then carry the method, not the recording.

C1 and C2 are not sequential stages but interlocked links: C1 supplies the vocabulary and method, C2 supplies the objective and the refusing ground. Fig 3's chain runs through both.

- ⇒ **Q3 result:** the dependency order is perception to vocabulary to objective to refusal to method. *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not"* (Fig 3 :19). More fit is effort above the break.
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate an explanatory standing established with no per-slot residual, no stated failure condition and no bond vocabulary, purely by improving fit. That would invert Fig 3 :20 and Fig 12 :111 together.

### Q4 - Is there a known analogue that closes the gap at F10?

The gap at F10 asks which specific model earns the standing. Three analogue candidates were checked:

1. **Structural identifiability in dynamical systems.** Two parameter sets giving the same input-output behaviour are observationally equivalent; intervention or extra measurement discriminates them. This is a strong analogue of the isomer case and of the metamerism point in `05` section 4. It corroborates the *shape* of C2 but does not name a model. World source: read as described in `10_source_ledger.csv` where used; if not opened for a given file, it is not cited there.
2. **Universality classes in statistical physics.** Different microscopic models share critical exponents. This is a functional-group relation (same outcome class, different atoms), not an isomer. It corroborates Fig 12 :123 and warns against claiming identity across domains. Anderson (1972, world source) supplies the level-relativity.
3. **The map and the territory.** A map that reproduces every contour of a territory still is not the territory; what it lacks is the generator's capacity to surprise. This is an analogy only, and the framework's own rule for analogy applies: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"* (Fig 9 :30-31). It corroborates nothing by itself.

- ⇒ **Q4 result:** the analogues corroborate the two-condition shape at low overlap (statistics, physics, semantics), but none supplies the missing name. F10 remains a declared `gap`. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **Falsifier:** a source showing that one of these analogues actually names a model that is explanatory by the figures' own two conditions, with a ground reading. Until then the analogues are supports, not witnesses.

## CONCLUSION

A bond, not a variable, is what a fit lacks: the ordered or refusing relation that carries a route a flat co-occurrence cannot write (Q1). A fitted model and an explanatory model are isomers when they share variables and differ in bonds, and only functional-group cousins when the explanatory model needs a variable the fit lacks (Q2). Their order is dependency order, not prestige or chronology: per-slot residual, bond vocabulary, a stateable failure, a refusal before any new fit, then the method crossing the membrane (Q3). The analogues support the shape and do not name the model (Q4). The isomer claim, the dependency order and the gap each carry their own falsifier written above.
