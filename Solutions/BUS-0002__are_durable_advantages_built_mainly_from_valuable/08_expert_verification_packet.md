# Expert verification packet - the Tether on sources of durable advantage

**What this is:** a claim in a form a strategy or organisation scholar can check. Mode, derivation and
refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No instrument separating duration from level, or rent from
legitimacy, is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are durable advantages built mainly from valuable resources, distinctive capabilities,
market position, or institutional relationships?" has the wrong shape. The four candidates are a union
of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate levers; each governs a different subset, so the
disagreement does not shrink with effort. Durable advantage is a property of firm-and-habitat contact,
indexed per firm-slot, and no standing list of good sources survives a change of the residual. The four
frames are low overlap, so their union, ranged against one unmoving performance anchor, reaches what
none reaches alone. "Institutional relationships" must be split into rent-generating interfirm ties
and legitimacy-producing field isomorphism; the second predicts growing sameness, so it cannot be the
main source of durable *differential* advantage. The framework names no winner. It offers the shape,
four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and structural; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 11
- **Statement:** the four candidates form a union, not a partition, and capability is not an
  agent-alone property.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition,
  unbundle before you search again."* Also Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat,
  never of the agent alone"* and Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of
  residual."*
- **What to check:** does the candidate list in the question behave like a partition (one lever clears
  it) or a union (overlapping conditions, each binding in its subset)? Is there any sense in which a
  capability is a property of the firm considered in isolation from its habitat?
- **Refuter:** show one frame predicting the full persistent residual across sectors and time with the
  others adding no surplus; or show a capability change that moves the ground with no contact change.

### C2 - literature-facing; checkable against the strategy and organisation literature
- **Statement:** the four schools ask different questions, and the empirical decomposition refuses a
  single winner.
- **What to check:** Barney (1991) qualifies internal resources (value, rareness, imitability,
  substitutability); Teece, Pisano and Shuen (1997) analyse processes of coordinating and combining;
  McGahan and Porter (1997) decompose profitability variance into year 2 percent, industry 19 percent,
  corporate-parent 4 percent and business-specific 32 percent, with large sector differences; Dyer and
  Singh (1998) locate rents in the interfirm relationship; DiMaggio and Powell (1983) explain
  homogenisation, not differential advantage. Are those five the same question? Do the percentages
  rank levers, or do they describe a union with interactions?
- **Figure anchor:** Fig 8 :45 - *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* And Fig 2
  :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **Refuter:** a rigorous decomposition in which one effect dominates *persistent* variance stably
  across sectors and decades, with the others contributing no out-of-sample surplus.

### C3 - the unbundling claim; checkable by an organisation scholar
- **Statement:** "institutional relationships" is two mechanisms, not one, and only one of them is a
  plausible source of durable differential advantage.
- **What to check:** Dyer and Singh (1998): *"a firm's critical resources may span firm boundaries"*
  and the four interfirm sources of advantage (relation-specific assets, knowledge-sharing routines,
  complementary resources, effective governance). DiMaggio and Powell (1983): organisations *"tend to
  be increasingly and inevitably homogenous in their forms and practices"*, and isomorphic processes
  *"do not necessarily increase internal efficiency."* Do these predict the same output for the same
  residual? If not, can they be scored as one candidate?
- **Figure anchor:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a
  list of separate ones."*
- **Refuter:** a decomposition in which rent-generating and legitimacy-producing mechanisms predict
  the same persistent-residual outcome in the same firms.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that resources, capabilities, position or relationships are unimportant. Each governs a subset.
- Not that any school is false in its own domain. The error is declaring one "mainly".
- Not that McGahan and Porter's percentages settle BUS-0002. They measure variance of profitability,
  not duration of a small residual. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap."*
- Not that the four schools are isomers over a shared atom set. They are members of a functional group
  (Fig 12 :124); the isomer claim is a separate, within-firm claim with its own falsifier in `06`.
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that the framework is the only true account of firm performance.
- Not a ground reading. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against
  the figures, produced by an LLM. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A duration instrument that separates a small persistent residual from a high-variance sector, then
   a repeated decomposition across sectors and decades.
2. A relation discriminator separating relational rents from field isomorphism in the same firms.
3. A union-surplus test: fit each frame alone and all four together against the persistent-residual
   anchor. No out-of-sample surplus means the union claim fails.
4. The contact test from Fig 11 :40: can a capability move the ground without changing contact?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 11 :39 and :49-51, Fig 8 :45, Fig 2 :35 (five short passages).
2. Say whether the four candidates in the question behave as a partition or a union in your field.
3. Say whether "institutional relationships" names one mechanism or two, and which one you would
   expect to produce durable *difference*.
4. Name one instrument that would separate duration of advantage from level of profitability, or say
   why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
