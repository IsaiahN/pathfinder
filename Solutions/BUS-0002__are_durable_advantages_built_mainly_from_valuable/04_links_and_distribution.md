## What the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The schools are not rivals over one quantity.** Barney (1991) asks which *internal resource*
   attributes qualify (value, rareness, imitability, substitutability). Teece, Pisano and Shuen (1997)
   ask about *processes of coordinating and combining* under rapid technological change and explicitly
   contrast this with strategising that keeps rivals off balance. Porter's positioning tradition and
   McGahan and Porter (1997) ask about *industry and business-unit effects*. Dyer and Singh (1998) ask
   about *interfirm* rents. DiMaggio and Powell (1983) ask why organisations become *alike*. Five
   different questions wearing one "source of advantage" heading. That is Fig 9 :4-5 in the literature.
2. **The empirical decomposition refuses a single winner.** McGahan and Porter find industry effects
   of 19 percent and business-specific effects of 32 percent, with the balance varying by sector and
   a negative industry/corporate-parent covariance. This is a union with interactions, not a ranking
   of levers. It is corroboration for the union shape, not a measurement of *duration*.
3. **A low-overlap witness supports agent-plus-habitat.** Chollet (2019) shows that task skill can be
   *"bought"* with priors or training data, masking generalisation power. Skill at a task is not the
   capability of the agent alone. This is an AI-measurement witness for Fig 11 :39, arriving from a
   different evidence pool.
4. **Institutional legitimacy pulls the other way.** DiMaggio and Powell's isomorphism makes firms
   more similar *"without necessarily making them more efficient"*, so it cannot be the main source of
   *durable differential* advantage. It can explain why an apparent advantage diffuses and disappears.

## Distribution: offer, not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22 and :25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* and *"A recording
  carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full framework before
  anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and the
  refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold
  the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the
  frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what they would expect to see if the union shape were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** - agreement from a shared evidence pool is disqualifying for a verdict. A send list
  selected for people already sympathetic to a "no single winner" conclusion produces a convention.
- **Fig 10 :59** - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is
  disqualifying."* Declare the stake: this derivation is produced inside the framework it evaluates.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what the
recipient would count as a failure, and record the verdict; (3) only then the three links, with the
note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) offer the formula as a method to reuse
with the request that each user write their own refuter and state their own mode.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and
  Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks
  the shape without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, agreement stops
  being evidence. The stage-1 verdict is recorded *before* any theory is shown and is never pooled
  as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: methods cross up. The formula is a
  generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** Formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the
  direction of its own question. Each user must write down what would refute their result before
  running it, and label the mode of what they get.

## AI-use and disclosure caveat

Several target venues now require explicit AI disclosure. AOM 2027 asks that the title page identify
the model used and its purpose, and states that disclosed use does not independently affect a
proposal's quality score. ASQ asks submitting authors to take responsibility for accuracy and to
disclose AI-assisted preparation. Sage's policy and the AOM policy should be read before any
submission. This package is a simulated derivation (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7) and must be
described as such; it is not an empirical test of the strategy question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. If a refuter fires, do
not proceed to stage 2.
