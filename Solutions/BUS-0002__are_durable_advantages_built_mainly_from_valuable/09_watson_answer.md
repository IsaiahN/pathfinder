# WATSON answer - BUS-0002

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide; world sources are secondary
corroboration only. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the
figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

Are durable advantages built mainly from valuable resources, distinctive capabilities, market
position, or institutional relationships?

Residual in effect terms: a firm's library predicts a market return; the market returns an
observation; R equals the gap between prediction and observation, indexed per firm-slot and never
aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Durable advantage is the case where one slot's R stays small over a long
duration. The question asks which single candidate mainly produces that persistence. An answer must
name the conditions for a small persistent residual, say whether they form a partition or a union, and
name an instrument that discriminates failure of one condition from another.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R is unary per firm-slot, asymmetric among the four candidates (a resource is not a position), and
multi-scale. D1 has no nameable anchor in isolation and is not yet a domain; it becomes one only as
firm-and-habitat contact. D2, D3 and D4 have anchors.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 firm-internal library | managers, resource bundles, routines | resource rents, reconfiguration capacity | none inside the firm alone | not standalone; the agent half of the pair |
| D2 market / industry structure | firm, rivals, entrants, buyers, suppliers | profitability, entry barriers | measured industry profitability differential | anchored |
| D3 interfirm dyad / network | partner firms, alliances | relational rents above solo return | measured relationship performance net of partners | anchored |
| D4 institutional field | state, professions, regulators, peers | legitimacy, isomorphism | measured homogenisation of form and practice | anchored, but predicts sameness |

Union, not partition. Dependency order: field and structure conditions, then a vocabulary of
affordances, then a stated objective, then planning and reconfiguration, then the ground-facing
return (Fig 3 :20-21).

### T2 On D1, the internal library

"Mainly valuable resources" and "mainly distinctive capabilities" fail as general claims because both

### T3 On D2, market position

### T4 On D3, interfirm relationships

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor

A tie is a relation, not a fixed kind; its valence depends on the slot. Interfirm rents are a genuine

### T5 On D4, institutional relationships

Fig 9 :54 (quoted in T1) governs: unbundle. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* The

main source of durable *differential* advantage, and it cannot be scored together with interfirm
rents. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 + Fig 12 + W4 and W5, two low-overlap world sources).

### T6 On a single winner

## WORLD

### W1 Barney 1991, strategic management
- Field: strategic management. Author, venue, year: Jay Barney, Journal of Management 17(1), 1991.
- Quote: the paper examines *"value, rareness, imitability, and substitutability"* as empirical
  indicators of a resource's potential to generate sustained advantage.
- Prediction: internal resource attributes govern which resources qualify.
- Status: **corroborates** T2 in scope (it identifies one real subset), and **conflicts** with any claim
  that it alone answers the question.

### W2 McGahan and Porter 1997, strategic management
- Field: strategic management, empirical variance decomposition. Author, venue, year: Anita M.
  McGahan and Michael E. Porter, Strategic Management Journal 18(S1), 1997.
- Quote: effects account for *"2 percent, 19 percent, 4 percent, and 32 percent"* of aggregate
  variance, and their importance *"differs substantially across broad economic sectors."*
- Prediction: no single effect dominates across sectors; effects interact.
- Status: **corroborates** T1 and T3. It does not settle duration, only profitability variance.

### W3 Chollet 2019, artificial intelligence measurement
- Field: AI and measurement, low overlap with strategy. Author, venue, year: François Chollet,
  arXiv:1911.01547, 2019.
- Quote: *"skill is heavily modulated by prior knowledge and experience"*, so measuring skill at a
  task masks generalisation power.
- Prediction: task skill is not the capability of the agent alone; history and priors are part of it.
- Status: **corroborates** T2 from a different evidence pool. It is a parallel, not a source for the
  figure claim.

### W4 Dyer and Singh 1998, organisation and management theory
- Field: strategic management and interorganizational theory. Author, venue, year: Jeffrey H. Dyer and
  Harbir Singh, Academy of Management Review 23(4), 1998.
- Quote: *"a firm's critical resources may span firm boundaries"*, with relation-specific assets,
  knowledge-sharing routines, complementary resources and effective governance as sources.
- Prediction: a low-overlap interfirm frame contributes relational rents.
- Status: **corroborates** T4 and supports the unbundling in T5.

### W5 DiMaggio and Powell 1983, organizational sociology
- Field: organizational sociology, low overlap with strategy. Author, venue, year: Paul J. DiMaggio
  and Walter W. Powell, American Sociological Review 48(2), 1983.
- Quote: organisations *"tend to be increasingly and inevitably homogenous in their forms and
  practices"*, and isomorphic processes *"do not necessarily increase internal efficiency."*
- Prediction: institutional relationships produce convergence, not durable differential advantage.
- Status: **corroborates** T5 and **conflicts** with treating institutional relationships as a fourth
  source symmetrical with the other three.

### W6 Teece, Pisano and Shuen 1997, strategic management
- Field: strategic management. Author, venue, year: David J. Teece, Gary Pisano and Amy Shuen,
  Strategic Management Journal 18(7), 1997.
- Quote: advantage rests on *"distinctive processes (ways of coordinating and combining)"* shaped by
  asset positions and evolution paths.
- Prediction: reconfiguration processes are a distinct, cycle-like subset.
- Status: **corroborates** T1 (a different question) and the bond analysis in `06`.

## VERDICT

From the figures: the question's partition premise is refused. Durable advantage is a union-level
property of firm-and-habitat contact, indexed per firm-slot, with the four candidates acting as
low-overlap frames whose union, ranged against one unmoving performance anchor, predicts the shape.
Each governs a subset; institutional legitimacy predicts sameness and is not symmetrical with the
other three. No single "mainly" answer is licensed. Mode `specified`, not `grounded`.

**Cost if wrong:** if the union verdict is wrong and one source genuinely dominates persistently,
researchers and firms are told to maintain four frames of contact where one lever would do, wasting
measurement effort and obscuring a real concentration of advantage.

**Undoable:** yes. The verdict is a shape plus four refuters, not an institution. Publish the
refuters, invite a decomposition of persistent variance against an independent anchor, and withdraw
or revise the shape if one fires. Nothing was deleted to reach this verdict, so the record can carry
its own correction (Fig 6 :42-43).

**Reversed by:** (1) one frame predicting the full persistent residual across sectors and time with no
surplus from the others; (2) one effect dominating persistent variance stably across sectors and
decades; (3) a capability change moving the ground with no contact change (breaks Fig 11 :40); (4) a
decomposition showing rent-generating and legitimacy-producing mechanisms predict the same output for
the same residual.
