## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question as posed asks which of four candidate stores "mainly" builds durable advantage: valuable
resources, distinctive capabilities, market position, or institutional relationships.

Stated as an effect: a firm's library Γ predicts a market return Γ(b,a); the market returns o′;
R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is the gap. Durable advantage is the case where R for one firm-slot stays small over
a long duration, while other slots see R grow after a short run.

Two immediate consequences, both figure-derived:

1. **Per slot, not per average.** *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
   (Fig 1 :19) and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
   (Fig 1 :22). So a single winner cannot be decided by averaging over all firms or all industries.
   Any answer must say which slot and which duration.
2. **The ground is the only metric.** *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a
   quantity it also produces."* (Fig 1 :27). A school that measures advantage by its own preferred
   stock (resources by resource stock, position by market share) commits the named error.

**Binding what an answer would look like (Formula, "Direction").** Before the run I bind the sought
shape as an ordinary term: an answer must name (a) the condition or conditions that must hold for a
slot's R to stay small over a long duration, (b) whether those conditions form a partition (one lever)
or a union (overlapping conditions, each binding in its subset), and (c) an instrument that
discriminates failure of one condition from failure of another. Only then can the preference be
refused by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 Describing R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** unary per firm-slot (one residual per object slot). The candidate "sources" are not
  operands of R; they are candidate frames whose closure might predict R's shape.
- **Symmetry:** the four candidates are not interchangeable under relabeling. A resource is not a
  position, and a position is not a relation. They are four frames with different closures, so the
  relation among them is a union claim, not the k! spellings of one symmetric term (Fig 12 :92).
- **Scale:** multi-scale and recursive. The four operate at firm-internal, interfirm, industry, and
  institutional-field scales. A composition that settles at one scale becomes an atom at the next
  (Fig 12 :104), so "mainly" may be true at one scale and false at another.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor, meaning something that settles a question and
does not move when any frame pushes it (Fig 2 :29-30).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | admitted? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 firm-internal library (resources, routines, competences) | managers, resource bundles, organisational processes | resource rents, reconfiguration capacity | none inside the firm alone; the effect registers only against market return o′ | **not standalone.** It is the agent half of firm-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39) |
| D2 market / industry structure (position) | focal firm, rivals, entrants, buyers, suppliers | profitability, barriers to entry | measured industry-level profitability differential, not moved by any one firm's theory | **yes, partially anchored** |
| D3 interfirm dyad / network (relationships) | partner firms, alliances, supply chains | relational rents above solo returns | measured performance of the relationship net of each partner's standalone return | **yes, anchored** |
| D4 institutional field (rules, legitimacy) | state, professions, regulators, peers | legitimacy, isomorphism | measured homogenisation of form and practice across a field | **yes, anchored, but it predicts sameness, not differential advantage** |

D1 fails the anchor test in isolation, so by the method's own rule it is not a domain yet. It becomes
one only as firm-and-habitat contact. D4 is anchored but its predicted output is convergence, which
is a strain on calling it a source of *durable differential* advantage. Both strains are carried, not
smoothed over.

### 1.3 Partition or union?

Union, not partition. Three independent signals:

1. The four candidates each keep working on a different subset of cases. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements
   that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not
   one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
2. Bundling is the named failure. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a
   partition, unbundle before you search again."*
3. The empirical variance decomposition supports the union reading directly: effects differ by
   sector and are *"related in complex ways"* (McGahan and Porter, 1997), with a negative covariance
   between corporate-parent and industry effects. That is a union with interactions, not a list of
   separate levers.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20-21). The working order is:

```
institutional field + market structure  (outer habitat, not authored by the firm)
        -> firm-internal library and its contact with that habitat
                -> realised market position and persistent return (ground-facing output)
```

Fig 3 :6-7 gives the middle link its content: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be
moved, opened, joined, blocked"*. A firm with resources but no terms for what its habitat affords
has a broken middle link, and *"a link with no instrument is not a link that works"* (Fig 3 :27).
Effort spent above the break (more resources) cannot rescue it.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The four candidates form a union, not a partition (decisive on the question's shape)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a
different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than
search."*

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search
again."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate
ones."*

The strategic-management literature has exactly this shape: each school keeps working on a different
subset, so the debate does not shrink with effort. McGahan and Porter (1997) decompose profitability
variance into year 2 percent, industry 19 percent, corporate-parent 4 percent, and business-specific
32 percent, then add that the importance of the effects *"differs substantially across broad
economic sectors"* and that the effects are *"related in complex ways."* A single "mainly X" answer
erases the union.

**Verdict: triangulated**, two figures (Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :54-57) with one empirical decomposition
that is low overlap with the figures (accounting variance, not the metatheory).

### F2 - Advantage is a property of firm-and-habitat, never of the firm alone

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

This refuses "mainly valuable resources" and "mainly distinctive capabilities" as *general* claims,
because both are internal-to-agent stories. It equally refuses a purely external story: the habitat
is only half of the pair. The grounded reading in `05_what_shape_means.md` keeps this as a shape, not
a name.

The same figure admits this is an uncited claim of the framework (Arrival of the Fittest, "a note on
what is not cited"). Chollet (2019), in a different field, is a parallel: skill at a task *"is heavily
modulated by prior knowledge and experience"*, so measuring skill alone masks generalisation power.
That is agent-plus-history, not agent alone.

**Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 11 + Chollet 2019, low overlap: AI measurement versus metatheory).
**Strain recorded:** the figure's capability claim is an axiom inside this framework, not a derived
result; its corroboration is cross-field, not a proof.

### F3 - No standing list of good sources survives a change of residual

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

So "valuable resource", "distinctive capability", "market position" and "institutional relationship"
are not kinds with fixed valence. A patent (resource) is an advantage in one regime and a sunk cost in
another. An alliance (relationship) is relational rent in one setting and a dependency in another.
McGahan and Porter's sector variation is the empirical face of this: industry effects are larger in
lodging, services, trade and transport than in manufacturing.

**Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 11 :49-51 + McGahan and Porter 1997, different methods and fields).

### F4 - Triangulate the four against one unmoving anchor, do not negotiate among them

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

The anchor here is a sound measure of the persistent performance differential, which none of the four
schools may move. If the four schools agree because they draw on the same cited corpus of strategy
texts, that is one witness, not four (Fig 8 :48-49). The source ledger records overlap so this is
visible.

**Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 2 :29-46 + Fig 7 :35, two figures with different mechanisms).

### F5 - The union has surplus exactly where the closures differ

Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

Fig 8 :48-49: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

The four frames are genuinely low overlap: internal resource stocks (Barney, 1991), internal
reconfiguration processes (Teece, Pisano and Shuen, 1997), industry structure (Porter, 1980; McGahan
and Porter, 1997), interfirm relational rents (Dyer and Singh, 1998), and institutional fields
(DiMaggio and Powell, 1983). Their union has surplus. Corroboration that no one of them explains all
variance is not the same as a figure-derived proof, so this stays `specified`.

**Verdict: single-source for the mechanism** (Fig 8), with empirical corroboration, not triangulation
of the mechanism itself.

### F6 - "Institutional relationships" is itself a bundled term

Dyer and Singh (1998) argue that *"a firm's critical resources may span firm boundaries"* and identify
relation-specific assets, knowledge-sharing routines, complementary resources and governance as
sources of interorganizational advantage. That is a **rent-generating** relationship.

DiMaggio and Powell (1983), by contrast, explain why organizations *"tend to be increasingly and
inevitably homogenous in their forms and practices"*, and note that isomorphic processes *"do not
necessarily increase internal efficiency."* That is a **legitimacy-producing** relationship, and it
predicts convergence, not durable differential advantage.

These are two different things filed under one phrase in the question. Fig 9 :54 governs: unbundle
before searching. Fig 11 :43 adds *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* List both actors and
both currencies before asking which contributes to which residual.

**Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 9 :54 + Fig 11 :43 with two low-overlap world sources, organization
studies and organizational sociology). **Strain recorded:** the question's phrase "institutional
relationships" is not a single candidate and cannot be scored as one.

### F7 - No single-source answer is not a silence

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*

Had the census found no figure line governing after a genuine re-census, the honest output would be
`gap`, not a forced winner among the four. Here the union verdict is a positive result, so `gap` is
not claimed. The distinction matters: a single "mainly X" answer is not merely unfound, it is the
wrong shape for a union.

**Verdict: single-source on the rule itself** (Fig 8 :39 and Fig 13 :134 agree, but share the framework
closure, so this is internal consistency, not independent corroboration).

### F8 - Frame-internal scoring is disqualified in all four schools

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

A "resource-based" answer scored by resource stock, a "capability" answer scored by routine count, a
"position" answer scored by market share, and an "institutional" answer scored by legitimacy all score
themselves with a quantity they also produce. None is the ground. This is why the first contact in
`02_outreach_email.md` asks what the recipient would count as a failure, before any theory is shown.

**Verdict: single-source** (Fig 1 :27, Fig 5 :24-27 on frame-internal quantities).

### F9 - Cross-domain borrowings carry a round-trip gap

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

Applying VRIN to institutional relationships, or applying a variance decomposition to "duration of
advantage", carries a method across a boundary. The transform is lossy. The gap R_T = gap(x,
(T_E ∘ T_A)(x)) is the honest report of what was given up, not an error to eliminate (Fig 4 :31-32).

Concretely: McGahan and Porter decompose the **variance of profitability**, not the **duration of a
residual staying small**. Importing their percentages as an answer to BUS-0002 is exactly a lossy
cross-scale move. They are corroboration for the union shape, not settlement of the duration claim.

**Verdict: single-source** (Fig 4 :27-32 plus Formula step 6).

### F10 - Prestige is not action in the schools debate

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a
budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

The currently dominant school in strategy (whichever it is this year) is a prestige proxy, not a
verdict. Fig 2 :40 forbids selecting the winner by how much its framing agrees with ours. Fig 10 :59
adds: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

**Verdict: triangulated** (essay + Fig 2 :40 + Fig 10 :59, different registers).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a
design instrument.

The union-and-contact shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single one of the four frames, ranged against an independent anchor, predicts the full
   persistent-residual variance across sectors and time, with the other three contributing no
   surplus (breaks Fig 8 :45, Fig 9 :4-5).
2. A rigorous variance decomposition finds one effect accounting for the overwhelming majority of
   *persistent* (not merely contemporaneous) variance, stable across sectors and across decades
   (breaks F1's reading of McGahan and Porter, and Fig 11 :39).
3. A firm's capability is shown to raise its persistent return with no change in contact with its
   habitat (breaks Fig 11 :40).
4. The four frames are shown to draw on one shared evidence pool such that their union adds no
   surplus, and the apparent sector differences are artefacts of one dataset (breaks Fig 8 :48-49).
5. A decomposition of "institutional relationships" into relational-rent and legitimacy mechanisms
   shows both contribute the same predicted output to the same residual (breaks F6's unbundling).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be
labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer "mainly resources" or "mainly position". Answer the union: persistent advantage is
   a property of firm-and-habitat contact, per slot, with each candidate governing a subset.
2. Unbundle "institutional relationships" into rent-generating interfirm ties and legitimacy-producing
   field isomorphism before scoring either (F6).
3. State the anchor explicitly: a persistent performance differential that no school may move, and
   range each frame against it separately (F4).
4. Treat the empirical variance literature as corroboration of the union shape, not as settlement of
   the duration question (F9).
5. Lead outreach with the residual and the refuter, not with the framework (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a
   characterised residual, never the source."*).
6. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
   Send the generator: the shape, the refuter and the instrument, not the conclusion.

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives after re-census and canonical check: durable advantage is not "mainly" any one of
the four. It is a property of firm-and-habitat contact, indexed per firm-slot, and the four candidates
are low-overlap frames whose union, ranged against an unmoving performance anchor, predicts its shape.
Each governs a subset; the intersections are the hard part and are a specific compound, not a harder
case of any one frame (Fig 9 :57-58).

Mode: `specified`. Strains after re-census: two, both named and kept (the uncited capability axiom,
and the bundled phrase "institutional relationships"). Status: candidate, not settled.
