## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone before reading the primary's §2, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between predicted and measured function when an actor or interaction changes, per function, site and time. Arity: five functions times sites and times, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55), not one question. Symmetry: symmetric in neither actor nor function, because valence flips with the residual (Fig 11 :49). Scale: direct contact first, then cascade to second and third order.
- **Domain 1, the five function residuals.** Actors: five measurements in distinct units. Currency: change under removal or addition. Anchor: the measurement. Question type: union with overlaps, not partition.
- **Domain 2, the habitat.** Actors: species, conditions, relations in contact. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the world enumerated, which updates and so is not the ground. Question type: enumeration, neither partition nor composition.
- **Domain 3, management.** Actors: managers. Currency: interventions. Anchor: D1's measurement. Question type: partition of maintained and unmaintained.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence in this simulated run is zero; all four domains draw on one evidence pool.

Order by dependency: enumerate before measuring, measure before comparing, compare before intervening (Fig 3 :20); refuse the standing roster before minting a new "keystone" name (Formula step 2); describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :54-55 (union split), Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual), Fig 11 :43-53 (enumeration, valence, cascade), Fig 2 :29-40 (anchor and correlation), Fig 12 :123 (functional group).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: five ledgers, enumerate the habitat, valence relative to the residual, species and interactions as one kind of entry, no standing roster, no cross-function sum.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a real conservation decision, for example choosing which species to protect or reintroduce for pollination and carbon storage at a site.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the entries** in each ledger, which the shorthand deliberately leaves open and the site must supply by enumeration;
- the **true extent of the cascade**, which the shorthand expresses as a stopping rule but which has to be discovered in the field;
- the **units of each function measurement**, which the shorthand declares incommensurable but which a decision must still compare at a threshold;
- the **maintenance burden**, which the shorthand names as asymmetric but which a manager must actually fund over time.

Conversely, three things survive cleanly: the per-slot unit, the union split with intersections, and the rule that valence is relative to the residual. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the figures are the same as the five-ledger summary" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
