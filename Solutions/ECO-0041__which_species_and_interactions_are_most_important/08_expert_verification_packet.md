# Expert verification packet: the Tether on important species and interactions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a keystone-species researcher or ecosystem-function analyst can check in twenty minutes. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the refuters, not `grounded`.** No removal or addition measurement is claimed here. This is a form and a route, not a list of names.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The five named ecosystem functions are a union of overlapping conditions, so one importance ranking is not a well-formed answer. For each function, enumerate every actor, condition and relation in contact with it, outward until the cascade stops mattering. An entry, whether a species or an interaction, is important for that function at a place and time if removing or adding it changes the measured function. Species and interactions are the same kind of entry, valence is relative to the residual rather than carried by the actor, and there is no standing keystone roster. Cross-function comparison is a threshold relation at declared units, never a sum, and a managed ranking is maintained work rather than a solved state.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 11

- **Statement:** the five functions must be handled as five function-indexed ledgers over enumerated habitats, not one averaged ranking.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- **Check:** do these three lines say what the claim says, without a strained reading? The functions overlap, so this is a union, not five disjoint boxes.
- **Refuter:** show that one measurement ranks an actor across all five functions and does not change when the function changes; or show a direct-partners-only enumeration that captures a third-order effect.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable by a researcher against their own function

- **Statement:** importance is a relation to a function-residual, not a property an actor carries; species and interactions enter the same ledger.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :47 *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** for the function you know best, can you name an actor that helps it and harms another, or a provider that switches with conditions? Oliver et al. 2015 report context-specific contributions; Jordán 2009 reports that a key species may have few direct partners and that importance rank changes with the number of steps considered.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an actor whose measured contribution is invariant across all five functions, all sites and all times; or show a centrality ranking that predicts the removal residual without a per-function measurement.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** the anchor is a per-function measurement, cross-function ranking is a comparison and not a sum, and the only structural form available to "keystone" is a functional group whose qualifier is supplied by the function.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Operators table :48 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* for ⋛.
- **Check:** are the five function measurements in different units? If so, an overall importance score is an addition the framework does not license.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a declared common unit in which the five functions are additive and the addition predicts a removal residual; or show that the defining property of a keystone species is surviving its own use (Fig 12 :119), which would break the functional-group reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of important species for any of the five functions. No row is filled.
- Not that biodiversity loss is harmless. The wider literature is the opposite; this packet refuses the compression, not the effect.
- Not that the five functions are independent. Their intersections are kept as compounds.
- Not that centrality, abundance or citation count is importance. Those are budget filters (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that managed and unmaintained habitats give transferable rankings (Fig 11 :61 and :63).
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that expert agreement settles any of it (Fig 2 :35 and :40).
- Not a claim that the framework returns the name of the actor. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 5. What would settle it

1. For each function, a removal or addition experiment with a control, read in that function's own units.
2. An enumeration carried out to where the cascade stops mattering, with the third-order refuter tested.
3. A declared common unit making the five functions additive, if one exists, tested against a removal residual.
4. A real exclusion of a species or habitat, which would reopen the management verdict.
5. Consensus changing a measured function, which would break the anchor claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :43 and :49-51. Four short passages.
2. For one function you know, name an actor that helps it and harms another, or a provider that switches with conditions. That is a direct test of C2.
3. Say whether the five functions share a unit. If not, confirm that an overall score is a comparison, not a sum.
4. Name one instrument that would read removal residuals for soil formation or water regulation at the scale of a species, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
