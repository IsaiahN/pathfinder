## 0. The question under audit

> Which species and interactions are most important for pollination, soil formation, nutrient cycling, water regulation, and carbon storage?

Stated as an effect: someone must decide where to spend attention and intervention. Option A produces one ranked list across five functions. Option B splits the residual by function, enumerates the habitat around each, and refuses to name a winner until a per-function measurement settles it. A averages away the slot that carries the signal and installs a standing roster of actors. B produces five checkable ledgers and an honest open slot.

The binding term is **"most important"**. Per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction", what an answer would look like must be written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run, so the preference can accrue a residual like anything else. Bound here: *"An actor or interaction is most important for function F at place P and time T if removing it leaves a positive residual in the measurement of F at P and T, with the habitat enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering."* That is an ordinary, refutable term. It is not a name of a species.

## 1. The residual, in effect terms

Let the slots be `s = (F, P, T)` where F is one of the five functions. For each slot:

`R_s = | Gamma(b, a)_s - o'_s |`

where `a` is the presence, removal or addition of a species or interaction, `o'_s` is the observed function measurement, and `Gamma` is what the current library predicts. Nothing here is computed numerically; the expression states what would have to be measured.

- **Arity:** five functions multiplied by sites and times, so at least five residuals, not one. The question bundles them into a single ranking.
- **Symmetry:** valence is not symmetric. An actor can help one function and harm another, so the ranking cannot be a property of the actor. This is a relation, not a kind.
- **Scale:** first-order direct contact, then the cascade to second and third order. A direct-contact-only census is exactly the failure the figures name.

Symbols table :38: *"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

**Anchor test applied to each.** D1 has a nameable anchor (the function measurement, an experiment or counter). D2 has an anchor (the world enumerated from contact) but it is not the ground; it updates, the ground does not. D3 borrows D1's anchor, so it is a legitimate domain only because D1 settles it. D4 has an anchor only if the reader ranges against the measurement rather than against the claim. Where no instrument exists for soil formation or water regulation at a site, D1's sub-slot is not a domain yet; it is a residual that cannot be read. That is recorded as unreached, not answered.

**Union, not partition, and why it matters.** A species can pollinate and store carbon; the intersection is a compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either function. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

**Order by dependency.** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Ranking species before per-function measurement exists is effort spent above the break; the reading below it would be a reading of nothing.

## 4. Findings

### F1:The five functions are a union, not one question; the unit of the answer is a slot (decisive)

Fig 9 :5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."**

Fig 9 :55 and :54:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A single importance ranking over pollination, soil formation, nutrient cycling, water regulation and carbon storage averages across the slots. By Fig 1 :22 that is precisely how a live signal disappears. The union has intersections (a bee pollinates and a forest stores carbon), so the five ledgers cannot be treated as disjoint; the intersections must be kept as their own compound.

**Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 9 (union/partition logic) and Fig 1 (per-slot metric) are low-overlap routes to the same domain boundary. **Refuter:** show that one measurement ranks an actor across all five functions without changing when the function changes.

### F2:Importance is a relation to a residual, not a property an actor carries (decisive)

Fig 11 :49, :50 and :51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

The phrase "keystone species" invites a standing list. The figure forbids exactly that. Pollinators are beneficial for pollination and can be pests in another stage or context; the world source Oliver et al. 2015 records the same context dependence. A species is not "important"; it is important *to a measured function*.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 9 :27-28 (the residual must be described before a frame is chosen). Low overlap: one is habitat law, the other is search discipline. **Refuter:** exhibit an actor whose contribution score is invariant across all five function-residuals and all sites.

### F3:The habitat is enumerated, not composed, and the cascade reaches past direct contact

Fig 11 :43, :44 and :48:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

For pollination, direct contact is visitor and flower. The cascade includes predators of pollinators, nesting habitat, and landscape structure. For soil formation and nutrient cycling, the cascade includes microbes and earthworms that the available recording schemes do not measure; Oliver et al. 2015 names that gap explicitly. A "most important species" derived from a direct-interaction subgraph will miss the third-order actor that carries the residual.

**Status:** `single-source` in the figures (Fig 11), corroborated by Jordán 2009 (indirect effects can be long and strong) and Oliver et al. 2015 (unmeasured soil groups). **Refuter:** show that a direct-partners-only enumeration captures a documented third-order effect on the function.

### F4:Interactions are relations, and must be answered in the same per-residual form as species

Fig 11 :47:

> **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**

Fig 11 :50 (above): *"mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

The question asks for "species and interactions" as if they were two kinds of answer. Under Fig 11 they are the same kind of object: entries in an enumerated habitat, each with a valence relative to the residual. Fig 13 :24 names network as a structure: *"that entities relate, and influence travels."* So "which interaction is most important" has the same answer form as "which species": the one whose removal leaves the largest positive residual in a specified function slot.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 :47/:50 and Fig 13 :24. **Refuter:** show that an interaction carries importance independent of any function measurement, for example a ranking that is stable across all five functions.

### F5:The ground is a removal or addition measurement, not agreement among experts

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :35 and Fig 1 :23:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

Paine's 1966 removal of *Pisaster* is the model anchor: keep one area predator-free, maintain an adjacent control, read the diversity difference. That is a measurement that did not move when anyone changed their mind. A survey of researchers, however knowledgeable, is a frame; if they share the same review literature they are one frame with many names.

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 2 and Fig 1. **Refuter:** show a case where consensus among researchers with a shared evidence pool changed the measured function.

### F6:Management is a maintained arrangement, not a solved ranking

Fig 11 :61 and :63:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Fig 11 :65:

> **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994."**

You can add a pollinator and you cannot subtract the habitat. So "most important for management" is not a one-time list; it is a maintained intervention whose residual must be reread. In an unmaintained habitat nothing selects and what survives is what the ground kept; in a maintained one the seat acts but still did not author the habitat. The two modes are a partition, and verdicts from one do not transfer to the other.

**Status:** `single-source` in the figures (Fig 11), corroborated by the seat map's habitat section, which shares the framework evidence pool. **Refuter:** demonstrate a real extraction of a species or a habitat from an existing ecosystem, which would make exclusion available.

### F7:Ranking before measurement is effort above a broken link

Fig 3 :19, :20 and :27 (quoted in §2). For soil formation and water regulation, the instrument is often missing at the scale that matters. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* An unmeasured function is not an unimportant function; it is a closed channel. Fig 4 :30 warns that a condensed answer navigates by dead reckoning unless the round trip is measured.

**Status:** `single-source` in the figures (Fig 3, with Fig 8 and Fig 4 corroborating on adjacent questions). **Refuter:** exhibit a working per-function instrument at the site and show it was read before the ranking was made.

### F8:What survives is a shape, and the structural names are narrow

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures README: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."*

Fig 12 offers three structural forms that could describe an important actor:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

A "keystone species" is closest to a **functional group** claim: a substructure whose presence fixes an outcome, with the qualifier supplied by the specific function. It is not a catalyst claim (surviving use is not disproportionate effect), and it is not an isomer claim unless two species are shown to share the same actor set and differ only in arrangement. The shape is: *an actor or relation whose removal opens a positive residual in a named function slot, enumerated out to where the cascade stops mattering.* The name of the actor is owed the ground.

**Status:** `single-source` for each structural definition (Fig 12), with the shape limit triangulated across Fig 9 :32 and the canonical README. **Refuter:** derive a universal keystone name from the six-term set and the bond rules alone, with no measurement and no imported literature.

### F9:Centrality and publication frequency are filters, not witnesses

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Network degree, betweenness and citation counts tell you where to look. Jordán 2009 explicitly warns that a key species may have few direct partners, and that importance rank changes with the number of steps considered. Oliver et al. 2015 show that the dominant provider can switch with environmental conditions. A ranked list built from those measures is a budget allocation; it is not a settlement of the residual.

**Status:** `single-source` in the figures (Fig 9 :60), corroborated by two low-overlap world sources. **Refuter:** show a centrality ranking that predicts the removal residual across all five functions without a per-function measurement.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One measurement ranks an actor as most important across all five named functions and does not change when the function changes (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19 and Fig 9 :55).
2. An actor's importance is shown to be a property it carries, invariant to the residual and to place and time (breaks F2, Fig 11 :49-51).
3. A direct-partners-only enumeration captures a documented second- or third-order effect on one of the five functions (breaks F3, Fig 11 :52-53).
4. Agreement among experts sharing an evidence pool changes a measured function (breaks F5, Fig 2 :35, :40).
5. A real intervention removes a species or a habitat rather than substituting one (breaks F6, Fig 11 :61 and :66).
6. A universal keystone name is derived from the six-term set with no ground reading and no import (breaks F8, Fig 9 :32).

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Replace the single ranking with five function-indexed ledgers. Keep the intersections as a sixth compound where a species serves several functions (F1).
2. State explicitly that importance is a relation to a measured residual, and refuse the standing list (F2).
3. Enumerate each habitat outward from contact until the cascade stops mattering; include the unmeasured groups as marked residuals, not as zeros (F3, F7).
4. Treat interactions and species identically in the ledger: both are entries with a valence relative to the residual (F4).
5. Use removal or addition measurements as the anchor. Literature-derived rankings go in a separate column labelled as search filters (F5, F9).
6. Keep managed and unmaintained verdicts separate; a managed ranking is a maintained intervention, not a solved state (F6).
7. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, as done in §0, so it can be refused by the same test as everything else.

### Round trip note

The condensation from the figures to the five-ledger shape is lossy. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* What survives the trip is the per-slot unit, the union split, the habitat enumeration and the relation-of-valence rule. What is dropped is the causal detail of each real habitat, the actual removal residuals and the identity of every third-order actor. `11_audit.md` records the gap `R_T` as an enumerated list, not a number, because no ground reading exists.

## 8. Strains remaining

- **Unmeasured functions.** Soil formation and water regulation often lack a per-site instrument at the scale of a species removal. This is a channel problem, not a figure conflict. Marked `unreached`, not `gap`, because the figure lines do govern the shape; the ground is simply not read.
- **Cross-function comparison.** The five function measurements are in different units. The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, which permits a comparison but not an addition. So a cross-function "most important overall" ranking is a comparison at a declared threshold, never a sum. This is a standing constraint, not a strain.
- **Shared evidence pool.** All world sources here are ecology and ecosystem-services literature, and this audit is a simulated seat. Their agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40). `11_audit.md` states this explicitly.

## 9. Verdict of the audit

The shape survives; the requested list does not. **Mode `general` for the shape, `specified` for the six refuters, nothing `grounded`.** The figures decide that the answer must be five function-indexed ledgers over enumerated habitats, with species and interactions treated as the same kind of entry, with valence relative to the residual, and with the ground a per-function removal or addition measurement. They forbid a single cross-function importance list, a standing keystone roster, and any verdict sourced from agreement alone. No figure line was found to strain after the re-census in §7; the two remaining limits are an unread channel and a units mismatch, both recorded rather than hidden.
