# Union structure and the order of enumeration, measurement and intervention

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles five ecosystem functions into one importance ranking. Two supporting derivations are owed: (1) what structure the bundle has, union or partition, and what that permits us to conclude; (2) in what order enumeration, measurement, comparison and intervention must run. A third, smaller derivation asks which Fig 12 structural form, if any, a "keystone species" actually is.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the five function measurements (distinct units), the habitat entries (species, conditions, relations) and the removal or addition operation. Bonds held: Fig 12's seven, of which ⋛ comparison and → sequence bear here; negation ¬ for refusal. No new atom is available without an import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1:Is the five-function bundle a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Hector and Bagchi 2007 report that the sets of species influencing different functions overlap only by about 0.2 to 0.5. Gamfeldt et al. 2008 report that multifunctional redundancy is lower than single-function redundancy.

**Result:** union. A species can serve pollination and carbon storage; the intersection is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :57-58: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the five function ledgers share no actor across any site, the union reading is unnecessary and a partition would do. A single counterexample, one species measurably changing two functions, settles the union.

### Q2:Are the five functions commensurable?

- The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- Pollen deposition, soil formation rate, nutrient flux, water regulation and carbon stock are in different units. There is no figure-sanctioned addition across them.
- Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."* The structural point transfers: two readings in different units are held apart, not summed.

**Result:** no single "importance overall" score. A cross-function statement is a ⋛ comparison at a declared threshold, with the units stated. A score that adds the five is a notation error, not a synthesis.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a declared common unit in which the five functions are additive and show that the addition predicts a removal residual. Until then the sum is refused.

### Q3:What is the order of the work?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The chain for this question is:

1. **Perception.** Can we measure pollen deposition, soil formation, nutrient flux, water regulation and carbon stock at the site? Fig 3 :5: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before."*
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for what the habitat affords: what can be removed, introduced, isolated? Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*.
3. **Objective.** What counts as progress for each function, in its own units? Fig 3 :10: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*.
4. **Planning.** Only now choose an intervention under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the measurement and carry it to the next site.

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

**Result:** enumeration precedes measurement (you cannot measure what you have not listed), measurement precedes comparison, comparison precedes intervention. Effort spent on intervention design while the soil-formation instrument is missing is effort above the break.

**Falsifier:** show an intervention that improved a function at a site where no measurement of that function existed, with the improvement independently verified. If it cannot be verified, the claim is not a counterexample.

### Q4:Which Fig 12 form is a keystone species?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A keystone species is not defined by surviving its own use, so this mapping fails.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Some species do close nutrient cycles, but that describes a class of process, not disproportionate importance.
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** This fits: the role class is fixed by presence, and the qualifier (which function, which site) selects the member.
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two species are not isomers merely because both are "important"; isomerism needs the same atom set under different bonds. The claim is available but unmeasured.

**Result:** "keystone" is a **functional-group** claim in this framework, not a catalyst claim and not automatically an isomer claim. Fig 11 :51 still applies: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A role class is permitted; a permanent roster is not.

**Falsifier:** show that the defining property of a keystone species is that it survives its own use, or derive a universal keystone name from the six-term set alone with no measurement. Either would break the functional-group reading.

### Q5:Enumeration versus composition in the habitat

- Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
- Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
- Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The enumeration stops when the cascade stops mattering, not when a convenient taxonomic list ends. For the five functions this means the blank cells (soil microbes, earthworms, predators of pollinators) are recorded as *unread residuals*, not as zeros. Oliver et al. 2015 makes the same point from the measurement side: those groups matter and lacked sufficient data for analysis.

**Falsifier:** show that a habitat scoped to direct partners captures a documented third-order effect on one of the five functions.

## CONCLUSION

The bundle is a union of five overlapping conditions over incommensurable units. The work order is enumerate, measure, compare, intervene, and a missing instrument stops the chain before planning. A keystone species is a functional-group claim whose qualifier is the specific function, site and measurement, never a standing roster and never a sum across functions. These are structural results from the figures, not names of species.
