## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can settle it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- A shape: *importance is a per-function residual over an enumerated habitat, with species and interactions as the same kind of entry.*
- A name: *the most important pollinator of oilseed rape in Great Britain is species X*, which needs a measurement.

The shape is checkable without a field season. The name is not.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, then follow recurring terms that overlap with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the near vocabulary is "importance", "keystone", "redundancy", "service provider"; the narrowing term supplied by the figures is **residual per slot**.
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* "Most important" is described as "whose removal changes the measurement", not as "who holds a role".
- *Library of Babel* on malformed questions: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually"*. This is the essay-level statement of Fig 9 :54-55, and it applies directly to the five bundled functions.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place."* For this question the finish line is the return of the per-function measurement; before that, the answer is a route, not a result.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For ECO-0041 the ground is a removal or addition experiment with a control, as in Paine's *Pisaster* work.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23), with a source gate and a shadow test. A name imported from a meta-analysis is permitted only if it is attributed as an import and carries a source.

**Correction to my own reading, §5b style.** I first wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any species as important. That overstates it. The canonical phrase governs a *missing* thing, one not held in the library. A species name is not a missing primitive; it is a binding onto an actor already in the habitat. Naming one is not forbidden. What is forbidden is presenting a name as settled by the shape alone, or as a universal roster independent of the residual. The correct form is: name it as a binding, with its function, site, measurement and date, or withhold it.

## 4. Why the source matters more than the reading

*The Hard Problem is About Source* is listed in the exemplar with the passage: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* I did **not** reopen that essay this session, so I cite it here only as recorded in the exemplar, not as a fresh reading. The structural point still governs: a single "importance score" cannot tell you which path produced it (network centrality, abundance, removal effect, citation count), so the score cannot be the verdict. Fig 8 :50-52 makes the same claim at figure level: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

## 5. The shape for ECO-0041, stated plainly

> For each of the five functions, enumerate every actor, condition and relation in contact with that function, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Then the important entries are those whose removal or addition changes the measured function. Species and interactions are the same kind of entry. Valence is assigned per function, per site and per time, never as a standing property. Cross-function ranking is a threshold comparison at declared units, never a sum. The result is five ledgers plus their intersections, with a refuter and a provenance column on every row.

This is a `general` structural claim. It says nothing about which species occupy the rows. That is the honest stop.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A list of celebrated keystone-species authors whose agreement is collected as support is the named collapse. The legitimate ask gives the shape a budget to be tested and lets the measurement settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of which species matter and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. One more consequence: capability lives in the habitat

Fig 11 :39-40: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Transposed to this question: a species is not important in isolation. Its contribution is a property of species-and-habitat. Adding a species to a site is an intervention only if it changes contact with the function; otherwise it is a bookkeeping change. This is why the ledger must record the habitat, not just the species name.
