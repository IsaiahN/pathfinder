# The Tether's answer: are physical laws fundamentally algorithmic, and what limits algorithmic description?

**Mode: `specified` on the limits and the shape, `general` on the reframing, `gap` on the ground-level ontological verdict. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We hold a compositional description frame, its `closure(Γ)`, and the ground. `R` is the gap between prediction and observation, per slot, never averaged. The question bundles two conditions: is the ground wholly inside some closure, and what bounds any such closure. This is a union compound (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*), so the verdicts are kept separate.

## 2. The answer, per domain

**D2, the ontological half: `gap`.** Fig 9 :44 (verbatim): **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** "Physical laws are algorithms" would require certifying completeness from inside; its negation would require proving absence. Neither is available. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. So the honest verdict is a recorded gap, not a forced yes or no.

**D1, what an algorithmic closure can do: `specified`, triangulated.** An algorithm is a closure, and composition inside a closure cannot escape it. Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** New atoms come only by import. Meanwhile the arrangements do not run out: Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** Fig 13 :118: **"still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."** So an algorithm can run forever from a finite base and never arrive, and there is no last room (Fig 7 :6: **"the limit: no last room"**).

**D3, what limits algorithmic description: `specified`, four barriers held apart.**

1. **Self-limit.** Fig 8 :37: **"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."** An algorithm cannot prove its own completeness.
2. **Closure limit.** Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Novelty requires a frame with a different closure.
3. **Combinatorial limit.** Fig 13 :116-118: **"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held. Meanwhile the arrangements over those six do not run out. So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."**
4. **Settlement and cost limit.** Fig 5 :43: **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."** Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Bits and actions are separate prices, and an algorithm's own output is not a measure of success (Fig 5 :48: **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."**).

**The reframing (the central result).** "Fundamentally algorithmic" asks for a primitive status for discreteness. The figures refuse that status. Fig 13 :43: **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this"**. Fig 13 :124-125: **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."** Algorithmic character, if it appears, is a level-relative remainder of composition, not a member of the substrate. Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**. So the question is not answered yes or no. It is re-asked with a level and an instrument.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a yes, and not a no, on the ground-level question. That verdict is `gap` until an instrument settles it.
- Not "the universe is a quantum computer." That is a world-source proposal, recorded as corroborating candidate in `09_watson_answer.md`, not a figure derivation.
- Not a theory of everything. No last room; the chain approaches the truth only as the world runs out of ways to refuse it (Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**).
- Not a single limit score. The four barriers are a union over different objects, never averaged.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, opened 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does a measurement at the relevant scale discriminate a discrete, algorithmic source from a continuum-limit source, where both yield the same predictions? An instrument that does would name the level at which algorithmic character is the settled remainder.
- Does a formal description of law certify its own completeness? Fig 8 :37 says it cannot; an actual demonstration against that line would be a ground reading.
- Does an import occur, that is, does a prediction require an atom absent from the current closure? Fig 6 :23 makes that the only way the wall moves.
- Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: a composition adding an atom, a self-certifying system, a last room, a seventh primitive, a single additive price for bits and actions, or `R_T = 0`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** We can say what any answer must look like: finite closed kinds, unbounded arrangements, an import-only wall, a self-limit no internal proof crosses, a settlement only the ground can give, and a derivative rather than primitive status for algorithmic character. We cannot say which, if any, physical law instantiates that shape at the ground. The six-term set is a human guess at compression axes, not nature's own list. The stop is recorded so it is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to "are physical laws algorithms." It says the question bundles two things, coverage and limits, and both total answers are barred from inside a frame: a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence. What it can give is a shape. Any algorithmic description is a closure with a finite set of kinds and unbounded arrangements, so it can run forever without arriving and there is no last room. Four limits bind it: a frame cannot certify its own completeness (Gödel, Tarski, Chaitin); composition cannot add an atom, so only an import from a different frame moves the wall; a finite set of kinds exhausts in no arrangement; and description cost and settlement cost are two currencies that do not add. Crucially, discreteness is not a primitive in the substrate: the continuum was removed for being a derivative, and discreteness is a derivative too. So being algorithmic is a level-relative remainder, and the question becomes "at which level, settled by which instrument?" The framework returns a shape, not a name, and records the stop until the ground settles it.
