## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The limit is a theorem family, not an opinion.** The uncomputability of Kolmogorov complexity (SEP, Scholarpedia) and Chaitin's incompleteness result (Wikipedia) are the world-side counterparts of Fig 8 :37 (*"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."*). This is low-overlap corroboration from mathematics and computer science. It does not prove the figure claim; it agrees with it from a different closure.
2. **The algorithmic hypothesis is not settled.** Lloyd (2000, 2013) offers a quantum-computational model and explicit physical bounds, while the Digital physics survey records that no version has yielded a confirmed novel prediction and that discrete models strain against continuous symmetries and Bell tests. These two agree on the research programme and conflict on its status. Per the method, a conflict sends us back to the census: it cannot flip the figure verdict, and it reinforces recording D2 as `gap`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 7 :35** - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the three framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three framework links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: Fig 2 :40 says *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
