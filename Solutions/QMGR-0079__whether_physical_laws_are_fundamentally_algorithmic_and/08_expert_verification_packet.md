## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are physical laws fundamentally algorithmic?" bundles two conditions, coverage and limits, and both total answers are barred from inside a frame: a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence. The figure-derived shape is this. Any algorithmic description is a closure with a finite set of primitive kinds and an unbounded space of arrangements, so it can run forever without arriving and there is no last room. Four independent limits bind it: a frame cannot certify its own completeness; composition cannot add an atom so only an import from a different closure moves the wall; a finite set of kinds exhausts in no arrangement; and description cost and settlement cost are two currencies that do not add. Crucially, discreteness is not a primitive of the six-term substrate: the continuum was removed for being a derivative, and discreteness is a derivative too. So being algorithmic is a level-relative remainder, and the question becomes "at which level, settled by which instrument?" The framework returns a shape, not a name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13 and 8

- **Statement:** discreteness and algorithmic character are derivatives of composition, not primitive members of the substrate. A final algorithmic theory is forbidden (no last room).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :43 - **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this"**. Fig 13 :124-125 - **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."** Fig 7 :6 - **"the limit: no last room"**.
- **Refuter:** add algorithmic-ness as a seventh member and show it is indivisible from the other six and load-bearing in an established law; or exhibit a last room; or show a finite set of kinds whose arrangements exhaust.

### C2 - cross-field; checkable by a physicist or computer scientist against their own field

- **Statement:** an algorithmic description cannot certify its own completeness, and cannot manufacture new atoms by search. These are two distinct barriers, not one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :37 - **"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."** Fig 8 :32 - **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Fig 6 :23 - **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
- **What to check:** is the self-limit barrier being stated within its proper scope (consistent formal systems strong enough for arithmetic), and is the closure claim being used as a limit on composition rather than as a verdict on the ground?
- **Refuter:** produce a formal system that certifies its own consistency under the stated conditions; or exhibit a composition inside a closure that adds an atom, or an import with no second frame.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a world-facing consequence

- **Statement:** description cost and settlement cost are separate currencies, and an algorithmic description's own output is not evidence of ground contact. Therefore "elegant but untestable" is a named failure, and a single algorithmic-quality score is the error.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 - **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126 - **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** Fig 5 :48 - **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."**
- **Refuter:** give a single unit in which the two prices add without residue, or show a case where an algorithm's own output is a sound measure of its ground contact.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a yes and not a no on the ground-level question. That verdict is `gap` until an instrument settles it (Fig 13 :134 - **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**; Fig 8 :39 - **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**).
- Not a theory of everything, and not a final room.
- Not "the universe is a cellular automaton / a quantum computer / a specific program." Those are bindings from the physics frame and from world literature, provenance as such, never figure derivations.
- Not a single limit score. The four barriers are a union over different objects, never averaged.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.
- Not a claim that the six-term set is nature's own list. It is a visible guess; another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six.
