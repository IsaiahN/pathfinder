**Subject:** Check one figure-derived shape before you decide whether the question is well posed

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the limits, `gap` on the ontological verdict, not `grounded`):** the Tether's six-term substrate has three states (space, time, energy) and three structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with the rule that composition runs across the two kinds and never within one. Figure 13 says the continuum was removed as a seventh member because a generating set must not contain what its own members produce, and that discreteness is likewise "what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." So "fundamentally algorithmic" looks like a category error: algorithmic character, if present, is a level-relative remainder of composition, not an ontological floor.
>
> Four limits on any algorithmic description follow, and they are kept separate:
> 1. a frame cannot certify its own completeness (Gödel, Tarski, Chaitin);
> 2. composition inside a closure never adds an atom, so only contact with a frame whose closure differs moves the wall;
> 3. the kinds are finite and the arrangements do not run out, so a search can run forever without arriving and there is no last room;
> 4. description cost and settlement cost are two currencies that do not add, and a system's own output is not a measure of success.
>
> On the question "are physical laws algorithms," the framework returns `gap`, not yes or no. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence, and "it is not in here" cannot be proved from inside. That is a verdict of unreached, not a silence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the universe is a cellular automaton or a quantum computer, that a specific program is the law, that there is a single limit score, or that this is grounded. Those are bindings from the physics frame or from world literature. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. exhibit an algorithm that proves its own completeness under the stated conditions;
> 2. exhibit a composition inside a closed set that adds a new atom, or an import with no second frame;
> 3. exhibit a last room, a finite set of kinds whose arrangements exhaust, or discreteness established as an indivisible load-bearing primitive;
> 4. give a single unit in which description bits and settlement actions add without loss, or show a round trip from the figures to "law is algorithmic" and back that loses nothing.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the formula on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
