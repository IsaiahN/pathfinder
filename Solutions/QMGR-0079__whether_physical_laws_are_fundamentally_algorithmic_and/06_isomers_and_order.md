# Discreteness, the continuum, and the order of the limits

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether physical laws are *fundamentally* algorithmic. The word "fundamentally" asserts a primitive status for discrete, algorithmic structure. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) is discreteness a member of the substrate or a remainder of composition, and (2) in what order do the four limits on algorithmic description bind, and can that order be averaged into one score?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six of Fig 13, space, time, energy (states) and spectrum, gradient, network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No eighth member is available without import. The continuum is explicitly not held (Fig 13 :43). Currency: description bits and settlement actions, held apart. Anchor: the figures as metalaws.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is discreteness a primitive?

- Fig 13 :43 (verbatim): **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this"**. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce; continuum is a spectrum without gaps, and spectrum is already held.
- Fig 13 :124-125: **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**. So discreteness can serve as an atom *at a level* after a composition settles, but that is a role, not a primitive status.
- ⇒ Discreteness (and therefore "digital" and "algorithmic" as its cousins) is a **level-relative remainder**, not a floor. Calling it fundamental repeats the error the continuum removal forbids, with the sign reversed.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** add "algorithmic-ness" as a seventh member and show it is indivisible from the other six and load-bearing in an established law. If that succeeds, Q1 fails and F5 of `01` is withdrawn.

### Q2 - Are the two currencies additive?

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Fig 12 :127: *"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."*
- ⇒ A generator can be cheap in bits and unaffordable in actions. "Elegant algorithm, untestable consequences" is a real and named failure, not a paradox. Any verdict that scores "algorithmic quality" in one number commits Fig 12's named error.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a single unit in which the two prices add without residue, or show a case where lowering description length necessarily lowers settlement cost.

### Q3 - What is the order of the limits?

The four limits bind in dependency order (Fig 3), not by importance:

1. **Self-limit (F3 of `01`).** Fig 8 :37: **"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."** This is first because the other three are only meaningful if you cannot certify completeness from inside.
2. **Closure limit (F2).** Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Given a fixed wall, only import moves it.
3. **Combinatorial limit (F4).** Fig 13 :118: **"still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."** Given the wall, unbounded arrangements keep the search going without end. This is why SEARCHED comes before INWARD and OUTWARD in formula step 7.
4. **Settlement limit (F6).** Fig 5 :48: **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."** A generator is only a candidate until the ground pays.

Order matters because optimizing a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19). Spending budget on step 3 (more arrangements) cannot repair step 1 (no self-certification). A campaign that offers more computational power as an answer to "is it algorithmic" is effort above the break.

### Q4 - Partition or union of the limits?

These four are not four versions of one limit. They are a **union** of conditions over different objects (self-reference, composition, combinatorics, settlement). Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So the correct output is four separate verdicts with four separate refuters, as `01` F9 records, not a single "the limit of algorithmic description."

### Q5 - Does level-relativity dissolve the question?

Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."* If being algorithmic is a property a composition leaves at a level, then "is physical law algorithmic" is not a single yes/no question. It is a request to name a level and an instrument. At some levels a discrete, algorithmic description may be the settled remainder; at others a continuum description may be. The framework does not choose between them. It says what each choice would have to look like and what would settle it. This is the honest shape, and it is why D2 of `01` is recorded as `gap` rather than forced to a verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Discreteness is a derivative, not a primitive (Q1, triangulated with `01` F5). The two currencies do not add (Q2, single-source for the figure definition, with world corroboration from Lloyd 2000 and the SEP entry on Information). The limits bind in a fixed dependency order and form a union, not a partition (Q3, Q4). Level-relativity means the original question must be re-asked with a level and an instrument before it can be settled (Q5).

This rescues what is defensible in the question without pretending it has a yes/no answer: "algorithmic" is a shape a composition leaves at a level, finitely grounded, unbounded in consequence, bounded by barriers no internal search crosses, and settled only by the ground. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no single score is needed.
