## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual

> Whether physical laws are fundamentally algorithmic and what limits algorithmic description.

**Residual in effect terms.** Let `Γ` be a description frame holding a finite vocabulary of atoms and a set of composition rules. Its `closure(Γ)` is everything expressible by combining those atoms at any depth. Let the ground be whatever a measurement returns. The residual `R` is the gap between `Γ`'s prediction and the observation, indexed per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*).

The question bundles two conditions:

1. **Coverage.** Is the ground wholly inside some `closure(Γ)`? If yes, physical law is algorithmic in the sense of being generatable from a finite stock by rules. If no, something must be imported, and the import is not a rearrangement of what is held.
2. **Boundary.** What bounds any such closure? Which barriers hold regardless of how long the search runs, and which are merely budget limits?

This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The two conditions need separate loops and separate verdicts. Coverage is a verdict question of the form Fig 9 :6 names: *the verdict question of whether the missing primitive is in here at all* Boundary is a characterization question about the wall. Dependency order (Fig 3): settle the coverage question's provability status before spending budget characterizing the wall, because the wall description assumes the answer to "is it in here."

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term.** A generator, not a list: finite atoms, unbounded arrangements, an import operator that alone moves the wall, a metaframe barrier no internal search crosses, and a settlement requirement only the ground can satisfy. Binding this shape now makes the sought-for preference refusable later (formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Both total verdicts on coverage are barred from inside (decisive for the verdict)

Fig 9 :6 names the verdict question. Fig 9 :20 (verbatim): **"cannot be proved from inside"**. Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."** And Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

"Physical laws are fundamentally algorithmic" is a claim that the ground lies inside a closure. Its negation is a claim that some atom is absent from every closure. Neither is certifiable from a frame: the first would require certifying completeness, the second would require proving absence, and Fig 9 rules both out. Fig 8 :40-41: **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."** A failure to find a non-algorithmic law at this budget is not evidence that none exists.

The honest shape is a question form plus a stop, not a yes or no. Verdict: `gap` at the ground; `specified` for the shape of what any answer must look like. **Refuter:** produce an instrument or proof that settles whether the ground lies in `closure(Γ)` from outside the frame, or show that Fig 9's "no frame is a sealed room" is false. Until then neither total verdict is licensed.

### F2 - Structural limit: an algorithm cannot manufacture new atoms

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Any algorithmic description is a closure. It can recombine its primitives without end, but the set of primitive kinds is fixed until an import from a frame with a different closure occurs. This is a limit on what algorithmic description can *contain*, independent of how much compute is spent. It is triangulated across Fig 8 (why nothing else can move the wall) and Fig 6 (what does move it): two figures with different objects, low overlap.

**Strain test.** Read as "physics cannot be algorithmic because closure cannot add atoms," this line strains: it would be governing the ontological verdict when it actually governs composition inside a frame. Re-census sends it to D1 as a limit statement, never to D2 as a coverage verdict. After re-census: no strain.

**Refuter:** exhibit a composition inside a closure that adds a new atom, or show an import that needs no second frame. Either breaks the limit claim.

### F3 - Metaframe limit: no algorithm can certify its own completeness

Fig 8 :34: **"A frame cannot reach its own metaframe."** Fig 8 :37: **"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."** Fig 8 :44: **"Godel 1931; Tarski 1936; Chaitin 1974."**

This is the sharpest figure-derived statement of "what limits algorithmic description." A formal system, and therefore any algorithm offered as a description of law, cannot prove from inside itself where its own reach ends. Chaitin's constant is the concrete form: there are true statements of complexity that the system cannot prove. This is not a budget limit and more search does not touch it. It is triangulated across three theorem lines (Gödel, Tarski, Chaitin) inside one figure, and corroborated from a low-overlap field by the world sources (Hutter 2007; Wikipedia, Kolmogorov complexity, 2026).

**Refuter:** show a formal system that proves its own consistency or its own complexity limit while remaining within the conditions Fig 8 states, or show that the three theorem lines govern a different quantity than algorithmic self-description.

### F4 - Combinatorial limit: finite kinds, unbounded arrangements, no last room

Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** Fig 13 :116-118: **"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held. Meanwhile the arrangements over those six do not run out. So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."** Fig 7 :6: **"the limit: no last room"**; Fig 7 :30-31: **"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"**.

An algorithmic description can be finite at the base and inexhaustible at the surface. That cuts both ways, and both are kept: it is why "a short algorithm generates a rich world" is coherent, and why "a final algorithm of everything" is forbidden. No last room means no terminal algorithmic theory. This is a limit on the *finality* of algorithmic description, not on its reach at any finite stage.

**Strain test.** Read as "algorithms are weak because the reach is finite," the line strains: the reach is finite in kinds but the arrangements do not run out, so the line actually says an algorithm can run forever. Re-census: it governs the space of arrangements over a closed set of kinds (D1/D3), not the power of any particular algorithm. Kept as a two-sided limit.

**Refuter:** exhibit a finite set of kinds whose arrangements exhaust, or a last room, or a finite base that yields a seventh kind by arrangement alone.

### F5 - Algorithmic character is a derivative, not a primitive (the reframing)

Fig 13 :43: **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this"**. Fig 13 :124-125: **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."** Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

The substrate removes the continuum as a primitive because a generating set must not contain what its own members produce. Continuity is a spectrum without gaps, and spectrum is already held. By the same rule, discreteness (the prerequisite for "digital" and "algorithmic") is not a primitive either. It is what a composition leaves behind. So the question "are physical laws *fundamentally* algorithmic?" commits a category error of the same shape as making continuity fundamental: it promotes a level-relative remainder to a member of the substrate.

This is the strongest positive result of the census. It does not answer yes or no. It refuses the frame of the question and replaces it with a shape: algorithmic character, if present, is a derivative of composition with a stated level, not an ontological floor. Triangulated across Fig 13 :43, :124-125 and Fig 12 :104 (level-relative irreducibility via Fig 4's lossy transform).

**Refuter:** add a seventh primitive that is algorithmic-ness itself and show it is indivisible from the other six and load-bearing in an established law, or show that discreteness is a member rather than a remainder.

### F6 - Settlement and cost limits: bits and actions do not add

Fig 5 :43: **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."** Fig 5 :45: **"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."** Fig 5 :48: **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."** Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

An algorithmic description pays in two incommensurable currencies. A compact generator can be cheap in bits and ruinous in actions to settle; a verbose one can be easy to check and expensive to state. Collapsing them into one score is the named error, not the fix. And a frame cannot score itself: producing an algorithm is not evidence that the algorithm matches the ground (Fig 1 :27: **"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**).

**Refuter:** give a single unit in which description length and settlement actions add without loss, or show a case where an algorithm's own output is a sound measure of its ground contact.

### F7 - The round trip is lossy and must be measured

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** Fig 13 :133: **"what it returns is a shape rather than a name"**.

"Physical law is algorithmic" is a condensation of the figure set into four words. Sent up and brought back down, it loses: the distinction between coverage and boundary, the self-limit barrier, the derivative status of discreteness, and the two currencies. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured `R_T` (computed in `11_audit.md`), not an identity. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**.

**Refuter:** show that the condensed claim preserves all four dropped constraints, i.e. that `R_T = 0`.

### F8 - The verification frame cannot turn agreement into a verdict

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

A list of readers selected for receptiveness is a list selected to agree, which manufactures the disqualifying correlation. An outside frame must not hold the interiors. This governs the outreach artifacts (`02`, `03`, `04`, `08`) as strictly as the derivation.

**Refuter:** show that agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool adds a ground reading, or that a verifier who can reconstruct the claim still verifies it.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion and before outreach)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An instrument or proof settles, from outside the frame, whether the ground lies inside `closure(Γ)`, and returns a total verdict.
2. A composition inside a closed set adds a new atom, or an import occurs with no second frame (breaks F2).
3. A formal system certifies its own consistency or its own complexity limit under Fig 8's conditions (breaks F3).
4. A finite set of kinds exhausts its arrangements, a last room is exhibited, or a seventh kind is produced by arrangement alone (breaks F4 and F5).
5. Discreteness or algorithmic-ness is exhibited as an indivisible, load-bearing primitive of the six-term substrate (breaks F5).
6. A single unit is given in which description bits and settlement actions add without loss (breaks F6).
7. The round trip from the figure set to "physical law is algorithmic" and back preserves all dropped constraints, giving `R_T = 0` (breaks F7).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.
