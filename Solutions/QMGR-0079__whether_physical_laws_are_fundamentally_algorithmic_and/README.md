# Tether outreach - figure-grounded package

**Mode:** `specified` on the structural claims about algorithmic description and its limits, `general` on the shape of the answer, and `gap` on the ground-level ontological question of whether physical law is in itself algorithmic. No measurement settles the ontological binding, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Boundary.** Nothing in this folder is in the Tether repository. No repo file was read for the purpose of editing and no repo file was changed. Sources used were the canonical figure text at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, the figures README at `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`, the seat map (read via the master plan's authority order), and the public web pages listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`, all opened 2026-11-23.

## Question

Whether physical laws are fundamentally algorithmic and what limits algorithmic description.

**Source:** `Questions/QM & GR Questions.txt` line 101. **Section:** Computation, Information, and Complexity.

Restated as an effect: we hold a compositional description frame (an algorithm in the broad sense of a finite vocabulary plus rules of combination) and a ground (what measurement returns). The residual `R` is the gap between what the closure of that frame predicts and what the ground returns. One half of the question asks whether the ground lies wholly inside some such closure. The other asks what bounds the closure. Option A asserts both that laws are algorithms and that the limits are knowable from inside the algorithm. Option B keeps the figure-derived shape, refuses the total verdicts, and records the limits as a triangulated set of barriers with an honest stop. A sends a `general`-mode verdict the figures forbid and spends the independence of the people checking it. B sends a checkable shape with refuters written in advance.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term for the loop's "Direction" step: a generator with a finite closed set of kinds, an unbounded space of arrangements, barriers that no internal search crosses, an import operator that alone moves the wall, and a settlement requirement that only the ground can satisfy.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not supply an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Adding readers raises the vocabulary and does not add a ground.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the exact correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in institutional channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
