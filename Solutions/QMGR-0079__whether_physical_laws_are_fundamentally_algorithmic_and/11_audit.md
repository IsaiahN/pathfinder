## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured, not assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 13 :43 and :124-125 (discreteness as remainder), Fig 8 :32 and :37 (closure and self-limit), Fig 12 :125-126 (two currencies), Fig 9 :44 (no proof of absence).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: "algorithmic character is a level-relative remainder; four barriers bind any algorithmic description; the coverage verdict is a gap."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the question's own phrasing, "physical laws are fundamentally algorithmic and what limits algorithmic description," and the world candidates "the universe is a quantum computer" / "cellular automaton."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **self-limit barrier**, which the concrete phrasing "what limits algorithmic description" flattens into a single engineering limit rather than a theorem about self-certification;
- the **union structure**, which the concrete phrasing bundles into one question and one answer;
- the **derivative status of discreteness**, which the concrete phrasing promotes to a primitive by the word "fundamentally";
- the **two-currency split**, which the concrete phrasing loses by asking for "limits" in one list.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the no-proof-of-absence result (so the `gap` verdict holds) and the observation that a finite base has unbounded arrangements. Those are the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "physical law is algorithmic" as an equivalent condensation of the figures would set `R_T` to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
