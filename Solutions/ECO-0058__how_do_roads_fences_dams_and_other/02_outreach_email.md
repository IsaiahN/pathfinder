# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before deciding whether the framework matters

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Replace `[your name]`; do not add a signature block that implies an endorsement.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim below survives your own check against `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. A two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output is wrong.

---

**Subject:** One claim about roads, fences and movement that you could refute in twenty minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm [your name]. I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question it comes from:** how roads, fences, dams and other infrastructure alter the movement of organisms and ecological processes. I am treating that as a bundle of overlapping conditions, not one thing, so I am not offering a single averaged effect.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the ecology):**
> 1. The habitat must be enumerated, not derived from the noun "infrastructure." List everything in contact with the movement you care about, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering.
> 2. An actor's effect is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same fence can block one species, shelter another and perch a third. So there is no verdict that averages across species.
> 3. A barrier is a directional filter, not a wall. A passage that works upstream may not work downstream. A proximity map is not a route, and a route is not a direction.
> 4. Isolation is substitution, not removal. A crossing, fishway or restored reach substitutes a habitat; you reproduce something of the old goal and inherit actors you did not choose. What you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts. This makes it permanent work rather than a solved state.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this email):**
> 1. One structure shown to have the same sign of effect on movement across taxa, life stages and directions, with no live slot residual.
> 2. A mitigation that restores full pre-barrier contact without substitution, verified by before/after measurement.
> 3. A single averaged connectivity metric that predicts per-species outcomes with no residual on withheld sites.
> 4. Direct mortality counts that fully predict a third-order process outcome, using an instrument that would have detected the cascade.
> 5. A barrier that is equally permeable in both directions for all taxa at all life stages.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. What I am asking for is the shape of a failure, not a verdict on the framework. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **A relevant caveat, stated up front:** the figure used here calls its contact field a "habitat," but the author's own architecture essay explicitly declines the mapping to the ecological niche. I am using the rule structurally, not claiming the ecology term is the same thing.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, public repo (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - The habitat figure's own declined mappings and citation list: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state whether your claim is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you,
> [your name]

## Stage boundaries

- **Stage 1:** send the claim and five refuters. Record the reader's verdict before any theory is shown.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, send the three links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode.
