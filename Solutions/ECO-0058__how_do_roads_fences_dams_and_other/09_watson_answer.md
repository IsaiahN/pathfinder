## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the figures say about the bundled claim that roads, fences, dams and other infrastructure alter the movement of organisms and ecological processes, and that connectivity should be restored. Option A: accept the bundle and report one averaged effect of infrastructure on movement, with a standard mitigation prescription. Option B: treat the question as a union of overlapping conditions, enumerate the habitat per residual, treat each barrier as a directional filter, keep verdicts per slot, and refuse to name a design until a ground settles it. What each leads to: A gives a number with no slot, no direction and no cascade, manufactures agreement from a shared evidence pool, and mistakes substitution for removal; B gives a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and an open site-specific result until the ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, and order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R (the residual).** The gap between an unchanged-habitat prediction and the observed redistribution after an installation. Arity: many slots, taxon x life stage x barrier x process x direction. Symmetry: asymmetric, installation is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62). Scale: first order at the barrier, second and third order in the cascade, and across levels from individual to population to community.
- **Domain 1, organism movement.** Actors: dispersers, migrants, foragers and their predators. Currency: successful crossings, survival, reproduction, gene flow. Anchor: telemetry, mark-recapture, passage counts, genetic structure.
- **Domain 2, ecological processes.** Actors: water, sediment, nutrients, fire, pollinators, predators. Currency: flux and regime. Anchor: gauges, sediment loads, nutrient measurements, predation rates.
- **Domain 3, infrastructure and mitigation.** Actors: roads, fences, dams, culverts, crossings, fishways, planners. Currency: design and intervention. Anchor: before/after monitoring, mortality counts, passage efficiency.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers in infrastructure and connectivity research. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so agreement among them is one witness.
- **Non-domain.** "Landscape connectivity" as an abstract index has no anchor until tied to a measurement. It is a budget filter (Fig 9 :60), not a domain.
- **Question type: union, not partition.** Fig 9 :55 says a bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions and a single lever leaves the intersections standing. Roads, fences and dams must be unbundled, and their compounds searched separately.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception (contact change registers) then vocabulary (what a barrier affords) then objective (connected for whom, measured by what) then planning (design) then monitor. The middle link lacks an instrument (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: Fig 4 :21 says filter, while the field literature says barrier. Re-census resolves this as primitive versus zero-permeability limit for a given residual, not a contradiction. A second, more serious limit is recorded: Fig 11's "habitat" is explicitly **not** the ecological niche; the architecture essay declines that mapping. Fig 11 governs structurally only.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the bundled averaged answer does not. For each residual, enumerate the habitat outward; keep valence relative to the residual; treat a barrier as a directional filter whose zero-permeability limit is the literature's "barrier"; expect first-order counts to underdetermine third-order outcomes; treat mitigation as addition producing a substituted habitat, hence permanent work; unbundle the union before searching; report per slot; and refuse to name a design until an import supplies a source gate and the ground settles it. Fig 11 governs structurally, not by identity with the ecological niche, and that declined mapping is recorded rather than hidden. This is a `general` shape with `specified` ecology-facing predictions, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, five refuters and the declined niche mapping before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a framework instead of a residual. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. No figure verdict depends on a specific design, and no primitive is withdrawn if a refuter fires. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet; the five refuters say exactly what to narrow.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that separates filter from sink from relocation; a before/after study showing full contact restored with no substitution; an averaged metric predicting per-species outcomes with no live slot on withheld data; a case where first-order counts fully predict the third-order outcome with an instrument that would have detected the cascade; a barrier equally permeable in all directions for all taxa. Any of these fires a refuter in `01` §3. A figure re-census that admitted the niche mapping as definitional would also change the status of Fig 11 from structural to literal, and is recorded as a correction, not a quiet edit.
