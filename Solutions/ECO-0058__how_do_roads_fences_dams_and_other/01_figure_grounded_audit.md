## 0. The claim under audit and the residual

### 0.0 The claim, as the question usually arrives

> Roads, fences, dams and other infrastructure alter the movement of organisms and ecological processes. They do so by blocking movement, killing animals, fragmenting habitat and reducing connectivity. We should therefore build crossings, fish passes and corridors to restore connectivity.

The claim arrives as a **bundle**. It joins a causal assertion (infrastructure alters movement), a mechanism assertion (blocking and mortality), a diagnosis (fragmentation) and a prescription (restore connectivity). Fig 9 :54 refuses bundles: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** The Library of Babel names the same failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

### 0.1 The residual in effect terms

State R without the vocabulary of the answer. Under a prediction of an unchanged habitat, an organism or process should arrive where it used to arrive. After a road, fence or dam is installed, three things can happen: something fails to arrive where it used to, something new arrives that did not used to, or something that used to arrive arrives changed. **R is the gap between the unchanged-habitat prediction and the observed redistribution, indexed per slot** (species, life stage, process, barrier type, crossing point), never as a single global connectivity score.

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

Consequences for what an answer would look like (Formula step "Direction"): bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term. The answer we are looking for has the form *a per-slot map of changes in contact, with the cascade enumerated outward and the substitutions named*, not a scalar "impact of infrastructure." Writing that down before the run makes it refusable.

### 0.2 Domain census, done before choosing any figure line

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet; it is a filter or a wish.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: many slots (taxon x life stage x barrier x process). Symmetry: strongly asymmetric, installation is available and removal is not (Fig 11 :61-62). Scale: first order at the barrier, second and third order in the cascade (Fig 11 :52-53), and across levels from individual movement to population to community (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, organism movement.** Actors: individual dispersers, migrants, foragers, and their predators. Currency: successful crossings, survival, reproduction, gene flow. Anchor: telemetry, mark-recapture, passage counts, genetic structure.
- **Domain 2, ecological processes.** Actors: water, sediment, nutrients, fire, pollinators, predators. Currency: flux and regime. Anchor: stream gauges, sediment loads, nutrient measurements, predation rates.
- **Domain 3, infrastructure and mitigation.** Actors: roads, fences, dams, culverts, crossings, fishways, planners. Currency: design and intervention. Anchor: before/after monitoring, mortality counts, passage efficiency.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers in infrastructure and connectivity research. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement.
- **A non-domain, named so it can be refused.** "Landscape connectivity" as an abstract index has no anchor until it is tied to an actual measurement of movement or flux. It is a **filter for the budget**, not a witness for the verdict: Fig 9 :60, **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

**Partition or union?** Union. Roads, fences and dams are overlapping conditions, not a list of one kind of thing. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."** A fence-only intervention clears the fence-only region; a road-plus-fence intersection still binds.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (a change in contact registers) -> vocabulary (terms for what a barrier affords: blocks, filters, kills, fragments, substitutes) -> objective (what counts as connected, for which residual) -> planning (crossings, fishways, removal) -> learn and carry (monitor and adapt). The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance, Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** Effort overwhelmingly lands at planning, while the vocabulary/objective link lacks an instrument. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The habitat is enumerated, never composed (decisive, `general`)

Fig 11 :43-48:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

There is no term "road-effect" from which the answer can be derived. The effect is read off the world: actors (what acts on the residual and what it acts on), conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain) and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) (Fig 11 :45-47). The "how" question is answerable only as a habitat census, never as an intrinsic property list of infrastructure.

- **Mode:** `general` for the rule; `specified` for which actors are in the list at a given site.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 11); the Seat Map (local doc, same author and evidence pool) repeats it. Same pool, so this is one witness, not two.
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated derivation of a barrier's effect from the barrier's type alone, with no enumeration of contact, would break this.

### F2 — Valence is relative to the residual; there is no averaged verdict (decisive, `general`)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A fence is not "a barrier" as a kind. To a dispersing ungulate it blocks; to a small mammal it may be a refuge from a predator; to a seed-dispersing bird it may be a perch. The road ecology source (Wikipedia, *Road ecology*, fetched 2026-11-23) states independently: **"Roads are a permeable barrier to some organisms and impermeable to others."** The wildlife crossing source adds that effectiveness is **"highly site-specific (due to differences in location, structure, species, habitat, etc.)"**.

- **Mode:** `general`. The figure governs; the world sources corroborate on a different evidence base.
- **Status:** **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus independent empirical ecology; moderate overlap because Fig 11 is written in ecological examples).
- **Verdict:** the question "how do roads/fences/dams alter movement" has **no single answer**. It has one answer per (residual, actor, direction) slot. Averaging is the error Fig 1 :22 names.
- **Refuter:** one structure shown to have the same sign of effect on movement across all taxa, life stages and directions, with no live slot residual.

### F3 — Contact cascades: first-order counts miss the residual that matters (`specified`)

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Roadkill at the pavement edge is first-order. Genetic isolation of subpopulations is second-order. A trophic shift three steps out is the DDT case. The independent literature supplies concrete cascades for linear infrastructure: the road ecology source reports that de-icing chemicals and nutrients **"can trigger trophic cascades in adjacent waterways"**; the habitat fragmentation source reports that for boreal woodland caribou, roads and pipelines **"have allowed their natural predators, the wolf, and the black bear to more efficiently travel over landscapes and between patches of land."** Neither example is in the figure text; the figure's DDT example is figure-internal and is not used as a world witness.

- **Mode:** `general` for the cascade rule, `specified` for which cascade runs at a site.
- **Status:** **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus two independent ecology pages).
- **Refuter:** a case where direct mortality/crossing counts fully predict the third-order process outcome with no residual, measured with an instrument that would have detected the cascade.

### F4 — The barrier is a directional filter, not a wall (`general`)

Fig 4 :21:

> **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**

Fig 13 :36:

> **"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."**

A road changes adjacency (space) and network (influence travels), but a proximity map is not a route and a route is not a direction of travel. The fish ladder source (Wikipedia, *Fish ladder*, fetched 2026-11-23) states: **"A fish passage that is designed to allow fish to pass upstream may not allow passage downstream, for instance."** A dam with a fishway is a **directional filter**, not a restored continuum.

- **Mode:** `general` for filter-not-wall; `specified` for the permeability curve of any given crossing.
- **Status:** **triangulated** (Fig 4, Fig 13 and the independent fish-passage source).
- **Refuter:** a barrier that is equally permeable in both directions for all taxa and all life stages, with measured passage rates.

### F5 — Isolation is substitution, not removal (`general`)

Fig 11 :66-69:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

A wildlife crossing, a fish pass or a restored reach is a substituted habitat. It is not the old habitat with a obstacle deleted. The NOAA Cape Fear source (fetched 2026-11-23) describes the new passage structure as one that **"mimics natural rapids"**, that is, a designed substitute. The fish ladder source reports a mixed record: **"only three percent of American Shad make it through all the fish ladders on the way to their spawning ground."** The wildlife crossing source reports that at Banff, the same crossings that cut ungulate mortality by more than 80 percent coincided with bear mortality rising **"by an average of 116 percent in direct parallel to an equal doubling of traffic volumes."** Two silent failure modes are exactly the ones Fig 11 names: what was not reproduced and what was unintentionally introduced.

- **Mode:** `general` for the substitution law; `specified` for any particular structure's success.
- **Status:** **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus independent agency and encyclopedic sources).
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated restoration of full pre-barrier contact with no substitution and no inherited new actors, verified by before/after measurement.

### F6 — Introduction is available, exclusion is not; connectivity is permanent work (`general`)

Fig 11 :61-63:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Fig 11 :32: **"→ introduce, never subtract"**. Mitigation is addition. The IENE Biodiversity and Infrastructure Handbook (fetched 2026-11-23) dedicates separate sections to **"Evaluation & monitoring"** and **"Maintenance"**, which is the practitioner's version of the same asymmetry: a crossing is commissioned, then it must be kept working. The fish ladder source notes that in one case **"The very narrow slots and the accumulated debris is likely hindering the effectiveness of this particular passageway"**, that is, an introduced structure whose contact degraded.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** **triangulated** (Fig 11 and the independent practitioner handbook).
- **Refuter:** a mitigation that subtracts contact cleanly and stays effective without maintenance, measured over more than one season.

### F7 — The residual is per slot; an averaged connectivity score is the named error (`general`)

Fig 1 :19, :22, :28 (quoted in §0.1). The Banff result is the empirical shape: a single averaged "crossing success" number would hide a live ungulate slot and a live bear slot with opposite signs. This is why the ICOET 2027 call for abstracts (fetched 2026-11-23), which gathers **"researchers, biologists, engineers, planners, project managers, administrators, and policy makers"**, is useful as a venue but not as a verdict: a room of agreeing specialists is still a room (Seat Map :283).

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** **triangulated** (Fig 1 plus the independent wildlife crossing case).
- **Refuter:** a single averaged metric shown to predict per-species outcomes with zero live slot residual on withheld data.

### F8 — The question is a union; unbundle before searching (`general`)

Fig 9 :54-55 (quoted in §0.0). The road ecology source lists distinct linear infrastructure modes: **"roads, railroads, power lines, and canals"**, and the handbook's filter menu lists **"Airports, Pipelines, Ports, Powerlines, Railways, Roads, Waterways"** as separate transport modes. The union is real and the intersections are real: a road plus a fence plus a dam is a compound, not a harder road.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure, corroborated by independent practitioner taxonomy (same conclusion, different pool).
- **Refuter:** a single lever shown to clear all regions of the union, including the intersections, on measured outcomes.

### F9 — The middle link has no instrument (`specified`)

Fig 3 :19, :20, :27:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :25-26 names why: *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither."* In this field the link is "what counts as connected, for which species, at which life stage." The agency source shows the right instinct: NOAA says **"we will also monitor fish passage, conduct surveys, and analyze striped bass populations to determine if fish stocking is warranted"**, but the definition of the objective is still site-specific and often implicit.

- **Mode:** `specified`, because whether the middle link is broken at a given project is measurable.
- **Status:** single-source figure for the dependency claim; independent sources corroborate that monitoring exists, not that the link is unmeasured. Kept separate.
- **Refuter:** a project where the objective ("connected for whom") is defined with an instrument before planning, and planning is shown to have followed it.

### F10 — The framework returns a shape, not a name (`general`)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Canonical figures README (local): *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. So the derivation can say *a directional filter, a substituted habitat, a per-slot residual and an enumerated cascade*; it cannot say *build this particular culvert design*. Naming a design is a binding from the engineering frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation.

- **Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus canonical README; low overlap in wording, shared authorship noted).
- **Refuter:** a figure derivation that selects a specific engineering design without an import from engineering.

### F11 — The ground, not agreement, decides (`general`)

Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before any outreach and before the verdict is finalized. It is a design instrument (Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"*). Honest note: this folder was assembled after reading the figure text and the world sources; the refuter is not claimed to come from a temporally prior experiment.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A barrier's effect on movement is shown to be an intrinsic property of the structure, invariant across taxa, life stages and directions (breaks F2, Fig 11 :49-51).
2. A mitigation is demonstrated that restores full pre-barrier contact without substitution, adding nothing and inheriting no new actors, by before/after measurement (breaks F5, Fig 11 :66-67).
3. A single averaged connectivity metric predicts per-species outcomes with no live slot residual on withheld data (breaks F7, Fig 1 :19/28).
4. Direct mortality and crossing counts fully predict a third-order process outcome with no residual, using an instrument that would have detected the cascade (breaks F3, Fig 11 :52-53).
5. A barrier is found to be equally permeable in both directions for all taxa at all life stages (breaks F4, Fig 4 :21).

If any fires, withdraw or narrow the corresponding finding and record it here. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
