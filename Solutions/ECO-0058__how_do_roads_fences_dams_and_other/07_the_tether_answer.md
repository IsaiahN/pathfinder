# The Tether's answer: roads, fences, dams and ecological movement

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the ecology-facing predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a field result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An unchanged-habitat prediction says an organism or process should arrive where it used to. After a road, fence or dam is installed, something fails to arrive, something new arrives, or something arrives changed. R is that gap, indexed per slot (species, life stage, barrier type, process, direction), never as a single connectivity average (Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**).

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :55). Roads, fences and dams must be unbundled before they are searched, and the intersections searched as their own compounds.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **Enumerate the habitat; do not derive the effect from the noun "infrastructure."** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** List everything in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44). Actors, conditions and relations are read off the world, not invented. What cannot yet be perceived is the residual.

2. **There is no averaged verdict.** An actor's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). The same fence blocks one species, shelters another, perches a seed disperser. Verdicts are kept per domain and per slot, never pooled. A global "connectivity score" is the error Fig 1 :22 names.

3. **The structure is a directional filter, not a wall.** Fig 4 :21: **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."** A "barrier" is the limit case of near-zero permeability for one residual in one direction. Fig 13 :36: **"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."** A proximity map is not a route and a route is not a direction of travel.

4. **First-order scoping misses the residual that matters.** Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Roadkill counts are first order; genetic isolation and trophic shifts are second and third. The independent literature supplies caribou/predator and salt/trophic-cascade examples (see `09`).

5. **Mitigation is addition; isolation is substitution.** Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** You must reproduce everything the goal required and you inherit actors you did not choose (Fig 11 :67-69). Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Therefore a managed ecosystem is permanent work, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63). The phrase *restore connectivity* is not a physical subtraction.

6. **Order: define the objective before the design.** The dependency chain runs perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> monitor (Fig 3). Most effort lands at planning while the objective link has no instrument (Fig 3 :27). A crossing must be settled at its own level before it becomes an atom for the population or community level (Fig 12 :104).

7. **The framework names a shape, not a design.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** It can say *directional filter, substituted habitat, enumerated cascade, per-slot residual*. It cannot say *this culvert design*. A specific design is an import from engineering with a source gate and shadow test, not a derivation.

8. **The ground, not agreement, decides.** Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Ten review papers citing the same twenty studies are one witness (Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single averaged effect of infrastructure on movement. The question is a union (F8).
- Not that Fig 11's "habitat" is the ecologist's habitat or niche. That mapping is **explicitly declined** in the canonical essay *Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture* (see `05` §5). Fig 11 governs structurally.
- Not that crossings, fishways or corridors fail. It says their effect is a substitution whose result depends on the residual and on the enumerated cascade; success or failure is a ground question.
- Not that any particular design is licensed by the figures.
- Not that removing a barrier restores the pre-barrier habitat. Substitution, not removal.
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement settles any slot here.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

The instrument, not another argument (*The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it). Concretely:

- **Per-slot before/after control:** passage, survival and gene flow for each taxon and each direction, not a pooled index.
- **Source discrimination:** an instrument that distinguishes a filter from a mortality sink from a relocated population when they produce the same crossing count.
- **Cascade reach:** measure the third-order process (predation regime, genetic structure, nutrient regime) and test whether first-order counts predict it with no residual.
- **Substitution audit:** list what the mitigation failed to reproduce and what it unintentionally introduced, and check both after one full season.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §3:** invariant barrier effect; restoration with no substitution; averaged metric with no live slot; no cascade where the instrument would have detected one; a nondirectional filter for all taxa.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books When They Should*); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Here we do not even use the six as an ecology vocabulary. We use the figures' rules: enumerate, keep valence relative to the residual, treat isolation as substitution, range against the ground. The derivation stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (*How Intelligence Actually Works*).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give one number for the effect of infrastructure on movement, because the question is a bundle of overlapping conditions rather than one thing. It says that for each barrier and each species you must enumerate the habitat outward, since an actor's effect is relative to the residual and the cascade that matters can be three steps from the road; that a barrier is a directional filter, not a wall; that a crossing or fishway substitutes a habitat rather than restoring the old one, so it is permanent work and you inherit actors you did not choose; and that the verdict is per slot, never an average across species. It refuses to name a design, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any checkable study must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which barrier alters which process at which site stays open until the ground settles it.
