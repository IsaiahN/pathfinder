# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues for infrastructure effects

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond/order rules, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a field reading.

## RESIDUAL

The question writes "roads, fences, dams and other infrastructure" as if the noun phrases shared one structure. They do not. Two questions are owed:

1. **Structure:** are these members of one functional group, or isomers of one another, and what is the bond that carries the difference?
2. **Order:** does mitigation subtract the barrier, or add a substituted habitat, and in what order must refusal and minting run?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is "linear infrastructure" a functional group or a set of isomers?

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

- A road, a fence and a dam share the substructure *a linear interruption in a field of contact*. That fixes the kind: a directional filter (Fig 4 :21). They are a **functional group**, with the qualifier naming the member.
- Two sites with the same ingredient list (e.g. a culvert, a fence line and a vegetated bank) can still be **isomers** if the bond differs: one arrangement permits downstream passage and blocks upstream; another does the reverse; a third passes only a narrow body size. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The wildlife crossing source's observation that effectiveness is **"highly site-specific"** is the empirical face of this.
- **Falsifier:** if two arrangements with identical atoms and different bonds produce identical passage rates for every taxon in both directions, the isomer claim fails and the flat ingredient list would be sufficient.

### Q2 — What carries the difference, the parts or the arrangement?

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**

A mitigation report that lists only its parts (one culvert, two guide fences, 90,000 plants on a bridge) has recorded half of itself. The other half is the typed relation between them: which side each fence guides toward, which direction the structure passes, which species encounters it first. Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms; swapping guide fence and opening is not a relabeling.

- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that swapping two components of a crossing leaves every measured outcome unchanged for every taxon. Then the bond was carrying nothing and the flat formula was adequate.

### Q3 — Is "restoration" a subtraction?

Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

Operators table :42: subtraction is **"A with B removed or absent"**.

The word "restore connectivity" is written as subtraction: take the barrier away and the old habitat returns. Fig 11 :66 refuses this: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** What actually happens is introduction (Fig 11 :32: **"→ introduce, never subtract"**): a crossing, a fishway, a environmental flow, a monitoring regime. Subtraction (dam removal) also leaves a substituted habitat, because sediment, inherited actors and downstream regime are not the pre-dam ones; the dam removal source reports that releasing impounded sediment can **"harm fish, scour the landscape, and bury infrastructure."**

- So: **the operation is addition; the result is substitution; the word "restoration" is a negation of a frame we hold, not a physical operation.** The correction is a refusal (¬), not a bond, and it is owed before the mint (Formula step 2).
- **Falsifier:** a measured case where removing a barrier returns every measured flux and community composition to its pre-barrier state with no introduced actor and no maintenance. Then substitution is refuted for that case.

### Q4 — What is the dependency order of a connectivity project?

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Fig 3 (local extract): perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The typical project order in the wild is reversed: design (planning) is funded first, and the objective link ("connected for whom, measured by what") is filled in afterwards, if at all. That is effort spent above the break (Fig 3 :20). The sequence that survives the figures is:

1. Register a change in contact (which slot has residual?).
2. Build the vocabulary of what the barrier affords (filter, mortality sink, fragmenter, substitute).
3. State the objective per slot and bind an instrument for it.
4. Then design the crossing.
5. Then monitor and carry the method, not the recording (Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**).

- **Falsifier:** a project that defines the objective with a working instrument after design and still passes a withheld-site test with no residual. Then the dependency order claimed here is wrong.

### Q5 — Level order: what becomes an atom at the next level?

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

A crossing whose passage rate has been settled against the ground becomes an atom for the next composition: the population's gene flow, the community's predation regime, the catchment's sediment budget. Before settlement it is only a candidate structure. This is why a crossing that "works" at the passage-count level can fail at the population level: it was composed over before it was settled. The Banff result (ungulate crossings reduce mortality, bear mortality rises) is the empirical shape of an unsettled atom being composed over.

- **Falsifier:** a structure shown to serve as a reliable operand at the next level before its own passage claim was settled by measurement. That would break Fig 12's necessity condition.

## CONCLUSION

Roads, fences and dams are a **functional group** of directional filters, and two sites sharing the same components can be **isomers** whose difference is carried entirely by the bond. Mitigation is **addition** producing **substitution**, not subtraction producing restoration; "restoration" is a refusal of a held frame, and refusals are owed before mints. A connectivity project is ordered perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> design -> monitor; the usual reversal is effort spent above the break. Each crossing must be settled at its own level before it becomes an atom for the next.

**Falsifier for the whole derivation:** a single site where one averaged connectivity score, an ingredient list, and a post-hoc objective predict every per-species, per-direction outcome with no residual and no cascade. That would collapse the union to a partition, the bond to a free listing, and the substitution to a removal, and this file would be withdrawn.
