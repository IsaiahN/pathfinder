# What crosses the membrane, in what order, and under which analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how per-agent learning changes collective long-term behavior. Three supporting questions are owed that the main audit does not settle:

1. What exactly is the object that crosses the membrane, and what is its type across the two scales?
2. In what order do the crossings and the contact changes occur?
3. Which of Fig 12's structural analogues (catalyst, cycle, functional group, isomer) applies to a learned method that is reused by many agents?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What crosses up: a method, not a term, and not a recording

- Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
- Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
- Fig 12 :107-109: *"Where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."*

Inside one agent, a learned term φ is settled against that agent's ground. To cross up it must be compressed into a method m that can be reapplied in a context the originating agent never saw. Compression is not summarising a transcript. It is throwing away the occasion while keeping what can be spent again. Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*

A recording crosses as a candidate for one occasion and produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one (Fig 4 :26).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a transcript of a particular success, with no compression and no stated reapplicability, raises the population's ground-measured reach, then the method/recordings distinction is wrong and F1 in `01` must be narrowed.

### Q2 - What comes down: a head start, not a replay

- Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**
- Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**
- Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size... Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

The head start h is a set of candidates, not an answer. The new agent still ranges each candidate against its own ground. This is why the transform is lossy in both directions and why R_T is a measurement, not a detail. The seed/seedline line from Fig 13 :142 (*"The seed was always the seedline."*) applies: what is inherited is a lineage of prior settlements, not a packed result.

**Falsifier:** if a downward replay of a recorded success produces equivalent or better ground-measured reach than a head start of candidates, with the agent's own ground check disabled, then the head-start rule is wrong.

### Q3 - Order: refuse and settle before crossing

- Formula step 2: the REFUTED bin sorts what is WRONG; *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*
- Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* (the source prefixes this with the step name SETTLE).
- Fig 5 :72: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."*
- Fig 3 :15: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"*

The dependency order is: perceive, build vocabulary, fix objective, plan, learn and carry. Within the learning link, the order is: sort the residual, refuse what is wrong, settle what is right, compress the settled item into a method, then carry it up. Crossing an unsettled term is carrying a recording. Carrying before refusing is how a shared error propagates into the commons, which is exactly the Lorenz et al. 2011 result corroborating Fig 7 :13.

**Falsifier:** if carrying an unsettled, unrefused term upward increases the population's ground-measured reach more reliably than carrying a settled method, the order is wrong.

### Q4 - Which analogue applies

Fig 12 :117 - **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :119 - **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Fig 12 :121 - **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :124 - **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Applied here:

- **A shared method is a catalyst.** It survives its own use: many agents reapply it without spending it (Fig 12 :119). But a method that is never re-derived against a changed ground becomes a *spent* catalyst, and nothing in the record marks which it is. This is the hot-stove/decay problem: a verdict that cannot be re-tested is a catalyst treated as spent.
- **The agent-method-agent-collective loop is a cycle.** Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input."* The sign decides whether it closes. A positive loop amplifies a shared error through the population; a negative loop corrects it against the ground. A chain model of "more learning leads to better collective" cannot close by construction, which is why Option A in `01` fails.
- **Independent learners over the same atom set are isomers.** Same atoms (library primitives), different bonds (which method mediates which state), different collective substance. A system that varies only the number of identical learners is not producing isomers, it is producing the same spelling many times (Fig 12 :92: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"*; Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names"*).
- **All learning agents share a functional group.** Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Every learner has perceive, route, mint, settle; the qualifier is which residual it is ranged against. Two agents in the same functional group but with different residuals are different members, not degrees of one member.

**Falsifier for the analogue set:** if a shared method is consumed by use (so that reapplication requires re-derivation every time), the catalyst reading fails; if the collective loop cannot be shown to re-enter its output as input, the cycle reading fails; if two systems with identical atoms but different method-to-state bindings behave identically, the isomer reading fails. Each analogue is a `specified` structural claim, not a measurement.

### Q5 - The type question still open

Fig 12 :125-127: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them."*

A compressed method is cheap in bits and may be expensive to test in a new habitat. A recording is expensive in bits and may take one action to refute. Neither cost predicts the other, so a system that counts only method tokens in the commons will not notice the shortfall. The population's ranking of what agents produce is a budget instrument, not a verdict instrument (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*). This remains an open measurement, not a figure conflict.

## CONCLUSION

The cross-scale object is a reapplicable method, not a learned term and not a recording. It crosses upward after refusal and settlement, and what descends is a head start of candidates, not a replay. The loop is a cycle with a sign; the method is a catalyst that can be spent; distinct learners over shared atoms are isomers, while identical learners are one term spelled many times. All four analogues carry falsifiers. Nothing here names which collective behavior a given system will exhibit: that is the `gap` of `01` F9.
