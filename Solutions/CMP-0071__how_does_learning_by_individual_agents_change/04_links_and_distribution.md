## 2. World sources (secondary, opened)

Grouped by field so overlap is visible. Agreement within a group is one witness, not several.

**Agent-based modeling and the philosophy of simulation (one pool).**

- Epstein, *Why Model?*, JASSS 11(4):12, 2008, https://www.jasss.org/11/4/12.html. Quote: *"One of these is that the goal is always prediction. The lecture distinguishes between explanation and prediction as modeling goals."* Predicts that an ABM can explain a macro regularity without predicting its timing. Corroborates the F9 distinction between a shape and a named outcome.
- Epstein, *Generative Social Science*, Princeton University Press, 2006, http://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/s8277.pdf. Quote: *"there will generally be feedback from macrostructures to microstructures... In this sense, micro and macro will, in general, co-evolve."* Predicts that the individual-to-collective map is not one-way. Corroborates F6's union compound.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Agent-Based Modeling in the Philosophy of Science*, 2023, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agent-modeling-philscience. Quote: *"ABMs are computational models that simulate behavior of individual agents in order to study emergent phenomena at the level of the community."* Predicts that the question is answerable in principle by instrumented models. Corroborates the method, not the verdict.

**Collective learning in animal groups (one pool, low overlap with the first).**

- Kao, Miller, Torney, Hartnett and Couzin, *Collective Learning and Optimal Consensus Decisions in Social Animal Groups*, PLoS Computational Biology 10(8):e1003762, 2014, https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003762. Quote: *"Individuals that learn in isolation and subsequently join together as a group make substantially worse decisions."* Predicts that learning in isolation does not transfer. Direct low-overlap corroboration of Fig 11 :40. Also: *"correlations decrease the independence of observations made by different group members, they limit the benefits derived from aggregating observations"*, corroborating Fig 2 :31 and Fig 8 :48.
- Couzin, *Collective cognition in animal groups*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 13(1):36-43, 2009, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19058992. Quote: *"social interactions, individual state, environmental modification and processes of informational amplification and decay can all play a part in tuning adaptive response."* Predicts multiple coupling mechanisms, not a single learning lever. Corroborates F6.
- Moussaid, Garnier, Theraulaz and Helbing, *Collective information processing and pattern formation in swarms, flocks, and crowds*, Topics in Cognitive Science 1(3):469-497, 2009, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25164997. Quote: *"the individual's ability to learn from past experiences can have a feedback effect on the collective dynamics."* Predicts a two-way map. Corroborates F1 membrane direction and F6 union.

**Independence, diversity and correlated evidence (one pool, low overlap with the others).**

- Hong and Page, *Groups of diverse problem solvers can outperform groups of high-ability problem solvers*, PNAS 101(46):16385-16389, 2004, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC528939. Quote: *"a team of randomly selected agents outperforms a team comprised of the best-performing agents. This result relies on the intuition that, as the initial pool of problem solvers becomes large, the best-performing agents necessarily become similar."* Predicts that ability and independence are different quantities. Corroborates Fig 8 :48 and Fig 2 :31.
- Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer and Helbing, *How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect*, PNAS 108(22):9020-9025, 2011, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108. Quote: *"even mild social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect in simple estimation tasks."* Predicts that convergence without new evidence destroys collective accuracy. Corroborates Fig 7 :13.
- Khan et al., *Surfacing Disagreement Beats Resolving It*, arXiv:2611.22461, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22461. Quote: *"preserving contradictions and passing the ranked conflict set to the debate, rather than collapsing it to a winner, improves F1 by +0.340"*. Predicts that keeping per-agent disagreement beats averaging it away. Corroborates Fig 1 :22 and Fig 2 :40.

**Micro to macro and resource sharing (lower overlap).**

- Schelling, *Dynamic Models of Segregation*, Journal of Mathematical Sociology 1:143-186, 1971, https://acoustique.ec-lyon.fr/chaos/Schelling71.pdf. Quote: *"there is no simple correspondence of individual incentive to collective results. Exaggerated separation and patterning result from the dynamics of movement."* Predicts macro outcomes that no individual intended. Corroborates the F1 claim that collective behavior is not the sum of individual behavior.
- Ezaki, Imura and Nishinari, *Groups of AI agents underuse resources despite shared evidence of increased capacity*, arXiv:2611.22632, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22632. Quote: *"After sharing, all five models tested left capacity unused."* and *"distinct numbers spread simultaneous requests across servers and increased job completions even though overall choice frequencies barely changed."* Predicts that shared availability evidence is not a head start and that distinct local states help. Corroborates Fig 4 :24 and Fig 1 :22.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and its seven falsifiers; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 reads shape without referent. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a wrong answer about how to design collective-learning systems, so independence is worth real contact cost.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its falsifiers; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) offer the formula as a method for them to reuse on their own model, with their own refuter written first.

## 5. The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape and the seven falsifiers. Ask for a verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a generator. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. The seven falsifiers in `01` section 4 are written down and unrun, so this is a genuine offer to be refuted, not a request for endorsement.
