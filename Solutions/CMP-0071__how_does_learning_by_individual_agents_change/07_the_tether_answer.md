# The Tether's answer: individual learning and collective behavior

**Mode: `general` on the membrane and habitat laws, `specified` on the application to agent-based and collective-learning systems. Nothing is `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An individual agent's residual is R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| on a slot s, per agent (Fig 1 :19). Learning reduces that residual and settles terms in that agent's library. The question is what this does to the long-term behavior of the system the agents form.

Option A says the collective is the aggregate of individual improvements and that pooled agreement is evidence. Option B says the collective changes only through a directed membrane, a change of contact, independent frames and a measured round-trip gap. Fig 9 :55 decides the form: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* This is a union compound, so A's single lever fails before measurement.

## 2. The shape

1. **Learning does not transfer by summation.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A learned success is a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25). What crosses up is a reapplicable method; what comes down is a head start that makes the next agent's search cheaper (Fig 4 :24). The collective does not inherit the individual's improvement directly.
2. **Capability is a property of the agent and its habitat.** Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* An agent that learns while its contacts, ranking and conditions are held fixed has not moved the system.
3. **Keep the residuals indexed.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Across a population the index becomes the agent-slot. A single collective learning curve can be flat while one agent carries the signal that decides the next move.
4. **Independence, not headcount, is the parameter.** Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names"*; Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Ten learners reading the same signal are one opinion said ten times.
5. **The transform is lossy; measure the round trip.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Any claim of individual-to-collective causation owes R_T.
6. **Learning is the last link in the dependency chain.** Fig 3 :15: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem."* If perception, vocabulary or objective is broken and unmeasured, more learning spends effort above the break (Fig 3 :19).
7. **A growing library is not a growing reach.** Formula step 8: *"IT IS THE REACH THAT DRIVES THE LOOP, NOT THE LIBRARY."* Counting learned terms as progress is counting the denominator.
8. **The specific long-term behavior is not named.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework returns the dials, not the value. Whether a given system becomes collectively intelligent or amplifies shared errors needs a ground reading.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that more agents or more learning is better. Headcount is a budget filter, never a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that pooled agreement among learners is corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- Not that a learned term transfers as content. Only compressed methods and head starts cross (Fig 4 :23-24).
- Not that the framework names collective intelligence, resilience, brittleness or shared error for a specific system. That is the `gap` of F9.
- Not that this is measured. No agent-based model was run here, and the society-of-agents essay it draws on says of itself *"Nothing here has been measured for a society of Tether agents."*
- Not that a room of agreeing experts supplies an anchor. Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label."*

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrumented multi-agent system or an empirical collective with a defined outcome, not further argument. The seven pre-registered falsifiers from `01` section 4 are the operational form:

1. Collective behavior changes with no method crossing, no head start descending and contact held fixed.
2. Averaged per-agent residuals preserve the live-slot signal.
3. A maximally correlated evidence pool matches an independent pool on a ground measure.
4. An agent-only improvement at fixed contact moves a collective ground reading.
5. A cross-scale transform has an identically zero round-trip gap on a named ground and code.
6. One intervention restores both union conditions with no categorically different residue.
7. Library growth at fixed reach and fixed contact increases population reach.

The measuring instruments the shape calls for are the dials named in the society-of-agents essay: how different the members are, how much of the problem must be done in sequence, how costly it is to stay in sync, and how many generations the group keeps building. None of these is a constant to pick; each can be measured on a running group. That is the honest route from shape to name.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that more learning by more agents makes a better collective. It says that individual learning reaches the collective through a directed membrane: a learned success is a recording of one occasion, what crosses up is a reapplicable method, and what comes down is a head start for the next search. It says capability belongs to the agent and its habitat, so an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. It says residuals stay indexed per agent and slot, that independence rather than headcount decides whether a union of learners has any surplus, and that the lossy cross-scale transform owes a measured round-trip gap. It refuses to name whether a given system becomes collectively intelligent or amplifies shared errors, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, seven falsifiers written in advance, and the dials an instrumented system would measure. The outcome stays open until the ground settles it.
