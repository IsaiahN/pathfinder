# Outreach draft - check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its seven falsifiers in `01` section 4 are copied verbatim into the message. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about individual learning and collective behavior

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in complexity science: how learning by individual agents changes the long-term behavior of the system they collectively form. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the application, not `grounded`):** individual learning does not reach the collective by summing learning curves. It reaches it through a directed membrane. A learned success is a recording of one occasion; what can cross upward is a reapplicable method, and what can come downward is a head start that makes the next agent's search cheaper. Separately, capability is a property of the agent and its habitat, so an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves. The transform between the one-agent scale and the population scale is lossy, and the round-trip gap is the measurement owe, not a detail to assume away.
>
> Two further constraints follow. Residuals must stay indexed per agent and per slot, because aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. And independence, not headcount, is the parameter: agents drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names, so their agreement compounds without adding evidence. This is a union of conditions, not one lever: transport through the membrane and change of contact both have to hold.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not naming which collective behavior will appear. The derivation returns a shape, not a name, so it does not say whether a given system will become collectively intelligent or amplify shared errors, more resilient or more brittle. Those need a ground reading, an instrumented model or an experiment. I am also not claiming that the figures settle a specific agent-based model, and I am not claiming this is measured.
>
> **What would refute the shape. I wrote these down before sending this.**
> 1. A multi-agent system changes its long-term collective behavior while no method crosses the membrane, no head start descends, and contact is held fixed.
> 2. Averaging per-agent residuals preserves the same guidance signal as per-agent and per-slot tracking where one live agent changes the outcome.
> 3. A maximally correlated evidence pool yields the same ground-measured collective accuracy as an independent pool of equal ability.
> 4. An agent-only improvement, with contact demonstrably unchanged, moves a ground reading of the collective.
> 5. A transform is demonstrated for which the round-trip gap is identically zero on a named ground and a named code.
> 6. One intervention restores both union conditions with no categorically different residue.
> 7. Library growth at fixed reach and fixed contact strictly increases population reach.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong or needs narrowing and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. What would you count as a failure here? If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Agent-Based Modeling in the Philosophy of Science, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agent-modeling-philscience
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an agent-based model you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
