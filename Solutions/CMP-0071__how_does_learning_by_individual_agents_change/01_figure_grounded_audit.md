## 0. The question as an effect

Question 71 of section 8 reads: *"How does learning by individual agents change the long-term behavior of the systems they collectively form?"*

Stated in effect terms, the residual is R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per agent and per object slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* An individual agent learns when it reduces its own R and admits a term to its own Γ. The effect to be decided is the map from a set of such per-agent reductions to the long-term behavior of the population.

Two options, written down before the derivation:

- **Option A, the aggregate reading.** Collective behavior is the sum or average of individual learning curves. More learning by more agents yields a proportionately better collective. Agreement among the learners is taken as confirmation. A single lever, more learning, clears the question.
- **Option B, the membrane reading.** Individual learning reaches the collective only through a directed filter, only when contact in the agent-habitat system changes, with a measured round-trip gap, and only where independent frames make the union surplus real. Agreement among learners is one evidence pool. No single lever clears a union of conditions.

The derivation decides between these. It does not decide which specific long-term behavior (collective intelligence, shared error, resilience, brittleness, lock-in, cycling) will appear in a named system. That is reported as a `gap` in F9.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** R is a three-place relation for one agent: belief b, action a, observation o′, indexed by slot s. Across a population it remains indexed per agent and per slot, not reduced to one number (Fig 1 :19, :22). Arity goes up with population; the index does not collapse.
- **Symmetry.** Learning is ordered and irreversible in the record. A refinement may be refuted and lose its standing to be composed over, but it is never deleted (formula step 3, Fig 6 :42-46). The membrane is a *directed* filter, not a symmetric exchange (Fig 4 :21-24). Any reading that treats collective learning as a symmetric blend of agents is a strain and sends us back to census.
- **Scale.** There are two loops at two sizes: the one-agent step-by-step loop and the population loop with a shared library and a ranking of what agents produce (Fig 4 :4-17). The transform between them is lossy (Fig 4 :27-32). The kernel is the same at both sizes: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change"* (Fig 4 :20).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none can be named, the item is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The one-agent learning loop** | one agent, b, w, Γ, per-slot R_s | settlement of terms against what pays | the ground, Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* | admitted |
| **D2 The population and its membrane** | many agents, a shared library, *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5) | reapplicable methods up, head starts down | the ground plus the measured gap R_T (Fig 4 :30) | admitted |
| **D3 The habitat and contact** | agents, conditions, relations (Fig 11 :45-47) | contact | the ground, which does not update (Fig 11 :73) | admitted |
| **D4 Independence and the union of frames** | frames with differing closures | overlap of evidence pools | the ground via triangulation (Fig 2 :31-32) | admitted |
| **X1 Collective intelligence as a free-floating property of the group** | none nameable beyond D2 and D3 | none distinct | none nameable | not a domain yet; absorbed as the D2+D3 compound |
| **X2 The aggregate learning curve as a single number** | none beyond D1 | none distinct | none nameable | absorbed; this is the Fig 1 :22 aggregation error |

### 1.3 Partition or union

This is a **union, not a partition**. D1 through D4 overlap. An agent is in D1 and D3 at once; the shared library is D2 and the evidence pool of D4; the membrane uses the same ground as D1 but a different currency. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The operative conditions are at least two:

- **C1, transport.** Something learned by one agent must become a reapplicable method that crosses up, and a head start that comes down (D2).
- **C2, contact.** The change must alter agent-and-habitat contact (D3).

A lever that increases individual learning clears the region where only C1 or only C2 binds and leaves the intersection standing, because the other condition still binds. Option A is exactly such a single lever. This is why A fails before any measurement.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The links are perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, and at the top *"learn and carry"* with the gloss Fig 3 :15: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem."*

Learning is the last link, not the first. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* So the first diagnostic question for a system whose agents learn but whose collective does not move is which upstream link stops the chain, not how much more to train the agents.

## 2. Figure census: verbatim lines quoted before concluding

Each finding quotes the governing line first, then applies it. A line that needs a strain to fit is a line governing another quantity and is sent back to census in section 3.

### F1 - Learning does not transfer as an aggregate. Only methods cross up and only head starts come down (decisive against Option A)

Fig 4 :5: *"many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce"*

Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it."*

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*

Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

The one-agent loop and the population loop are not joined by summing learning curves. They are joined by a filter with a direction. A particular learned success is a recording; carried up it resembles knowledge while remaining a description of one occasion. What crosses is the compressed, reapplicable method, and what descends is a head start that makes a later agent's search cheaper. Option A assumes the recording itself transfers. That assumption is exactly the failure Fig 4 names.

Mode `general` for the membrane law, `specified` for ABM populations. **Refuter:** a controlled multi-agent system in which agents' individual learning changes the long-term collective outcome while no reapplicable method is carried up, no head start descends, and the ranking of produced work is unchanged. If such a case is demonstrated and survives a provenance check, F1 is wrong.

### F2 - Capability is a property of agent and habitat, not of the agent alone

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

This is the decisive line for the phrase "learning by individual agents." Improving a learner while holding its contact fixed does not move the collective system. The improvement must change what the agent is in contact with: other agents, the ranking, the conditions, the relations. Kao et al. 2014 (world source, read 2026-11-22) found that animals that learn in isolation and then join a group make substantially worse decisions than animals that learn inside the collective. That is a low-overlap corroboration of this figure line, not a replacement for it.

Mode `general`. **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement, with contact demonstrably held fixed, that moves a ground reading of collective behavior. Fig 11 :72 already lists this as the experiment that would refute the capability claim; it is recorded here before the conclusion, not after.

### F3 - Per-agent residuals stay indexed. Averaging throws away the guidance signal

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*

Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

The kernel is the same at both sizes, so the per-slot indexing survives crossing the membrane with a changed actor set. A collective learning curve that averages all agent residuals can be near zero while one agent or one slot carries the live signal that determines the system's next move. This is a measurement rule, not a metaphysical claim about groups.

Mode `general` for the indexing rule, `specified` for its application across the membrane. **Refuter:** a demonstration that averaging per-agent residuals preserves the same "where to look" signal as per-slot tracking, measured on a system where a single live slot changes the collective outcome.

Strain noted and resolved: Fig 1 governs measurement inside one agent's loop; applying it across a population is a membrane transform, which is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30). The extension is legitimate because the kernel does not change with size, but the index must be re-stated per agent at the new scale. It is a `specified` extension, not a verbatim figure statement about populations.

### F4 - Independence is the parameter. Shared evidence makes many agents one searcher

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names"*

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7: *"Distribute the search and keep the searchers independent."*

So the number of learning agents is not the quantity that matters. What matters is how little they draw on the same evidence. Ten agents reading the same signal are one agent with ten names, and their converging beliefs are agreement, not added evidence. This is the figure-level reason that "more learners" is a budget filter and not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

World corroboration is strong and low-overlap: Hong and Page 2004 show a randomly selected diverse team outperforms a team of the best agents because the best become similar; Lorenz et al. 2011 show even mild social influence narrows diversity and undermines collective accuracy; Khan et al. 2026 show that preserving contradictions in multi-agent debate beats collapsing to a winner. Three distinct fields, one mechanism shape. Their agreement is corroboration of the figure line, not a new verdict.

Mode `general`. **Refuter:** a controlled group in which agents with maximally correlated evidence pools gain the same collective accuracy, against a ground, as an equal-ability group with independent evidence. That would break the independence parameter.

### F5 - The crossing is lossy. Measure the round trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

Fig 4 :31: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*

Symbols table: R_T is *"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."*

Any claim of the form "individual learning produced collective change" is a cross-scale claim. The method carries a method up and a head start down, so the two are not the same object. The difference is R_T. Treating the learned term and the collective behavior as the same thing sets R_T to zero by assertion, which is the dead-reckoning error. The honest report names what the coarser description drops.

Mode `general`. **Refuter:** a demonstrated transform between the one-agent method and the population head start for which the round trip gap is identically zero on a named ground and a named code. Until such a measurement, R_T is recorded qualitatively in `11_audit.md` as nonzero with dropped constraints enumerated.

### F6 - The question is a union compound. One lever does not clear it

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Fig 9 :57-58: *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* (the verbatim extract wraps "compound" onto the next numbered line; the quote is the joined text).

The conditions C1 (transport through the membrane) and C2 (change of contact) overlap. A system can satisfy C1 and fail C2: a method crosses up, but agents' contact with the habitat is unchanged, so capability does not move. It can satisfy C2 and fail C1: agents' contact changes locally, but nothing reapplicable reaches the population library, so the change is not carried. Option A is a single lever aimed at neither intersection.

Mode `general` for the union rule, `specified` for these two conditions. **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions at once with no categorically different residue, demonstrated on a ground. Until then the union verdict stands.

### F7 - Learning is the last link in the dependency chain

Fig 3 :15: *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"*

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Fig 3 :26: *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

This reverses the usual prescription. When a population of learning agents fails to change, the first question is not "are they learning enough" but "which link does the chain stop at, and was that measured or assumed" (Fig 3 :29). A learning link above a broken vocabulary or objective link spends compute on a step whose input never arrives.

Mode `specified`. **Refuter:** a case where increasing the learning rate or the number of learned terms rescues a chain whose vocabulary or objective link has no instrument and is demonstrably broken. That would break the dependency order.

### F8 - A growing library is not a growing reach

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 8: *"IT IS THE REACH THAT DRIVES THE LOOP, NOT THE LIBRARY."*

Same step: *"Γ can grow while the reach does not move, so a loop that stops when Γ stops growing stops at the wrong place, and a system measuring progress by terms minted is counting the denominator."*

Fig 5 :72: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."* (the figure line continues with the necessity and insufficiency distinction; the shorter form is a truncation, not a different quote).

Seat Map :194 restates it: *"So a record that grows is not a reach that grows, and a seat reading volume as progress is counting the denominator."*

An individual agent can accumulate learned terms while the set of collective behaviors reachable from those terms is unchanged. Counting learned terms as progress is counting the denominator. This is a distinct failure from F1: F1 concerns what crosses, F8 concerns whether the crossed material extends what the population can reach.

Mode `general`. **Refuter:** a demonstrated case where library growth at the individual level, with no change in what crosses and no change in contact, strictly increases the population's reach on a ground. That would break the reach/library distinction.

### F9 - Which specific long-term behavior appears is a `gap`, not a hidden answer

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The figures return the shape of the map from individual learning to collective behavior. They do not return the sign: whether a given system becomes more intelligent or amplifies shared errors, more resilient or more brittle. The world literature contains both outcomes under different conditions, which is what a union compound predicts. Lorenz et al. 2011 show shared influence destroying crowd accuracy; Kao et al. 2014 show coupled learning in the collective context approaching the provable optimum. Those are different branches of the union, not a contradiction, and neither is a figure line. Naming which branch a specific system is on requires a ground reading, an instrumented model or an experiment.

Mode `general` for the limit, `gap` for the specific name. **What is owed:** an instrument, not another argument. This is reported as unreached rather than unreachable, since no census of all possible collective systems was run.

### F10 - Settling is necessary for composition and not sufficient

Fig 5 :70: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one."*

Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."*

Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* (the source prefixes this with the step name SETTLE).

A learning agent can settle a term against its own ground and still produce nothing the population can compose over. This is why F1 and F2 are not redundant: a settled term may be unusable across the membrane even when it is perfectly usable inside the agent. Fig 5 :49: *"Machinery firing is not the milestone. A running system can mint and still return nothing new, which is exactly what the third guard exists to record."*

Mode `general`. **Refuter:** a demonstrated settled term that composes at the population level without crossing the membrane as a method, with provenance showing no head start descended.

## 3. Strain tests and re-census

Four readings strained during the derivation. Each was sent back to census rather than smoothed over.

1. **Fig 7's mirror chain versus coupled collective learning.** My first reading mapped Kao et al. 2014 directly onto Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* and predicted that any group learning is mirror-chain failure. Re-census: Fig 7 governs handoff between rooms sharing a closure; Kao's group is coupled through consensus so each individual's experience changes (their contact changes, Fig 11 :40). Different objects. Mapping withdrawn, both readings kept. The correct figure for Kao is Fig 11 :40 plus Fig 8 :45, not Fig 7. Fig 7 governs the Lorenz case, where agents converge on one another's estimates without a changed ground reading.
2. **Fig 4 :22 as a ban on all transfer.** First reading: nothing individual can reach the collective. Corrected: methods and head starts do cross, recordings do not. The ban is on the wrong currency, not on crossing. This correction is kept visible.
3. **Fig 1 :19 applied to populations.** Within one agent the index is a slot; across agents it becomes an agent-slot. The kernel does not change with size (Fig 4 :20), so the extension is legitimate, but it is a `specified` extension, not a verbatim population claim from Fig 1. Marked as such.
4. **Hong and Page versus Fig 11 :40.** Apparent conflict: if improving agents in isolation changes nothing, why does a group of lower-ability diverse agents outperform a group of the best? Re-census: Fig 11 governs capability relative to contact for one agent; Hong and Page govern team composition from a pool, which is Fig 8's independence quantity. Different domains. Both feed the union; neither flips the verdict.

**After re-census: no live strain in the figure verdict.** The one open item is the operand type of a comparison at a higher level, which this question does not use, and the `gap` of F9, which is an unreached instrument, not a strain.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is: individual learning changes collective long-term behavior only through (C1) a reapplicable method that crosses up and a head start that comes down, with a measured round-trip gap, and (C2) a change in agent-and-habitat contact; residuals remain indexed per agent and slot; independence, not headcount, decides whether the union has surplus.

The shape is refuted if any of the following holds on a ground:

1. A controlled multi-agent system changes its long-term collective behavior while no method crosses the membrane, no head start descends, and contact with the ground is held fixed. Breaks F1 and F2.
2. Averaging per-agent residuals preserves the same guidance signal as per-agent and per-slot tracking where a single live agent changes the outcome. Breaks F3.
3. A maximally correlated evidence pool yields the same ground-measured collective accuracy as an independent pool of equal ability. Breaks F4.
4. An agent-only improvement, with contact demonstrably unchanged, moves a ground reading of the collective. Breaks F2 directly (this is Fig 11 :72's own standing falsifier).
5. A transform is demonstrated for which the round trip gap is identically zero on a named ground and a named code. Breaks F5.
6. One intervention restores both union conditions with no categorically different residue. Breaks F6.
7. Library growth at fixed reach and fixed contact strictly increases population reach. Breaks F8.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed to the surviving conditions. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is `grounded`. These seven are repeated in `07`, `08` and `09` so a reader can check that they were written before the run and not fitted afterwards.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer "more learning makes a better collective." That is Option A and it fails F1, F2 and F8 before any measurement.
2. Diagnose the chain first: which of perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn-and-carry is the stop, and was it measured (Fig 3 :29).
3. For any claimed individual-to-collective effect, name what crossed up, what came down, what changed in contact, and the round-trip gap R_T. If any of the four is missing, the claim is a recording, not a method.
4. Keep residuals indexed per agent and slot. Refuse a single aggregate learning curve as evidence about where the system will move.
5. Treat independence as the design parameter. Buy it in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). More agents with the same evidence buy more spelling, not more search.
6. Unbundle the union before searching. A single lever aimed at individual learning leaves the contact condition standing.
7. State the `gap` of F9 openly. Do not name which collective behavior will appear without a ground reading.

Fig 4 :25 applies to this folder itself: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the derivation and the refuter, not the conclusion.

## 7. Verdict summary

The figure frame decides a shape, not a ranked list of outcomes. Individual learning changes the collective system through a directed membrane, through a change in agent-habitat contact, and through independence between frames; it does not change the collective by summing learning curves. The crossing is lossy and its gap R_T is owed as a measurement. Residuals stay indexed per agent and slot. The question is a union compound, so one lever aimed at individual learning leaves the contact condition binding. Which specific long-term behavior emerges is a declared `gap`, unreached until an instrument settles it.

Verdicts per domain, kept separate and never averaged:

- **D1, one-agent loop:** learning reduces per-slot R and settles terms. `general` and triangulated across Fig 1 :19/:23 and Fig 5 :70/:72. Status: `triangulated`.
- **D2, population membrane:** only methods cross up and head starts down, with R_T. `general` and triangulated across Fig 4 :22/:23/:24/:30 and formula step 6. Status: `triangulated`.
- **D3, habitat and contact:** capability is agent-and-habitat; isolation changes nothing. `general`, single-source in the figures (Fig 11 :39-40) but corroborated by a low-overlap world source (Kao et al. 2014). Status: `single-source` in the figures, corroborated.
- **D4, independence and union:** independence is the parameter and the union needs both conditions. `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :31/:40, Fig 6 :53, Fig 7 :13, Fig 8 :45/:48 and Fig 9 :55-58. Status: `triangulated`.
- **Specific outcome name:** `gap`. Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134. Status: `gap`.

No verdict was averaged with another. The world sources corroborate shape and mechanism; they do not supply the verdict, and none was allowed to flip it after a re-census.
