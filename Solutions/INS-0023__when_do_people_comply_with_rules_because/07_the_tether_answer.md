# The Tether's answer on legitimacy versus sanction fear

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

People comply. We want to know whether the operative source is a belief that the rule is legitimate or fear of punishment. The difficulty is not the amount of compliance but its attribution: the same act can come from either source. Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* This is a union compound of two overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55), not one hard question. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **An observed act does not determine its source.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Compliance is a lossy projection; several sources can give the same reading. Attribution without a discriminator is the metamerism error.
2. **Compliance is a property of person-and-habitat, never of the person alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* A change that does not change contact does not change the source (Fig 11 :40).
3. **The two sources carry different bonds.** Sanction fear is a threshold comparison, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48); legitimacy is an internalized conjunction that behaves like a catalyst, *"a part that survives its own use"* (Fig 12 :119). They are isomers over the full shared atom set (Fig 12 :117), or at minimum members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123).
4. **Sanctions can change sign.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A fine that deters in one frame can displace an internalized norm in another; contact cascades (Fig 11 :52). The two sources are therefore a union, and one lever cannot clear both conditions (Fig 9 :57).
5. **The discriminator is variation of detection and sanction, indexed per slot.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Where compliance persists at p = 0 and S = 0, the internalized-norm source is live. A compliance rate that pools slots throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :22): *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
6. **A self-report cannot score the verdict.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
7. **The person-level switching threshold is a gap.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that legitimacy and fear are mutually exclusive. They overlap; this is a union, not a two-box split.
- Not that compliance is evidence of legitimacy. That is the error the shape exists to prevent.
- Not that a self-report of "it felt legitimate" settles the source. It is a frame-internal quantity.
- Not that there is a population-wide switching number. That is a gap.
- Not a derivation of a particular institution's legitimacy, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the required resolution has been made here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating design, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- At p = 0 and S = 0, does this person-slot still comply? That is the live-mass test for the internalized-norm source.
- Does manipulating perceived procedural justice change no-surveillance compliance? That tests the legitimacy mechanism.
- Does varying p times S leave no-surveillance compliance unchanged, or does it displace it? That tests the crowd-out reading.
- **Standing falsifiers:** no no-surveillance residual anywhere; no procedural-justice effect; additive and independent sources with no displacement; invariance across a habitat substitution that removes the legitimacy anchor; an instrument that returns the source from a single act without manipulating p or S.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The institutional vocabulary we bring (legitimacy, sanction, norm) is a visible choice, not nature's own list. Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* The answer stops at the limit of the available discriminators and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you from a compliant act whether the person obeyed because the rule was legitimate or because they feared punishment. It says that one observed act is a lossy projection: both sources can produce it, and only a discriminator separates them. That discriminator is variation of detection and sanction, read per person and per rule, not a pooled compliance rate. Legitimacy behaves like an internalized norm that survives its own use; sanction fear behaves like a threshold comparison, and the two are isomers over the same observable compliance. Sanctions can even displace legitimacy, so this is a union of overlapping conditions, not a two-box split. A self-report from the same population cannot settle it, and the person-level switching point is a declared gap. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the design that would close it. The gap stays open until the ground settles it.
