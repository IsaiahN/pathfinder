# Expert verification packet - the Tether on legitimacy versus sanction fear

**What this is:** a claim in a form a compliance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A single observed act of compliance does not tell you why the person complied. An act produced by an internalized norm and an act produced by fear of a fine look the same from the outside, so compliance is a lossy projection whose source is not determined by the reading. The two sources carry different bonds: sanction fear is a threshold comparison, legitimacy is an internalized norm that survives its own use, and over a shared atom set they stand as isomers. Sanctions can displace legitimacy, so the two conditions overlap as a union, and one lever cannot clear both. The discriminator is variation of detection probability and sanction severity, indexed per person and per rule and never pooled into a compliance rate. The person-level switching number is a declared gap. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the design that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal and field-facing: the act does not determine the source
- **Statement:** compliance is a lossy projection; legitimacy and sanction fear can produce the same reading.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*; Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Secondary corroboration: *The Hard Problem is About Source* reports that a reading *"cannot comment on the source"* and that *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*
- **Refuter:** an instrument that returns the operative source from a single compliance observation without manipulating detection or sanction, verified on a held-out sample.

### C2 Field-facing: the two sources have different bonds and can displace each other
- **Statement:** sanction fear is a threshold comparison; legitimacy is a catalyst-like internalized norm; they are isomers over the full shared atom set; sanctions can change sign.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :48 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*; Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*; Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** is deterrence genuinely a cost-benefit threshold in the field's own models (the rational deterrence literature writes it as a product of probability and cost)? Does the crowding-out literature support the claim that an external incentive can reduce an internalized motive? Are the two sources additive, or is there displacement?
- **Refuter:** showing both sources can be written with the same bond, or that a within-subject increase in expected cost leaves no-surveillance compliance unchanged with no displacement. Either kills the isomer/catalyst reading; the weaker functional-group claim may still stand.

### C3 Framework-internal: the discriminator is per-slot variation of detection and sanction
- **Statement:** the anchor is behavior under manipulated detection probability and sanction severity, indexed per person-slot; a self-report cannot score the verdict; the threshold is a gap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*; Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*; Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*; Fig 13 :134 - *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **What to check:** does the field already have a discriminator that does not require manipulating detection or sanction? Would a self-report predict held-out no-surveillance behavior as well as the behavioral test?
- **Refuter:** an alternative instrument returning a stable person-level attribution without manipulating p or S; or a self-report that predicts held-out no-surveillance compliance as well as the behavioral discriminator, which would demote the frame-internal objection.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that legitimacy and fear are mutually exclusive. The claim is a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55).
- Not that compliance is evidence of legitimacy. That is the metamerism error the packet refuses.
- Not that a self-report settles the source, and not that there is a population-wide switching number.
- Not a derivation of any institution's legitimacy, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating design, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. At p = 0 and S = 0, does this person-slot still comply? This is the live-mass test for the internalized-norm source.
2. Does manipulating perceived procedural justice change no-surveillance compliance? This tests the legitimacy mechanism.
3. Does varying p times S leave no-surveillance compliance unchanged, or displace it? This tests the crowd-out reading.
4. Does compliance survive a habitat substitution that removes the legitimacy anchor? This tests person-and-habitat.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :50, Fig 9 :32, Fig 12 :117 and Fig 11 :49, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the field's own models: is deterrence a threshold, is legitimacy internalized, and is there evidence of displacement rather than additivity?
3. Name one source-discriminating instrument that does not require manipulating detection or sanction, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
