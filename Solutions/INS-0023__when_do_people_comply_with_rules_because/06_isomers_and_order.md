### Q3 Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- As written, L = {person, norm} and D = {cost, benefit, comparison}. They do **not** share an atom set, so by Fig 12's strict definition they are **not** isomers; they are two different molecules in the same library.
- The stronger version: write both over the full shared set {person, rule, action, cost, benefit, norm, comparison}, and they become isomers, same atoms, different arrangement, different long-run behavior (persistence versus collapse when p and S fall). That is the claim worth testing.
- The weaker, safe claim is that they share a **functional group**: Fig 12 :123, **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The functional group is "produces compliance"; the qualifier is which bond does it.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either source can be written without an atom the other requires, even over the full set (for example, if legitimacy-based compliance can be fully described with no norm atom, or deterrence with no cost atom), the isomer claim fails and the two-molecule reading stands instead.

### Q4 What order is the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

- Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52). A sanction applied at the micro level can alter the norm at the meso level, so the union is ordered by dependency and not by importance (Fig 3 :20): *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- **Strain, named:** Fig 12 :112 says *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison."* Deterrence fits the comparison; legitimacy fits a conjunction that has become an identity. Mapping "identity" onto "internalized norm" is an analogue from the bond vocabulary, not a measured claim. It is recorded as a strain, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

The two sources are not isomers as naively written, because they share no atom. Over the full shared set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier; as the sources are usually described they are members of one functional group whose presence fixes the observable outcome, compliance. The union is first-order until a discriminator settles each source per slot; an institution-level legitimacy claim is second-order and is not yet licensed. This rescues what is defensible in the "legitimacy versus fear" framing without claiming to have measured either source.
