# Outreach draft - check the discriminator before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 design in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 and F6 is stated as a refutable test. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One check on how compliance is attributed, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in rule compliance research. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a single observed act of compliance does not tell you why the person complied. An act produced by an internalized norm and an act produced by fear of a fine look the same from the outside. In the framework's terms, compliance is a lossy projection: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* What is owed is not a better description of the act but a discriminator, a condition that separates the two sources.
>
> The discriminator I am proposing is variation of detection probability and sanction severity, indexed per person and per rule, never averaged into a compliance rate. Where compliance persists with detection and sanction both at zero, the internalized norm is the operative source. Where compliance tracks expected cost, sanction fear is the operative source. Both can hold in the same person, so this is a union, not a two-box split.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the two sources are mutually exclusive, that a self-report of "feeling it is legitimate" settles the source, that there is a single population-wide threshold, or that I have measured anything. A self-report comes from the same frame as the compliance it is meant to explain; a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces. The person-level switching point is a declared gap until an instrument returns it.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. No-surveillance compliance is statistically indistinguishable from zero across every person tested, leaving no internalized-norm residual anywhere.
> 2. Manipulating procedural justice does not change no-surveillance compliance.
> 3. Sanctions and legitimacy are additive and independent, with no displacement and no sign change.
> 4. Compliance is invariant across a habitat substitution that removes the rule's legitimacy anchor.
> 5. An instrument returns a stable source attribution from a single compliance observation without manipulating detection or sanction.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked narrative on source and instruments: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a compliance problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
