## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a gauge symmetry is a primitive of the description (F) or a derived effective structure (E). Option F requires an atom composition cannot produce and a provenance for importing it; option E requires a composition that adds no atom and a scale above which the symmetry dissolves. What each leads to: F asserted absolutely entails a bottom level and an unprovenanced import, both refused by the figures; E asserted absolutely entails a first level from which the symmetry is absent, also refused. A third reading, that the binary is a level error and the two descriptions are the same term at two levels, preserves what is defensible in both and leaves one named gap.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The binary is refused; the shape survives. Symmetry is never a substrate member (D1). Gauge equivalence is identity of descriptions and the overcount is a pricing fact (D2). Irreducibility is a fact about a level and never about the world, with no bottom and no top, so F and E are the same term under a level crossing rather than isomers at one level (D3). Composition adds arrangements, never atoms, so an absolute F would be an unprovenanced import. The same-level discrimination of F from E is a `gap` because the anchor that would settle it is withheld by the definition of gauge equivalence. Many-body emergence is an empirically settled part of D3 and is kept separate. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the identity reading fails, C2 in `08` breaks and the packet is withdrawn before it reaches anyone. If the level rule fails, the absolute-F and absolute-E options reopen and the question has to be re-censused. The avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the interior before the residual; Fig 2 :42 prices it as *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. The verdict is a shape rather than a binding to a particular gauge group, so it can be narrowed at a level without withdrawing a term. The gap in D3 can be closed by an instrument without disturbing D1 or D2.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument whose gauge-invariant reading differs between a primitive gauge field and an effective one at the same level would close the gap; a demonstration that orbit representatives differ observably would break identity; a derivation of symmetry as a seventh member or the exhibition of a last level would break the substrate and level rules. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
