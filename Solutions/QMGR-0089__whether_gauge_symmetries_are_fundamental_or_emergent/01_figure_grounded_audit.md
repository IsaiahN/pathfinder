## 0. The claim under audit and the bound shape

**The residual, in effect terms.** The question presents two options. Call them F and E.

- **F (fundamental).** A gauge symmetry is an atom of the description. It is posited before the dynamics, it cannot be derived from more primitive terms, and removing it removes physics. Its proper ground is a measurement that would fail without it.
- **E (emergent).** A gauge symmetry is a molecule. It is composed from more primitive atoms, it holds only below a scale, it dissolves above that scale, and treating it as basic freezes an effective description into an axiom. Its proper ground is a measurement at or above the scale where it dissolves.

The residual is not "which word is right". It is the gap between the two commitments: F requires an atom that composition cannot produce; E requires a composition that cannot add an atom. Both are claims about what the closure can and cannot contain, so the figures bear directly.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The links here are: (1) what counts as a primitive, (2) what counts as an equivalence of descriptions, (3) what changes when the level changes, (4) whether an instrument can discriminate F from E at one level. A verdict at (4) is a reading of nothing until (1) and (2) are settled. The derivation runs in that order.

**Direction, bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** A frame cannot check the direction of its own question because the sought-for shape is a preference, not a term. So write it down now: *an answer to this question is a statement of (a) the level at which the gauge symmetry is irreducible, (b) the atom set it composes from or the import that would have to supply it, and (c) an instrument whose reading would differ if F were true and E false.* This sentence is bound before the run, so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else. Anything that does not deliver (a), (b) and (c) is not an answer to this question, however plausible it sounds.

## 1. Domain census

Done before choosing any figure line. R is described by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain gives actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

**R (the residual).** Arity: two conditions, F and E, treated by the question as one binary predicate. Symmetry: the question assumes the two options are ordered opposites on one axis; that assumption is itself a symmetry claim and must be tested, not used. Scale: F is a claim about the bottom of the description, E is a claim about a scale-relative effective layer. So the residual spans at least two levels. Anchor candidate: a gauge-invariant instrument. Problem: gauge-equivalent configurations are empirically identical by definition, so a gauge-invariant instrument returns the same reading under F and E at the same level. The anchor is therefore **candidate but not yet nameable for the binary**; it is nameable for E alone in condensed-matter systems, where scale-dependent dissolution is an experimental fact (Bass 2021, opened 2026-11-23). ## 3. Findings

### F1 — Symmetry is not a substrate member; it is what a composition leaves behind (decisive against F as stated)

Fig 13 :124-125:

> **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."**

Fig 13 :43-44, on why a seventh member was removed:

> **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."**

`THE_FORMULA.md` substrate section says the same in compact form: derived quantities, including symmetry and a continuum, are what a turn leaves rather than members of the set. (Attribution to the formula, not an independent figure quote.)

If "fundamental" means "a member of the generating set", the figures refuse it for symmetry in the same move that refused the continuum. Symmetry is downstream of the six. This is not a claim that gauge fields are unreal; it is a claim about the vocabulary's floor.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural). The applied claim that gauge symmetry specifically is derived is `specified`, ungrounded.
- **Refuter, written before the conclusion:** derive symmetry as a seventh member from the six, or show a derivation of one of the six from the other five that promotes symmetry into the set (breaks Fig 13 :41, *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."*).
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 13 (substrate membership) and Fig 12 :92 (*"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*) bear on different objects with low overlap: one on the set's floor, one on arrangements over the set.

### F2 — Gauge equivalence is the identity bond, not a new substance

Fig 12 :67 and :69 define identity:

> **"two names, one referent"**
> **"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."**

Fig 12 :92-93:

> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed."**

A gauge transformation maps one representative to another representative of the same physical state. That is *"two names, one referent"*, with the substitution test returning *"Is anything lost? No."* The k! line prices the same situation in the counting register: a code that counts gauge-related configurations as distinct states overcounts by the volume of the group, which is the framework's flat-formula error at the level of description cost. This is why the overcount is a bookkeeping fact and not a new physical degree of freedom.

- **Mode:** `specified`. The identity reading is structural; applying it to local gauge groups is a binding from the physics frame, provenanced as such (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*).
- **Refuter:** show that two gauge-related configurations yield a gauge-invariant difference in any measurement (breaks identity), or show that the overcount is not the group volume (breaks the pricing analogue).
- **Status:** `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 12 only). World corroboration from Dougherty (SEP, 2025) and Brading and Castellani (SEP, 2003/2023) is secondary and shares no evidence pool with the figures, but one corroborating field is not two witnesses for a figure verdict.

### F3 — Fundamentality is a level-relative status, so F and E are not contradictory

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

Fig 4 :33:

> **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**

This is the hinge of the whole derivation. "Irreducible" is not a property a thing carries across levels. It is a status at a level. A symmetry that is emergent relative to a constituent level is an atom for whatever composes over it at the effective level. So E at level n and F at level n+1 are compatible descriptions of the same item. The question's binary treats them as mutually exclusive; the figures say the exclusivity is a level error.

Neither end is available as an absolute: no bottom (Fig 12 :104) and no top (Fig 4 :33). An answer that says "gauge symmetry is fundamental, full stop" would require a last level, which the figures forbid. An answer that says "gauge symmetry is emergent, full stop" would require a first level from which it is absent, which the same recursion declines to supply.

- **Mode:** `general` for the level rule, `specified` for its application to gauge symmetry.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a term that is irreducible at every level, or exhibit a last room in which nothing further composes (breaks Fig 12 :104 and Fig 4 :33 simultaneously).
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 12 and Fig 4 are separate figures reached by different routes (bond recursion and cross-scale membrane). Low overlap.

### F4 — Composition cannot add a primitive; an emergent symmetry is an arrangement, not a new atom

Fig 6 :21:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 13 :115:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Fig 6 :14 names the alternative:

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"**

So E, if it means "composed from more primitive terms", adds no atom. It produces a new arrangement over atoms already held. Calling that arrangement a new fundamental ingredient is a category error. Conversely, F, if it means "an atom not derivable from anything we hold", cannot be reached by composition at all. It would have to be an import with a provenance gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :9-13). A claim that gauge symmetry is fundamental therefore owes an account of which frame supplied the atom and what gap it explains. Most physics presentations supply the gauge principle as a postulate, which is exactly an unprovenanced import under this rule.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** show a composition adding an atom to `atoms(Γ)`, or show an import of the gauge principle with provenance and a shadow test that survives an independent check. The second does not refute the shape; it supplies the missing record and moves the claim from `candidate` to sourced.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 6 (reachability) and Fig 13 (closure of the set) are different figures with different machines.

### F5 — Noether ties symmetry to the states, not the reverse

Fig 13 :62 and :65, from the row that removes space and time:

> **"no spatial symmetry for Noether, and no region to make isolated mean anything"**
> **"no time-translation symmetry, and no finite operations to leave a remainder"**

Within the six-term set, space and time are load-bearing for the Noether symmetries. Remove them and the symmetries go with them. Symmetry is not sitting above the states as a free-standing principle; it is a derivative of the arrangement of states and structures. This is the figure-internal counterpart of the physics fact that conservation laws follow from symmetries of the spacetime or internal structure, not the other way round.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** derive spatial symmetry without space, or time-translation symmetry without time, inside the framework.
- **Status:** `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 13 only). Brading and Castellani (SEP, opened 2026-11-23) describe Noether's two theorems and the different status of global and local symmetries; this corroborates but does not triangulate a figure verdict.

### F6 — The binary has no single anchor, so the same-level question is a `gap`

Fig 2 :29-30 defines the anchor:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 9 :4-5 governs the failure:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :27-28 adds the discipline:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Apply the anchor test to "F or E at the same level". The only readings that could discriminate are gauge-invariant observables. But the defining property of a gauge transformation is that it leaves those observables unchanged. So the instrument returns the same answer under F and E; the anchor does not move, which here means it cannot move, and the question cannot be settled at that level. This is not silence. Following Fig 13's rule, *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, it is a `gap` with a named reason: the missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, not more argument.

The compound splits cleanly:

- **Mode:** `general` for the split, `specified` for the claim that no gauge-invariant instrument discriminates.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument whose gauge-invariant reading differs between a primitive gauge field and an effective one at the same level. That would supply the anchor and convert the `gap` into a ground reading. Until then the gap stands.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 2 (anchor test) and Fig 9 (union split) are different figures with low overlap.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before the WATSON search, per the standing instruction that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Primitive promotion.** A symmetry is derived as a seventh substrate member, or one of the six is derived from the other five in a way that promotes symmetry into the generating set (breaks F1, Fig 13 :41, :43-44, :124-125).
2. **Identity failure.** Two gauge-related configurations are shown to produce a gauge-invariant observational difference, or the overcount is shown not to be the orbit volume (breaks F2, Fig 12 :67, :69, :92-93).
3. **Anchor supplied.** An instrument is exhibited whose gauge-invariant readings differ between a fundamental gauge field and an effective one at the same level (closes F6's gap and moves the claim toward `grounded`).
4. **Last level.** A room or level is exhibited beyond which nothing composes, giving an absolute bottom or top (breaks F3, Fig 12 :104, Fig 4 :33).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.
