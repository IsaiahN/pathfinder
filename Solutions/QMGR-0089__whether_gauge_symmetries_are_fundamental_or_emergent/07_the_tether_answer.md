# The Tether's answer: fundamental or emergent gauge symmetry?

**Mode: `specified` on the applied claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers two options: a gauge symmetry is a primitive of the description (F), or it is a derived effective structure (E). Each leads somewhere different. F requires an atom that composition cannot produce and a provenance for importing it; E requires a composition that cannot add an atom and a scale above which the symmetry dissolves. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The two conditions must be kept separate, never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Symmetry is not a primitive.** Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* And Fig 13 :43-44: *"A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."* So "fundamental in the sense of substrate member" is refused for symmetry. This is not a claim that gauge fields are unreal.

2. **Gauge equivalence is identity of descriptions.** Fig 12 :67: *"two names, one referent"*; Fig 12 :69: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* Two gauge-related configurations are spellings of one physical state. Fig 12 :92-93 explains the cost: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges."* The gauge overcount is a pricing fact, not new physics.

3. **Irreducibility is level-relative.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*. Fig 4 :33: *"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."* A symmetry can be emergent at one level and an atom at the next. F and E are not contradictory; the binary is a level error. They are not isomers at one level; they are the same term under a level crossing (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1).

4. **Composition adds arrangements, not atoms.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* E adds no primitive. F would require an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :14, Fig 8 :9-13), which most presentations of the gauge principle do not supply.

5. **Noether ties symmetry to the states.** Fig 13 :62: *"no spatial symmetry for Noether, and no region to make isolated mean anything"*; Fig 13 :65: *"no time-translation symmetry, and no finite operations to leave a remainder"*. Remove the states and the symmetries go with them. Symmetry sits downstream of the six, not above them.

6. **The same-level question is a `gap`.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A gauge-invariant instrument returns the same reading under F and E at one level, by the definition of gauge equivalence. So no anchor discriminates them there. Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.

7. **There is one empirically settled part.** In many-body systems, gauge descriptions do dissolve above a scale; this was read in Bass (2021) and belongs to D3 alone. It corroborates level-relative symmetry but does not decide same-level F versus E.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that gauge fields are unreal, redundant in the dismissive sense, or dispensable in physics. The claim is about the vocabulary's floor and the level-relative status of terms.
- Not that any gauge group is basic. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the Aharonov-Bohm effect or the Higgs mechanism settles the question. Both are readings that gauge equivalence already accommodates.
- Not a derivation of one option from the other, and no new primitive (F4).
- Not `grounded`. No instrument in this folder discriminates F from E at one level, and no measurement settles the bottom of the six-term set.
- Not that consensus among physicists helps. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The Hard Problem is About Source (opened 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Identity Test.** Does any gauge-invariant observable differ between two representatives of one orbit? If yes, they are two terms and F2 breaks.
- **Dissolution Test.** Does a gauge description disappear above a measured scale in a system where the constituent degrees of freedom are known? This is already answered affirmatively in the many-body cases reported by Bass; it settles level relativity, not the bottom question.
- **Anchor Test.** Is there an instrument whose gauge-invariant reading differs between a primitive gauge field and an effective one at the same level? If yes, F6's gap closes and the claim can move toward `grounded`. If no such instrument can exist in principle, the gap is permanent and the binary should be retired rather than answered.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** derive symmetry as a seventh member; show orbit identity fails; exhibit a last level. Any one refutes part of the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, for level relativity; unreachable as posed for the binary.** The six-term set is a visible guess at the compression axes, not nature's own list. There is no bottom (Fig 12 :104) and no top (Fig 4 :33), so no final level can be named. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* (Canonical README.) The line between "we have not found the discriminator" and "no discriminator can exist" is not decided here; Fig 8 :39 says *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side between fundamental and emergent gauge symmetry, because the figures say the choice is a level error. Symmetry is never a substrate member: it is what a composition leaves behind (Fig 13 :124-125). Gauge equivalence is identity of descriptions, two names for one physical state, so the gauge overcount is a pricing mistake rather than extra physics (Fig 12 :67, :69, :92-93). Irreducibility is a fact about a level and never about the world, with no bottom and no top (Fig 12 :104, Fig 4 :33), so a symmetry can be emergent at one level and an atom at the next. Composition adds arrangements, never atoms (Fig 6 :21); an absolute claim to fundamentality would be an import with no provenance. The one thing the figures cannot supply is a gauge-invariant instrument that reads differently under the two descriptions at the same level, because gauge equivalence is defined as sameness of reading. That is recorded as a gap, not hidden as a silence. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the tests that would close it.
