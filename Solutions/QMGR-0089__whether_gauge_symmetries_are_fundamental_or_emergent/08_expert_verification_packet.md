# Expert verification packet: the Tether on fundamental vs emergent gauge symmetry

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the applied claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to discriminate a primitive gauge field from an effective one at the same level.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A gauge symmetry is an identity relation among alternative descriptions of one physical state, not a new substance. Its status as primitive or derived is a fact about the level of description, not about the world, so "fundamental or emergent" is a level error rather than a binary: a symmetry emergent relative to a constituent level becomes an atom for whatever composes over it at the effective level. Symmetry is never a member of the generating set, gauge equivalence is the identity bond with an overcount priced like k! spellings of one term, composition adds arrangements and never atoms, and there is neither a bottom nor a top level. The same-level discrimination of fundamental from emergent has no gauge-invariant anchor and is reported as a gap. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the tests that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 13

- **Statement:** symmetry is a derived quantity, not a primitive of the six-term substrate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :124-125 — *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* Also Fig 13 :43-44 — *"A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."*
- **What to check:** is the six-term set being presented as a guess at compression axes rather than nature's list? If so, the claim is only that symmetry is downstream of the set, not that gauge fields are unreal.
- **Refuter:** derive symmetry as a seventh member, or derive one of the six from the other five in a way that promotes symmetry into the set.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable by someone who works with gauge orbits

- **Statement:** gauge-related configurations are one physical state, and counting them separately is the flat-formula overcount.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :67 — *"two names, one referent"*; Fig 12 :69 — *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*; Fig 12 :92-93 — *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed."*
- **What to check:** is the substitution test faithful? For a local gauge transformation, does substituting one representative for another change any gauge-invariant observable? And is the overcount the orbit volume rather than an extra degree of freedom? This is the live check: if it fails, the packet does not survive stage 1.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a gauge-invariant observational difference between two representatives of one orbit, or show the overcount is not the group volume.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a physics-facing consequence

- **Statement:** irreducibility is level-relative, so emergent at level n and atom at level n+1 are compatible. Composition adds arrangements, not atoms. No absolute fundamental or emergent verdict is available.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 — *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*; Fig 4 :33 — *"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."*; Fig 6 :21 — *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- **What to check:** does the physics of effective field theories and emergent gauge symmetry in many-body systems match a level-crossing description, where a derived gauge field serves as an atom for the effective theory above it? The many-body evidence is reported by Bass (2021) and is secondary corroboration only.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a last level beyond which nothing composes, or show a composition adding an atom, or show a level at which the symmetry is both primitive and derived under the same record.

### C4 — the stated gap, included so it can be attacked

- **Statement:** no gauge-invariant instrument discriminates F from E at the same level, so that question is a `gap`, not a hidden verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 9 :4-5 — *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument whose gauge-invariant reading differs between a primitive gauge field and an effective one at the same level. If none can exist even in principle, the binary should be retired rather than answered.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Identity Test:** any gauge-invariant observable that differs between two orbit representatives (kills C2).
2. **Dissolution Test:** a measured scale above which a gauge description disappears, in a system whose constituents are known. Already affirmative in the many-body cases reported by Bass; this settles C3's level relativity, not the bottom question.
3. **Anchor Test:** an instrument whose gauge-invariant reading differs between a primitive gauge field and an effective one at the same level (closes C4).
4. **Primitive Promotion:** a symmetry derived as a seventh substrate member (kills C1).
