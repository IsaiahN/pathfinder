# The Tether's answer: can an AI system recognize underdetermination?

**Mode: `specified` for the substantive claims, `general` for the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading. No experiment on a real system was run.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system holds a problem P and information I. The report "P is not fixed by I" is wanted as a reliable capability. The question is a union compound: one condition is whether a candidate answer is reachable from what the system holds; the other is whether the system can certify that the world itself leaves the answer open. Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

## 2. The shape

1. **Yes, relative to a declared library.** A system that can enumerate closure(Γ) can decide reachability before searching. Fig 13 :132-133: *"The second is decidability: given the library as it stands, could this have been reached at all. A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* The output is the effect-term description of what would have to be added, not a name of a primitive.

2. **No, as a world-level verdict from inside.** Depth, genuine absence and a closed channel are indiscernible from within a frame. Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."*

3. **The vocabulary is a prerequisite, not a bonus.** "Not fixed by I" is a negation. Operators table :73-74: *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."* "Underdetermined" is also a comparison (⋛, a threshold relation between constraints and unknowns), so the two operators must both be present and must not be merged.

4. **Detection is per slot.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*. A global uncertainty score can report "determined" while one slot is open.

5. **The sought shape is invisible to the loop.** Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."* The discipline is to write the shape down as an ordinary term and let it be refused, which is what the pre-registered refuter in `01` does.

6. **The honest output is a three-part report:** not reachable in closure(Γ) as declared; here is the effect-term shape of what would settle it; here is what would refute this report. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any current LLM has an enumerable closure(Γ). That is a measured capability claim and no measurement is made here.
- Not that a model's verbalised uncertainty or calibration score is the anchor. Calibration is an instrument reading; the ground is what settles it.
- Not that a failure to find an answer proves absence. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*
- Not that "underdetermined" is a single operator. It is ¬ plus ⋛.
- Not that this derivation is `grounded`. It is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* says an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"* and that the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved the case. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Channel test.** Can an instrument distinguish a closed channel from a converged one while the system is still inside its frame? Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. If yes, F4 and F3 weaken.
- **Metaframe test.** Can a system certify its own limit with a derivation the outside ground pays? Fig 8 :37 says no. If a case is produced, the barrier claim falls.
- **Slot test.** Does a global residual score suffice to locate every open slot? Fig 1 :22 says no. If a case is produced, the detection-resolution claim falls.
- **Decidability test.** Give a real system a declared Γ and ask it to classify candidates as in or out of closure(Γ) before search. Accuracy against an independent derivation would move D1 from `specified` toward `grounded`.

The standing falsifiers from `01` section 4 are copied there in full and were written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached is not the same as unreachable.** The three instruments (SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD) remain distinct. More search at the same reach answers the first; a better instrument answers the second; an import from a frame with a different closure answers the third. Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*

What this derivation reaches is a border, recorded with its lineage. What it does not reach is a measurement of any actual system. The framework itself says it stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* That is where this answer stops.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The question splits in two. For a system that exposes its library of expressible terms, underdetermination relative to that library is decidable before searching: the system can say "this answer is not reachable from what I hold" and describe the kind of thing that would have to be added. That is a shape, not a name. What the system cannot do, from inside, is promote that report into a verdict about the world, because an answer that is genuinely absent, one that is merely far beyond the search budget, and a channel that has quietly stopped delivering evidence all look the same from within. Recognizing underdetermination is also not a single skill: it needs a negation operator to say "not fixed," a comparison to say "too few constraints," and per-slot detection so an open slot is not averaged away. The honest output is a border with a refuter attached, not a confident verdict. The five things that would refute it were written down before this answer, and none of them has been run.
