# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two-stage order below is intact. The stage-1 output must survive being checked on its own, or the offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** A reachability claim about underdetermination that you can refute in a few minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in underdetermination detection research and have one specific output I would like you to check against your own field. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "can an AI recognize when a problem is underdetermined by the available information" is two conditions, not one. On the first condition the answer is yes: a system that can enumerate its own library of expressible terms can decide, before searching, whether a candidate answer is reachable from what it holds. It returns a shape, namely the effect-term description of what would have to be added, not a name. On the second condition the answer is no: the system cannot promote that report to a verdict about the world. Depth (the answer is there but far), genuine absence (the information does not fix it), and a closed channel (nothing is arriving) look the same from inside. This is a claim about the border of what a frame can certify, not a measurement of any particular model.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any current LLM has such an enumerable library; that a failure to find an answer proves absence; that a model's expression of uncertainty is a ground verdict. Those are separate questions and I am not smuggling them in.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A system with a declared library is shown to certify, from inside alone, that a problem is underdetermined in the world rather than merely relative to that library.
> 2. A search that finds nothing is shown to be a proof of absence for a frame that is not sealed.
> 3. Depth and genuine absence are discriminated inside one frame with no imported instrument and no outside ground reading.
> 4. A global score is shown sufficient to locate every open slot of a problem.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them, and I would rather you check the figures than take my word for the reading.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Underdetermination of Scientific Theory, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-underdetermination/
> - Identifiability (the statistical name for the same border): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identifiability
> - Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know, arXiv:2207.05221: https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step loop is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

## Stage 2, opt-in only

Once a recipient has recorded a verdict on stage 1, and only if they ask, offer the canonical figures README and the formula as a reusable method. Their later agreement is **never** pooled as corroboration: Fig 2 :40 says *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
