# Solution BUS-0079

**Question:** How should managers compare the costs of quality failures, safety incidents, delays, and prevention when those costs fall on different parties?

**Source:** business.txt line 104

**Section:** 8. Operations, supply chains, and resilience

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain separators and on the comparison procedure, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. Every substantive file states that this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

Per the formula step "Direction", the sought-for shape is written down and bound before the run so it can accrue a residual like anything else. Here an answer would look like this: not one total with a conversion rate, but a **per-party, per-slot vector** of costs, each kept in its own currency and ranged against that party's own non-updating anchor, with the shared fraction between cost classes left unassigned, with prevention recorded as a reapplicable method rather than a one-occasion replay, and with a failure condition written down in advance. A number that converts a worker's injury into a dollar amount and adds it to a supplier's delay is not that answer. It is the flat form the figures forbid.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No universal dollar value of a safety incident, no single cost-of-quality percentage, and no conversion rate between a lost-time injury and a supplier delay. Those are declared `gap`, not silence and not an invitation to invent a number.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." A list of managers who already agree on one total produces a convention, not a verdict.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
