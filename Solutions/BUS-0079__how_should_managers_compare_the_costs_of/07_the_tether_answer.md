# The Tether's answer on comparing costs that fall on different parties

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators and the comparison procedure, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A manager must choose where prevention money goes. The costs of quality failures, safety incidents, delays and prevention are read by different parties, in different currencies, at different anchors. The accounting frame produces one total from its own report and uses that total to decide, while the party-bearing costs remain unmoved. The effect to explain is that the total looks rigorous and decides a budget, yet a customer's return, a worker's injury and a supplier's delay are still three different numbers that the total cannot range against one ground.

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-57), so one lever leaves the intersections standing.

## 2. The shape

1. **Do not sum across parties.** Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." The money in a prevention budget and the harm in a lost-time injury are different units from different anchors. Report a **per-party vector** instead, with the currency and the anchor named on each line.
2. **Range each party against its own non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." A return, an injury record, a stopped line and a fine do not change because a report says quality is on target. A budget meeting that averages them has replaced the anchors with a room (Fig 2 :35).
3. **The total is frame-internal and cannot score the frame that produces it.** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." An allocation that decides where prevention money goes is a verdict, not a convention.
4. **Unbundle the union before deciding.** Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Describe each condition separately; leave the shared fraction unassigned.
5. **Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.** Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Three reports from one ledger are one frame wearing three names. Read customer, worker, supplier and regulator data from instruments the accounting frame did not author (Fig 10 :52).
6. **Classify prevention as method, failure cost as recording.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 12 :107-110: where the arrangement is not recorded, "every lineage pays again." Prevention spend buys a generator; a failure replay buys a repeat.
7. **Use typed bonds, not only addition.** Fig 12 :112-113: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A budget decision is a threshold ⋛ and an incident reduction is a subtraction −. Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."
8. **No standing dollar value of an injury.** Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A local value for one defined residual is allowed; a standing conversion table is not.
9. **The universal rate is a declared gap.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The figures return a shape, not a universal conversion name.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a safety incident is worthless or beyond comparison. It says a *standing* value across residuals is not licensed; a scope-bounded local value needs a declared anchor and a source-discriminating instrument.
- Not that cost of quality is unimportant. The vector is intended to make more of it visible, not less.
- Not that prevention always dominates. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), so prevention can be a benefit to one party and a delay to another.
- Not a universal percentage, a universal rate, or a number for any specific firm. No firm was measured.
- Not `grounded`. No party anchor was read in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

- **Party-anchor tests.** Can a customer return, a worker injury, a supplier delay and a regulator fine each be read at an instrument that does not update on the manager's own report? If not, F2 fails.
- **Source discrimination.** Does one total return the same number for a fatality and a warranty claim? If an instrument can separate them, a local rate becomes grounded for that scope.
- **Independence test.** Are the party readings drawn from different evidence pools, or from one ledger? If one ledger, they are one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- **Generator test.** Is a given prevention spend a reapplicable method or a replay of one occasion (Fig 4 :25)?
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** a figure line licensing an additive total with a non-updating conversion anchor; a measurement showing two currencies are one at the same anchor; convergence from one instrument at maximum independence; collapse of the method/recording distinction; a partition with assignable shared fraction; an existing per-party progress instrument.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** A firm with its own anchors and instruments can ground a local comparison; the figures do not forbid that. What they return is the shape such a comparison must have, plus a written failure condition. The stop is recorded so it is checkable. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." Indiscernibility is not identity, and a simulated derivation is not a measurement.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a single cost-of-quality total, and it does not claim a universal dollar value for a safety incident. It says the residual is indexed per party, so aggregating across parties throws away the signal that says where to look; that money, harm, time and delay are different currencies that do not add; that each party's cost must range against its own non-updating anchor; that the four classes form a union of overlapping conditions rather than a partition, so one lever leaves the intersections standing; that prevention is a reapplicable method while a failure replay is one occasion; and that a budget decision is a threshold comparison, not a sum. What it offers is the shape any defensible comparison must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it locally. The universal rate stays open until the ground settles it.
