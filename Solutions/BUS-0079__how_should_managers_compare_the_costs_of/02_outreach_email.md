**Subject:** Check one output about comparing failure costs before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when the costs of quality failures, safety incidents, delays and prevention fall on different parties, the figures do not license a single additive total. The residual is indexed per slot, and "aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" (Fig 1 :22). Money in the prevention budget, a worker's lost time, a customer's returned unit and a supplier's delay are in different units, and "a composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add" (Fig 12 :125). What survives is a shape: a per-party vector, each cost in its own currency ranged against that party's own anchor, with the overlap between cost classes left unassigned, and with the difference between prevention as a reapplicable method and failure cost as a one-occasion recording made explicit.
>
> The four classes form a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition, so one lever (spend more on prevention) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57). No universal conversion rate is returned. That is a declared `gap`, not silence and not an invitation to invent a number.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a safety incident has a standing dollar value, that the correct cost-of-quality percentage exists, that a variance threshold is a witness for which party's cost caused an outcome, or that the figures settle which local number your firm should use. A local, scope-bounded conversion may be legitimate if its anchor is declared and an instrument discriminates its source. Promoting it to a universal rate is the step I am refusing.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a figure line that licenses a single additive total across parties with a non-updating anchor for the conversion;
> 2. a measurement showing one currency's reading determines another's at the same anchor with no information loss;
> 3. all parties' readings shown to come from one instrument and still triangulate at maximum independence;
> 4. a prevention spend shown to be a replay of one past success rather than a reapplicable method.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1.** Send the separator claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence, so their stage-1 verdict must be recorded first and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Stage 3 sends a generator, which is permitted by Fig 4 :22, but it still needs a ground per problem.
