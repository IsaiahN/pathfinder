# Are two cost regimes isomers? Bonds, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the business application, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

A manager choosing between a prevention-heavy regime and a failure-heavy regime over the same budget is told they are choosing "how much to spend on quality." Two questions are owed: (1) are the two regimes **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what is the correct order of operations for a comparison that includes a refusal and a threshold?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the management frame: money, time, defect counts, injury counts, return counts, return rates, test results, schedules, fines, standards. Bonds held: the seven, including `+` conjunction, `⋛` comparison, `→` sequence, `−` subtraction, and the unary `¬` negation. No universal conversion primitive is available without import. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming money or injury counts? CORRECTION TO MY OWN `05` SECTIONS 3 AND 4

- The figures README: "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The subject is a missing primitive, one not held in the library.
- Money, time, defect counts, injury counts and schedules are already held terms in a management or operations frame. None is missing.
- Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." This governs the absent conversion atom, not the selection among held terms.
- ⇒ Naming money or an injury count is a **binding** from the business frame onto a held atom. What is forbidden is inventing a universal conversion term and claiming it was derived. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." It is also forbidden to claim the binding moves the wall.
- **I overstated this earlier.** "The framework returns a shape, not a name" governs missing primitives, not the selection of atoms already in the library. Retracted as applied to held cost terms; kept visible. Triangulated (README + Fig 6 + Fig 13).

### Q2 Are a prevention-heavy and a failure-heavy regime isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Over the fixed atom set `{money, time, defect counts, injury counts, return counts, schedules, standards}`, two regimes that spend the same total arrange the atoms differently: one binds money to tests and training (`money → appraisal → fewer defects`), the other binds money to rework and warranty (`money → internal failure → external failure`).
- As a **single total** they are indistinguishable: same atoms, same sum. The difference is carried entirely by the bond, which is who bears the cost and at which anchor. By Fig 12 :117 they are isomers, and a report that records only the sum has recorded half of itself.
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing; they are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either regime can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, a regime with no defect count, or one with no injury count because the hazard was designed out entirely), the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import or subtraction instead. Record what would be introduced or dropped, not just what is rearranged.

### Q3 What bond actually holds between the cost classes?

- Operators table :14-15 for `+` conjunction: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." A total built only by `+` treats a prevention spend and a worker injury as interchangeable operands. They are not, because swapping who bears them changes the term (Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing.").
- Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities". Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." A budget decision is a comparison ⋛ at a threshold, not a sum.
- Operators table :46: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is." A reduction in incidents or returns is a subtraction −, not a conjunction.
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A cost report with only `+` can say what was spent and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere for each party.
- **Live type strain (S3 from `01`):** ⋛ is defined over "two quantities." A money amount and an injury count are not obviously two quantities at the same level. Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom at the next level, but whether a harm reading counts as a quantity there needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not smoothed.

### Q4 What order is the comparison?

- Formula step 2 routes a REFUTED residual before a mint: "a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation." Fig 5 :53: "refused: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". The ordinary cost-of-quality total is the held term to refuse here, not the thing to repair. Refuse it first (¬), then offer the per-party vector (a mint that composes from held terms).
- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Without ¬, the accounting frame can only add another cost category to the total, never withdraw the total's standing to decide.
- Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A manager can introduce a new measure and cannot subtract the habitat of existing obligations. So the vector is an addition to the report, and the total loses its standing as a verdict by refusal, not by deletion.
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The condensation from the full figure set to "report a vector" drops the typed bond, the non-updating anchor and the source-discriminating instrument; that is R_T and it is nonzero.

## CONCLUSION

A prevention-heavy and a failure-heavy regime over the same atom set are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117) whose difference is invisible in a single total. The bond that distinguishes them is typed and ordered: a goal is a comparison ⋛, progress is a subtraction −, a refusal is a negation ¬, and only the inventory is a conjunction +. The correct order is refuse the universal total, then introduce the per-party vector, then set a threshold per party at its own anchor. What survives is the isomer and bond analysis; what does not survive is any claim that the figures choose the dollar amount or name the conversion atom. The ⋛ operand type at the business level remains a live strain.
