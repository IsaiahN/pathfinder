## QUESTION

Why do successful companies often struggle to respond to technologies that later transform their industries?

Restated as an effect: why does `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` stay small for years in some firm-slots and grow quickly after a technology changes what the market pays for? R is indexed per firm and per time, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Anchor: realized market payment and survival, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29. Option A: answer with a list of firm attributes or a named technology. Option B: answer with the conditions that keep R small across a habitat shift, keep their failures separate, and declare what no instrument reaches. Each leads somewhere different: A averages across slots and names a source from a projection; B gives a checkable shape with refuters and an open slot.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A technology transforms the habitat, not the ground. A successful firm keeps its residual low when C1 (channel to what pays), C2 (reach renewed by a differing closure) and C3 (vocabulary and objective for the changed situation) hold, and loses it quickly when any one fails; the capability that once helped can change valence when the residual changes. The three conditions are isomers over a shared atom set and must be kept separate, never averaged. Which technology wins and whether success caused the failure rather than selected for it are declared `gap`s. Mode `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural shape. The disruption and decline literature corroborates the shape but mostly shares one evidence pool, so it is one witness; ecology and cognition corroborate the mechanism shape from lower-overlap fields. This is a candidate shape, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A firm failing C2 would be sold customer-research instruments, or a firm failing C1 would be told to import more, and one lever would be applied to a union that needs three. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right one. The deeper cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its pre-registered falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet without withdrawing any primitive.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A prospective per-firm study that fixes habitat exposure would close the causal gap; a per-firm instrument that discriminates contact from reach from vocabulary failure would close F10; a firm that renews reach with no import across a habitat change would break idempotence (Fig 8 :32); a capability whose measured valence never flips would weaken the valence claim; a technology shift with total payment collapsing to zero would break the habitat reading. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
