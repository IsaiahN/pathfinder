**Subject:** Check one output about incumbent response to new technology before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know about disruption and firm decline. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** take a firm whose prediction of what the market will pay has stayed close to what the market actually returns for years. When a new technology changes what the market pays for, that closeness usually breaks. The derivation says it breaks when any one of three conditions fails, and the conditions are different compounds that need different instruments, not degrees of one problem:
>
> 1. **Contact:** the firm's channel to what pays stops being reachable. The market did not stop paying; the firm's readings did. The named decay modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings, where "pooled" means averaging over the firm's most profitable customers so the new signal disappears into an average.
> 2. **Reach:** the firm keeps composing inside its own library. Composition inside a closed set cannot add an atom, however long the search runs, so the atom the new market pays for has to come from a frame whose closure differs, typically an entrant. The technology can even be invented inside the incumbent and commercialised elsewhere; what crosses is the import, not the recording.
> 3. **Vocabulary and objective:** at the moment of the shift the firm has no terms for what the new situation affords and cannot say what would count as progress there. More planning budget lands above that break, where the measurements already exist.
>
> A fourth claim follows from the first three: the capability that produced the success can change sign when the residual changes, without anything about the capability changing. That is the mechanism shape behind "success trap" and "core rigidity", stated in effect terms rather than as a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can tell you which technology will win, that past success caused the failure rather than selected for firms exposed to the change, that any named routine is the answer, or that this is settled by measurement. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The first two are declared gaps in the packet, not oversights. The verdict is `specified`, and no market measurement pays for it here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a firm's low residual is unchanged after the market stops paying for what it does;
> 2. a transformation in which total payment collapses to zero rather than shifting basis, with the firm's low residual intact;
> 3. a firm loses its low residual while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining;
> 4. a firm keeps the same closure for two decades and keeps producing ground-verified new readings with no import;
> 5. a capability's measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual;
> 6. a firm with perception, vocabulary and objective all measuring as working fails at planning;
> 7. one lever restores all three conditions in every failure type and leaves no categorically different residue;
> 8. a prospective study that fixes habitat exposure removes the success-to-failure association.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
