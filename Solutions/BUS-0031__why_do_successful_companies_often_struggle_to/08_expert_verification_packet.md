# Expert verification packet : the Tether on incumbent response to transformative technology

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on disruptive innovation or organizational decline can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which technology wins and on cause versus selection. Not `grounded`.** No market-ground measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A transformative technology changes the firm's habitat, not the ground that settles whether the market pays. A successful firm keeps its residual low when three conditions hold: its channel to what pays stays reachable; its reach is renewed by contact with a closure that differs from its own; and it has terms for what the changed situation affords plus a way to say what progress there would count as. These are three compounds, not degrees of one problem, and over a shared atom set they are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, so a blended "innovation capability" score cannot tell their failures apart. The capability that produced the success can change valence when the residual changes without anything about the capability changing. Which technology wins, and whether success caused the failure rather than selected for it, are declared gaps, not hidden answers.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the new technology is a habitat actor with a cascade; it is not a change in the ground. The ground, realized payment and survival, does not move because a frame thinks about it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :73 *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Also Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades."* and Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** is the habitat / ground distinction drawn correctly for an industry transformation? Does the market keep paying while the basis of payment shifts?
- **Refuter:** a transformation in which total market payment collapses to zero rather than shifting basis, with the firm's low residual intact. That is ground loss, and this claim does not describe it.

### C2 : framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figure 12 and Figure 8

- **Statement:** C1 (contact), C2 (reach renewal) and C3 (vocabulary / objective) are isomers over the shared atom set {firm loop, habitat, library, channel}; they share atoms and differ in the charged bond, so the difference is invisible in a flat formula and decisive in behaviour. Renewal of reach specifically requires an import from a differing closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **Refuter:** any one condition can be written without an atom the others require (which kills the isomer claim and leaves only the functional-group claim, Fig 12 :122); or a firm renews its reach purely by internal composition with no new atom and still passes the ground test across a habitat change (which breaks idempotence).

### C3 : business-facing; checkable by a researcher against the disruption and decline literature

- **Statement:** the literature's "success trap", "competency trap", "core rigidity", "threat-rigidity" and "lock-in" findings are different names for the same valence flip, and they do not discriminate the three conditions. The literature supplies the effect description but not the instrument that separates them.
- **Sources read:** Bower and Christensen 1995, *Harvard Business Review*: *"they fail - not because they make the wrong decisions, but because they make the right decisions for circumstances that are about to become history."* March 1991, *Organization Science*: *"adaptive processes, by refining exploitation more rapidly than exploration, are likely to become effective in the short run but self-destructive in the long run."* Levitt and March 1988, *Annual Review of Sociology*: *"a competency trap can occur when favorable performance with an inferior procedure leads an organization to accumulate more experience with it."* Sydow, Schreyögg and Koch 2020, *Academy of Management Review*: *"a lock-in and its accompanying path-dependent capabilities can be fatal."* Lower-overlap witness: MathisonSlee et al. 2022, *Ecology and Society*: *"a 'rigidity trap,' where a system stuck in one phase of the adaptive cycle becomes highly connected, rigid, and inflexible."*
- **What to check:** is each characterization faithful? Is there a source in this field that already supplies the three-way discriminator? If so, the declared gap F10 is already closed and should be credited.
- **Refuter:** a source with a genuinely different evidence pool that prospectively and per firm predicts which named condition will fail, or that shows the three conditions are degrees of one variable rather than categorically different compounds.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of disruption and not a replacement for the disruption literature. The figures constrain a shape; they do not settle the business question.
- Not a prediction of which technology wins or which incumbent survives. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that success caused the failure. Habitat exposure may select for it. This is a `gap`, not an omission.
- Not that the disruptive technology was absent inside the incumbent. Often it was present; what failed to cross was the import into the firm's reach.
- Not that market consensus, analyst ratings or the firm's own revenue metrics count as the ground. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a market measurement.
- Not a claim that the disruption literature independently confirms the figures. Most of it shares one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*).

The residual and its refuter first, not the framework's interiors. C1 to C3 and their refuters are checkable from the figure text and from public literature. The full derivation in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is offered only after a verdict on these three is recorded, because Fig 10 :42 says *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :73, Fig 8 :32 and Fig 12 :117, three short passages.
2. Check C3 against their own knowledge of the disruption and decline literature: are the four characterizations faithful, and is any of them already a three-way discriminator?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate contact failure from reach failure in a named firm, or say why none is available. That answer closes or confirms gap F10.
4. Say whether past success caused later failure or selected for it in the cases they know, and what would discriminate those readings. That answer bears on gap F11.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
