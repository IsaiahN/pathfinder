## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What kind of bond is "transform"?

- Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Operators table :48 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and :51 *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- Fig 12 :74 *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* for the ordered bonds.

"Transform the industry" is not `+` (both hold at once) or `≡` (two names, one referent). It is `⋛`: a threshold at which the old arrangement no longer pays and the new one does. It is also embedded in a `→` sequence: the firm's old low residual comes first, the habitat shift fires, the new residual arrives. Swapping the order produces a different thing, so the sequence bond is correctly ordered. **Reading:** the technology is a threshold event in a sequence, not a substance that fuses with the firm. **Falsifier:** a case in which the industry transformation is demonstrably symmetric and orderless, that is, the old and new payment bases coexist with no threshold and no before/after. Then `⋛` and `→` are both misapplied and this derivation fails.

### Q2 : Are C1, C2 and C3 isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :122 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."

Write each condition over the same atom set `{firm loop, habitat, library, channel}`:

| condition | bond that binds the atoms | substance |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | channel `→` ground payment; the bracket no longer matches the residual | lost touch |
| C2 | library `→` differing closure `→` imported atom | wall moved |
| C3 | vocabulary `→` affordance; objective `⋛` progress | middle link unread |

All three use the same four atoms and differ only in which bond is charged and which atom mediates. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, and the difference is invisible in a flat "innovation capability" formula. That is the structural reason a single blended score cannot tell them apart.

The weaker reading is also worth recording. If the question is stated as it usually is in the literature, with only "the firm" common to all three and no explicit library or channel atom, the conditions share a **functional group** (a firm in a habitat) with different qualifiers (Fig 12 :122): *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The isomer claim is the stronger one and is the test worth running.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any condition can be written without an atom the other two require, for example C1 with no capability library or C3 with no instrument channel, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands. Mode `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q3 : Why the order is dependency order, not importance order

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Formula step 2: the REFUTED bin says *"what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*, and the first four bins sort by what is missing while the fifth sorts by what is wrong.
- Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

The order for a check is:

1. **Describe the residual** in effect terms before naming a condition or a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise any of the three will seem to fit.
2. **Refuse before mint.** If the firm holds a false term (for example "our best customers define the market"), that is a REFUTED bin and owes `¬`, not a new routine. The formula's fifth bin cannot be reached by rearranging the other four.
3. **Perception then vocabulary then objective then planning then learn and carry** (Fig 3 :4-16). C3 sits at links 2 and 3, which is where the instrument is missing.
4. **Settle the first-order loop before composing the second-order one.** Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A firm's first-order routine must have paid against the old ground before it becomes an operand at the new level.
5. **Keep C1, C2 and C3 separate when the verdict is stated.** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

**Falsifier:** a documented case in which a firm is refuted at step 2 yet makes progress by minting a new routine first, with no refusal recorded. That would break the order claim without breaking the dependency chain itself.

### Q4 : Is success a cause or a selector?

- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" and :31 "rather than the cause".

The observed association is a compound of two conditions: mechanism exposure (success builds rigidity) and habitat exposure (successful firms are the ones that had grown large in the old habitat). A case in both is not a harder case of either. Fig 9 forbids naming the cause from the effect description, so this stays a `gap` (F11). **Falsifier:** a prospective design that fixes habitat exposure and retains the association (mechanism), or removes exposure and loses it (selection). Either closes the gap.

## CONCLUSION

"Transform the industry" is a `⋛` threshold inside a `→` sequence, not a symmetric fusion. The three conditions are **isomers over a shared atom set** under Fig 12 :117, which is why a single blended innovation score cannot discriminate them; in the weaker literature form they are a functional group with different qualifiers (Fig 12 :122). Their check order is dependency order (Fig 3), with refusal before mint and residual description before frame choice. Success as cause versus success as selector remains a declared union gap. The isomer claim and the order claim are `specified` and unmeasured.
