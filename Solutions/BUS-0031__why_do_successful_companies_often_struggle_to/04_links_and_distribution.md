## Four things in the literature that bear directly on the claim

1. **Bower and Christensen 1995 supplies the effect description, not a derivation.** *"customers wield extraordinary power in directing a company's investments"* is the habitat-contact condition C1 stated in management vocabulary. The paper's prescription to put disruptive technology in an independent organisation is the figure-derived C2 remedy (a differing closure), but the prescription is a world-source binding, not a figure line. It must be recorded as such.
2. **March 1991 and Levitt and March 1988 name the success-trap mechanism but not the instrument.** March's *"self-destructive in the long run"* and Levitt and March's competency trap match Fig 12 :119 and Fig 11 :50-51. Neither paper supplies the discriminator that would separate C1, C2 and C3 failure in a named firm, which is the gap this packet offers.
3. **The literature largely shares one evidence pool.** Bower, Christensen, March, Levitt, Rumelt, Wang, D'Aveni, Cameron and Sydow cite one another across the same strategy and organisation journals. Under Fig 2 :31 their agreement is one witness, not many. Ecology (MathisonSlee 2022) and cognition (Dane 2010, Lieberman 2023) are the lower-overlap corroborations of the mechanism shape.
4. **None of it settles causality.** The observed association is compatible with success causing rigidity and with success selecting firms late in a habitat cycle. That is the declared gap F11, and no source opened here closes it.
