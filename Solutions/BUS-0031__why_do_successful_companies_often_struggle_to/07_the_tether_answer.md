# The Tether's answer: why successful firms struggle with transformative technology

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which technology wins and on cause versus selection. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm whose prediction of what the market will pay has matched the market's return for years loses that match after a technology changes what the market pays for. R is indexed per firm and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The question is a **union** of three conditions and one declared gap, not one hard problem: Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

## 2. The shape

1. **The technology changes the habitat, not the ground.** Success was a low residual against the old habitat. Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." The market still pays; it pays for something else. The old capability's valence changed with the residual.
2. **C1, contact can be lost while the ground stays intact.** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" and Fig 10 :26 "saturated metrics, pooled readings". The incumbent listens to the customers who pay now, so the signal from the new market is a dropped transient. Fig 7 :39 "the ground's pressure on the room" is the second factor: when the bracket stops matching the residual, the chain runs in place.
3. **C2, reach must be renewed from a differing closure.** Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." The atom the new market pays for is typically already held by an entrant; for the incumbent it is an import, gated by provenance and a shadow test. The technology may have been invented inside the incumbent and commercialised outside, because what crosses is the import, not the recording.
4. **C3, the break is usually at the middle link.** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The firm has no terms for what the changed situation affords and cannot say what would count as progress there. Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
5. **The strength changes valence.** Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Nothing about the routine has to change for it to become a rigidity.
6. **The three conditions are isomers over a shared atom set.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." A blended "innovation capability" score cannot tell them apart, which is why C1, C2 and C3 are never averaged.
7. **Success does not hand itself a different room.** Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." A firm that keeps appointing the same kind of leader and running the same review is booking a room like the last one, which is how a chain stops travelling.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a name for the winning technology, not a roster of winning routines, and not a forecast. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that past success **caused** the failure. It may have selected firms that had grown large in the old habitat. That is a declared `gap` (F11); Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" and :31 "rather than the cause".
- Not that the disruption literature confirms the figures. It mostly shares one evidence pool with the question it would check (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- Not that the firm's own success metrics are evidence. Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- Not that the market stopped paying. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- Not `grounded`. No market-ground measurement pays for any of this here.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Contact test:** does the firm's bracket fidelity (Fig 7 :38) and the ground's pressure (Fig 7 :39) remain high and non-declining through the loss? If both do, C1 is not the mechanism.
- **Reach test:** does the firm's reach stop moving while its library keeps growing (Fig 6 :21), with no atom entering from a differing closure? If reach keeps moving with no import, idempotence fails.
- **Vocabulary test:** do perception, vocabulary and objective all measure as working while failure sits at planning? If so, F7 is false.
- **Valence test:** does a named capability's measured valence flip across a documented change of residual? If it never does, F6 weakens to `general`.
- **Causal test:** a prospective, per-firm design that fixes habitat exposure and retains the success-to-failure association (mechanism) or removes exposure and loses it (selection). This closes F11.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the full pre-registered list in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The map of conditions is a shape, not a name, and the instrument that would discriminate C1 from C2 from C3 in a named firm is not held here. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Indiscernibility is not identity: if no available instrument distinguishes the three compounds, they remain three compounds and the lineage must record which one a verdict was for. The essays add that a *"minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*, so no firm-level claim is being smuggled in through the physical substrate.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the technology that will win, and it does not claim that success causes failure. It says that a technology transforms a firm's habitat, not the ground that settles whether the market pays; that a firm loses its low residual when its channel to what pays degrades, when its reach stops being renewed by contact with a closure that differs from its own, or when it has no terms for what the changed situation affords and no way to say what progress there would count as; and that these three failures are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, so a single blended "innovation score" cannot tell them apart. The capability that once helped can change sign without changing, because valence is relative to the residual. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the instruments that would discriminate the three compounds, and falsifiers written in advance. Which technology wins, and whether success caused the failure rather than selected for it, stay open until the ground settles them.
