## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *value* of the Hubble constant but the *status of one inferred slot* in a model-dependent prediction. In effect terms: the early-universe library Γ is ΛCDM plus a set of assumptions b; the action a is measuring the CMB acoustic scale; Γ(b,a) predicts that scale from a sound horizon r_s and an expansion history; o′ is the observed acoustic scale. The fit is excellent, so the global residual is near zero, yet the H0 slot inferred from that fit disagrees with the later, more direct measurement. That is a live single-slot residual, which Fig 1 :13 calls *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"* and Fig 1 :19 calls *"indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

- Option A, "the inferred rate is fixed by the data and new physics cannot move it": leads to the prediction that no change of the early-universe closure can alter H0, and that the residual must be a calibration error or a fluctuation.
- Option B, "the inferred rate is closure-dependent and an imported early-universe term can move it": leads to the prediction that a new component which reduces the sound horizon r_s permits a higher H0 under the same acoustic-scale constraint, and that the question is settled by independent ground readings, not by how many models fit the CMB.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* stating (i) that H0 inferred from the CMB is a function of the assumed closure, (ii) what any new-physics import must do to that closure to shift H0, (iii) which independent instruments would discriminate a real shift from a systematic, and (iv) a refuter that fires before any model is named. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, with the crucial qualification that the framework returns a shape and a route, not a name and not a measurement: new physics *can* shift the inferred rate in principle because that rate is closure-dependent, but no specific proposal is ground-settled, and the honest answer is the shape any viable import must have plus the instrument that would settle it. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the Hubble tension, no endorsement of a named model such as early dark energy, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of cosmology.

Four figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), and the cost here is a wrong atom in the standard model, which Fig 6 :24 prices *"more than starting from primitives."*
5. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
