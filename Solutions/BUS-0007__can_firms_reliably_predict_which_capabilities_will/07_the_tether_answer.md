# The Tether's answer on predicting which capabilities will matter

**Mode: `specified` for firms, `general` for reachability. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

At decision time t a firm bets on capabilities; at t+Δ the future market pays or does not. The residual is whether that gap can be closed in advance. The question's own "predict or adapt" framing is a union masquerading as a disjunction (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). It has its own loop and its own ground, and its two conditions need different instruments.

## 2. The shape

1. **The specific capability is unreachable at decision time.** Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* At t the firm cannot tell whether the capability is in Γ and unfound or genuinely absent. Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Verdict: `unreachable` at t, not silence and not proof that foresight is worthless (Fig 13 :134, *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*).
2. **What is reachable is a shape, not a name.** Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* The shape: a capability whose valence survives a change in what the market rewards, reached and renewed through contact with a closure that differs. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
3. **The capability's value is a relation, not a property.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* The current capability description does not record whether it regenerates when the residual changes.
4. **Reliable strategy keeps the union's two conditions separate.** C1: reach the shape before the market settles. C2: maintain the channel and keep moving reach outward so the firm can rebind, refit, or import when it settles. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* A firm failing C1 and a firm failing C2 are not degrees of one failure (Fig 9 :57).
5. **Foresight quality is selection between rooms, not agreement within one.** Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :37-39 give the two factors: bracket fidelity and ground pressure. Zero either and the chain runs in place.
6. **A forecast carries a round-trip gap.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31), not an error to eliminate.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that firms never forecast well, and not that experts are useless. Tetlock's own caveat is recorded in `09_watson_answer.md`.
- Not that dynamic capabilities theory names the winning capability. Teece's own words: *"dynamic capabilities theory is not tightly predictive."*
- Not a single lever. "Invest more in foresight" clears one union condition and leaves the intersection standing.
- Not a `grounded` answer. No market at t+Δ has paid for this shape here.
- Not that the future is unknowable in general. The verdict is limited to the name at decision time under the closure the firm holds; importing against a real budget limit is a different question (Fig 8 :40-43).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads a value *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the analogue. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Prospective name test:** a per-firm prediction issued at t that names the capability, carries a source gate (where the atom came from) and a shadow test (which gap it explains), confirmed by the market at t+Δ across repeated slots.
- **Valence test:** does the capability's measured value change sign when the residual changes? An instrument that discriminates a regenerating catalyst from a spent part (Fig 12 :119) would close the F6 gap.
- **Closure test:** can a firm renew reach across a habitat change with no new atom entering from a differing closure? If yes, idempotence fails and F7 falls.
- **Round-trip test:** measure R_T for a forecast carried from t to t+Δ and back. If it is zero across repeated slots with changed valence, F8 falls.
- Standing refuters from `01` section 4: seven, including a single lever that restores both conditions and leaves no categorically different residue.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The missing thing is a discriminator that would tell, at t, depth from genuine absence and a regenerating capability from a spent one. It is characterized in effect terms, not held (Fig 6 :34: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."*). The answer stops where the instruments can no longer distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity. Which named capability will win is a `gap` in the precise sense of Fig 13 :134, not a hidden answer.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that firms cannot forecast, and it does not name the capability that will win. It says that at the moment of the bet the firm cannot tell whether that capability is already in its library and merely unfound or genuinely absent and owing from outside, so the specific name is unreachable; what is reachable is a shape, a capability whose value survives a change in what the market rewards. The question "predict or adapt" is therefore a union of two conditions with different instruments: reach the shape before the market settles, and keep the channel and the reach alive so the firm can rebind, refit, or import when it does. A same-closure scenario process is a mirror chain, and a forecast carries a round-trip gap that must be measured rather than hidden. What is offered is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close the stop. The market at t+Δ decides.
