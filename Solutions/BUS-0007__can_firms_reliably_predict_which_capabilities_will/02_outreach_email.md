**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survives the check below. The output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one claim about predicting future capabilities before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about strategic foresight and dynamic capabilities. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` for firms, `general` for the reachability structure).** At the moment a firm chooses which capabilities to build, it cannot tell whether the capability that will matter in the future market is already in its library and merely unfound, or genuinely absent from that library and owing from outside. From inside the library the two look the same. Internal analysis composes only inside the closure it starts in, so no amount of it adds the missing capability. What is reachable at that moment is a **shape**: a capability whose value survives a change in what the market rewards, reached and renewed through contact with parties whose way of seeing the market differs from the firm's own. The specific name is not reachable. This is a verdict of `unreachable` at decision time, not a claim that foresight is useless and not silence.
>
> The practical consequence is that "predict or adapt" is a badly posed either/or. The two conditions need different instruments: reaching the shape before the market settles, and keeping the channel and the reach alive so the firm can rebind, refit, or import when it does. A single lever, for example "do more scenario planning", clears the first failure and leaves the second standing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that scenario planning is worthless, that experts never forecast well, that dynamic capabilities theory settles which capability wins, or that the firm should stop investing in foresight. I am not naming a winning capability. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The business literature I have read is one evidence pool, so its agreement is one witness, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. A prospective, per-firm prediction issued before the market settles that names the capability, shows where the idea came from and which gap it explains, and is confirmed across repeated cases.
> 2. A firm that renews its capabilities purely from inside its own existing knowledge across a real change of market and still passes the market's test.
> 3. A capability whose measured value stays the same through a documented change in what the market rewards.
>
> I wrote these down before writing this email. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this package's project, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
