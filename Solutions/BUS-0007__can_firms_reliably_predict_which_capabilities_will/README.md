## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether firms ever guess right, but whether the specific capability that will matter at a future market o′(t+Δ) is reachable from the firm's library Γ at decision time t. In effect terms:

- **b** is the firm's belief, at t, about which capabilities will be rewarded at t+Δ.
- **a** is the capability investment or build action taken now.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library predicts the future market will return.
- **o′(t+Δ)** is what the future market actually pays.
- **s** is the firm and the capability slot. R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is indexed per firm-slot and per future time, never averaged across firms (Fig 1 :19).

- Option A, "foresight predicts the capability": the capability that will matter is already in closure(Γ) at t and can be named by search, scenario work, or analysis. This leads to committing resources now on the strength of the prediction.
- Option B, "strategy must adapt": the specific capability is unreachable at t, because from inside Γ you cannot tell whether it is in the closure and unfound or genuinely absent. Only a shape is reachable. Reliable strategy maintains the channel to the ground and keeps moving reach outward through contact with closures that differ, so that when o′ arrives the firm can rebind, refit, or import (Fig 6 :27).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): (1) a figure-governed verdict on the reachability of the specific capability at decision time, (2) the shape that is reachable instead, and (3) an instrument that would discriminate a failure of foresight from a failure of adaptation. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The two branches are a union, not a clean either/or (Fig 9 :55).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the Tether is the only true account of strategic foresight.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable foresight practitioners manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). The strategy and foresight literature is one pool; its agreement is one witness.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
