# Structure, order and analogues for BUS-0007

**Mode: `specified` for the firm application, `general` for the structural claims.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether a capability name can be obtained at t or only after t+Δ. Supporting questions are owed: (1) what structure distinguishes two firms that hold the same capabilities, (2) what order does the forecast-to-settlement relation carry, (3) what do the two reading currencies (bits and actions) price, and (4) which cross-scale analogue is licensed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in D3: routines, know-how and assets as the library's primitives. Bonds held: the seven, including sequence (`→`) and comparison (`⋛`). No new atom is available without import from a differing closure. The time crossing between t and t+Δ is a membrane (Fig 4), not a same-scale composition.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two firms with the same capability set isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Two firms holding the same capability atoms but arranging them differently (one senses and reconfigures through contact with a differing closure, the other merely lists them) are **isomers**, not variants of one thing. An capability-attribute list records the ingredients and not the arrangement, so it cannot tell them apart. This is why a capability inventory is not a forecast: the behaviour difference is carried by the bond.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a capability list with only the atoms and no recorded arrangement predicts future market performance as well as the bonded arrangement, the isomer claim fails and the flat-form reading stands.

### Q2 - What order does the forecast carry?

- The bet-before-settlement relation is a **sequence**: `→`, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). Reversing it produces a different thing: adapting before the market has changed is not the same act as adapting after it.
- "Which capability will matter" is a **comparison**: Operators table :51, *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*; Fig 12 :87, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. A forecast that only lists capabilities has no goal, because a goal is a comparison it cannot state until o′ arrives.
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** A firm can hold a plan (sequence) and a capability list (ingredients) and still lack the comparison that would make progress sayable at t. That is the middle-link break of Fig 3 :24.
- **Falsifier:** a firm that states a falsifiable comparison for what will count as progress in the future market at t using only atoms it already holds, and is confirmed at t+Δ. That would show the comparison was reachable and narrow F3.

### Q3 - What do the two currencies price?

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126-127: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."*
- A scenario set is cheap to state (bits) and may take a whole budget to settle (actions). A single capability bet can be cheap to describe and expensive to reverse. A firm that reports only the elegance of its scenarios is holding one accounting. Fig 12 :128-129: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."*
- **Falsifier:** a single forecast metric that prices both the description and the settlement actions in one unit without losing the shortfall the figure names. That would collapse the two-currency claim.

### Q4 - Which cross-scale analogue is licensed?

- Tetlock's individual forecasters and a firm's foresight unit are the same kernel at two sizes. Fig 4 :20: **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** At the individual scale the currency is calibration; at the firm scale it is market payment. The anchor must be re-named at the new scale; a scenario score is an instrument at both, not an anchor.
- Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* What crosses from the cognition finding to the firm is the method (treat beliefs as testable hypotheses, keep frames independent), not a recorded forecast.
- Teece's "working hypotheses that can be updated as evidence emerges" (2007) is a business-pool binding to the same method; it carries one witness, not three.
- **Falsifier:** an individual-level forecasting improvement that does not change any firm-level contact yet moves the firm's market-ground result. Under Fig 4 :20 the kernel transfers only with the anchor re-named; if it transfers without that, the analogue needs re-census.

### Q5 - What is the union's order of operations?

- Fig 9 :43: **"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."**
- Applied here: first ask whether the named capability is reachable at all from Γ at t (F2, `unreachable`); then look up which differing closure might hold the atom (Fig 6 :30, *"Cast it in effect terms"*); then, after o′ arrives, eliminate against the ground (Fig 9 :42, *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*).
- Reversing this order, naming the capability before describing the residual, is the apophenia Fig 9 :27-28 warns against: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- **Falsifier:** a case where elimination at the ground before any closure lookup beats the verdict-then-lookup order for the same budget. That would require re-census of the dependency order.

## CONCLUSION

Two firms with the same capabilities are isomers when their bonds differ, so a capability inventory cannot substitute for a forecast (Q1). The forecast carries a sequence and owes a comparison it cannot state until the market settles (Q2). Description bits and settlement actions are two currencies that do not add (Q3). The cognition-to-firm analogue transfers a method and requires the anchor to be re-named (Q4). The union runs verdict, lookup, elimination, never the reverse (Q5). Each result has its falsifier written before the run.
