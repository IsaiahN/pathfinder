# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 have been read and the refuter is intact. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about crisis reform, with its refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about crisis and institutional reform. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A crisis is a registered failure on a specific slot, not a general change in the weather. A global picture that looks stable while one slot is failing is a legal state, so I am asking about the slot, not the headline. I claim the branch after a crisis is a union of three conditions, not one lever:
>
> 1. the failure is registered against something the institution cannot move (a count, an outcome, a court, a market),
> 2. a frame whose closure differs from the incumbent's is available, and something crossed from it with a record of where it came from,
> 3. the channel to that ground is still reachable after the change.
>
> When all three hold, the crisis is an opportunity for reform. When the third fails, the same event is processed inside the incumbent's own vocabulary and strengthens the incumbent, because the ground does not decay, the channel does. I do **not** claim that a crisis has a fixed valence: the same event can break concentrated power in one setting and intensify it in another, depending on the residual it meets.
>
> **What this does not say.** Not that a crisis is necessary for reform. Arrangements over a closed set can change incrementally without a shock. Not that "critical juncture" explains anything: that is a filter for which cases get studied, not a verdict. Not which specific policy works: the framework returns a shape, not a name. Not `grounded`: nothing here was measured.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a crisis produces a ground-verified increase in constraint on power while the incumbent closure is unchanged and no atom from a differing closure crossed;
> 2. the valence of the same event is invariant across a documented change of residual and ground;
> 3. the ground moves when people push on it, yet the arrangement still settles;
> 4. a reform keeps the channel reachable and changes contact and still entrenches power;
> 5. a single lever clears cases where two or three of the three conditions bind and leaves a residue of the same kind.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I have deliberately kept them behind the refuter.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (public repo): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README (usage rule): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The Tether architecture (Medium): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** residual, three conditions, five refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the verdict **before** any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode (Fig 4 :22-25).

One-to-one only. No list ranked by receptiveness, no bulk send, no opt-out-free message. Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it (Fig 10 :60).
