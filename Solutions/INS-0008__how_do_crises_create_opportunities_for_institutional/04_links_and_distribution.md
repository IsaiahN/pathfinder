## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Historical institutionalism (one witness).** Capoccia and Kelemen 2007 reconstruct the critical-juncture concept and warn that not all junctures produce change; its footnote 1 names layering, conversion and drift as alternative models of institutional change. This is the strongest direct corroboration of the union and of the refusal to make crisis necessary.
- **Path dependence (same pool).** Pierson 2000 supplies the increasing-returns mechanism and the "almost impossible to reverse" claim. It shares the historical-institutionalism pool with Capoccia and Kelemen, so the two count as one witness.
- **Political economy (different discipline, overlapping concept).** Acemoglu and Robinson 2012, read through LitCharts, state the double-edged claim: the same disruptive event can open the way to inclusive institutions or intensify extractive ones. Corroborates F3, with the caveat about the study guide.
- **Ecology and social-ecological resilience (low overlap).** The Resilience Alliance's adaptive cycle gives brief windows of experimentation nested inside stabilising hierarchies, which is a structural analogue of both the reform window and the entrenchment ratchet. It corroborates the mechanism shape, not an institutional measurement.
- **Cognitive and intergroup psychology (low overlap).** Tadmor et al. 2026 show threat-induced cognitive rigidity is conditional, not automatic: deliberation plus cognitive readiness can restore integrative complexity. This corroborates the channel claim at the individual stratum, with the anchor renamed per Fig 4 :20.
- **Comparative politics of autocracy (different subfield).** Ong and Cayton 2026 show that winning the reform moment does not suffice; inherited authoritarian institutions are reused by new elites and coalitions fracture. Corroborates Fig 4 :25 (a recording replayed) and the entrenchment branch.

Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40). The Capoccia, Pierson and Acemoglu-Robinson sources share the "critical juncture" concept. Their agreement corroborates the vocabulary, not the verdict. The verdict comes from the figures.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22-25:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the whole framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the three conditions and the refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163:** *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for people already likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 11 :59-61:** stakes are structural. Declare the author's interest in the reading; do not present preference as evidence.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the three conditions and the five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as failure; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then, if they are curious, the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, with the third stage

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. No theory shown. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the theory that produced it. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** use the formula recursively on other problems, each with its own ground, its own pre-registered refuter and its own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The output here is a shape and a union, not a policy name. If a recipient asks "which reform", the honest answer is that the framework declines to name one until a ground settles it.
