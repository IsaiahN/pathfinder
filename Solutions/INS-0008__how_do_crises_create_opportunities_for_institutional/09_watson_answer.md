## QUESTION

How do crises create opportunities for institutional reform, and why do some crises instead strengthen entrenched power? Restated as an effect: why does `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` on one slot lead to widened constraint on power in one case and concentrated power in another? Option A treats a crisis as a cause with a fixed valence. Option B treats a crisis as an event whose valence is relative to a residual, and asks which conditions conjoin. A sends a label where an explanation is owed and selects recipients for agreement; B sends a union of conditions, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: a two-outcome fork with at least three conditions. Symmetry: asymmetric, since introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62). Scale: individual, organisation, institution, system, with a lossy transform between levels (Fig 4 :27-30). Fig 9 :55 names a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question.
- **D1, incumbent institutional frame.** Actors: rules, routines, offices, power holders. Currency: authority, compliance, standing. Anchor: the mandate the institution is constitutively about, when nameable. No nameable mandate means this is not yet a domain. Status admitted where a mandate exists.
- **D2, crisis as contact.** Actors: the perturbing event, new actors, new conditions. Currency: live-slot residual mass. Anchor: the world that registers the failure, a count, a measurement, a market, an outcome. Status admitted.
- **D3, reform and selection.** Actors: reformers, a maintainer seat, a habitat, low-overlap peer frames. Currency: methods crossing up, imported atoms, rooms selected against the residual. Anchor: the ground, since change that does not change contact changes nothing. Status conditional on D2.
- **D4, entrenchment collapse.** Actors: incumbents, channel maintainers, coherent frames. Currency: agreement, correlation, standing. Anchor: the D1 ground through a degraded channel, or in pure collapse an anchor that has become another frame. Status admitted as the failure mode of D1 and D3, not a separate force.
- **X1, crisis as a cause with fixed valence.** No nameable anchor. Not a domain yet, refused under Fig 11 :49-51.
- **X2, institutional design as one lever.** Absorbed into D3.

Union, not partition: C1 registered live-slot residual, C2 closure-different import with provenance, C3 channel still reachable. Dependency order: perception then vocabulary then objective then planning then learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). The middle link has no instrument (Fig 3 :27).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The branch after a crisis is a union of three conditions: a live-slot residual registered against an unmoving ground, an import from a closure that differs with provenance and a shadow test, and a channel to the ground that survives the change. Where they conjoin, the window is real. Where the channel fails, the same event drifts coherently inside the incumbent frame, the anchor becomes another frame, and power is entrenched through the very measure meant to restrain it. The event has no fixed valence, and reform and entrenchment are isomers over the same actors under different bonds. Crisis is not necessary for institutional change; "critical juncture" is a filter for the budget, never a witness. Which policy works is a declared gap. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Verdicts were kept per domain and never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** misdiagnosis of a union as a partition. A single lever is applied where two or three conditions bind, producing a reform that changes vocabulary but not contact, which Fig 4 :25 calls a recording replayed downward. The second cost is spending outside independence by asking people to endorse the claim before they have checked it (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). Both costs are avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A refutation adds a term and removes nothing, so the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :42-43). The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64). No institutional primitive is withdrawn by a failed check.
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) a ground-verified widening of constraint with no differing closure crossing, (2) invariant event valence across a change of residual, (3) a ground that moves when pushed yet still settles, (4) a reform with a reachable channel and changed contact that still entrenches power, (5) no live-slot residual before the branch, or (6) one lever clearing two-condition cases with the same residue. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing the branch is fully predicted by the aggregate headline would also reverse it. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
