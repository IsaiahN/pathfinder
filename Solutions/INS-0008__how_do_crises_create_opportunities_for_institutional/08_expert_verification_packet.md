# Expert verification packet: crisis, reform and entrenchment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of crisis and institutional reform can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the institutional claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No institutional measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A crisis matters only as a registered failure on a specific slot. The branch after it is a union of three overlapping conditions: (C1) the failure is registered against a ground the institution cannot move; (C2) something crosses from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test; (C3) the channel to the ground survives the change. Where all three hold, the window is a real opportunity. Where C3 fails, the same event is processed inside the incumbent's vocabulary, the anchor becomes another frame, and power is entrenched by the very measure meant to restrain it. The event has no fixed valence; reform and entrenchment are isomers over the same actors under different bonds. The framework does not name the policy that works.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9, 6 and 8
- **Statement:** the split is a union of three conditions, and reform requires an import rather than more search inside the incumbent closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
- **Refuter:** a ground-verified widening of constraint on power while the incumbent closure is unchanged, no atom from a differing closure crossed, and the channel stayed reachable. Also: one intervention that clears cases where two or three conditions bind and leaves a residue of the same kind.

### C2: institution-facing, checkable against a case the expert knows
- **Statement:** entrenchment is the same loop with a degraded channel, and the crisis event has no fixed valence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25-26 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 2 :34 *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, was the failure registered on a live slot or averaged into a headline? Did the metric saturate or pool two things? Did an emergency actor get introduced with its cascade unenumerated? Was the event's valence the same across a change of ground?
- **Refuter:** a case in which the channel is measurably dead, yet a crisis produced a ground-verified increase in constraint on power and restored the reading; or an event whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a structural test
- **Statement:** reform and entrenchment are isomers over the same institutional actors, and a reform that does not change contact changes nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
- **What to check:** take one set of actors (oversight body, budget, emergency decree, public report) under two arrangements. Does a list of "reforms enacted" hide the difference that behaviour reveals? Did the oversight body survive its own use? Was the transplanted law a method or a recording?
- **Refuter:** two arrangements with identical actors and bonds that behave identically under a ground reading, or a reform that changes no contact with the residual yet produces a durable ground-verified change in constraint on power.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a crisis is necessary for reform. Arrangements over a closed set change incrementally too.
- Not that "critical juncture" is a cause. It is a filter for the research budget, never a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the framework names the policy. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the six physical terms map onto political atoms. That mapping was refused after re-census.
- Not that repeal subtracts an actor. Refusal is negation added; nothing is deleted.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was measured.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Slot:** is there a live slot residual before the branch, hidden by an aggregate near zero?
2. **Provenance:** what crossed from a differing closure, with a source gate and a shadow test?
3. **Channel:** are readings still returning after the change, or are they stale, pooled or saturated?
4. **Room:** did the next room differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced?
5. **Contact:** did the change alter contact with the residual, or only the vocabulary?
6. **Cycle:** did the oversight part survive its own use across instances?
