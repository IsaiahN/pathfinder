## RESIDUAL

The question asks why one crisis opens a reform window and another entrenches power. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) what is the structure of the split, (2) what is the order of the work, and what do the analogues in other fields actually license.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- The three conditions C1 (registered live-slot residual), C2 (closure-different import with provenance), C3 (reachable channel) overlap. Reform appears where they conjoin; entrenchment appears where C3 fails or C2 is replaced by a blend.
- A single lever clears only the region where one condition binds. The residue where two bind is **categorically different**, not a harder case of the one-condition region.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** one intervention that clears cases where two or three conditions bind, leaving a residue of the same kind, refutes the union reading.

### Q2: What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :24: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Order: perception (which slot failed?) → vocabulary (what does the situation afford: import, anchor, room?) → objective (what counts as progress in our own terms?) → planning (SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD) → learn and carry.
- The objective is itself a bond: Fig 12 :112 *"A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A reform vocabulary that holds only nouns can say what the emergency contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it.
- **Falsifier:** a programme where "what would count as progress" is measured by an instrument external to the programme and failure to move it blocks further input funding would show the middle link is not where this chain breaks.

### Q3: Are reform and entrenchment isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Take the same institutional atoms (incumbent rules, an oversight body, a budget, an emergency decree, a public report). Under arrangement A, the oversight body survives its own use and the report feeds back into the next decision: a catalyst plus a negative-sign cycle. Under arrangement B, the decree is issued under the incumbent's own sequence bond and the report is pooled into the incumbent's metric: a one long room.
- Same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The difference is invisible in a list of "reforms enacted" and decisive in behaviour. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, not variants of one thing.
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Whether the oversight body is a catalyst or a spent part is not recorded by naming it; it is a question about use across instances.
- **Falsifier:** two arrangements with identical actors and identical bonds that behave identically under a ground reading. Then the isomer claim fails and the difference is nominal only.

### Q4: What do the analogues license, and what do they not?

**Adaptive cycle (Resilience Alliance, read 2026-11-23).** Four phases: growth, conservation, release, reorganisation, with *"shorter periods that create opportunities for innovation"* and *"windows of experimentation open briefly"* while nested hierarchies stabilise the whole. This corroborates the shape of a brief opening inside a stabilising structure. It does **not** license claiming that a political crisis *is* an ecological release; that would be an adopted import with no source gate.

**Critical juncture template (Collier and Collier, via the Wikipedia article's synthesis, read 2026-11-23).** Antecedent conditions → cleavage or shock → critical juncture → aftermath → legacy. The template's own definitions say *"Cleavages, shocks, or crises are triggers of critical junctures"* and the legacy is *"an enduring, self-reinforcing institutional inheritance"*. This corroborates the sequence bond and the self-reinforcement, but "trigger" is a correlation filter, not a verdict (Fig 9 :60). The template does not decide the branch.

**Threat-rigidity and its regulation (Tadmor et al. 2026, PNAS Nexus).** Threat induces cognitive rigidity and suppresses complex reasoning, but regulation is *"possible but conditional on both situational prompts and individual cognitive readiness"*. At the individual stratum this is a low-overlap corroboration of C3: the channel is a conditional capability, not a fixed property. It does not license a claim about institutions without re-naming the anchor per Fig 4 :20.

**Victory trap (Ong and Cayton 2026).** Elite and supporter conflicts after an opposition victory *"easily open[ing] the door to autocratic comebacks"*, and new elites deploy inherited authoritarian institutions rather than reforming them. This corroborates Fig 4 :25: a recording replayed downward repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. It is a different subfield from the critical-junctures pool, so it is genuinely low overlap. It still does not decide the figure verdict.

**Status of the analogues:** all four are secondary. Their agreement with the figures is corroboration at the mechanism level, not a ground reading of the institutional question. No analogue is allowed to flip the verdict.

### Q5: What is the order of the loop after the window opens?

- THE_FORMULA.md step 7 asks three questions. **SEARCHED** first: is the reform already in the closure and unfound? Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."*
- **INWARD:** extend the instrument from one already returning something. Fig 6 :32-33: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- **OUTWARD:** import, after the residual is described in effect terms. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and never read against each other. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget issue different instructions (Fig 8 :40).

## CONCLUSION

The split is a union of three overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55-57), ordered by dependency rather than importance (Fig 3 :20), and reform and entrenchment are isomers over the same institutional atoms when their bonds differ (Fig 12 :117). The window opens briefly, as the adaptive-cycle analogue independently suggests, but the figure verdict does not rest on that analogue. SEARCHED precedes INWARD and OUTWARD; the sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term so it can be refused.
