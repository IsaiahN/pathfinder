## 0. The residual, in effect terms

Restate the question as an effect before choosing any figure line.

A crisis is a registered failure: an institution's prediction about a slot does not match what happened, so `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` on that slot. The question asks why the same class of event is followed, in some cases, by a widening of what the institution can do and a tightening of the constraint on powerful actors (reform), and in others by a narrowing and a concentration of power (entrenchment). The residual is the **branch difference**: given an event that registers as a failure, what decides which outcome follows?

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity.** A two-outcome fork from one input class, with at least three conditions in play. Not one hard problem. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric. Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62). The shock itself is slot-specific, not aggregate (Fig 1 :19).
- **Scale.** Individual decision, organisation, institution, system. The membrane between scales is lossy and the grounds differ per level (Fig 4 :27-30, Fig 10 :68).

What an answer would look like is written down and bound before the run: a shape with (i) a registered live-slot residual, (ii) a closure-different frame available with provenance, and (iii) a channel to the ground that survives the change. The name of a specific policy is **not** part of the answer shape. This binding is the "Direction" discipline of `THE_FORMULA.md`: make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

| **D2 The crisis as contact** | the perturbing event, new actors, new conditions | live-slot residual mass `R⁺_s` | the world that registers the failure: a count, a measurement, a market, an outcome | admitted |

| **X2 "Institutional design" as one lever** | the designer | laws passed | already inside D3 | absorbed, not admitted separately |

**Partition or union.** The reform branch and the entrenchment branch are not a partition of two independent questions. They share the event, the institution and the channel. The split lands on a **union of overlapping conditions**: C1 (a live-slot residual is registered against a ground), C2 (a closure-different frame is available and imported with provenance), C3 (the channel to the ground survives the change). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-58). The residue becomes a specific compound, not a harder case of either branch.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** perception (name the live slot) → vocabulary (terms for what the situation affords: import, anchor, room) → objective (what counts as progress: contact restored) → planning (route: SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD) → learn and carry. The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The middle link has no instrument (Fig 3 :24-27), which is why reform effort lands on laws passed and budgets while the break goes unmeasured.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The reform branch requires an import, not more search (decisive for D3)

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."*

Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*

A crisis that is processed entirely inside the incumbent's own vocabulary is SEARCHED at the same reach. It can produce a report, a commission, a law, and still leave the wall where it was. This is the structural reading of a window that opens and closes without reform. Reform, under the figures, is the arrival of an atom from a closure that differs, with a source gate and a shadow test.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which reach demonstrably expanded, power was measurably constrained, the ground stayed reachable, and no atom from a differing closure crossed. If that holds, F1 is false.

**Mode:** `general` for idempotence; `specified` for the institutional binding. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (two distinct roles: what moves the wall vs why nothing else can).

### F2: Entrenchment is the same loop with a degraded channel, not a separate force (D1 and D4)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 10 :23: *"R never arrives"*. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

An institution whose mandate has been replaced by its own survival is a convention deciding an outcome. Its ground still exists; the reading of it is stale, pooled or saturated, so the residual does not arrive. In the pure collapse the anchor itself becomes another frame, and the drift is coherent, which is exactly why it is invisible from inside. This is why a crisis can strengthen power: the event is routed through a channel that cannot carry its residual, and the incumbent's frame is confirmed rather than corrected.

Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Standing earned on an earlier crisis is a stake that outlives that crisis, so the keeper of the emergency is disqualified as a check on it.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which the channel to the ground is measurably dead (stale, pooled, saturated) yet a crisis still produced a ground-verified increase in constraint on power and a restored reading. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 (channel) and Fig 2 (anchor), low overlap.

### F3: "Crisis" has no fixed valence (refusal of X1)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50-51: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds. No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The event is an actor in a habitat. Its valence is a relation to a residual, not a property of the event. So the question's premise, "crises create opportunities", is accepted only in effect terms: a crisis is an opportunity **for a specific residual against a specific ground**, and it can be a malfactor to that same residual under a different arrangement. The same shock that breaks extractive power in one setting intensifies it in another, which is precisely the double-edged claim in Acemoglu and Robinson (world source, section 6).

**Refuter:** an event whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual and ground. **Mode:** `general` for relativity, `specified` for event actors. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 and the world source pool, but the world source is secondary only.

### F4: The union must be unbundled before it is searched (D1 to D4)

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Reform and entrenchment are not two halves of a single variable. They are overlapping conditions. A single lever (an election, a transparency law, a new agency) clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The label "critical juncture" is a filter for which cases to look at, paid for by the budget; it cannot hand us the verdict about which branch occurs.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that clears both branches across cases where any two of C1, C2 and C3 are simultaneously binding, with the residue remaining the same kind of case. **Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 and the seat map's union rule).

### F5: The middle link is where chains break

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :24: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

Institutional reform programmes are rich in input metrics (laws passed, money spent, meetings held) and output metrics (reports published), which are the links with obvious instruments. The link between them, having terms for what the situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress in the institution's own terms, has neither. Fig 12 names why: a vocabulary of nouns can name what a situation contains and cannot name a route to one. A goal is a comparison and progress is a subtraction, and a vocabulary without those bonds cannot state either.

**Refuter:** a reform programme in which "what would count as progress" is measured by an instrument that is not one of the programme's own outputs, and in which failure to move that instrument blocks further input funding. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 3 and Fig 12, low overlap.

### F6: A reform that does not change contact changes nothing

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

A reform that improves the institution in isolation, without changing what it is in contact with, is not a reform of capability. Introduction is available and exclusion is not, so an emergency measure can be added and the habitat it entered cannot be removed: the crisis framework persists after the crisis. The figure's own warning is cane toads and kudzu, an actor introduced with its cascade unenumerated. This is the clearest figure-grounded account of why a crisis can entrench power even when the stated intent is reform.

**Refuter:** a reform that changes no actor and no condition in contact with the residual, yet produces a ground-verified, durable change in constraint on power. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 4 (membrane), low overlap.

### F7: Policy copying is a recording, not a method

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 4 :34-35: *"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

The standard "window of opportunity" story often says a reform was copied from elsewhere. Under Fig 4, a copied law is a recording of one occasion. Replayed downward it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. What can cross is a method that can be reapplied, and the landing level remakes it. The round trip gap `R_T` has to be measured, not assumed zero.

**Refuter:** a policy transplanted across a documented level boundary with the same ground and no measured `R_T`, yet producing the same ground-verified effect. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the membrane rule (Fig 4), corroborated at practitioner level by the world literature only weakly.

### F8: The room selected must differ from the last one

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :32: *"one long room: motion, no progress"*. Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

A reform process that convenes the same actors with the same vocabulary after a shock is a chain of mirrors. It generates motion and agreement and no evidence. Progress per handoff needs both bracket fidelity and ground pressure (Fig 7 :37-39). The next room is selected by the residual, not by the incumbent's roster.

**Refuter:** a reform sequence in which every room overlapped heavily with its predecessor on evidence and actors, yet ground-verified reach increased at each handoff. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 7 and Fig 2 (agreement vs anchor), low overlap.

### F9: Same actors, different arrangement gives different institutional substance

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

Reform need not introduce new actors. Reordering the same actors under a different bond changes the substance: an oversight body that survives its own use is a catalyst; a feedback loop whose output re-enters as input either closes or does not according to its sign. Two currencies are charged and they do not add: a law is cheap to describe and can take a whole budget to settle, and a long chain of cheap parts can be expensive to state and cheap to refute. Summing them is the error the figure names.

**Refuter:** two institutional arrangements with identical actors and identical bonds that are behaviourally identical under a ground reading. **Mode:** `specified` for institutions, `general` for the bond rule. **Status:** single-source for the structure definitions (Fig 12); the institutional binding is unmeasured.

### F10: Crisis is not necessary for institutional change

Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 8 :32 (above): closure is idempotent. The set of kinds does not grow by arranging, and arrangements never run out, so a frame can compose without end inside a finite reach (Fig 13 :116-118). Nothing in this requires a shock. Incremental change (layering, conversion, drift, the alternatives named in Capoccia and Kelemen footnote 1) is composition inside the existing reach.

So the question's implicit premise that a crisis is the unique or necessary trigger is refused. A crisis is one route to an import, not the only route, and the figures do not make it necessary.

**Refuter:** a derivation from the figures that arrangements over a closed set are exhausted, or a measurement showing institutional change never occurs without a discrete shock. **Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 13 and Fig 8), with the incremental-change literature secondary only.

### Strains named, not hidden

- **Applying the six physical terms of Fig 13 to institutions is a strain.** Re-census: Fig 13 governs the abstract composition rule (closed set, open arrangements, cross-kind composition) with actors, currency and anchor supplied by the domain, per Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The direct mapping space=territory, time=crisis, energy=power is **refused**, exactly as the exemplar refused metric tensor = Gradient. No figure line supports it.
- **"Introduce, never subtract" vs repeal.** Read naively, Fig 11 :61-62 seems to forbid repealing a rule. Re-census: a habitat actor is not the same object as a term in Γ. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 3: *"NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED."* Repeal is a refusal (¬) added on top of the rule, which loses its standing to be composed over. The figure governs; the naive reading is wrong. Kept as a `specified` strain because the institutional binding has not been measured.
- **Fig 7 room selection vs Seat Map's authored-room rule.** Rooms must be unauthored (lottery, everyone who qualifies, a set assembled before the question existed). This constrains F8: a reform "room" hand-picked by an incumbent is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned. No strain, but a constraint that would otherwise be missed.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A crisis produces a ground-verified widening of constraint on power while the incumbent closure is unchanged and no atom from a differing closure crossed (breaks F1 and Fig 6 :14, :23).
2. The valence of the same event is invariant across a documented change of residual and ground (breaks F3 and Fig 11 :49-51).
3. The ground moves when frames push on it, yet the arrangement still settles (breaks Fig 2 :30, *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*).
4. A reform passes a source gate and a shadow test, changes contact, keeps the channel reachable, and still entrenches power (breaks the union reading in F4 and D1/D3).
5. A live-slot residual is not registered before the branch, or a global aggregate near zero with one live slot fails to predict the branch (breaks Fig 1 :19 and :13).
6. A single lever clears cases where two or more of C1, C2 and C3 bind and leaves a residue of the same kind (breaks Fig 9 :55-58).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union and the refuter, not with "critical junctures". The label is a filter for the budget, never a witness (F4, Fig 9 :60).
2. Ask for the live slot first. Aggregate stability with one live slot residual is a legal state (Fig 1 :13), so a stable headline is not evidence that no crisis is registered.
3. Ask which closure differed and what crossed, with provenance and a shadow test. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents (Fig 8 :51).
4. Ask what would count as progress in the institution's own terms, measured by something the programme does not produce (F5, Fig 3 :27).
5. Declare the stake. Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*; Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*
6. Send the generator, not the conclusion (Fig 4 :22-25). Offer the three source links only after the stage-1 verdict is recorded.

### The three step-7 questions, kept distinct

- **SEARCHED.** Is the reform already inside the incumbent closure and unfound? Same atoms, same wall, more of the space visited (Fig 6 :49). Asked first, because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer.
- **INWARD.** Is the representation adequate to hold the live slot? Instruments are improved from worse instruments already returning something, never built from a description (Fig 6 :32-34).
- **OUTWARD.** Does another frame already hold the atom? Characterise R in effect terms first, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape (Fig 6 :30, Fig 9 :27-28). Source gate, shadow test, debited against independence.

Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts (Fig 8 :40). A reform that is unreachable at this budget is in the closure and needs more search, not another atom; importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the branch is decided by a union of a registered live-slot residual, a closure-different import with provenance, and a channel to the ground that survives the change. Entrenchment is the same loop with a degraded channel and an authored room, not a separate force. Strains after re-census: one `specified` strain on the repeal/negation binding, named and not hidden; zero figure-to-figure contradictions. Which specific reform works in a given crisis is a declared `gap`: Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* and Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.
