## 0. The residual, in effect terms, and the bound sought-for shape

**Question as given:** can black holes form and evaporate without leaving a remnant, and what would a remnant be?

**Effect statement.** Formation produces a horizon and an interior region that, semiclassically, cannot signal outward. Evaporation drains mass through Hawking quanta and shrinks the horizon. The gap (the residual `R`) is at the **endpoint**: Hawking's semiclassical calculation gives radiation independent of the infalling state, so if the hole disappears entirely the map from a pure initial state to a mixed final state violates unitarity. The question asks whether that endpoint is empty, and if not what occupies it.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** two conditions plus a dependent shape question. Formation and evaporation are one kind of condition (settled physics); the endpoint is a second, open condition; the remnant characterisation depends on how the endpoint resolves.
- **Symmetry:** the endpoint question is not symmetric between the options. "Nothing remains" is an absence claim; "something remains" is a presence claim. The figures treat these asymmetrically (F1).
- **Scale:** first order in the semiclassical evaporation; the endpoint is where the description loses its grip, so it is a boundary between descriptions.

**Sought-for shape, written down and bound before the run (formula step "Direction").** An answer would look like: (a) a verdict on whether the endpoint is empty that states which frame could settle it, and (b) a characterisation of any residual in effect terms that does not name microphysics the framework does not hold. We bind that shape as an ordinary term now, so it can accrue a residual and be refused like any other claim. It is not allowed to steer the search from outside it.

**Union, not one hard question.** Fig 9 :54 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*. "Can black holes form and evaporate without leaving a remnant" bundles three conditions: forms, evaporates, leaves no remnant. Only the third is open. Effort spent re-proving the first two arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*). The honest reading is that the question is a compound with its own loop and its own ground.

## 3. Findings

### F1: "Leaves nothing, information destroyed" is an absence claim, and absence is not provable from inside the frame (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44:

> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."**

The horizon is the maximal sealed room of the semiclassical frame. If the proponent of complete evaporation says "we looked inside the evaporation and found no carrier of the information, therefore it is gone", they have exactly the inference these two lines forbid. The two figures are low overlap: Fig 8 argues from idempotent closure (composing inside a closed set never adds a member); Fig 9 argues from the impossibility of a sealed room. Different routes, same verdict.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated:** Fig 8 and Fig 9. **Refuter:** a ground reading that displays the final state as mixed and shows the missing carrier is absent in principle, not merely unfound. Until then the correct label for "information destroyed" is *owed and unreached*, not *established*.

### F2: What decays is the channel, not the ground (triangulated)

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The information paradox is generated by treating the horizon as a sink: stuff goes in, the hole shrinks, nothing comes back, so contact is lost. The figures say the ground, the thing that settles the question, does not decay. What decays is the channel. Read structurally, the horizon is the channel, and at the endpoint the channel fails; the contact (the quantum state and its information) is not thereby destroyed. This is a shape claim, not a physical calculation. World corroboration from the Page-curve program is secondary and is recorded in `09` and `10`.

**Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Triangulated:** Fig 10 and Fig 11. **Refuter:** a verified pure-to-mixed evolution at the endpoint, or a figure re-census showing the ground itself can decay. Either would fire F2.

Fig 13 :134:

The canonical usage rule (read this session) states the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. The endpoint state is not held in Γ. ### F5: A remnant, if any, is a settled composition at a level, not a new primitive or a continuum (triangulated)

Fig 12 :103:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**

Fig 13 :115:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Fig 13 :43-44:

> **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."**

A remnant cannot be a seventh kind: the set is closed and a continuum was explicitly removed for being a derivative rather than a member. It must be a composition of the six that has settled at the endpoint, and whatever is irreducible about it is irreducible at that level only. The frequent picture of a remnant as a smooth continuation of the evaporation into a Planckian endpoint is therefore a derived quantity promoted to a member, the exact error Fig 13 :44 names.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated:** Fig 12 and Fig 13. **Refuter:** derive a seventh kind from the six (breaks Fig 13 closedness), or show the endpoint state cannot be written as any composition of the six.

### F6: Whether a remnant is owed is an import question, not a search inside semiclassical closure (triangulated)

Fig 6 :21:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

Fig 6 :24:

> **"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**

Semiclassical evaporation composes inside a fixed closure. No amount of finer semiclassical calculation adds the missing atom (unitarity). The closure moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. Two moves are then available and they are opposed, never read against each other: expand the closure by importing an entropy prescription from holography (the island/Page-curve route, which keeps the information in the radiation and needs no remnant), or import a new atom (a remnant). Both are imports and both owe a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap). Fig 8 :40-43 must be honoured here: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"*. Treating a budget-limited failure of semiclassical evaporation as proof that a new atom is owed would buy a wrong atom above the price of primitives.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated:** Fig 6 and Fig 8. **Refuter:** derive unitarity inside the unexpanded semiclassical closure with no new entropy rule and no new atom, which would remove the import requirement.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 10's own discipline and the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A ground reading displays the endpoint as a genuinely mixed final state and shows the missing carrier is absent in principle, not merely unfound (kills F1 and F2).
2. A derivation yields unitary evaporation inside the unexpanded semiclassical closure, with no new entropy rule and no new atom (kills F6).
3. A demonstration shows the endpoint state is a new primitive rather than a composition of the six, or that the set admits a seventh member (kills F5).
4. A source-discriminating instrument at the Planck scale names the endpoint state uniquely (converts F4 from shape to name and closes the open slot; the shape claim as stated is then superseded, not merely confirmed).
5. A figure re-census admits a line that permits a frame to prove its own closure's absence claim (kills F1 at the framework level).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.
