# Isomers, catalysts and order: supporting derivation for the endpoint question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the definitions.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The endpoint residual has two unresolved features: what order the endpoint composition sits in, and whether the two candidate resolutions (information in radiation, information in a remnant) are variants of one thing or different substances. Two questions are owed: (1) can the endpoint be ordered against the settled evaporation chain, and (2) are the two resolutions isomers under Fig 12's definition?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six (Space, Time, Energy states; Gradient, Network, Spectrum structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. A black hole is a settled composition at its own level; its evaporation is a process over that composition. No new primitive is available without import. Actors, currency and anchor per domain are set out in `01` section 1; this file works only on the structure within that census.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the endpoint a first-order or second-order composition?

- Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*
- A black hole is settled against the ground (its exterior geometry is observed and its thermodynamics is consistent). It can therefore serve as an atom for whatever composes over it.
- Evaporation is the first composition over that atom. The endpoint is where the **next** composition is owed: the residual of the evaporation turn re-enters as the input to the endpoint turn (Fig 13 :88-89, *"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."*).
- So the endpoint is **second order** relative to the settled black hole. It requires no new primitive. This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import of a seventh kind.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the endpoint cannot be written as a composition over the settled hole using only the held atoms and bonds, the second-order claim fails and a genuine import is owed.

### Q2 - Are the two resolutions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Write both resolutions over the full shared atom set {Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}. The radiation route arranges them so the information is carried outward by subtle correlations; the remnant route arranges them so a localised late state retains the contact. Same atoms, different arrangement, decisively different observable consequences at the endpoint.
- **They are isomers under Fig 12's definition**, provided both can be written without a private atom. If either requires an atom the other does not (for example a dedicated information-bearing primitive that the radiation route lacks), the isomer claim fails and the weaker **functional-group** reading stands: both are members of the class "settled endpoint composition" with different qualifiers.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a candidate resolution that cannot be written over the shared six atom set, or one that is written identically to the other under every held bond. Either kills the isomer claim.

### Q3 - A remnant as a catalyst: the structure that survives its own use

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A stable remnant is exactly this figure: a part that survives its own use. The evaporation uses the hole; the remnant is what is left that can be used again (composed over at the next level).
- Fig 12 :119 also warns that nothing in the notation records whether a part is spent or regenerating. So calling a candidate a "remnant" does not establish that it survives; the distinction needs an instrument, not a name. This is a second, independent reason to withhold the name (the first is F4 in `01`).
- **Falsifier:** a ground reading that shows the candidate late state is spent or regenerating rather than surviving its use. That would remove the catalyst analogue without touching the shape claim.

### Q4 - Chain or cycle at the endpoint?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Hawking's original picture is a chain: collapse, radiation, nothing. Fig 12 :121 says a chain cannot close by construction, which is exactly why a pure state ending in mixed radiation is a structural surprise under a chain reading.
- A remnant picture is a cycle: the late state re-enters as input to further composition (it radiates slowly, or it is stable and composable). The sign of the loop decides whether it closes. Whether the sign is positive (contact preserved) or negative (contact lost) is the open empirical question.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the endpoint process is a closed chain with no re-entry, which would retire the cycle reading and leave only the chain.

### Q5 - What order is the union of the two candidate frames?

- The two frames (holography-based radiation route, quantum-gravity remnant route) are related as **imports into D2 and D3**, not as a composition inside either closure (Fig 6 :21-23). Their union is searchable from contact only after R has been described in effect terms (Fig 8 :53-54).
- Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* So the record of which frame supplied which atom is part of the claim, not documentation.
- **Falsifier:** show that one frame can derive the other's endpoint atom in its own closure, which would collapse the union into one frame and remove the surplus.

## CONCLUSION

The endpoint is a **second-order composition** over a settled black hole, licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule and needing no new primitive. The two candidate resolutions are **isomers** over the shared six atom set, with a stated falsifier that demotes them to a functional group if either needs a private atom. A stable remnant is the figure's **catalyst**, a part that survives its own use, but the notation cannot tell survival from regeneration, which is a second reason to withhold the name. Hawking's chain cannot close by construction; a remnant is a cycle whose sign is still unmeasured. This rescues what is defensible in both camps without pretending the figures pick one.
