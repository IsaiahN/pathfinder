# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the endpoint

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union unbundling in `01` section 0 and F3 are respected. If the email asks one person to adjudicate formation, evaporation, endpoint and remnant identity at once, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about black hole evaporation endpoints, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question and would like you to check one output against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A black hole forms (settled), it radiates (settled as a description), and at the endpoint either nothing remains or a localised late state remains. The live question is only the endpoint. Formation and evaporation are not in dispute here, so I have unbundled them rather than asking you to re-adjudicate them.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Three structural constraints follow from the figure set I am using:
>
> 1. "Evaporated completely, information destroyed, nothing left" is a claim that a carrier is absent. Fig 8 :39 says *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* and Fig 9 :44 says *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* A horizon is the sealed room of the semiclassical frame. So the absence conclusion is not something that frame can hand you.
> 2. What fails at the endpoint is the channel, not the ground. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* The story in which the information itself decays is a category error about which thing changes.
> 3. On "what a remnant would be": the framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). It can say a residual would have to be settled, keep contact with the information, and be composable at the next level. It cannot tell you it is a Planck-mass extremal hole, a regular black hole or a loop-quantum-gravity white hole. Those are bindings from a physics frame, and until their provenance is shown they carry no weight here.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures settle whether nature leaves a remnant. They do not. They constrain the shape of an admissible answer, and they say the physics is still open at the endpoint. I am not claiming a theory of everything, not claiming a preferred endpoint, and not claiming the last word on the information paradox.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a ground reading that displays the endpoint as a genuinely mixed final state and shows the missing carrier is absent in principle, not merely unfound;
> 2. a derivation of unitary evaporation inside the unexpanded semiclassical closure, with no new entropy rule and no new atom;
> 3. a demonstration that an endpoint state is a new primitive rather than a composition of the held set.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
