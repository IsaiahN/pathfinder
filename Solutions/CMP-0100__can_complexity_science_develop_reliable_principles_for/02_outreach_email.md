# Outreach draft: check one decision rule before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three-way routing rule below survives the checks in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one decision rule before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a complex system produces a gap between what a model predicts and what the ground returns, the figures route that gap per object slot, not as one averaged number. They distinguish three regimes, and they give three different instructions:
>
> 1. If the live gap on a slot is **shrinking** under the current model, refine that forecast. That is PREDICT.
> 2. If the disagreement **does not shrink with effort** and each rule keeps working on a different subset, the problem is a union of conditions, not one hard question. Split it and hold several scenarios until the ground selects between them. That is MULTIPLE SCENARIOS.
> 3. If the channel to the ground is degraded (stale readings, saturated metrics, pooled observations) or the habitat has changed so that contact is the binding constraint, no forecast closes the gap. Keep the anchor reachable and add capability to the agent-and-habitat. That is RESILIENCE, and it is a precondition on the other two, not a last resort.
>
> This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement. It does not tell you the numerical point at which to switch, and the framework explicitly refuses to give one: the switch point is a fact about a level and is settled by the ground in each case. What it offers is the routing discipline and the reasons for it, so any candidate rule can be checked before it is trusted.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a final or universal theory of decision, that it yields a single scoring function, that a list of forecasts from one model counts as multiple scenarios (it does not, independence is the quantity), or that resilience is a solved state rather than permanent work. A universal self-certifying switch is not something the framework can produce.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A domain-independent numerical switch is derived that works at every level without a ground reading, and a frame can certify it for itself.
> 2. A case is shown where the same gap is simultaneously a single live slot, a union compound, and a degraded channel, with no instrument that tells the three apart.
> 3. An improvement to the agent in isolation, with no change of contact and no change of habitat, measurably moves the ground.
>
> If any holds, I retract the relevant part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule (figures README): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a decision you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the routing shape, the mode, and the four refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
