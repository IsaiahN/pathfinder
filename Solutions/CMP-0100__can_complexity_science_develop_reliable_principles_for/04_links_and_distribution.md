## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **The predict branch has a measured horizon, not a universal rule.** Lorenz's finite predictability result motivated ensemble forecasting: several perturbed forecasts rather than one. This is the world's own version of the figures' predict-to-scenarios transition, and it is why `01` F1 and F2 are kept as separate verdicts rather than merged.
2. **The scenarios branch is weaker than its popularity suggests.** The scenario-planning source states that support for the method is "only anecdotal" and comes mostly from one company. So a figure-derived preference for splitting unions is corroborated in *form* but not in *effect size*. This is recorded as honest corroboration, not as a result.
3. **The resilience branch is a capability of the system-plus-habitat, not an ingredient.** Woods' graceful extensibility, Ashby's requisite variety and Holling's ecological resilience all relocate the property from the agent to the agent-and-its-field of contact. That is the same move the figures make in Fig 11 :39. Agreement here is genuine but it is still secondary: the verdict comes from the figures.

- **Fig 9 :60** - "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." The public-contact list is a way to spend a budget of attention, not a source of verdicts.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the routing shape and its four refuters, (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure and record the answer, (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
