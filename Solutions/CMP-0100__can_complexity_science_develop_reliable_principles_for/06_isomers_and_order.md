# Are the three stances one thing or three? Bonds, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The trichotomy in `01` could be a genuine three-way distinction, or it could be one stance wearing three names. Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12's definition, are PREDICT, MULTIPLE SCENARIOS and RESILIENCE different substances over the same residual, and (2) what is their dependency order, and which analogues are shadows (figure-predicted) versus echoes (borrowed)?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the residual R on a slot s, the library Γ, the ground, and the channel. Operators held: the seven bonds plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The question is how R is bonded in each stance, not what new thing each stance adds.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Three bonds over one residual

Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Read the three stances as different bondings of the same residual:

| stance | operative bond | figure reason | decision it produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| PREDICT | sequence (→) plus subtraction (−) | Operators table: "a plan is a sequence" and "progress is a subtraction" | a route to a closing forecast; stop when left(R,φ) falls |
| MULTIPLE SCENARIOS | disjunction (∥) plus comparison (⋛) | "either suffices; not both required"; "a goal is a comparison" | hold a set of conditions; the ground selects between them |
| RESILIENCE | comparison (⋛) plus negation (¬) | threshold on channel integrity; "a refutation is a negation" | refuse the held forecast, restore contact, introduce actors |

These are not three vocabularies for one decision. Under Fig 12 they are three different molecules: same residual atoms, different typed relations, different behaviour. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if all three stances can be written with the same bond over the same atoms and produce the same decision on a test residual, the isomer claim fails and the trichotomy collapses to one stance with three names. Triangulated for the operator definitions (Fig 12 + Operators table), single-source for the specific three-bond assignment, which is a derivation, not a measurement.

### Q2 — Dependency order is not importance

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Ordered for this decision:

1. **Channel integrity** (Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). If the channel is stale, the per-slot residual is not a reading of the world. This link is upstream.
2. **Per-slot residual** (Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Without it there is nothing to route.
3. **Union check** (Fig 9 :54, *"unbundle before you search again"*). Decide whether the residual is one question or several conditions before choosing a stance.
4. **Stance selection** (refuse before mint, Formula step 2; SEARCHED before INWARD before OUTWARD, Formula step 7).

Practical consequence: running a more elaborate scenario process on a stale channel is effort spent above the break. It arrives at a step that cannot use it. This is why `01` F4 places resilience as a precondition rather than last.

**Falsifier for the order:** a case where a correct stance chosen on a degraded channel produces a ground-settled decision anyway. That would break the dependency claim and make step 1 optional.

### Q3 — The currencies do not add

Fig 12 :126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :127: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

For a decision:

- **Bits:** how much must be stated to describe a stance. A single forecast is cheap to state; a full scenario set over a union compound is expensive.
- **Actions:** how much must be spent to settle it. A flat residual may cost one action to refute; a resilience programme over a changed habitat may take a whole budget (Fig 12 :128).
- **Independence:** a third accounting again, priced by Fig 2 :42, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Summing all three into one decision score is the error Fig 12 names.

**Falsifier for Q3:** a lossless conversion among bits, actions, and independence demonstrated on a real decision. None is supplied by the figures.

### Q4 — Which analogues are shadows and which are echoes

A **shadow** is a structure the figure-derived residual predicts and that appears in another field, arrived at independently. An **echo** is a structure found elsewhere and given somewhere to live. The discriminator is whether the analogue carries the residual shape (per-slot, union-splittable, contact-bound), not whether the vocabulary resembles ours.

| analogue (read 2026-11-22, see `09`) | field | shape carried? | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lorenz's finite predictability horizon and ensemble forecasting | mathematics / weather | a forecast closes for a while, then multiple perturbed forecasts are needed | shadow for the predict-to-scenarios transition |
| Ashby's requisite variety: "only variety can destroy variety"; average temperature misses a single fever | cybernetics / engineering | per-slot monitoring and enough regulator variety to cover disturbance variety | shadow for Fig 1's per-slot rule and for scenarios needing independence |
| Holling's ecological resilience and regime shifts | ecology / complexity science | multiple stable states, thresholds, capacity to reorganize | shadow for the resilience branch |
| Woods' graceful extensibility and fundamental surprise | safety engineering | capability of the system-plus-habitat; surprises inconceivable in advance | shadow for resilience as precondition |
| Scenario planning's "several alternative future developments" | business / futures | a union held open; ground selects | shadow for the scenarios branch, but the source admits support is mostly anecdotal |
| Taleb's antifragility: "the resilient resists shocks and stays the same; the antifragile gets better" | risk / mathematics | a boundary marker, not the same property | echo for the resilience branch if used as a synonym; useful only as a contrast |

**Falsifier for Q4:** an analogue retained after it is shown not to carry the residual shape (per-slot, union-splittable, contact-bound). Then it is an echo and must be removed from `09_watson_answer.md`.

## CONCLUSION

The three stances are **isomer-like under Fig 12**: same residual atoms, different typed bonds, different decisions. Their order is by dependency, with channel integrity upstream (Fig 3, Fig 10). Their costs are in currencies that do not add (Fig 12). The world analogues are shadows where they carry the residual shape and an echo where they merely resemble the vocabulary; the echo is named and excluded. This framing rescues the trichotomy as a checkable structural claim without promoting it to a universal law.
