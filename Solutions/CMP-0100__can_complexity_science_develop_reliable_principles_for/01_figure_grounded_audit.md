## 0. The residual, in effect terms

A decision-maker with a bounded library faces a prediction gap R between what their model expects and what the ground returns. They must commit scarce budget to one of three stances:

| stance | what it commits to | what it spends on | what it leads to if right | what it leads to if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PREDICT | R closes under one model | refining a single forecast | cheap, decisive | budget spent on a term that cannot close; a live signal averaged away |
| PREPARE FOR MULTIPLE SCENARIOS | R is a set of conditions, not one | holding several candidate models | a union held open until the ground votes | a bundle mistaken for a list; single levers leave the intersections standing |
| RESILIENCE | the exact outcome need not be named | keeping contact with the ground and the capacity to refuse | survives surprise and regime change | a solved-state illusion; capability lost when the habitat changes |

The question asks whether complexity science can develop **reliable principles** for this choice. In effect terms, "reliable" can mean two different things, and the whole audit turns on keeping them apart:

- **Strong reading:** a domain-independent numerical rule that says "switch here" and works at every level without further measurement.
- **Weak reading:** a checkable routing shape, with a stated refuter, that narrows which stance fits a given residual before any frame is chosen, and is settled per case against the ground.

The figures license the weak reading and forbid the strong one. That is the finding, and it is stated before the world sources are consulted.

**What an answer would look like (bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, Formula step "Direction"):** a three-row mapping from a describable property of R to a stance, each row with its own refuter, plus an explicit statement of what the mapping cannot do. Binding it now makes it refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** R is a vector over object slots, not a scalar. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The three-way choice is therefore **per slot**, not a property of a whole system. Different slots of one organisation can need prediction, scenarios, and resilience at the same time.
- **Symmetry.** R is not symmetric between "new" and "refuted." Fig 5's fifth kind carries direction: a held regularity is false. The decision has a refusal branch that the four "what is missing" kinds cannot reach. The three stances are also not interchangeable operations on one quantity (Fig 12 :126, two currencies, below).
- **Scale.** R has a within-scale component (the per-step prediction gap) and a cross-scale component, the round trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :30). A term unreachable at one scale may be reachable at another, so a stance can flip when the scale changes. The trichotomy is level-relative, not absolute.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

1. **Domain 1, the prediction frame (forecaster's loop).** Actors: the agent, object slots s, the library Γ, the per-step loop perceive-plan-act-predict. Currency: per-slot residual R, predictions Γ(b,a) against observations o'. Anchor: the ground as the only metric (Fig 1 :23, *"The ground enters as the only metric."*). Question type: **partition** (which slot is live; refuse versus mint).
2. **Domain 2, the model-plurality frame (scenario planner).** Actors: multiple frames with differing closures, candidate terms φ and candidate scenarios. Currency: independence and overlap between frames, the union surplus. Anchor: the ground, where elimination compounds (Fig 9 :38, *"elimination compounds here, and only here"*). Question type: **union** (unbundle before searching again).
3. **Domain 3, the habitat / ground-maintenance frame (resilience operator).** Actors: the seat, the habitat (actors, conditions, relations), the channel. Currency: contact with the ground; capability as a property of agent-and-habitat. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Question type: **enumeration** (the habitat is enumerated, never composed).

### Partition or union

After unbundling, the three stances partition the **per-slot** decision. Before unbundling they can be a union compound: one system can need all three at once on different slots, and a single lever clears only the regions where one condition holds, leaving the intersections. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

So the honest claim is: the trichotomy is a partition of the per-slot decision **after** a union check. It is not a global three-way switch on a system as a whole. This distinction is load-bearing and appears again in F3.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 orders links by dependency, not importance: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* For this decision:

1. The channel to the ground must be reachable before any per-slot residual can be trusted (Domain 3 precondition).
2. The residual must be perceived per slot and described in effect terms before a stance is chosen (Domain 1).
3. Only then can a union compound be split into scenarios (Domain 2).
4. Refusal precedes mint (Formula step 2), and SEARCHED is asked before INWARD and OUTWARD (Formula step 7).

Order by dependency means resilience is a *precondition* on the other two, not the last resort in a temporal sequence. That is a non-obvious consequence of the census and it governs F5.

## 3. Findings F1..F8

### F1 — PREDICT is figure-governed, per slot, with a built-in stop condition (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

Fig 5 sorts the gap by kind: held (nothing owed), new (extend perception), mis-attached (refit, do not mint), mechanism broken (a mint is owed), and refuted (a term we hold is wrong). Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."*

So the predict rule is not "always refine the forecast." It is:

> Predict on a slot whose live residual is **trending down** under the current model. When the residual **persists and does not trend down**, the figures route it to REFUTED, and what is owed is a refusal, not a better forecast (Fig 5 amendment :59-60: a term whose residual "PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at").

Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* This is the exact reason a flat residual cannot be read as "predict harder." More search at the same reach is a third, distinct question (Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change."*).

**Refuter for F1:** a case where refining a single forecast closes a residual that is flat and persistent under a source-discriminating instrument, with no new term and no refusal. That would collapse the refuse/refine distinction.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot census), Fig 5 (routing taxonomy) and Fig 6 (depth versus absence), three lines with low overlap. `general` on the shape, `specified` on the trend-down criterion.

### F2 — MULTIPLE SCENARIOS is the correct stance for a union compound (triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* This is almost a verbatim definition of the scenario case: disagreement that does not shrink, each rule right on a subset.

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :54-56 require unbundling before searching again, because a single lever clears only where one condition holds and leaves the intersections.

Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."* Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* A set of scenarios earns its keep only when the frames behind them draw on different evidence. Several forecasts from one model are one frame wearing several names.

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Scenarios are held open; the ground, not the plurality, selects.

**Refuter for F2:** a case where the disagreement between candidate models shrinks monotonically with effort and one rule works across all subsets. Then it is one hard question, not a union, and the scenario stance is waste.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 (split/unbundle), Fig 8 (union surplus, independence) and Fig 7 (selection between rooms), low overlap. `specified`.

### F3 — The trichotomy is per slot after a union check, not a global switch (triangulated, and a correction to my first reading)

My first draft wrote the three stances as a partition of the whole decision. That was wrong. Fig 9 :54-57 makes the union case categorical, and Fig 1 :19 makes R a vector. A system can need all three stances at once on different slots.

Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* and *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The corrected claim: **check for a union first; then apply the three-way routing per slot; keep every cut ranked and reversible** (Fig 9 :40-41: *"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*).

**Refuter for F3:** a demonstrated case where one global stance optimises every slot simultaneously with no residual left on any other slot. Unlikely, but it would collapse the per-slot claim.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 + Fig 9). Recorded as a correction, not silently erased. This is the main retraction in this audit; see §6.

### F4 — RESILIENCE is a precondition on the other two, not a last resort (triangulated)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Fig 10 :25-28 names the failure modes, *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings,"* and says the output of that state is not a verdict but *"you have lost touch,"* with the instruction at :29: *"→ keep the anchor reachable."*

Fig 11 :39: *"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

So the resilience rule is: when the channel is degraded or the habitat has shifted so that contact is the binding constraint, no forecast closes the residual. The available move is to restore contact and introduce actors, not to subtract them, and the work is permanent. Because a degraded channel corrupts the per-slot residual that F1 and F2 depend on, resilience is upstream of them in the Fig 3 dependency order.

**Refuter for F4:** a case where improving the agent in isolation, with no change in contact and no change in habitat, measurably moves the ground. Fig 11 :72 already names this as the standing falsifier of the capability claim.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 (channel decay), Fig 11 (capability and asymmetry) and Formula step 8 (*"Nothing here maintains the ground. The ground does not decay; the channel does, and that is a seat's office, not the loop's."*). `specified`, with the capability half stated as `general`.

### F5 — The strong "reliable" claim is refused; the weak one survives (single-source for the refusal, and strained)

This is the crux of the question and it must not be averaged with F1 to F4.

The figures forbid a universal self-certifying decision rule:

- Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room."* A final three-way switch would be a last room.
- Fig 8 :34-37: a frame cannot reach its own metaframe, cannot certify its own limit, and cannot prove that any particular atom is far away. A principle that certified its own reliability across all domains would be doing exactly what these rules forbid.
- Fig 12 :104: *"So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* The switch point is level-relative.
- Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A numerical threshold is a filter for the budget; it is not a witness for the verdict.

What survives is the weak reading: a routing shape, per slot, with a refuter, settled per case against the ground. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and :138: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

**Status of the refusal:** single-source within the figures for the specific claim "no universal threshold" (it follows from the conjunction of Fig 7, Fig 8, Fig 9 and Fig 12, which are one evidence pool, not four witnesses). It is **strained** in the following precise sense: the world literature in `09_watson_answer.md` uses the word "principle" in the strong sense (Ashby's law, the law of requisite variety), so the term transfers with a strain. The strain governs the word "reliable," not the routing shape, so no re-census of the three domains is owed. It is named, not hidden.

**Refuter for F5:** a derived, domain-independent switch threshold that a frame can certify for itself and that survives a round trip across levels without a ground reading. If one is produced, this refusal is withdrawn.

### F6 — The three stances do not add, because they are priced in two currencies (single-source, clean)

Fig 12 :126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :127: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :128: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute."*

Prediction is cheap to state and may cost a small action to check on a live slot. Resilience over a changed habitat is expensive to state and may take a whole budget to settle. Scenarios buy independence, which is a third accounting again (Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*). Summing these into a single decision score is the error Fig 12 names, not the fix.

**Refuter for F6:** a single currency in which description cost, action cost and independence convert without loss, demonstrated on a real decision. None of the figures provides one.

**Status:** single-source (Fig 12 and the Symbols table share a pool). Clean, no strain.

### F7 — A frame cannot check the direction of its own three-way question (general, structural)

Formula "Direction": a frame enumerating its bound terms cannot check the direction of its own question, because the sought-for shape is a preference, not a term. The remedy is a discipline: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, let it accrue a residual. This is why §0 binds the sought-for shape, and why `02_outreach_email.md` asks a checker what they would count as a failure *before* showing the figures.

Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

**Refuter for F7:** a frame that derives, from its own bound terms alone, the correct choice of stance for a residual whose shape it has not characterised. This would dissolve the direction limit.

**Status:** `general`, structural, from Formula "Direction" plus Fig 10 and Fig 9. The three sources overlap, so this is one witness, honestly counted as one.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim is withdrawn if any of the following holds:

1. **Universal threshold fires.** A domain-independent numerical switch for predict / scenarios / resilience is derived that works at every level without ground settlement, and a frame can certify it for itself. (Breaks F5, Fig 7 :6, Fig 8 :34-37, Fig 12 :104.)
2. **No discriminator fires.** A case is demonstrated where the same residual is simultaneously a single live slot, a union compound, and a degraded channel, and no instrument distinguishes the three. (Breaks F1 and F2, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :54-57.)
3. **Capability claim fires.** An improvement to the agent in isolation, with no change of contact and no change of habitat, measurably moves the ground. (Breaks F4, Fig 11 :40, and Fig 11 :72's own standing falsifier.)
4. **Currency conversion fires.** A lossless conversion is shown between description bits, action cost, and independence. (Breaks F6, Fig 12 :126.)

If any fires, the relevant row of F8 is retracted and the answer is rewritten. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Use the trichotomy as a **per-slot routing discipline after a union check**, not as a global switch (F2, F3).
2. State the mode on every claim; never call a `specified` routing shape a settled law (F5, Formula mode rule).
3. Never let a priority score, probability, or tier hand the verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
4. Keep the anchor reachable first (F4). Prediction on a stale channel is motion, not progress (Fig 7 :32: *"one long room: motion, no progress"*).
5. Offer the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the routing rule and its refuter; offer the figures only after a verdict is recorded.
6. Report the round trip gap R_T when the rich figure set is condensed into a three-row table (see `11_audit.md`). Do not treat the condensation as identity.
