## 0. The question under audit

> "Do people across cultures use the same basic forms of reasoning but apply them to different problems?"

**The residual stated in effect terms.** The thing that fails to resolve is the *location of variation*. We observe differences in how people reason in different cultural settings. The question asks whether those differences are differences in the **form** (the reasoning mechanism itself) or differences in the **application** (the problems, contents, anchors, and habitats the same form is applied to). Stating the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, as the Formula's "Direction" requires:

> **SHAPE TERM (bound before the run):** an answer has the form `variant-slot = FORM | APPLICATION | BOTH`, plus a statement of the invariant's shape when one exists, plus a nameable ground that would settle which slot varies. It is refusable by the same test as every other term.

Four things are at stake, and they are kept separate:

| option | claim | leads to |
|---|---|---|
| A | the forms differ | no shared kernel; each culture its own closure; no common measure |
| B | one form, different problems | find the kernel, then map the contents |
| C | a union of shared and instituted forms | keep per-domain verdicts separate; the overlap is a compound with its own loop |
| D | the question itself is malformed | split it before searching; Fig 9 :4-5 |

What would count as an answer is **not** a list of cultural differences. It is a determination of which slot carries the residual, with an anchor that does not move when a frame is pushed (Fig 2 :29-30).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1a. R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** The comparison has at least two frames (two cultural settings) and two slots (FORM and APPLICATION). It is a relation, not a property of one culture. Under an identity reading it is a two-operand equality; under a union reading it is a compound of overlapping conditions.
- **Symmetry.** "Same form, different problem" is close to an **identity** claim (Operators table: *"two names, one referent"*, test *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*). Symmetry is therefore the test that decides the case: if swapping the cultural labels loses nothing, the form is shared; if the swap changes the term, the form is bound to its habitat. This is why the identity bond is examined in `06_isomers_and_order.md` rather than assumed.
- **Scale.** Two scales are present and must not be conflated: the individual reasoning event and the cultural aggregate. Fig 1 :11, *"residual R indexed by object slot"*, and Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global average across cultures is not a reading of the form; it is the erasure of the signal.

### 1b. One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. "No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet."

1. **Domain 1, the reasoning form (kernel).** Actors: the cognitive system and its primitives and bonds. Currency: whether an inference pays (predictive or explanatory success). **Anchor:** human performance judged independently of the researcher's frame. Fig 2 :37 supplies the rule: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* This question is constitutively about people, so people's reasoning is the anchor, not an external proxy. Nameable anchor, so this is a domain.
2. **Domain 2, the problems and the habitat.** Actors: languages, institutions, ecologies, practices, the problems actually posed. Currency: what counts as a problem and what resources are available for it. **Anchor:** the habitat in contact with the residual, which updates but is not the ground (Fig 11 :73, *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*). Nameable anchor, so this is a domain.
3. **Domain 3, the cross-cultural comparison (verification frame).** Actors: researchers, tests, instruments, cultural frames. Currency: residuals and agreement. **Anchor:** the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*). Nameable anchor, so this is a domain. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so its independence is low and declared.
4. **Domain 4, the framework itself (metatheory frame).** Actors: the thirteen figures, the kernel, the bonds, imports. Currency: composition inside `closure(Γ)` and settlement. **Anchor:** the figures as metalaws, plus the ground for any measurement. Nameable anchor, so this is a domain. Its verdict is about the *shape* of an answer, not about the empirical truth of universality.

### 1c. Partition or union

This is a **union**, not a partition. The question binds a kernel claim and an application claim in one sentence, and the two conditions overlap. Fig 9 :54-55: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* So "same forms, different problems" cannot be tested as one hypothesis. It must be unbundled into Domain 1 (is the form shared?) and Domain 2 (does the variation live in the problem?), and the overlap gets its own loop. **Verdict: union compound.**

### 1d. Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."*

1. **Perception.** Can we tell a form from a test of familiarity? (Fig 3 :5)
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for FORM and APPLICATION that are not the same term? This is the middle link with no instrument (Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*)
3. **Objective.** What would count as progress: showing which slot varies, not showing that cultures differ.
4. **Planning.** Find low-overlap frames (independent cultures, independent measures) (Fig 2 :31-32).
5. **Learn and carry.** Carry the method, not the recording (Fig 4 :22).

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Effort spent collecting more cultural differences cannot rescue a broken form/application distinction.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8 (verbatim quotes before conclusions)

### F1 — The framework's default shape is one kernel with variable cast

Fig 4 :19-20, verbatim:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

And the canonical usage rule generalizes the same distinction beyond size: the framework is *"domain-agnostic"*, and *"What changes between domains and levels is only the cast, never the rules"* (FIGURES_README_canonical.md). `FIGURES_README_canonical.md` states: *"This is why the same thirteen figures govern a board game, an organism, a market, or a physical theory: name the actors, the currency, the anchor, then apply the loop."*

**Conclusion (mode `general`, structural).** If human reasoning is a composing system of the kind the figures describe, then the framework's default prediction is **Option B**: the basic loop is invariant, and what varies across cultures is the actors (who reasons), the currency (what is reasoned about and what pays), and the anchor (what settles it). "Same basic forms, different problems" is therefore the *framework's predicted shape*, not a finding about human beings.

**Strain test and re-census.** Fig 4's own panel is about **scale** (one agent versus a thousand), not about cultural content. Read literally against cultures, the line strains. Re-census: the kernel/cast distinction is generalized to all domains by the canonical README, so the line legitimately governs the kernel-versus-cast distinction everywhere, while the *identification of cultures as instances with a shared kernel* remains a prediction of a domain-agnostic theory, not a derivation from cultural data. Strain named, not smoothed. Triangulation status: **triangulated** (Fig 4 plus the canonical domain-agnostic rule plus Fig 11 below), low overlap between the scale figure and the domain rule.

**Refuter.** Show a composing human system in which the loop itself changes with the culture while actors, currency and anchor are held fixed; or show that the figures' kernel/cast distinction does not generalize beyond scale.

### F2 — The anchor for this question is human judgment itself

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :36-38:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

**Conclusion (mode `specified`).** This question is constitutively about people, so the ground is people's actual reasoning. A test battery is an **instrument**, not the anchor, and instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* That is the figure-grounded reason a cross-cultural test that measures familiarity with the test format cannot settle the question: it is measuring the instrument's axes, not the anchor. This links directly to neighbouring question 37 in the source file (distinguishing reasoning ability from test familiarity).

**Refuter.** Show that the question is not constitutively about people (then human judgment is only an instrument), or exhibit an instrument whose readings do not move when frames are pushed and that discriminates form from familiarity. Triangulation status: **triangulated** (Fig 2 :29-30 and :36-38).

### F3 — Only convergent derivation under low overlap corroborates a shared form

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

**Conclusion (mode `specified`).** This gives the precise evidentiary rule for the cross-cultural question. If two cultural frames with **low evidence overlap** arrive at the same reasoning form independently, that is convergent derivation at maximum independence and it corroborates a shared kernel. If they share the form through contact, diffusion, or a common test instrument, their agreement is one witness wearing two names and carries no verdict weight. The world sources that report "the same reasoning form everywhere" must therefore be checked for independence, not counted. Triangulation status: **triangulated** (Fig 8 and Fig 2, different figures, low overlap).

**Refuter.** Exhibit two frames with demonstrably separate evidence chains that still fail to converge on any shared form; or show that agreement from a shared pool has the same verdict weight as convergent derivation.

### F4 — Do not average; keep the per-culture and per-slot residuals separate

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

**Conclusion (mode `specified`).** A single cross-cultural mean is not evidence about the form. Each culture and each form-slot keeps its own residual, and progress is made by selecting the next frame against the residual the last one surfaced, not by pooling votes. This is also why the world literature's "overall effect size" cannot by itself answer the question. Triangulation status: **triangulated** (Fig 1 and Fig 7, distinct figures).

**Refuter.** Show that the residual for "form" is correctly aggregated across cultures and that the pooled mean still discriminates which slot varies.

### F5 — The question's own separation of form from application is strained

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

**Conclusion (mode `specified`, and a correction to the question as asked).** "Same forms applied to different problems" assumes the form can be detached from the problems and examined alone. The figures deny that: capability is a property of the agent **and its habitat**, and the problems are part of the habitat. So the "different problems" are not an accessory applied to a finished form; they are part of what the form *is* at that stratum. This is the figure-grounded reason the union reading (1c) is forced rather than chosen. The naive Option B must be rewritten as: **an invariant loop instantiated only in agent-and-habitat pairs, where the habitat is in the term and not beside it.** Triangulation status: **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 9 union).

**Refuter.** Exhibit a reasoning capability that transfers to a new culture with no change in contact and no habitat dependence. Fig 11 :71 records that this claim has not been tested forward, so a refutation is live.

### F6 — The framework returns a shape; it cannot name which forms are universal

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :133:

> **"reached at all. A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."**

**Conclusion (mode `general`).** The Tether can say: *if* there is a shared kernel, it has the shape of the loop instantiated in agent-and-habitat pairs, and variation appears in cast. It **cannot** say which specific forms (analogy, categorization, causal attribution, dialectical reasoning) are the shared ones. Naming those requires the ground. So this derivation does not answer "which forms?" and must not pretend to. Triangulation status: **triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 13).

**Refuter.** Derive a specific universal reasoning form from the six-term set alone, with no import and no ground reading.

### F7 — STRAIN: the six-term substrate must not be imported into cognition here

Fig 13 :3 and :41:

> **"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."**
> **"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."**

**Strain.** A tempting reading is that all cultures reason by composing space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient and network, so the six-term set names the universal cognitive forms. This only fits with a strain. Fig 13 is a claim about the substrate of any composing system, and whether human reasoning is such a system *at that level* is an empirical question nobody has settled here. Fig 13 :143-144 itself says its indivisibility and minimality claims are *"refutable"* and that the gradient is *"the standing candidate for the first."*

**Re-census.** Fig 13 governs the primitives of composition in a composing system; it does not, by itself, census human cognitive primitives. The six are not used anywhere in this solution to name a reasoning form. They are invoked only as the framework's general cross-kind composition rule (Fig 13 :74, *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*) in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, and even there the operand type is flagged. Strain recorded and quarantined. Triangulation status: **single-source and strained**; no verdict rests on it.

**Refuter.** Derive one of the six from the others, or show a human reasoning system whose composition is empty of any of the six, or demonstrate that the substrate maps onto cognitive primitives without an import.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Two frames with demonstrably **low evidence overlap** fail to converge on any shared reasoning form across multiple tasks, while a common instrument is shown not to explain the divergence (breaks F1 and F3).
2. A reasoning capability is shown to transfer to a new habitat with no change in contact and no habitat dependence (breaks F5, Fig 11 :40).
3. A test is exhibited whose readings are invariant to the frames pushed on it **and** that discriminates reasoning form from test familiarity, and it shows the forms differ while the problems are held fixed (breaks F2 and Option B).
4. The kernel/cast distinction is shown not to generalize beyond scale, so Fig 4 cannot support a cross-domain prediction (breaks F1).
5. A specific universal reasoning form is derived from the six-term set with no import and no ground reading (breaks F6, and would move the claim toward `general` rather than `grounded`).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or rewritten. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`. This refuter was written before the world search in `09_watson_answer.md`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the **shape**, not with an answer to the yes/no question. The honest output is *"the variation is in the cast if the framework is right, and here is how to find out"* (F1, F6).
2. Unbundle the union before searching. Test Domain 1 and Domain 2 separately and keep the overlap as its own loop (1c, F5).
3. Name the anchor: people's reasoning. Treat test batteries as instruments that can be wrong on unmeasured axes (F2).
4. Require independence. Count convergent derivation across low-overlap frames, not pooled agreement or a shared literature (F3, F4).
5. Do not import the six-term substrate into cognition (F7).
6. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :23, *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* Lead with the refuter; offer the links only after a verdict is recorded.
