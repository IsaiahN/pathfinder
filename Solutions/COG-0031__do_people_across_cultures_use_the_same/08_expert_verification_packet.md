# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cross-cultural reasoning

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural psychologist or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the kernel/cast structure, not `grounded`.** No cross-cultural measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do people across cultures use the same basic forms of reasoning but apply them to different problems?" is a union compound and must be unbundled before it is searched. The framework's structural prediction is one invariant kernel with variable cast (actors, currency, anchor), but the "different problems" are not an outside input: capability is a property of the agent and its habitat, so the habitat is inside the capability. For a question constitutively about people, the anchor is human judgment and a test battery is only an instrument that can be wrong on unmeasured axes. Agreement across cultures corroborates a shared form only when the frames converged from low-overlap evidence; a shared pool is one witness. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so it does not say which forms are universal. The empirical yes or no is a `gap` owed to the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 11 and 2

- **Statement:** the kernel/cast distinction predicts that variation lives in actors, currency and anchor, and the habitat is inside the capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20, *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 11 :39, *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 2 :37, *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
- **What to check:** is the generalization from scale (Fig 4's own panel) to culture legitimate under the framework's domain-agnostic rule, or is it a strain? Is the anchor condition met, given that this question is constitutively about people?
- **Refuter:** show the kernel/cast distinction fails outside scale; or show a reasoning capability that transfers to a new habitat with no change in contact (breaks Fig 11 :40).

### C2 - the evidentiary rule; checkable against any cross-cultural "same form" result

- **Statement:** pooled agreement does not corroborate a shared form; only convergent derivation under low overlap does.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50-52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
- **What to check:** take a published cross-cultural result claiming a universal reasoning form. Trace the two frames' evidence chains. Do they draw on a common instrument, literature, or test translation? If so, discount the agreement. Do they converge from separate experience? If so, count it.
- **Refuter:** exhibit two frames with demonstrably separate evidence chains that still fail to converge across several tasks, with a common instrument ruled out.

### C3 - the measurement demand; checkable against test design

- **Statement:** because the question is about people, human judgment is the anchor and a test is an instrument that *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :38). What is owed is a discriminator between reasoning form and test familiarity, not more agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36-38, *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* and *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
- **What to check:** name a design that changes with reasoning form and not with familiarity. If no such design is possible, the question is unreached rather than answered, and the shape claim stays `candidate` (Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a test invariant to the frames pushed on it that discriminates form from familiarity and shows forms differing with problems held fixed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all cultures reason identically, nor that differences are only superficial.
- Not that any specific form is universal; the framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the six-term substrate names cognitive primitives. That reading is strained and quarantined (audit F7).
- Not that agreement across cultures is corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that a test battery is the anchor (Fig 2 :36-38).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a theory of everything; no last room (Fig 7 :6).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A form/familiarity discriminator that moves with form and not with familiarity.
2. An independence audit of each claimed universal form: convergent derivation counted, shared-pool agreement discounted.
3. A habitat swap: does the capability survive a change of habitat with contact held?
4. The identity/isomer/functional-group tests of `06_isomers_and_order.md` applied to each candidate form.

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
