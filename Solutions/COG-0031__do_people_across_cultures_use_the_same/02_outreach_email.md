# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the recipient's own terms. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cross-cultural reasoning, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do people across cultures use the same basic forms of reasoning but apply them to different problems?" bundles two claims that should be tested separately. The framework I am working from predicts a shape: if reasoning is the kind of composing system the figures describe, the loop is the invariant and what varies across cultures is the cast, that is, the actors, the currency (what is reasoned about and what pays), and the anchor (what settles it). Fig 4 puts it as *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* That is a prediction about a **shape**, not a measurement of any culture.
>
> Two corrections come with it, and they are part of what I want checked. First, the "different problems" are not an accessory applied to a finished form: capability is a property of the agent **and its habitat**, so the problems are inside the capability (Fig 11). Second, the framework returns a shape, not a name: it cannot tell you *which* reasoning forms are universal. That is for the ground to settle.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have shown people share basic forms, that cultural differences are only superficial, or that any particular test battery measures reasoning rather than familiarity. This question is constitutively about people, so human judgment is the anchor and a test is only an instrument, one that *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2). Nor am I counting agreement across cultures as evidence. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not two.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this search):**
> 1. two frames with demonstrably low evidence overlap fail to converge on any shared form across several tasks, and a common instrument is not the explanation;
> 2. a reasoning capability transfers to a new habitat with no change in contact and no habitat dependence;
> 3. a test is shown to discriminate form from familiarity, and with problems held fixed it shows the forms themselves differ;
> 4. the kernel/cast distinction fails to generalize beyond its original scale.
>
> I wrote these down before looking at the literature. If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures and the actual studies are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Cross-cultural variation review, Nichols 2025: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41809-025-00166-w
> - Core knowledge, Spelke and Kinzler 2007: https://cognitionandculture.net/wp-content/uploads/SpelkeKinzler07.pdf
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
