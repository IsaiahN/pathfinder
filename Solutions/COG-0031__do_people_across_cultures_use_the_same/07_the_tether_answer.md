# The Tether's answer: do people across cultures use the same basic forms of reasoning?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and `general` on the kernel/cast structure; not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We observe differences in reasoning across cultures and ask whether the variation lives in the **form** or in the **application**. The question as asked bundles both into one sentence, which Fig 9 :54 calls a union: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single search over "cultural differences in reasoning" will keep landing on a mixture. The residual is the unbundled pair: (a) is the loop invariant, and (b) where exactly does the variation sit?

## 2. The shape

1. **The framework's default prediction is one kernel with variable cast.** Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Generalized by the canonical domain-agnostic rule, this predicts that variation lives in actors, currency and anchor rather than in the loop. It is a `general` structural prediction, not a measurement of any culture. Strained as applied from scale to culture; corrected in `01` F1.
2. **The "different problems" are inside the capability, not beside it.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* So the naive "same form, different inputs" is wrong. The correct shape is an invariant loop instantiated only in agent-and-habitat pairs. This is a `specified` correction to the question itself (F5).
3. **The anchor for this question is human judgment, and a test is only an instrument.** Fig 2 :36-38: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* So the ground is people's reasoning, and a test that measures familiarity is wrong on the axis that matters.
4. **Only low-overlap convergent derivation corroborates a shared form.** Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* Plus Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Agreement that comes from diffusion, a common test, or a shared literature is one witness wearing two names.
5. **Do not average the cultures.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Each culture and each form-slot keeps its own residual; the next frame is selected against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :36).
6. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* It cannot say which specific forms are universal. "Same basic forms" could mean identity, isomer, or functional group (`06_isomers_and_order.md`), and only the ground can decide which.
7. **Empirical verdict: `gap` after a genuine re-census.** No figure line closes the yes/no question. The world literature genuinely conflicts: some sources find qualitative process differences, others find universal core systems with variable content, and one direct test found the same analogical ability with different attentional styles. That is a disagreement in the world, and it is owed to the ground, not to a figure re-reading.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the question is settled. Nothing here is `grounded`.
- Not that all cultures reason identically, nor that the differences are only superficial.
- Not that any particular form (analogy, categorization, causal attribution, dialectical reasoning) is the universal one. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the six-term substrate names cognitive primitives. That application is strained and quarantined (`01` F7).
- Not that agreement across cultures is corroboration. A shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that a test battery is the anchor. It is an instrument that can be wrong on unmeasured axes (Fig 2 :38).
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room."*

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A **source-discriminating instrument**, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Form/familiarity discriminator.** A measure that changes when the reasoning form changes and does not change when only test familiarity changes. Without it the anchor cannot be read.
- **Independence audit.** For each "same form" result, trace the evidence chain. If the frames drew on a common pool, discount the agreement (Fig 8 :50-52). If they converged from separate experience, count it.
- **Habitat swap.** Test whether a reasoning capability survives a change of habitat with contact held, or whether it moves with contact. Fig 11 :40 predicts the latter.
- **Three-way classification.** Apply the identity/isomer/functional-group tests of `06` to each candidate form.
- **Standing refuters from `01` section 4:** low-overlap frames that never converge; a capability that transfers with no habitat change; a discriminator that shows forms differing with problems held fixed; failure of the kernel/cast distinction to generalize.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books*), and declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Here the stop is earlier still: the framework gives the shape and the evidentiary rule, but the empirical claim needs a measurement nobody has taken in this derivation. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer whether people across cultures reason the same way, and it says so. It gives the shape any answer must have. If reasoning is a composing system of the kind the figures describe, the loop is the invariant and the variation lives in the cast, that is, in who reasons, what is reasoned about, and what settles it. But the "different problems" are not an accessory: capability is a property of the person and their habitat, so the problems are part of the form. The anchor for a question about people is people's judgment, not a test that may measure familiarity instead. And agreement across cultures is not evidence unless the frames converged independently, since a shared pool is one witness wearing two names. The framework cannot name which forms are universal, it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close the question. The empirical yes or no stays open until the ground settles it.
