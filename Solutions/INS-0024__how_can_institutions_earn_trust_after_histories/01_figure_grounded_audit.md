## 0. The residual in effect terms

Let Γ be an institution's library: its charter, its self-model, its published values, its history. Let b be the weighting that history gives its self-model, a an act of repair (an apology, a report, a reform, a payment), and o′ what affected people actually observe. The residual is

`R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` for each affected group s, indexed per slot, never aggregated.

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So a single institution-wide "trust score" can be near zero while one harmed group's slot carries a live positive residual. That is a legal state under the framework, not an inert one (Fig 1 :13).

The question "how can an institution earn trust" is therefore constitutively about the affected people's judgment of the institution's conduct. That makes the affected people the legitimate anchor. Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." The institution's own reputation metric is not this anchor, because it moves when frames push on it. Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

The word "after histories of discrimination, violence, or corruption" fixes the asymmetry. Harm came first, so the prior b is heavy and the residual persists. The psychology literature states the same shape independently: "Once trust is lost by violation of some combination of these three determinants, it is very hard to regain. There is asymmetry in the building versus destruction of trust" (Wikipedia, *Trust (sentiment)*, opened 2026-11-23).

**Direction bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** The sought-for shape is: *repair that is legible to the harmed group, verified by a seat the institution cannot reconstruct, and that changes contact rather than the institution in isolation*. This is written down now so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else. Before writing it, no internal check of the institution's frame could see the preference.

## 1. Domain census (arity, symmetry, scale; actors, currency, anchor)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: a three-place relation (institution Γ, repair act a, observed outcome o′), indexed by affected group s and ordered in time. Symmetry: "earned" is a threshold relative to what the harmed group would accept, a comparison bond, not a symmetric join (Fig 12 :87 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"). Scale: the individual institution loop, the population of affected groups, and the channel across the history; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point").

One line per domain. A set with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Institution's internal frame | leadership, staff, internal audit, PR | reputation metrics, compliance reports, self-assessment | none inside it: a self-rating updates on what frames think | admitted as a FRAME, not a ground |
| D2 Affected community and civil society | harmed groups, victims' organisations, advocacy bodies | lived outcomes, willingness to be vulnerable | their observed experience of the institution's conduct, which does not update on PR | admitted, legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :36-38) |
| D3 Independent verification seat | inspectors general, courts, commissions, journalists, researchers | checkable evidence with provenance | the same community ground, held via records | admitted, must not author what it checks (Fig 10 :42) |
| D4 The habitat in contact with the residual | harmed groups, bystanders, regulators, media, future affected people | contact and its second-order cascade | the ground; habitat itself updates and is enumerated | admitted (Fig 11 :43-52) |
| X1 "Institutional reputation" as a property held by the institution alone | the institution | standing | none nameable, it moves when pushed | NOT a domain, folded into D1 |
| X2 "Trust as a communications campaign" | the institution | messages | none, changes the institution not contact | absorbed into D1, see F3 |

**Partition or union.** D1 is a frame; D2, D3 and D4 form a compound. The whole is a union of two conditions, not one hard question: C1 (the anchor is reachable and correctable) and C2 (the institution's reach is renewed by contact with a differing closure). Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** perception of the residual per slot → vocabulary for what repair affords (correctability, voice, compensation) → objective (what would count as progress for each group) → planning (which mechanisms to introduce) → learn and carry (verified change restarts the loop). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link, vocabulary and objective, is where institutions usually break the chain, because a communications department has terms for messaging but not for what the harmed group would count as progress.

## 2b. Findings F1..F9

### F1: Trust is a relation to a ground the institution cannot move (decisive)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."

"Did the institution earn trust" is constitutively about the people it harmed. Their judgment is the anchor. The institution's reputation score is an instrument inside D1, and Fig 2 :38 warns that "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." A trust survey that pools the harmed group into a population average is exactly such an instrument failure.

**Refuter:** a controlled case in which a per-group residual falls durably while the harmed group's own reported outcomes and willingness to be vulnerable stay flat. If the institution's metric can move without the ground moving, it is not an anchor and this finding is wrong. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 :29 and :36-38, two low-overlap passages: anchor definition and legitimacy condition).

### F2: Asking for agreement cannot manufacture trust

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not."

An institution that asks stakeholders to affirm that it is trusted is running Fig 2 panel 1 (mutual update) or panel 2 (common cause). The psychology source adds the mechanism: in low trust relationships, "distress-maintaining attributions" focus on negative behaviour while "any positive acts on their part are met with skepticism" (*Trust (sentiment)*). A single reform act therefore does not close a persistent residual; the observer's weighting b keeps reading the history.

**Refuter:** a replicated case where one institution-issued reassurance, with no independent verification and no change in contact, produces a durable per-slot fall in the residual across low-overlap frames. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 7, anchor rule vs room chain).

### F3: Improving the institution in isolation changes nothing; contact is the lever

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

An ethics training programme, a new values statement, or an internal audit that never reaches the harmed group improves the agent, not the contact. The restorative justice literature reaches the same structure from criminology: repair is "a process where all stakeholders affected by an injustice have an opportunity to discuss how they have been affected by the injustice and to decide what should be done to repair the harm" (Braithwaite, quoted in Wikipedia, *Restorative justice*, opened 2026-11-23). The same source warns that "restorative justice programs often become offender-focused, minimizing the needs and experiences of victims," which is the D1 capture failure returned to census.

**Refuter:** an institution-only intervention that lowers the per-group residual with no measured change in contact with affected people. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 vs Fig 6, habitat capability vs closure/import; low overlap).

### F4: The channel decays, not the ground; and the history cannot be subtracted

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

The harm did not expire; the channel to the affected community did (reporting dropped, metrics saturated, groups pooled). Repair can introduce accountability, voice, and restitution. It cannot subtract the history, because contact is a fact about the world. Rebranding or spinning off a unit substitutes one habitat for another and inherits new actors that were not chosen. Procedural justice research supplies the instrument side: giving people "a voice regardless of whether it is instrumental ... or non-instrumental ... is sometimes enough for a process to be viewed as fair" (Tyler and colleagues, quoted in Wikipedia, *Procedural justice*, opened 2026-11-23). Voice is a way of keeping the channel reachable.

**Refuter:** a case where a history was removed by declaration alone, with no introduced mechanism, and the per-group residual fell. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 10 channel vs Fig 11 asymmetry).

### F5: Independence is load bearing, and a keeper with a reputation is disqualified

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."
Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

The cost of being wrong here is discrimination, violence, or corruption, so independence should be bought generously. An oversight body whose own reputation depends on a favourable finding is disqualified, as is an institution that authors and grades its own trust metric. The truth commission literature shows the failure concretely: a commission can be criticised "for promoting impunity and further traumatising victims," and in South Africa victims asked "We've heard the truth. There is even talk about reconciliation. But where's the justice?" (*Truth commission*, opened 2026-11-23). Truth-telling whose consequences are authored by the institution it examines is a convention, not a verdict.

**Refuter:** a replicated case where a fully reconstructible, reputation-exposed verifier produces per-slot residuals that match a genuinely independent verifier at maximum independence. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 10 seat laws vs Fig 2 independence/cost rule).

### F6: Repair is a union of mechanisms, not one lever

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

Transitional justice names the union explicitly: mechanisms "include criminal prosecutions, truth commissions, reparations programs, and various kinds of institutional reforms" and "apologies, amnesty, memorials" (*Transitional justice*, opened 2026-11-23). A public apology is itself a molecule, not a word: "The process consists in three components: acknowledgment of wrongdoing, admission of responsibility and the action of the wrongdoer to compensate damages produced" (*Public apology*, opened 2026-11-23). A lever that clears acknowledgment without compensation leaves the intersection standing.

**Refuter:** a case where a single mechanism clears both conditions and leaves no categorically different residue. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 union rule vs Fig 10 convention guard; corroborated by two distinct transitional-justice pages, which are one witness pool, not two).

### F7: A repair claim with no stated residual is not a mint

Fig 5 :20: "is there anything to explain?"
Fig 5 :41: "The three guards are a product, not a checklist."
Fig 5 :48: "Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."
Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

An institution that announces "we have restored trust" without naming which group's residual failed to resolve has no support term. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." So this audit does not name truth commissions or reparations as *the* answer. It characterises the shape any answer must have and leaves the specific mechanism to the ground.

**Refuter:** a figure line that, after re-census, names a specific repair mechanism for this class of case. Then the gap closes. **Status: gap by design**, corroborated by Fig 9 and the canonical README (see §5).

### F8: Source discrimination, not more argument

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

An institution's reading (high satisfaction, low complaint volume) can come from several sources: genuine repair, a silenced group, a pooled metric, or a surveyed sample that excludes the harmed. The reading does not determine the source. This is the metamerism error applied to trust. What is owed is a discriminator that ranges against the harmed group separately, not another argument about sincerity.

**Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that shows one source for a given trust reading across low-overlap frames. **Status: triangulated** (essay mechanism vs Fig 1 per-slot and Fig 2 anchor).

### F9: Prestige is not action

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened 2026-11-23): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A respected institution, a famous auditor, or a high public-approval score funds a repair attempt. It does not settle whether the repair worked. Selecting expert checkers for how likely they are to agree would manufacture the disqualifying correlation (Seat Map :163). The first contact must therefore carry the residual and its refuter, not a request for endorsement.

**Refuter:** a case where prestige alone settled a per-group residual without ground contact. **Status: triangulated** (essay vs Seat Map :163 and Fig 2 :40).

## 2c. Strains and re-census

- **Apparent strain, Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not" vs the practice of vetting and removing abusive officials.** Re-census: vetting introduces a new personnel rule and new actors; it does not subtract the historical fact that the institution caused harm. The habitat (the harmed community, the record) remains. No strain after re-census. Corroborated by *Transitional justice*, which lists vetting and lustration as mechanisms of institutional reform.
- **Apparent strain, Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." vs Fig 2 :29 listing markets and counters as anchors.** Re-census: legitimacy is constitutive. For "did the institution earn trust" the question is about people, so human judgment is the legitimate anchor. For "did the payment clear" a counter is legitimate. Two different questions, different anchors. No strain.
- **Apparent strain, F5 verdict that reputation-exposed keepers are disqualified vs the practical need for institutions to publish their own data.** Re-census: institutions may publish records as a maker produces a failure record (Seat Map Seat 1), but may not author the verdict that grades those records. No strain.
- **Remaining strain, named not hidden:** the harmed group is both the legitimate anchor and a participant in the habitat, so it can be captured or pressured. Fig 10 :85 says the position, not the occupant, selects the seat. How to keep an affected community's judgment unpressured in a real case is an instrument question, not settled here.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Seat Map :287 "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A replicated, controlled case where an institution-issued reassurance with no independent verification and no changed contact produces a durable per-slot fall in the residual across two low-overlap frames.
2. A case where aggregate trust rises while the harmed group's slot residual does not trend down, and the framework has no way to refuse the aggregate (breaks Fig 1 :19-22).
3. A case where a verifier fully reconstructible by the institution, with a reputation to protect, agrees at maximum independence with a genuinely independent verifier (breaks Fig 10 :42, :93 and Fig 2 :40).
4. A single repair mechanism that clears both C1 (reachable, correctable anchor) and C2 (renewed reach) and leaves no categorically different residue (breaks Fig 9 :55-57).
5. A source-discriminating instrument showing that the harmed group's reported trust reading comes from one source, with no lossy projection among the alternatives (breaks F8).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the per-slot residual and the anchor test, not with a claim that any institution is trustworthy (F1, F2).
2. Instrument the channel before pricing reforms: voice, correctability, disaggregated reporting per affected group (F4, Fig 3 :27).
3. Buy independence in proportion to the harm: a verifier the institution cannot reconstruct and whose reputation does not depend on the finding (F5).
4. Introduce mechanisms as a union, with provenance for each, and expect a categorically different residue after each single lever (F6).
5. Name the anchor source that is missing in effect terms, then import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 6 :23). Do not name a policy as the answer.
6. Send the generator, the residual, the shape, and the refuter. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

### The three formula questions, kept distinct

1. **SEARCHED (cheapest, asked first).** Is the repair mechanism already inside the institution's closure and simply unfound? Many institutions already hold complaint channels, ombudsman offices and review boards; more internal search at the same reach adds no atom (Fig 8 :32).
2. **INWARD.** Sharpen the instrument already returning something: disaggregate the trust survey by affected group, add correctability and voice measures (Leventhal's rules include "correctability" and "representativeness"). Improve from the existing survey, never from a description of an ideal survey (Fig 6 :32).
3. **OUTWARD.** Import the affected community's frame, described in effect terms first. The world literature supplies candidates (truth commissions, reparations, procedural-justice voice, restorative conferences), each with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence.

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and were not read against each other. "Underivable-in-principle" and "unreachable-at-this-budget" were kept distinct: naming the specific policy is underivable from the figures; finding the already-held complaint data is a budget question.

## 6. Verdict summary

The shape survives after the canonical check. Trust after a history of harm is not a property the institution can assert or manufacture by agreement. It is a relation settled by an anchor the institution cannot move: the observed experience and judgment of the people it harmed, measured per group. Repair requires a reachable and correctable channel (voice, disaggregated reporting), a verifier the institution cannot reconstruct and whose reputation does not depend on the finding, and mechanisms introduced as a union that change contact. The history cannot be subtracted and rebranding only substitutes habitat. The specific policy that closes a given case is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` on the shape, `general` on the anchor and independence laws, `candidate` until a ground reading pays. Strains after re-census: none undecided; one instrument question (protecting the affected group's judgment from pressure) is named and left open.
