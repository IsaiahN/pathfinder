## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about an institution earning trust after discrimination, violence or corruption. Option A: accept the institution's own claim that it is reformed and ask affected stakeholders to affirm it. Option B: range the claim against an anchor the institution cannot move, keep the channel to that anchor reachable, and require a verifier the institution cannot reconstruct. What each leads to: A produces agreement inside a shared evidence pool, which the figures disqualify as a verdict, and a population-average trust score that can hide a live harm slot. B produces a checkable shape, a pre-registered refuter and an open slot until the ground settles which policy works.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per affected group s. Arity: a three-place relation (institution Γ, repair act a, observed outcome o′), indexed per slot and ordered in time. Symmetry: "earned" is a threshold relative to what the harmed group would accept, a comparison bond (Fig 12 :87). Scale: the single institution loop, the population of affected groups, and the channel across the history; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4). Fig 1 :19 and :22 require per-slot reading and forbid aggregation as a way to find where to look.
- **D1 Institution's internal frame.** Actors: leadership, staff, internal audit, communications. Currency: reputation metrics, compliance reports, self-assessment. Anchor: none nameable inside it, because a self-rating updates on what frames think. Status: admitted as a frame, not as a ground. Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
- **D2 Affected community and civil society.** Actors: harmed groups, victims' organisations, advocacy bodies. Currency: lived outcomes, willingness to be vulnerable. Anchor: their observed experience of the institution's conduct, which does not update on public relations. Status: admitted, legitimate anchor because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38).
- **D3 Independent verification seat.** Actors: inspectors general, courts, commissions, journalists, researchers. Currency: checkable evidence with provenance. Anchor: the same community ground held through records. Status: admitted; must not author what it checks (Fig 10 :42).
- **D4 The habitat in contact with the residual.** Actors: harmed groups, bystanders, regulators, media, future affected people. Currency: contact and its second-order cascade. Anchor: the ground; the habitat itself updates and is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43-48).
- **X1 Reputation as an institution-held property.** No nameable anchor, moves when pushed. Folded into D1. X2 "trust as a communications campaign" changes the institution, not contact, and is absorbed into D1 (Fig 11 :40).
- **Union, not partition.** D1 is a frame; D2, D3 and D4 form a compound. The whole is a union of C1 (a reachable and correctable anchor) and C2 (renewed reach through contact with a differing closure). Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the residual per group → vocabulary for what repair affords (voice, correctability, compensation) → objective (what counts as progress for each group) → planning (which mechanisms to introduce) → learn and carry (verified change restarts the loop). Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Strain test applied: vetting and lustration look like subtraction of history but are re-censused as introduction of new personnel rules; the history of harm remains in contact. The plurality of anchors in Fig 2 is resolved by legitimacy being constitutive: for a question about people, human judgment is the legitimate anchor; for a question about a payment, a counter is. No strain after re-census. One instrument question remains open and named: how to keep the harmed group's judgment unpressured.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: trust after a history of harm is not manufactured by the institution's agreement or by an average score. It is a relation settled by an anchor the institution cannot move, the harmed people's judgment read per group. Repair requires a reachable and correctable channel, a verifier the institution cannot reconstruct and whose reputation does not depend on the finding, changed contact rather than institution-only improvement, and a union of introduced mechanisms whose arrangement carries the meaning. The history cannot be subtracted and rebranding substitutes habitat. Which policy works is a declared `gap`. The verdict is kept separate per domain, never averaged. Mode `specified` on the shape, `general` on the anchor and independence laws, `candidate` until the ground pays.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected repair. An institution failing the channel condition would be sold an outside reform, or one failing the contact condition would be told to publish more, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and reversible. The avoidable part is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted when a refutation arrives. The refusal is added to the record and the reach falls "because something was added rather than because anything was deleted," so a wrong shape can be corrected without erasing the lineage (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-group instrument that discriminates genuine repair from a silenced group or a pooled metric would name the missing discriminator; a replicated case of reassurance alone lowering every group's residual would break the anchor claim; aggregate trust rising while the harmed slot stays flat would break Fig 1 :19-22; a reconstructible, reputation-exposed verifier matching an independent one would break Fig 10 :42 and :93; one lever clearing both conditions with no categorically different residue would break the union claim; a source-discriminating instrument showing one source only would close the metamerism gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
