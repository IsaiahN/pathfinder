## Restating the question as an effect

"Earn trust after a history of harm" is decided as follows: an institution holds a model of its own trustworthiness (Γ), it acts (a), and affected people observe something (o′). The residual `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` is indexed per affected group, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Option A: the institution issues reassurance, reputational metrics, and internal review, and asks to be trusted. Option B: the institution ranges its claims against an anchor it cannot move, the observed outcomes and judgment of the people it harmed, and keeps that channel reachable through independent verification. A leads to agreement inside a shared evidence pool, which the figures disqualify as a verdict (Fig 2 :40). B leads to a checkable shape with a stated refuter and an open slot until the ground settles which mechanism works. The answer below is B.

## What a finished answer would look like

An answer is a **shape** with three properties: (1) it names the anchor that settles "is trust earned" and shows the institution cannot move it; (2) it states, per affected group, what would have to change in contact for the residual to fall; (3) it states, before any claim, what would refute it. It does not name a single policy as the answer, because the framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. **Agreement is not the anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies their agreement (Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention").
2. **A verifier that holds the interior cannot verify.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." So the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
3. **Reputation is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

Legally: unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
