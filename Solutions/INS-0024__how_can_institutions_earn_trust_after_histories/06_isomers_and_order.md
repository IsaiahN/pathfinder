## RESIDUAL

The question uses the word "earn" as if trust were a substance accumulated by one act. Two questions are owed: (1) is a repair a single act or an ordered molecule, and (2) are two repairs with the same ingredients the same thing? Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in Γ (available to any repair): acknowledgment of wrongdoing, admission of responsibility, compensation or restitution, institutional reform, independent verification, voice for the affected, disaggregated reporting, and the historical record. Bonds held: the seven, of which → (sequence) and ⋛ (comparison) are ordered, and ¬ (negation) is the unary operator for refusal. No new primitive is available without import from a world frame. Symmetry: the atoms do not commute; swapping acknowledgment and compensation gives a different repair (Fig 12 :74 "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes.").

Scale: one repair molecule per affected group slot (Fig 1 :19), with a second-order comparison when the institution's aggregate claim is ranged against the group's outcome (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it").

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is a repair an ordered molecule?

- Fig 12 :30: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (the → test).
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

The world source *Public apology* (opened 2026-11-23) independently gives the same structure: "The process consists in three components: acknowledgment of wrongdoing, admission of responsibility and the action of the wrongdoer to compensate damages produced." Three atoms in an order, not one word. Compensate before acknowledging, or acknowledge without admitting responsibility, and the molecule is different. ⇒ (production) is the right bond for compensation: "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77). The reparations literature supports the distinction: material reparations (money, land) and symbolic reparations (apologies, memorials) are different axes, "material or symbolic and individual or collective" (*Transitional justice*, opened 2026-11-23).

**Claim (specified):** an apology without an admission of responsibility is not a weaker instance of the same repair; it is a different molecule. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two arrangements of the same atoms behave identically on a per-group outcome measure across low-overlap frames, the order claim fails and the bond is symmetric after all.

### Q2: Are different repairs isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

A truth commission with amnesty and a truth commission with criminal prosecutions share most atoms (truth-seeking, public hearings, a final report, acknowledgment) and differ in the bond to accountability: one uses ¬ on prosecution, the other uses → to it. By Fig 12 they are isomers, not versions of one thing with different intensity. The transitional justice literature reports the behavioural difference: commissions can be criticised for "allowing crimes to go unpunished, and creating impunity for serious human rights abusers," and the field's own debate contrasts the "justice" school and the "peace" school (*Truth commission*; *Transitional justice*). That is the "decisive in the behaviour" clause.

A weaker reading is available: all such repairs share the functional group "acknowledge → repair → verify" (Fig 12 :124), and differ in which member fills each slot. **Falsifier:** if two mechanisms with the same atoms but different bonds produce the same per-group residual trajectory, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3: What is the order of the union?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

The union of C1 (reachable, correctable anchor) and C2 (renewed reach through contact) is not a list of two tasks. Fixing C1 alone (a complaint channel nobody uses) leaves C2 binding; importing C2 alone (an outside reform the harmed group cannot correct) leaves C1 binding. The intersection is a specific compound with its own loop. Dependency order from Fig 3: perceive the residual per group → build vocabulary for what repair affords → say what counts as progress → plan mechanisms → carry verified change. Effort spent above the break (more communications) cannot rescue a missing instrument below it (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not").

**Falsifier:** one intervention that clears both conditions and leaves no categorically different residue breaks the union claim (same as refuter 4 in `01` §3).

### Q4: Is the repair a cycle or a chain?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 10 :94 in the formula's closure note (THE_FORMULA.md step 8): "the ground does not decay; the channel does, and that is a seat's office, not the loop's."

A repair whose verified outcome changes the residual, which restarts the loop, is a cycle. Its sign decides whether it closes: a reform that increases contact and lowers the per-group residual closes toward repair; a reform that substitutes a synthetic habitat (an internal survey, a test harness) closes toward self-confirmation. Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A chain, by contrast, cannot close: a one-off apology with no feedback path is a chain that ends, and Fig 12 says it cannot close by construction.

**Falsifier:** a one-off repair with no feedback path that nonetheless produces a durable, ground-confirmed fall in the per-group residual across two low-overlap frames would refute the cycle requirement. It would show a chain can close in this domain, which the figure says it cannot.

### Q5: What is refused, not added?

- Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- Fig 5 :53-55: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."
- Fig 6 :42-43: "the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."

An institution that holds the term "we are a trusted institution" and receives a ground reading showing otherwise owes a refusal of that term, not a new explanation. The record grows (the refusal is added); the reach falls (the old self-description can no longer be composed over). This is why a truth commission's finding can change what an institution may subsequently claim, without deleting anything from the historical record. Seat Map :188 states the same law for arrangements.

**Falsifier:** a framework that deletes the refused term from the record rather than adding its refusal would lose the ability to distinguish never-held from held-and-given-up; if a real institution could do that and still be checkable, this claim fails.

## CONCLUSION

A repair is an **ordered molecule**: acknowledgment → admission → compensation (production) → institutional reform → independent verification, with → and ⇒ as ordered bonds, ⋛ as the comparison that decides "earned", and ¬ for the refusal of a self-description the ground has overturned. Repairs sharing atoms but differing in the bond to accountability are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117, not intensities of one thing; the weaker **functional group** reading (Fig 12 :124) is always available and is the honest fallback if the isomer test fails. The union of the two conditions is a **compound**, and a durable repair is a **cycle** whose sign can run toward contact or toward self-confirmation. Every claim has a written falsifier.
