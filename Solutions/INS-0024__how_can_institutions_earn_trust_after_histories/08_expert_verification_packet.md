# Expert verification packet: the Tether on institutional trust repair

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher or practitioner in institutional trust repair can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. One-to-one use only.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the anchor and independence laws. Not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Earned trust" after discrimination, violence or corruption is not a property an institution can assert or manufacture by agreement. It is a relation to an anchor the institution cannot move: the observed experience and judgment of the people harmed, measured per group so an average cannot hide a live slot. Trust is therefore repaired by change in contact, not by changing the institution in isolation: a reachable and correctable channel (voice, disaggregated reporting), an independent verifier the institution cannot reconstruct and whose reputation does not depend on the finding, and mechanisms introduced as a union (acknowledgment, admission, compensation, reform, verification) whose arrangement carries the meaning. The history cannot be subtracted; rebranding substitutes habitat. The framework returns this shape and a refuter written in advance, but does not name which policy closes a given case.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the anchor that settles "is trust earned" is the affected people's judgment, read per group; an institution-owned trust score is an instrument, not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
  - Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
  - Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
  - Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
- **Refuter:** a replicated case in which an institution-issued reassurance, with no independent verification and no change in contact, durably lowers the residual for each harmed group across two low-overlap frames; or a case where aggregate trust rises while the harmed group's own outcome measure does not trend down and the aggregate cannot be refused.

### C2: world-facing; checkable against the trust-repair and transitional-justice literature

- **Statement:** repair is a union of mechanisms, not one lever, and the arrangement of the parts carries the result. An apology is a molecule (acknowledgment → admission → compensation), not a word.
- **What to check:**
  - *Public apology* defines apology as three components: "acknowledgment of wrongdoing, admission of responsibility and the action of the wrongdoer to compensate damages produced."
  - *Transitional justice* lists mechanisms "include criminal prosecutions, truth commissions, reparations programs, and various kinds of institutional reforms" as well as apologies and memorials.
  - *Truth commission* records the contrary failure: a process can be criticised "for promoting impunity and further traumatising victims," with victims asking "We've heard the truth. There is even talk about reconciliation. But where's the justice?"
  - Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Refuter:** a single mechanism that clears both the reachable-anchor condition and the renewed-reach condition and leaves no categorically different residue; or two mechanisms with the same atoms but different bonds producing identical per-group residual trajectories.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a world check

- **Statement:** repairing trust requires changed contact, not just an improved institution, and independence is load bearing in proportion to the harm.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
  - Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
  - Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
  - Fig 10 :93 "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
- **World check:** restorative justice research reaches the contact claim independently ("all stakeholders affected by an injustice have an opportunity to discuss how they have been affected"), while also reporting the capture failure that programmes "often become offender-focused, minimizing the needs and experiences of victims." That internal conflict was returned to the census and strengthens C3; it did not flip the verdict.
- **Refuter:** an institution-only intervention that lowers the per-group residual with no measured change in contact; or a fully reconstructible, reputation-exposed verifier agreeing at maximum independence with a genuinely independent one.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named institution is trustworthy or untrustworthy; no institution was measured.
- Not that a single policy (truth commission, reparations, vetting, apology) is the answer. That is a `gap` to be settled by the ground.
- Not that the harmed group's judgment is infallible. It is legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people; instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :38).
- Not that more experts, or more prestigious experts, corroborate the claim. Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."
- Not that this packet is `grounded`. It is a simulated derivation, and it cannot settle what it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-group outcome instrument with voice and correctability that discriminates genuine repair from a silenced group, a pooled metric or an exclusionary survey.
2. An independence test: can the institution reconstruct the verifier's finding before it is returned? A yes disqualifies the verifier.
3. A stake test: does the verifier's reputation depend on a favourable result? A yes disqualifies it.
4. A contact test: did any measured contact with affected people change, or only internal conduct?
5. A lever test: after one mechanism, is the residue categorically different?

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument, closes the declared gap.
