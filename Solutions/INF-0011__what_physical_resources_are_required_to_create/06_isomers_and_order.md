# Are the four operations isomers, and what order are they in?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading
(see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

`01` established that create, store, transmit and erase are four slots with different symmetry. Two
questions remain: (1) if each is written over the same six-term set, are they **isomers** under Fig 12,
or merely members of one functional group; and (2) what is the dependency order of the four, and does
their composition close a cycle?

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) plus Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held:

- **R.** Arity four, asymmetric, scale first order (one bit in a bath) and second order (a memory cycle).
- **D1 composition.** Actors: six terms, seven bonds, negation. Currency: composition in closure(Γ).

- **D2 physics.** Actors: memory, bath, barrier, channel. Currency: work, heat. Anchor: calorimetry.
- **D3 order.** Actors: the four operations. Currency: dependency. Anchor: which link can be measured.

Dependency order: a state must be created before it can be stored, stored before it can be transmitted,
and the measurement must be reset before the cycle repeats (Fig 12 :121).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the four operations isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible
in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose
presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Write the four operations over the shared atom set:

| operation | minimal reading | atoms used | bond that does the work |
|---|---|---|---|
| create | a state is set in a region | Space, Energy, Spectrum | ⇒ production of a new record |
| store | a state is held against thermal noise | Space, Time, Gradient | held Gradient over space (barrier) |
| transmit | influence travels between regions | Space, Network, Time | Network, with a gradient to launch |
| erase | a state is reset to one value | Energy, Gradient, Time | spent Gradient, remainder to bath |

The four do **not** share an identical atom set, so under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**;
they are members of one **functional group** (a state is brought to a known value in a bounded region)
with different atoms in the slots. That is the weaker, safer claim.

The stronger form: if all four are written over the **full shared set**
`{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}`, differing only in which structure mediates which
state pair, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is the
claim worth testing, and it needs an instrument that holds the atom set fixed across operations.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any operation requires an atom none of the others use (for
example transmit requires Space but erase does not, and no common superset can be held fixed), the
isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 - What is the order, and why does erase come last in the cycle?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one
above produces"*. The order is create → store → transmit, then erase to close the loop. Fig 12 :121:
*"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain
cannot close by construction."*

Erase is last not because it is least important but because it consumes the record that the first
three produce. The one-bit memory must be back at a standard state before the next measurement. World
source read 2026-11-23 (Georgescu 2021): it is *"the erasure of the information in the memory (to
enable the next measurement to be recorded)"* that saves the second law, as Bennett pointed out in
1982. This is world corroboration of the order, not a figure ruling on it.

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave
differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* The bath survives the cycle; the
memory does not survive its own use unless reset. **Limit of the analogy:** a chemical catalyst is
unchanged by the reaction, whereas the bath absorbs heat and is only restored ideally at cycle end.
The analogy identifies the role, not the mechanism. Named as a limit, not hidden.

### Q3 - Which bonds express the four operations?

Fig 12 :131-132 of the extract gives the bond tests. The relevant ones:

- ⇒ production: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Fig 12 :76). Create is production of a
  record in a region.
- − subtraction: *"A with B removed or absent"* (Fig 12 :82). Erase resembles subtraction at the
  level of the memory's state set: a possibility is removed. But Fig 12 :136 (amendment) says negation
  is not subtraction: *"− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation
  is the refusal of a claim."* Erase is closer to removal of an operand (a state is taken out of the
  set) than to a refusal of a claim, so − is the better bond of the two. This is a `specified`
  structural reading, not a physics result.
- → sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). The cycle order.
- ⋛ comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87). Erase succeeds when
  the barrier exceeds kT, a threshold relation between the barrier height and the thermal energy.
  This is a genuine use of ⋛ at first order between two quantities, unlike the mistyped second-order
  use in the QMGR exemplar. Still `specified`: whether the comparison is the right bond for the
  physical threshold needs the instrument, not an argument.

**Falsifier:** if erase cannot be written as removal of a state from the accessible set, or if the
barrier threshold fails to behave as a comparison between two quantities, Q3 is withdrawn.

### Q4 - Is the cycle second-order?

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a
fact about a level and never about the world."* Each first-order operation, settled against the ground,
can serve as an atom at the next level. The memory cycle is therefore a second-order composition of
four settled first-order operations. It needs no seventh primitive and no import that moves the wall.

**Strain, named:** at second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. The
figure permits the demotion to atom, but whether an atom counts as a quantity for ⋛'s threshold test is
a type question that needs an instrument. This is the same live strain the exemplar records for QMGR;
it is not smoothed over here either.

## CONCLUSION

The four operations are **not isomers as written**: they share a functional group, not an identical
atom set. The stronger isomer claim (all four over the full shared six-term set) is a real test with a
stated falsifier. Their dependency order is create → store → transmit → erase, with erase last because
it consumes the record and closes the loop. Create is production (⇒), erase is removal (−) with a
threshold comparison (⋛) against kT, and the cycle is a legal second-order composition under Fig 12's
settled-molecule rule. The bath is the catalyst-like part that survives; the memory is the spent part.
One type strain (⋛ at second order) remains named.
