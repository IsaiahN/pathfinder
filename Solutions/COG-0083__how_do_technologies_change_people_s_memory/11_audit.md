## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down,
`gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the
round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (slot-indexed residual), Fig 4 :22 and :30 (membrane direction and round trip), Fig 6 :23 and :25 (import and closure), Fig 9 :54 (union).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: four domains, contact not agent, record versus reach, channel not ground, import not new atom, gap for the number.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim a reader would actually make, for example "a note-taking app damages memory", "a feed shortens attention spans", "social media makes people believe false things".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **four-way union**, which the concrete claim collapses into one verdict about technology;
- the **record/reach distinction**, which the concrete claim restates as erasure, a claim the figures explicitly refuse;
- the **anchor requirement**, which the concrete claim replaces with agreement or self-report.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a technology changes what a person is in contact with, and the record and the reachable set are different quantities. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
