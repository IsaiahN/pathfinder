# Union, order and analogues: supporting derivation for COG-0083

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles four effects. Three structural questions are owed: (1) are the four domains
isomers, a functional group, or something else; (2) in what order must they be examined; (3) does
the same technology have a fixed valence, or is valence relative to the residual? A fourth,
practical question follows: what is the bond that distinguishes a record that generates from a
record that only repeats?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: frame, library Γ, closure(Γ), ground, habitat, channel, instrument, slot, residual R,
bond types including → sequence, − subtraction, ⋛ comparison, and ¬ negation. No new primitive is

over these atoms, not new atoms.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Are the four domains isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

As the question writes them, the four domains do **not** share one atom set: attention is built on
slot and residual, communication on channel and frame, epistemics on closure and instrument, memory
on library and reach. Under Fig 12's definition they are therefore **not isomers**. They are a
**functional group**: each has the substructure `frame ranges against ground through a changed
contact`, and the qualifier (which contact) says which member.

The compound cases are where the stronger word applies. "Remember where, not what" can be written
over the same atoms `{store, cue, method, frame}` with two different bonds: store as a head start
for a new search, or store as a replay of one occasion. Same atoms, different bond, different
substance. That is a real isomer claim, and it is the one worth testing.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either arrangement behaves identically under every
instrument we can build, the bond carries nothing and the isomer distinction collapses to the flat
formula Fig 12 warns about.

### Q2  -  What order are the four domains in?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Order derived: attention (perception, link 1) before memory (vocabulary and learn-and-carry, links 2
and 5) before the sense of what can be known (the objective, link 3) before communication (the
membrane, Fig 4). The middle link, "what would count as progress", is the one Fig 3 :27 says *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

This is why the most common intervention order is backwards. Improving the instrument that
searches the web (link 1 extended) cannot repair an unmeasured objective (link 3), and effort spent
above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20-21).

**Falsifier:** if a change in the knowable (link 3) can be demonstrated with perception and
vocabulary held fixed, the dependency order is wrong and must be re-censused.

### Q3  -  Does a technology have a fixed valence?

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11
:51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

A search engine can be a benefactor to the residual "find a source in an unfamiliar field" and a
malfactor to the residual "retain what you encoded ten minutes ago". The same object is both; the
relation changes with the residual, not the object. This dissolves the familiar question "is
technology good or bad for cognition" into four well-formed questions, one per residual, which is
the union finding F1 restated at the level of valence.

**Falsifier:** produce a standing list of technologies that are beneficial for every residual
without exception. By Fig 11 :51 none should survive, so such a list would break the claim.

### Q4  -  What is the bond between record and reach?

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 5 :72: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."*

The atoms are the same in both cases: a store, a cue, a frame, a ground. What differs is the bond.
A **head start** is a `→` sequence whose end is a cheaper search for the next agent; a **recording**
is a replay of one occasion. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* The two arrangements are invisible in
an ingredient list ("the person has an external note") and decisive in behaviour: one produces new
successes, the one that is replayed produces a repeat.

This is where the framework's pricing rule bites. Fig 12 :99: *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."* A vocabulary that can only say "recorded" has one bond type and cannot
charge for the choice between head start and replay. It will propose arrangements it cannot afford
to test.

**Falsifier:** show that a system which stores only recordings, and never methods, gains reach at
the same rate as one that stores methods. That would make the bond free, which Fig 12 :101 forbids
(*"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing"*).

### Q5  -  Where does the round trip enter the order?

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The round trip belongs between D2 and D3:
before claiming that a communication technology changed what people know, send the claim up as a
coarse description, bring it back down, and measure what the coarse description could not hold.
This is the gap R_T of the Symbols table.

**Falsifier:** a demonstration that the round-trip gap is zero for a given communication medium at
every resolution actually available. Then the measurement step could be skipped for that medium.

## CONCLUSION

The four items are a **functional group**, not four isomers; the compound "remember where, not what"
is a genuine isomer claim over a shared atom set. The dependency order is attention, memory,
knowable, communication. Valence is relative to the residual, so no technology has a fixed good or
bad standing. The bond that separates record from reach is a head start versus a replay, and the
round trip R_T sits between memory and communication. All of this is `specified` and unmeasured.
