## Wider literature that bears on the four domains

1. **Memory, record versus reach.** The Google-effect page and the 2024 meta-analysis are one
   evidence pool, not two witnesses. They corroborate the direction "remember where, not what"
   and the caution that effects are heterogeneous and replication is mixed. They do not establish
   the figure distinction between record and reach; that comes from the figures.
2. **Attention as allocation.** The attention-economy frame (Simon) predicts that an information-
   rich technology reallocates a scarce resource. This is a low-overlap corroboration of Fig 1's
   slot-indexed residual, from economics rather than cognitive science.
3. **Communication as a channel.** Cognitive-load work on GPS and tool deskilling predicts that
   offloading changes what is retained. Transactive memory predicts that the group holds what no
   individual holds. These are two different frames and their agreement is weak corroboration of
   the contact claim.
4. **The knowable.** The filter-bubble literature is explicitly mixed: some studies find strong
   personalisation, others find little. That mixed result is exactly what the figures predict for
   a closure that cannot certify its own limit. It is not evidence for a specific mechanism.
5. **Secondary status.** Three of the framework essays were read directly, and all share an author
   with the figures. Their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight. Overlap is
   recorded per row in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Fig 4 :22  -  *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A recording carried upward looks
like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Sending the whole framework before anyone has
described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.

Fig 10 :42  -  *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has
absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.

Fig 9 :27  -  *"describe it before you go looking, or"* the frame you pick will seem to fit. Ask what
they would expect to see if each output were true before giving them any theory.

Fig 2 :40  -  *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four outputs and their failure conditions; (2) ask which
of the four they work on and what they would count as a failure; (3) record their stage-1 verdict;
(4) only then, if they are curious, the three source links with the note that the figures, not the
summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 10 :52  -  *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
  The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement
  stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict is recorded first and never pooled later.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 again. The formula is a generator, so
  giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must state their own ground and write
  their own refuter before running it.
- **Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 outputs survive. The four outputs
  in `02` are stated with refuters precisely so they can fail in front of the reader.
