## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13, on decidability: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name" and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (carried): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of a governance arrangement you can state before you can name the committee, the checklist or the threshold. It constrains: the residual must be indexed per slot; the reference must not update on the audited team; the reach must be renewed through imports; a seat must be able to refuse; the habitat must be enumerated to second order. It does not fill: it does not name the risk-appetite number, the reporting cadence or the framework to adopt.

Concretely: "govern per deployment against a non-updating reference with a refusal seat" is a shape. "Adopt framework X with committee Y and a 3 percent threshold" is a name.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried): use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping (carried): the answer is reached when "the finish line will also be the starting place" and by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it."

This is why `01` writes the residual in effect terms before it quotes any figure line. A board problem described as "we need an AI policy" invites any governance framework to seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28). Described as "the reference used to judge a deployment moves when the audited team reports success, so the signal is lost," it selects safety science, human factors and cybernetics as the frames that already predict its shape.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest." For boards, that means a deployment-level outcome, an incident, a measured harm, a market or a regulator, read by a seat that cannot move it.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Adopting a public governance framework is an import: it is legitimate only if its provenance is recorded and it explains a gap this board actually has.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried) sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." A governance framework declared as the answer becomes invisible as a choice. That is the failure F10 refuses by calling it a `gap`.

## 4. Why "checklists alone" is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened directly 2026-11-22) gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source" - one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

A checklist score is exactly such a projection. Many different deployment realities (a genuinely safe system, a system whose harms happen three steps out, a system whose assessor shares the builder's evidence pool) can produce the same green reading. The reading does not determine the source. What is owed is a discriminator, not more checklist items. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." And Fig 5 :33: "The three guards are a product, not a checklist." The framework's own question about *alone* is therefore answered structurally: a checklist is not a second witness; it is one frame's projection.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened directly): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." A self-reported control score is a frame validating itself.

## 5. Q - what licenses the board's specific oversight arrangement?

**Nothing in the framework names it.** The framework returns a shape. A particular board structure is licensed only by the ground settling a minted term (an incident record, an independent audit with a different evidence pool, a measured reduction in harm at that slot) or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until then, the arrangement is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does this folder commit the forbidden error?** Partly, and the partial failure is recorded. It names conditions (non-updating anchor, renewed reach, refusal seat, enumerated habitat), but these are bindings onto figure-held quantities, not new primitives. The remaining error is the one F4 and S2 name: practitioner fluency advice can be read as naming the board's interior. The correct form is the ask, not the name: bring the characterised residual and the refusal criterion, not the source.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3 and §4 (kept visible)

I initially overstated "shape, not a name" as a ban on naming any governance object. The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Its subject is a primitive not held in the library. Naming a held binding, for example "keep the anchor reachable", is allowed as provenance; what is forbidden is inventing a seventh primitive or claiming the binding moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom" - the governance bindings add none). My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: the checklist reading still cannot discriminate among sources, and the reason is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held quantities. Both claims are preserved so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened directly): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." And: "If the maintainer is swayed by prestige, the channel to the ground is broken. And a broken channel looks exactly like a working one, until the drift is so large that the system collapses."

For boards this rules out the most common governance shortcut: a well-known framework, a prestigious auditor or a board member with an AI credential standing in for proof that the ground paid. It also explains why the outreach in `02` asks for a refutation and never for endorsement. A budget to be tried is the legitimate ask; an endorsement is a prestige transfer into the verdict.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay (opened directly): "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." LLM output is a simulation. So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of whether this governance shape reduces harm in a real board, and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 8. Consequences for this question

1. The deliverable is a shape plus refuters, not a governance framework, policy template or legal opinion.
2. The checklist items, committee, cadence and threshold are `gap`, recorded as unreachable rather than silently filled (Fig 13 :134).
3. The first contact is the residual and its refuter. Links and the formula come only after a verdict is recorded (Fig 10 :52).
4. The board's stake in the outcome is declared; its interest in the reading is refused (Fig 10 :59-60).
5. Any agreement from a shared evidence pool, including a second seat in this folder, is one witness, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
