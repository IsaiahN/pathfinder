## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a board should govern when it oversees AI. Option A: govern through one enterprise risk score and a technical checklist, treating a green score as a safe deployment. Option B: keep the residual indexed per deployment, keep a reference that does not update on the audited team, keep reach renewed through independent imports, and hold a seat able to refuse. What each leads to: A produces a frame-internal score that cannot grade its own frame, hides the live slot in an average, and can drift coherently without anyone noticing; B produces a shape with eight pre-registered refuters and an open slot until a ground settles it. The question is a union of three conditions, not one dial with an optimum.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per deployment slot s, where Γ is management's governance model, a is the permit/change/refuse decision and o′ is the ground return. Arity: a three-place relation indexed per slot, carrying a comparison bond (a risk threshold) and an ordered bond (refuse before mint). Symmetry: "risk" and "safe" are not symmetric readings of one quantity; refusal is a unary operator, not a bond. Scale: one deployment loop, a portfolio of deployments, and the lossy board-to-floor channel. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **D1 Governance frame.** Actors: directors, committees, management reports, controls, risk appetite. Currency: assurance, approvals, compliance attestations. Anchor: the ground only when it does not move when management reports success (Fig 2 :29, :33-34). Status: admitted. The named collapse is circular governance.
- **D2 Experimentation frame.** Actors: builders, models, data, red teams, deployments. Currency: residual reduction, new settled terms, reach. Anchor: deployment outcomes. Status: admitted.
- **D3 Habitat.** Actors: users, affected people, vendors, third parties, regulators, data sources. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the ground that settles harm. Status: admitted. Enumerated, never composed.
- **D4 Oversight seat.** Actors: board, internal audit, external audit, incident records. Currency: independence, proximity to ground, ability to refuse. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35). Status: admitted.
- **X1 "enterprise AI risk score".** No nameable anchor if it moves when pushed. Not a domain yet; absorbed into D1 as a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26).
- **X2 "more AI expertise on the board".** Dissolved by re-census into D4's independence plus the instrument question. Absorbed.

**Union, not partition.** D1/D4 (verification) and D2/D3 (experimentation and habitat) overlap: the board books the room and the habitat supplies the ground the room is selected against. "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones" (Fig 9 :55). One lever leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged.

**Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). The likely break is between vocabulary and objective, where a board has model metrics but no instrument for what counts as safety against a reference it cannot move. Strain test: mapping Fig 13's six-term physical substrate onto governance functions was tried and retracted; Fig 13 governs the composition grammar, not a governance menu (S1 in `01` §7).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the specific arrangement does not exist yet and is declared `gap`. Govern per deployment slot (Fig 1 :19/:22). Keep a reference that does not update on the audited team (Fig 2 :29/:33-34). Treat checklists and risk scores as filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 5 :33, Fig 9 :60, Fig 1 :27). Keep reach renewed through imports with provenance and a shadow test, and steer the schedule of independent rooms rather than serially approving every run (Fig 6 :23/:24, Fig 7 :35, Fig 8 :52). Enumerate the deployment habitat to second order; standing lists expire with the residual (Fig 11 :51/:52/:70). Hold a seat able to refuse a held claim, with provenance, because a vocabulary that can only accumulate cannot carry a correction (Fig 10 :82, Operators :58). The likely break is the objective link, which has no instrument (Fig 3 :27). No committee, checklist item, cadence or threshold is returned. The result is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate, with one live strain (S2) named.

- **cost if wrong:** a board could keep a self-scored, averaged, updating reference and believe itself governed. The cost is bounded and locatable because this packet states its mode, writes refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable cost is spending an independent reviewer's attention on the wrong question, or converting potential outside frames into believers by sending them the framework before their verdict.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because "something was added rather than because anything was deleted" (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its eight refuters are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A reference test showing the board's anchor does not move and still resolves harm; a discriminator that separates a green score from a ground that did not pay; a refusal that fires with provenance; or any of the eight falsifiers in `01` §4. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :40). Under S2, a demonstration that a verifier reconstructing the model performs equally would move the seat reading and require re-census.
