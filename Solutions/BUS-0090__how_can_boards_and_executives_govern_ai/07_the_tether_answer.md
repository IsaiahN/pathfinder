# The Tether's answer on board and executive AI governance

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the named checklist, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a validated governance standard. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A board must decide, per AI deployment, whether to permit, change or refuse an experiment, with three constraints in play: govern real harm, do not stop useful learning, and do not substitute a technical score for a verdict. The residual is R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o′|, indexed per deployment slot, never aggregated. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The question is a **union** of overlapping conditions, not one dial: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

**D1, the governance frame.** The reference used to judge a deployment must not move when the team being judged reports success. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33-34: "The anchor must not update" and "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." The world name for that collapse is "circular governance" (NACD); the world mechanism is the normalization of deviance (Vaughan). Mode `specified`, **triangulated** across Fig 2, a board-practice source and a safety-science source of low overlap.

**D2, the experimentation frame.** Useful experiments are imports, not re-arrangements. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." So governance steers the schedule of independent rooms (Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes") rather than approving every run. Freezing experimentation cuts the only channel that moves the wall. Mode `specified`, **triangulated** across Fig 6, Fig 7 and Fig 8.

**D3, the habitat.** Governance enumerates the deployment's actors, conditions and relations out to second order; harm is often three steps out (Fig 11 :52). Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A benchmark is a substituted habitat and "a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability" (Fig 11 :70). Mode `specified`, **triangulated** across Fig 11 and the public incident record.

**D4, the oversight seat.** The board is a ground maintainer, not a model reader. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :82: "Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal." Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." The distinctive governance move is a refusal with provenance, not a weighted score (Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"). Mode `general` for the seat rule, `specified` for boards, **triangulated** across Fig 10 and human-factors work (Bainbridge 1983).

**Across all domains, checklists.** A checklist, a coverage percentage and a control count are frame-internal quantities: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces" (Fig 1 :27). Fig 5 :33: "The three guards are a product, not a checklist." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A score routes attention. It does not decide. Mode `general`, **triangulated** across Fig 1, Fig 5, Fig 9 and the NIST text ("Actions do not constitute a checklist").

**The link that breaks.** The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20), and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27). The board's likely break is between vocabulary and objective: it can name model metrics but has no instrument for what would count as safety here against a reference it cannot move. More metrics and more meetings land above the break. Mode `specified`, **single-source** on the figure side for where the break sits; the board measurement is owed, which is why this is a prediction, not a finding.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a governance framework, policy template, checklist or legal opinion. No committee, cadence, threshold or risk-appetite number is returned.
- Not "stop experimenting." Governance changes the object of control from serial permission to the schedule of independent rooms and the reachability of the ground.
- Not "boards need no technical understanding." Literacy is a head start for questions (Fig 4 :22), not standing to grade the model. The live tension S2 is recorded in `01` and `06`.
- Not "all checklists are useless." They are filters for the budget, and filters are necessary. They are disqualified as witnesses only.
- Not that any named public framework is the answer. Adopting one is an import and needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :52).
- Not `grounded`. No board measurement settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here:

1. **Reference test.** Does the board's reference move when the audited team reports success? Measured as the change in the reference after a self-report, with the audited team holding no channel to it.
2. **Discriminator test.** Can the available reading separate a genuinely safe deployment from one whose harm is two or three steps out, or one whose assessor shares the builder's evidence pool? If not, the instrument is inadequate, not the deployment safe.
3. **Refusal test.** Can the oversight seat say no to a held management claim with provenance, and does the reach fall safely when it does (Fig 6 :41-43)? A system that can only accumulate controls fails this.
4. **Import test.** Does reach grow with new ground-verified atoms while experiments are blocked? If yes, the "freeze experimentation" reading is refuted.
5. **Standing falsifiers** from `01` §4: aggregation with a live slot intact, a self-scored control that predicts ground, an unmoved reference that resolves harm, a reconstructing verifier that performs equally, accumulation-only catching a novel failure, frozen reach with growing atoms, invariant valence, synthetic transfer.

The one live strain, board literacy versus verifier independence, is named, not hidden (S2).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand a board a governance framework, and it does not say to stop experimenting. It says that one enterprise AI risk score throws away the per-deployment signal, that the reference used to judge a deployment must not move when the team being judged reports success, and that a control score is a filter for attention, never a witness for safety. Useful experiments are the imports that move the wall, so governance steers which independent rooms get booked next rather than approving every run. The board's distinctive power is a refusal with provenance, not another accumulated control, and it has to enumerate the deployment habitat out to second order because harm often arrives three steps from the model. The framework does not name the committee, the checklist or the threshold, because it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is that shape, eight falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific arrangement stays open until a ground settles it.

**Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A reference test showing the board's anchor does not move and still resolves harm; a discriminator that separates green readings; a refusal that fires with provenance; or any of the eight pre-registered falsifiers. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :40).
