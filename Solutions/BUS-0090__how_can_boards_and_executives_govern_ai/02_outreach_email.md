# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no ground measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about board AI oversight before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about how a board can oversee AI risk. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an enterprise-wide AI risk score destroys the signal a board needs. The residual has to stay indexed per deployment, per decision and per affected group. Three conditions then have to hold at once, and they are not degrees of one problem:
>
> 1. the reference the board uses to judge safety must not move when the team being judged reports success (an incident, a harm, a market, a regulator, an independent audit drawing on a different evidence pool);
> 2. the board must keep reach renewed by contact with situations and reviewers whose evidence base differs from the builder's, because a governance system that only re-arranges what it already holds cannot learn anything new;
> 3. the board must hold a seat able to say no to a claim management has made, with the reason recorded, and a checklist that can only accumulate controls cannot do that.
>
> The claim is that a control score, a policy coverage percentage or a completed impact assessment is a filter for where attention goes, not a witness for whether a deployment is safe. The same frame that produces the score cannot score itself with it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the right committee, the checklist items, the reporting cadence or a risk threshold. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming this is settled by evidence, that it is a legal opinion, or that it is the only account of board oversight. I am not asking you to trust anything.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a board keeps a live, ground-paid per-deployment signal after all deployment residuals are collapsed into one enterprise score;
> 2. a control score predicts ground outcomes at low overlap with the team that produced and reported it, against a reference that does not update;
> 3. a governance arrangement whose reference does not move when the audited team reports success still resolves a live harm;
> 4. a verifier that reconstructs the model catches ground errors at the same rate as one that does not, with independence measured separately;
> 5. a system that can only add controls, cannot refuse any held claim, still catches a novel failure no accumulated control anticipated;
> 6. a firm's reach grows with new ground-verified atoms while all external experiments are blocked and only internal re-arrangement occurs.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim and re-census. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, ground and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a governance problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or a channel the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
- If the recipient asks for the legal or regulatory answer instead, say plainly that this is a structural claim, not legal advice, and point to the public governance sources in `04`.
