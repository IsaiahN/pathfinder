# Expert verification packet - the Tether on board AI governance

**What this is:** a claim in a form a board-governance researcher, risk practitioner or corporate-law scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the specific arrangement, not `grounded`.** No board measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on. This is not legal advice.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An enterprise-wide AI risk score destroys the signal a board needs, because the residual is indexed per deployment and "aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" (Fig 1 :22). Three conditions have to hold at once and are not degrees of one problem: the reference used to judge a deployment must not move when the team being judged reports success; reach must be renewed through contact with frames whose evidence base differs; and the oversight seat must be able to refuse a held claim with provenance. A checklist, coverage percentage or control count is a filter for where attention is spent, never a witness for whether a deployment is safe, because "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces" (Fig 1 :27). The framework returns this shape, not the committee, the checklist items or the threshold. Eight falsifiers were written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and governance-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** a risk score produced by the governance frame cannot grade that frame, and a single averaged number hides the live deployment.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
  - Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
  - Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
  - Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
  - Fig 5 :33: "The three guards are a product, not a checklist."
- **What to check:** does the claim overread these lines? The figures disqualify *self-scored frame-internal quantities* and make checklists *filters*, not witnesses. They do not ban checklists. NIST's AI RMF independently says "Actions do not constitute a checklist", which is corroboration, not a figure line.
- **Refuter:** a control score that predicts ground outcomes at low overlap with the team that produced and reported it, against a reference that does not update; or a board that keeps a live per-slot signal after all residuals are collapsed into one score with no second instrument.

### C2 - governance-facing; checkable by a board or corporate-law expert

- **Statement:** the board's reference must not update on the audited team's report, and its distinctive power is a refusal with provenance, not a weighted gate.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
  - Fig 2 :33-34: "The anchor must not update." and "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
  - Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
  - Fig 10 :82: "Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal."
  - Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
  - Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"
- **What to check:** is this faithful to how boards actually work? The world sources say a similar thing: NACD warns of "circular governance" where "the oversight terms are dictated by oversight subjects"; the Caremark reporting-system duty is the legal form of keeping the channel reachable; Bainbridge (1983) shows why a human monitor of a system it cannot reconstruct has "an impossible task". Also check the live tension S2: practitioner guidance asks for board AI fluency while Fig 10 :50-51 says acquiring the frame's model disqualifies the verifier. The packet resolves this as a head start for questions, not standing to grade the model. Is that resolution adequate?
- **Refuter:** a governance arrangement whose reference does not move when the audited team reports success and still resolves a live harm; or a verifier that reconstructs the model and catches ground errors at the same rate as one that does not, with independence measured separately; or a system that can only add controls and still catches a novel failure no accumulated control anticipated.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** useful experimentation is the import channel, so a governance arrangement must book differing independent rooms and enumerate the habitat to second order. Two governance modes over the same atoms are isomers, not spellings of one thing.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
  - Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
  - Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
  - Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
  - Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
  - Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** does the isomer claim hold over a genuinely shared atom set (see `06` Q1 and its falsifier)? Does "book differing rooms" actually follow from Fig 7 :35/:36/:42 rather than being an attractive analogy? The word *import* is technical here: it adds no atom to the world and is debited against the imported frame's independence (Fig 6 :24, :25).
- **Refuter:** a firm whose reach grows with new ground-verified atoms while all external experiments are blocked and only internal re-arrangement occurs; an invariant control and actor list whose adequacy does not change across a documented residual change; a synthetic evaluation pass that transfers to deployment ground with no increment in contact.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a governance framework, policy template, checklist, risk-appetite number or legal opinion.
- Not which committee, cadence, threshold or named public framework a board should adopt. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 13 :134).
- Not that checklists are useless. They are filters for the budget.
- Not "stop experimenting." The arrangement steers which independent rooms are booked and keeps the ground reachable.
- Not that a board needs no technical understanding. Literacy is a head start for questions, not standing to grade the model (live strain S2).
- Not that the named world sources prove the claim. They are secondary corroboration and the governance sources share one evidence pool.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a board measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Reference test:** measure whether the board's reference moves after an audited self-report, and whether it still resolves a live harm.
2. **Discriminator test:** can the reading separate a safe deployment from one whose harm is two or three steps out, or whose assessor shares the builder's evidence pool?
3. **Refusal test:** can the seat say no to a held management claim with provenance, and does the reach fall safely when it does?
4. Plus the eight pre-registered falsifiers in `01` §4, repeated in `07` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19/:22/:27, Fig 2 :29/:33-34, Fig 9 :60, Fig 10 :42/:82 and Operators :58, eight short passages.

4. Check whether S2, the literacy-versus-independence tension, is a genuine live constraint or an artefact of reading Fig 10 too literally.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
