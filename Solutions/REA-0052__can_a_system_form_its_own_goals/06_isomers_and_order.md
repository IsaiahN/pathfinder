### Q3 - Is "goal" an isomer of anything here?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- A goal (`A ⋛ B`, a comparison) and a plan (`A → B`, a sequence) share operands but not a bond. Under Fig 12 :40 ("same two atoms, three substances") they are **different substances**, not variants of one thing. A system holding only → can write a route and cannot write a goal.
- The stronger isomer claim: two systems over the same shared atom set, one with the goal as `current ⋛ target` and one with the goal as `target ⋛ current`, are isomers in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different bond order, different behaviour. This is a real checkable claim about goal structure.
- **Falsifier:** if swapping the operands of ⋛ leaves behaviour unchanged in a tested case, the isomer claim fails and the two are one substance under the comparison bond.

### Q4 - What analogue governs the provenance question?

- Fig 8 :50-52: "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

- **Falsifier:** an instrument whose reading identifies the origin of a comparison without the provenance record would refute the claim that only the record separates convergent derivation from adopted import.

## CONCLUSION

The goal question is a **union**, not a partition. Its order is fixed by Fig 3: objective at link 3, before planning and after vocabulary. "Goal" and "plan" are Fig 12 isomers over shared operands, different bonds, different substances, not two spellings of one thing. The provenance question is governed by the source-discrimination analogue (Fig 8 plus the spectrometer essay), which is why it ends in a declared `gap` rather than a name. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no naming of a seventh term is needed for any of this.
