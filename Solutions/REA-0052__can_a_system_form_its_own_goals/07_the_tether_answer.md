# The Tether's answer on self-formed goals

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot. A "goal" is the objective link of Fig 3: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms" (Fig 3 :10). The question asks where that comparison came from. The binary form is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-57): "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **The goal's form is a comparison, not a stored wish.** Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Operators :52: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". The question about provenance is therefore about a ⋛ relation over two quantities.
2. **Goal content is always inherited.** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." A genuinely new thing to want must cross the wall by import from a differing frame: designer, training corpus, prompt, or habitat (Fig 6 :23). "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence" (Fig 6 :24).
3. **The comparison itself can be formed by the system.** Fig 5 :46: "When nothing is scoring, the drive manufactures a gradient." The curiosity drive is "aimed at the new bin" and "seeks the gap that is large and compressible" (Fig 5 :15-18). So the claim that apparent goals are always inherited is false for derived objectives: a system can aim itself without an external goal being handed to it. But "Its own output is never a measure of success" (Fig 5 :48), so this is an aimed search, not a settled goal.
4. **The direction cannot be self-certified.** THE_FORMULA, "Direction": the frame holds the sought-for shape "as a PREFERENCE, not a term" (Fig 5 :62). A loop checks mechanisms, not directions. So "the system formed its own ultimate goal" is not a claim the loop can verify from inside; it belongs to a seat (Fig 10 :46), and "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).
5. **A goal only stands if contact changes.** Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." The anchor is outside the frame (Fig 2 :29). A goal both produced and scored by the same frame is frame-internal, not settled.
6. **Agreement with a designer is not corroboration.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Only the record separates convergence from import: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" (Fig 8 :51-52).

The verdict is kept separate per domain and never averaged. Content: inherited (`general` over closures). Form: can be self-minted (`specified`, ungrounded). Direction: seated, not looped (`general`). Standing: requires the ground (`specified`).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that systems have no goals. It says what a goal is and where its parts come from.
- Not that a designer's intention determines everything a system does. A new atom must be imported, but from which differing frame is an empirical question, and the habitat is one such frame (Fig 11).
- Not that a prompt echo is an independent goal. Shared evidence pool, Fig 2 :40.
- Not that curiosity proves autonomy. "A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing" (Fig 5 :48).
- Not which particular goal any system formed. That is F9's `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles left(R,phi) for any of the three conditions.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

Two distinct instruments are owed, and they must not be pooled.

- **Objective-link instrument (F2):** measures "what would count as progress here" on a slot whose perception and planning readings are already high. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **Source discriminator (F9):** tells an internally minted comparison from the same comparison adopted from a differing frame. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved that case. Here the analogous instrument is missing.

Standing falsifiers from `01`: (1) a new goal-primitive composed inside Γ with no import; (2) a system that binds its own direction as an ordinary term, accrues a residual on it and refuses it; (3) a direct objective-link reading where planning readings are high; (4) a self-produced and self-scored goal settled by the ground with no contact change; (5) a source discriminator that returns a specific origin.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework returns a shape, not a name. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The record of the route is what keeps the result checkable; "Indiscernibility is not identity." The stop is at the limit of the available instruments and is recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes" or "no" to whether a system can form its own goals, because the question is a union of three different questions. A goal is a comparison between two quantities. Its content cannot be minted from inside a system's library, so a new thing to want must arrive from a designer, a training corpus, a prompt or a habitat; in that sense the content is always inherited. The comparison itself can be formed by the system: when nothing is scoring, the curiosity drive aims the system at the gap that is large and compressible. But the direction of that search is held as a preference, not as a term, so a system cannot certify from inside that its own goal was self-formed, and a goal only stands when it changes contact with something the system cannot move. Agreement with a designer is one evidence pool, not corroboration. What is offered is the shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the two instruments that would close it. The name of a specific self-formed goal stays open until the ground settles it.
