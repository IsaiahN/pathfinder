**Subject:** One checkable claim about goal formation, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can a system form its own goals?" is not one question. It comes apart into three, and the binary answer is wrong for each.
>
> 1. Goal *content* cannot be minted from inside a system's library. A composition over what the system already holds never adds a primitive, so a new thing to want must arrive from a differing frame: a designer, a training corpus, a prompt, or a habitat. In that sense the content of a goal is always inherited.
> 2. The goal *form* is a comparison between two quantities, not a stored wish. A system that already holds the comparison operator and two quantities can aim itself at the gap that is large and compressible, with no external goal being handed to it. That is a real internally formed objective, but it is a derived comparison, not a new terminal value.
> 3. The *direction* of the search cannot be checked by the loop that runs it. It is held as a preference, not as a term, so a system cannot certify from inside that its own goal was self-formed. That check belongs to a seat outside the loop.
>
> The dependency chain puts the objective at link 3, between vocabulary and planning. That link has no instrument of its own in most work I can find; perception and planning have obvious tests, and "what counts as progress here" does not. So a system that plans well is not evidence that it formed its goal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a system has no goals, that everything a system does is its designer's intention, that a prompt echo is an independent goal, or that any of this is measured. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not naming which particular goal any system formed; I do not have an instrument that could tell an internally minted comparison from an adopted one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. a system composes a new goal-primitive from inside its library, with no import and no prior holding of that primitive;
> 2. a system binds its own direction as an ordinary term, lets it accrue a residual, and refuses it under the same test used for everything else;
> 3. an instrument on the objective link measures goal formation directly in a slot whose perception and planning instruments are already high and not declining;
> 4. a goal produced and scored entirely inside one frame is settled by the ground with no change in contact.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
