# Union, source and order: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the order, `general` on the analogy.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §2).

## RESIDUAL

A life-detection claim can fail in two different ways that look alike from outside. It can be **one question with too little evidence**, or it can be **several overlapping conditions bundled into one sentence**. These have different remedies. Fig 9 :44 says *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The supporting derivation owes (1) a test for union versus partition, (2) an account of what makes two lines independent rather than identical, and (3) the order in which the parts of a claim must be assembled.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a reading, a source hypothesis, a competing source, an observation, a slot, an environment, a control. Operators held: negation (refusal), comparison (is this reading more likely under biology than under the abiotic set), sequence (the order of checks), conjunction (co-localised features). No new primitive is available without import. Import here means an astrobiology result with a source gate, never a silent absorption.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Take atmospheric O2 on an exoplanet. Condition A: biology can produce it. Condition B: photolysis of CO2 can produce it. Condition C: meteoritic infall can deliver oxidants. The observed feature sits in the intersection A and B and C. Searching harder for "the" cause of O2 is searching a region where the second and third conditions still bind.

The decision rule: if removing one condition leaves the question unchanged, the conditions were a list (partition) and the lever works. If removing one condition leaves a residue that still needs explaining, it was a union and must be split. For O2, removing biology leaves a perfectly good photochemical question, so it is a union. **Triangulated** (Fig 9 plus Wikipedia's O2 false-positive example plus Neveu et al. 2018 reliability criterion).

**Falsifier:** an abiotic route to the feature that is demonstrated to be impossible under the stated environmental conditions would collapse the union to a partition and make the single-feature claim much stronger. This is a matter for an instrument, not an argument.

### Q2: What makes two lines two?

- Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
- Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

Two lines are independent iff their evidence sets do not overlap. This is checkable without knowing the answer: list the inputs to each line (instrument, calibration, sample, reduction code, prior). If the lists intersect, the lines are one line with two names, regardless of how different the conclusions look.

Convergent derivation is the strong case, and it is **not** the same as copying. Two instruments of different design, pointed at the same object, reaching the same source attribution are at maximum independence. They must still be logged separately, because the contents alone cannot tell convergence from adoption.

**Falsifier:** a shared calibration standard, shared reduction pipeline, shared prior or shared sample between the two lines. Any one of these sets independence below the level the claim assumes.

### Q3: Are the two routes to a detection isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

Two candidate detection arguments can use the same observational atoms (a gas, an isotope ratio, a morphology, an environmental context) and differ only in the relation they assert among them. Under Fig 12 they are different substances: one is a detection claim, the other a geochemical description. The ingredient list cannot tell them apart. This is why a paper that reports only "we detected X, Y and Z" has not yet made a detection claim.

**Strain, not hidden.** Fig 12's isomer is about chemical molecules. Using it for evidential arguments is a structural analogy. I keep it as `general`, single-source, and it is the first thing the second seat is asked to reject if it does not hold. There is no chemistry claim here.

**Falsifier:** if the two arguments can be written with different atom sets rather than different bonds, the isomer reading fails and the plain "different evidence" reading stands.

### Q4: What is the order of assembly?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 3 :20 (census): the chain is *"ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

The dependency order of a detection claim is:

1. **Perception.** Is there a signal on this named object, above the blank and the positive control?
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for the competing abiotic mechanisms and for contamination, each with a losing condition?
3. **Objective.** What would count as progress? Here: biology becomes the last-resort hypothesis (Fig 5 REFUTED bin, not a new confirmation).
4. **Planning.** Which independent lines will be acquired, and which shared inputs must be avoided?
5. **Context and carrier.** Enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43, *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*), record the lineage and declare the stake.

Steps 2 and 3 are the middle of Fig 3's chain, the part with the fewest instruments. A claim that jumps from step 1 to a verdict has skipped the only link that can turn a reading into evidence. Effort spent on another spectrograph cannot rescue a missing vocabulary of alternatives.

**Falsifier:** a demonstration that the vocabulary and objective links can be omitted without changing the verdict, for example a case where the source is named by a single reading with no competing mechanism conceivable. If such a case exists, the order claim is wrong for that class.

### Q5: Where does the comparison sit?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
- Operators table: negation *"this does not hold"*, a refutation is a negation.

Once a competing abiotic mechanism is refused against the ground, that refusal settles and becomes an atom at the next level. The final claim composes over **refusals**, not over an ever-growing pile of positive readings. This is why a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate: it can report that a mechanism stopped paying, but cannot carry that it is wrong.

**Falsifier:** a framework that cannot write a refusal, or a claim whose final step is an additional positive feature rather than the elimination of the alternatives. Either breaks the composition and leaves the claim at the previous level.

## CONCLUSION

The detection question is a **union compound** in its interpretation and a **dependency chain** in its assembly. Two independent lines are two only when their input sets do not intersect. Two arguments over the same atoms with different relations are, by structural analogy, isomers rather than variants. And the order is fixed: perception, then the vocabulary of alternatives, then the last-resort objective, then the plan for independent lines, then context and lineage. Nothing in this derivation is settled by a measurement. It is `specified` on the order and `general` on the analogy.
