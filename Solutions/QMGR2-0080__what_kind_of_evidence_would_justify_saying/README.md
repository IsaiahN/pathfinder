## The residual, in effect terms

The question is not which biosignature to look for. It is what a claim would have to look like before we are licensed to say the words "life has been detected beyond Earth."

Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A:** one strong-looking signal, one instrument, one team, one headline. This leads to a reading with an unknown source. A biosignature is a projection; several abiotic processes can produce the same reading. Agreement among people who read that same signal is one evidence pool talking, and the claim cannot be settled because nothing in the frame can discriminate the source.
- **Option B:** a claim whose shape is fixed before the observation, per object rather than averaged, ranged against a ground that no frame can move, built from low-overlap independent lines, with abiotic alternatives enumerated and refused, with the environmental context recorded, and with a pre-registered refuter. This leads to a checkable shape, an honest stop, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

An answer would look like a **constraint set on evidence**, not a list of molecules. That is the sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the derivation so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No claim that any particular molecule or telescope would settle the question. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- No personal emails of named individuals, no purchased lists, no ranking of people by likelihood of agreement.
- No bulk-send plan. One-to-one, opt-in only.
- No verdict on whether life exists anywhere. The question is about standards of evidence, not about a detection.
