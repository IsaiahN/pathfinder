# Outreach draft: check the shape of a life-detection claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the pre-registered refuter below is final. The recipient must be able to fail the claim before they see any theory.

---

**Subject:** What would it take for you to sign a life-detection claim?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am [your name]. I have one specific claim about standards of evidence, and I would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I am working on:** what kind of evidence would justify saying that life has been detected beyond Earth?
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` in what it predicts, not `grounded`):** adequate evidence has a shape, and that shape has seven parts. It attaches to a named object rather than to a sample average. It ranges against an observation that no frame can move. It discriminates the source of a reading rather than reading the source off the reading. Its lines are genuinely independent, which means they do not share an instrument, a calibration, a reduction pipeline or a prior. It unbundles the case where several abiotic mechanisms can produce the same feature before searching, rather than treating that feature as one hard question. It names the environment that makes the feature diagnostic. And it is written down before the observation, with the losing conditions stated in advance.
>
> Two things this deliberately does **not** say:
>
> - It does not name a molecule or a telescope that would settle the question. The framework I am using returns a shape, not a name.
> - It does not say that enough experts agreeing would make a detection real. Agreement is a currency that has to be cashed against the observation. A room does not add an anchor.
>
> **What would refute the claim.** I wrote these before sending this:
>
> 1. A detection accepted on one reading with no independent line and no discrimination of abiotic sources.
> 2. A source named from a reading where several sources could produce it and no instrument discriminates them.
> 3. Evidence pooled across objects so the one live object disappears into the average.
> 4. A non-detection at one instrument resolution reported as proof of absence.
> 5. A claim closed by consensus, by a convention, or by a clean chain of custody rather than by the observation.
>
> If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers, in the local framework: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The public architecture post, including the figures and the citation list: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the architecture post. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The author's own warning is that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
