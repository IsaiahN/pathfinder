# Expert verification packet: evidence standards for a life detection claim

**What this is:** a claim in a form a life-detection researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` in what it predicts a detection claim must contain. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at any target is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Adequate evidence for saying "life has been detected beyond Earth" has a seven-part shape: it attaches to a named object, ranges against an observation no frame can move, discriminates the source of a reading from its abiotic alternatives, uses lines with disjoint evidence sets, unbundles overlapping conditions before searching, carries the environmental context that makes a feature diagnostic, and is written down before the observation with losing conditions stated in advance. Community scrutiny is the procedure for checking these; it is not itself the ground. The framework returns this shape, not the name of a molecule or a telescope, and stops where the instruments stop.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: The source of a reading is not determined by the reading

- **Statement:** a biosignature is a projection. Naming biology as its source requires a discriminator or separately derived converging lines; an abiotic alternative that keeps producing the same reading must be refused, not merely noted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :30-31 *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*; Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."*
- **World check:** NASA, "What Would It Take to Say We Found Life?" (2025): *"Carl Sagan once said, 'Life is the hypothesis of last resort,' meaning that we ought to work hard for such a claim to rule out alternative possibilities."* Neveu et al. 2018 define reliability as being measurably different from expected abiotic signals.
- **Refuter:** produce a case where a single reading licenses a source attribution with no discriminator and no refused alternative.

### C2: Two lines are independent only if their evidence sets do not intersect

- **Statement:** two confirmations sharing an instrument, calibration, reduction pipeline, prior or sample are one line with two names. Convergence from disjoint inputs is corroboration at maximum independence, but the record must show the two were reached separately, because convergence and adopted import look identical in their contents.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 8 :50 *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*
- **World check:** NASA (2025) asks *"do we have separate independent lines of evidence that corroborate what we've seen and increase our confidence of life?"*
- **Refuter:** identify a shared input between the two lines, or show that the provenance record does not distinguish convergence from borrowing.

### C3: A single feature that has several abiotic routes is a union compound, not one hard question

- **Statement:** a case in two or more conditions is not a harder case of one condition; it is a different object and must be split before search. Searching harder for "the" cause of O2 leaves the photochemical and meteoritic conditions standing after the biological one is cleared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **World check:** Wikipedia's Biosignature article: *"Finding oxygen alone in a planet's atmosphere is not enough to confirm a biosignature because of the false-positive mechanisms associated with it."* Neveu et al. 2018 require the biological interpretation to be the last-resort hypothesis.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that removing the biological condition leaves no residual question under the stated environment and instrument sensitivity, which collapses the union to a partition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any molecule, feature or telescope would settle the question. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that a probability, signal-to-noise ratio or confidence tier is a verdict. Filters decide the budget, not the truth (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that agreement among experts makes a claim true. Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."*
- Not that a non-detection proves absence (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that this derivation has inspected any target data, and not `grounded`. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.
- Not that the seven constraints are nature's own list. They are a visible choice, recorded so it can be revised.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. (1) Does an available instrument separate biology from the abiotic routes for this feature in this environment? (2) Which of the competing conditions actually bind? (3) Do the lines have disjoint inputs? (4) Were the losing conditions written before the observation? A "no" on (1) or (3) leaves the claim at `candidate`, and a "no" on (4) means it never met its own standard.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 2 :31-32, four short passages.
2. Pick one biosignature you know well. List the abiotic routes to it under a stated environment. Does the union test in C3 hold?
3. Name two independent instruments or measurements for it, then list their inputs. Do the input sets intersect?
4. Name one instrument, existing or plausibly near term, that would discriminate biology from the abiotic routes for that feature. If none, say so.
5. Write down what would make you withdraw a detection claim. Compare it with the refuter in section 2.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
