## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe a bounce happened.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures settle the astronomical question. They constrain the shape of an answer; the ground, an instrument, decides the fact.

Three figure-grounded reasons for the omissions, quoted verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`:

1. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for receptiveness is a list selected to agree.
2. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Agreement among people who already accept the framework is one pool talking.
3. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Handing an outside frame the whole architecture before asking for a verdict spends the independence it came with.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
