# Isomers and order: what kind of thing a bounce is

**Mode:** `specified` on the structural classification, `general` on the order rule. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim says an outside room is required but does not name it. This file answers the supporting question: given the figure vocabulary, what kind of term would a bounce be, what kind would a cyclic universe be, and what order must the derivation follow so that no step smuggles in the answer?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (condensed; full census in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1)

Atoms held in the metatheory: six terms (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures), seven bonds plus negation, rooms, membranes, imports. No new primitive is available without an import from a physics frame.

Atoms proposed by Domain 2 (early-universe frames): a contracting branch, a crossing, an expanding branch, a brane, discrete quantum geometry, an inflaton history. These are not in the metatheory's six; they are imports and must be provenanced.

## Q1: Is "before" an ordered relation?

- Operators table :29-33 defines → as "A then B; B needs A to have happened", symmetric **no**, with the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- Fig 13 :12 defines time as "the ordered succession of differences".
- ⇒ The relation "prior phase before hot Big Bang" is an ordered bond. Swapping the operands gives a different claim (a future phase, not a prior one). The question cannot be read as symmetric. Reading it symmetrically would be a strain, so the re-census assigns it to → and dissolves the strain.

**Falsifier:** show that the prior-phase relation is reversible without changing the claim, that is, that the order of operands does not matter. If so, the ordered-bond reading fails.

## Q2: Is a bounce a cycle, or a crossing through a membrane?

Two different figure structures are available and they are not interchangeable:

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A **cyclic universe** is this term: the expansion output re-enters as the contraction input, and the loop closes.
- Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." A **single bounce** is a one-time crossing: contraction on one side, expansion on the other, with a directional filter between. The output does not necessarily re-enter.

So a single bounce and a cyclic universe are **different structures**, not two spellings of one idea. Conflating them is the flat-formula error Fig 12 warns about: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not", so "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost" (Fig 12 :14-16).

**Falsifier:** exhibit a single-bounce model in which the output genuinely re-enters as input with a closed loop, or a cyclic model with no re-entry. Either would collapse the distinction.

## Q3: Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Write both over the shared atom set {contracting branch, crossing, expanding branch}. The single bounce uses a directional membrane bond (Fig 4 :21). The cyclic model uses a closing cycle bond (Fig 12 :121). Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour.
- ⇒ **Over that shared set, they are isomers in the figure's sense.** This is a structural claim about the arrangement, not a claim that either exists physically.
- The weaker reading: if the cyclic model requires an extra atom (an eternal-return condition, a specific mechanism that resets the loop) that the single bounce does not, they are not isomers; they are two members of the same functional group (Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.").

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either model can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is exactly the test used in the exemplar for GR and QM, applied here.

## Q4: What order does the derivation require?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Ordered steps for this question:
  1. **Refuse** the terminal-room term before minting a prior frame (Formula step 2: a refusal is owed before a mint; negation is not a bond).
  2. **Describe** the residual in effect terms before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
  3. **Enumerate** the habitat of observables (Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual").
  4. **Classify** the proposed prior term (bounce as crossing, cyclic as cycle) before asking which is true.
  5. **Settle** against the ground. Fig 5 :41: "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely." Here settlement is at zero: no discriminating reading exists yet, so no specific prior term is admitted.
- An instrument cannot be specified from an account of the desired answer. Fig 6 :33: "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."

**Falsifier:** show that a specific prior-phase term can be admitted before a discriminating reading exists, without breaking the three-guard product. If so, the order above is not required.

## Q5: What the analogy does not license

The bounce/cycle classification is a structure borrowed from the metatheory and applied to cosmology. Fig 9 :30-31 warns: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." The effect here is a boundary condition that the hot-Big-Bang frame cannot compose. The classification says what kind of term would fit that effect; it does not say that the term exists in nature. The cross-domain strain is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7 and is not smoothed over.

A head start is allowed across the boundary, a recording is not. Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." What crosses to an outside reader is the method (describe the residual, write the refuter), not a report that a bounce was found.

## CONCLUSION

- "Before" is an ordered bond (→), not a symmetric one.
- A single bounce is a directional membrane crossing; a cyclic universe is a closing cycle. They are different structures.
- Over the shared atom set they are isomers under Fig 12 :117; if the cyclic model needs an extra atom, the weaker functional-group reading applies.
- The derivation must refuse before minting, describe before choosing a frame, enumerate before classifying, and stop at settlement.
- None of this licenses the claim that a prior universe exists. That remains a ground question, currently unreached.

**Strains after re-census:** one live cross-domain strain (frame-level rules applied to physical time), named in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7. No internal figure contradiction remains.
