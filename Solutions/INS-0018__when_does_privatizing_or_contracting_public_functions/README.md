## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals and no contact list of researchers selected for likely agreement.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to endorse the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan and no claim that a public submission venue guarantees a reply.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A venue is a filter for where to try, never a witness for whether the answer is true.
3. **Hand the recipient a residual, not a finished frame.** Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.

Legally: public submission portals and opt-in listserves avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM style failures that attach to unsolicited bulk email. That is a practical reason on top of the structural one.

## The answer in one line

Privatization improves performance only when it changes the function's contact with the ground, not merely the label of the actor; accountability weakens when the change lengthens the channel and lets the measurement become a convention that decides the outcome instead of reporting it. The two halves have different anchors and are never averaged into a single net verdict.
