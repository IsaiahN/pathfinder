# Expert verification packet: the Tether on contracting public functions (INS-0018)

**What this is:** the question in a form a public-administration researcher, procurement auditor or public-sector accountant can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the two domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No service outcome or accountability record is claimed as measured here.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When a public function is contracted out, two different questions are being asked and they have different anchors. Performance is a question about contact with the service outcome: changing the actor is not the same as changing contact, so a gain does not follow from the ownership label; what can change contact is a genuine contest between providers whose evidence does not overlap, or a method the old arrangement could not reach. Accountability is a question about whether that outcome stays reachable for correction: contracting lengthens the channel, and accountability weakens when the channel decays, a target becomes a substitute for the outcome, the move fails to reproduce the complaint and appeal conditions of the old habitat, or per-case failures are averaged away. The two verdicts are kept separate and are never combined into a net score. The framework returns the shape any answer must have, not a name of a contract, firm or jurisdiction.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 11, 7 and 2

- **Statement:** an actor swap alone does not establish a capability change; a change in contact does. Contestability with independent providers is the operative mechanism; a self-reported metric is not an anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
- **What to check:** are these four lines present verbatim in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, and does the D1 verdict follow from them without importing an extra premise? Is "contestability" a structural condition (selection between rooms) rather than a named instrument?
- **Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which provider identity changes while method, habitat and contact are held fixed, and the service outcome reliably moves. The figures name this themselves at Fig 11 :72 "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."

### C2: Accountability-facing, checkable against audit practice

- **Statement:** contracting weakens accountability when the channel decays, a target decides the outcome, or the habitat substitution fails to reproduce goal-required conditions; per-case residuals must remain visible.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** in a contract you know, can a single case failure still be detected and corrected within a stated time, and can the reported metric absorb the failure? Is the metric a report of the outcome or a substitute for it? Are complaint, appeal and record-keeping conditions reproduced after the move, or merely assumed?
- **Refuter:** a contracting arrangement in which every per-case failure remains detectable and correctable within a stated time and the reported metric demonstrably cannot absorb the failure, across providers and years.

### C3: Framework-internal derivation, checkable in Figures 9, 3 and 12

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound requiring two loops with separate anchors, checked in dependency order; two arrangements with the same ingredients but different bonds are isomers, not variants of one thing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is the union reading justified by the shared-but-different role of the metric, or are performance and accountability actually a partition? Is the dependency order right, in particular the claim that "what would count as progress" (link 3) is the usual silent break (Fig 3 :25-27)?
- **Refuter:** a single scalar that predicts both the service outcome and the correctability of failures out of sample (collapses the union), or a controlled comparison in which two differently bonded arrangements produce the same outcome and correctability profiles across repeated rounds (collapses the isomer claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict that privatization works or fails, and no net performance-accountability score.
- Not that private provision is always cheaper or public provision always accountable; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not a distributional weight between cost and access. This is a `gap` with no figure line.
- Not a legal enumeration of which duties may be delegated. This is a `gap`, read off a jurisdiction's law.
- Not a name of a contract, firm or jurisdiction as the framework's answer.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The three candidate instruments in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §5 are: a fixed-contact provider-label comparison for D1; a per-case reachability and metric-absorption audit for D2; and a union test for the split. Until one fires, the verdicts stay `candidate`. A list of researchers who agree with the shape is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 7 :35-36, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 10 :24 and :47 in the local figure text. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against a contracting case you know: did the provider change alter contact, or only the label?
3. Check C2: name one per-case failure and say whether it was detectable and correctable, and whether the metric could absorb it.
4. Say which is right, the union reading or the partition reading, and give one reason.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
