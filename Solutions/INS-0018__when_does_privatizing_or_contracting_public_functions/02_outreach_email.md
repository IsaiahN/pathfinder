# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

---

**Subject:** Two conditions on contracting out that you could refute in ten minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question of when privatizing or contracting a public function helps performance and when it weakens accountability is one question in wording but two in structure, because the two halves are settled by different things. A single net score over "efficiency" and "accountability" throws away the signal that says where to look.
>
> The first condition concerns capability. Changing the actor who delivers a service is not the same as changing the service's contact with the outcome. If the method, the habitat and the contact are unchanged, a capability gain does not follow from the ownership label. What can change contact is a real contest between providers whose methods and evidence do not overlap, or a method the old arrangement could not reach. With one incumbent and a self-reported metric, the arrangement is one long room: motion, no progress.
>
> The second condition concerns whether the ground stays reachable. A contract lengthens the channel between the public and the service. The ground does not decay; the channel does. Accountability weakens when a failure at one user or one case can no longer be detected and corrected, when the reported number becomes a target that decides the outcome rather than a measurement of it, or when the move into a private habitat fails to reproduce the complaint, appeal and record-keeping conditions the old habitat supplied. Those failures are invisible until the goal fails.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that private provision is always cheaper, that public provision is always accountable, or that the two can be traded off on one scale. I am not supplying a social welfare weight between cost and access, and I am not claiming to settle which duties may legally be delegated. Those are gaps, and I record them as gaps.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a controlled case where the provider label changes while method, habitat and contact are held fixed, and the service outcome reliably moves;
> 2. a contract in which every per-case failure stays detectable and correctable within a stated time, and the reported metric demonstrably cannot absorb the failure, across providers and years;
> 3. a single scalar that predicts both the service outcome and the correctability of failures out of sample. If one exists, the two halves were one question after all.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw the affected condition. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** not whether you agree, but what you would expect to see if each condition were true, and what you would count as a failure. Please give that verdict before reading the sources below, so it is not shaped by them.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. Public links:
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract used for this derivation): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace.
> - The canonical figures README and usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
> - The empirical literature this output was checked against is listed with quotes in `10_source_ledger.csv`, including Esteve et al. on user accountability, Blomqvist and Winblad on contractor accountability, Petersen et al. on contracting out, and Kikeri and Nellis on privatization.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a service you know and can check against an outcome. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

**Stage 1.** Send the two conditions and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would expect to see and what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three public links and the source ledger. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because a recipient who has absorbed the framework has joined the frame being checked (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").

**Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method for their own problems, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Never asked:** trust, agreement, endorsement, or a public statement of support. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
