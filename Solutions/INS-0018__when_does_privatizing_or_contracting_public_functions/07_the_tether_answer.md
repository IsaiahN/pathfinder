# The Tether's answer: when does contracting public functions help performance, and when does it weaken accountability?

**Mode: `specified` on both domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A public function fails to meet its outcome. The proposal is to change who delivers it or how it is procured. Two predicates are asserted: performance improves, accountability weakens. They have different anchors, so they are settled separately and are never averaged. Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## 2. Loop one, domain D1: performance (capability / contact)

- **Actors:** the function, the provider, the users, the outcome. **Currency:** contact with the ground. **Anchor:** the service outcome. Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."
- **Finding 1 (the actor swap is not the change).** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Therefore "private is more efficient" is not a verdict; it is a `general`-mode claim with no measurement. Refuter: a controlled case with label changed and method, habitat and contact held fixed, where the outcome reliably moves. The figures themselves name this refuter at Fig 11 :72: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."
- **Finding 2 (the operative variable is contestability).** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 2 :31-32: independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes agreement mean anything. So a sole provider with a self-reported metric is one long room; several providers using the same methods and data are one room wearing two names (Fig 13 :39: "Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names").
- **Finding 3 (the measurement must qualify as an anchor).** Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." A self-reported figure moves when the reporter is pushed; a user-facing outcome that does not is admissible.
- **D1 verdict (`specified`, triangulated across Fig 11, Fig 7 and Fig 2, low overlap):** contracting improves performance when and only when it changes contact with the outcome, through a genuinely new method (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs") or through selection between independent rooms. Changing the actor label alone does not establish improvement. This verdict is candidate, not settled: Fig 11 says the capability claim "have not been tested forward, so they can still come back wrong."

## 3. Loop two, domain D2: accountability (ground reachability)

- **Actors:** the public principal, the contractor, oversight, the metric, the users. **Currency:** whether a failure can be detected and corrected. **Anchor:** the ground, which must not update.
- **Finding 4 (the channel decays).** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." Fig 10 :29: "→ keep the anchor reachable." Contracting lengthens the channel; each added step is a place for the ground to go out of reach.
- **Finding 5 (a target can stop being a measurement).** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." When hitting the number replaces contacting the outcome, the dashboard can stay green while the service fails. Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." An oversight body that is spent after one case and one that regenerates look the same on the organigram.
- **Finding 6 (substitution has two silent failures).** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails." Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." Complaint routes, appeal and open records are not cancelled by a contract; they fail to be reproduced, and the failure is invisible until the goal fails.
- **Finding 7 (per-case residuals must stay visible).** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single aggregate satisfaction score can hide the cases that matter most.
- **D2 verdict (`specified`, triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 11 and Fig 1, low overlap):** accountability weakens when the channel decays, a target decides the outcome, the habitat substitution fails to reproduce goal-required conditions, or per-case residuals are averaged away. It is not weakened by the actor change as such; an arrangement that keeps the ground reachable per case is not pre-refuted. Refuter: a contract in which every per-case failure remains detectable and correctable within a stated time and the metric demonstrably cannot absorb the failure, sustained across providers and years.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a net verdict. No trade-off score between D1 and D2, and no claim that one trades against the other at a fixed rate.
- Not that private provision is always cheaper or that public provision is always accountable. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- Not a social welfare weight between cost and access. That is a `gap`, not a derivation.
- Not a legal enumeration of delegable duties. That is a `gap`, read off a jurisdiction's law, not off the figures.
- Not a name of a contract, firm or jurisdiction as the answer. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not `grounded`. No service outcome or accountability hearing was measured.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

1. **A contact instrument for D1.** A comparison in which provider label is varied while method, habitat and contact are held fixed. If the outcome reliably moves, F1 and the capability claim fall.
2. **A reachability instrument for D2.** A per-case audit that shows whether every failure is detectable and correctable within a stated time, and whether the reported metric can absorb it.
3. **A union test.** A single scalar that predicts both the service outcome and correctability out of sample. If it survives, the two domains were one after all.
4. **An instrument for source discrimination.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here, an audit that distinguishes real improvement from quality shading, creaming and gaming would name what a clean metric conceals. "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing. Two gaps are recorded as verdicts: the distributional weight and the legal delegability question. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." The capability claim carries its own forward-testing caveat from Fig 11 :71-72 and is therefore `candidate`. Record the lineage, the refuters and the stop, and let a ground reading move it.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that privatization works or that it fails. It says two things that can be checked separately. First, changing who delivers a public service is not the same as changing that service's contact with its outcome: if the method, the habitat and the contact are unchanged, no capability gain follows from the ownership label, and what can change contact is a real contest between providers whose evidence does not overlap, or a method the old arrangement could not reach. Second, contracting lengthens the channel between the public and the service, and accountability weakens when that channel decays, when a target stops being a measurement and starts deciding the outcome, when the move fails to reproduce the complaint and appeal conditions the old habitat supplied, or when per-case failures are averaged away. The two halves have different anchors and are never scored on one scale. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The distributional weighting and the legal question of delegable duties are left open as gaps, not filled by a plausibility story.
