# Expert verification packet: the Tether on species interactions and evolutionary response

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or evolutionary biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question's "constrain or enable" is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition of interaction types. For a focal species-slot and trait, a response is constrained when it stays inside the set of moves the lineage's current frame can compose, and enabled when contact with a differing frame changes what is reachable. The partner does not create the trait. Valence is relative to the residual, so the same predator can constrain foraging and enable defence in a season. Capability is agent-and-habitat, so an improvement measured in isolation changes nothing unless it changes contact. No figure line names a partner or returns a net percentage; what is returned is a separator with refuters and an owed instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and field-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 plus a species pair you know

- **Statement:** interaction valence is relative to the indexed residual. There is no standing list of constraining or enabling interaction types.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49, "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50, "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** pick one interaction you know well. Name two traits in the same focal species for which the partner's effect has opposite signs. If no such pair can be named for that system, say so; that is a real failure signal.
- **Refuter:** an interaction whose sign against a fixed named trait is invariant across every enumerated habitat, condition and generation sampled. That would restore the standing list the question assumes.

### C2 — mechanical; checkable against Fig 6, Fig 8 and the population-genetics record

- **Statement:** constraint is closure and reachability; enablement is an import across a frame whose closure differs. The partner changes access, not the world's inventory of traits.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21, "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32, "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :23, "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :25, "An import adds no atom to the world."
- **What to check:** in a documented case of rapid evolution following an interaction, was the responding trait already present as standing variation, or did it arrive by migration or new mutation? If it was present, the interaction changed reach or selection, not trait genesis. If it arrived from outside, the provenance and the gap it explains are the claim.
- **Refuter:** a new heritable response atom produced by composition inside a frame held fixed, with no contact with a differing frame, no new mutation and no migration.

### C3 — design-facing; checkable against Fig 1, Fig 3 and your own measurement practice

- **Statement:** the residual must stay indexed per species-slot, and the word "response" cannot be used as an anchor until its source is discriminated.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **What to check:** find a published community-level mean interaction effect and ask whether a focal slot carried a live residual while the mean sat near zero. Then ask which of genetic evolution, plasticity, immigration and age-structure change the reported trait shift came from. If the study cannot distinguish them, that is the owed instrument, not a defect in the study's competence.
- **Refuter:** an aggregate interaction index that predicts every focal slot response without losing a live-slot signal in cases where per-slot analysis disagrees, plus a measurement that reads the source of a trait change from the same observation that gives its magnitude.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a named interaction is intrinsically constraining or enabling.
- Not that an interaction creates a trait or a new primitive.
- Not that a community mean effect is a verdict on any species.
- Not that a lab or microcosm result transfers to the field without a measured round-trip gap.
- Not that the word "response" already discriminates genetic change, plasticity and movement.
- Not a theory of everything, not a final room, and not `grounded`.
- Not a claim about any particular species pair. A named pair is a field binding with its own provenance.

## 5. What would settle it

An anchor measurement, not agreement. The owed discriminator separates genetic adaptation, phenotypic plasticity and movement for one trait in one season. Then, for each focal slot:

1. Is the residual live at that slot when the community mean is near zero?
2. Was the responding atom already reachable, or did it arrive from a differing closure, with provenance and a shadow test?
3. Does the effect survive partner removal, and what actors came with the substituted habitat of the test?
4. What is the measured round-trip gap between the lab shorthand and the field case?

Rejections of C1 to C3 are listed in section 2 and repeated in `01` section 4. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the corresponding claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 6 :21/:23/:25. Four short passages.
2. Name one species pair from your own work and two traits for which its effect flips sign. This checks C1 directly.
3. Say whether the responding variation in a case you know was standing, imported or new. This checks C2.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish evolution, plasticity and movement for that trait. If none exists, say so. This checks C3.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
