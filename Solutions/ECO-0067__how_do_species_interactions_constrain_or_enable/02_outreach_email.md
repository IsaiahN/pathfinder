**Subject:** One checkable separator on species interactions and evolutionary response

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about one specific output, not about a theory. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the framework first. If the output fails your check, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Take a focal species and one trait in one season. The question "do species interactions constrain or enable evolutionary responses?" assumes that interactions fall into two lists. That is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. The same predator can constrain a prey's foraging efficiency and enable its defence in the same season. The same pollinator can enable reproduction and constrain specialization. What decides the sign is not the interaction type. It is the residual the observer indexes: the gap between the response the inherited frame predicted and the response the interaction actually returned.
>
> In effect terms, a response is **constrained** when it stays inside the set of moves the lineage's current frame can compose. No amount of searching inside that set adds a new move. A response is **enabled** when contact with a different frame adds a move the lineage did not have access to. The partner does not create the trait. It changes what the lineage can reach, and the ground decides whether the new reach actually paid.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a standing list of good and bad interactions, not claiming that "interaction" means "selection pressure," not claiming that a named partner is derived from the framework, and not claiming any net percentage for the effect of interactions on evolution. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. A named binding such as "predation enabled defence in this population" is a claim from your field, with its own provenance, not something the framework produced.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before contacting you:
>
> 1. An interaction whose sign against a fixed named trait is invariant across every habitat and generation sampled.
> 2. A new heritable response arising by composition alone inside a frame held fixed, with no contact with a differing frame, no new mutation and no migration.
> 3. A community-level aggregate that predicts every focal response without losing a live-slot signal where per-slot analysis disagrees.
> 4. A lineage made more responsive in a substituted habitat with no change of contact in the original habitat, whose gain survives return.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as a check:** name the focal slot, name the residual, say whether the response stayed inside the lineage's existing reach or arrived through contact with a different one, and name the measurement that would settle it. If you know a case where that separator cannot be applied, that is the failure I am looking for.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** it is in the figures link. Run it on a system you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state whether what you got is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the separator and the four refuters. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** if the reader is curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground, mode and refuter.

Later agreement from a reader who has seen the framework is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
