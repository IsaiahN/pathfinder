## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question as posed offers two options: interactions constrain evolutionary responses, or interactions enable them. Stated as an effect rather than a label:

> For a focal species-slot `s` and a trait `t` in season `k`, the inherited frame predicts a response `Γ(b,a)`, the interaction returns an observation `o'`, and the miss `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` is live in some slot while the community mean may sit near zero. **What an answer would look like:** a rule that says, for each slot and each residual, whether the response stays inside the closure the lineage already holds (constraint) or whether contact with a differing closure adds an atom to what that lineage can reach (enablement), plus the instrument that would settle which occurred.

This binds the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run, as the Formula's "Direction" section requires, so the preference can be refused by the same test as everything else.

Two commitments follow, both checkable:

- The residual is indexed per species-slot, never averaged into a community-level "effect of interaction." Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- The ground is the only metric. Fig 1 :23, "The ground enters as the only metric." What settles whether a response occurred is what happened to the lineage (survival, reproduction, trait change measured), not the coherence of the story told about it.

The word "response" is itself a claim to be settled. A change in mean trait value may be genetic evolution, phenotypic plasticity, movement of individuals, or a shifted age structure. The question does not license reading any one of these into the term. Section 7 returns to this as an owed discriminator.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1.1 The residual by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** A species interaction is a typed relation between at least two actors, but the residual is carried by a focal slot and its partners, and third parties enter by cascade. Fig 11 :44, "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." A binary pair is the first enumeration, not the boundary of the habitat.
- **Symmetry.** The relation itself is directed (a predator acts on prey, a pollinator on a plant), while the valence of the relation is not carried by the actor. Fig 11 :49, "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50, "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." In bond terms, `→` and `⋛` are ordered, `+` and `∥` are symmetric (Fig 12 :92, "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing"). The interaction's arrow is ordered; the good/bad label is a comparison against a chosen residual.
- **Scale.** Ecological time (within a generation) and evolutionary time (across generations) are two scales with a lossy transform between. Fig 4 :20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :30, "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." A fast ecological response and a slow allele-frequency shift are not the same quantity at two resolutions; the gap between their descriptions is R_T and must be measured, not assumed zero.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor, verdict status

**Domain 1, the interaction habitat.** Actors: predators, prey, competitors, mutualists, parasites, pollinators, dispersers, conspecifics, and the abiotic conditions of light, water, temperature and season. Currency: contact and its fitness consequences. Anchor: what actually happens to the focal lineage, measured independently of any model of the interaction. Nameable anchor exists, so this is a domain. Fig 11 :47 lists "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency" among the things the habitat supplies. Verdict: `general` on the shape, `specified` on any named system, triangulated (Fig 11 with Fig 1).

**Domain 2, the response frame.** Actors: alleles, genotypes, regulatory and developmental pathways, plastic reaction norms, behaviour, and dispersing individuals. Currency: heritable variation, selection gradient, and generation time. Anchor: a measurement that survives removal from the interaction, for example a common-garden reading, a mark-recapture estimate, or a reciprocal transplant. Nameable anchor exists, so this is a domain. Fig 5 :70, "A term is settled when the ground pays for it." Verdict: `specified`, single-source on the figure side for the partition of response kinds, with world corroboration from Grant and Grant 2002 and Bell 2017.

**Domain 3, the verification frame.** Actors: field ecologists, modellers, reviewers, data archives. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is low and no domain's agreement with another is a witness. Verdict: `general` on the rule, triangulated (Fig 2 with Fig 7).

**There is no fourth domain for "the interaction itself."** An interaction that has no focal residual to range against is not yet a domain; it is a relation in D1 waiting for a slot. This is the anchor test applied at the census stage.

### 1.3 Partition or union

The question's grammar invites a partition: a list of constraining interactions and a list of enabling interactions. That is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A predator that constrains a prey's foraging trait can enable the same prey's defence trait in the same season; a mutualist that enables a plant's range expansion can constrain its local specialization. One lever aimed at "the interaction" leaves the intersections standing.

The union has at least three conditions: (i) which contact is enumerated, (ii) which slot the residual is indexed on, and (iii) which response atom is already in the lineage's closure. They cannot be averaged into a single net "effect of interactions," because their currencies differ (Figs 12 :125-127).

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception.** Detect that a trait observation misses the prediction at a slot. Fig 3 :19, "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
2. **Vocabulary.** Have terms for what the habitat affords, including the typed bond that connects actor to residual. Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." This is the link that usually breaks: a literature can name pollination and predation while having no term that distinguishes a response carried by an imported atom from one composed from held atoms.
3. **Objective.** Say what counts as progress: a residual that stops arriving at the slot, measured against a non-updating anchor.
4. **Planning.** Choose the probe (common garden, exclusion cage, reciprocal transplant, selection gradient) under incomplete information. Fig 6 :32, "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
5. **Learn and carry.** Settle the term and carry the method, not the recording. Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance. Fig 3 :20, "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Effort spent on a global "effect size" above the break cannot rescue a missing discriminator below it.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 — The interactions are the habitat, enumerated not composed (`general` shape, triangulated)

Fig 11 :43, "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44, "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :47 names "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency." The Symbols table :109 gives the compact definition: "Everything in contact with the residual: actors, conditions, relations. Enumerated, never composed."

So the first move is not to decide whether predation is constraining. It is to enumerate what is in contact with this slot, then what is in contact with those. Fig 11 :52-53 gives the reason: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

The figure does not supply the list. It supplies the rule for obtaining it. A published list of interaction types is a candidate habitat, not an enumerated one for this residual.

**World corroboration.** Thompson 2010 (*Evolution: Education and Outreach*, open access) reports that "interacting species often coevolve in some parts of their geographic ranges, creating coevolutionary hotspots where selection is reciprocal, but not in other parts, creating coevolutionary coldspots where reciprocal selection does not occur." That is a habitat that must be enumerated at each place, not inferred from a species pair's name. Yoder 2025 (*Evolution*, preprint read via search extraction) notes that species "end up interacting in the course of community assembly, simply because their traits are already compatible when they first encounter each other" (ecological fitting). Contact can therefore be new without being co-evolved, which is exactly the import case of F3.

**Refuter.** An interaction partner whose relevant effect on the focal residual is invariant across all enumerated slots, all conditions and all cascade depths would make enumeration unnecessary. None of the sources read reports such a partner.

### F2 — Valence is relative to the residual; there is no standing list of constraining or enabling interactions (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49, "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50, "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Applied here: a predator constrains a prey's foraging efficiency and enables its defence response; a pollinator enables a plant's reproduction and constrains specialization on a single pollinator; a competitor constrains a resident's abundance and enables character displacement. These are not contradictions. They are one relation ranged against two different residuals. The question's "or" is the error; it is a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :54).

**World corroboration.** Holland and Bronstein 2008 (Encyclopedia of Ecology, open PDF text read via search extraction) state: "The outcomes of interspecific interactions are not simply (+, 0, -), but instead vary along a continuum." Thompson 2010 adds that "A parasite might impose strong selection on its host population in some environments, but elsewhere live as a commensal on that same host species." Yoshida et al. 2003 (*Nature*) found that "single-clone algal cultures (lacking genetic variability) produced short cycle periods" while multi-clonal cultures produced long cycles, so the same predator changed the evolutionary trajectory only when the response atom was present. Grant and Grant 2002 (*Science*) found that "Natural selection occurred frequently in both species and varied from unidirectional to oscillating, episodic to gradual." Fig 11 :50 and these four sources are two different frames: one a metalaw about valence, the other field measurements across systems. Low overlap, so this is triangulated rather than a single reading.

**Refuter.** Show one interaction type whose sign against a fixed, named trait is invariant across every residual in every enumerated habitat and every generation sampled. That would restore the standing list the question assumes and break Fig 11 :49-51.

### F3 — Constraint is closure; enablement is an import across a differing closure (`general`, triangulated)

This is the core mechanical claim. "Constrain" in effect terms means the response stays inside what the lineage's current library can compose. Fig 6 :21, "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32, "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Constraint is not a force applied from outside; it is the shape of a closed reach.

"Enable" in effect terms means contact with a frame whose closure differs adds an atom to the focal frame. Fig 6 :23, "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :22, "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 8 :45, "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

Crucial limit: Fig 6 :25, "An import adds no atom to the world." The partner does not create the trait. It changes which traits the focal frame can reach and which are selected. This is why the answer cannot be "predation created defence." The defence atom must already be reachable, or must be imported from a differing closure with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52), and even then the world gained access, not a new primitive.

The import has a price. Fig 6 :24, "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." A borrowed adaptive story that does not explain the focal residual is a wrong atom.

**World corroboration.** Nowak 2006 (*Science*, full text read at PMC) derives five mechanisms by which cooperation becomes reachable: "natural selection implies competition and therefore opposes cooperation unless a specific mechanism is at work." Each mechanism is a condition that changes what can be composed; none creates a new organism. Laland, Matthews and Feldman 2016 (*Evolutionary Ecology*, full text read at PMC) define niche construction as "the modification of selective environments by organisms," and give the three criteria for when it affects evolution; the modifying organism changes contact, not the world's inventory of primitives. These are low-overlap frames (mathematical biology and human/evolutionary ecology) that both corroborate the mechanism shape. The figure verdict governs; the world sources only back it.

**Refuter.** A focal lineage that produces a genuinely new heritable response atom by composition inside a frame whose closure is held fixed, with no contact with a differing closure and no new mutation or migration, would break Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32.

### F4 — Per-slot residual, never an aggregated "effect of interactions" (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 and :22 are decisive here. A mean across all species in a community can sit near zero while one slot carries live mass. Yoshida et al. 2003 show exactly this structure: the presence or absence of clonal variability in one prey population changed the phase relation of an entire predator-prey cycle. Phillips et al. 2006 (*Nature*) show the same at a spatial slot: "toads at the front have longer legs than toads in older (long-established) populations," while the range-wide mean spread rate hides the front/background split.

Effect sizes are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. Fig 9 :60, "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A meta-analytic mean tells us where to spend field seasons; it does not tell us which slot responded.

**Refuter.** A demonstration that an aggregated community-level interaction index predicts a focal trait response with no loss of a live slot signal, across systems where a per-slot analysis disagrees, would break this finding.

### F5 — Improving the focal species in isolation changes nothing (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39, "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40, "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

So a species' "capacity to evolve" is not a property it carries into any habitat. It is agent-and-habitat. A common-garden result is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another"), and a synthetic microcosm proves wiring, never capability (Fig 11 :70, "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability").

**World corroboration.** Bell 2017 (*Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics*, abstract read; direct fetch 403, recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`) states: "The likelihood of rescue will depend both on attributes of the population, particularly abundance and variation, and on properties of the environment, particularly the rate and severity of deterioration." That is a two-factor contact claim, not an intrinsic capacity claim. Yoshida et al. 2003 supply the experimental instance: genetic variability mattered only in the presence of the oscillating predator.

**Refuter.** A controlled intervention that raises a lineage's evolutionary response in a substituted habitat while leaving every contact with the original habitat unchanged, and whose gain survives return to the original habitat, would break Fig 11 :40.

### F6 — The ground settles; agreement inside one literature does not (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29 gives the anchor test. Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 makes shared evidence disqualifying for a verdict. Fig 7 :35, "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

For this question, the ground is a measured trait shift, a selection gradient, or a survival/reproduction outcome read independently of the model that predicted it. A model fitted to the same dataset it explains is panel 1 of Fig 2, not panel 3. This also governs the world search: the ecological-evolutionary sources and the mathematical/human sources are counted as different frames only where their evidence pools genuinely differ; Thompson, Bronstein and the field studies share much of the same contemporary-evolution literature and are one witness for that literature's claims.

**World corroboration.** Yoder 2025 argues that demonstrating coevolution requires "quantifying natural selection exerted on one species by another, or finding population genetic evidence of that selection," not pattern matching. That is the same anchor demand in field language.

**Refuter.** A case where two models built on disjoint data and disjoint assumptions agree on a focal response and a third, independent measurement contradicts both would show agreement governing the verdict. The figures require the measurement to win.

### F7 — The response is often a cycle or a catalyst, not a linear chain (`specified`, single-source on the figure side)

Fig 12 :121, "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Frequency-dependent selection is the standard case: the prey's defence changes the predator's selection pressure, which changes the prey's defence. Fig 13 :88, "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."

Fig 12 :119 names the other structure: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A pollinator, a seed disperser, a mycorrhizal partner, or a niche-constructing organism survives its own use and changes selection on later generations. Spent and regenerating partners behave differently across uses, and most interaction vocabularies do not record which one they have.

**World corroboration.** Nowak 2006 gives the repeated-encounter condition `w > c/b`, which is a cycle condition in effect terms. Laland et al. 2016 describe "ecological inheritance", the persistence of modified selection beyond the constructor's lifetime, which is catalyst structure in field language. Triangulated at the mechanism-shape level, but the figure definition itself is single-source.

**Refuter.** A demonstration that frequency-dependent predator-prey or host-parasite dynamics can be represented by an acyclic chain with no loss of sign or closure behaviour would break Fig 12 :121.

### F8 — Two currencies, bits and actions, and they do not add (`specified`, single-source on the figure side)

Fig 12 :125, "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126, "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A short statement such as "predation enables defence" is cheap in bits and can take a decade of field seasons to settle. A long model over many parameters is expensive in bits and may be refuted by one exclusion experiment. Fig 12 :130 records the failure mode: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

This bears directly on the question's practical form. A literature review is cheap in bits and does not settle a response; a long-term selection study is expensive in actions and can settle one slot. Grant and Grant 2002 make the same point in field terms: "Continuous, long-term studies are needed to detect and interpret rare but important events and nonuniform evolutionary change."

**Refuter.** A single common currency that prices both the description and the settling actions without loss, demonstrated on a case where the two currently rank candidates in opposite orders, would break Fig 12 :125-130.

### F9 — Interaction regimes over a fixed atom set are isomers (`specified`, single-source figure definition with world corroboration)

Fig 12 :117, "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

A coevolutionary hotspot and a coldspot in Thompson's sense, or a mutualistic and a parasitic outcome of the same partner pair in Bronstein's sense, are **isomers** if the atom set is held fixed and only the arrangement or the bond changes. If one regime requires an atom the other does not (a third partner, a different abiotic condition, a dispersal stage), the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. Full derivation and falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Refuter.** If one regime can be written only with an atom absent from the other, the isomer claim fails and the compound reading stands. That is a real possibility for hotspot versus coldspot comparisons, because the hotspot may require a third party that the coldspot lacks.

### F10 — The figure set returns a separator, not a named partner or a net percentage (`gap`)

After the re-census there is no figure line that names which partner constrains which trait, or that returns a sign or magnitude for the net effect of interactions on evolution. Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 13 :135, "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." And Fig 13 :138-139, "narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."

So the honest output is the separator: enumerate the habitat, index the residual per slot, decide for each slot whether the response stays inside closure or arrives by contact with a differing closure, name the anchor that would settle it, and write the refuter. The particular binding (predator X enables defence trait Y in population Z) is a provenanced import from the field with a source gate and a shadow test, not a derivation from the figures.

This is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreached, not as silence. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence. Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

**Instrument owed.** A discriminator that separates genetic adaptation, phenotypic plasticity and movement to a new habitat for a given trait in a given season. Without it the word "response" stays unresolved and F3 to F9 cannot be settled for any particular case. This is the question's own neighbour, ecology.txt line 85 ("What evidence can distinguish genetic adaptation from behavioral flexibility or movement to a new habitat?"), and it is dependency link 2 of Fig 3.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, as required by the seat-map discipline: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. An interaction is found whose valence against a fixed named trait is invariant across every residual in every enumerated habitat and every generation sampled (breaks F2, Fig 11 :49-51).
2. A focal lineage produces a genuinely new heritable response atom by composition alone inside a closure held fixed, with no contact with a differing closure and no new mutation or migration (breaks F3, Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32).
3. A community-level aggregate interaction index predicts every focal slot response without losing a live-slot signal in cases where per-slot analysis disagrees (breaks F4, Fig 1 :19 and :22).
