# The Tether's answer: can metabolism evolve before genes encode enzymes?

**Mode: `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on G1 to G3. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §5). It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles three conditions with different grounds (Fig 9 :55 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."):

1. Can a chemical reaction network persist and be differentially retained without genes?
2. Can it *accumulate adaptations* across generations, that is, evolve in the Darwinian sense?
3. What has to arrive before catalysts can be encoded by genes?

A single yes or no averages away the only signal that says which condition is being answered (Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."). Verdicts are kept separate.

## 2. The shape, per domain

### D1. Yes at the structural level (`specified`, triangulated)

A self-maintaining network needs a catalyst that survives its own use, a loop that can close, and a gradient that can be spent:

- Fig 12 :119 - "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :121 - "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 13 :76 - "Energy and a gradient compose a flow."
- Fig 11 :36 - "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."

No gene appears in any of those lines. Selection runs where nothing maintains anything. The capability belongs to the network and its habitat together, not to the network alone (Fig 11 :39 - "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"). So a geochemistry-first account of *persistence and differential retention* is figure-licensed. This is the narrow sense in which the answer is yes.

### D2. Conditional: cumulative evolution needs a carrier (`specified` on the condition, `gap` on the measurement)

- Fig 7 :37 - "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"
- Fig 7 :38 - "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"
- Fig 7 :39 - "the ground's pressure on the room"

Chemistry supplies the pressure. The question is whether a pre-genetic compartment has a bracket with fidelity. The figures do not answer that with a number; they return the condition. Where fidelity is low, a network can change or show limited selectable heredity, but it does not accumulate adaptations open-endedly. What crosses a generation boundary is a method or a head start, not a recording of one success (Fig 4 :22 - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."). And the response must be read per compartment or per core, never as a pooled network average (Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

The heredity link is the dependency chain's middle link, the one with the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :27 - "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."). Whether a chemical carrier has enough fidelity is gap G2, `unreached`, not answered no.

### D3. The encoding handoff is an import of a new atom (`general` shape, identity `gap`)

- Fig 6 :21 - "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
- Fig 6 :22 - "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."
- Fig 8 :32 - "no search, however long, adds an atom"
- Fig 13 :115 - "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

Re-arranging metabolites explores arrangements but cannot add a new kind of atom. A hereditary polymer with sequence-like storage and templated copying is a new kind of atom relative to a metabolite-only library. Its appearance is the wall-moving import (Fig 6 :14 - "the only operator that moves the wall"), not a mint inside the chemical closure. Once settled it becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104 - "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"), which is how gene-encoded enzymes become primitives for the cell.

The framework cannot name that carrier:

- Fig 13 :135 - "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
- Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

RNA, PNA and coenzyme-lipid composomes are world candidates, not figure outputs. This is gap G1.

### Overall verdict, not an average

Metabolism can arise, persist and be differentially retained before genes (`specified`, yes). It can do so with limited, compartment-dependent heredity (`specified`, conditional). It cannot, on the figure derivation, run full gene-level cumulative evolution until a hereditary polymer is imported into the habitat it prepared (`general` shape). A global "yes" is true only of the first two clauses; a global "no" is false of both. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a named molecule was the first carrier. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that selection alone is evolution. Fig 7 :37-39 makes it a two-factor product, and the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap agrees: natural selection is a special case of the loop, not the whole answer.
- Not that metabolism *caused* genes or that the "before" was historical priority. Fig 12 :30 defines sequence, but the figures have no instrument for historical order (gap G3).
- Not that catalyst equals encoded enzyme. A metal or cofactor survives its own use at D1; the gene-encoded enzyme is the same functional role at D3 (Fig 10 :68 - "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between.").
- Not that habitat equals ground. Fig 11 :73 - "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- Not `grounded`. No reactor result or phylogenetic measurement is claimed by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

1. **D1, selection.** A reactor comparison showing differential retention among otherwise identical networks under a non-updating ground, or its absence.
2. **D2, fidelity.** Artificial selection on compartmentalized chemical networks with an assay whose response to selection is separable from the background dynamics. A response whose residual trends down across generations would fire refuter R2 and demonstrate pre-genetic Darwinian evolution. No response, with the background dynamics intact, would settle the limit.
3. **D3, import.** An experiment that identifies a new kind of atom appearing in a metabolite-only system and tests whether it is composable from held molecules. This would name the carrier and close G1.
4. **Historical order (G3).** Geochemical and phylogenetic reconstruction, which is a world ground, not a figure derivation.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §5: no differential retention; a heritable response to selection; a polymer composed from metabolite atoms; or template polymers preceding networks. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, on D2 and G1; open on G3.** The shape stops where the instruments stop distinguishing. The molecule sets a metabolism-first scenario declares are a visible guess, not nature's own list, and declaring them does not make them neutral. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable: which figure line governed which verdict, which refuter is live, and what would move the answer to `grounded`. Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes, metabolism evolved before genes" and it does not answer "no." It splits the question into three. A chemical network can persist and be differentially retained before any gene: it has a catalyst that survives its own use, a loop that can close, and a gradient that can be spent, and selection runs where no one maintains anything. But cumulative evolution is a two-factor quantity, transmission fidelity times ground pressure, and chemistry supplies only the pressure; whether a pre-genetic compartment has enough fidelity is an open measurement, with the world evidence leaning toward limited heredity only. The step to gene-encoded enzymes is an import of a new kind of atom, a hereditary carrier with sequence-like storage and templated copying, into the habitat that chemistry prepared. The framework can state that shape but refuses to name the molecule, because naming a missing primitive would mean already holding it. What it offers is the separator, the shape, four refuters written in advance, and the experiments that would close the three gaps.
