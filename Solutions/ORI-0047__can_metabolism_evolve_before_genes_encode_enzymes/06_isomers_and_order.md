# Are metabolism-first and genetics-first isomers? Bonds, cycles, catalysts and the "before" ordering

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `general` on the shape, `gap` on historical order. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §5).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three questions that this supporting derivation must answer: (1) what kind of thing is a metabolic network under Fig 12, a set of ingredients or a bonded arrangement; (2) are metabolism-first and genetics-first isomeric accounts, or is the relation between them an import; (3) what license does the figure set give the word "before" in the question?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - A list of molecules is not a metabolism

Fig 12 :5 - "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Fig 12 :111 - "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

A catalogue of prebiotic molecules names a state. It cannot name the route by which one molecule produces another, which molecule catalyses which reaction, or where the loop closes. Those are bonds. The world literature's shift from "which molecules formed" to "which reaction networks are reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated" (RAF theory, Xavier et al. 2020) is exactly the shift from ingredients to bonds. The figure does not derive RAF theory; it predicts that the bond is the half being lost when only ingredients are recorded.

**Falsifier:** a complete ingredient list that determines the reaction routes and catalytic dependencies without any additional information would break this. In chemistry it does not; the same molecules under different conditions give different networks.

### Q2 - Catalyst, cycle and the sign of the loop

Fig 12 :119 - "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

Fig 12 :121 - "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Three consequences for D1:

1. An autocatalytic core is a **catalyst** in the figure's sense: it survives its own use and is regenerated. A food molecule is a spent part. Nothing in an ingredient list records which is which, which is why a network can be mistaken for a pile of reactants.
2. A metabolic pathway is a **cycle**, not a linear `→` chain. A chain cannot close by construction; a cycle can. Vasas et al. 2012 put it in chemical terms: an autocatalytic loop is a closed circular path where each molecule depends on the previous one for its production, and a loop can be viable (exponentially growing) or suicidal (self-decomposing). That distinction is the figure's "sign of the loop decides whether it closes."
3. The figure's catalyst line explains why metals matter before enzymes. Fe/Ni sulfides and cofactors survive their own use; the Knowable Magazine report quotes Martin that the metals remain the essential catalysts even after enzymes incorporated them. Metal catalyst and encoded enzyme are the same *functional role* at different levels (Fig 10 :68: different actors, currency and ground per level).

**Falsifier:** a self-maintaining network demonstrably built from linear chains with no re-entrant loop would break the cycle claim. Note the world nuance: a network can contain chains internally, but its self-maintenance requires at least one closing loop.

### Q3 - Are the two scenarios isomers? The fixed-atom condition

Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :123 - "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two readings, and the difference between them is the whole point:

- **Fixed atom set.** If both scenarios are written over the same held actors (a network, a catalyst, a compartment, a gradient, and some information-bearing structure), arranged so that the network is primary in one and the template is primary in the other, then they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond and order, different substance. The shared substructure, cycle plus catalyst plus gradient plus compartment, is a **functional group**; the qualifier "metal-catalysed" versus "gene-encoded" says which member.
- **Open atom set.** If genetics-first requires a hereditary polymer that is not in the metabolite closure, then the two are **not** isomers. The relation is composition plus import (Fig 6 :22 - IMPORT adds an atom). This is the physically realistic reading for a metabolite-only starting set, and it is the one the main audit uses in F3.

The fixed-atom condition is a **live strain** on the isomer claim. It is named, not hidden. Both readings are kept because each predicts a different experiment: the fixed-atom reading predicts converging functional organization from different starting conditions (isomers, same substance-level outcome); the open-atom reading predicts a wall-crossing event with a new kind of molecule.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either scenario can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the import reading stands. Conversely, if a hereditary carrier is shown to be composable from the metabolite set alone, the import claim fails and the isomer reading stands. This is the same test as refuter R3 in `01` §5, carried here to the structural claim.

### Q4 - What the "before" ordering is licensed by

Fig 12 :30 - "A then B; B needs A to have happened"

`→` is sequence, an ordered bond: swap the operands and the meaning changes. The question's "before" is exactly this bond, and it is given by the question, not derived by the figures. Two cautions:

1. The figure's `→` says B needs A to have happened. It does not say A caused B, nor that A is sufficient for B. "Metabolism before genes" would at most license "genes need a prepared metabolic habitat," which is the habitat claim of F6, not a derivation of genes from metabolism.
2. Historical priority is not checkable from inside a frame (Fig 8 :34 on the metaframe; Fig 9 :44 - "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."). The only ground is geochemical and phylogenetic reconstruction, which is a world source. Gap G3 remains open.

**Falsifier:** a result showing template polymers in the same lineage before any self-sustaining network reverses the ordering. It is refuter R4.

### Q5 - The transition as import, restated in bond terms

Fig 6 :21 - "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Fig 6 :22 - "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

Fig 13 :115 - "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

In bond terms the appearance of a template polymer is not a new arrangement of held metabolites; it is a new operand. Once imported and settled, it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it (Fig 12 :104 - "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"), which is why enzymes and genes can then be treated as primitives by the next level of organization. The recursion runs both ways: a settled catalytic core becomes an atom for the compartment, and a settled gene becomes an atom for the cell.

**Falsifier:** an unbounded re-arrangement of the held metabolite set that yields a template-capable polymer without adding an operand would collapse this claim into Q3's fixed-atom reading.

## CONCLUSION

Under Fig 12 a metabolic network is a bonded arrangement (catalyst plus cycle plus gradient), not an ingredient list. A pre-genetic catalyst is a part that survives its own use; the encoded enzyme is the same functional role at a different level. The two origin scenarios are isomers **only** over a fixed shared atom set; with the hereditary polymer absent from the metabolite closure, the realistic relation is composition plus import. The "before" in the question is an ordered bond supplied by the question, licensed as a parameter and not as a derived historical result. The live strain is the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim, stated with its falsifier.

This rescues what is defensible in both camps without importing a name: D1 chemistry is figure-derived, D2 needs a fidelity instrument, and D3 needs an imported atom whose identity the framework refuses to supply.
