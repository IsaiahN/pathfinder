# Outreach draft: check one claim about early-warning signals before deciding anything else

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the two-stage offer only works if the stage-1 claim survives. The claim below is a shape claim, not a frequency, and it carries its own refuter. Do not add a number to it before it has been checked.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about early-warning signals of collapse

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` and `specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how often do early-warning indicators fail?" is a union of two different questions, and a single pooled rate answers neither. A false alarm is measured against systems that did not collapse. A missed warning is measured against systems that did. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." So an honest rate has to be per system class, per indicator, per lead-time window and per threshold convention, with the collapse outcome settled independently of the indicator.
>
> **What would refute this claim, written down before I wrote to you:**
> 1. a single pooled frequency shown sufficient to decide where an indicator fails, invariant to per-system and per-indicator decomposition;
> 2. false alarms and missed warnings shown to share one mechanism, one denominator and one cost structure;
> 3. a source-discriminating instrument showing that a named "failure to appear" was genuine absence at every resolvable scale, not a weak signal and not a decayed monitoring channel;
> 4. a collapse definition and study-selection rule under which the reported rate does not move.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that early-warning indicators never work, that a particular statistic is bad, or that I have a failure rate to report. I do not. The figures return a shape, not a number: Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The literature I have read does not supply a transferable rate either; it supplies reasons a reading can be right about a signal and wrong about the source.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - [Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`](https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures)
> - [Scheffer et al. 2009, Early-warning signals for critical transitions](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08227)
> - [Boettiger and Hastings 2012, Early warning signals and the prosecutor's fallacy](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2012.2085)
> - [Hirsch and Koren 2026, Early-warning signals of critical transitions in global ecoregions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.117964)
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
