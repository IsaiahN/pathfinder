## 2. What the wider literature contributes

Three findings bear directly on the shape claim:

1. **No single distribution.** Lempert and Collins 2007 and the IPCC Chapter 17 both reject a single best probability distribution as a description of a deeply uncertain future. This is the decision-science version of Fig 1 :22, and it arrives from a different field, so it is a genuinely low-overlap corroborator.
2. **Reversibility and path dependency.** IPCC Chapter 17 explicitly says integrated pathways are best when "path dependencies are avoided in order to not limit future options". This is the policy version of Fig 11 :61 and Fig 9 :41. Same broad assessment pool as finding 1 here, so it is one witness, not two.
3. **Diversification under unresolved extremes.** Feldman and Kettenring 2025 found that extreme drought and flooding overwhelmed single treatments, and recommend diverse mixes with a range of tolerances. This is the ecological version of Fig 2 :42 and is genuinely low overlap.

A fourth source, the Ellsberg paradox, supplies the human-cognition mechanism: unknown probability is not merely a less certain known probability. It corroborates the refusal to fold the live slot into an expected value.

One conflict is recorded rather than hidden. The strong cost-blind precautionary principle holds that one should not balance costs against benefits when irreparable damage is possible. That conflicts with Fig 5 :43 (the acceptance test is a bargain) and Fig 12 :125 (two currencies). It was sent back to the census, assigned to a different quantity (a convention that became an outcome), and refused. The weak, proportional formulation in Rio Principle 15 is compatible. See `01` section 7 S5.

## 3. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the generator (the per-slot residual, the search order, the refuter), not a recording of one model run.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40** agreement from a shared evidence pool is worthless as a verdict. A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies agreement.
- **Fig 2 :42** buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. One recipient who can refute is worth more than a hundred who can agree.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority; (4) only if asked, the formula as a reusable method with the instruction to write a fresh refuter and state a mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim and pre-registered refuter. No theory, no links, no request for agreement or trust. The recipient's verdict is recorded here.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only:** the three source links, after the stage-1 verdict is on record. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3, opt-in only:** the formula as a method to reuse, with each user writing their own refuter and mode. Fig 4 :22 permits giving away a generator; Fig 5 :65 requires the wanted shape to be bound as an ordinary term first.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. The refuter in `01` section 4 is unrun, so every claim is `candidate`, and the email says so.

## 5. Legal and policy constraints recorded

- Copernicus Earth System Dynamics requires disclosure of AI tool use in Methods or Acknowledgements. Springer Climatic Change states that an LLM cannot satisfy its authorship criteria and that use must be documented in Methods; AI-assisted copy editing is treated differently from generative content.
- GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules apply to unsolicited outreach in many jurisdictions. The venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are public institutional or opt-in channels. No personal emails, no scraped lists, no bulk send, and an opt-out line in every one-to-one message.
- IPCC expert review windows are announced; unsolicited transmission of an unreleased claim to the Secretariat should not assume confidentiality.
