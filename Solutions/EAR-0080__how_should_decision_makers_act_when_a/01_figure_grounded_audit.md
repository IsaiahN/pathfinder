## 0. The residual in effect terms

A decision maker holds a library `Γ` of actions and beliefs. For action `a` and tracked slot `s`, prediction `Γ(b,a)` against observation `o'` leaves a residual `R_s`. The Symbols table names `R` as "The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."

The question selects a special kind of live slot. Its consequence is near-irreversible. Its timing and probability are unresolved. Its plausibility is positive. In effect terms the residual is: **which action keeps the ground reachable and the cuts revisable when the slot is live, the consequence is asymmetric, and additional deliberation cannot move the wall?**

State `R` by arity, symmetry and scale before any figure line is chosen:

- **Arity: three overlapping conditions.** (i) plausibility, meaning positive mass in some slot; (ii) irreversibility, meaning a one-way consequence; (iii) unresolved timing and probability. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question with opposite answers. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Symmetry: asymmetric.** Crossing a threshold and un-crossing it are not the same action. Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." The comparison bond is ordered, not symmetric (Operators table :46-51).
- **Scale: two strata with a lossy transform between.** The decision sits at a board or policy stratum; the ground is the physical system at another, and the ground does not update on what the board believes. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". The round trip gap `R_T` between model output and the physical system is therefore load bearing (Fig 4 :30).

`R` is **not** a single expected loss to be minimised. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The question's "plausible but uncertain" is precisely a live slot that aggregation would erase.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Do not aggregate the live slot into a single expected value (decisive)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"
Fig 1 :20: "Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

The question describes exactly the legal state Fig 1 names: a global expected loss can look small while one slot carries large, positive, poorly characterised mass. A single expected-value number is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 9 :60 (filter versus witness), low overlap between the agent figure and the leave/arrive figure. World corroboration from decision science: Lempert and Collins 2007, as recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`, define robust decision making as using "multiple representations of the future rather than a single set of probability distributions". Human cognition corroborates from a different field: the Ellsberg paradox is "generally taken to be evidence of ambiguity aversion, in which a person tends to prefer choices with quantifiable risks over those with unknown, incalculable risks." Both are secondary, neither decides.

**Refuter:** show that a single aggregate score preserves the per-slot signal for the live slot and that a decision made from the aggregate keeps a channel to the ground at that slot; or show that the slot has zero positive mass after a measurement, in which case the residual is no longer live.

### F2 - Unresolved probability is `unreachable` or `underivable`, a verdict and not silence (decisive)

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."
Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."
Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."

The absence of a settled probability is not a probability of zero. It is a report about the reach of the instruments. The framework requires three distinct questions, in a fixed order (Formula step 7, Fig 6 :49-51):

1. **SEARCHED**, is the probability or timing already in `closure(Γ)` and unfound? Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." This is asked first because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer.
2. **INWARD**, is any instrument at any resolution already returning something that fails to resolve? Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :34: "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."
3. **OUTWARD**, does another frame already hold a discriminating model? Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." Importing a precautionary atom merely because the budget ran out adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :41-43).

**Verdict:** `general` on the barrier, triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 13 and Fig 6, three figures with low overlap between the metaframe theorem, the reach semantics and the instrument rule. World corroboration: Lenton et al. 2008 report that "Many of the systems we consider do not yet have convincingly established tipping points" and used expert elicitation to rank sensitivity and uncertainty, which is a shape rather than a settled probability. Rio Declaration Principle 15, as recorded from the public text: "Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, lack of full scientific certainty shall not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation."

**Refuter:** an instrument already returning a resolved timing and magnitude at the relevant slot, with a stated failure condition and provenance, would close the residual and remove this finding. So would a proof that the probability was in `closure(Γ)` all along and the failure was budget only: then the remedy is more search, not a new frame.

### F3 - Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (decisive)

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Irreversibility raises the cost of being wrong, so the figure raises the required independence. Concretely: an ensemble of related models is one frame wearing many names; a physical model, a palaeo reconstruction, an early-warning time-series analysis and affected communities' local knowledge are lower-overlap frames if they do not draw on the same evidence. Their verdicts are ranged separately against D1 (Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."). The pool must be unauthored before it is examined (Fig 10 :73).

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 8 and Fig 7, three different figures with low overlap. World corroboration from IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17, which states that co-production linking "scientific, Indigenous, local, practitioner and other forms of knowledge can make climate risk management processes and outcomes more effective and sustainable". A genuinely low-overlap corroborator is Feldman and Kettenring 2025 in *Wetlands*, which finds that "passive recolonization" was insufficient and recommends "bet-hedging strategies (e.g., seeding diverse mixes with species with a range of hydrologic tolerances)". One climate-risk evidence pool counts as one witness, not several.

**Refuter:** demonstrate two frames with genuinely low overlap that disagree on the decision shape and that no available instrument can discriminate; or demonstrate that the consequence of being wrong is reversible and cheap, which lowers the required independence and weakens the prescription.

### F4 - The intervention is asymmetric: introduction is available, exclusion is not (decisive for reversibility)

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :62: "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."
Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
Fig 9 :40-41: "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible"
Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

An irreversible risk is not just a large loss. It is a one-way move in the space of actions. Once an emission, an infrastructure commitment, or an introduced species is in the habitat, contact is a fact that does not consult the decision maker. Two failure modes are silent: what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced. A policy that relocates the risk to another jurisdiction is a substituted habitat, not a removed one (Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another.").

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 9 :41 (low overlap: habitat intervention asymmetry versus the ordering of elimination cuts). World corroboration: IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17 states that integrated pathways are most suitable when "path dependencies are avoided in order to not limit future options for climate resilient development". The precautionary-principle literature records the economic version: "irreversibility of possible future consequences" creates a "quasi-option effect" favouring decisions that "allow for more flexibility in the future." Two different fields, one shape.

**Refuter:** show that the specific intervention is genuinely subtractable at acceptable cost, including the second-order contacts it created. Then Fig 11 :61 does not bind and staged reversibility is not required for that action. A moratorium on a not-yet-introduced activity is the clean case; an already-committed physical change is not.

### F5 - Keep the ground reachable; the ground does not decay, the channel does

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :25: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
Fig 10 :29: "→ keep the anchor reachable"
Fig 10 :19: "→ install what can be violated"
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."

The physical system is unaffected by the board's confidence. What decays is the channel: stale monitoring, dropped transient signals, saturated indicators, readings pooled across a change. The correct output when the channel dies is "contact has been lost", not a verdict about the world (Fig 10 :27). For D3 the legitimate anchor is human judgment, because the question is about people; climate models are instruments here and "can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37). A decision threshold that becomes the thing that decides has stopped being a convention and must be checked against the ground (Fig 10 :47).

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 2 (two figures, different objects: channel maintenance versus anchor legitimacy). World corroboration: IPCC Chapter 17 treats monitoring and evaluation as "essential for tracking adaptation progress and learning about adaptation success and maladaptation"; Lenton et al. 2008 examine whether "an incipient bifurcation in a dynamical system could be anticipated" from time-series properties.

**Refuter:** a channel that is demonstrably non-stale, unsaturated, unpolarised and still returning violations against a non-updating ground; and a decision rule whose compliance is not being substituted for contact with the ground.

### F6 - Hold the two currencies apart: description cost and action cost do not add

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :127: "They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."
Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A policy can be cheap to state and take a whole budget to settle. A probability estimate is a price in bits; the cost of acting, monitoring and reversing is a price in actions. Summing them into one expected-value score is the named error. Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold." Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."

**Verdict:** `general` on the two-currency rule, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 9 :60. World corroboration: robust decision making uses "robustness rather than optimality as a criterion to assess alternative policies", including "keeping options open", and describes tradeoffs rather than a strict ranking. The Ellsberg paradox shows that "a choice based on maximizing the expected utility is also impossible" when probabilities are unknown, which is the human-cognition version of the same refusal.

**Refuter:** a decision formalism that commits both quantities in the same unit without loss and demonstrates that the conversion preserves the ordering of actions under a stated ground reading.

### F7 - A plan, a goal and progress are bonds, not ingredients

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
Fig 12 :113: "A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

A decision maker who can list hazards, exposures and vulnerabilities but cannot state a route, a comparison and a subtraction has ingredients and no decision grammar. The dependency chain breaks at the objective link: the middle link "has neither" an obvious failure nor an obvious test (Fig 3 :25). This is why "reduce risk" is not a decision and "keep this slot's channel reachable, keep this cut reversible, and stop when this stated failure fires" is.

**Verdict:** `general`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 12 plus Fig 3, two figures but the same structural vocabulary, so not counted as independent corroboration). World corroboration from IPCC Chapter 17: "iterative risk management is most suitable in situations characterised by large uncertainties, long time frames, the potential for learning over time", an ongoing cycle of assessment, action, reassessment and response rather than a one-shot optimum.

**Refuter:** show a decision vocabulary that states a route, a comparison and progress using only atoms and no typed relations, and that still ranges against a ground.

### F8 - Write the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run

Fig 5 :65: "The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."
Fig 5 :66: "Then the preference is refusable by the same test as everything else. Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."
Fig 5 :67: "a frame can refuse the right kind of thing and still aim the refusal at the outcome it did not want rather than at the belief it holds."
Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a preference, not as a term, so no internal check can see it. The remedy is a discipline: write down the wanted decision shape before the analysis, bind it, let it accrue residual, and expose it to the same refusal test. That is the pre-registered refuter in section 4.

**Verdict:** `general`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 5 and the seat map rule, one evidence pool). World corroboration from robust decision making's vulnerability-and-response-option framing, which characterises uncertainty in the context of a particular decision "rather than a predict-then-act decision framework".

**Refuter:** show a decision process that commits no wanted shape in advance and nevertheless refuses its own failed commitments at the same rate as one that does.

### F9 - Declare the stake; an interest in the outcome is unavoidable

Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist."
Fig 10 :59: "The condition is that nothing about which value the ground returns today serves the occupant: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."
Fig 9 :51: "what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"

Decision makers and analysts have interests in outcomes. The figures do not ask for a stake-free position, which does not exist; they ask that the interest in the reading be disqualifying and that what cannot be removed be declared. A reviewer should see evidence bearing on the residual, not evidence bearing only on the maker.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 9 (two figures, different objects). No world source is needed; this is a rule the framework imposes on itself.

**Refuter:** exhibit a verifier with no interest in any outcome and no interest in the reading, which would falsify the claim that a stake-free seat does not exist.

### F10 - Enumerate the habitat; the cascade is three steps out, not one

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 11 :52-54: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

A decision scoped to direct contact misses the cascade. The same intervention is a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another, so there is no standing list of good policies. IPCC Chapter 17 reports that "complex risks result from multiple climate hazards occurring concurrently, and from multiple risks interacting, compounding overall risk and resulting in risks transmitting through interconnected systems and across regions." The habitat must be enumerated per residual, outward until the cascade stops mattering.

**Verdict:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 11). World corroboration from the IPCC assessment (same broad climate pool as other sources here, so not an independent witness) and from the wetland restoration study, which found that two geographically close sites had different outcomes because of salinity and hydrology, a second-order condition not visible from a single-site average.

**Refuter:** show a habitat enumeration that stops at first-order contact and still catches every residual that matters at second and third order.

### F11 - The gap: no figure line returns a probability, discount rate or named optimal action

Fig 9 :32: "→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 13 :136: "it can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."

No figure returns a numerical probability of crossing, a discount rate, a threshold probability for action, or the name of one correct policy. The framework returns a kind of thing: a decision process with these eight properties. Calling that absence an answer would be a `mode` violation, because an unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict. The honest report is `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not `gap` as silence.

**Verdict:** `general` structural, `gap` on the numerical policy. `unreached`, not `unreachable in principle`: a source-discriminating instrument could close it.

**Refuter:** a figure line that does return a universal numerical policy, produced by derivation rather than by reading a number off a world source.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Slot aggregation is harmless.** A single aggregate score preserves the per-slot signal for the live irreversible slot, and a decision from it keeps a channel to the ground at that slot (against F1).
2. **The residual is closed.** A measurement resolves timing and magnitude at the relevant slot with a stated failure condition and provenance, so the slot no longer carries unresolved positive mass (against F2).
3. **Wrong classification of the barrier.** The probability was in `closure(Γ)` all along and only the budget was short, so more search closes it and no import or new frame is owed (against F2, Formula step 7).
4. **The consequence is reversible.** The specific intervention is subtractable at acceptable cost, including the second-order contacts it created, so Fig 11 :61 does not bind (against F4).
5. **Independence is not bought.** Two genuinely low-overlap frames, each ranged separately against a non-updating ground, converge on a different decision shape and the ground settles it against this one (against F3).
6. **The union is a partition.** One lever clears plausibility, irreversibility and unresolved probability together, including their intersections, so Fig 9 :55 does not apply (against the census).
7. **The channel is sound.** Monitoring is demonstrably non-stale, unsaturated and still returning violations, and a decision rule's compliance is not substituting for contact with the ground (against F5).
8. **The two currencies convert.** A formalism commits description cost and action cost in one unit without loss and preserves the action ordering under a stated ground reading (against F6).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the shape is revised. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`. Vague agreement with the shape is not a refutation and is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Keep the live slot separate** from the aggregate score. Report per-slot residual mass, not just expected loss (F1).
2. **Ask SEARCHED before anything else**, then run INWARD and OUTWARD as two opposed tracks. Report `unreachable` as a verdict where it applies, and do not import against a budget limit (F2).
3. **Buy independence in proportion to the asymmetry of consequence.** Use low-overlap frames with an unauthored pool, and range them separately against the ground (F3, F10).
4. **Prefer revisable cuts.** Keep every cut ranked and reversible; stage commitments; write the reversal condition before the commitment; count path dependency as a cost (F4, F5).
5. **Keep two accounts.** Bits for the description, actions for finding out and reversing. Never sum them into one score (F6).
6. **Bind the wanted shape as an ordinary term** before the analysis and let it be refused (F8).
7. **Declare the stake** and deny the verifier evidence bearing only on the maker (F9).
8. **Report the gap.** Do not invent a probability, a discount rate or a named optimal policy (F11).

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." What should travel to a decision maker is the generator: the per-slot residual, the three-question search order, the independence rule, the reversal condition and the refuter. Not a recording of one model run.

## 7. Strains and re-census

**S1, human judgment versus physical measurement as anchor.** Fig 2 :37 and Fig 2 :29 appear to name different anchors. Re-census: legitimacy is question relative. D3 asks "how should people act", so human judgment is the legal anchor and models are instruments. D1 asks "when does the ice sheet cross", so the physical system is the anchor. Dissolved, recorded.

**S2, exclusion versus preventing introduction.** Fig 11 :61 says exclusion is not available, yet a regulator can stop a not-yet-started activity. Re-census: the figure governs actors already in a habitat, where contact is a fact that does not consult the decision maker. A moratorium prevents introduction; it does not subtract committed warming or built infrastructure. Narrowed and left **live** as a type question: the asymmetry binds differently for cumulative emissions, for built infrastructure and for revisable policy. A falsifier is attached (section 4, item 4).

**S3, a decision threshold as a convention that decides an outcome.** Fig 10 :47 says a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. But a decision rule has to decide. Re-census: the rule is a shared meaning with no truth value; when compliance with it replaces contact with the ground, it has become a verdict carrying the seat's authority (Fig 10 :48). The constraint is a live design requirement: keep the threshold revisable against the ground and declare who authored it. Named, not hidden.

**S4, expert elicitation versus unresolved probability.** Lenton et al. 2008 use formal elicitation to rank elements by sensitivity and uncertainty, which looks like the probability the figures call unreachable. Returned to census: the elicitation produces a partial ordering, a shape, not a settled distribution, and Fig 8 :54 says underivable is not unknowable. No verdict flipped; dissolved.

**S5, strong precaution versus the MDL bargain (live world/figure conflict).** The strong precautionary principle says one should not balance costs against benefits when irreparable damage is possible, with no regard to cost. Fig 5 :43 requires that stating a term plus what remains unexplained cost less than what was unexplained before. This is a genuine conflict. It was returned to the census, not used to flip the verdict. Re-census: the strong version is a convention that decides an outcome (Fig 10 :47) and a refusal to price actions, which the two-currency rule forbids (Fig 12 :125-127). The weak, cost-effective form recorded in Rio Principle 15 is compatible. The figure verdict stands and the conflict is recorded.

**After re-census:** three live constraints (S2, S3, S5), two dissolved (S1, S4), none erased.
