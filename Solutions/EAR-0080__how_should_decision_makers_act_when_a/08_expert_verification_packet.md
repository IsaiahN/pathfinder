# Expert verification packet - the Tether on decision making under plausible irreversible risk

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate-risk researcher, decision scientist or risk practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the decision-process shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural barriers, `gap` on the numerical policy. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed and no probability is estimated. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A plausible irreversible risk with unresolved timing and probability is not one decision problem with an optimum. It is a union of three overlapping conditions, and each needs its own verdict. Keep the risk indexed as a live slot instead of averaging it into an expected loss. Treat unresolved probability as `unreachable` or `underivable`, which are verdicts, not zero and not silence; ask first whether the answer is already in the closure and unfound, then run the inward instrument question and the outward import question as separate tracks. Buy independence from genuinely lower-overlap frames in proportion to the asymmetric cost of being wrong. Keep every cut ranked and reversible with the reversal condition written before the commitment. Keep a non-updating ground reachable through a channel that can report its own failure, hold description cost apart from action cost, declare the decision maker's stake, and refuse any rule that has become the outcome it was meant to describe. No figure returns a probability, a discount rate, a threshold or a named policy, and that is reported as a gap, not filled.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework internal and decision facing: do not aggregate the live slot

- **Statement:** a global expected loss near zero is compatible with a live irreversible slot carrying positive mass. Aggregation removes the only signal that says where to act.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :20 "Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** in a real assessment, does the headline expected damage hide a low-probability, high-consequence, poorly characterised element? Is the per-slot residual mass reported, or only the aggregate?
- **Refuter:** a single aggregate score that preserves the per-slot signal for that slot and keeps a channel to the ground at it, or a measurement showing zero positive mass at the slot.

### C2 - framework internal: absence of a settled probability is a verdict, not silence

- **Statement:** no search result that fails to resolve a threshold proves its absence, and `unreachable` is a report about the reach of the instruments, not a claim that nothing exists.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"; Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
- **What to check:** is the correct next step SEARCHED (already in closure but unfound), then an inward instrument improvement from something already returning a weak signal, then an outward import from a lower-overlap frame? Are these kept as separate tracks rather than one blended argument?
- **Refuter:** a measurement that resolves timing and magnitude at the slot, or a demonstration that the answer was in `closure(Γ)` all along and only more search was owed.

### C3 - decision facing: asymmetry and the price of independence

- **Statement:** the intervention is asymmetric, so cuts must be kept reversible; and the cost of being wrong raises the required independence between checking frames.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."; Fig 9 :40-41 "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible"; Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."; Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **What to check:** does the proposed action create contacts that cannot be subtracted, including second-order effects three steps out? Are the reviewing frames genuinely lower-overlap, or do they share models, data and priors?
- **Refuter:** show that the specific cut is subtractable at acceptable cost including its second-order contacts, or that the frames converge by genuinely convergent derivation with separate provenance and a ground that settles between them.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a probability of crossing, a discount rate, a threshold probability for action, or any named optimal policy. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not "apply the precautionary principle" in its strong, cost-blind form. That is a convention that decides an outcome (Fig 10 :47) and ignores the two currencies (Fig 12 :125-127). The weak, proportional form is compatible.
- Not "wait for better information" or "maximise expected value". Both leave the irreversibility intersection standing (Fig 9 :56) or erase the live signal (Fig 1 :22).
- Not a verdict that any tipping element will cross. No measurement was taken.
- Not a room consensus. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not `grounded`. No instrument has settled any part of this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement:

1. A source-discriminating instrument resolving timing and magnitude at the live slot, with a stated failure condition and provenance.
2. A demonstration that the barrier was a budget limit, so the remedy is more search rather than a new atom (Formula step 7).
3. A reversal test showing the cut is subtractable at acceptable cost, including second-order contacts.
4. A lower-overlap frame predicting a different decision shape with its own falsifier, and a ground that settles between the two.
5. A threshold test: does a `⋛` relation hold between the monitored hazard and the tolerated consequence at that level? The Operators table defines `⋛` as "a threshold relation between two quantities"; the operand type is an open question, not a settled one.

The standing refuter is in `01` section 4 and was written before the conclusion. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19-22 and Fig 9 :55-57, four short passages. Is a per-slot live risk legitimately different from an aggregate expected loss?
2. Read Fig 8 :39 and Fig 13 :134. Does `unreachable` here say something about instruments rather than about the world? Name one instrument that could close it, or say why none is available.
3. Read Fig 11 :61 and Fig 2 :42. For one real policy option, is the intervention subtractable, and are the checking frames genuinely lower-overlap?
4. Send the refutation, not the endorsement. If none of the eight refuter conditions in `01` section 4 fires, say which one you would test first and why.
