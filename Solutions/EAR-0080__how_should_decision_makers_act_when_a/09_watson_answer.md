## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what action a decision maker should take when a plausible risk is near-irreversible and its timing and probability are unresolved. Option A: wait for a settled probability, then optimise expected value. Option B: treat the live slot as a union compound and choose a revisable process that keeps the ground reachable and buys independence in proportion to the asymmetric cost of being wrong. What each leads to: A aggregates away the only per-slot signal, treats absence of evidence as evidence of absence, and leaves the irreversibility intersection standing; B narrows the shape a defensible decision must have, states refuters in advance, and reports the numerical policy as a gap rather than inventing it. The question is about people, so human judgment is the legitimate anchor for the decision claim (Fig 2 :37), while the physical system is the anchor for the physical claim (Fig 2 :29).

- **Domain 1, the physical ground.** Actors: tipping elements, forcings, ice sheets, carbon cycle, exposed people and infrastructure. Currency: physical state changes and their timing. Anchor: the physical system, which does not update on any frame's opinion (Fig 2 :29; Fig 10 :24). The ground of the compound.
- **Domain 2, the risk-characterisation frame.** Actors: models, elicitation, monitoring, early-warning indicators, assessors. Currency: prediction versus observation, residual mass per slot. Anchor: D1 through a channel that can fail (Fig 10 :25). Partition boundary question: which slots carry positive mass, and which are in `closure(Γ)` but unfound.
- **Domain 3, the decision and governance frame.** Actors: decision makers, regulators, affected communities. Currency: actions, consequences, rights, reversibility. Anchor: human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-37). Compound: the same event is a residual in D2 and a right or loss in D3.
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: reviewers, competing models, other disciplines, local knowledge. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35). Union member overlapping D2 and D3 but ranging each separately against D1.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Do not wait for a settled probability and do not maximise a single expected value. Keep the plausible irreversible risk indexed as a live slot; report unresolved timing and probability as `unreachable` or `underivable`, which are verdicts and not silence or zero; ask SEARCHED before the two opposed instruments; buy independence from genuinely low-overlap frames in proportion to the asymmetric cost of being wrong; keep every cut ranked and reversible with the reversal condition written before the commitment; keep the ground reachable through a channel that can report its own failure; hold description cost and action cost in separate accounts; declare the stake; and refuse any rule that has become the outcome it was meant to describe. The figures return this shape and route only. No probability, discount rate, threshold or named policy is returned, and that is a reported `gap`, not an omission. This is `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the barriers, `gap` on the numerical policy, and not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the shape is sent with its refuter. A wrong cut is kept reversible, so an error can be corrected without withdrawing a primitive. The real cost is spending the independence of outside frames by sending a recording of one model run instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 prices that cost: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable part is leading with the shape and the refuter rather than a conclusion.
- **undoable:** yes. The numerical gaps are not filled by inventing numbers, so nothing has to be retracted later for having been named prematurely. A sent shape can be superseded by a corrected shape when a refuter fires or an instrument resolves a slot. Staged, reversible commitments are undoable by construction; a one-way commitment is not, which is why F4 governs.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measurement that resolves timing and magnitude at the slot closes F2; a demonstration that the barrier was only a budget limit changes the remedy to more search (Formula step 7); a subtractable intervention weakens F4 for that action; two genuinely low-overlap frames that disagree on the shape and a ground that settles between them reverse F3; a lever that clears all three conditions including the intersections reverses the union reading; a unit-preserving conversion would reverse F6. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
