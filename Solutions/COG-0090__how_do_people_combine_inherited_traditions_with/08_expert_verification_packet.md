# Expert verification packet: the Tether on tradition and innovation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cultural-evolution researcher, institutional scholar, heritage practitioner, or methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No community was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a field result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

People combine inherited traditions with new information because the framework's belief is already formed from private history and inherited prior mixed by a learnable weight, and because new material is an import that expands a community's reachable set without deleting anything. Correction works by adding a refusal that removes a term's standing to compose while keeping the term in the record; what crosses generations is a method or a head start, not a recording. What people call replacement is usually one of three other things: an added refusal, a substituted habitat whose unchosen introductions and failed reproductions are not yet measured, or a convention that has begun to decide a factual outcome. The question is a union of three domains with three grounds, and their verdicts must not be averaged. The framework identifies the shape, not which tradition or institution anyone should keep.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading the Symbols table and Figures 5 and 6

- **Statement:** combination is the default state of a belief, and correction is additive rather than destructive.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Symbols table :20-21, *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation"*; Symbols :24-26, *"w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings"*. Fig 6 :42, **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."** Fig 5 :55, **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."**
- **What to check:** is there any operation in the framework that strikes a held term from the record? If not, a literal replacement is not available, and the question's dichotomy has to be re-censused.
- **Refuter:** a documented case in which a tradition is struck from the community's record with no refusing term added and no habitat substituted, and the outcome is still correctly described as combination. Or evidence that the inherited/private weighting is fixed by architecture and does not move with outcomes.

### C2: world-facing; checkable against institutional and engineering evidence

- **Statement:** the new institution is a habitat substitution whose costs are what failed to be reproduced and what was introduced unchosen, and the combination is a bond whose type must be identified, not an ingredient list.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66, **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :68-69, **"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"**. Fig 12 :46, **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** Fig 12 :94, **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- **What to check:** in a case you know, list (a) what the new institution did not reproduce from the old habitat, (b) what it introduced that nobody chose, (c) which bond holds between the old and new rule under the seven tests. Swapping the operands: does the practice change? If yes, the bond is ordered, not conjunction. Does an outside field use the same four-way split? Historical institutionalism names layering, conversion, drift, displacement, and exhaustion (Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-22). Legacy-system engineering names backward compatibility and brownfield deployment (Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-22).
- **Refuter:** an institution that removes a habitat outright without substitution and without inheriting unchosen actors while still meeting its goal; or two arrangements over the same operands that pass all seven bond tests identically yet are treated as different cultural outcomes.

### C3: verification-frame derivation; checkable by anyone who separates evidence pools

- **Statement:** agreement has two valences. For a claim about what happened, agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is disqualifying; for a tradition as a convention, shared meaning is the product. The three domains get separate verdicts.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40, **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**; Fig 2 :41, **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**. Seat Map :163, **"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."** Fig 7 :35, **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 1 :22, **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
- **What to check:** identify which domain a given agreement belongs to. Are the agreeers drawing on separate experience, or on one literature? Are they settling a factual claim or maintaining a convention? Does an aggregate index hide a live per-practice signal?
- **Refuter:** agreement across independent, differently sourced instruments shown to be disqualifying for a factual claim about community change; or shared meaning inside a convention shown to be a defect of that convention's own operation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not which tradition or institution a community should keep. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that `w` has been measured or that any case's bond has been identified.
- Not that continuity of outward behaviour proves continuity of source (the metamerism limit in `05_what_shape_means.md` §4).
- Not that agreement among researchers or tradition-bearers settles a factual claim.
- Not `grounded`, and not a recommendation to preserve everything. The record never deletes; the reach can still fall.
- Not an independent corroboration from the author's essays. All eight share a pool with the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A lineage plus a discriminator, not an argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The instrument questions are:

1. Does `w` move with outcomes in a documented case, with separate-experience checks?
2. Under the seven bond tests, does swapping inherited and new change the practice? That names the bond.
3. When a part of a tradition loses authority, is a refusing term added while the record keeps the old term?
4. What did the new habitat fail to reproduce, and what did it introduce unchosen? These must be enumerated before the change.
5. Are the community and the researcher drawing on separate evidence, or one pool?
