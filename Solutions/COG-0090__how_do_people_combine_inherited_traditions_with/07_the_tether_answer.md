# The Tether's answer on combining inherited traditions with new information

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a field result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks how people keep an inherited tradition operative while taking in new information and new institutions. The apparent alternatives are combination and replacement. The residual is in effect terms: what composes inherited and new without deletion, what carries that composition across a generation or an institutional change, and what anchor would let an outside observer tell combination from a substitution that merely preserves the surface? Fig 9 :55-58 says the question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It has three loops and three grounds, kept separate.

## 2. The shape

1. **Combination is the default state of a belief, not a special achievement.** The Symbols table defines `b` as *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation"*, with `w` *"learnable from the outcomes of past weightings"*. The framework does not model tradition versus new information as two mutually exclusive states. It models one belief with both sources and a learnable weight.

2. **Replacement is not an operation in the record.** Fig 6 :42: **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."** Fig 5 :55: **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."** A false part of a tradition is refused by adding ¬ (Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**). The term stays; its authority to compose is removed. That is the figure-grounded meaning of change without erasure.

3. **New information and institutions are imports, not substitute parts.** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 6 :26: **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."** The community's `closure(Γ)` expands, so things previously unreachable become reachable. Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** But this holds only where closures differ: Fig 8 :49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

4. **What crosses generations is a method, not a recording.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** A tradition persists by making the next person's search cheaper, not by being replayed verbatim.

5. **An institution is a habitat, and the apparent replacement is usually a substitution.** Fig 11 :61: **"the seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** The two invisible costs are Fig 11 :68-69: **"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"**. Asking only whether the old ritual is still performed misses both.

6. **The arrangement, not the ingredient list, does the work.** Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** Both rules holding is `+`; a new step depending on an old one is `→`; a genuinely new practice is `⇒`; refusing a claim is `¬`, which is unary and not a bond. Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** The price is in two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**).

7. **Agreement has two valences and the verdicts must never be averaged.** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**. For a researcher claiming what happened, shared literature is disqualifying. For a tradition as a coordination device, shared meaning is the product. Averaging them produces the unanswerable question "is agreement good for continuity?" Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular tradition or institution should be kept. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that `w` has been measured, or that any particular case's bond has been identified. Both are `specified` bindings awaiting a ground.
- Not that continuity of outward behaviour proves continuity of source. A habited substitution can reproduce the surface (`05_what_shape_means.md` §4).
- Not that agreement among researchers, or among tradition-bearers, settles a factual claim (Fig 2 :35, :40).
- Not a recommendation that a community preserve everything. The record never deletes; the reach can still fall, and the missing instrument is named in §4.
- Not `grounded`. No community was observed in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A lineage plus a discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The corresponding questions are:

- **Weighting test:** does `w` actually move with outcomes in a documented case, or is the inherited/private mix fixed? A panel study with separate-experience checks would settle this.
- **Bond test:** applying the seven tests, does swapping inherited and new operands change the practice? If yes, the bond is ordered (`→` or `⇒`), not `+`. If no, `+` is a candidate.
- **Refusal test:** when a part of the tradition stops being authoritative, is a refusing term added while the old term remains in the record, or is the record actually erased? Fig 5 amendment and Fig 6 amendment predict the first.
- **Habitat test:** what did the new institution fail to reproduce, and what did it introduce unchosen? Fig 11 :68-69 makes these the two observable failure modes; they have to be enumerated before the change, not inferred from its surface.
- **Independence test:** are the researcher and the community drawing on separate evidence? Fig 2 :46: **"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it"**. If they share one pool, their agreement is one witness.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §4:** deletion from the record; fixed `w`; surplus at full overlap; replay-only persistence; collapsed agreement valence; habitat subtraction.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The eight essays warn that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question and that this output is a simulacrum if produced by an LLM. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The link here is "what would count as progress in combining rather than replacing". I have not found an instrument that discriminates combination from habitat substitution at the ground. The derivation stops there, records the lineage, and does not claim that the absence of an instrument is proof that none could exist (Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell a community which tradition to keep or which institution to adopt. It says that a belief is already formed from inherited and private history mixed by a learnable weight, so combination is the default; that the record never deletes, so a false part of a tradition is refused by adding a term that removes its standing to compose, not by erasing it; that new information is an import which expands what a community can reach without adding a primitive to the world; that what survives across generations is a method or a head start, not a recording; and that an institution substitutes a habitat, which is why change often looks like replacement while what was failed to reproduce and what was introduced remain unseen. The answer is a shape, not a name: identify the bond, keep the two costs separate, give the community, the institution, and the researcher each their own ground, and do not average their verdicts. What would settle it is a lineage and a discriminator, written down before the case is examined, not agreement among people who share a literature.
