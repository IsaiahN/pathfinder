# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. A two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold up.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about tradition and innovation, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about cultural change. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's Symbols table defines a belief as formed from a person's own history and from what they inherited, mixed by a weighting `w` that is learnable from past outcomes. So it does not model tradition and new information as two states where one must win. It models one belief with both sources in it.
>
> From that and four other figure lines, a shape follows:
>
> 1. New information or a new institution is an import from a frame whose closure differs. It expands what the community can reach; it does not add a primitive to the world and it deletes nothing.
> 2. A false part of a tradition is corrected by *adding* a term that refuses it. The old term stays in the record; what falls is its standing to be composed over.
> 3. What crosses generations is a method or a head start, not a recording of a past success.
> 4. Whether a change is combination or substitution of a habitat has to be settled by the ground, separately for the community and for the researcher. Agreement among people who share the same evidence pool cannot settle it.
>
> The claim is that "combine without replacing" is not one problem but a union of three: belief composition, transmission and habitat, and verification. Each has its own ground. Averaging them destroys the signal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming to know which tradition or which institution a community should keep. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming to have measured `w`, identified which of the seven bonds holds in a real community, or shown that any particular case is combination rather than substitution. This is a simulated derivation, not a field result, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. A demonstrated case where a tradition is struck from the record with no refusing term added and no habitat substituted, and the outcome is still correctly called combination.
> 2. Evidence that the inherited/private weighting `w` is fixed by architecture and cannot move with outcomes.
> 3. A genuine new reachable term produced by two frames with identical evidence pools, or an import that adds a primitive to the world.
> 4. A tradition that survives by verbatim replay alone, makes the next search no cheaper, and still solves its original problem.
> 5. Agreement across independent, differently sourced instruments shown to be disqualifying for a factual claim about what happened, or shared meaning inside a convention shown to be a defect of that convention.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1 question:** what would you count as a failure of this shape in your own field? Please answer that before looking at any sources. I am recording the verdict at this stage only.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three public links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** I can point you to the formula. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Distribution rules

- One recipient at a time. No list, no automation, no misleading subject line, opt-out line in every message.
- No personal emails of named individuals. Use the public institutional or opt-in routes in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
- Never pool a later "I agree" as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
- Record the stage-1 verdict with date and source before sending stage 2. Provenance is what keeps the exchange checkable (Fig 10 :62-65).
