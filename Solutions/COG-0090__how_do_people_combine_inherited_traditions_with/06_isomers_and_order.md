## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is "combination" a symmetric bond?

Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** Operators :15: **"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."** (test for `+`). Operators :33: **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."** (test for `→`). Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Applying the test: a practice where the inherited rule and the new rule both hold at once is `+` (conjunction). A practice where the new step can only be taken after the inherited step is `→` (sequence); swapping them makes a different thing. These are not variants of one another; they are different substances over the same two operands. Fig 12 :96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

So asking "do they combine?" is under-specified. The answerable question is "which bond holds here, and does swapping the operands change the practice?" `specified`, unmeasured for any case. Fig 12 + Operators, `single-source` (same operator set described twice).

### Q2: Is the new item a production or an addition?

Operators :39: **"is there a B before A fires? No."** (test for `⇒`). Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**. Fig 6 :25: **"An import adds no atom to the world."**

If the institution brings into being a practice that did not exist before, the bond is `⇒`. If it merely makes an existing practice reachable from outside the old closure, the import adds access, not a world atom (Fig 6 :26: **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."**). The distinction is not rhetorical: `⇒` commits to the non-existence of the second operand before the first fires, while import commits only to a change in the frame. A claim of pure novelty that turns out to be a rename fails the shadow test.

### Q3: What carries the correction: `−` or `¬`?

Operators :45: **"is the second operand's absence the point? Yes."** (test for `−`). Operators :57: **"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."** (test for `¬`). Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** Fig 5 :55: **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."**

Subtracting a tradition from a set of practices is `−`: it makes the second operand absent. Refusing a claim inside a tradition is `¬`: it makes the claim false while keeping the term in the record. The question's "without replacing" requires the second, not the first. Fig 12 :139: **"So the operators divide by arity, and this figure describes the binary ones."** Negation is unary and is not priced in `|bonds|`; its price is open (Operators :77-78). This is a real open item, not a hidden one.

### Q4: Is this a union or a partition, and what is the order?

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

The question is a union with overlap: the institution is both habitat and refusable term. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54). The dependency order, taken from Fig 3 and the formula:

1. Describe `R` in effect terms before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Refuse a false held term before minting a new one (formula step 2).
3. Import from a differing closure with source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).
4. Settle the first-order composition before promoting it (Fig 12 :104).
5. Measure the round-trip gap rather than assuming it (Fig 4 :30).

### Q5: What does the arrangement cost?

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** Fig 12 :127: **"and will not notice the shortfall."**

A tradition kept alive by a short rule (low bits) may take a whole field season to check (high actions); a long oral genealogy (high bits) may be refutable in one interview (low actions). Neither price predicts the other. Summing them into one "cost of change" figure is the named error, not the fix. This is the reason a single index like "degree of traditionality" cannot answer the question: it is a price in one currency masquerading as a verdict.

### Q6: Analogues from low-overlap fields

These are outside witnesses. They corroborate a shape; they do not decide it.

| field | source actually read | analogue | what it does not give |
|---|---|---|---|

| Engineering | Legacy system (Wikipedia, 2026-11-22): *"Brownfield deployment is an upgrade or addition to an existing software or network architecture that retains legacy components."*; *"Backward compatibility ... is a goal that software developers often include in their work."* | retaining a component while adding a new layer is an engineering discipline; NASA heritage systems are trusted where verification, validation, testing, and operational history exist | no account of the community whose goals the system serves |
| Cultural evolution | Cultural Evolution Society (2026-11-22): *"genes are inherited with very high fidelity, whereas cultural traits are often transformed and reconstructed as they are passed from person to person"*; dual-inheritance theory's guided variation and transmission biases | transmission is lossy and directed, matching Fig 4's membrane and the goal-as-comparison reading | no bond identification, no per-slot residual |
| Animal behaviour | Animal culture (Wikipedia, 2026-11-22): Whiten's *"alternative foraging techniques seeded in different groups of chimpanzees spread differentially...across two further groups with substantial fidelity"*; teaching, imitation, conformity | combination and transmission do not require a human institution; the habitat can be social alone | no measure of whether a term was refused or deleted |

**Overlap note.** The institutional and engineering sources are the lowest-overlap witnesses and agree on the four-way split (add, reinterpret, drift, substitute). Cultural evolution and animal behaviour share an evidence pool with each other at several points (both draw on transmission experiments), so their agreement is partly one pool. The author's eight essays share a pool with the figures and are not counted as independent corroboration.

## CONCLUSION

"Combine without replacing" decomposes into a bond question and an order question. The bond is `+` when both rules hold at once, `→` when the new step depends on the old, `⇒` when a genuinely new practice is produced, and `¬` (not `−`) when a part of the tradition is refused. The order is refuse, import, settle, promote, with the residual described before any frame is chosen. The union must be unbundled before search, and the two currencies of cost must be held apart. All of this is a shape. None of it identifies a real community's bond.

This derivation fails if any of the following is established: (1) two cultural arrangements over the same operands behave identically under all seven bond tests and yet are correctly treated as different outcomes; (2) a new institution produces a practice that plainly existed before it arrived, yet the bond must still be called `⇒`; (3) a tradition can be corrected only by literal erasure from the community's record; or (4) an instrument discriminates combination from habitat substitution from outward behaviour alone, with no lineage. If any fires, the bond table reading or the refusal reading is narrowed accordingly.
