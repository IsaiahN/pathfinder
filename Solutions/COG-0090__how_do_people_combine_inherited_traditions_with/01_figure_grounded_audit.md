## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question offers two outcomes and asks how the first is achieved without the second:

- **Combination:** inherited tradition and new information or institution are both operative after the encounter.
- **Replacement:** one gives way to the other, so the tradition is no longer a live determinant of what people do.

Stated as a residual `R`: a community holds a composed, settled structure (a tradition) and encounters material from a frame whose closure differs (new information, a new institution). The gap is not "which one wins". The gap is: **what operation keeps both compositions reachable, and what anchor would let an outside observer tell that this happened rather than a substitution wearing the language of continuity?** The residual is always a slice: Fig 1 :24 says *"Everything else either produces a residual or consumes the library"* and the Symbols table defines `R` as *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."* What a given community cannot yet perceive about its own change is part of `R`, not noise outside it.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** Before searching, I write down what an answer would look like so it can accrue a residual like anything else:

> An answer must name (a) a composition operation over inherited and new material that does not delete either operand, (b) a mechanism by which that composition survives a change of generation or institution, and (c) an anchor that discriminates combination from replacement in a specific case. It must also state which of these is a figure-derived shape and which is a binding from a world frame.

This binding is the refusable term. If a derivation cannot produce (a), (b), and (c), it has not answered the question, and that is a verdict, not a silence. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**First move: refuse the framing where it is false.** The question says "without simply replacing one with the other", which treats replacement as an available operation. In the figures **the record never deletes**. Fig 6 :42 says *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."* A correction adds a refusal; it does not strike a term. So a literal replacement of a held tradition is not an operation the framework contains. What people *call* replacement must be re-censused into three different things: an added refusal that narrows reach (Fig 5 amendment), a substitution of habitat (Fig 11 :66), or a convention that has started deciding an outcome (Fig 10 :47). This is a re-census of the question, not a dodge of it.

## 2. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least two sources (an inherited tradition, new information or a new institution), and a third term that is often smuggled in as a source but is not one: the institution acts as a habitat or seat (Fig 11), not as another belief source. So `R` is two-source at the cognitive scale and three-domain at the social scale. This is a union, not a single two-body problem.
- **Symmetry:** combination is *not* generally symmetric. Inherit-and-new is ordered: the tradition is already settled and its settlement is what makes the import cheap (Fig 4 :23-24, Fig 12 :104). Under a symmetric bond `+`, order would carry no information (Operators :15). The question's symmetry is therefore a fact to be determined per case, not assumed.
- **Scale:** first order within a person or a single practice; second order across generations or institutions, where a settled composition becomes an operand (Fig 12 :103-104). A failure to separate these scales is the main source of the "replacement" illusion.

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**Domain 1, the individual cognitive frame.**
Actors: a person, belief `b`, weighting `w`, library `Γ`, and the incoming information as an import from a differing closure. Currency: residual `R` and settlement against what pays. Anchor: whatever actually happens when the person acts. Nameable? Yes, an observable practice or outcome. This is a domain. The most direct figure line in the whole set is in the Symbols table: `b` is *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."* Combination is the default state of a belief; replacement would require the architecture to delete a source, which it does not do.

**Domain 2, the intergenerational and institutional frame.**
Actors: many agents, a shared library, the membrane, the institution as habitat or seat. Currency: methods carried up, head starts sent down, actors introduced into a habitat. Anchor: the community's continued contact with what the tradition was for, plus community recognition. Nameable? Yes. UNESCO's 2003 Convention definition (opened 2026-11-22) says heritage is recognized *"by the communities, groups or individuals that create, maintain and transmit it"*, and that it *"have evolved in response to their environments"*. That is a nameable anchor for whether continuity is real. This is a domain, and it is a different one from Domain 1.

**Domain 3, the research and verification frame.**
Actors: cultural innovation and tradition researchers, expert readers, instruments. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never the agreement. Nameable? Yes, separate experience and an instrument that can discriminate. In this *simulated* run all three domains draw on one evidence pool (the same figures, the same essays, the same web pages), so this domain's independence is zero. Its agreement is one pool talking, not a second witness.

**Overlap as compound.** An institution is both a habitat (Domain 2) and a held term that can be refused (Domain 1 at population scale). That overlap is not a reason to average the verdicts. Per Fig 9 :54-58: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*, because *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The institution-as-habitat and the institution-as-refusable-term get separate loops.

### 2.3 Partition or union

This is a **union compound with an overlap**, not a partition and not one hard question. Domain 1 is about belief composition; Domain 2 is about transmission and habitat; Domain 3 is about who may settle a claim. A single lever (say, "teach the old and the new together") can clear the region where only Domain 1 holds and leave the Domain 2 intersection standing, because the habitat condition still binds. Fig 9 :55 puts it directly: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

### 2.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Describe `R` in effect terms before naming any frame (Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).
2. Check the vocabulary link: does the community have terms for what the new institution affords? Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
3. Refuse before mint: a false held term must be refused before a new one is offered (formula step 2).
4. Settle each first-order composition before promoting it to a second-order operand (Fig 12 :104).
5. Only then compose across domains, and bracket the transform (Fig 4 :30).

Fig 3 :18: *"Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."* The break in a real community has to be measured, not assumed from this document.

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

Every quoted string below is verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`. Mode is stated on each substantive claim. Refuters are written per finding so a checker can fail them separately.

### F1: The record never deletes; "replacement" has to be re-censused (decisive for the framing)

**Mode: `general` (structural); `specified` when applied to a named community.**

Fig 6 :42: **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."** Fig 6 :42: **"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."** Fig 5 :55: **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."** Fig 12 :140: **"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."** Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."**

A tradition can therefore lose its authority to compose new practice without being erased from the record. That is the figure-grounded meaning of "change without replacement": the record keeps the old term; a refusing term is added; the reach narrows. What looks like replacement is usually one of three different mechanisms: an added refusal, a substituted habitat (F8), or a convention deciding an outcome (F7).

**Refuter:** a documented case in which a community's own record of the tradition is destroyed or never represented, with no refusing term and no substituted habitat, and in which the observers still correctly describe the result as combination rather than loss. That would break the claim that deletion is unavailable.

**Status: `triangulated`** across the Fig 5 amendment (mint pipeline), the Fig 6 amendment (closure/reach), the Operators table (negation), and Fig 12 :140 (arity of operators). These are different figures with low conceptual overlap: a pipeline, a closure law, an operator table, and a bond grammar. The agreement is not one pool restated four times.

### F2: Belief already mixes inherited and new, with a learnable weight

**Mode: `general` for the architecture; `specified`, unmeasured for any person.**

Symbols table :20-21: **"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."** Symbols :24-26: **"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."** Symbols :25-26: **"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."**

This bears directly on the question. The framework does not model "tradition versus new information" as a choice between two mutually exclusive states. It models a single belief formed from both, with a weighting that is learnable from outcomes. So the default, in this model, is combination. The interesting empirical question is not whether combination happens but what moves `w`, and whether a new institution changes `w` without erasing the inherited prior.

**Refuter:** evidence that `w` is fixed by architecture and cannot change with outcomes, or that belief is formed from exactly one of history or inheritance with no mixture term. That would remove the framework's own mechanism for this question.

**Status: `single-source`** in the figures (the Symbols table). It is corroborated in the world by dual-inheritance theory (Boyd and Richerson, via Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-22: *"Genes and culture (DIT suggests) continually interact in a feedback loop"*), but that is a world witness, not a second figure.

### F3: New information is an import: it expands access, not the world's primitives

**Mode: `general`, with a `specified` prediction that closures must differ.**

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 6 :25: **"An import adds no atom to the world."** Fig 6 :26: **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."** Fig 6 :20: **"closure expands; φ* now reachable"**. Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

The new information or institution is not a replacement part dropped into an old machine. It is contact with a frame whose closure differs, and the effect of the contact is that the community's `closure(Γ)` expands: things were unreachable before and are reachable now. Nothing was deleted, and the world did not acquire a new primitive. Cultural Evolution Society (opened 2026-11-22) describes the same shape from inside the field: *"Or migration and mutation can bring new cultural traits into a population, just as it brings in new genes."*

**Refuter:** a documented combination in which the community's reachable set did not expand at all (so nothing was imported), or one in which the new item genuinely added a primitive to the world rather than access to one. Either breaks the import reading.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 6 (closure mechanics) and Fig 8 (union surplus conditions), with Fig 13 :115 (*"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*) as a low-overlap third. Good triangulation: closure law, union law, and minimum-set law answer different questions.

### F4: Surplus exists only where closures differ; high overlap is one frame with two names

**Mode: `general`.**

Fig 8 :48-49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** Fig 8 :47: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

This is the filter that decides whether a claimed "combination" is real. If the new institution draws on the same evidence pool as the tradition, the union has no surplus and the claimed synthesis is a rename. It also governs the research domain: a group of researchers who all learned the same canon cannot corroborate a claim about what happened by agreeing.

**Refuter:** a case in which two frames with demonstrably identical evidence pools produce a genuine new atom that neither held. That would falsify the idempotence reading.

**Status: `single-source`** (Fig 8), triangulated only in the sense that Fig 2 :40 restates the same arithmetic for the verdict domain. I keep it `single-source` because the overlap between Fig 8 and Fig 2 is high.

### F5: What is combined is a bond, not an ingredient list; the arrangement is priced

**Mode: `general`; `specified` for which bond holds in a case.**

Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

"Tradition plus new information" is not an answer; `+` is only one of seven bonds and is symmetric (Operators :15: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*). A new institution that could not exist before the old one is a production `⇒` (*"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :77). An ordered practice where the new step needs the old one is a sequence `→` (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72). Keeping a part that survives its own use is a catalyst (Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*). The answer to "how do they combine" is a bond identification plus its price, and the price is in two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*).

**Refuter:** a case in which the operators do not discriminate, meaning two different arrangements over the same operands behave identically under all seven tests and yet are treated as different cultural outcomes. That would break the priced-bond claim.

**Status: `single-source`** (Fig 12 plus the Operators table, which is the same operator set described twice, so overlap is high).

### F6: Across generations the tradition travels as a method or head start, never as a recording

**Mode: `general`; `specified` that a particular case transmits method rather than replay.**

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."** Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

This is the persistence mechanism the question asks for. A tradition survives not by being copied verbatim but by making the next person's search cheaper. The pedagogic analogue in the world sources is explicit: the tacit-knowledge essay (opened 2026-11-22) says *"what passes down is ways of doing things and head starts, never the experience itself"*; UNESCO's heritage page (opened 2026-11-22) says the importance of heritage is *"the wealth of knowledge and skills that is transmitted through it from one generation to the next."*

**Refuter:** a tradition that persists solely by verbatim replay, with no reapplication and no cheaper search for the next generation, and that nonetheless continues to solve the problem it originally solved. That would falsify the method-only crossing rule for this domain.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 4 (membrane direction), Fig 12 :103-104 (a settled molecule becomes an atom), and Fig 7 :36 (*"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced"*). Low overlap: a boundary rule, a recursion rule, and a selection rule.

### F7: Agreement has two valences; the research anchor and the convention anchor must be kept separate

**Mode: `general`; the per-domain verdicts are `specified` and must never be averaged.**

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**. Seat Map :163: **"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**.

This is the finding most easily missed and the one that makes the question answerable. In the **research domain**, agreement among researchers who share a literature is disqualifying for a claim about what happened. In the **tradition domain considered as a convention**, shared meaning is the product: a tradition *is* a coordination device, so high internal agreement is not corruption. The two verdicts are not averaged. If you average them, you get the false question "is agreement good for cultural continuity?", which has no answer because it conflates two quantities. Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

**Refuter:** a case where agreement among independent instruments on a factual claim about community change is disqualifying, or a case where shared meaning inside a convention is a defect in the convention's own operation. Either would collapse the two-valence rule.

**Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (two clauses about different quantities), Fig 7 (room agreement is variation), and Fig 10 (a convention deciding an outcome). The three figures govern different domains: evidence, room selection, and the ground-maintainer's authoring rule.

### F8: An institution is a habitat: introduction is available, exclusion is not

**Mode: `general` for the asymmetry; `specified` for the effects of a named institution.**

Fig 11 :61: **"the seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :68-69: **"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"**. Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

This explains why combination often *looks* like replacement. When a new institution arrives, it substitutes a habitat. Two things then become invisible until the goal fails: what the new habitat failed to reproduce from the old one, and what it unintentionally introduced. That is not deletion of a tradition; it is a substitution whose losses are not yet measured. Historical institutionalism (opened 2026-11-22) names the same family of moves from a completely different field: *"Layering: grafting new rules onto old rules"*, *"Conversion: old rules are reinterpreted and redirected to apply to new goals, functions and purposes"*, *"Drift: old rules fail to apply to situations that they were intended for because of changing social conditions"*, and *"Displacement: when relevant actors leave existing institutions and go to new or alternative institutions"*. Layering and conversion are combination; displacement is the habitat substitution; drift is the unseen failure to reproduce.

**Refuter:** an institution that can subtract a habitat outright, without substitution and without inheriting unchosen actors, while still satisfying its goal. That would break the asymmetry law for this domain.

**Status: `single-source`** in the figures (Fig 11), with a strong low-overlap world corroboration from institutional research. I do not upgrade the figure verdict on that basis.

### F9: Describe the residual before choosing the frame; union compounds must be unbundled

**Mode: `general`.**

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 9 :54-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Applied here: if a researcher begins with "tradition versus modernity", any field will seem to fit. If they begin with the effect (what stopped being reachable, what was introduced, what failed to be reproduced), the relevant frame is selected by the residual. The Library of Babel essay (opened 2026-11-22) states the same limit in the vocabulary of search: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Refuter:** a case in which naming the frame first and the residual second produces the same narrowed shape, with no apparent-fit error. That would weaken the residual-first rule for this domain.

**Status: `single-source`** (Fig 9), with world corroboration kept secondary.

### F10: Verdicts stay per domain and per slot; never average across them

**Mode: `general`.**

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :27: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Fig 1 :25-26: **"coverage, and compression achieved"** and **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** are *"frame-internal"* (Fig 1 :29).

A single index like "how traditional is this community now?" averages away the live signal. The three domains of §2 get three separate verdicts. Counting how many institutions were added, how many rituals preserved, or how many researchers agree counts frame-internal quantities; none of those settles anything.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated sufficient statistic for cultural replacement that aggregates across practices without losing the per-practice signal. I know of none; if one exists, F10 is wrong.

**Status: `single-source`** (Fig 1).

### F11: The middle link is where the derivation must stop if no instrument exists

**Mode: `general`; `specified` that this derivation has not found the instrument.**

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :29-30: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The middle link here is "what would count as progress in combining rather than replacing". Ethnography and historical records can describe practices; laboratory experiments can test transmission biases (dual-inheritance theory, opened 2026-11-22, notes that *"there have been little ethnographic fieldwork designed to explicitly test these hypotheses"*). But an instrument that discriminates *combination from substitution of habitat* in a live community is not something this simulated derivation has. Saying so is the honest stop, not a gap in the prose.

**Refuter:** produce a validated, published instrument that discriminates addition-of-a-refusal from substitution-of-habitat in a specific community, with separate-experience checks. That would move this finding from `specified` toward `grounded` and would be a result, not a contradiction.

**Status: `single-source`** (Fig 3).

### F11b: The source of a reading is not determined by the reading

**Mode: `general`.**

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The essay's spectrometer case shows that a reading of "green" cannot say whether the source was one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or something not yet discovered. Applied here: an observer who sees a tradition still being performed cannot tell from that reading whether the source is continuity, a refusal-added-in-place, a substituted habitat that reproduced the surface, or a convention that decided the outcome. The reading underdetermines the source. Fig 6 :27: **"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."**

**Refuter:** an instrument that reads a community's continuity directly and discriminates all four sources without additional lineage. That would dissolve the metamerism problem for this domain.

**Status: `single-source`** for the figure line, with the world essay as the instrument argument. The two are kept distinct.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. Seat Map :287: **"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."** The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is established:

1. **Deletion exists in the record.** A community's tradition is demonstrably struck from the record with no refusing term added and no habitat substituted, and the result is still correctly described as combination. (Breaks F1.)
2. **`w` is not learnable.** The weighting between inherited prior and private history is fixed by architecture and does not move with outcomes. (Breaks F2.)
3. **No closure difference, yet surplus.** Two frames with demonstrably identical evidence pools produce a genuine new reachable term, or an import adds a primitive to the world. (Breaks F3, F4.)
4. **Replay only.** A tradition persists solely by verbatim replay, makes the next search no cheaper, and still solves its original problem. (Breaks F6.)
5. **Valence collapse.** Agreement across independent, differently sourced instruments is disqualifying for a factual claim about what happened, or shared meaning inside a convention is a defect of that convention. (Breaks F7.)
6. **Habitat subtraction.** An institution removes a habitat outright without substitution and without inheriting unchosen actors, while meeting its goal. (Breaks F8.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the re-census, not with an answer: the question is a union compound, and "replace" is not an available operation in the record (F1, F9).
2. State the mode on every claim. An unmeasured weighting is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict (Seat Map :281: *"An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."*).
3. Keep the three verdicts separate. Never average the research anchor with the convention anchor (F7, F10).
4. Ask for the bond, not the ingredient list: which of `+`, `→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛` actually holds, and what does it cost in bits and actions? (F5).
5. Ask for the failure to reproduce and the unchosen introduction, not for a narrative of continuity (F8).
6. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :23-25: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The generator here is the shape plus the refuter.
7. Do not rank the people you ask by receptiveness. Seat Map :163; Fig 2 :40.

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives. People combine inherited traditions with new information by importing from a frame whose closure differs, by weighting inherited and private history through a learnable `w`, by carrying the result forward as a method rather than a recording, and by refusing false parts additively while keeping them in the record. What is called replacement is usually a substituted habitat or a convention that has begun to decide an outcome. The arrangement, not the list of ingredients, is what does the work, and it must be identified bond by bond. The verdicts for the cognitive, institutional, and research domains stay separate and are never averaged.

What does not survive: any claim that this derivation has measured `w`, identified the bond in a specific community, or discriminated combination from substitution at the ground. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Nothing here settles which tradition anyone should keep.
