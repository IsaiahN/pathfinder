# Supporting derivation: order of operations, reachability order, and the noise/signal isomer

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground
reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit (`01_figure_grounded_audit.md`) establishes that a receiver cannot decide source
from residual magnitude. Three further questions are owed: (1) in what order must the operations
run, (2) what order governs the search when no term fits, and (3) is "same reading, different
source" an isomer claim under Fig 12, or only a loose analogy?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: per-slot residual R, observation o', belief b, action a, candidate term φ, criterion,
channel state. Bonds held: the seven, including → sequence, ⋛ comparison, − subtraction, and the
unary operator ¬ negation. No new primitive is available without import. Noise is not an atom: it

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - ROUTE before MINT: a noise verdict is a refusal, not a new term

Formula step 2 routes R into five bins: TRANSFERRED, NOVEL, BROKEN rebinding, BROKEN mechanism,
and REFUTED. The first four sort by what is **missing**; the fifth sorts by what is **wrong**.
Fig 5 :50-53 names the fifth kind: *"the gap has a fifth kind, and the pipeline has no branch for
it... refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."*

This matters for detection. When a receiver decides a candidate source is wrong, what it owes is
a **refusal** of that candidate, not a new detector mechanism. Operators table :58: *"a refutation
is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A receiver whose
vocabulary holds only ingredients and comparisons can report a detection and cannot report that a
specific source hypothesis is false. It will keep accumulating candidate sources without ever
crossing one off.

Order: route the residual first (signal-like structure? channel failure? wrong source?), then mint
or refuse. Minting before routing produces a detector for a hypothesis that has not been
classified. This is the INF-0027 instance of the general rule that a refusal is out of order if it
is applied after a mint (the retraction pattern recorded in the QMGR exemplar, here avoided).

### Q2 - The order of the three reachability questions

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)
> **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."** (Fig 6 :34)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)

When no term in Γ closes the residual, Formula step 7 gives three questions in a fixed order:

1. **SEARCHED**, is it in here and unfound? Same closure, same wall, more of the space actually
   visited. Asked first because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer. Distribute
   the search and keep searchers independent (Fig 6 :53).
2. **INWARD**, is the representation adequate to hold it? Extend an instrument that already
   returns something failing to resolve. For detection: refine a readout that is already showing
   an unresolved anomaly, not invent a sensor from a description.
3. **OUTWARD**, does another frame hold it? Import a term from a differing closure, with source
   gate and shadow test, debited against independence. Characterize the residual in effect terms
   first (Fig 9 :27-28).

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and are never read against one another. Underivable in principle
sends you outward; unreachable at this budget sends you to more, independent search. Importing
against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 6 :51-52).

**Order falsifier:** a case where INWARD succeeds by importing, or where OUTWARD succeeds merely by
more search inside the same closure, with provenance showing the closure did not change. That
would collapse the three questions into one and falsify the ordering.

### Q3 - Is "same reading, different source" an isomer claim?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Two readings with the same per-slot residual vector are not automatically the same thing. If one
is bonded to a source-confirming measurement (C3 of the primary census) and the other is not, they
are the same atoms under a different bond, hence different substances in the figure's sense. The
difference is invisible in the residual vector, which is exactly why magnitude alone cannot
discriminate (F1), and decisive in behaviour: one is acted on as a signal, the other is discarded.

So the isomer reading is **generated by the figure's own definition**, not borrowed from chemistry
as decoration. It is a `specified` claim about readings, not a `grounded` one, because no
source-confirming measurement is taken here.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two readings with identical per-slot residual vectors,
identical channel states and identical provenance can be one signal and one noise with no
difference in bond to the ground, the isomer claim fails and the readings are duplicates. A
related failure: if a single reading uniquely determined its source across a closed source set, the
bond would be recoverable from the operands and Fig 12 :94 would not apply.

### Q4 - Which bonds appear in the final answer, and why the vocabulary must include negation

- `→` sequence: a transient is defined by *when* it occurs (Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*).
- `⋛` comparison: the criterion is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87). It prices the miss/false-alarm trade but does not name a source.
- `−` subtraction: noise is what the crossing removed, *"A with B removed or absent"* (Fig 12 :81). Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."*
- `¬` negation: refusing a candidate source is *"this does not hold"* (Operators table :55). It is not a bond; it acts on one operand, so its price and symmetry question are different (Operators table amendment :70-78).

A vocabulary holding only the first three can describe a detection, a threshold and a loss, and
cannot say that a candidate source is false. That is the same structural gap the seat map names:
*a seat that cannot write it can report that a claim stopped paying and cannot report that it is
wrong.* The answer packet therefore includes a refusal condition explicitly (refuter 3 in
`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4).

## CONCLUSION

The order is ROUTE, then MINT or REFUSE; SEARCHED, then one of INWARD or OUTWARD; and the
criterion last as a budget filter. The noise/signal distinction is a genuine isomer claim under
Fig 12 :117 when the residual vector is held fixed and the bond to the ground differs. Its
falsifier is stated above and has not fired. No new primitive is needed for any of these moves,
and no import that moves the wall has been asserted.
