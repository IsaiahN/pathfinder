## 0. The residual under audit

> Can institutions enforce rules consistently while allowing discretion for unusual cases?

**In effect terms.** An institution holds a general rule R and must decide cases. R predicts a ruling for each case. The residual R_s is the gap between that prediction and what the case actually requires, measured **per case**, not per institution. The unusual case is not a hard instance of the ordinary case. It is a case that falls under two conditions at once: the general rule's condition and an exceptional condition. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

**Option A (consistency as uniformity).** Remove discretion so R applies identically to every case. Leads to: a rule no real occupant could meet (Fig 10 :54); a convention that decides an outcome and so stops being a convention (Fig 2 :43); the unusual case swallowed by the general condition; a vocabulary that can say what a case contains but cannot say the rule is wrong for it (Operators table :73); and aggregate compliance figures that hide the live slot (Fig 1 :22).

**Option B (consistency as an anchor plus a written refusal).** Keep the rule non-updating, and build discretion inside the rule set as a negation with provenance, checked per case by a frame that did not author the rule. Leads to: the same rule answer for the same case regardless of who asks; a checkable record of each exception; and a channel to the ground that can be lost and restored rather than a verdict that is merely asserted.

**The sought-for shape, written down before searching.** A shape with four parts: (i) a non-updating anchor, (ii) a negation operator that can refuse a rule in a case, (iii) a per-case residual plus a dated ruling record, (iv) an unauthored and rotated checker. Bind it as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM") so it can fail against the figures rather than silently steering the search.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two conditions (general rule condition plus exceptional condition), so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Symmetry: asymmetric. A ruling is a sequence that produces a status (→ and ⇒), not a symmetric join; swapping the rule and the exception changes the thing. Scale: first order at the case, second order at the institution's rule set. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

- **Domain 1, case application.** Actors: the case, the official, the rule. Currency: rulings. Anchor: the case's actual facts and the outcome the rule is about, a record or measurement that does not move when the official pushes on it. Legitimate because the question is constitutively about whether the rule fits the case. If the "ground" is defined as what the institution says, it updates on the frame and is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor").

- **Domain 2, rule formation.** Actors: the institution, rule designers, the library Γ of rules and precedents. Currency: norms, conventions, precedents. Anchor: the social fact the rule regulates. Partition question: which norms are rules and which are conventions, and what happens when a convention decides an outcome (Fig 2 :43).

- **Domain 3, discretion oversight.** Actors: auditor, review frame, the person exercising discretion. Currency: provenance records, refusals, residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement; the checker must be outside the frame it grades (Fig 10 :42). In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero and its agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

- **Domain 4, the unusual case compound.** Actors: the case in two conditions, the general rule, the exception. Currency: the residual neither rule-only nor discretion-only clears. Anchor: the ground for the compound, not for either condition alone. This is a **union** sitting at the intersection of Domains 1 and 2, with its own loop (Fig 9 :57-58).

**Partition or union.** Domains 1, 2 and 3 are partitions of the institution's loop. Domain 4 is a union compound at their intersection and must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again").

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** (1) Establish that the channel to the case facts is reachable (Fig 10 :29). (2) Keep the rule a convention that does not decide the outcome (Fig 2 :43). (3) Equip the vocabulary with a refusal operator, otherwise step 2 has nothing to refuse with (Operators table :57). (4) Record each ruling with provenance (Fig 10 :62). (5) Select the checker from an unauthored pool and rotate it (Fig 10 :73, :81). (6) Measure residuals per case, not in aggregate (Fig 1 :19). A break between (2) and (3) is decisive: effort spent on consistency cannot rescue a missing exception operator, because a step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed (Fig 3 :22: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can").

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Zero discretion is an idealisation no real occupant could meet (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 10 :53: "And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."
Fig 10 :54: "A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."
Fig 10 :56: "an idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support".

An institution that tries to be consistent by removing all discretion has stated a rule no real occupant can meet. The cost is not only that the rule describes nobody. It licenses inferences no real position would support, and those inferences will be drawn by whoever finds them useful. Here that inference is: "the official did not apply the rule, therefore the official was arbitrary." With no exception operator, every justified refusal is indistinguishable from arbitrariness.

The figure itself records the same correction in its own case. Fig 10 :57: "Below is the one this figure had." Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist." The impossible absolute ("no stake") was replaced by a satisfiable one: declare the stake (Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."). The institutional analogue is the answer to Option A: do not demand zero discretion, require a declared stake and a structured exception.

Triangulated: Fig 10 (ground maintainer) and the Seat Map (:169: "And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong.") with low overlap between the figure's ruling-provenance context and the seat map's failure-genus context. World corroboration: SEP *The Rule of Law* lists "practicable" among the eight desiderata for governing through enacted rules and notes that discretion is "ineliminable in the modern administrative state" (Davis 1969, as reported there).

**Refuter:** show a real institution where a rule with no exception mechanism meets every case it was designed for, with the ground paying for each ruling. If that exists, F1 fails at the ground.

### F2 - A rule that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention (triangulated)

Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Consistency is not the rule deciding every outcome. If the rule decides the outcome, it has crossed from a shared meaning (a convention) into a verdict, and a verdict needs a ground. Writing the rule down does not ground it. The consistency worth keeping is the rule ranging against a ground that does not change, not the rule replacing the ground.

World corroboration: SEP *The Rule of Law* reports the formal desideratum of "congruence between official action and declared rule" and the demand that "people in positions of authority should exercise their power within a constraining framework of well-established public norms rather than in an arbitrary, ad hoc, or purely discretionary manner". It also reports the opposing view that discretion's rule-of-law worries "can be resolved by ensuring official discretions are properly authorized and structured through legislation". Both sides accept that the rule alone cannot be the ground.

**Refuter:** show a convention that decides an outcome and nevertheless remains a convention by the figures' own test (it makes no claim about the world). If a decision-producing rule makes no claim about the world, F2 fails.

### F3 - Discretion is the negation operator a rule vocabulary must contain (triangulated)

Operators table :55: "this does not hold"
Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"
Operators table :73: "A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."
Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
Fig 5 :56: "Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain."
Fig 12 :113: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

The seven bonds can compose rules, sequences and thresholds. None of them can say that a held rule is wrong for this case. Subtraction can say what is absent from an arrangement; it cannot say the arrangement is false. So a rule set with only the seven bonds can accumulate rules and exceptions-by-listing forever without ever carrying a correction as a correction. Discretion, in figure terms, is the unary operator ¬ applied to a rule in a case: "this rule does not hold here". It is not a new rule and it is not a missing operand. Any institution that grants discretion only as an unwritten favour has put the refusal outside the rule set, where it cannot be inherited, checked or carried to the next case.

This is the link that was missing between the two halves of the question. Consistency without ¬ is accumulation. Discretion without a record is an unwritten exception. Both fail the same structural test.

Triangulated: Operators table (unary amendment), Fig 5 (fifth bin) and Fig 12 (bond versus unary operator), three passages with different subjects and low overlap. World corroboration: SEP *Non-monotonic Logic* defines the exact capability: "Reasoners draw conclusions in a defeasible manner when they retain the right to retract these inferences upon the acquisition of further information." Its Tweety example is the unusual case: "Although we infer that Tweety flies on the basis of the information that Tweety is a bird and the background knowledge that birds usually fly, we have good reasons to retract this inference when learning that Tweety is a penguin or a kiwi." It even names the legal variant: "In the context of legal reasoning, we may have the principles lex superior resp. lex posterior, according to which the higher-ranked, respectively, the later issued law dominates."

**Refuter:** demonstrate a rule system that carries a correct refusal of one of its own held rules without any negation or equivalent retraction operator, and whose refusal can be inherited by a later case. If it can, F3 fails.

### F4 - Consistency is a non-updating anchor, not identical outcomes (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

If the rule changes its answer because the applicant pushes, the rule has become another frame and the arrangement is the mutual-update collapse (Fig 2 :34). The consistent institution is the one whose rule ranges against a ground separately for each case, not the one whose rule gives the same answer by never listening. This separates consistency from rigidity: a rule can refuse itself in a case (F3) without updating on the case's opinion of itself.

World corroboration: SEP *The Rule of Law* contrasts "standing Laws, promulgated and known to the People" with "extemporary Arbitrary Decrees" (Locke 1689, as reported) and identifies predictability as the central worry: "the central worry was unpredictability". The Hayek formulation it quotes is "government in all its forms is bound by rules fixed and announced before-hand".

**Refuter:** a measured case in which a rule that updates on the pushed-on frame beats a non-updating rule on the very residual the rule is about, with the ground paying for the difference. Then the anchor condition is wrong here and F4 fails.

### F5 - Measure per case, not per institution (single-source on the figure, corroborated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

An aggregate compliance rate of 99 percent is exactly the form in which the one unusual case disappears. The slot here is the case. A live exception is a slot with positive residual that must remain visible. Consistency measured institution-wide is a frame-internal quantity; it cannot tell you where to look. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Compliance statistics are filters for budget allocation. They are not witnesses for the ruling.

World corroboration: the World Justice Project's Rule of Law Index assesses "Regulatory Enforcement" among eight categories using "more than 214,000 household and expert surveys", that is, case-level and experience-level readings rather than a single institutional average. SEP *The Rule of Law* lists "a right to hear reasons from the tribunal when it reaches its decision, which are responsive to the evidence and arguments presented before it" as a procedural demand, which is a per-case instrument.

**Refuter:** show that an institution-wide aggregate settles a particular case's ruling correctly, including the unusual case, without any per-case reading. If it does, F5 fails.

### F6 - The unusual case is a union compound, not a harder ordinary case (triangulated)

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
Fig 9 :58: "it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

The case that needs discretion is not at the tail of the ordinary-case distribution. It is in the intersection of two conditions. Treating it as a harder ordinary case is the wrong census, and no amount of effort on the general rule will clear it: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56). The compound needs its own loop and its own ground.

World corroboration: SEP *Non-monotonic Logic*: "we may learn that X is an exceptional A with respect to being a B, and in view of this we retract our previous inference." Its Specificity Principle result, "an inference with a more specific antecedent overrides a conflicting defeasible inference with a less specific antecedent", is the formal shape of the exception. The *Library of Babel* essay gives the same instruction for the search: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

**Refuter:** show a case in two conditions cleared correctly, with the ground paying, by treating it only as a harder case of one condition and never opening a separate loop for the intersection. Then F6 fails.

### F7 - Each exercise of discretion needs provenance, not appeal (triangulated)

Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."
Fig 10 :63: "The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."
Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that"
Fig 10 :66: "A perfectly provenanced wrong ruling is an unimpeachable record of nothing, and it is still to be corrected."

The record is not so the ruling can be appealed, but so a later reader can "locate the error rather than only feel it" (Fig 10 :64). This is the honest form of discretion: the exception is written down with the residual it answered, the reasoning and the date, and it remains wrong if the ground says so however clean the record. Provenance is not a licence. It is the difference between an arrangement that can be corrected and one that can carry the correction.

World corroboration: SEP *The Rule of Law* procedural list includes "a right to hear reasons from the tribunal when it reaches its decision, which are responsive to the evidence and arguments presented before it". Also "a government writing its own rules for how to govern is not exactly what the ideal of the rule of law has in mind": self-authored reasons without an external ground are the failure.

**Refuter:** a ruling whose provenance is shown to establish correctness without any ground reading. If that succeeds, F7 fails.

### F8 - Who exercises discretion must come from an unauthored pool and be rotated (triangulated)

Fig 10 :71: "The room composition is a function of the question."
Fig 10 :72: "A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."
Fig 10 :73: "The residual selects the members, but only from what is available."
Fig 10 :75: "a set assembled before the question existed"
Fig 10 :81: "frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated"
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

A review panel chosen by the person whose ruling is being reviewed is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned. The unauthored mechanisms the figure allows are: a lottery, everyone who qualifies, a set assembled before the question existed, or the habitat itself enumerated (Fig 10 :76-79). Independence decays with use, so a reviewer who has been calibrated against for months is more replaceable, not less (Fig 10 :80). A standing committee that reviews the same official's exceptions indefinitely has correlated itself with the thing it reviews, and its agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40).

World corroboration: SEP *The Rule of Law* requires "a hearing by an impartial and independent tribunal" and treats "the independence of the judiciary" as a rule-of-law requirement. SEP *Legal Positivism* notes that Hart's rule of recognition "exists only because it is practiced by officials" and that this division of labour sets up "the possibility of law becoming remote from the life of a society", which is the authored-room failure in institutional form.

**Refuter:** show a review set chosen by the party being reviewed whose disagreement rate with that party stays at its initial value over repeated use, with ground readings to confirm it. Then F8's rotation claim fails.

### F9 - Repair order: searched, inward, outward, in that order (single-source from the formula, figure-corroborated)

Formula step 7 asks SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."

When a rule fails on a case, the first question is whether the exception is already in the library and unfound. A new rule minted against an exhausted case is an import that adds an atom that was not needed; Fig 6 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives. Only if the library genuinely lacks the exception is an import owed, and then R must be described in effect terms before the frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"). This mirrors the legal technique reported in SEP *Non-monotonic Logic*, where a more specific antecedent already in the knowledge base overrides a general one, so no new norm is needed.

**Refuter:** show that minting a new rule before searching the existing exception set is never more costly, in actions or in bits, across a set of cases with ground readings. If it is never more costly, F9's order is not load-bearing.

**Verdict on the question.** Yes, but not by removing discretion. Consistency is achievable as a non-updating anchor plus a written, per-case, provenanced refusal checked by an unauthored and rotated frame. Uniformity of outcomes is not achievable, because the rule that demands it describes an idealisation no real occupant could meet. The two halves of the question are therefore not a trade-off between two goods. They are one structure: the refusal operator and its record are what make the non-updating rule possible at all. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Adding discretion changes contact; adding more uniformity clauses alone does not.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before outreach. Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A real institution is measured in which a rule with no exception mechanism meets every case it was designed for, with the ground paying for each ruling (breaks F1).
2. A rule system carries a correct refusal of its own held rule, inheritable by later cases, with no negation or equivalent retraction operator (breaks F3).
3. A case in two conditions is cleared correctly, with the ground paying, by treating it only as a harder case of one condition and never opening a separate loop (breaks F6).
4. An institution-wide aggregate is shown to settle a particular unusual case correctly with no per-case reading (breaks F5).
5. A rule that updates on the pushed-on frame beats a non-updating rule on the very residual the rule is about (breaks F4).
6. A review set chosen by the party under review keeps its initial disagreement rate over repeated use, confirmed against ground readings (breaks F8).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused, not softened. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the residual per case, not per institution (F5). A compliance percentage cannot be the answer to this question.
2. Write the exception as a refusal inside the rule set (F3). An unwritten favour cannot be checked or carried.
3. Replace the ideal of zero discretion with a declared stake and a satisfiable rule (F1). The figure itself shows the correction from "no stake" to "declare it".
4. Date and provenance each ruling; never treat a clean record as a correct ruling (F7).
5. Select reviewers from an unauthored pool and rotate them (F8). Ask what would refute the ruling before showing the rule set.
6. Search the existing exception set before minting a new rule (F9). Describe the unusual case in effect terms before choosing a review frame.
7. Send the shape and the refuter, not the theory (Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").

## 8. Strains after re-census

- **Fig 10 "no stake" versus Fig 10 :54.** Not strained. The figure records its own earlier impossible prohibition at :57 and replaces it at :59-60. This is a retraction inside the source, and it is the strongest single figure-grounded point in this solution.
- **Fig 1 per-slot residual versus institutional aggregate.** Not strained. They are different quantities at different scales; the membrane (Fig 4) price is the round trip gap R_T, which `11_audit.md` records qualitatively.
- **Rule versus standard (world source) versus rule as anchor (figure).** Not strained after re-census. The figure's anchor is a ground relation; a "standard" in SEP *The Rule of Law* is a norm form. Different objects.
- **After re-census: no hidden strains.** The live limit is empirical, not structural: no institution was measured, so the verdict is `specified`, `candidate`, and the empirical question is unreached, not answered.
