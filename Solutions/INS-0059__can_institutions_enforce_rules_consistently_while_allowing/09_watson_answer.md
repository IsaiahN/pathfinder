## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that institutions can enforce rules consistently while allowing discretion for unusual cases. Option A: uniformity, meaning a rule with no exception mechanism applied identically to every case. Option B: an anchor plus a written refusal, meaning a non-updating rule whose exceptions are negations with provenance, read per case and checked from outside. What each leads to: A gives a rule that describes an idealisation no real occupant could meet and licenses inferences no real position would support; B gives a checkable shape with six falsifiers written in advance and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two conditions, a general rule condition plus an exceptional condition, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Symmetry: asymmetric, because a ruling is a sequence that produces a status rather than a symmetric join. Scale: first order at the case, second order at the institution's rule set.
- **Domain 1, case application.** Actors: case, official, rule. Currency: rulings. Anchor: the case's facts and outcome, which do not move when the official pushes on them. Question type: partition, which compositions of rule and case are legal.
- **Domain 2, rule formation.** Actors: institution, rule designers, the library of rules and precedents. Currency: norms and conventions. Anchor: the social fact the rule regulates. Question type: partition, what happens when a convention decides an outcome (Fig 2 :43).
- **Domain 3, discretion oversight.** Actors: auditor, review frame, the person exercising discretion. Currency: provenance records, refusals, residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero and agreement is not corroboration.
- **Domain 4, the unusual case compound.** Actors: the case in two conditions, the rule, the exception. Currency: the residual neither rule-only nor discretion-only clears. Anchor: the ground for the compound. This is a union at the intersection of Domains 1 and 2 with its own loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Channel to case facts, then rule as a non-deciding convention, then the negation operator, then the dated ruling record, then an unauthored and rotated reviewer, then per-case residuals. The third link is the one with no instrument and therefore the likely silent break (Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at").

Strain test: "rule" and "standard" in the world literature are a bond-level difference (sequence versus comparison), while the exception is a unary-operator difference. Different objects, so no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**Yes, but not as a trade-off between uniformity and favour.** What is achievable is a four-part shape: a non-updating anchor; discretion as the unary negation operator inside the rule set; a per-case residual with a dated ruling record; and an unauthored, rotated reviewer. The unusual case is a union compound with its own loop, not a tail of the ordinary distribution. What is not achievable is a rule with no exception mechanism: that is an idealisation no real occupant could meet, and it licenses inferences no real position would support. The figure's own retraction of its impossible "no stake" absolute (:57-60) is the template: replace the impossible absolute with a satisfiable rule and a declared stake. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. No named institution is the answer.

- **cost if wrong:** two opposite failures. If the shape is wrong in forbidding zero discretion, institutions that do achieve uniformity are wrongly told they need an exception machinery they do not need. If it is right and ignored, every justified refusal stays indistinguishable from arbitrariness, because a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate, and the cost of being wrong is paid by the person whose case was refused. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable part is spending a reader's independence by asking for agreement before they have checked the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four parts are additions to a rule vocabulary and a record practice. Formula step 3: nothing is deleted; a refusal is added and the reach changes by derivation. An institution can add a written refusal, a dated reason and an unauthored reviewer without withdrawing its existing rules. A superseding packet can correct this one, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The no-exception test (a real institution whose rule with no exception mechanism meets every designed-for case with the ground paying), the operator test (an inheritable refusal without any negation or retraction operator), the union test (a two-condition case cleared as a harder one-condition case), the aggregate test (an institution-wide aggregate settling the unusual case with no per-case reading), the update test (a pushed-on rule beating a non-updating one on its own residual), or the rotation test (a party-chosen review set preserving its disagreement rate). Any one fires the corresponding refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and moves that finding from `candidate` to withdrawn. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
