**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about rules and exceptions, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about rules and discretion. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** An institution cannot be consistent by removing discretion. A rule with no exception mechanism is an idealisation no real occupant could meet, and it licenses inferences no real position would support. What can be consistent is this shape:
>
> 1. the rule is an anchor that does not change its answer because the person in front of it pushes on it;
> 2. discretion is written inside the rule set as a refusal, "this rule does not hold in this case", not an unwritten favour;
> 3. the residual is read per case, so the one unusual case does not disappear into a 99 percent compliance average;
> 4. each refusal carries the residual it answered, the reasoning and the date, and the reviewer comes from a pool the rule's author did not choose and is rotated.
>
> This is a shape any candidate answer would have to have. It is not a measurement and it does not name a particular legal system.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. a real institution where a rule with no exception mechanism meets every case it was designed for, with the ground paying for each ruling;
> 2. a rule system that carries a correct, inheritable refusal of its own rule with no negation or retraction operator;
> 3. a case in two conditions cleared correctly by treating it only as a harder case of one condition;
> 4. an institution-wide aggregate shown to settle the unusual case correctly with no per-case reading;
> 5. a review set chosen by the party under review that keeps its initial disagreement rate over repeated use.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If the machinery is useful to you:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a rule set you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula". A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
