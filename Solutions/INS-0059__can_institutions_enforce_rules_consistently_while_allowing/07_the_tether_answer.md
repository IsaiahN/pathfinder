# The Tether's answer: can institutions enforce rules consistently while allowing discretion?

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two conditions: a general rule and a case to which the rule does not fit. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." This is a **union compound** with its own loop and its own ground, not one hard question about compliance. Effort spent making the rule more uniform clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56).

## 2. The shape

1. **A zero-discretion rule is an idealisation, not a position.** Fig 10 :54: "A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position." And :56: "an idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support". The figure corrects its own earlier impossible absolute at :57-58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist", replaced by "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." (:60). So consistency cannot be purchased by deleting discretion. It is purchased by declaring the stake and structuring the exception.

2. **Consistency is a non-updating anchor, not identical outcomes.** Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A rule that changes its answer because someone pushes has become another frame, and the arrangement is coherent drift. The same ruling for the same case, regardless of who asks, is the achievable consistency. Identical outcomes across all cases is not the same claim.

3. **Discretion is the negation operator inside the rule set.** Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 5 :56: "Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain." A refusal is not a missing operand (that would be subtraction, −) and not a new rule. It is ¬ applied to a held rule in a case. Without it, an institution can accumulate rules and case lists forever and still cannot carry a correction. `06_isomers_and_order.md` shows rules and exceptions are therefore not isomers: the vocabularies differ in operator arity.

4. **The refusal needs a dated record, not an appeal path.** Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom." Fig 10 :63: "The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read." And :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that". A clean record of a wrong ruling "is an unimpeachable record of nothing, and it is still to be corrected" (:66).

5. **The reviewer must be unauthored and rotated.** Fig 10 :73: "The residual selects the members, but only from what is available." A panel chosen by the official whose ruling is in question is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned. The allowed unauthored mechanisms are a lottery, everyone who qualifies, a set assembled before the question existed, or the enumerated habitat (Fig 10 :76-79). Independence decays with use, so frames "whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated" (:81). And Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

6. **Residuals are per case.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A 99 percent compliance figure is the form in which the one justified exception disappears. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Compliance tells you where to look, never whether the ruling was right.

7. **Repair order: searched, inward, outward.** Ask first whether the exception already exists in the rule library and was unfound (Formula step 7). Minting a new rule before that search adds an atom that was not needed, and Fig 6 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives. Only then extend the instrument to the case facts, then import from a frame whose closure differs, with R described in effect terms first (Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second.").

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named institution, statute or review board is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that discretion is unlimited. It is a refusal with a record, checked from outside, bounded by the anchor.
- Not that review-panel agreement validates a ruling. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- Not that compliance auditing is the ground. It is a filter for the budget.
- Not that a standard (a graded threshold) removes the need for a refusal operator. It moves the threshold question one level.
- Not `grounded`. No institution was measured, no ruling was checked against a ground in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A ground reading, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **The no-exception test:** find a real institution where a rule with no exception mechanism meets every case it was designed for, with the ground paying for each ruling. This would refute F1.
- **The operator test:** find a rule system that carries a correct, inheritable refusal of its own rule with no negation or retraction operator. This would refute F3.
- **The union test:** find a case in two conditions cleared correctly by treating it as a harder case of one condition only, with no separate loop. This would refute F6.
- **The aggregate test:** find an institution-wide aggregate that settles the unusual case correctly with no per-case reading. This would refute F5.
- **The update test:** find a rule that updates on the pushed-on frame and beats a non-updating rule on the very residual it is about. This would refute F4.
- **The rotation test:** find a review set chosen by the party under review whose initial disagreement rate is preserved over repeated use, confirmed against ground readings. This would refute F8.

All six are written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 before this conclusion. None has been run.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is figure-governed and triangulated. Whether any actual institution realises it is an empirical question this budget did not measure. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that consistency and discretion are a trade-off, and it does not name a model institution. It says a rule with no exception mechanism is an idealisation no real occupant could meet, so uniformity cannot be the goal. Consistency is achievable as an anchor that does not change its answer when someone pushes on it. Discretion is the negation operator that lets the rule refuse itself in a case, and it has to be written into the rule set, carry the residual, reasoning and date, be checked by a reviewer chosen from a pool the rule's author did not pick, and be measured per case rather than folded into a compliance average. The general rule and the unusual case are a union compound with their own loop, not one hard question. What is offered is that shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Nothing here is settled until a real case against a real ground settles it.
