# Expert verification packet - the Tether on rules and discretion

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of rules, discretion or administrative process can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Institutions cannot achieve consistency by removing discretion, because a rule no real occupant could meet is an idealisation that licenses inferences no real position would support. Achievable consistency is a rule that does not change its answer because of who pushes on it. Discretion is the unary negation operator that lets a held rule refuse itself in a case; it must be inside the rule set, carry the residual it answered with reasoning and date, be read per case rather than in aggregate, and be checked by a reviewer drawn from an unauthored and rotated pool. The general rule and the unusual case form a union compound with its own loop, so effort spent on uniformity alone clears only the region where one condition holds.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 10 and the Operators table
- **Statement (two parts).** (a) A rule with no exception mechanism is an idealisation, not a position. (b) Discretion is ¬, a unary operator, not a seventh bond and not subtraction; without it a rule set can only accumulate.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 10 :54: "A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position." Fig 10 :56: "an idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support". Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Fig 5 :56: "Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain."
- **Figure's own correction:** Fig 10 :57-58: "Below is the one this figure had. A seat with no stake at all does not exist." Replaced at :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."
- **What to check:** is the arity claim right? ¬ takes one operand and joins nothing (Operators :70-74). − is absence of an operand, not refusal of a claim.
- **Refuter:** a real institution where a rule with no exception mechanism meets every designed-for case with the ground paying, or a rule system carrying an inheritable refusal with no negation or retraction operator.

### C2 - institution-facing; checkable against a real case record
- **Statement.** The residual must be indexed per case, and each refusal must carry the residual, reasoning and date, with a reviewer from an unauthored and rotated pool. Compliance statistics are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the ruling.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom." Fig 10 :63: "The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read." Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that". Fig 10 :81: "frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated". Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** take one real exception in a real institution. Is there a per-case residual, a dated reason, a reviewer not chosen by the decision maker, and a rotation rule? If any is absent, the shape does not describe that institution.
- **Refuter:** an institution-wide aggregate shown to settle the unusual case correctly with no per-case reading; or a review set chosen by the party under review that preserves its initial disagreement rate over repeated use, confirmed against ground readings.

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading Fig 9 and Fig 2
- **Statement.** The general rule plus the unusual case is a union compound with its own loop, and the rule's consistency must be an anchor that does not update, not a convention that decides the outcome.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- **What to check:** does the institution treat the exceptional case as a tail of the ordinary distribution (wrong census), or open a separate loop for the intersection? If the rule changes its answer because the applicant pushes, the anchor condition fails.
- **Refuter:** a case in two conditions cleared correctly by treating it only as a harder case of one condition, with ground readings confirming; or a pushed-on rule that beats a non-updating rule on the residual it is about.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named institution, statute or country is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that discretion is unlimited or that reviewers must always be outsiders with no stake. Fig 10 :58-60: a seat with no stake at all does not exist; declare the interest that cannot be removed.
- Not that compliance metrics are useless. They are filters for the budget. They are not witnesses for a verdict.
- Not that review-panel agreement validates a ruling. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Not that this packet is `grounded`. Nothing in it has been checked against a real ruling by a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The six refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 4 are the instruments. The most decisive is the no-exception test: a real institution whose rule with no exception mechanism meets every designed-for case with the ground paying. If that is found, the central claim fails. The next most decisive is the operator test: an inheritable refusal without negation.
