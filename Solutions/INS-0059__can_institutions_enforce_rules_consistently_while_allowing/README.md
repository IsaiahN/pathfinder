**Question:** Can institutions enforce rules consistently while allowing discretion for unusual cases?
**Source:** institutional questions.txt line 72 (section 6, "Law, rights, and rule enforcement", heading at line 63)
**ID:** INS-0059

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. No institution was measured, so nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive claim in this folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

**Status:** done
**Registry status:** pending

**Field for public contact points:** rules and discretion research (administrative law, sociolegal studies, legal reasoning and computational models of rules, rule-of-law measurement).

## Residual in effect terms

An institution holds a general rule R and must decide a case. Option A: remove discretion so that R is applied identically to every case. This leads to a rule that describes an idealisation no real occupant could meet (Fig 10 :54), a case in two conditions treated as a harder case of one (Fig 9 :57), a convention that decides an outcome and therefore stops being a convention (Fig 2 :43), and a vocabulary that cannot write a refusal so it can only accumulate (Operators table :57). Option B: keep the rule as an anchor that does not update on the case (Fig 2 :33), and build discretion as a written refusal with provenance, per case, checked by an unauthored and rotated frame (Fig 10 :62, :73, :81). What each leads to is the whole question.

## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

A shape with four parts: a non-updating anchor; a negation operator that can refuse a rule in a case; a per-case residual with a dated ruling record; and an unauthored, rotated checker. Bind that shape before searching so it can accrue a residual instead of remaining a preference (Fig 5 :65, Formula "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons: a room's agreement is variation, not a verdict (Fig 7 :35); correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention (Fig 2 :40, Seat Map :163); and a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42). One-to-one contact only, through the public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
