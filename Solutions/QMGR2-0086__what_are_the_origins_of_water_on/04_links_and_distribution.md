## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The fingerprint is smeared.** Meech (2019) writes that because of *"the complex chemistry and dynamical mixing as the planets grew, the chemical fingerprints are 'smeared'. Multiple reservoirs can also have the same fingerprint."* Alexander (2017) writes that *"D/H alone is not sufficient to determine a formation distance to compare to dynamical models."* These are the same shape as Fig 8 :50-52: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. Neither result was produced by the framework; both are independent corroboration of the shape, from one planetary evidence pool.
2. **The consensus is a union, not a winner.** Russell, Ballentine and Grady (2017) report that *"a consensus emerged that volatiles were likely incorporated into the terrestrial planets both during planetary accretion and later by asteroidal impacts."* Piani et al. (2020) add enstatite-like inner-disk material as a substantial contributor; Alexander (2017) requires a solar component in addition to CI/CM. This is why the folder refuses a single-origin answer and keeps per-route verdicts separate.
