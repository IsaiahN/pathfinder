## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks: *what are the origins of water on Earth?* Stated as an effect, present-day Earth carries roughly 1.4 x 10^21 kg of surface water plus an uncertain mantle inventory, and that water has a measurable isotopic signature (D/H, 15N/14N, O isotopes, noble gases). The residual is not the existence of the water. It is the **gap between what the present reading can discriminate about its own source and what the question asks**, namely which route produced it.

Describe the effect before choosing a frame, per Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The effect is this: several distinct source routes can yield the same present reading. D/H alone does not select a route. Meech (2019) writes that because of *"the complex chemistry and dynamical mixing as the planets grew, the chemical fingerprints are 'smeared'. Multiple reservoirs can also have the same fingerprint."* Alexander (2017) writes that *"D/H alone is not sufficient to determine a formation distance to compare to dynamical models."* Russell, Ballentine and Grady (2017) report that *"while a minority of comets have a D/H ratio, for example, similar to the Earth, the majority have highly enriched D/H, ruling them out as major sources of Earth's water."* The reading is a projection with lossy information.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

| property | reading |
|---|---|
| arity | Multiple source conditions (nebular gas on grains or in a magma ocean, enstatite-like inner-disk material, carbonaceous chondrite material, cometary and interplanetary dust, mantle degassing and recycling). Not one cause with opposite answers. |
| symmetry | The D/H comparison is a threshold relation, not a symmetric identity that would name one referent. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."* |
| scale | Molecular isotope ratio, planetary water inventory, disk-scale transport and four-plus billion years of mantle recycling. The same loop at several strata, with different actors, currency and anchor (Fig 4 :20). |

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term.** Before the run, the sought-for shape is made refusable, per Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* Bound shape: an answer must (1) name a production history from a source reservoir to the present water, (2) state which measurement discriminates that route from the others, (3) keep the provenance of each imported sample or model, and (4) say which part of the residual remains open. Any claim that fails one of these is recorded as failing, not silently dropped.

## 3. Findings F1 to F12

### F1: The literal single-origin answer is a `gap`, not a silence (decisive for the question form)

Fig 13 :133-135: *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name. So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

The question asks for *the origins*, as if one name were owed. No figure line returns a single origin for terrestrial water. What the figures license is the shape any answer must have. That is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not an underivable-in-principle claim and not silence. The framework's own usage rule says it can say *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and then stops at the limit of the instruments.

**Refuter:** show a figure line that directly governs which single reservoir supplied Earth's water, or return an instrument that maps a present reading to one route uniquely. Until then the name stays owed.

### F2: The candidate sources are a union, not a partition (triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :55-57 are quoted in section 1. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

The measured record anchors each route separately. Russell et al. (2017): *"From discussions at the meeting, a consensus emerged that volatiles were likely incorporated into the terrestrial planets both during planetary accretion and later by asteroidal impacts."* That is two conditions with one shared outcome, exactly Fig 9's compound. Alexander (2017) and Piani et al. (2020) add further routes. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 (figure-internal union rule) and the planetary-science record (low overlap with the figure text).

**Refuter:** a single source that reproduces all discriminating isotope ratios, the mass budget and the mantle inventory with no residual left would collapse the union to a partition and kill this finding.

### F3: The present reading cannot comment on its source (triangulated)

Fig 8 :50-52: *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 6 :26: *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."*

A D/H value is the same number whether it came from inner-disk material, outer-disk material or a fractionated reservoir. The contents do not carry the route. Meech (2019) writes that *"Multiple reservoirs can also have the same fingerprint"*; Alexander (2017) writes *"D/H alone is not sufficient to determine a formation distance to compare to dynamical models."* Isaiah N, *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28): *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* The framework names the mechanism, metamerism, and the world sources supply the same shape in planetary chemistry.

**Refuter:** a measurement in which one route yields a signature no other route can yield, with the discriminating quantity named and read. That would break the convergence and restore a name.

### F4: More argument on one ratio is not the remedy; a discriminator is owed (triangulated)

Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve. Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 6 :29-31 keeps INWARD and OUTWARD opposed: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*

There is already something returning a failing reading: isotope mass spectrometry on meteorites, mantle plumes and cometary samples. So this is not the absent-sensor case. The instrument to extend is the one already returning D/H and 15N/14N, widened to a multi-isotope, multi-reservoir discriminator tied to dynamical models. Meech (2019) titles a section *"Multiple Fingerprints are Needed"* and says *"to understand the origins of inner solar system volatiles, we need to measure multiple isotopes, comparing these to the dynamical models."* The Hard Problem essay: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

**Refuter:** show that no extension of an existing returning instrument can separate the routes, and that no new returning reading exists at any resolution. Then the finding becomes genuine absence, not unreached depth.

### F5: A single-route story fails the MDL bargain (triangulated)

Fig 5 :43-44: *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*

"Water came from comets" matched one reading (Halley, then Hartley 2) and left the others unexplained; "water came from carbonaceous chondrites" matches bulk D/H and N better but still requires a solar component (Alexander 2017: *"an additional volatile source is required. The most obvious source would be a solar component acquired either directly from the nebula or as solar wind implanted into the surfaces of accreted dust and small planetesimals."*). Alexander also names the coincidence argument: *"given their different sizes and formation histories, it would be an extraordinary coincidence if they all experienced such similar degrees of H isotopic fractionation."* That is the one-occasion failure the figure prices.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that one route plus one fractionation mechanism states the whole record more cheaply, with the leftover residual quantified, and the union claim is withdrawn.

### F6: Same molecule, different route: the arrangement is the substance (single-source for the figure, with a named caveat)

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Fig 13 :127-129: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available. The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*

Water from a comet and water from a chondrite parent body are the same H2O. What differs is the production history, and that difference is carried by the bond, not the ingredient list. The chemistry analogue in Fig 12 :97-98 (silver fulminate and silver cyanate) is an analogue for provenance, not a claim that the isotopologues are chemical isomers. **Caveat recorded:** the figure's isomer definition is about typed bonds between operands; here the "bond" is a production route in a lineage, so this is a level-relatives use, not a literal molecular isomer claim. Kept as `single-source` and strained-for-now, not upgraded.

**Refuter:** show that the route is recoverable from the molecule alone, or that the lineage record carries no information the ingredient list lacks.

### F7: Provenance is the whole issue for imported samples and models (triangulated)

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 8 :12 *"source gate: provenance"* and Fig 8 :13 *"shadow test: explains a gap"* name the import gates. Fig 10 :62-66: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom. The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."*

Each imported item (a meteorite fall with a recovery history, a Rosetta measurement with an activity state, a Grand Tack model with its assumptions) needs a source gate and a shadow test. Piani et al. (2020) are careful about terrestrial contamination in enstatite chondrites and report their measured H contents and delta-D values with that gate visible. Alexander (2017) warns that *"the comet compositions in figure 1 are probably not their bulk compositions"*, a provenance gap that changes what the reading can support.

**Refuter:** show an import that moves the wall with no provenance and no shadow test, and is still admitted by the figures. None was found; the figures forbid it.

### F8: Index the residual per reservoir, never aggregate it (single-source, structural)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The slots here are the water reservoirs (ocean, deep mantle, plume samples, chondrite water, cometary water, adsorbed nebular H). A single percentage for "the origin of Earth's water" averages them and hides which one carries the live residual. Russell et al. (2017) explicitly note that *"the Earth's oceans are not a closed system"* in the related chapter, and Alexander (2017) distinguishes water from organics within the same chondrite. The verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

**Refuter:** show that all reservoirs share one residual with one reading and no slot-specific signal survives. Then aggregation is harmless and this finding fails.

### F9: The habitat is enumerated from the world, and contact cascades (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43-44 and :48 are quoted in section 1. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

A comet is not a benefactor or a malfactor in itself. Its valence flips with the residual: for the D/H residual it is mostly excluded, for the noble-gas budget it contributes (Russell et al. 2017: *"Terrestrial Kr isotopic compositions, nevertheless, show that later comet addition, while not contributing significantly to the C,N,H2O, may have played an important role in sourcing the noble gas budget of the Earth's atmosphere."*). That is Fig 11 :49 applied to a real case.

**Refuter:** show a standing list of good and bad source bodies that survives a change of residual. Fig 11 :51 says *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

### F10: SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD (general, structural)

THE_FORMULA step 7 separates the three. SEARCHED asks whether the term is in here and unfound, is asked first because it is cheapest, and is assumed by the other two. INWARD extends the instrument from one already returning something. OUTWARD imports: characterize R in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Applied here: first, more measurements of the existing reservoirs at the same read (search, cheap, keeps the closure unchanged). Second, sharpen the returning instrument (multiple isotopes, better sample handling, plume and meteorite comparisons). Third, import from frames whose closures differ (dynamical disk models, sample-return missions, the hydrology/equifinality literature for the shape of non-uniqueness). Underivable in principle is not what we have; unreached at this budget is.

**Refuter:** show that the term is already in the closure and simply unfound (then more search, not an import, is the right instruction), or that importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

### F11: The ground is the sample record, not agreement (triangulated)

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The measured isotope compositions and the physical samples are the non-updating anchor. Agreement among research groups drawn from the same samples is one evidence pool talking. The low-overlap witness here is the equifinality literature in hydrology and systems theory, not another review of the same meteorites. Wikipedia contributors, *Equifinality* (2026): *"a given end state can be reached by many potential means."* That is the same shape as Fig 8 :50-52 from a different field.

**Refuter:** show the anchor moving when a frame pushes on it (then it was a frame, not an anchor), or show two genuinely independent instruments reading the same sample and still converging on a single route.

### F12: No settlement, so no `grounded` label (general)

Fig 2 :29-30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely."*

No measurement of the 4.5 Ga delivery event was taken; only its surviving record is read. The channel (samples, isotope systems) can be stale, pooled or saturated even though the ground does not decay. So no term in this folder is stamped `grounded`. Status of every verdict: candidate.

**Refuter:** return a source-discriminating measurement that settles a route against the anchor, and that route moves to `grounded` with the measurement cited.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is withdrawn or revised if any of the following fires:

1. A single source reproduces all discriminating H, N, O and noble-gas ratios and the Earth's water mass budget with no leftover residual. The union collapses to a partition.
2. An instrument maps a present-day bulk reading uniquely to one delivery route, with the discriminator named and read on independent samples. The shape-not-a-name `gap` closes.
3. A re-census shows a figure line that directly governs which single reservoir supplied the water, or shows that the union rule (Fig 9 :55-57) does not govern this residual. The `general` shape claim fails.
4. A returned cometary bulk sample (not only its water) matches Earth's bulk H, N and noble gases, so the per-route exclusion of most comets is re-opened.
5. The route is shown to be pure sequence (→), with the water pre-existing and only moved, so the production claim (⇒) for that route is misnamed.

If none fires, the result stands as a `specified` shape with one declared `gap` and one live strain (F6). Written before any conclusion in this file.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there one origin?** No name is returned. Fig 13 :133-135 and Fig 9 :32 give a shape. Status: `gap`, not silence.
2. **What kind of question?** A union of overlapping source conditions, not a partition. Fig 9 :55-57, corroborated by Russell et al. (2017).
3. **Can the reading name the route?** No. Fig 8 :50-52 plus metamerism and equifinality. Provenance is the only separator.
4. **What would close it?** A source-discriminating instrument already returning something. Fig 6 :34-35, Fig 3 :27.
5. **Import?** Dynamical models, sample returns and low-overlap fields are imports with a source gate and shadow test. Fig 8 :9-13. They add access, not atoms to the world (Fig 6 :25).
6. **Settlement?** None at the delivery event. Only the surviving record is read. Status: candidate, not `grounded` (Fig 5 :41).
7. **Route of the search?** SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, kept distinct (THE_FORMULA step 7). A wrong atom against a budget limit costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :52).
