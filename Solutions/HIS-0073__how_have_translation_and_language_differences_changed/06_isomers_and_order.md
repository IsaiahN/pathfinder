# Isomers and order: what the two language versions are, and what must come first

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary comparison calls a source claim and its translated version "the same idea in two languages." The residual is that this treats the word list as the whole object. Two questions follow: (1) under Fig 12's definition, are the two versions copies, isomers, or members of a functional group; and (2) in what order must the derivation run so that a verdict at one level is not a reading of nothing?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held at the slot: the source claim's conceptual atoms, the receiving frame's available atoms, the ground that paid for the source claim, and the bond between claim and ground. Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus negation. No new atom is available without import. Transform: the language boundary, which is not a frame with its own ground but a typed operator on the two frames.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are a source claim and its translation copies or isomers?

- Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :14 *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*
- Fig 12 :16 *"and nothing signals that anything was lost"*
- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

A source claim and a target version that share the conceptual atoms but differ in which bond connects them to the ground, to a derivation, or to the permissible next composition are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a term-by-term comparison, which is exactly why word-for-word fidelity is a poor test of whether the idea survived.

**World instance 1 (history of science).** Du Châtelet's French *Principia* (published 1756) was not a recording of Newton's Latin. It added a derivation of conservation of energy and extensive commentary, including corrections drawn from Clairaut and Bernoulli. The atoms were shared; the bond to the ground and what could be composed next were not. The French version became the standard translation and was still the one used by I. Bernard Cohen for his English version.

**World instance 2 (history of science in China).** Yan Fu rendered natural selection as *tianze* and framed Huxley's *Evolution and Ethics* through Social Darwinism and his own "faithfulness, expressiveness, elegance" standard. The term crossed; the arrangement changed what Chinese readers could build on the claim, which is a bond difference, not a lexical error. He also served as head of the State Terminology Bureau, which is the habitat condition for imports.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two language versions compose identically in every ground-paying respect, with no difference in prediction and nothing buildable in one frame that is not buildable in the other, the isomer claim fails for that slot and the copy reading stands.

### Q2: When are they merely a functional group?

- Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Where two versions share only a scaffold, such as "an observation connects to a law through a measurement," they are not isomers. They are two members of a functional group with different atoms in the slots. This is the weaker and safer claim when the shared atom set has not been established. The exemplar made the same distinction for GR and QM: sharing one atom gives a functional group, sharing the full set gives isomers.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a shared substructure whose presence fixes the outcome in both frames while the remaining atoms differ; then the functional-group reading is confirmed and the isomer reading is withdrawn for that slot.

### Q3: Does the transform have a direction?

- Fig 4 :21 *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*
- Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*
- Fig 4 :28 *"But it means the trip is not reversible"*

The language boundary is a filter, not a wall. What crosses well in one direction is a method that can be reapplied; what crosses in the other is a head start that makes the next search cheaper. The transform is lossy and non-invertible. The round trip does not restore the source even when every word matches. This is why "fidelity" cannot be the only measure of a translation of a scientific claim: fidelity measures the ingredient list, not the bond or the round-trip gap.

**Falsifier:** a measured round trip that returns the source exactly and leaves the receiving frame able to reproduce the same ground-paid predictions. That would make the transform reversible at that slot and F1 of `01` would be refused there.

### Q4: What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

1. **Perception.** Is there a reading at all? A paired source and target term that fails to resolve against the source ground. Fig 6 :33 *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."*
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the receiving frame have terms for what the claim affords, or is an import owed (Fig 6 :22)?
3. **Objective.** Can the receiving frame say what would count as progress on this claim in its own terms? Fig 3 :10 *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*.
4. **Planning.** Which instrument or paired study would measure the gap?
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry the method up as a method and down as a head start, never as a recording (Fig 4 :23).

Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* More translation volume optimises a link that is usually already working; the broken link is between source settlement and target composition, and Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

**Falsifier:** an existing instrument that measures the round-trip gap routinely and is tied to a non-updating ground. Then the middle link has been looked at and the diagnosis changes.

### Q5: Is the union of two language frames a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
- Fig 8 :48 *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*

The sources of divergence overlap. Loss at the transform, missing atoms, channel decay, and habitat substitution can all be present in one case. Unbundling leaves a categorically different residue, not a smaller mixture. Where the two frames genuinely differ at the evidence level, their union has a surplus; where they share one pool, it has none. The same independence parameter decides both.

**Falsifier:** a clean partition in which each observed case falls under exactly one condition and a single lever clears all cases. That would refute the union reading.

## CONCLUSION

Source and target versions of a scientific claim are **not copies**. Over a shared conceptual atom set they are Fig 12 isomers when the bond to the ground or the composition differs, and members of a functional group when only a scaffold is shared. The transform is a directed, lossy filter, so fidelity of wording is not evidence of survival. The derivation must run in dependency order: reading, vocabulary (import if needed), objective, instrument, then carry. The divergence conditions form a union, not a partition, and the surplus of a two-language union depends on the same independence that makes agreement meaningful.

What does not follow: that any particular translation lost or gained knowledge. That needs the ground.
