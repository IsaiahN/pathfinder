# Expert verification packet: the Tether on translation and scientific circulation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a translation scholar, historian of science or scientist studying communication can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No paired, ground-tied measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A scientific claim crossing a language boundary is not copied. It passes through a typed, lossy transform with a measurable round-trip gap. What crosses usefully is a method or a head start, not a recording of one conclusion. If the receiving closure lacks an atom for the claim, more translation of existing words cannot produce the claim; an import with provenance is owed. Source and target versions over a shared conceptual atom set are isomers, same atoms with a different bond to the ground and the derivation. The ground does not decay but the channel does, so repeated editions of one source multiply agreement rather than evidence. The framework returns that shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument that would close a slot. It does not return the particular verdict for any particular language pair.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 12

- **Statement:** the transform is lossy and not reversible; the ingredient list is not the thing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :28 *"But it means the trip is not reversible"* Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- **What to check:** does the figure actually license a transform between language frames, or only within and between scales of one frame? The binding from scale to language is a structural mapping offered by this solution, provenanced as such. If that mapping fails, C1 is a claim about scales only.
- **Refuter:** a paired translation where back-translation returns the source exactly and the receiving frame reproduces the same ground-paid predictions, with the gap measured at zero.

### C2: History and translation studies facing; checkable against known cases

- **Statement:** source and target versions are isomers when they share conceptual atoms but differ in the bond to the ground or the derivation; they are a functional group when only a scaffold is shared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** du Châtelet's French *Principia* added a derivation of conservation of energy and commentary, so the French version had a different bond to the ground. Yan Fu rendered natural selection as *tianze* and framed the whole claim through Social Darwinism. Are those bond changes, or only lexical and rhetorical differences that leave the claim composable in the same way? Translation studies itself records Skopos theory and the treatment of translations as "facts of the target culture"; does that corroborate the bond claim or merely the purpose claim?
- **Refuter:** show that two versions compose identically in every ground-paying respect, with no difference in prediction and nothing buildable in one frame that is not buildable in the other. Then the copy reading stands for that slot.

### C3: Verification frame; checkable by any researcher who knows the literature

- **Statement:** agreement across language editions that share one evidence pool is a convention, not a verdict, and a chain of translations compounds agreement without adding evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
- **What to check:** find a case where several language editions of one claim are treated as independent confirmations. Is there any ground-paid prediction in the later edition that was not already present in the first? If the answer is yes, the chain claim narrows for that case.
- **Refuter:** two frames with measured low overlap whose agreement changes the ground verdict, or a translation chain in which each link adds a new ground-paid prediction.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that translation always distorts, and not that it always enriches. Loss at a transform and surplus at a union of differing closures are different quantities.
- Not that a missing term means a concept is impossible. It means an import or a better instrument is owed at this budget.
- Not that word-for-word fidelity is irrelevant. It measures the ingredient list; it simply does not measure the bond or the round-trip gap.
- Not that the framework names which translated term wins. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that prestige, citation count or the number of translations settles anything. Those can fund a study; only the ground settles it.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Round-trip measurement:** translate and back-translate a ground-paid claim; measure the gap against the source ground.
2. **Source discrimination:** for an ambiguous target term, find a reading that distinguishes its possible sources, as a spectrometer distinguishes metamerers.
3. **Method versus recording:** compare target frames given only the translated conclusion versus the translated derivation and refuter.
4. **Independence:** measure evidence overlap between the frames before interpreting their agreement.
5. **Standing refuters:** zero measured gap, a recording that suffices, no import needed, a shared pool changing a ground verdict, or a uniform slot-indexed effect.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :27-30 and Fig 12 :5, :117, :124. Four short passages.
2. Pick one case you know well (a translated scientific term or a cross-language edition). Name the conceptual atoms held in both versions and the bond each has to its ground.
3. Say whether the two versions are isomers, a functional group, or copies in your case, and give one ground-paid difference that decides it.
4. Name one instrument that would measure the round-trip gap for that case, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
