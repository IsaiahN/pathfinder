# Outreach draft: check one cross-language output before you decide about the framing

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the recipient can answer stage 1 without seeing the framework. If the first contact carries the theory, the check is already spoiled (Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).

---

**Subject:** One question about a claim that crossed a language boundary

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on how scientific claims move between languages. I have one narrow output I would like you to check against your own field. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** take one claim that was settled against a ground in its source language, and the same claim as it circulates in a receiving language. The figures' account predicts three things at that slot. First, the transform is lossy and not reversible, so a round trip will not return what was sent; the gap is a measurement, not an error to erase. Second, what crosses usefully is a method or a head start, not a recording of one occasion, so a translated conclusion with no derivation gives the receiving frame less than a translated argument with its refuter. Third, if the receiving frame lacks an atom for the claim, recombining its own words cannot produce the claim; an import with provenance is owed, and the honest report is a gap in effect terms, not an invented equivalent.
>
> It also predicts that two language editions repeating one source are one evidence pool, not two witnesses. That is fine for a shared technical vocabulary, which is a convention and a success. It is not fine as evidence that the claim became more true by crossing.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. a paired translation where back-translation returns the source exactly and the receiving frame reproduces the same ground-paid predictions, with the gap measured at zero;
> 2. a translated conclusion, with no derivation or refuter, that reliably generates new settled terms in the receiving frame across several cases;
> 3. a receiving frame that derives a supposedly absent atom from its own atoms and reaches the same ground-paid predictions;
> 4. two frames sharing one evidence pool whose agreement changes the ground verdict;
> 5. a slot-indexed study showing the same translation effect, same sign and magnitude, across all ideas and receiving closures.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any fires, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. Your verdict on this paragraph is all I am asking for at this stage. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** I can send the formula and the discipline that goes with it. Write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

**One-to-one only.** No list ranked by receptiveness, no personal emails harvested from private posts, no bulk send. Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40) and breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions.
