## 0. The claim under audit

> How have translation and language differences changed the circulation of scientific ideas?
>
> Restated in effect terms: when a claim that was settled against a ground in one language enters another language frame, what is the relation between the claim that left and the claim that arrives? Option A: a neutral copy, so difference is removable friction. Option B: a typed transform with a measurable gap, so the receiving closure and its ground decide what the idea becomes.
>
> Bound sought-for shape (written before the run, so it can be refused): per idea-slot, identify what crossed (method or recording), what atom was imported, the round-trip gap, and the ground that pays.

No figure line returns a universal direction for this effect. What the figures do return is the shape of the transform, the conditions under which it loses information, the conditions under which it creates a surplus, and the instruments that would discriminate between loss and gain at a given slot. That shape is the substance of the answer.

## 1. Residual

**R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: at least three, a source claim, a channel (translator, terminology standard, machine system, scribe), and a receiving closure. Symmetry: the transform is not symmetric and its inverse is not the source; the round trip does not return what was sent (Fig 4 :28, :30). Scale: first order inside one language frame, second order at the boundary, third order along the chain across languages and generations.

**The per-slot rule.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* and Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* So the residual lives in the pair (claim, receiving closure). "Translation helped science" is an average that discards the only signal that says where to look. Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* also disqualifies citation counts and translation counts as self-scores.

**Anchor.** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The anchor for each slot is the ground the original claim paid against (an experiment, an instrument, a dataset, a patient outcome). Where a receiving frame has no nameable ground contact for that claim, it is not yet a domain and the disagreement is a channel artifact, not yet a verdict.

| **D2 Transform / channel** | translator, dictionary, terminology bureau, commentary, machine translation, printer | the round-trip gap `R_T`; bond choice; description length | the source text and target text read against the source ground | typed transform, not a frame with its own ground |

**Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. Loss at the transform, missing atoms in the target closure, channel decay, and habitat substitution overlap in the same case. One lever, such as "produce more translations," clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*, Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Settle the source claim before translating it (links 1 to 2); measure the transform before judging the target composition (link 3); only then read the circulation chain (link 4). Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* and Fig 3 :22 *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."* A circulation-level claim made before the transform is measured is a reading of nothing.

## 4. Findings

### F1: The transform is lossy and not reversible (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*
Fig 4 :28 *"But it means the trip is not reversible"*
Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :14 *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*
Fig 12 :16 *"and nothing signals that anything was lost"*

A translation can preserve the apparent ingredient list (the term equivalents) while dropping the bond (the relation of the term to its derivation, its ground, its evidential weight). Nothing in the target text signals the loss. The round-trip gap `R_T` is not an error to eliminate; Fig 4 :31 *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*. So the honest report is the measured gap, not "translation distorts" or "translation preserves."

**Refuter:** a paired translation task in which back-translation returns the source term and the receiving frame reproduces the same ground-paid predictions, with the gap measured at zero, not assumed. If that holds for a claim, F1 does not govern that claim.

### F2: Methods and head starts cross; recordings do not (triangulated)

Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :23 *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*
Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
Fig 5 :49 *"Machinery firing is not the milestone."*

What changes circulation is not the number of translated pages. A translated conclusion gives the target frame a recording of one occasion; a translated derivation plus its refuter gives a head start that makes the next search cheaper. This is the difference between du Châtelet's translation of the *Principia*, which added a derivation of conservation of energy and extensive commentary, and a bare term loan. The figures decide the category; the history supplies the instance.

**Refuter:** show a translated recording, conclusion and term with no method, that reliably produces new ground-paid terms in the receiving frame across several slots.

### F3: A missing atom is not reached by more translation (triangulated)

Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 6 :22 *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*
Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

When a receiving language has no term for a concept, recombining its existing terms will not produce the concept. An import is owed: a borrowing, a coinage, a calque, a terminology decision, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12 *"source gate: provenance"*, Fig 6 :13 *"shadow test: explains a gap"*). This is the structural reading of untranslatability. The world source on untranslatability says a lexical gap is a case where there is no one-to-one equivalence and that a translator can compensate by procedure; the figure adds that compensation by procedure is not the same as having the atom.

**Refuter:** derive the absent atom from the receiving frame's existing atoms with no import and no source gap, and show the receiving frame reaching the same ground-paid predictions.

### F4: Unreachable is a verdict, not silence (triangulated)

Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 13 :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

A term that cannot be composed in the receiving closure is reported as a gap with a characterisation in effect terms. It is not reported as "science could not cross this language," and it is not filled with an invented equivalent. Fig 6 :27 distinguishes underivable in principle from unreachable at this budget: a missing terminology institution is a budget and habitat fact, not a theorem about the language. The shape travels; the name is earned later by a ground or a provenanced import.

**Refuter:** show that the gap was a search failure at the same closure (the term was already composable and simply unfound), which changes the instruction from import to more search.

### F5: Isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance (single-source on the figure definition, with world instances)

Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
Fig 13 :121 *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*

Two language versions of one scientific claim, over a shared conceptual atom set, are not two spellings of one thing if the bond to the ground, the derivation, or the permissible composition differs. They are isomers, and the difference is invisible in a word-for-word comparison and decisive in behaviour. Where they share only a scaffold such as "a mechanism connects two states," they are members of a functional group, not isomers. Yan Fu rendering natural selection as *tianze* while framing the whole claim through Social Darwinism is a world instance of a changed bond changing the substance, not a mistranslation of a word.

**Refuter:** show that two versions compose identically in every ground-paying respect, with no difference in prediction and nothing buildable in one frame that is not buildable in the other. Then the isomer claim fails and the copy claim stands for that slot.

### F6: The ground does not decay, the channel does (triangulated)

Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 10 :25 *"stale percepts, dropped transients,"*
Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Most cross-language divergence is a channel failure: terms that no longer point to a reading, saturated words that carry too much, pooled readings from several sources treated as one. Adding copies amplifies the same channel. The repair is to keep the anchor reachable, which means pairing the translated term with the reading and the instrument that pays for it, not issuing another glossary.

**Refuter:** a case where the source ground itself changed and the cross-language divergence tracks that change rather than channel decay.

### F7: Multilingual agreement is not corroboration (triangulated)

Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*
Fig 8 :48 *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*
Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*

When ten language editions repeat one source, that is one evidence pool with ten names. It is efficient for a convention (a shared technical vocabulary is the product of translation), but it is worthless as a verdict about the world. Genuine corroboration requires two frames that reach the same shape from different evidence, and the record of which route each took is the only thing that separates adoption from convergence.

**Refuter:** show two language frames with measured low overlap whose agreement changes the ground verdict, then the verdict must be updated; show low-overlap frames with no surplus at all and the union-surplus claim narrows.

### F8: Translation chains manufacture consensus (triangulated)

Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
Fig 7 :42 *"the next room you book is the direction you travel"*

A claim passed through many languages gains apparent consensus without gaining evidence, because each room inherits the same closure. Progress along the chain requires the next room to differ from the last and to be chosen against the residual the last room surfaced. This is why a bibliography of translations is a map of motion, not a map of progress.

**Refuter:** a translation chain in which each link adds a new ground-paid prediction and the last room differs materially from the first.

### F9: The habitat must be enumerated (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

The actors in circulation are translators, dictionaries, terminology bureaux, printers, journals, academies, reviewers, schools and funders, outward until the cascade stops mattering. The same actor can help one idea and block another. A corpus translation is a substituted habitat, with two silent failures (Fig 11 :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*, Fig 11 :69 *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*).

**Refuter:** a case where adding a translation actor changed no ground contact at any depth of the cascade for any slot.

### F10: Verdicts stay per slot; no universal direction (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Translation can widen circulation for one claim (a method crosses into a frame that can pay for it) and narrow it for another (a recording crosses into a frame that cannot discriminate the source). The two verdicts are not averaged. A count of translations is a filter for where to look, never a witness for what happened to the idea.

**Refuter:** a slot-indexed measurement in which a single translation change moves every idea and every receiving closure in the same direction with the same magnitude against a non-updating ground.

### F11: The transform link has no instrument (single-source on the figure, corroborated by the world)

Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 6 :33 *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."*

The link between source settlement and target composition is where cross-language chains usually break, because it has neither the obvious tests of perception nor those of planning. What exists already is a reading: a paired source and target term that fails to resolve against the source ground. The instrument is built from that, not from a description of what a perfect translation would be.

**Refuter:** an existing, routinely used instrument that measures the round-trip gap for scientific claims across language pairs and is tied to a non-updating ground.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. **Reversibility.** A paired translation returns the source exactly on back-translation, and the receiving frame reproduces the same ground-paid predictions, with the gap measured at zero (breaks F1).
2. **Recording suffices.** A translated conclusion with no derivation or refuter reliably generates new settled terms in the receiving frame across several slots (breaks F2).
3. **No import needed.** A receiving frame derives an absent atom from its own atoms with no import and reaches the same ground-paid predictions (breaks F3).
4. **Shared pool changes the world.** Two frames demonstrably sharing one evidence pool produce a verdict change at the ground (breaks F7).
5. **Uniform effect.** A slot-indexed study shows one translation effect of the same sign and magnitude across all ideas and closures against a non-updating ground (breaks F10).

Also refused: any claim that translation is always harmful or always beneficial. A single measured case at the ground settles that in either direction, and the figures do not license a universal sign.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Replace "how has translation changed circulation" with the per-slot question: what crossed, what was imported, what is the round-trip gap, and what pays.
2. Lead with the gap and the refuter; offer the theory only after a recipient records a verdict on stage 1.
3. Treat shared technical vocabulary as a convention and a success, not as corroboration of a world claim (Fig 2 :41).
4. Enumerate the habitat before naming a culprit. Do not pick the actor first (Fig 8 :53 *"Residual first, frame second."*).
5. Report unreachable as a verdict with an instrument owed, not as silence and not as an invented equivalent.
