# The Tether's answer: translation and the circulation of scientific ideas

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A claim was settled against a ground in one language. It crosses into another language and circulates there. The question asks what translation and language difference did to that circulation. The residual is the gap between what the source claim predicts at its ground and what the receiving frame reconstructs and can build on. It is indexed per claim, per receiving closure and per channel (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). It is a union of overlapping conditions, not one effect with a sign (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).

## 2. The shape

1. **Translation is a typed, lossy transform, not a copy.** Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :28 *"But it means the trip is not reversible"* The receiving text can preserve the ingredient list while dropping the bond to the ground and the derivation. Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* The round-trip gap is the measurement, not an error to hide (Fig 4 :31 *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*).

2. **What crosses usefully is a method or a head start, not a recording.** Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A translated conclusion with no derivation gives the target frame a description of one occasion; a translated argument with its refuter gives it something it can build on (Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*).

3. **A missing atom needs an import, not more copies.** Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Where the receiving language has no term, recombining its words cannot produce the concept. An import with provenance and a shadow test is owed; the honest report is a gap in effect terms (Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).

4. **Source and target versions are isomers, not spellings.** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Over a shared conceptual atom set, a changed bond to the ground or to the derivation changes the substance. Where only a scaffold is shared, they are members of a functional group (Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*). Du Châtelet's added derivation and Yan Fu's *tianze* are world instances of changed bonds.

5. **The ground does not decay; the channel does.** Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Stale terms, saturated words and pooled readings are channel failures. Adding copies amplifies the same channel. The repair is to keep the anchor reachable (Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*).

6. **Multilingual agreement is not corroboration when the pool is shared.** Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Ten editions of one source are one witness. That is fine for a shared technical vocabulary, which is a convention and a success (Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*). It is not evidence that the claim became truer by crossing.

7. **Chains of translations manufacture consensus.** Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Progress requires the next room to differ and to be chosen against the residual, not merely to be another translation of the last.

8. **Verdicts are per slot and never averaged.** Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Translation can widen circulation for one claim and narrow it for another. Counts of translations are filters for where to look, not witnesses for what happened (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that translation always distorts. Fig 4 :31 calls the gap a measurement, and Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Loss at the transform and surplus at a union of differing closures are different quantities.
- Not that untranslatable means impossible. It means an import or a better instrument is owed at this budget (Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
- Not that a translated term is wrong because a word does not match. The bond and the round-trip gap decide, not lexical fidelity.
- Not that the framework names which translated term wins. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
- Not `grounded`. No paired, ground-tied measurement was taken in this run.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved it. Here:

- **Round-trip measurement.** Translate a claim into a target language and back. Does the returned claim reproduce the source's ground-paid predictions? Measure the gap; do not assume it.
- **Source-discrimination.** For a target term with several plausible sources, find a reading that distinguishes them. Naming a source from the term alone is the metamerism error.
- **Method versus recording.** Compare a target frame given only the translated conclusion with one given the translated derivation and refuter. Which produces new settled terms against the ground?
- **Independence check.** Measure the overlap between the language frames. If they share one evidence pool, their agreement is a convention, not a verdict (Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*).
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` §5: reversibility at zero gap, a recording that suffices, no import needed, a shared pool changing the world verdict, or a uniform slot-indexed effect.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The mapping from a figure's cross-kind composition to a language boundary is a structural binding from the framework, provenanced as such. The particular language pairs and claims were not measured. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* No amount of multilingual agreement substitutes for that vote.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not say that translation is good or bad for science. They say that a claim crossing a language boundary undergoes a typed, lossy transform whose round-trip gap must be measured rather than assumed; that what crosses usefully is a method or a head start, not a recording of one conclusion; that a receiving language lacking an atom needs an import with provenance rather than more copies; and that source and target versions over a shared set of ideas are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, not two spellings of one thing. The ground does not decay but the channel does, so ten translations of one source are one witness and a multilingual consensus can be a convention rather than corroboration. What is offered is the shape any ground-paid study of a language pair must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. The verdict for any particular idea stays open until the ground pays.
