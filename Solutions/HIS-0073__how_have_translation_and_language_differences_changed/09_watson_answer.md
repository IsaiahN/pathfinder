## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a claim that was settled against a ground in one language and then circulates in another. Option A: translation is a neutral copy, so language difference is friction and more translation means more of the same idea. Option B: translation is a typed, lossy transform, so the receiving closure and its ground decide what the idea becomes. What each leads to: A invests in more copies and treats cross-language disagreement as error to be erased; B measures the round-trip gap per slot, requires provenance for imports, treats shared vocabulary as a convention rather than corroboration, and reports unreachable as a verdict with an instrument owed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a source-language claim paid for at its ground and what a receiving language frame reconstructs and can build on. Arity: at least three, source claim, channel, receiving closure. Symmetry: asymmetric and non-invertible, so the round trip does not restore the source. Scale: first order inside one frame, second order at the boundary, third order along the chain. Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Domain 1, source frame.** Actors: original authors, instruments, source terminology. Currency: settlement against the ground and composition inside `closure(Γ_source)`. Anchor: the experiment or measurement that paid for the claim. Type: partition boundary.
- **Domain 2, transform and channel.** Actors: translator, dictionary, terminology bureau, commentary, printer, machine system. Currency: the round-trip gap, bond choice, description length. Anchor: source and target readings against the source ground. Type: typed operator, not a frame with its own ground.
- **Domain 3, target frame.** Actors: receiving readers, their instruments and terminology, their schools. Currency: what composes in `closure(Γ_target)`, admitted imports, methods versus recordings. Anchor: the target community's own ground contact, where it has one. Type: import and closure question.
- **Domain 4, circulation habitat.** Actors: journals, academies, reviewers, indexers, funders, translation bureaux. Currency: agreement versus evidence, union surplus. Anchor: the ground, which does not update on citation. Type: union of overlapping conditions.
- **Order by dependency.** Settle the source claim before translating it; measure the transform before judging target composition; then read the chain. Describe the effect before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53 *"Residual first, frame second."*).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a universal direction does not. A scientific claim crossing a language boundary undergoes a typed, lossy transform with a measurable round-trip gap; what crosses usefully is a method or a head start rather than a recording of one conclusion; an absent atom needs an import with provenance, and the gap is reported as a verdict rather than silence; source and target versions over a shared atom set are isomers, not spellings; the ground does not decay but the channel does; multilingual agreement from one evidence pool is a convention, not corroboration; translation chains compound agreement, not evidence; and every verdict is per slot and never averaged. The specific claim that translation always helps or always harms circulation is refused. The binding of "scale" in Fig 4 to "language frame" is a structural mapping offered by this solution, not a figure line, and is the first thing an outside check may refuse. This is `specified` on the domain claims, `general` on the shape, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Each claim carries its own refuter, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished conclusion instead of a slot and its refuter. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the five falsifiers.
- **undoable:** yes. The scale-to-language binding can be withdrawn without touching the per-slot findings; the isomer reading falls back to the functional-group reading if the shared atom set is not established; and a superseded packet can replace a sent one. No primitive has to be removed.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A paired translation study with a measured zero round-trip gap against a non-updating ground would break the lossy-transform claim; a recording that generates new settled terms would break the method claim; a target frame deriving an absent atom with no import would break the closure claim; two low-overlap frames whose agreement changes the ground verdict would break the shared-pool rule; and a slot-indexed study with a uniform effect would break the per-slot rule. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
