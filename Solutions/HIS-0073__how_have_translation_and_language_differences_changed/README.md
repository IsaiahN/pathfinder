## Restating the question as an effect

The ordinary wording asks for one effect of translation on circulation. The residual is not one effect. It is the gap between what a claim paid for against a ground in its source language and what a receiving language frame reconstructs and can build on after the claim crosses the boundary. That gap must be indexed per claim, per receiving closure and per channel (Fig 1 :19, :22). Option A treats translation as a neutral copy, so language difference is friction to be removed and more copies mean more of the same idea. Option B treats translation as a typed, lossy transform with a measurable round-trip gap, so more copies do not necessarily mean more of the same idea, and the receiving frame's own atoms and ground decide what the idea becomes.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: for each idea-slot, a characterisation of what crossed (a method or a recording), what had to be imported into the receiving closure, what the round-trip gap was, and which ground paid for the result. That bound shape is the sought-for term. It is written down here so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as anything else (THE_FORMULA.md, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*; Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
