## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule: the framework returns "*what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)*." Fig 9 :32 "*you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself*."

For this question, the shape is **not** a list of named translations that changed science, and **not** a verdict that translation is good or bad. It is the constrained form that any cross-language account of a scientific claim must have: a typed transform between closures, a non-zero round-trip gap to be measured, a distinction between methods and recordings, an import condition when an atom is absent, a non-updating ground, and per-slot verdicts. `SPACE(???)TIME` in the exemplar is the analogous shape. Here the analogous notation is `SOURCE CLAIM -> (???) -> TARGET CLAIM`, with the `(???)` standing for the measured transform rather than an invented mechanism.

From *The Library of Babel* (carried): *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a shape narrows; it does not fill. It can be stated before the particular translated term is known.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried): use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30 *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- Fig 9 :30 *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*

Applied here: describe the effect (a claim that paid against a source ground fails to pay against the same ground after a round trip), then look for the frame that predicts that shape (a lossy transform with a bond the ingredient list does not record), rather than picking the frame called "translation" and reading everything into it.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A paired translation study tied to a non-updating ground would name the slot.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51).

*They're Scanning Books* (carried): *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

A list of "key translated terms" is a visible choice, not nature's list. Naming one as *the* reason an idea circulated turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Source discrimination is the governing essay here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* was opened directly. It gives the exact mechanism this question needs:

> *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*

The spectrometer example: an instrument reads green and *"it cannot comment on the source"*; the source could be one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The paragraph concludes that the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists, and that this was shown *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied to translation: a target-language term is a reading of a source claim. It does not comment on the source. Several different source structures can produce the same translated term. Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error of `01` F5. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (a paired reading with the ground), not a more confident glossary.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming translated terms?

**No, narrowed.** The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in `Γ`. A translated term such as *tianze* or du Châtelet's French rendering is an atom already available in the receiving frame's library, or an import from the world frame. Naming it is a provenanced binding, not the invention of a new primitive.

What remains forbidden: claiming the framework selected it. Fig 13 :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 13 :129 *"It is recoverable from the lineage."* The lineage of the binding is part of the term (Fig 13 :130 *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."*), which is why a translation without provenance is not the same term even when the words look right.

## 6. Prestige is not action

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* was opened directly. Its governing statement:

> *"Prestige must* never *be allowed to validate an action* directly*."*

And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

For this question, the prestigious translation, the famous translator, and the highly cited edition can fund a translation project. They cannot settle whether the idea changed when it crossed. That requires the ground. A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that "translation mattered" is the named collapse, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 7. This whole folder is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay, opened directly:

> *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too"*

and

> *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01`, `06`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not a test of the claim and cannot settle anything they report on. Mode is `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, never `grounded`.

## 8. Consequence for the answer

The honest answer to "how have translation and language differences changed circulation" is a shape plus a method:

- lossy transform with a measurable round-trip gap, not a neutral copy;
- method or head start crosses, a recording does not;
- a missing atom needs an import with provenance, not more copies;
- source and target versions over a shared atom set are isomers, not spellings;
- the ground does not decay, the channel does;
- multilingual agreement from one pool is a convention, not a verdict;
- translation chains compound agreement, not evidence;
- every verdict is per slot and never averaged.

That shape is what any ground-paid study of a particular language pair would have to satisfy. It is not the particular answer for any particular pair. That answer is owed, and the instrument that would give it is described in `07` and `08`.
