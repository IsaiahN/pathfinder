## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone before using the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The gap between a source-language claim's ground contact and what a receiving frame reconstructs. Arity three or more: source claim, channel, receiving closure. Symmetry: non-invertible, so the round trip is not the identity. Scale: within a frame, across the boundary, along the chain. Union, not partition: loss, absent atoms, channel decay and habitat substitution overlap in one case.
- **Domain 1, source frame.** Actors: original authors and instruments. Currency: settlement at the ground. Anchor: the experiment that paid for the claim. Partition boundary.
- **Domain 2, transform.** Actors: translator, dictionary, terminology bureau. Currency: round-trip gap and bond choice. Anchor: paired source and target readings against the source ground. Typed operator, not a frame with its own ground.
- **Domain 3, target frame.** Actors: receiving readers and their instruments. Currency: what composes in its closure, admitted imports. Anchor: its ground contact where nameable. Import and closure question.
- **Domain 4, habitat.** Actors: journals, academies, reviewers, funders. Currency: agreement versus evidence. Anchor: the ground, which does not update on citation. Union of overlapping conditions.

Order by dependency: reading first, then vocabulary and import, then objective, then instrument, then carry as a method (Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*). Residual before frame (Fig 8 :53 *"Residual first, frame second."*).

**Comparison.** My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x).** The verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :27-31 (lossy transform, round trip), Fig 6 :21-23 (closure and import), Fig 12 :5 and :117 (bond and isomer), Fig 2 :40 (shared pool).
2. **Abstraction (T_A).** The shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: translation is a lossy typed transform; methods cross, recordings do not; missing atoms need import; versions are isomers; the ground does not decay; shared pools do not corroborate; verdicts are per slot.
3. **Concretisation (T_E).** The concrete claim about a particular language pair: that a named translated term caused a particular idea to circulate, that a particular edition preserved the original theory, or that a multilingual consensus confirms a claim.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **scale-to-language binding**. Fig 4 speaks of scales and readings, not of natural languages. The mapping is this solution's structural binding, not a figure line. `09` declares it; the concretisation in step 3 does not.
- the **bond type**. Fig 12 says the bond carries the missing information, but nothing in the figures says which bond holds between a translated term and its ground in a given language pair. That needs a reading.
- the **source discrimination**. The metamerism lesson says one reading has several possible sources. The concretisation names a source from a term, which is exactly the error the essay warns against.
- the **union condition**. Fig 8 :45 requires differing closures for a surplus. A concrete claim that translation helped because it produced more editions has not measured closure overlap, so it cannot claim a surplus.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a transform that loses information has a measurable gap, and what crosses as a method is not the same as what crosses as a recording. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no paired study was run; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the figures are the same thing in fewer words" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
