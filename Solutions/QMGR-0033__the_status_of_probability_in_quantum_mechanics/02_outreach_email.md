**Subject:** One checkable shape claim about quantum probability, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** The Born rule predicts stable detector frequencies. The dispute about whether quantum probability is fundamental or epistemic does not shrink with effort: each side works well on a different subset of the data. Under the rule that a disagreement which does not shrink is several well-formed questions rather than one hard one, the binary has to be split before it is searched.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The framework has two structurally distinct roles for probability. One is epistemic: a belief `b`, mixed with an inherited prior by a weight `w`, explicitly *"not the observation"*, and frame-internal, so a frame cannot score itself with the quantity it produces. The other is ontic-looking: randomness is a remainder that re-enters the next turn of the loop, compounding into chaos and reappearing as noise. The framework records genuine indeterminacy as *"the open case, unresolved."* It does not name either role as the one true status of physical probability, because it returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What is settled and what is not.** The counter is a real anchor: frequencies are settled. But aggregating across slots is how a live per-event signal disappears into an average. So the same frequencies are compatible with an objective-chance reading and with an ignorance reading. Bell type tests and the PBR theorem constrain real sub-questions, local hidden variables and ψ-epistemic models under preparation independence. They do not, by themselves, settle the binary.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that quantum probability *is* fundamental, that it *is* merely epistemic, that the wavefunction is knowledge, that collapse is real, or that any interpretation has been refuted by the figures. Those are bindings from a physics frame onto terms in a different vocabulary. Until provenance and a ground reading are shown, they carry zero evidentiary weight here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an instrument whose readings differ between an objective-chance model and an ignorance model while agreeing on all Born frequencies, demonstrated and run;
> 2. a derivation of one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or a required seventh primitive;
> 3. a demonstration that `b` or `w` is not frame-internal, i.e. that a frame can settle its own probability status without ground contact;
> 4. a derivation of the loop's randomness from a single primitive with no crossing;
> 5. a barrier theorem proving observational equivalence of the two readings for every possible instrument (this would change the verdict from unreached to underivable, not confirm it).
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract or re-census. A one-line *"this fails because..."* is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README and the formula: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
