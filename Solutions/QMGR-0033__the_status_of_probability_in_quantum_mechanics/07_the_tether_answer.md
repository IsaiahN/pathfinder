# The Tether's answer on the status of quantum probability

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The Born rule predicts stable frequencies. The dispute over whether probability is fundamental or epistemic does not shrink with effort, and each side works on a different subset of the data. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The binary is a union compound, not a single question (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape

1. **The question as posed is refused.** It asks for one status where there are several unbundled conditions: settled frequencies, a settled predictor, an unsettled interpretation of state, an unsettled question of single-case chance, and a structural account of randomness. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

2. **The ground settles the frequencies, not the source.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The counter is that anchor for frequencies. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The live per-event signal is discarded by the very aggregate that confirms the Born rule. Both readings fit the same aggregate.

3. **The epistemic side is a controller role.** Symbols table :20-21: *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* So an epistemic reading of probability in this framework is `b` and `w`, and a frame cannot settle its own status with them. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

4. **The ontic-looking side is a structural remainder, and the framework keeps the question open.** Fig 13 :28: *"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."* Fig 13 :29-32: *"because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Fig 13 :144: *"genuine indeterminacy is the open case, unresolved."* Randomness is a shape derived from composition, not a name for objective chance.

5. **The two readings are isomers, not synonyms.** Over the shared atom set `{state, observation, residual, ground, instrument, slot}` they satisfy Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The Born frequency formula cannot tell them apart; the instruments they point to can. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

6. **A source-discriminating instrument is what is owed.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The framework cannot name the status; it returns the shape any candidate answer must have and the falsifiers written in advance.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that probability is fundamental. No ground reading settles single-case chance here.
- Not that probability is merely epistemic. `b` and `w` are controller terms, and the framework's own rule disqualifies a frame scoring itself with them.
- Not that the wavefunction is knowledge or that collapse is real. Those are bindings from the physics frame, provenanced as such and unproven here.
- Not that Bell's theorem or the PBR theorem settles the question. They constrain narrow sub-questions and are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not `grounded`. The only grounded items are the frequencies and the Born rule as predictor.

## 4. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the test is:

- **Discriminator Test:** an instrument whose readings differ between an objective-chance model and an ignorance model while agreeing on all Born frequencies. Running it would name the status.
- **Substrate Test:** derive one of the six from the other five, or show a required seventh primitive. That breaks the substrate.
- **Controller Test:** show that `b` or `w` is not frame-internal, i.e. that a frame settles its own probability status without ground contact.
- **Remainder Test:** derive the loop's randomness from a single primitive with no crossing, or show the framework no longer records indeterminacy as unresolved.
- **Barrier Test:** prove observational equivalence of the two readings for every possible instrument. This changes the verdict from `unreached` to `underivable`, a different fact with a different instruction (Fig 8 :40-43).

Standing falsifiers are written before the conclusion in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, and not a silence.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"Being 'below the union horizon' is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet."* The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not decide whether quantum probability is fundamental or epistemic. It says the question is badly posed: the counter settles frequencies but not the source of a single event, and averaging across slots discards the live signal that a single-event reading would need. In the framework, the epistemic side is a controller quantity, belief `b` mixed with an inherited prior by `w`, explicitly not the observation and frame-internal, so a frame cannot settle its own status with it. The ontic-looking side is randomness as a remainder that re-enters the next turn of the loop, compounding into chaos and reappearing as noise, and the framework records genuine indeterminacy as unresolved. Over a shared atom set the two readings are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, invisible in the Born formula and decisive in which instrument would separate them. What the derivation offers is the shape any answer must have, the unbundled sub-questions, and refuters written in advance. The status stays open until a source-discriminating instrument runs.
