## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is quantum probability fundamental or epistemic" is a union compound that must be unbundled before it is answered. The counter is an anchor for frequencies, but averaging across object slots discards the live single-event signal, so the same Born frequencies are compatible with both readings. In the framework the epistemic side is a controller quantity, belief `b` mixed with an inherited prior by weight `w`, explicitly not the observation and frame-internal, so a frame cannot score itself with the probability it produces. The ontic-looking side is randomness as a remainder that re-enters the next turn of the loop, compounding into chaos and reappearing as noise, and the framework records genuine indeterminacy as unresolved. Over a shared atom set the two readings are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, invisible in the Born frequency formula and decisive in the instrument that would separate them. The framework does not name the physical status; it returns the shape any candidate answer must have, with refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2, 9 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound; the ground settles frequencies but not the single-event source; the epistemic role is frame-internal and is not the observation.

- **Refuter:** show that the five unbundled conditions are one condition under a single predicate; or show that an aggregate frequency retains the live single-event signal that a source discriminator needs; or show that `b` or `w` is not frame-internal.

### C2: physics-facing; checkable by a physicist or philosopher of physics against QM and the no-go results

- **Statement:** the Born rule is settled as a predictor and not as an interpretation. Bell type tests and the PBR theorem constrain narrow sub-questions (local hidden variables; ψ-epistemic models with preparation independence) but do not settle the ontic/epistemic binary. The readings are observationally equivalent at the frequency level.
- **What to check:** is that a faithful reading of Bell and PBR? The SEP Bell entry states the theorem is *"not a no-go theorem for hidden-variables theories tout court"* and that de Broglie-Bohm is a functioning hidden-variables theory. The PBR abstract conditions its conclusion on independently prepared systems having independent physical states. Does either result discriminate the single-event ontic/epistemic binary, or only a narrower assumption?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a demonstrated instrument whose readings differ between an objective-chance model and an ignorance model while agreeing on all Born frequencies. That settles the question and breaks the `gap`.

### C3: framework-internal derivation, with a named type strain

- **Statement:** randomness in the loop is a structural remainder, and the framework explicitly records indeterminacy as unresolved. The two readings are isomers over a shared atom set; their bond is a comparison with operands that are arrangements rather than bare quantities.
- **Source, verbatim:**

The operand type of `⋛` at this level is an **open strain**, not a correction; an instrument is needed, not an argument.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that probability is fundamental, and not that it is merely epistemic. No single-event ground reading is available here.
- Not that the wavefunction is knowledge, that collapse is real, or that any interpretation has been refuted by the figures.
- Not that Bell or PBR settles the binary. They are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a derivation of one interpretation from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. The only grounded items are the frequencies and the Born rule as a predictor.
- Not a claim that the framework can name the status. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Discriminator Test:** readings that differ between an objective-chance model and an ignorance model while agreeing on all Born frequencies.
2. **Substrate Test:** derive one of the six from the other five, or show a required seventh primitive.
3. **Controller Test:** show `b` or `w` is not frame-internal.
4. **Remainder Test:** derive the loop's randomness from a single primitive with no crossing, or show indeterminacy is no longer recorded as unresolved.
5. **Barrier Test:** prove observational equivalence for every possible instrument (moves the verdict to `underivable`, not to a grounded answer).
