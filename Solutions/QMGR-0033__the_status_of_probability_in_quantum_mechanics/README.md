## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the framework settles whether probability is fundamental or epistemic.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies the verdict (Fig 2 :40).
2. **The ground decides.** Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*. Prestige or consensus cannot stand in for a source-discriminating instrument.
3. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Naming probability as fundamental or epistemic is outside what the derivation claims it can do.
