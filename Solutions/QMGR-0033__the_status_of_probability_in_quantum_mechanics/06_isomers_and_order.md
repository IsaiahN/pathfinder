# Are the two readings of probability isomers? Order and structure

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The frequency data underdetermine the ontic and epistemic readings of a single QM event. Two questions are owed: (1) are those two readings **isomers** over a shared atom set under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what is the dependency order among the single-event residual, the aggregate frequency and the interpretation? A wrong order would explain why the debate does not shrink.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Do the two readings share atoms?

- Both readings require a **state**, an **observation**, a **gap between prediction and observation**, and a **ground**. Symbols table :38-40: *"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*
- The epistemic reading puts the gap in the controller: `b` and `w` (Symbols :20-21), frame-internal (Fig 1 :27).
- The ontic-looking reading puts the gap in the crossing: the remainder that re-enters the next turn (Fig 13 :29-32).
- Shared atom set: `{state, observation, residual, ground, instrument, slot}`. Different arrangement: the residual is bonded to the controller on one side and to the crossing on the other.

So the formal structure supports an isomer claim, not a synonym claim.

### Q2: Are they isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :46-47: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."** *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*
- The Born frequency formula is the same for both readings. The difference is invisible in that formula, exactly as Fig 12 :117 describes. It is decisive in behaviour: the two readings point at different instruments (one looks for a controller update, the other for a source property of the system).
- ⇒ Over the shared atom set the two readings are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same atoms, different bond arrangement, different substance.
- Weaker form: they share a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The shared group is `state / residual / observation`; the qualifier is where the residual lives.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either reading can be written with the same atoms *and the same bond arrangement*, the difference is a relabeling and they are one frame with two names (Fig 8 :50-52), which kills the isomer claim. If they share no atom at all, they are unrelated frames, which also kills it.

### Q3: What is the bond between them?

- They are not joined by `+` (conjunction: both hold at once). The dispute is about which arrangement governs.
- They are not joined by `≡` (identity: two names, one referent). If they were identical, there would be no dispute and no need for a discriminator.
- The appropriate relation is `⋛` comparison: Operators table :48-50: *"comparison / a threshold relation between two quantities"*. But the operands here are arrangements, not bare quantities. This is a **type strain**, named, not hidden, and it needs an instrument rather than an argument. Same strain as exemplar `06` Q4; it is not resolved here.
- A refusal (`¬`) is also owed: the binary question "fundamental OR epistemic" must be refused as a badly posed partition before any comparison is minted (formula step 2: REFUTED owes a refusal).

### Q4: What order do the pieces actually arrive in?

- Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The single-event residual arrives first, per slot.
- Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The aggregate frequency arrives second, and in arriving it discards the live per-event signal.
- The interpretation is a third-order claim about what the first two mean. It arrives last, if at all.
- So the dependency order is: **single-event residual → aggregate frequency → interpretation**. This is Fig 3's dependency order applied to the question: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- The debate reverses this order. It starts with the interpretation (fundamental or epistemic) and then reads the data through it. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."*
- *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the same ordering in another field: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* An instrument is built from a worse one already returning something (Fig 6 :32), not from an interpretation.

### Q5: Does the union compose at a higher level?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*.
- The frequency claim is settled against the ground (D1). It can serve as an atom at the next level. The interpretation is not settled, so it cannot yet serve as an atom. Composition over an unsettled interpretation would be premature.
- ⇒ The only legal higher-level composition here is over the settled frequency claim. Any claim composed over "probability is fundamental" or "probability is epistemic" is built on an ungrounded term and is not composable yet.

## CONCLUSION

The two readings are **isomers over a shared atom set** `{state, observation, residual, ground, instrument, slot}`: same atoms, different bond arrangement, difference invisible in the Born frequency formula and decisive in which instrument would settle them. The weaker functional-group claim also holds. The bond between them is a comparison with a type strain, and the binary question itself owes a refusal before comparison. The correct order is single-event residual, then aggregate frequency, then interpretation; the debate runs the order backwards. Only the settled frequency claim may serve as an atom for higher composition.

This rescues what is checkable in the question without answering it: it gives a structural relation between the two readings and a dependency order that explains why further argument inside either camp does not shrink the disagreement. It does not name either reading as the true status of quantum probability.

## Falsifier (pre-registered)

1. Same atoms and same bond arrangement for both readings, so the difference is only a label (kills the isomer claim).
2. No shared atom between the readings (kills the isomer claim outright).
3. An instrument whose readings discriminate the two arrangements at a single event (settles the question and replaces the `gap` with a grounded verdict).
4. A proof that the aggregate frequency is not "a live signal lost to averaging" for this case (breaks the Fig 1 :22 ordering claim).
