## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the residual

1. **SEP Interpretations of Probability** distinguishes three concepts of probability and says the calculus is *"silent regarding the initial assignment of probabilities."* This corroborates F1: the formal rule does not by itself decide the status. It does not decide it either. Cite it as secondary.
2. **SEP Chance versus Randomness** separates chance from randomness and reports that *"There is no agreement over which, if any, of these accounts are right."* It also says the evidence for chances is strongest where *"our best physical theories"* feature probabilities, while noting the Everettian program of deriving quantum probabilities from subjective uncertainty. This is an honest conflict in the world literature, not a reason to flip the figure verdict.
3. **Pusey, Barrett and Rudolph** and **Bell** are real ground constraints. They narrow which epistemic and which local models survive. They are filters on the space of interpretations (Fig 9 :60), not witnesses for the binary verdict. The SEP Bell entry explicitly warns that the theorem is *"not a no-go theorem for hidden-variables theories tout court"*, so it must not be overread.

2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the figure text, the canonical README and the three world sources. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
