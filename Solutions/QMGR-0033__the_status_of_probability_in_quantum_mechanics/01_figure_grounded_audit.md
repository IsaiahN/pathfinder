## 0. The residual in effect terms

**Question as given:** Is probability in quantum mechanics fundamental or epistemic?

**Restated as an effect, not a theory:** Given that the Born rule predicts stable detector frequencies, what would have to be true for probability to be (A) a feature of the world itself, something no further information could remove, or (B) a feature of an observer's state of knowledge, something a more complete specification would in principle remove? What each option leads to:

- **A, fundamental / ontic.** Probability is a law about objective single-case chance. The state transition is genuinely non-deterministic (collapse) or branch weights are real. No further information can remove the randomness.
- **B, epistemic.** Probability measures ignorance. The state represents knowledge; a complete hidden specification would remove the randomness; measurement reveals a pre-existing value.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** An answer would be a statement of the form *"probability in QM has status S, and S is settled by instrument I"*. Binding both halves now makes the sought-for shape refusable: if no I can be named, the first half must not be asserted. This is the pre-registered discipline required by Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"* (the full figure line continues *"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else"*).

**Immediate structural reading.** The question looks like a binary partition. Fig 9 :4-5 warns: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The ontic and epistemic camps each work well on a different subset (interference and no-hidden-variable results on one side; the observer-relative character of the state and the success of Bayesian state assignment on the other), and the dispute does not shrink. So the binary is first treated as a **union compound**, not a single hard question (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least three conditions, not two. (i) long-run detector frequencies, (ii) the formal Born rule that predicts them, (iii) the interpretation of state and randomness. A fourth condition, the source of a single event, is only reachable through an instrument. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **Symmetry:** the epistemic reading is agent-relative (it lives in the controller, `b` and `w`); the fundamental reading is claimed to be frame-independent (a property of the ground). These are not symmetric frames; they differ in what they can express. A symmetric swap of the two labels is not a symmetry of the problem.
- **Scale:** the single-event residual is first order (one slot, one bet, one observation); the aggregate frequency is a second-order composition over many slots; the interpretation is a third-order claim about what the first two mean. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

No nameable anchor means it is not a settling domain yet. D3 fails that test. D1, D2 and D4 pass. The framework's rule is that an anchor which moves with a frame is a frame (Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."*).

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. The unbundled parts are:

1. Are Born frequencies stable and lawlike? (D1, settled.)
2. Does the Born rule predict them? (D2, settled.)
3. Does the state represent reality or knowledge? (D3, constrained, not settled.)
4. Is there objective single-case chance? (D3, not settled here.)
5. Where does randomness sit in the loop? (D4, structurally described.)

These do not compose into one answer. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever over the whole bundle clears the regions where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56).

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

D1 before D2 before D3. D4 is a re-description of D1 to D3 in the framework's terms, so it comes last. D3 sits above the break: Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Effort spent at D1 or D2 cannot rescue a missing instrument at D3. Refuse before mint (formula step 2); settle a term before composing over it (Fig 12 :104).

## 3. Findings

### F1: The binary is a union compound, not a partition (governing)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

**World corroboration, secondary.** *Interpretations of Probability* (Hájek, SEP, first published 2002, revised 2023) says there are *"arguably three main concepts of probability"*: an epistemological concept, an agent's degree of confidence, and a physical concept independent of what anyone thinks. *Chance versus Randomness* (Eagle, SEP, 2010/2018) reports Carnap's two conceptions: *"probability_1 corresponds to an epistemic notion"* and *"probability_2, which is the concept of a non-epistemic objective kind of probability, better known as chance."* Two independent scholarly sources (low overlap with each other) corroborate that the binary undercounts the concepts. They do not decide anything; the figures do.

**Triangulation:** Fig 9 and Fig 2 (two low-overlap figures) both yield "split, do not average". `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that all five unbundled conditions are the same condition under one predicate, such that answering one answers all, would break the union reading and restore the binary.

### F2: Epistemic probability is frame-internal in the framework's own terms

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Symbols table :20-21: *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."*

Symbols table :24-26: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."*

The framework's own epistemic quantity is `b`, explicitly **not the observation**, and its mixing weight `w` is learned from past weightings. So the epistemic reading of probability in the Tether is a controller quantity. A frame cannot settle its own epistemic status with the quantity it produces. Promotion by declaration is refused: Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

**Triangulation:** Fig 1 (agent figure) and the Symbols table (notation) are different figures with different contents; both say frame-internal / not the observation. `triangulated`, with the caveat that they share an author and an evidence pool (declared in `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter:** show that `b` or `w` is not frame-internal, i.e., that a frame can score itself with the probability it produces without any ground contact. That breaks Fig 1 :27 and the Symbols entry together.

### F3: On the ontic side, randomness is a structural remainder, not a primitive, and the framework records the question as open

Fig 13 :28: *"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."*

Fig 13 :29-32: *"because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*

Fig 13 :144: *"genuine indeterminacy is the open case, unresolved."*

`THE_FORMULA.md`, substrate section: the derived quantities *"symmetry, smoothness, chaos, a continuum"* are *"what a turn leaves rather than members of the set."*

So the framework gives a **shape** for randomness: a remainder that re-enters the next turn, compounding into chaos and reappearing as noise. It does not name this as objective chance, and it explicitly records genuine indeterminacy as unresolved. Asserting *"probability is fundamental"* as a settled verdict would go beyond what the figure licenses.

The minimality table is consistent: Fig 13 :65 (*"no time-translation symmetry, and no finite operations to leave a remainder"*), :68 (*"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"*), :71 (*"no direction to drive the flow, and no remainder left after each step"*), :59 (*"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"*). Counting and remainder are load-bearing across the six terms, not a seventh attribute.

**Triangulation:** Fig 13 and `THE_FORMULA.md` substrate section; high overlap because the formula is a condensation of the figures. `single-source` at root, with a declared high-overlap restatement. The shape-not-a-name limit is separately triangulated with Fig 9 :32 and the canonical README.

**Refuter:** derive randomness from a single primitive with no crossing, or derive one of the six from the other five (breaking indivisibility, Fig 13 :41), or show that the framework no longer records indeterminacy as unresolved.

### F4: The ground settles frequencies, not the source of a single event

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The counter is a real anchor, but it returns an aggregate. Averaging across object slots is exactly how the live single-event signal disappears. So the experiment settles *"the frequencies obey the Born rule"* and does not settle *"why this event, rather than that one"*. Both the ontic and the epistemic readings predict the same aggregate, so neither is confirmed by it.

**World corroboration.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (Isaiah N, 2026-08-28): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* *How Intelligence Actually Works* (2026-09-16): *"The projection of green can't tell you whether it was RGB, CMYK or something else that composed it."* This is the metamerism point: a reading cannot comment on its source.

**Triangulation:** Fig 2 and Fig 1, two figures with different subjects, both give "anchor yes, but aggregate only". `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** an instrument whose readings differ between an ontic-chance model and an ignorance model while agreeing on all Born frequencies would settle the single-event source and break the `gap`.

### F5: No frame can certify the status from inside; the interpretation debate is a room chain

Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."*

Fig 7 :13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :31-32: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*

Every interpretation of QM reads the same Born-frequency reports. Their agreement or disagreement is the pool talking, not corroboration. Passing the debate from one forum to another adds vocabulary and no anchor. This is why the binary verdict is `gap`, not `unreached-because-nobody-tried-hard-enough`.

**Triangulation:** Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig 2, three distinct figures. `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** an interpretation that makes a novel, tested, source-discriminating prediction, rather than re-describing the same data, would leave the room and face the ground. That would break the room-chain finding.

### F6: What would discriminate the readings is an instrument, not a further argument

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*

Fig 6 :26: *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."*

Fig 9 :16: *"the edge can only be named from beyond it"*

The honest request is for a worse instrument already returning something that fails to resolve. Bell-type correlation tests and the PBR theorem are the closest real examples in the world literature: they constrain parts of the space (local hidden variables; ψ-epistemic models with preparation independence) but they discriminate locality and state reality, not the single-event ontic/epistemic binary itself. They are filters on the budget of possible interpretations, never witnesses for the binary verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

**Triangulation:** Fig 6 and Fig 9 (two distinct figures) plus the world sources as secondary. `triangulated` for the instrument requirement; `single-source` for each specific world theorem.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument already returning resolution-limited readings that separate the two readings, or show that none can exist in principle (which would move the verdict from `unreached` to `underivable`, a different fact, Fig 8 :40-43).

### F7: The stop is `unreached`, not silence, and not underivable

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*

Fig 8 :40-43: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."*

Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

The derivation has not found a discriminator. It also has not proved none exists. The report is `unreached` at the available instrument set, with the lineage recorded. Asserting either "fundamental" or "epistemic" as the settled answer would convert an unreached stop into a false verdict.

**Triangulation:** Fig 8 and Fig 13, two distinct figures. `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** a barrier theorem proving observational equivalence for every possible instrument would convert `unreached` to `underivable`. That is a different verdict and would be recorded as such, not treated as a confirmation.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Fig 5 :65-66 makes the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual; the exemplar notes the seat-map rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Source discriminator.** An instrument whose readings differ between an ontic-chance model and an ignorance model, while agreeing on all Born frequencies, is demonstrated and run. This settles D3 and reverses the `gap`.
2. **Substrate break.** One of the six terms is derived from the other five, or a seventh primitive is shown to be required and is imported with a source gate and a shadow test (breaks Fig 13 indivisibility and the Fig 8 import rule).
3. **Controller break.** `b` or `w` is shown not to be frame-internal, i.e., a frame settles its own probability status without ground contact (breaks Fig 1 :27).
4. **Remainder break.** Randomness is derived from a single primitive with no crossing, or the framework ceases to record indeterminacy as unresolved (breaks Fig 13 :28-32 and :144).
5. **Barrier theorem.** Observational equivalence of the two readings is proved for every possible instrument. This does not refute the shape; it changes the verdict from `unreached` to `underivable` and issues the opposite instruction (Fig 8 :40-43).

If 1 to 4 fires, the derivation is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the question one question?** No. Fig 9 :4-5 splits it; SEP gives three concepts. Union compound.
2. **What does the counter settle?** Frequencies only. Fig 2 :29-30 and Fig 1 :22.
3. **Does the formalism settle the status?** No. The axioms are silent about initial assignment (SEP Interpretations of Probability); Fig 9 :32 returns a kind of thing, not the answer.
4. **Is the epistemic side nameable as the answer?** No. It is `b` and `w`, frame-internal, not the observation (Fig 1 :27, Symbols :20-21).
5. **Is the ontic side nameable as the answer?** No. Randomness is a remainder, and indeterminacy is explicitly unresolved (Fig 13 :28-32, :144).
6. **Can argument close D3?** No. A room chain on one evidence pool (Fig 7 :13, Fig 2 :40). Only a source-discriminating instrument could (Fig 6 :32-35).
7. **Route.** Route the binary to REFUTED-as-posed, then offer the unbundled shape and the pre-registered refuter. A refusal, not an explanation (formula step 2).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: probability in this framework has two structurally distinct roles, a frame-internal epistemic role and a structural ontic-looking role, neither of which is a name for the physical status; the ground settles frequencies and not the single-event source; an instrument, not an argument, would be required to close the gap. The binary "fundamental or epistemic" is refused as written. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, `candidate`. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**; one open item is the exact observational reach of Bell/PBR type constraints, which are recorded as filters, not as the verdict.
