# Expert verification packet - the Tether on wealth and political influence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a political scientist, political economist or institutional scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at a named institutional slot is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

In a named institutional slot, wealth becomes political influence when the thing that should settle the outcome stops being pinned and starts updating on wealth. It is not a smooth function of wealth size. Three conditions have to hold: the outcome moves under a wealth push (anchor failure); a resource crosses from the economic frame into the political frame and is paid for out of that frame's independence (import failure); and influence returns as wealth so the loop closes (feedback failure). Wealth survives its own use, so one bounded stock composes many influence molecules. Institutions prevent the conversion only while they remain conventions that constrain the frame rather than decide which outcome is returned, and because contact cannot be subtracted from a habitat, that prevention is permanent work rather than a solved state. The framework names no threshold, country or culprit; it returns a shape.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal but ground-facing; checkable by anyone who knows a named slot

- **Statement:** conversion is the event of an unpinned political outcome, not a larger quantity of wealth. With no anchor, frames update on each other and converge on whoever had the most influence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 - *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :13 - *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*; Fig 2 :15 - *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."*
- **What to check:** in a slot you know, can you name a recorded outcome that stays fixed when wealth varies, measured by something that itself does not move when wealth varies? If you can, that slot has not converted, whatever the correlations say.
- **Refuter:** the recorded outcome tracks wealth while an independent instrument confirms the putative anchor stayed pinned. If this is demonstrated, the claim is withdrawn for that slot.

### C2 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 10

- **Statement:** political authority is not composable from money inside the economic closure. Conversion requires an import across the frame boundary, and that import is debited against the political frame's independence. An institution that decides the outcome has ceased to be a convention, and declaring independence does not ground it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :24 - *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* Fig 8 :49 - *"completely are one frame wearing two names."* Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :49 - *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*
- **What to check:** is there a real contact path (donation, appointment, shared personnel, media ownership), and does the formal rule constrain the frame or determine the outcome? Does near-total overlap between the two frames leave any independence to debit?
- **Refuter:** a wealth holder changes a political outcome with no cross-frame contact and no import of wealth as a legitimate input; or a formal convention demonstrably decides the outcome yet remains a working anchor under independent measurement.

### C3 - structural analogue; checkable against the literature on the loop

- **Statement:** wealth is a catalyst and influence can regenerate wealth. The resulting cycle closes; a linear accountability chain cannot represent it. Institutional prevention is permanent work, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 11 :62 - *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- **What to check:** in a named slot, does influence return as economic advantage over repeated uses? Does a reform introduce new actors whose behaviour was not enumerated, and does it require maintenance to keep working?
- **Refuter:** a measured loop sign of zero or negative with the conversion still closing; or a one-time institutional change that permanently severs the conversion with no maintenance and no new actors introduced.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a dollar threshold, a country ranking, or a unique institutional culprit. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that all wealth is corrupting. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

- Not that Gilens and Page 2014 or Stigler 1971 settle this. They are world corroboration only; the figures decide. Gilens and Page is a filter for where to look (Fig 9 :60), not a witness for a slot.
- Not a derivation of political science from the figures. Named mechanisms are imported bindings with recorded provenance (Fig 8 :50-52) and zero evidentiary weight until they explain a described gap.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, 2026): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Anchor test:** in a named slot, vary wealth and measure the recorded outcome with an instrument that does not move when pushed. Is it pinned?
2. **Boundary test:** enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43-48) and identify the contact path carrying wealth-as-input. Is the frame's independence measurably debited?
3. **Feedback test:** measure the sign of the loop from influence back to wealth over repeated uses. Does it close?
4. **Convention test:** does the rule constrain the frame, or decide the outcome (Fig 10 :47)? Does the keeper's reputation depend on one outcome (Fig 10 :93)?
5. **Standing refuters:** the six in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, fired in a named slot.

A further correlation from the same data tradition, a list of endorsers, or an expert survey of perceived independence would not settle it (Fig 2 :35, Fig 2 :40).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 6 :21-24, Fig 10 :47 and :49, Fig 11 :62 and :66, Fig 12 :119 and :121. Eight short passages.
2. Name one institutional slot you know well and say whether its outcome stays pinned under a wealth push, and with what instrument you would measure that.
3. Say whether the formal rule in that slot constrains the frame or decides the outcome, and whether any cross-frame contact path exists.
4. Send the refutation, not the endorsement. A failure is more useful than agreement.
