# The Tether's answer: when wealth becomes political influence

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the condition under which a stock of wealth becomes a standing capacity to determine political outcomes, and the condition under which an institution blocks that conversion. Restated in effect terms: when does R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| close into a standing capacity in slot s, and what keeps it open? R is indexed per institutional slot, never as a national wealth-influence average (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The object is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: conversion and prevention are overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1 / D2, the anchor condition (the conversion event).** Wealth translates into influence when the thing that should settle a political outcome stops being pinned and starts updating on wealth. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :15 describes the result: *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."* Conversion is categorical per slot, not a smooth function of the size of the stock.

**D1 / D2, the boundary condition.** Money cannot manufacture political authority by composing inside the economic closure: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"* (Fig 6 :21). It must cross on contact with the political frame, and the crossing is paid for out of that frame's independence: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence"* (Fig 6 :24). When the two frames overlap almost completely they are *"one frame wearing two names"* (Fig 8 :49), and their union has no independence left to debit.

**D1, the catalyst condition.** A wealth stock survives its own use: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* (Fig 12 :119). One bounded stock therefore composes many influence molecules in sequence. This is why annual flow is the wrong unit.

**D2, the feedback condition.** When influence returns as wealth the loop closes: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* (Fig 12 :121). A linear accountability chain cannot even represent this failure.

**D3, the institutional condition (the prevention event).** Institutions prevent the conversion only while they remain conventions that constrain the frame rather than decide the outcome. *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47). *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* (Fig 10 :49). *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* (Fig 10 :93). Prevention is therefore a property of a maintained relationship, not of a written rule.

**D4, the habitat condition.** Introduction is available, exclusion is not: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* (Fig 11 :62). Hence *"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"* (Fig 11 :63), and isolation is *"substitution of one habitat for another"* (Fig 11 :66), with new actors inherited that were not chosen (Fig 11 :67). Actor valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51), so there is no standing list of bad actors.

**D5, the evidence condition.** Correlations between wealth and policy are *"filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict"* only in the sense captured by Fig 9 :60: they are **filters**, never verdict witnesses, when they share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). The ground settlement needs an instrument that returns a pinned outcome while itself remaining unpinned.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a dollar threshold, a country ranking or a named culprit. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32; canonical README).
- Not "all wealth is corrupting". Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not "more rules equal more prevention". A rule that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47), and transparency alone leaves the intersections of the union standing (Fig 9 :55-56).
- Not a derivation of political science from the figures. Terms like "revolving door" and "judicial review" are imported bindings; their provenance is recorded and they carry zero evidentiary weight until they explain a described gap (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not a claim that any named institution is captured. No measurement was taken.
- Not `grounded`. Per-slot refuters are written but unfired.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer settled it. Here:

1. **Anchor test (C1):** in a named slot, vary wealth and measure the recorded outcome with an instrument that does not itself move when wealth pushes it. Is the outcome pinned?
2. **Boundary test (C2):** enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43-48) and identify any contact path carrying wealth-as-input into the slot. Is the crossing present, and is the slot's independence measurably debited?
3. **Feedback test (C3):** measure the sign of the loop from influence back to wealth over repeated uses. Does the loop close (Fig 12 :121)?
4. **Convention test:** does the rule constrain the frame in which outcomes are produced, or does it determine which outcome is returned (Fig 10 :47)? A rule whose keeper's reputation depends on a particular outcome is already disqualified (Fig 10 :93).
5. **Standing falsifiers:** any one of the six in `01` section 4, fired in a named slot.

What would *not* settle it: another cross-national correlation from the same data tradition; a list of prestigious endorsers; an expert survey of "perceived independence". Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is stated, the three conditions are unbundled, and the refuters are written down. No pinned-outcome measurement was taken here. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the lineage is what makes the stop checkable. The political-science vocabulary is a visible guess about the axes along which influence varies; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral (*They're Scanning Books*, essay 2026). The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments. Nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that wealth always buys influence, and it does not name a country, a dollar threshold or a culprit. It says that conversion happens in a named institutional slot when the thing that should settle the outcome stops being pinned and starts updating on wealth, when a resource crosses from the economic frame into the political one and is paid for out of that frame's independence, and when influence returns as wealth so the loop closes. Wealth is a catalyst: one stock survives its uses and composes many influence molecules. Institutions block the conversion only while they remain conventions that constrain outcomes rather than decide them, and because contact cannot be subtracted from a habitat, that blocking is permanent work rather than a solved state. A correlation between wealth and policy tells you where to look, not what is true. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
