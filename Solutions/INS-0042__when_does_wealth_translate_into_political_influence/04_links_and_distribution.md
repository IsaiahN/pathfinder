## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Gilens and Page is a filter, not a witness.** Its result is the strongest public empirical statement that economic elites have independent policy impact. Under Fig 9 :60 it is *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict"*, so it tells us where to look (which slots to test) and not what settles a slot. Its 1,779 issues are one data tradition, hence one witness. It also **conflicts** with Fig 2 :29 if read as a verdict, because the measurement moves when the same evidence pool is pushed. The conflict sends the reading back to the census, where it is reassigned as a filter, not softened into corroboration.
2. **Stigler, writing in economics, gives a low-overlap retrodiction of the convention rule (Fig 10 :47).** A direct quotation from that 1971 paper makes the point in the author's own words: *"regulation is acquired by the industry and is designed and operated primarily for its benefit"* is what a convention that decides an outcome looks like from the economics side. It arrived independently, so it is the most useful outside corroboration in this packet, but it is still only corroboration. The figures decide.
3. **Common mode failure is the design-side analogue.** Redundant channels only buy safety if they are statistically independent. A political system with multiple oversight bodies that all draw on the same donors, the same experts and the same media has negated its own redundancy. This is Fig 2 :31-32 and Fig 8 :47-49 stated in engineering.
4. **Dominance hierarchy is the habitat-side analogue.** Resource-holding potential converts into access when resources are clumped, and fails to convert when they are distributed. The habitat, not the actor, decides the conversion. This is Fig 11 :49 and Fig 11 :61 in animal behaviour.
5. **Motivated reasoning is the stake-side analogue.** A directional goal changes which evidence is accessible. This is why Seat Map :171 calls an interest in the reading disqualifying, and why an oversight body with a reputation to protect cannot anchor (Fig 10 :93).
6. **Arrow is the limit-side analogue.** No ranked aggregation satisfies all fairness conditions. This supports the claim that no institution is a perfect anchor and that the right question is which condition fails in which slot, not which institution is best.

No world source overrules a figure line. Where a source shares the framework's author (the two essays), agreement is expected and carries little independent weight; this is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22 and :25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* and *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the whole framework before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the interior before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask which slot they know and what they would count as a failed anchor before giving them the article.
- **Fig 1 :19** - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A national-average pitch is the wrong object from the first sentence.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot claim and its three refuters; (2) ask which institutional slot they know and what they would count as a failure there; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors; once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 claim is checkable. It is checkable only per named slot, with a pinned-outcome test and an instrument that does not itself move when pushed. Do not send a national-average version.

## Legal and practical constraint

Public institutional submission pages and opt-in association channels avoid the unsolicited-bulk-email failure. Use one-to-one messages only, no purchased lists, no automation, and an opt-out line in every message. The office addresses published on the APSA pages are institutional role addresses, not personal emails, and are recorded as such in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
