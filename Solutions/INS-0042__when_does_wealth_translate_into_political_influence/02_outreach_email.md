# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the per-slot shape claim and its three refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the recipient's own vocabulary. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about a named institution before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about your field. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** in a named institutional slot, wealth becomes political influence when the thing that should settle the outcome stops being pinned and starts updating on wealth. It is not a smooth function of how much wealth is involved. Three separate conditions have to hold: the outcome moves when wealth pushes it; a resource crosses from the economic frame into the political frame and is paid for out of that frame's independence; and the influence returns as wealth so the loop closes. An institution prevents the conversion only while it remains a constraint rather than the thing that decides which outcome is returned. Writing "independent" into a rule does not ground that independence, and a one-time reform does not end the work.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not naming a dollar threshold, a country, or a single institution as the culprit. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that every correlation between wealth and policy settles anything; those studies decide where to look, not what is true, when they draw on the same evidence as the claim. I am not claiming that wealthy actors are malfactors by kind: an actor's valence depends on the residual being tracked. And I am not claiming this is settled by measurement. It is a `specified` prediction with written refuters.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. In a slot you know well, the recorded outcome tracks wealth while an independent instrument shows the putative anchor stayed pinned.
> 2. A formal rule demonstrably decides the outcome, yet remains a working anchor under independent measurement.
> 3. A wealth holder changes an outcome with no cross-frame contact at all, no import of wealth as a legitimate input.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds for your slot, I withdraw the claim for that slot. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule and the figures as metalaws: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, `FRAMEWORK/reference/60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
