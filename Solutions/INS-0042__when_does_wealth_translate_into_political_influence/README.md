## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *amount* of influence a wealthy actor has but **when the conversion closes**: when does a stock of economic resources become a standing capacity to determine political outcomes, and what property of an institution interrupts that conversion?

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is what an actor takes to be the case before acting, formed from its own history and inherited priors (Symbols table, `b`).
- **a** is the action: a donation, a lobbying contact, an acquisition of a media outlet, a revolving-door appointment, a litigation strategy.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the actor's library predicts those actions will return: access, a favourable rule, an appointment, a suppressed report.
- **o'** is what the political system actually returns: a recorded vote, a published ruling, an allocation, an enforcement decision.
- **s** is the institutional slot: a regulator, a court, an election administration, a legislature, a media ecosystem. R is indexed per slot and never averaged into a single national "wealth influence" score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "wealth is a stock that directly buys influence": leads to the prediction that conversion is a monotone function of the size of the stock and that institutions are barriers whose strength is a matter of degree.
- Option B, "conversion is a cross-frame event that closes only when the political frame's anchor becomes movable": leads to the prediction that conversion is categorical per slot, that it requires an import from the wealth frame into the political closure, that it is self-reinforcing when influence regenerates wealth, and that institutions prevent it only while they remain unmoved anchors, which is permanent work rather than a solved state.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conjunction of conditions that closes the wealth-to-influence loop in a slot, plus an instrument that discriminates a failed anchor (the outcome moves when wealth pushes it) from a failed closure boundary (the import never crossed) and from a failed feedback break (influence regenerates wealth). Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The natural-language question receives no single yes/no answer: the figures yield a condition, not a measurement, and the condition is per slot.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of political power.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Seat Map :163).
2. **Correlation has valence.** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). The dominant empirical literature on wealth and policy shares one data tradition, so it is one witness wearing many names, and a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
3. **A verifier must be outside the interior.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42), and *"an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Seat Map :171). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
