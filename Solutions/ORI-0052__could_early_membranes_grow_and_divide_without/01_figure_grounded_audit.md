## 1. The residual, before any figure line is chosen

The question is "Could early membranes grow and divide without complex cellular machinery?" In effect terms, not cause terms (Fig 9 :27 to :28, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"):

- What failed to resolve is whether two operations, **growth** (adding membrane material, increasing boundary area) and **division** (splitting one compartment into two, retaining contents) can be carried out by chemistry plus environment alone, or whether an encoded apparatus is required for either.
- Arity: **two operations**, not one. Growth and division are two object slots. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So the answer must keep them separate; a single verdict on "reproduction" would average away a live signal on one slot.
- Symmetry: **not interchangeable**. Growth is roughly additive and symmetric (accretion of amphiphiles); division is a threshold or a comparison, an asymmetric cut. The Operators table defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities," with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?" (local Operators table, not a figure line). The two operations have different bond types.
- Scale: **cycle, then lineage**. One compartment runs a growth-division loop; across loops the lineage is selected. Fig 13 :90 "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route." A chain cannot close; a cycle can (Fig 12 :121).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula "Direction"): a shape with (i) a boundary that persists while it is used, (ii) a loop whose output re-enters as input, (iii) no maintainer seat, and (iv) a ground reading from a laboratory vesicle experiment. Anything naming oleic acid, a specific fission protein, or the word "life" is out of scope at this stage.

## 2. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

Described from the residual alone. One line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

- **Domain 1, molecular self-assembly (the chemical frame).** Actors: amphiphiles, water, ions, temperature, shear, wet-dry cycles, mineral surfaces. Currency: free energy, surface tension, critical aggregation concentration, monomer exchange. Anchor: the experiment (a vesicle that grows or divides under stated conditions). This is a real domain because there is a nameable anchor: a bench measurement.
- **Domain 2, figure-internal composition (the metatheory).** Actors: the six terms (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures), the seven bonds, negation, catalysts, cycles, functional groups. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ) and settlement against the ground. Anchor: the figures as metalaws, plus the ground for a measurement. The anchor is nameable (verbatim figure text), so this is a domain.
- **Domain 3, selection and lineage (the habitat).** Actors: compartments, environment, variants, descendants. Currency: differential persistence and reproduction. Anchor: the ground, that is, which lineages remain after selection. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Nameable anchor, so a domain.
- **Domain 4, the verification frame (outside seat).** Actors: experimentalists and reviewers. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."). In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool where the world sources are concerned; their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Domain 4 is included because the outreach artifacts in this folder act on it.

**Partition or union?** Domains 1 and 2 overlap at the word "membrane": a chemical lipid bilayer and the figure's between-scales membrane. That overlap is a **compound with its own loop**, not a shared term. It is handled in F1 and in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Otherwise the three substantive domains are a partition: different actors, different currency, different anchor. The union of growth and division inside Domain 1 is a second compound (two conditions in one compartment), so it gets its own loop rather than being averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Settle the chemical frame's anchor first (does a vesicle grow or divide at all, and under what conditions); then the figure-internal shape (what must be present for a persisting, self-reproducing compartment); then the selection frame (does a lineage persist and diversify); then the verification frame. Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A claim about selection above a missing chemical link is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :20 to :23).

## 4. Findings

### F1: The tempting figure is a homonym trap (allowance for strain, then re-census)

Figure 4 is titled "The Membrane Between Scales." Its most quoted line is Fig 4 :21:

> **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**

And Fig 4 :22:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

It is tempting to read these as a derivation of a lipid bilayer. That is a **strain**. The figure's membrane governs what crosses between a one-agent loop and a population loop (methods upward, head starts downward); it does not govern which molecules pass through a fatty-acid bilayer. Under the method's rule, a line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity, so the domain was misidentified. **Re-census:** Fig 4 belongs to Domain 2 at the scale boundary, not Domain 1 at the chemical boundary. Its line can be used as a structural analogy (a membrane is a directional filter), but never as evidence that a given bilayer grows or divides. Using it as evidence would be echo without shadow, the apophenia named in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Verdict: **single-source, and refused as a chemical derivation.** The analogy is recorded, not relied on.

The same re-census keeps the useful part of Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." That is a census instruction, which is how it is used in section 2.

### F2: "Without machinery" is the unmaintained habitat, decided by Fig 11 (decisive, triangulated)

Figure 11 :56:

> **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."**

Figure 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

Figure 11 :39 to :40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

An encoded division apparatus is a maintainer's tool, an instance of "improving the agent in isolation." In unmaintained mode there is no seat, no maintainer, and no such tool; the habitat is pure contact and the ground keeps what survives. So the figures' answer to the literal question is: **yes, growth and division do not require machinery in the sense of an authored maintainer term.** The capability belongs to the compartment-and-habitat, not to a machine inside the compartment. Complex cellular machinery is what Fig 11 :58 calls **arrival worked on**, a later maintained condition: "Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."

Triangulation: Fig 11 (habitat / seats) and Fig 12 (composition structures, F3) are two figures with low overlap (one about where actors come from, one about what a composition is) that agree on the same shape. **Triangulated.**

### F3: The shape is a catalyst plus a cycle, not a list of parts (triangulated with F2)

Figure 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Figure 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

These two structural definitions are the figure's answer to what a self-maintaining compartment must look like. A boundary that persists through repeated growth and division is a catalyst (it survives its own use). Growth feeding division feeding growth is a cycle whose output re-enters as input. A linear account of membrane biogenesis ("make a membrane, then split it, then stop") is a chain and cannot close; only the cycle yields a lineage. No term named "machinery" appears in Fig 12's set of four structures (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group, Fig 12 :117 to :124). So the framework composes a persisting, dividing compartment from its existing structures without importing an apparatus.

Figure 12 :123, the functional group: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." A bilayer boundary is the functional group here; "fatty acid" or "phospholipid" is the qualifier. The figures return the group, not the qualifier. **Triangulated** with F2; the chemical identification of the qualifier is single-source world evidence, not figure-derived.

For a hypothetical mint of the term "complex cellular machinery" as a *prerequisite*:

- **NOVELTY** is zero relative to the origin frame. Machinery is already in the modern-cell library; importing it into the origin frame would add an atom the frame does not need (Fig 6).

Fig 5 :29 to :30: "nothing new here the machinery worked; the answer was already known." ### F5: The ground is a bench experiment, not agreement (single-source, anchor check)

Figure 2 :29 (quoted above) names the anchor. Figure 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Figure 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

For this question the anchor is a measurement: a vesicle preparation under stated pH, ionic strength, temperature, and shear, observed to grow or divide. The world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` are such measurements. They are secondary corroboration of the figure shape, not the thing that decides the figure verdict. A claim that "many researchers agree membranes could grow and divide" is agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40), so it carries no verdict weight. **Single-source** for the anchor rule; corroborated by the world sources, which are themselves the ground-facing witness.

### F6: Growth and division cannot be averaged (Fig 1, decisive for method)

Figure 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Figure 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The literature distinguishes growth (incorporation of amphiphiles, flip-flop, area increase) from division (fission by shear, budding, osmotic or Rayleigh-type instability). Folding both into one score "the protocell reproduced" would average away exactly the live signal: a system can grow without dividing (filamentous or overgrown vesicles) or divide without net growth (fragmentation). Every verdict in this audit is therefore kept per slot. **Single-source** for the rule, triangulated with the chemical source that distinguishes the two operations.

### F7: "Could" is reachability, and absence of machinery is not proof of absence (Fig 8 and Fig 9)

Figure 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Figure 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Figure 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

A failure to find a machinery-free pathway in one laboratory preparation is not proof that none exists. Conversely, the figures do not prove that a pathway exists; they only constrain its shape. The honest verdict is a shape plus a refuter, not an existence proof. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134) applies to derivations inside a closure; whether a specific early-Earth pathway is reachable is a ground question and is left open. **Triangulated** across Fig 8 and Fig 9.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device" (Seat Map field guide, local). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Closure refuter.** A demonstration that growth or division requires a term outside the closure of simple amphiphiles plus plausible environment in every setting examined, that is, an imported encoded apparatus with no substitute. This breaks F2 and F4.
2. **Cycle refuter.** A showing that the growth-division loop cannot close by Fig 12 :121 ("a chain cannot close by construction") without machinery, so the lineage terminates after one iteration. This breaks F3.
3. **Ground refuter.** A controlled experiment under plausible early-Earth conditions in which compartments repeatedly fail to grow or divide unless an encoded apparatus is supplied. This breaks the `specified` claim at the ground.
4. **Slot refuter.** Evidence that growth and division share one slot (perfectly correlated in all preparations), so the per-slot method in F6 is unnecessary. This breaks the method, not the verdict.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Two operations or one?** Two slots (Fig 1 :19). Growth and division scored separately.
2. **What is the anchor?** The experiment, never agreement (Fig 2 :29, :35).
3. **Which frame decides "without machinery"?** The unmaintained habitat (Fig 11 :56), triangulated with the composition structures (Fig 12 :119, :121).
4. **Is a new primitive needed?** No. Catalyst and cycle are already in Fig 12's structures; the boundary is a functional group (Fig 12 :123).
5. **Can we name the molecule?** No. The framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32). Any lipid is a provenanced binding from the chemistry frame.
6. **Is absence proved?** No. "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). The world sources are witnesses, not a proof.
7. **What reverses it?** A ground experiment (refuter 3), a closure demonstration (refuter 1), or a cycle failure (refuter 2).
