## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is Fig 4's membrane a lipid bilayer? HOMONYM, REFUSED

- Fig 4 :21: **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**
- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
- Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

Fig 4's membrane sits between a one-agent loop and a population loop. What crosses it is a method upward and a head start downward. A fatty-acid bilayer sits between a compartment's inside and outside; what crosses it is matter and energy. The two share the word "filter with a direction" but govern different quantities: one governs what may be inherited between scales, the other governs permeability. Under the strain rule, a line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity, so the domain was wrong.

**Re-census:** Fig 4 belongs to the scale boundary (Domain 2 in `01`), not the chemical boundary (Domain 1). Using it as a chemistry derivation would be an echo without a shadow, the apophenia the framework names in `01` F1. The analogy is recorded; it is not relied on. Refused as a derivation. Triangulated with the canonical re-census rule ("a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain").

### Q2: Chain or cycle?

- Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 12 :48: "A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."
- Fig 13 :90: "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."

A one-off account of membrane formation (assemble, split, stop) is a chain: the output does not re-enter, so it cannot yield a lineage. Growth feeding division feeding growth is a cycle: the output re-enters as input, so the loop can close. Fig 12 does not make the cycle a different *kind* of object from the chain; it makes it a different arrangement with a different sign. This is why the shape claim in `07` says the loop must close and the refuter in `01` §5 tests exactly that closure.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a preparation shows growth and division occurring once and then terminating with no route for the product to re-enter as input, the cycle claim fails and only the chain remains. Triangulated (Fig 12 :121 plus the diagram's distinction between chain and cycle).

### Q3: Catalyst: why persistence is a separate structural requirement

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :120 area: Fig 12 names catalyst, cycle, functional group, and isomer as the four structures a compositional system exhibits that the flat form cannot write (Fig 12 :116).

A boundary that is consumed by the division it undergoes would not survive its own use; it would be a spent part, and each generation would need a fresh boundary minted from scratch. The world source Chen and Walde (2010) describes the opposite: fatty-acid membranes with fast flip-flop and monomer exchange, where the boundary persists while material is added. That is a catalyst in Fig 12's sense: the part that survives its own use. The figure gives the structure; the chemistry gives the instance.

**Falsifier:** if every division consumes the boundary so that no parent material persists into daughters, the catalyst reading fails. Single-source for the figure definition; world corroboration for the instance.

### Q4: Functional group, not a seventh primitive

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

A bilayer boundary is the **functional group**: its presence fixes the outcome (a compartment exists). "Fatty acid" or "phospholipid" is the **qualifier**: it says which member of the group. The six-term set is closed, so adding "membrane" as a seventh primitive is not available. Arrangements are not closed, so many qualifiers can occupy the group. This dissolves the apparent need to name a primitive: the framework returns the group (a shape), and the chemistry supplies the qualifier as a provenanced binding.

**Falsifier:** if a compartment property could be shown to be a new kind not composable from the six and their bonds, the set would need a seventh member and this claim fails. Triangulated (Fig 12 :123 plus Fig 13 :115).

### Q5: Are two protocell designs isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :96: "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."

Two candidate designs over the same chemistry (one where the boundary persists through shear-driven fission, one where it persists through osmotic budding) share the same atoms and differ in the arrangement of the growth-division loop. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in an ingredient list and decisive in behaviour, which is exactly why the public packet asks for a behaviour-level check, not an ingredient list.

**Falsifier:** if the two designs require different chemical atoms (not merely different arrangements), they are not isomers and the weaker functional-group reading (Q4) stands. Single-source for the figure definition, with the chemistry-binding unmeasured.

### Q6: Level order: settled molecules become atoms

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."

If a laboratory vesicle that grows and divides is settled against the ground (an experiment pays for it), that whole compartment becomes an atom for the next level: a lineage under selection. This is how the chemistry result in the world sources becomes an operand for Fig 11's habitat, without importing a new primitive. It is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import.

**Falsifier:** if a settled compartment cannot serve as an atom for a lineage (for example, if no descendants persist), the recursion claim fails at this level. Triangulated (Fig 12 :104 plus Fig 7's room-chain selection).

## CONCLUSION

The supporting derivation yields: (1) Fig 4's membrane is a scale boundary and is refused as a chemistry derivation, a named homonym strain; (2) the grow-divide structure is a cycle, not a chain, with a closure falsifier; (3) persistence is a catalyst structure, with a consumption falsifier; (4) a boundary is a functional group, with a qualifier from chemistry, not a seventh primitive; (5) two designs over the same atoms are isomers under Fig 12; (6) a settled compartment becomes an atom for lineage selection. The order is: chemical anchor first, then cycle closure, then persistence, then group/qualifier, then isomer comparison, then level recursion.
