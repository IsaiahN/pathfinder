**Subject:** Check one shape claim about protocell membranes before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's habitat figure distinguishes an unmaintained habitat from a maintained one. In the unmaintained case, there is no maintainer seat, no live frame acting on the habitat, and "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept." The framework's own wording is that where nothing maintains anything, "Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." Applied to early membranes, that yields a shape, not a molecule: a compartment that persists while it is used (the bond figure calls this a catalyst, "a part that survives its own use") and a loop in which "the output re-enters as input" (a cycle), so growth feeds division feeds growth. On that shape, complex cellular machinery is a later arrival, not a prerequisite for growth and division.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names a lipid, a fission protein, or a specific early-Earth pathway. It returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself," so the chemical identity stays open. I am also not deriving a fatty-acid bilayer from the figure called "The Membrane Between Scales"; that figure governs a boundary between levels of description, not a chemical membrane, and treating it as chemistry would be a homonym error. Which molecules did it is a bench question, not a figure question.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. Growth or division requires a term outside the closure of simple amphiphiles plus environment in every setting examined, an encoded apparatus with no substitute.
> 2. The growth-division loop cannot close without machinery, so the lineage stops after one iteration.
> 3. A controlled experiment under plausible early-Earth conditions in which compartments repeatedly fail to grow or divide unless machinery is supplied.
> 4. Growth and division turn out to share one slot in all preparations, so keeping them separate is unnecessary.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
