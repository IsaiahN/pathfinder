## Two things in the world sources that bear hardest on the question

1. **Villalobos et al. is a union, not a single ceiling.** The paper forecasts a stock limit, then immediately lists three ways progress might continue. That is exactly Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A "data limit" label hides at least two conditions with different remedies. Cite this when explaining why the two-bin partition fails.
2. **Acemoglu supplies the ground-availability clause from inside economics.** *"no objective outcome measures from which to learn successful performance"* is a statement that some tasks have no anchor. That is Fig 2 :29's anchor test, reached from a different field. Two frames drawing on different evidence converging on the same structural point is corroboration at low overlap, not a shared evidence pool.
