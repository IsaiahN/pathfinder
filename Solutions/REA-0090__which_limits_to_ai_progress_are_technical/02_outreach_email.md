# Outreach draft: check the discriminator before you decide about the list

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No trust request, no agreement request, no ranking of recipients by receptiveness.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the recipient has been given the residual and the refuter with no framework interior attached. If the stage-1 output does not survive their check, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable discriminator for "technical versus economic" limits on AI progress

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which limits to AI progress are technical?" hides two different facts that the figures keep separate.
>
> 1. A limit is technical in the strict sense when the term needed to close it is outside what the frame can compose, and no instrument already returning something can be sharpened into it. More search cannot add such a term. The remedy is an import from a frame whose closure differs, or a new instrument built from a worse one.
> 2. A limit is economic in the relevant sense when the needed term is already inside what the frame can compose and was simply not found. The remedy is more search, distributed among independent searchers.
>
> These two issue different instructions, so treating them as one list makes the wrong fix likely. A third kind is separate again: regulation and social choice change the habitat or install a convention, which changes contact, not what the frame can compose. Finally, actions and bits are two currencies that do not add, so a bigger budget cannot pay for a term the frame cannot compose, and a cleverer description cannot pay for actions nobody funded.
>
> **What the output is not:** it is not a ranked list of today's limits, and it does not say that compute, data, or any named statute is technical or non-technical. It returns the test you would apply to each candidate, the anchor that governs it, and the observation that would move it to a different category. That is a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a term outside the frame's closure produced by search alone, with no import and no instrument extension;
> 2. a case where a larger action budget changes what the frame can compose, so the two facts are one;
> 3. a single accounting in which description cost and testing cost are commensurable;
> 4. a regulation that adds a primitive to what a frame can compose, or a convention that decides a ground verdict while remaining a convention.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The broader metatheory article: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state whether your claim is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
