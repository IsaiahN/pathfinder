# Why same-sounding limits are different substances, and what order to test them in

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two candidate brakes on AI progress can have the same surface description ("we are not there yet", "data is the bottleneck", "this is too expensive") and still require opposite remedies. We need a structural account of why the surface description cannot decide the domain, and an order in which to test the domains.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the four census domains from `01` (technical reach, economic budget, habitat/convention, verification), the seven bonds plus negation (Fig 12), the dependency chain links (Fig 3). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are two "data limits" isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

Take two limits that both read "not enough training data":

- L1: the required regularities are not composable from what the frame holds. The bond is a reachability failure. Atoms: Γ, closure, a missing import. Remedy: import or instrument.
- L2: the required tokens exist in the closure and were not gathered. The bond is an action-budget failure. Atoms: Γ, closure, an unfunded search. Remedy: more search, distributed independently.

Same ingredient vocabulary (data, compute, model), different bond, different substance and different remedy. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not degrees of one limit. The world source in `04`, Villalobos et al., exhibits exactly this: it names a stock ceiling and then immediately lists imports (synthetic data, transfer, efficiency), so a single label hides two bonds.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if both limits have the same reversal condition under an instrument that discriminates source, the isomer claim fails and they are one limit described twice.

### Q2: Which bond is "progress"?

Operators table :46: **"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"**

Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

Applied to the question:

- "We need more compute" is a **sequence** claim (→): A then B, spend then arrive. Fig 12 :70-72 defines → as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*.
- "We are behind a target" is a **comparison** claim (⋛). Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.
- "What is missing is an operand" is a **subtraction** claim (−): Fig 12 :82: *"A with B removed or absent"*.
- "This limit has been misidentified" is a **negation** (¬), which is not a bond at all. Fig 12 :135: **"Negation is not one: it takes a term and yields a term, and joins nothing."**

A vocabulary holding only the ingredient names (data, compute, regulation) can name a state and cannot name a route to one. It cannot say what would count as getting somewhere, which is precisely the failure mode this question starts in.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a case in which a subtraction, a comparison, a sequence and a negation over the same limit issue the same instruction. That would collapse the bond distinction.

### Q3: What order do the domains come in?

Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Order by dependency, not by how loudly each limit is discussed:

1. **Perception** (Fig 3 :4-5): can the system tell what is in front of it and apart from what was there before? Without this, no limit can be measured.
2. **Vocabulary** (Fig 3 :6-8): are there terms for what things afford? This is where "hard", "expensive" and "forbidden" are still undifferentiated.
3. **Objective** (Fig 3 :9-11): can it say what would count as progress here, in its own terms? Fig 3 :25-27 warns this link attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments.
4. **Planning**, then **learn and carry** (Fig 3 :12-15).

The four domains from `01` map onto this order as follows: verification hygiene (D4) gates perception; technical reach (D1) gates vocabulary and objective; economic budget (D2) gates planning; habitat and convention (D3) determine which links are even permitted to run. Testing D2 before D1 is the "effort above the break" error, because more actions cannot supply a term the closure lacks (Fig 13 :118: *"infinite effort inside a finite reach."*).

**Falsifier:** show a chain in which planning effort reliably repairs a broken vocabulary link. That would break the dependency ordering.

### Q4: Is the union a higher-order composition?

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**

Each domain, once its verdict is settled against the ground, becomes an atom at the next level. A real "limit on AI progress" is usually a second-order molecule: a union of a reach condition, an action condition and a habitat condition (F1 of `01`). Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** The compound has its own loop and its own ground; it is not the sum of its parts.

**Strain, named:** the Operators table restricts ⋛ (comparison) to *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At second order the operands are settled domain verdicts now serving as atoms. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom, but whether a verdict counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a strain, not smoothed.

## CONCLUSION

Same-sounding limits are **isomers under different bonds** (Fig 12 :117): identical ingredient vocabulary, different remedy. "Progress" is a subtraction, a goal is a comparison, a plan is a sequence, and a misidentification is a negation, which is not a bond. The domains must be tested in dependency order (Fig 3), with technical reach before economic budget, because effort above a broken link arrives where it cannot be used. The compound limit is second-order and has its own ground. This rescues the useful part of the question (limits differ categorically) without the part that fails (that they differ as points on one list).
