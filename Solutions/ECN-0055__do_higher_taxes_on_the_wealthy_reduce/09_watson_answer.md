## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that higher taxes on the wealthy either reduce productive investment or mainly reduce rent-seeking and concentration. Option A: answer one side of the either/or. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, unbundle the union, and record the direction as a gap until an instrument closes it. What each leads to: A delivers a scalar in a currency nobody declared, averages across heterogeneous slots, and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B delivers a per-slot separator, separate verdicts in separate currencies, verbatim refuters, and an honest stop at the missing instrument. The residual is stated first: R is the unassigned change in the composition of effort and capital at the top of the distribution when the top marginal rate rises. Its arity is at least four overlapping channels, its symmetry is broken because the outcomes use different currencies and anchors, and it spans slot, habitat and rule scales.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** At least four channels: real effort or investment, tax avoidance, compensation bargaining, and entry into a rent-seeking sector. Arity is under-counted by the question's two options. Symmetry is broken: productive investment is real capital formation, rent-seeking is a transfer of existing output, concentration is a market state. Scale: slot (executive, firm, investor, sector), habitat (governance, enforcement, market structure), rule stratum (the tax law). Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Domain 1, effort and investment.** Actors: top earners, executives, inventors, investors, each a slot. Currency: after-tax return, hours, capital committed. Anchor: observed real investment and effort. Union member; per-slot verdict required.
- **Domain 2, rent extraction.** Actors: rent-seekers, incumbent firms, lobbyists, bargaining teams, the rent-seeking sector. Currency: rents captured, pay for luck, rent share, concentration. Anchor: measured rent activity and market structure. Union member; valence habitat-dependent.
- **Domain 3, rule and habitat.** Actors: legislature, tax authority, enforcement, corporate governance. Currency: rules, conventions, enforcement intensity. Anchor: the ground the policy claims to serve, not the convention itself. Habitat to be enumerated, never composed.
- **Domain 4, measurement and identification.** Actors: estimators, datasets, natural experiments. Currency: elasticities and intervals. Anchor: the ground, which must not move when a frame pushes on it. Upstream of Domains 1 and 2 by dependency.
- **Order.** Instrument (D4) before habitat (D3) before per-slot readings (D1/D2) before any aggregate. Fig 3 :20 orders links by dependency, not importance, and Fig 3 :27 says a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at.
- **Strain test.** The partition reading strained when "mainly" required an unstated common currency and an aggregate across slots; re-census assigned it to the question's framing and kept the union verdict. The six-term substrate mapping was tried in `06` Q4 and refused: Fig 13 governs the minimum viable set of states and structures, not a taxonomy of tax responses. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a bundled union of at least four overlapping channels with different currencies and anchors, not a two-way choice. The effect is per slot, with no standing productive or rent-seeking valence for "the wealthy". Investment and rent-seeking are isomers over a fixed atom set under a stated falsifier, not stocks that can be netted. The figures return the shape, the per-domain verdicts and the refuters, but no magnitude, sign or channel selection: that is a declared `gap`, recorded as a verdict. Mode `general` on the structure, `specified` on the per-domain findings, `gap` on the literal "mainly", not `grounded`. The source gate was applied and one secondary summary was withdrawn for contradicting its primary.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states every refuter before the conclusion and keeps the retraction log, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a scalar conclusion instead of the shape and its instrument question (Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*; Fig 2 :42, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no existing claim is overturned by retracting the "mainly" framing. The union reading can be narrowed without discarding the slot, currency and source-gate findings, and a wrong secondary summary can be removed from the witness set without touching the figure derivation.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A slot-level instrument returning separate readings for real effort, avoidance, bargaining and rent capture on the same rate change would move the direction from `gap` to `specified`; a demonstrated common currency for the two outcomes would revive the "mainly" comparison; a standing productive/rent-seeking valence, a policy-invariant response across regimes, or a verbatim figure line returning a magnitude would each fire one of the refuters in `01` §4. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
