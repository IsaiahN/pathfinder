# The Tether's answer: do higher taxes on the wealthy reduce productive investment or rent-seeking?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the literal direction. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The intervention is a rise in the top marginal rate. R is the unassigned change in the composition of effort and capital at the top of the distribution. It has at least four overlapping channels, two of which the question names. Its arity is under-counted by the question; its symmetry is broken because the two outcomes use different currencies and anchors; and it spans three scales, slot, habitat and rule, with a lossy transform between them.

Fig 9 :55 says: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* So the question is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground, not one hard question with opposite answers.

## 2. The shape

1. **The either/or is a union, not a partition.** Real effort, avoidance, bargaining and rent-sector entry all respond to the same rate change. No single lever clears the intersections. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus Fig 1).

2. **The effect is per slot, and "the wealthy" has no standing valence.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The world literature locates the rent response especially in poorly governed firms, which is a habitat condition, not a population label. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 1 plus Fig 11).

3. **The two outcomes cannot be averaged.** Productive investment and rent-seeking have different currencies and different anchors. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* A scalar "mainly" needs a common currency that the question does not supply and no figure line provides. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus Fig 12).

4. **The direction is a `gap`, recorded as a verdict.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The figures govern the admissible shape of an answer; they do not return a magnitude or choose a channel. Verdict `gap`, unreachable at this budget, not underivable in principle.

5. **The two activities are isomers over a fixed atom set, not two stocks.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Same atoms under a production bond versus a capture-and-transfer bond give different substances. The claim carries its falsifier: if one activity needs an atom the other does not, the relation is composition plus import and the weaker functional-group reading stands instead. Verdict `single-source` on the figure definition, with world corroboration.

6. **The instrument is the broken middle link.** Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The taxable-income elasticity reads one green and cannot name the source. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not more pooled estimates. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 3 plus Fig 9 :27-28).

7. **Past responses are recordings of one regime.** Fig 10 :72: *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."* A rate change changes the room, so a historical elasticity is not a structural constant. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 10 plus the Lucas and Goodhart corroborations).

8. **The answer to "mainly X" is a refusal, not a subtraction.** The scalar comparison is refused by negation. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*; *"this does not hold"* is the claim, not "X with Y removed". Verdict `single-source` on the operator table.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "higher taxes reduce productive investment". No such sign is derived.
- Not "higher taxes mainly reduce rent-seeking and concentration". That is the scalar the figures refuse.
- Not a revenue-maximizing rate, an optimal top rate, or an elasticity value. No number is returned.
- Not that the two channels are mutually exclusive, and not that they share a currency.
- Not that Rothschild and Scheuer or Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva settle the policy question. They are models with different objectives and are used only as corroboration of the union and slot structure.
- Not a derivation from the six-term substrate. That mapping was tried in `06` Q4 and refused by re-census: it governs the minimum viable set of states and structures, not a taxonomy of tax responses.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken in this folder.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

The ground is a slot-level instrument that returns separate readings for the four channels on the same rate change. The corresponding questions are:

1. For the same executive, firm or investor and the same rate change, how much of the response was real effort or capital committed, and how much was rent capture, avoidance, or bargaining pay? An instrument that answers this names the source the elasticity leaves as green.
2. Does the channel composition change with the governance habitat in the way the poorly-governed-firm results suggest, or is it invariant? The latter would refute the slot claim.
3. Does the relationship survive a rule-regime change measured with the same instrument? If not, the historical estimate is a recording, not a generator (Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*).
4. Standing refuters from `01` §4, including a figure line that returns a magnitude, a common currency for the two outcomes, or a standing productive/rent-seeking valence.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The shape is stated; the direction is not. The stop is at the limit of the available instruments and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. The figures say *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical README), so failing to distinguish the channels is not evidence that they are the same thing. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*). A source gate was applied to the world literature: a secondary summary that inverted its primary abstract was withdrawn as a witness (`01` F6). That withdrawal is part of the record, not a silent correction.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "investment" or "rent-seeking" to this question. It says the question is a union of at least four overlapping response channels, not a two-way choice, and that the two named outcomes have different currencies and anchors, so they cannot be averaged into a "mainly" number. The effect is indexed per slot, and the same actor can be productive or rent-seeking depending on the habitat and the residual, so there is no standing list of good or bad taxpayers. Over a fixed set of atoms, investment and rent-seeking are isomers, same atoms arranged under different bonds, not two stocks where one is subtracted from the other. The figures return this shape and its refuters, not a magnitude: which channel dominates is unreached with the instruments held here, and the next instrument is a slot-level measure that can tell real effort from rent capture on the same rate change. That instrument, not another pooled elasticity and not a list of prominent economists who agree, is what would close the gap.
