# Expert verification packet: the Tether on top taxes, investment and rent-seeking

**What this is:** a claim in a form a tax-policy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the direction. Not `grounded`.** No claim is made about the sign or magnitude of an effect. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do higher taxes reduce productive investment, or mainly rent-seeking and concentration?" is a bundled union of overlapping conditions, not a two-way choice. At least four response channels (real effort, avoidance, compensation bargaining, and entry into a rent-seeking sector) are in play, and the two named outcomes have different currencies and anchors, so they cannot be averaged into a "mainly" scalar. The effect is indexed per slot, and actor valence is relative to the residual and the governance habitat, so "the wealthy" is not a fixed kind with a fixed valence. Over a fixed atom set the two activities are isomers, same atoms under different bonds, not subtractable stocks. The figures return this shape, its per-domain verdicts and its refuters, but no magnitude; the direction is a `gap` that a slot-level, channel-discriminating instrument would close.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Union, not partition; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the two named effects overlap and use different currencies; "mainly" is not a well-formed comparison without a declared common currency.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 12 :125, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- **What to check:** are the channels in fact overlapping in the economics literature? Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva model three simultaneously (NBER w17616); Rothschild and Scheuer add sector entry (NBER w17035). If they are not overlapping, the union reading is wrong.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a taxonomy in which each top earner is in exactly one channel and the channels share a currency, so "mainly" reduces to comparing two numbers.

### C2 — Per-slot residual and habitat-relative valence; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11, then the firm-level evidence

- **Statement:** a pooled effect over "the wealthy" hides the live signal, and the same actor can be productive or rent-seeking depending on the residual and the habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :49, *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** Andersen and López (2019, *Contemporary Economic Policy* 37(2), via RePEc) find that rent-seeking is *"an important component of the response to changes in tax rates, especially among executives in firms with the worst corporate governance."* PSS et al. find the CEO-pay correlation *"stronger in firms with poor governance."* Is the slot reading faithful to those results, or is the governance modifier incidental?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a standing classification of taxpayers into intrinsically productive and intrinsically rent-seeking kinds whose valence does not change with the residual or the governance habitat.

### C3 — No figure returns a magnitude, and a summary is not a witness; checkable by reading Figures 9, 13 and 8, then two opened web pages

- **Statement:** the framework returns a shape, not a number, and the direction is a `gap`. Where a secondary summary contradicts a primary abstract, the primary decides and the summary is withdrawn.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134, *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* Fig 13 :135, *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 8 :51, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** open https://www.nber.org/papers/w17035 and https://www.progress.org/wiki/rothschild-scheuer-rent-seeking/ . The NBER abstract says optimal top rates *"can remain remarkably modest even if all high earners are socially unproductive rent-seekers"* and that the intuition linking rent-seeking to high top rates is *"not well grounded in formal theory."* The wiki says the opposite: *"higher marginal rates can be efficient because they discourage socially wasteful rent-seeking without sacrificing genuine production."* Does the primary support the summary? It does not.
- **Refuter:** show a figure line, checked verbatim against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, that returns a magnitude or selects a channel; or show that the primary abstract supports the summary's claim, in which case F6 of `01` is retracted.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a sign or magnitude: no claim that higher taxes raise or lower productive investment, rent-seeking, or revenue.

- Not that any named economist endorses this. No appeal to prestige or agreement is made.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The Green-reading failure in *The Hard Problem is About Source* is the analogue: a spectrometer, not an argument, resolved metamerism. Here:

1. For the same slot and the same rate change, separate real effort or capital committed from rent capture, avoidance and bargaining pay. Does the instrument exist and does it return something?
2. Does the channel mix depend on governance quality as the firm-level results suggest, or is it invariant across habitats?
3. Does the relationship survive a rule-regime change with the same instrument?
4. Do the standing refuters in `01` §4 remain unfired?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-55, Fig 12 :125, Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :49, Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134-135. Eight short passages.
2. Open the NBER w17035 abstract and the progress.org wiki entry. Confirm which one the primary supports.
3. Check C2 against your own knowledge of the executive-compensation evidence: is the governance modifier a slot condition or an aside?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish real effort from rent capture on the same rate change, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and a reply is a conversation, not corroboration.
