## 0. The residual, in effect terms

An intervention, a rise in the top marginal rate, is applied to a population of top earners. Two outcomes are named: productive investment and rent-seeking with concentration. The question asks which outcome the intervention *mainly* moves.

Stated as a residual: **R is the unassigned change in the composition of effort and capital at the top of the distribution when the top rate changes.** What fails to resolve is which channel carries the change, and whether the two named channels are even the complete set of channels. The word "mainly" presupposes a common currency in which the two outcomes can be placed on one scale and compared. No such currency is given in the question. Binding it as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction") is the first move, and testing it is F3 below.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity.** The question names two channels. The economics literature names at least three responses to a top rate change: labor supply, avoidance, and compensation bargaining (Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva, NBER w17616), plus a rent-seeking sector with free entry whose private return is crowdable (Rothschild and Scheuer, NBER w17035). So the arity of the real residual is at least four overlapping conditions, not two. Under-counting the arity is not a small error: it is what makes "mainly" look answerable.
- **Symmetry.** The two named outcomes are not mirror images. Productive investment is a flow into new capital and output. Rent-seeking is a transfer of existing output, and concentration is a state of a market structure. Their currencies differ (real output versusrent capture versus market share), and their anchors differ (investment measurement versus rent share or concentration index). A change in one is not the negation of a change in the other, so `−` is the wrong bond if someone tries to write "taxes reduce investment, therefore they free resources for production".
- **Scale.** Three scales are in play at once. The **slot** is the individual, executive, firm, or sector making the effort decision. The **habitat** is the governance and enforcement setting in which that decision is made. The **rule stratum** is the tax law and the convention that defines what is taxable. The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4), so an effect measured at the slot cannot be read off directly as an aggregate, and an aggregate cannot be pushed back down to a slot without the round trip.

**Partition or union?** A union, not a partition. The channels overlap: the same rate change can lower real effort, lower rent capture, and redirect effort between the two at the same time, and the overlap has no assignable fraction. A single lever clears the region where one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing. That is Fig 9 :55 in effect terms.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** D4 first: an instrument that can discriminate the channels is upstream of any directional reading (Fig 3 :20, :27). D3 next: the habitat must be enumerated before actor valence can be assigned (Fig 11 :43-44). D1 and D2 then, per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Any aggregate claim comes last and carries a round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30).

**Strain test applied during the census.** Reading the question as a two-way partition initially seemed to fit the word "or". When the two outcomes were required to share a currency so that "mainly" could be computed, the reading strained: it needed an unstated unit and an aggregation across slots. By the census rule, a line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. Re-census assigned the partition intuition to the question's framing, not to the residual, and the figure verdict below is union. The partition reading is preserved in the retraction log (§7).

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The either/or is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (decisive for the shape)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The two named effects are not mutually exclusive alternatives. A top rate change acts on real effort and on rent capture through different channels at the same time, and a third channel, avoidance, and a fourth, bargaining, sit across both. Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva model exactly this: three channels with three elasticities, and they derive a top rate formula as a function of all three. Rothschild and Scheuer add a sector choice whose private return is crowdable. So the question's "or" is a bundled union. No single lever picks one answer.

**Refuter:** show that the two outcomes are a partition with an assignable shared fraction of zero and a single common currency, so that "mainly" reduces to comparing two numbers. Until that is shown, the union reading stands. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 9 (union structure) and Fig 1 (per-slot residual, a separate figure with low overlap in subject matter).

### F2 — "The wealthy" is not one slot, and no actor kind carries a fixed valence

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

A pooled elasticity over "the wealthy" aggregates across executives, inventors, investors, and rent-seekers, and across firms with good and bad governance. Fig 1 says that is how a live slot signal disappears. Fig 11 says the same actor can be a productive worker or a rent-seeker depending on what the residual is and what the habitat pays for.

The world literature corroborates the slot structure from a low-overlap direction. Andersen and López (2019, *Contemporary Economic Policy*, via RePEc) report that *"rent seeking represents an important component of the response to changes in tax rates, especially among executives in firms with the worst corporate governance."* The modifier "especially among executives in firms with the worst corporate governance" is a slot condition, not a population average. Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva similarly report that the negative CEO-pay correlation with top rates is *"stronger in firms with poor governance."* Two instruments, different datasets, same slot-dependence: this is corroboration at some independence, inside the public-economics pool.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a standing classification of taxpayers into intrinsically productive and intrinsically rent-seeking kinds whose valence does not change with the residual or the governance habitat. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 1 slot indexing plus Fig 11 actor valence, two figures with different subject matter).

### F3 — The two outcomes have different anchors and currencies, so "mainly" is not a well-formed comparison

Fig 9 :4: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset,"*
Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

Productive investment is measured in real capital formation. Rent-seeking is measured in rent capture, bargaining pay, or concentration. These are different readings with different instruments. Fig 12 :125-126 says two currencies do not add and a system holding one accounting cannot compare them. Fig 9 :4-5 says a disagreement that does not shrink because each rule works on a different subset is several well-formed questions, and the instruction is to split, not to search harder for a single combined effect.

Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva report that *"top income share increases have not translated into higher economic growth"* in their 18-country evidence. That is the empirical version of the category error: the rent share moved, the productive outcome did not follow, so one cannot be read as a proxy for the other. The Laffer curve literature makes the same point about scalar summaries: the New Palgrave comparison gives a mid-range revenue-maximizing rate around 70 percent but the estimates "have varied widely", and the shape is "uncertain and disputed among economists" (Wikipedia, *Laffer curve*, opened 2026-11-23).

**Refuter:** produce a declared common currency and an instrument that measures both outcomes in it, with the conversion shown rather than assumed. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 fact-test plus Fig 12 two-currency rule).

### F4 — No figure line selects a dominant channel: this is a `gap`, recorded as a verdict

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134: *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine census of all thirteen figures, the Operators table, the Symbols table, and the formula, no line returns a magnitude, a sign, or a selection among the channels. The figures govern the shape of the answer and the conditions under which an answer would be admissible. They do not govern the empirical direction. This is the honest stop. It is not silence, and it is not an invitation to invent a percentage.

The world literature does not supply the missing selector either, and its disagreement is itself evidence for the union reading rather than a reason to flip the verdict:

- Rothschild and Scheuer's NBER abstract says *"optimal marginal taxes on the highest wage earners can remain remarkably modest even if all high earners are socially unproductive rent-seekers"* and explains that *"taxing their effort at a lower rate stimulates their rent-seeking efforts, thereby keeping private returns for other potential rent-seekers low and discouraging further entry."* The intuition that high rent-seeking calls for high top rates is, in their words, *"not well grounded in formal theory."*
- Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva conclude that *"If bargaining effects in fact exist, optimal tax rates should be higher than commonly assumed."*

These are not two frames ranging against a common anchor. They are models with different objectives, different assumptions about observability and entry, and different implicit currencies. Agreement or disagreement between them cannot decide the question for us (Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*).

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line, checked verbatim against `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, that returns a magnitude or selects a channel. **Status: `gap`** of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind (Fig 8 :40-43 distinguishes it from underivable in principle), reported as `general` on structure and `gap` on direction.

### F5 — Rent-seeking and productive investment are isomers over a fixed atom set, not two different stocks

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

The same atoms (effort, capital, a rule that can be captured) arranged under a production bond versus a capture-and-transfer bond give two different substances with the same ingredient list. That is the isomer claim. If the atom set is not fixed, if enforcement technology or a new market enters, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism, and the claim does not apply.

The world analogue is the Tullock paradox recorded in the rent-seeking literature: a rent-seeker who hopes to gain a billion dollars may need to spend only about one percent of that to obtain the favor, so the same rent can be pursued with a much smaller outlay. The observation does not change the atoms, only the bond and the price, which is exactly the isomer situation. Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva's "pay for luck" finding (*"pay for luck is quantitatively more important when top tax rates are low"*) is the same arrangement read from compensation data.

**Falsifier written with the claim:** if either activity requires an atom the other does not (for example, rent-seeking requires a enforceable privilege that productive investment does not, or investment requires a capital good rent-seeking does not), the fixed-atom condition fails and the relation is composition plus import. The functional-group reading then stands instead: both are members of the `effort → return` group with different qualifiers. **Status: `single-source`** for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the world analogue as corroboration at a different stratum.

### F6 — A secondary summary inverted the primary result: source gate, not corroboration

This finding came from an actual fetch on 2026-11-23 and is kept visible because it is the clearest failure mode in the folder.

The primary NBER abstract for Rothschild and Scheuer (w17035, opened directly) says optimal top rates can remain modest even when all high earners are rent-seekers, and that the intuition linking rent-seeking to high top rates is not well grounded in formal theory. A secondary page, progress.org's wiki entry on the same paper (opened directly), summarizes it as: *"higher marginal rates can be efficient because they discourage socially wasteful rent-seeking without sacrificing genuine production."* The entry is authored by "Progress LLM" and dated 2026-06-06.

Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
Fig 2 :31, quoted in F4, is the other half: two readers of the same report are not two frames.
Fig 6 :24: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame’s independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*

**Resolution.** The summary `conflicts` with the primary abstract. It is withdrawn as a witness and retained as evidence of the summary hazard. The figure verdict is unchanged: the conflict sends us back to the census (which already says the direction is a gap) and never flips the verdict. The ledger records the row with status `conflicts`. A source gate is not decoration here; without it this folder would have asserted the opposite of the primary paper.

**Refuter:** show that the primary abstract supports the summary's claim, in which case the conflict is retracted and the summary promoted to a witness. Until then, primary only. **Status: `single-source`** on the figure side (Fig 8 :51), with the two opened pages as the worked case.

### F7 — The broken link is the instrument that discriminates channels, not the amount of estimation

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

The elasticity of taxable income literature is large, but Saez, Slemrod and Giertz's critical review (NBER w15012, opened directly) says the elasticity is a sufficient statistic for efficiency and optimal tax analysis only *"under what assumptions"* and that *"what other parameters should be estimated when the elasticity is not a sufficient statistic"* must be discussed. In other words, a single elasticity cannot by itself tell which channel moved. That is a link-with-no-instrument problem: many estimates of one quantity, no instrument that separates real effort from rent capture and avoidance.

This is where a checker should spend effort. More pooled estimates land above the break. Fig 3 :19 says they cannot rescue the broken link.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument, already returning something, that discriminates real effort from rent capture at the slot and show it has been used on a rate change. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 3 dependency order plus Fig 9 :27-28 residual-before-frame).

### F8 — Historical effects are recordings of one regime, not invariant anchors

Fig 10 :72: *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*
Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

Two world sources at low overlap with the tax literature support this from different directions. The Lucas critique page (Wikipedia, opened) quotes Lucas: *"any change in policy will systematically alter the structure of econometric models"*, so a coefficient estimated under one tax regime is not a structural constant under another. Goodhart's law (Wikipedia, opened) states *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"*, which is the world-side statement of Fig 10 :47: a convention that fixes an outcome has left the convention class and become a cause.

The consequence for this question is direct: an estimate of how top earners responded to past rate changes is a recording of a habitat, not a generator that can be carried into a new regime without the round trip of Fig 4 :30.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a policy-invariant behavioral parameter across at least two regime changes with the same measurement instrument, and show that the convention did not enter the outcome. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 10 :72 plus Fig 4 :23).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single scalar is shown to be a sufficient statistic for both channels under all regimes, so the question is a partition with a common currency and "mainly" is well formed.
2. A figure line, checked verbatim against `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, returns a magnitude, a sign, or a selection among the channels.
3. Actor valence is shown to be intrinsic to a taxpayer kind rather than relative to the residual and the habitat (overrides Fig 11 :49-51).
4. A policy-invariant behavioral parameter is demonstrated across regime changes with a stable instrument (overrides Fig 10 :72 and the Lucas/Goodhart corroboration).
5. The primary Rothschild-Scheuer abstract is shown to support the progress.org summary's claim, so the source-gate conflict of F6 dissolves in the other direction.
6. The fixed-atom condition of the isomer claim fails: one activity requires a primitive the other does not, so the relation is composition plus import and F5 falls to the functional-group reading.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the audit redone from the census. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 8. Round trip and next instrument

The condensation carried by this folder is: *it is a union, effects are per slot, the currencies do not add, the direction is a gap, and the instrument is missing*. The concretisation someone might try is a single number for "how much investment falls". Fig 4 :30 requires the gap between the two to be measured, not assumed zero. The dropped constraints are the four in F9 plus the fixed-atom condition of F5. Recording them is the honest report of what the coarser description cannot hold.

The next instrument is not another pooled elasticity. It is a slot-level measure that can say, for the same rate change and the same actor, how much of the response was real effort or capital committed and how much was rent capture, avoidance, or bargaining pay, with the governance habitat recorded. Until such an instrument exists and returns something, `unreached` is the correct status and the verdict stays `candidate`.
