# Solution ECN-0055

**Question:** Do higher taxes on the wealthy reduce productive investment, or mainly reduce rent-seeking and concentration?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 74

**Section:** 6. Inequality, poverty, and distribution

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the literal direction ("mainly" one channel). Nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this folder.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the economic question it reports on. Every substantive file says so. The figures decide the shape; the world literature is secondary corroboration only, and no number was measured here.

## Restating the question as an effect

The intervention is a rise in the top marginal rate. The two named outcomes are (a) productive investment, measured in real capital formation and new output, and (b) rent-seeking and concentration, measured in capture of pre-existing output, rent shares, bargaining pay, and market concentration. The question asks which outcome the intervention mainly moves. An answer would look like a scalar allocation of the effect between two channels, so that scalar is bound here as an ordinary term and tested (Formula, "Direction"). It does not survive (F3 and F4 of `01`).
