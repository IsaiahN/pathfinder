## Wider literature relevant to the question

- **Public finance, three-channel model.** Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva (2011/2014) is the closest formal statement that the question's two channels are not the whole set. Its cross-country finding that top income share increases did not translate into growth is the empirical reason not to read rent share as a proxy for productive investment.
- **Public finance, rent-seeking sector.** Rothschild and Scheuer (2011/2016) is the primary that the secondary wiki inverts. Read the abstract, not the summary.
- **Corporate governance slot.** Andersen and López (2019) and the CEO-pay results inside PSS et al. both locate the rent-seeking response in poorly governed firms. This is the strongest world corroboration of the per-slot figure verdict.
- **Measurement.** Saez, Slemrod and Giertz (2009/2012) makes elasticity sufficiency conditional; this is the broken middle link of Fig 3.
- **Low-overlap corroborators.** Simpson's paradox (statistics, aggregation reversal), the Lucas critique and Goodhart's law (policy invariance and metric gaming), rent-seeking and the Tullock paradox (political economy), and the Library of Babel essay (search and shape). These are genuinely different fields from the tax-pool sources and carry more independent weight than another tax paper would.

**Overlap declaration.** The NBER papers, the RePEc record, the Wiley record and the progress.org wiki all draw on the same public-economics evidence pool and count as one witness for the shape of the disagreement, not four. The Wikipedia reference pages are low overlap with that pool. The essay is by the framework author and has maximal overlap with the figures; it is canonical reading, not independent corroboration.
