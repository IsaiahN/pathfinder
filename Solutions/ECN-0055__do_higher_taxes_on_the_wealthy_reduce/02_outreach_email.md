# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the tax claim is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the gap in `01` §3/F4 is understood as a gap. The output is a shape and a separator, not an empirical verdict. If it is sent as "higher taxes reduce rent-seeking", the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the tax claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in tax policy: when the top marginal rate rises, does productive investment fall, or does rent-seeking and concentration fall? I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `gap` on the direction, not `grounded`):** the question is not a two-way choice. It is a union of at least four overlapping response channels, not two. Two of them are the ones the question names (real effort or investment, and rent capture or bargaining), and at least two more sit across them (tax avoidance, and entry into a rent-seeking sector). The productive and rent-seeking outcomes also have different currencies and different anchors, so they cannot be averaged into a single "mainly" number. The figures I am using say: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"* and *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* What survives is a shape, not a verdict: the effect must be read per slot, the two outcomes kept in separate currencies, and the channels measured with an instrument that can tell real effort from rent capture.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that higher taxes do or do not reduce productive investment; that they do or do not reduce rent-seeking; that there is a rate beyond which revenue falls; and that any number describes the effect. The literature does not give one clean answer. One model finds that optimal top rates can stay modest even when all high earners are rent-seekers, and that the intuition linking rent-seeking to high top rates is not well grounded in formal theory. Another finds that if compensation bargaining is real, optimal rates should be higher. I am not choosing between them from inside this derivation.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. show that the two outcomes form a partition with a common currency, so "mainly" is a well-formed comparison;
> 2. produce a figure line, checked verbatim, that returns a magnitude or selects a channel;
> 3. show that a taxpayer kind has a fixed productive or rent-seeking valence independent of the residual and the governance habitat;
> 4. demonstrate a policy-invariant behavioral parameter across two regime changes with a stable instrument;
> 5. show that a secondary summary of a primary paper is a reliable witness where it contradicts the primary abstract (it is not; one did, and I withdrew it).
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** name the instrument you would trust to separate real effort from rent capture in the same rate change. If you think no such instrument is possible, say that instead. Your verdict on this output, recorded before any theory is shown, is the only thing that counts at this stage.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> — [name]

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape, and the five refuters. Ask what would count as a failure. Record the answer before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the public links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Do not pool their later agreement as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

A Track B reply, if one is ever received, is a conversation, not corroboration, and carries zero verdict weight.
