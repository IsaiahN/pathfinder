# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no control-backed measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about disturbance and resilience before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about disturbance and long-term resilience that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a deliberate disturbance increases long-term resilience only when it is an introduction that changes the system's contact with the habitat that settles it, when the cascade of that introduction has been enumerated outward beyond first-order effects, and when repeated disturbances are replaced by contact with situations that work differently from the system's own. Three consequences follow. First, a disturbance that leaves contact unchanged changes nothing, however strong the intervention looks. Second, the same move that helps can harm when its cascade is unenumerated, so this is a sometimes claim, not an always claim. Third, "resilience" is not one quantity: recovery speed, latitude, resistance, precariousness and cross-scale influence can move in opposite directions under the same disturbance, so the gain must be unbundled before anyone designs a test.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that disturbance is intrinsically good, that any particular disturbance such as prescribed fire is the answer, that this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of resilience. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a deliberate disturbance leaves measured contact unchanged yet produces a durable, per-patch improvement against a control across several rounds;
> 2. a disturbance reliably increases long-term resilience with no second-order cascade anywhere in a well-enumerated habitat;
> 3. a system renews its reach and keeps doing well across two decades by repeating an identical disturbance with nothing new entering from a different situation;
> 4. one intervention restores every component of resilience in both failure types and leaves nothing categorically different behind;
> 5. the improvement is visible only in a metric that moves when the manager pushes on it, or all the confirming studies share one evidence pool;
> 6. the proposed test cannot tell a genuine gain from a changed reading and no better instrument can be found.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A Track B style reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
