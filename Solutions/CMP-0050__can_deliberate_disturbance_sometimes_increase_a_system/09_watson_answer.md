## QUESTION

Can deliberate disturbance sometimes increase a system's long-term resilience, and how could that be tested?

Restated as an effect: why does R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| stay small for a long run in some disturbed slots and grow in others, and how could an outside observer distinguish a genuine gain from a changed reading without already believing the answer? R is indexed per patch and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: a controlled experiment or a natural experiment, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29. The word "resilience" is itself a union, so it must be split before the question is searched (Fig 9 :5).

Option A, disturbance is intrinsically resilience-building, predicts a monotonic benefit and one lever. Option B, a disturbance helps only when it changes contact, enumerates its cascade and meets a differing closure, and the gain is a union of components measured against a control, predicts a conditional, non-monotonic benefit with two instruments. The verdict below is Option B.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Scale: the disturbance is applied at one scale but contact cascades across scales, so the residual is intrinsically cross-scale and the membrane transform is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** a deliberate disturbance can increase long-term resilience when it is an introduction that changes contact with the habitat that settles the system, when its cascade is enumerated outward through the habitat, and when repetition is replaced by contact with a closure that differs; the gain is a union of distinct components with distinct instruments, and it is established only by a control-backed, source-discriminating measurement. It fails when contact is unchanged, when the cascade is left unenumerated (kudzu), when the same closure is handed along (motion, not progress), or when the metric that motivated the intervention is used as its own evidence. The verdict is kept separate per domain, never averaged. Mode `specified`. The one apparent world conflict (Walker's "resilience is not always a good thing") was returned to the census and re-mapped to "which basin, which component"; it did not flip the verdict. Which particular disturbance and metric work in a given habitat is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected intervention and misdirected diagnosis. A system failing C2 would be sold more of the same disturbance, or a system failing C1 would be told to import more novelty. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded: C1 and C2 are recorded separately, so the error is locatable.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the seven pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) contact unchanged yet a durable controlled gain, (2) a reliable gain with no second-order cascade in a well-enumerated habitat, (3) two decades of identical disturbance with new ground-verified readings and no import, (4) one intervention restoring all components with no categorically different residue, or (5) the gain visible only in the motivating metric. A source-discriminating instrument that separates a genuine gain from a changed reading would move the measurement half toward `grounded`; a figure re-census that admits idempotence failure inside a closed set would break the reach half.
