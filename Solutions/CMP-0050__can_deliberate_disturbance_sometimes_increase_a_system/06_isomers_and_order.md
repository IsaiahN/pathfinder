## Q1 - Are two systems that received the same disturbance isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Two patches with the same species list, the same disturbance regime and the same climate have the same atoms. If one composes those atoms as a tightly coupled, repeated sequence and the other as a reconfigurable network whose cascade crosses scales, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the species list and the disturbance prescription. This is the figure-grounded version of the ecological intuition that it is not what you burn, it is what the burning connects to, but it is stronger, because it says the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the two systems share only a pattern (both show `disturbance → release → recovery`) while differing in which actors and conditions occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A species-and-disturbance inventory that finds no difference between two patches cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bonds have to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Hamilton and Burton 2023 give this a concrete instance: they report *"mutually exclusive fire-reinforced bi-stable grassland and forest states"* under the same climate. Same climatic atoms, different fire-reinforced arrangement, different substance. That is an isomer reading observed in a forest-grassland mosaic, not an analogy.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two systems with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in long-term resilience with no difference in ground contact or imported actors, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat history (D2), which moves the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Hamilton and Burton's bistability, low overlap), single-source for the general ecology application.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a system facing a proposed disturbance:

1. **Perception.** Can the observer read the system's state before and after at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests. Hamilton and Burton's before-and-after sampling is an example of this link working.
2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for what this disturbance affords, and can the assessor say what would count as an increase in resilience rather than a change in readings? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Aslan et al. 2024 confirm the symptom: *"Achieving common and reliable resilience metrics that cross systems and contexts remains elusive."*
3. **Objective.** Can the assessor state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only indicators cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information: which disturbance, which control, which cascade distance to enumerate.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry the method to the next patch.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain: C1 (contact change with enumerated cascade) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (reach renewed) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the manager names what worked but never imports a new atom from a differing closure. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Link 1 has monitoring data; link 5 has almost none. Budget will naturally pile up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a system whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit, though not the dependency ordering.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adaptive cycle / panarchy** (Walker et al. 2004) | release and reorganization as the source of novelty; cross-scale influence | close to C2 and to Fig 4 :20, Fig 7 :36 | A mechanism-level prescription for which disturbance to apply; it is a heuristic, and Walker et al. say so |
| **Engineering resilience** (return time to equilibrium) | speed of recovery | one component, close to resistance | Multiple basins; Walker et al. say return time *"does not measure all of the ways in which a system may fail"*; it conflicts with the union split in F6 until re-mapped as one component |
| **Intermediate disturbance hypothesis** | a non-monotonic relation between disturbance and diversity | corroborates the shape's non-monotonicity only | Diversity is not resilience; primary not opened this session, so no substantive quote is used |
| **Rigidity trap** (MathisonSlee et al. 2022) | former strength becoming inflexibility | Fig 7 :36, Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 12 :119 | It names the failure, not the discriminating instrument; it is a resilience-pool witness |
| **Ecosystem engineering / prescribed fire** (Jones et al. 1994 via secondary record; Hamilton and Burton 2023) | introduction as the seat's asymmetric move | matches Fig 11 :61-65 and :49-51 | It is a binding from the ecology frame; without provenance and a shadow test it has no framework verdict weight (Fig 8 :50-52) |
| **Stress testing** | a deliberately applied perturbation as a probe | close to Fig 5 :46 (manufactured gradient) | The test harness is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70): a synthetic solve proves wiring, never capability |
| **Luck / information asymmetry** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40 *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first, second, fourth and fifth are all in one resilience evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that only the fire-ecology field supplies a genuinely low-overlap mechanism witness.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the adaptive-cycle emphasis on release and reorganization, as C2 and Fig 7 :36;
- the four-component decomposition of resilience, as F6 and Fig 9 :5;
- the rigidity-trap emphasis on the strength that turns bad, as Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119;
- the ecosystem-engineering emphasis on introduction, as Fig 11 :61-65;
- the stress-test emphasis on deliberate perturbation, as Fig 5 :46, with the harness caveat of Fig 11 :70.

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific disturbance or the specific metric that will work in a patch that has not been enumerated. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two systems with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two systems sharing only a `disturbance → release → recovery` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard resilience explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
