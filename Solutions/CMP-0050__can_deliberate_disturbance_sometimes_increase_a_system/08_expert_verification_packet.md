## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A deliberate disturbance increases long-term resilience only when three things hold: it is an introduction that changes the system's contact with the habitat that settles it, its cascade is enumerated outward beyond first-order effects, and repeated disturbances give way to contact with situations that work differently from the system's own. The same move with an unenumerated cascade can harm, so this is a sometimes claim, not an always claim. "Resilience" is not one quantity: recovery speed, latitude, resistance, precariousness and cross-scale influence can move in opposite directions under the same disturbance, so the gain must be unbundled before a test is designed. Even then the gain is established only by a control-backed, source-discriminating measurement, not by the metric that motivated the intervention. The claim is a shape that any candidate answer must have; it does not name which disturbance or which metric wins.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal plus one ecology-facing check

- **Statement:** a disturbance that leaves contact unchanged changes nothing; introduction is available and exclusion is not; this is permanent work, and an unenumerated cascade is the failure mode.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
  - Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
  - Fig 11 :61 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
  - Fig 11 :63 *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
  - Fig 11 :65 *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*
  - Fig 11 :52 *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Hamilton and Burton 2023 write that *"External factors - particularly past heavy livestock grazing and fire suppression - have caused ecosystem precariousness that can be addressed with management actions"* and that *"Historically, both grassland and forest ecosystems experienced high-frequency, low-severity fire regimes. Since European settlement and introduction of livestock grazing and fire exclusion, trees have encroached on grasslands"*. Does that not show exclusion of disturbance creating precariousness and reintroduction as the available remedy? A "no", with a case where exclusion improved long-term outcomes, is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a deliberate disturbance that leaves all measured contact variables unchanged yet produces a durable per-patch improvement against a control across several rounds; or a disturbance that reliably increases long-term resilience with no second-order cascade anywhere in a well-enumerated habitat. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one measurement-facing check

- **Statement:** "resilience" is a union that must be split before a lever is chosen; repeating the same disturbance adds no atom, and long-term gain is a chain of differing rooms selected against the surfaced residual.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
  - Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
  - Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
  - Fig 7 :32 *"→ one long room: motion, no progress"*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Walker et al. 2004 state that *"Different management interventions would be required to enhance resilience for each of these"* and list latitude, resistance, precariousness and panarchy. Aslan et al. 2024 write that *"Achieving common and reliable resilience metrics that cross systems and contexts remains elusive."* Is a single "resilience score" in your field not already conflating things that move in opposite directions? If it is not, show the instrument that keeps them separate.
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores all components in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or two decades of identical disturbance with new ground-verified readings and no import from a differing closure. Either collapses the union or breaks idempotence.

### C3 - The instrument question

- **Statement:** a manufactured gradient is not evidence for itself; the test needs an anchor that does not move when pushed, a source-discriminating instrument, and a real habitat rather than a synthetic harness.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 5 :46 *"When nothing is scoring, the drive manufactures a gradient."*
  - Fig 5 :48 *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*
  - Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
  - Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
  - Fig 6 :34 *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*
  - Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** from the framework essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Hamilton and Burton 2023 report that *"we treated the wildfire event as a natural experiment, sampling post-wildfire plant species composition and soil moisture"*. Name one instrument in your field that distinguishes a genuine gain in resilience from a changed reading when both produce the same recovery-time curve, or say why none exists.
- **Refuter:** a proposed test under which a genuine gain and a changed reading are indistinguishable and no better instrument can be built or found; or a synthetic result that transfers unchanged to a real habitat with all actors and cascades reproduced. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about ecosystems, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about a landscape-level resilience score or a percentage of variance explained.
- Not that disturbance is always good, or that fire suppression is always bad. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that any specific disturbance or metric is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the resilience literature independently corroborates this. Walker, MathisonSlee and Aslan share one evidence pool. Hamilton and Burton supply the one low-overlap mechanism witness, and it settles a case, not the general shape.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-patch study that splits the components of resilience, enumerates the cascade, records bonds as well as actors, and uses a controlled perturbation or a naturally occurring one with before and after measurements. Concretely:

1. **Contact test:** does the disturbance change what the habitat pays for, or only the system's internal state? Indicators: the system's own score moving while the ground does not (Fig 1 :27, Fig 10 :26).
2. **Cascade test:** were second- and third-order effects enumerated before introduction, and did any appear after? Fig 11 :52.
3. **Differing-closure test:** did a new atom enter from a situation whose closure differs, or was the same disturbance repeated? Fig 8 :32, Fig 7 :36.
4. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a genuine gain in resilience from a changed reading, a basin shift, reduced severity or luck when all produce the same return-time curve? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.
5. **Component split:** which of latitude, resistance, precariousness and panarchy moved, and in which direction? Walker et al. 2004.
