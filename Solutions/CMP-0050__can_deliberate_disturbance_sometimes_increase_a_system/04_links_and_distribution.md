## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One pool, one witness.** Walker et al. 2004, MathisonSlee et al. 2022 and Aslan et al. 2024 all sit in the social-ecological resilience literature and draw on the same conceptual lineage (Holling, the Resilience Alliance, the adaptive cycle). They agree that resilience has multiple components and that disturbance regimes matter, but per Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Their agreement is one witness, not three.

- Walker et al. 2004 define resilience as *"the capacity of a system to absorb disturbance and reorganize while undergoing change so as to still retain essentially the same function, structure, identity, and feedbacks"*, give four components, and state that *"Different management interventions would be required to enhance resilience for each of these."* This **corroborates F6**, the unbundling condition, and the seat-like role of adaptability. It also warns that *"resilience is not always a good thing"*, which was handled by re-census R1, not by a softer reading.
- MathisonSlee et al. 2022 report a *"rigidity trap, where a system stuck in one phase of the adaptive cycle becomes highly connected, rigid, and inflexible. System capacity for novelty is diminished"* and note that *"the release and reorganization phase is a natural part of an ecosystem often hampered by policies and management in the context of forest fire management"*. This **corroborates F4**: fire suppression can be the authored room that stops the chain, and release is where novelty enters. They also report an inverted U-shaped diversity-resilience relation, which corroborates the **non-monotonic, sometimes** shape.
- Aslan et al. 2024 report *"Achieving common and reliable resilience metrics that cross systems and contexts remains elusive"* and *"The power of meta-analysis in this arena remains limited due to few quantitative studies that test individual variables."* This **corroborates F5 and F7**: the measurement half of the question is the hard half, and pooled agreement is not a substitute for controlled tests.

**Low-overlap witnesses, each from a different field and each read directly:**

1. **Fire ecology and ecosystem state transitions.** Hamilton and Burton 2023, *Wildfire disturbance reveals evidence of ecosystem resilience and precariousness in a forest-grassland mosaic* (Ecosphere, via the opened Zenodo record). They write that *"we treated the wildfire event as a natural experiment"* and that *"External factors - particularly past heavy livestock grazing and fire suppression - have caused ecosystem precariousness that can be addressed with management actions."* They report *"mutually exclusive fire-reinforced bi-stable grassland and forest states"*. This corroborates F2 and F3 and supplies a real instance of a source-discriminating instrument arriving: a perturbation already running in the world, measured before and after. It is not the same pool as management strategy or cognition.
2. **Ecological concepts and terminology.** The opened Wikipedia records supply the Holling definition and the Jones et al. 1994 ecosystem-engineering concept, including kudzu as an introduced engineer that *"crowds out native plant species"*. These are secondary records, not primary readings of Holling 1973 or Jones et al. 1994. They corroborate the vocabulary of F2 only, and that limitation is recorded in the ledger.
3. **Human cognition and search.** The three framework essays are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. Their author wrote the figures, so they carry no independent corroboration weight (Fig 2 :40).

**Sources that were deliberately not used as witnesses.** The Intermediate Disturbance Hypothesis was considered as a candidate for the "sometimes" claim, but diversity is not resilience and the primary was not opened, so it appears nowhere as a substantive quote. The Wiley journal pages for Hamilton and Burton and for the Journal of Ecology article returned 403; the Zenodo record and the search-result metadata were used instead, and that is recorded honestly.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if the residual is not described first. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.

**A hard practical constraint.** The Stockholm Resilience Centre homepage lists a "Use of AI" link, and Ecology and Society requires an anonymised manuscript authored under its own rules with a data and code availability statement. This packet is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude, with the AI contribution disclosed under the venue's policy. The AI policy page itself was not opened this session, so its exact terms are not claimed here.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list. A Zenodo deposit is a reasonable way to make the pre-registered refuter citable before any theory is shown.
