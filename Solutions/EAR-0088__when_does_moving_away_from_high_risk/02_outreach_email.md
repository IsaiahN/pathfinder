# Outreach draft: check the shape before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive a check against the reading of a researcher in climate migration or planned relocation. The offer collapses if the first-stage output does not hold.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about when relocation is safer than defending in place

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about planned relocation or managed retreat. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "moving away" and "staying and rebuilding" are not risk versus no risk. Both are operations on a habitat. The figures put it this way: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." (Fig 11 :66) A move therefore has to reproduce everything the community's goal required, and it inherits actors at the destination that the movers did not choose: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." (Fig 11 :67)
>
> The shape claim I am asking you to check is a set of conditions, not a number. A move is safer than defending in place only if, measured per household and per subgroup rather than as a mean, the substituted habitat reproduces the goal-required conditions, does not inherit or introduce actors whose cascade defeats the goal, and does not shift risk onto the receiving community. The comparison has to be settled by outcomes that do not move when a planner revises a forecast. I am explicitly **not** claiming a universal probability, cost ratio or date at which moving wins. The framework returns a shape, not a threshold. I record that absence as a gap, not as silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a real relocation in which the destination has no inherited actors and no goal-required conditions to reproduce, with lower per-slot outcomes than defence;
> 2. an instrument that registers a missing condition before the goal fails, or an inherited actor before it acts, on independent readings;
> 3. a protection measure that removes hazard contact without renewed work and without new cascades, over a horizon long enough for second-order effects;
> 4. one variable that orders all cases monotonically, with no subset where the opposite lever wins.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part of the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the material is below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Mach, Kraan, Hino, Siders, Johnston and Field 2019, "Managed retreat through voluntary buyouts of flood-prone properties", Science Advances: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6785245
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures link. Run it on a relocation or defence decision you know well and can check against realised outcomes. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## What this email deliberately does not do

- It does not ask the recipient to endorse managed retreat, or to oppose it.
- It does not rank the recipient by how likely they are to agree.
- It does not present a benefit-cost ratio as the arbiter. Fig 12 :125 says "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- It does not send the full architecture before the first verdict is recorded.
- It does not pool a later agreement as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
