# The Tether's answer on moving away versus defending in place

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain conditions, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal switching number. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6). No outcome measurement was taken.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

A community faces hazard contact that is rising or persistent. Two levers are available: continued protection and reconstruction in place (P), and moving away through relocation or managed retreat (M). "Safer" is not a property of either lever. It is the realised outcome per household and per subgroup, read against an anchor that does not move when a planner revises a forecast. The residual must remain indexed by slot: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19) A mean gain can hide the household left behind or the receiving neighbourhood harmed.

The question is a union of four overlapping conditions, not one dial: whether the hazard outruns what protection can hold without perpetual work; whether the goal-required conditions can be reproduced in a destination; whether the destination's inherited actors defeat that goal; and whether the move shifts risk to the receiving community. "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." (Fig 9 :57)

## 2. The shape

1. **Both levers are habitat operations, and moving substitutes rather than removes.** "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." (Fig 11 :66) A relocation exits one contact field and enters another. It is not a move from risk to safety; it is a change of habitat.

2. **Two things are required of the destination and both are silent before they register.** "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." (Fig 11 :67) "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" (Fig 11 :68) and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts" (Fig 11 :69). A plan verified at the planning table proves wiring, not capability: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." (Fig 11 :70)

3. **Protection is permanent work and must change contact to matter.** "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." (Fig 11 :61) "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." (Fig 11 :62) "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." (Fig 11 :63) "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." (Fig 11 :40) And repeating the same rebuild is one long room: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." (Fig 7 :36)

4. **The comparison is second order.** Each path must be settled against the ground before it can be compared: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." (Fig 12 :104) "safer than" is a threshold comparison (⋛) between two settled molecules, not a property read off a hazard map.

5. **Stay and move are isomers under a generous atom binding, members of one functional group under the strict reading.** "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." (Fig 12 :117) When the atom binding is refused, the weaker form uses "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." (Fig 12 :123) See `06_isomers_and_order.md`, where the correction to my own first reading is kept visible.

6. **No standing safe-destination list.** "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." (Fig 11 :49) "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." (Fig 11 :51) A road, employer or host community changes sign with the residual being read.

7. **Two cost currencies, never one.** "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." (Fig 12 :125) Bits price the description of a plan; actions price finding out whether it holds. "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." (Fig 12 :130)

8. **The anchor is realised outcome, not forecast agreement.** "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." (Fig 2 :29) "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35) "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides" (Fig 9 :42)

**The resulting separator, per site and per slot:** moving is safer than protection and reconstruction only if (a) protection cannot hold the per-slot residual without perpetual work that does not actually change hazard contact, (b) the destination reproduces what the community's goal required, (c) the inherited and introduced actors at the destination do not defeat that goal and do not shift risk to the receiving community, and (d) all of this is read on outcomes that do not update when a planning frame revises its forecast. A site failing any condition is not a case for the opposite lever by default; it is a case still in the union.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that moving is always safer once a hazard exceeds a threshold. No figure line returns such a threshold, and that absence is a declared `gap`.
- Not that staying is always safer, or that defence is never justified. Condition (a) is empirical per site.
- Not that a benefit-cost ratio decides the case. The two currencies do not add.
- Not that an aggregated expected-loss figure is sufficient. Per-slot indexing is the measuring rule.
- Not that a plan's suitability model is the ground. It is a frame that must range against realised outcomes.
- Not `grounded`. No post-move or stay-put outcome measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A site-specific decision is settled by instruments, not by more argument:

1. **Per-slot outcome instruments** for both paths, with a named measure for each outcome slot, read separately by frames that do not share an evidence pool. Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
2. **A discriminator for the two silent failures.** Mach et al. 2019 documented "relocations to areas with equal flood risk and greater social vulnerability"; a hazard map alone reads green on that case and cannot comment on the source. An instrument that registers a missing reproduction or an inherited actor before it acts would close F2.
3. **A maintained monitoring channel.** "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24). The IPCC AR6 WGII chapter calls monitoring and evaluation "essential for tracking adaptation progress and learning about adaptation success and maladaptation"; the managed-retreat literature reports no agreed monitoring protocols for many sites.
4. **A refusal test.** For a defence whose residual persists and does not trend down, the owed term is a refusal, not another rebuild (Formula step 2; Operators table on ¬).
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** habitat-free relocation; a pre-failure instrument; permanent protection without renewed work; a single monotonic ordering of all cases; a verbatim figure line returning a universal threshold; a standing safe-destination list; a calibrated single scalar for both currencies.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached on the universal number, reachable per site.** The figures give the shape any answer must have and the conditions that must be read. They do not give a switching probability, cost ratio, return period or date. "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). The stop is recorded with the lineage, the gap and the refuters so a later reader can locate an error rather than only feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that moving away is safer than defending in place, and it does not give a threshold for when it becomes so. It says that both options are operations on a habitat: moving substitutes one habitat for another rather than removing risk, so a move is safer only if the new habitat reproduces what the community's goal required, does not inherit actors that defeat that goal, and does not push risk onto the receiving community. Protection is permanent work on a habitat nobody authored, and a defence that does not change contact changes nothing. The two options are different arrangements of the same kinds of thing, so the bond and not the ingredient list decides the outcome; their costs come in two currencies that do not add. The comparison is made per household and per subgroup, after each path is settled against realised outcomes that no planning forecast can move. What the framework offers is that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments a particular site would need. The universal switching number stays open.
