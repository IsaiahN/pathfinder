# Expert verification packet: the Tether on relocation versus defence in place

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate-migration researcher, planner or relocation practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal switching number. Not `grounded`.** No outcome measurement was taken. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

---

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Moving away" and "staying and rebuilding" are not risk versus no risk. They are two arrangements of a habitat. Moving substitutes one habitat for another, so a move is safer than defence in place only if, measured per household and per subgroup, the destination reproduces what the community's goal required, the inherited and introduced actors there do not defeat that goal or shift risk onto the receiving community, and defence cannot hold the same per-slot residual without perpetual work that fails to change hazard contact. The comparison is second order: each path must be settled against realised outcomes before the two are compared. There is no standing safe-destination list, because an actor's sign depends on the residual being read. Two cost currencies, description and action, do not add into one benefit-cost number. The framework returns this shape with conditions; it does not return a universal probability, cost ratio or date.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** relocation is habitat substitution, not risk removal, and it has two silent failure modes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67, "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68, "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"; Fig 11 :69, "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".
- **What to check:** is the substitution law stated at the right level? Does the line govern a habitat operation rather than only a laboratory practice? A re-census in `01` and `11` resolves the apparent strain: the law holds for a test harness and for a town, with the actor set changing and the operation fixed.
- **Refuter:** a relocation in which the destination has no inherited actors and no reproduction requirement, with lower per-slot outcomes than defence, settled on outcomes that do not move when a forecast changes. Or an instrument that registers a missing condition before the goal fails, or an inherited actor before it acts, with independent readings and a stated error rate.

### C2 — practice-facing; checkable against the managed-retreat literature

- **Statement:** the per-slot comparison and the warning about inherited actors are visible in the ground literature, so the shape is not an empty framework claim.
- **What to check:** Mach, Kraan, Hino, Siders, Johnston and Field 2019, *Science Advances* (open access at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6785245), analysed more than 40,000 FEMA-funded voluntary buyouts. Check two findings: (a) local governments in higher-population and higher-income counties were more likely to administer buyouts, while bought-out properties were concentrated in more socially vulnerable neighbourhoods inside those counties; (b) the discussion records "relocations to areas with equal flood risk and greater social vulnerability". Check IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17 (https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-17/): "Whether an adaptation is considered successful is context specific. It depends on who evaluates adaptation and at what time", and monitoring and evaluation are "essential for tracking adaptation progress and learning about adaptation success and maladaptation".
- **Figure side of the triangulation:** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Refuter:** show a scalar decision metric that preserves a live subgroup slot under a constructed case where the mean improves and the subgroup worsens; or show that the county and neighbourhood patterns in Mach et al. are artefacts that disappear when the data are corrected. If the empirical pattern fails, C2 loses its corroboration but C1 stands or falls on its own figure check.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 4, 7 and 12

- **Statement:** protection is permanent work, repeating the same rebuild is one long room, the round trip from plan to lived habitat is lossy, and the two cost currencies do not add.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :63, "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 7 :36, "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 4 :30, "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 12 :125, "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- **Refuter:** a protection measure that removes hazard contact without renewed work and without new cascades, with per-slot outcomes showing the residual does not reappear over a horizon long enough for second-order effects; or a suitability abstraction whose round-trip gap is zero on held-out relocations; or one scalar that calibrates both description cost and action cost.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that relocation is always better than defence, or that defence is never justified. The claim is a set of conditions to be read per site.
- Not a universal probability, return period, cost ratio or date at which moving wins. That number is a declared `gap`, not an approximation waiting to be filled in.
- Not that an aggregate expected-loss figure settles the case. Per-slot indexing is the measuring rule.
- Not that a suitability model is the ground. A model is a frame and must range against outcomes neither the model nor the planning team can move.
- Not that the two options are unqualified isomers. Under the strict atom binding they are members of a functional group; both readings and the correction are in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not `grounded`. The whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot outcome instrument for each path, read separately by frames that do not share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :31).
2. A discriminator for the silent failures: something that registers a missing goal-required condition before the goal fails, or an inherited actor before it acts (Fig 10 :24, the channel must be maintained).
3. A refusal test for defences whose residual persists and does not trend down (Formula step 2; Operators table on ¬).
4. A site-specific comparison made only after both paths are settled against the ground (Fig 12 :104; Fig 9 :42).
5. A verbatim figure line returning a universal switching quantity would close the declared gap; none was found after a full census.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :66-69 and Fig 1 :19. Four short passages. Say whether the substitution claim and the per-slot rule are claims you would accept as structural conditions.
2. Open Mach et al. 2019 and check the county-versus-neighbourhood pattern and the "equal flood risk and greater social vulnerability" sentence. Say whether they are consistent with C2 or whether they are artefacts.
3. Name one instrument that would register a missing condition or an inherited actor before the goal fails, or say why none is possible at this site.
4. Try to falsify C3 with a protection case whose residual genuinely trends down and needs no renewed work.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
