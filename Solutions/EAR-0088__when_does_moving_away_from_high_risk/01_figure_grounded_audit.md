## 0. The residual, stated as an effect

A location carries hazard contact that is rising, persistent, or both. Two levers are available. Lever P: keep protecting and rebuilding in place. Lever M: move the household or community away. The question asks when M becomes "safer."

"Safer" is not a property of a lever. It is the residual after the lever acts, measured against realised outcomes. The ground is what settles it, and it does not move when a planner changes a forecast: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." (Fig 2 :29)

Stated in effect terms:

> R = the difference, per household and per subgroup and per outcome slot, between what a protection-and-rebuild path predicts and what actually happens, versus what a move-to-a-new-habitat path predicts and what actually happens, over a horizon long enough for the silent failures of each path to register.

The residual must stay indexed. "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19) "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." (Fig 1 :22) A county-level mean safety gain can hide the household left behind or the receiving neighbourhood harmed. That is not a side caveat. It is the measuring rule.

The sought-for shape was bound in `README.md` before the census. It is an ordinary term with a residual, so it can be refused. It is not a number. Fig 13 says a system with a closure "returns" only a shape rather than a name, and Fig 9 :32 says "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** many slots, not one. At minimum one slot per affected subgroup crossed with one slot per outcome kind (life and safety, housing, livelihood and income, services including health and education, social and cultural continuity, and the receiving area's outcomes). A single scalar "net safety" is an aggregation error, not a simplification.
- **Symmetry:** the two levers are not interchangeable and the question is ordered. P keeps a habitat and changes the community's contact with the hazard. M substitutes a habitat. "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." (Fig 11 :66) The question of when M becomes safer than P is a comparison (⋛), not a conjunction or an identity.
- **Scale:** first order is direct hazard contact and direct outcome per household. Second order is the cascade, because "And contact cascades." (Fig 11 :52) A protection failure, a livelihood loss or an influx into a receiving area can be two or three steps out, exactly as "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." (Fig 11 :53)

**One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor, and whether a nameable anchor exists.**

- **D1, the origin habitat under protection and reconstruction.** Actors: the hazard, defences and infrastructure, local institutions, households, insurers, land market, ecosystems. Currency: realised outcomes and the repeated work needed to hold them. Anchor: measured outcomes at origin, which do not update when a model revises its hazard curve. Nameable anchor exists, so D1 is a domain.
- **D2, the destination habitat after a move.** Actors: host community, land tenure, labour market, services, local governance, destination ecology and climate, the relocated households themselves. Currency: realised outcomes after arrival. Anchor: post-move outcomes. This anchor cannot be read before the move, which is why the two failure modes are silent (F2). Nameable anchor exists in principle, so D2 is a domain.
- **D3, the decision and forecast frame.** Actors: planners, models, projections, benefit-cost ratios, consultancies. Currency: coherence and agreement among forecasts. Anchor: none of its own. A forecast frame must range against the ground outside it: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." (Fig 2 :45) A domain is still present, because its anchor is nameable (D1 and D2 outcomes), but the frame has no standing to supply the verdict.
- **D4, the receiving area and neighbouring communities.** Actors: host residents, their infrastructure and services, local employers, the new arrivals. Currency: their outcomes, which are part of R even though they are not the decision's nominal subject. Anchor: their measured outcomes. Nameable anchor exists, so D4 is a domain.

**Partition or union?** Union, not partition. "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." (Fig 9 :4-5) The conditions overlap: whether the hazard outruns protection, whether the goal can be reproduced in another habitat, whether the destination's inherited actors defeat that goal, and whether the move shifts risk onto D4. "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." (Fig 9 :55) The overlap of D2 and D4 is a compound with its own loop, as in Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." (Fig 3 :20)

1. Describe R per slot in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28) before choosing protection or retreat as the frame.
2. Enumerate the habitat by contact and cascade before evaluating either lever: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." (Fig 11 :44)
3. Bind "safer" as an ordinary term with a non-updating anchor (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65).
4. Evaluate D1 and D2 per slot, then D4, then compare. A reading taken before step 2 is "a reading of nothing" in Fig 3's sense.
5. Let the ground decide last: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides" (Fig 9 :42).

**Strain test applied before findings.** The phrase "isolation" in Fig 11 originally names laboratory isolation, not population relocation. Does applying it to a human move strain the line? No, after re-census. Fig 11 :66 states the invariant at the level of habitat substitution, not at the level of a discipline's laboratory practice: the habitat is substituted, not removed. The same law holds for a test harness (Fig 11 :70) and for a town. The actor set changes; the operation does not. Recorded as S1 in `11_audit.md` and resolved by re-census rather than smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

Every finding below states its mode. `triangulated` means two figure lines with low overlap between their subject matter. `single-source` means one figure line bears and is not upgraded.

### F1 — Moving substitutes a habitat; it does not remove risk (decisive, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

Fig 11 :28-30 names the two consequences: **"two silent failure modes"** and **"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"**.

A common framing treats protection as keeping risk in place and retreat as removing it. The figures refuse that framing. There is no habitat-free option. A household that moves exits the origin's hazard contact but enters a destination's contact field: housing market, land tenure, employer, services, host community, governance, ecology, and often a different hazard. What was required of the original place (livelihood, kin network, school, clinic, tenure, cultural practice) has to be reproduced, and what arrives with the new place is not chosen by the mover.

Triangulation: Fig 4 :27 says **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."** and Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip and its gap to be measured. The habitat law and the abstraction law are different objects with low overlap, so the agreement is weak corroboration inside the framework, not two world witnesses.

**Refuter:** show a real relocation in which every goal-required condition was reproduced and no inherited or introduced actor produced a cascade against the goal, with per-slot outcomes measured before and after and a non-updating anchor. If that holds on all outcome slots, F1's warning is falsified for that case and the substitution verdict narrows.

### F2 — The two failure modes are invisible until the goal fails or the actor acts (`specified`, single-source on Fig 11, corroborated by the round-trip law)

Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**

Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

This is the reason a relocation plan cannot be validated at the planning table. A model that lists the destination's services and jobs has verified the wiring of the plan, not the capability of the relocated community in that habitat. The missing condition registers only when the goal fails; the inherited actor registers only when it acts. In world terms, the Mach et al. 2019 study of more than 40,000 US buyouts documented "relocations to areas with equal flood risk and greater social vulnerability," which is exactly an inherited-actor failure that a pre-move hazard map would not show. The IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17 notes that "what is successful in the near term is not necessarily successful in the long term," which is the same time structure.

**Refuter:** an instrument that reliably registers a goal-required missing condition before the goal fails, or an inherited actor before it acts, on independent readings and with a stated false-positive rate. That would close the gap F2 names.

### F3 — Protection is work on an unauthored habitat, and protection that does not change contact changes nothing (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 11 :62: **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Protection and reconstruction add actors and structures to a habitat nobody authored: a wall, a pump, a levee, a code, an insurance pool. They cannot subtract the habitat or the contact. The work has to be repeated because the ground does not hold still, which is why a defended settlement is a maintained condition, not a solved one. The engineering literature corroborates the mechanism from a low-overlap field: the Wikipedia levee article records that artificial levees "can lead to an elevation of the natural riverbed over time" and that "The increased channelization and overall reduction in floodplain area amplifies the flood magnitudes in the highest impact storms, requiring even more engineering to compensate." More protection, in that reading, can grow the residual it was meant to shrink.

Fig 7 :36 adds a second, independent reason repeated reconstruction is not progress: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Rebuilding the same structure after the same flood is handing the question along a chain of identical rooms.

**Refuter:** a protection measure that permanently removes the hazard contact without renewed work, with per-slot outcomes showing the residual does not reappear and no new cascade is introduced. Levee-failure and riverbed-aggradation cases are counterexamples to the universal form, not to the conditional reading.

### F4 — The comparison must stay per slot and follow the cascade (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades."**

Fig 11 :53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

A decision that reports only a mean change in expected annual loss has thrown away the signal that says where to look. The residual that matters can be three steps out: a buyout cluster that removes households but leaves a service unviable for those who stay; a relocation site that raises land prices for hosts; a rebuilt defence that shifts scour to the next reach. Mach et al. 2019 found buyout deployment concentrated in richer, more densely populated counties while the bought-out properties sat in poorer, more vulnerable neighbourhoods inside those counties. That is two slots moving in opposite directions at once, invisible in a county average.

**Refuter:** a decision metric that preserves a live slot under aggregation, demonstrably, with a test in which a subgroup gets worse while the mean improves and the metric still fires.

### F5 — "Safer" needs a non-updating anchor; forecasts are frames (`general` on the rule, `specified` on the application, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 9 :42: **"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"**

A benefit-cost ratio, a hazard map and a suitability model are frames. They are useful for narrowing and useless as the final arbiter, because they update when the planning team revises its inputs. The anchor is the realised outcome, read separately by instruments that do not draw on the same evidence pool. Fig 2 :46: **"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."** The world literature agrees on the measurement structure: IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 17 says success is context specific, "it depends on who evaluates adaptation and at what time," and that "The ability to compare the risk situation post and prior adaptation is complicated through the long time horizons at which adaptation outcomes often become apparent."

**Refuter:** a decision rule whose anchor changes its answer when a planner's forecast changes, shown to still settle the question. Under Fig 2 that is not a better rule; it is panel 1 drift.

### F6 — The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a threshold on one variable (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5, quoted in section 1, requires splitting disagreements that do not shrink with effort. Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The four conditions are independent enough that each can bind alone: (a) the hazard outruns what protection can hold without perpetual work; (b) the goal-required conditions can be reproduced in a candidate destination; (c) the destination's inherited actors do not defeat the goal; (d) the move does not shift risk to D4. A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. This is why "when does moving become safer" cannot be answered with one number on one axis.

Hino, Field and Mach 2017 evaluated 27 cases resettling about 1.3 million people and organised them by two factors, who benefits and who initiates, finding that "Different sociopolitical dimensions emerge as particularly influential in each quadrant." A two-factor quadrant map is a union-style result, not a single threshold.

**Refuter:** evidence that one variable orders all cases monotonically, with no subset in which the opposite lever wins on any outcome slot. Then the union claim collapses to a partition and the search simplifies.

### F7 — No figure line returns the numerical "when"; the gap is declared (`gap`, unreachable at this budget)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134: **"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

After a genuine census across all figures and the Operators and Symbols tables, no line yields a universal probability, cost ratio or date at which M dominates P. This is `unreachable`, not silence, and not underivable in principle. A local decision can be settled by an instrument and a ground reading in a particular place; a universal switching number cannot be composed from the figure set. The honest output is the shape with conditions, plus the instrument list that would settle each site.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line, quoted verbatim, that returns a universal switching quantity for habitat substitution. Until one is shown, the gap stands. Refusing to invent a number is the correct response, not a failure to answer.

### F8 — Valence is relative to the residual, so there is no standing "safe destination" list (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

An employer, a road, a levee, a host community, a remittance flow or a resettlement agency is not safe or unsafe in itself. Its sign depends on which residual is being read. A road that evacuates one settlement can drain the receiving one. A buyout that reduces exposure for the household can reduce the tax base that keeps a clinic open for the household that remains. Managed retreat practitioners make the same point in effect terms: the Climigration Network describes communities deciding whether to "stay, move, or rebuild," and its community-led framing refuses a single externally authored answer.

Triangulation: Fig 12 :123 gives the structural form, **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Different-actor bindings over the same functional role are isomers (see `06`), and the bond decides the outcome.

**Refuter:** a standing list of destination properties that keeps its sign across changed residuals, validated on two different hazards with different goal sets.

### F9 — Description cost and action cost do not add into one benefit-cost number (`general`, single-source on Fig 12)

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

A relocation plan is cheap to describe once a site is chosen and expensive to settle because the silent failures only register after the goal runs. A protection plan can be expensive to describe and cheap to refute if one overtopping event settles it. Collapsing both into a single net-benefit figure makes the shortfall invisible. The two readings have to be held apart.

**Refuter:** a single scalar that predicts both the description cost and the action cost across cases, with demonstrated calibration on held-out relocations. Until then, one number is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

### F10 — Any imported comparison frame needs provenance and a shadow test (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :50-52: **"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."** and **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

A hazard-modelling frame and a community-knowledge frame can appear to agree while drawing on one evidence pool (the same consultancy, the same downstream data). That is one witness wearing two names. A genuine surplus requires low overlap, and the record of where each atom came from is the whole issue. The Platform on Disaster Displacement describes itself as a "multi-stakeholder forum for dialogue, information sharing as well as policy and normative development" and brings "policymakers, practitioners and researchers" together, which is the right structural arrangement only while those rooms keep separate evidence and separate readings.

**Refuter:** show two frames whose agreement survives an audit of their evidence sources and whose closures differ at the evidence level. Then the import has a source gate and the surplus is real.

### F11 — Monitoring is the seat's office, and a stake in the answer must be declared (`general` on the rule, `specified` on the application, single-source on Fig 10)

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :28: **"you have lost touch"**

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."**

Fig 10 :72: **"When the question changes, the room should change."**

Outcomes keep arriving; the monitoring channel is what fails through "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" (Fig 10 :25-26). This is why the world literature puts monitoring and evaluation at the centre: IPCC AR6 WGII says "Monitoring and evaluation (M&E) are essential for tracking adaptation progress and learning about adaptation success and maladaptation," while the Wikipedia managed-retreat article observes that "There are no agreed protocols on the monitoring of managed realignment sites" and that "the results of managed realignment schemes being extremely unpredictable." No protocol means no maintained channel to the ground.

A declared stake is structural, not a moral failing. A consultancy paid to design a retreat, a municipality defending a defence budget and a household that wants to stay all have interests in the outcome. Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"**. Declare the first; refuse to let it write the reading.

**Refuter:** a decision arrangement in which the same frame that authored the plan also supplies the verdict, and the verdict still ranges against an anchor that neither frame can move. Under Fig 10 that arrangement has stopped checking.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, in the words of the seat discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. Written here before the recommendation and before the WATSON synthesis.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Substitution refuted.** A relocation is shown in which the destination is habitat-free or risk-free in the relevant sense, with no inherited actors and no reproduction requirement, on per-slot post-move outcomes. (F1)
2. **Silent failures instrumented.** An instrument registers a missing goal-required condition before the goal fails, or an inherited actor before it acts, with independent readings and a stated error rate. (F2)
3. **Permanent protection.** A protection measure removes hazard contact without renewed work and without new cascades, settled on per-slot outcomes over a horizon long enough for second-order effects. (F3)
4. **Aggregation safe.** A single scalar decision metric preserves a live subgroup slot under a constructed case where the mean improves and the subgroup worsens. (F4)
5. **Union collapsed.** One variable orders all cases monotonically with no subset in which the opposite lever wins. (F6)
6. **Threshold found.** A verbatim figure line returns a universal switching quantity for habitat substitution. (F7)
7. **Standing list survives.** A destination-attribute list keeps its sign across changed residuals and two different hazards. (F8)
8. **Single currency works.** One scalar predicts both description cost and action cost on held-out relocations. (F9)

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the synthesis rewritten. Until one is run, no part of this folder is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Refuse the framing "move removes risk, stay keeps risk." Both levers operate on habitats. State the shape as substitution plus conditions (F1).
2. Describe R in effect terms per slot before choosing a frame, enumerate the habitat cascade, and only then compare (section 1; Fig 9 :27-28; Fig 11 :43-44).
3. Keep verdicts per domain separate. Never average D1, D2 and D4 into a single safety score (F4).
4. Build the anchor before the plan: independent post-move and stay-put outcome instruments, with a named seat that can refuse a verdict and a declared stake for every author (F5, F11).
5. Treat protection as permanent work and test whether it actually changes contact; treat repeated reconstruction as a chain of identical rooms (F3).
6. Unbundle the union. For each site, check the four conditions separately and report which one binds, rather than reporting a global answer (F6).
7. State the `gap` openly. No figure line returns a universal switching number, and inventing one would be a dead-reckoning error (F7).
8. Carry two cost readings, not one (F9).
9. Send the generator, the shape claim and the refuter, not a recording of this derivation: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** (Fig 4 :22)
