# Expert verification packet - the Tether on institutional change

**What this is:** a claim in a form an institutional theorist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structure, `gap` on empirical content. Not `grounded`.** No dataset or field test is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A general theory of institutional change must explain a two-way loop whose directions are not one mechanism. Rules shape choices through a frame, per person-situation, never by a direct rule-to-choice bond; the residual is R = |prediction - observation| per slot. People reshape rules by refusing a held rule whose residual persists and does not trend down; nothing is deleted from the record, but that rule loses its standing to be built on. New rules require contact with a frame whose closure differs, since search inside one closure adds no new kind. A settled rule is necessary but not sufficient for further composition, agreement inside a single evidence pool is a mirror chain rather than evidence, and there is no final rule set. The framework supplies that shape with falsifiers written in advance; which rules settle which outcomes is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and about the downward direction; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 3

- **Statement:** a rule shapes a choice only through a frame, and the effect is per person-slot, never a field average.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What to check:** does any figure permit a rule term to write a choice term with no belief, action and observation in between? Is the per-slot claim actually a constraint, or just a measurement preference?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated case where a rule determines a choice with no frame term between them, measured per person-slot; or a derivation of a direct rule-choice bond from the figures.

### C2 - framework-internal and about the upward direction; checkable by reading Figures 5, 6 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** rule change is a refusal (`¬`) of a held rule with a persistent flat residual, followed by a mint or an import from a different closure. The old rule stays in the record; the reach falls because something was added.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 - *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 5 :54-55 - *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Operators table :53 - negation means *"this does not hold"*.
- **What to check:** is the unary operator genuinely distinct from subtraction? Does the "reach falls by addition" rule match what institutional historians observe when rules are layered rather than repealed?
- **Refuter:** an institution changing a rule entirely inside its own closure, with no refused term and no external frame, and with the reach not falling.

### C3 - world-facing and about the verification arrangement; checkable against institutional theory literature

- **Statement:** conformity among organizations drawn from one evidence pool raises agreement without raising ground contact; the five gradual modes of change are observable forms of addition and refusal, not new kinds of institution.
- **Source, verbatim (figures):** Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** DiMaggio and Powell (1983) write that *"rational actors make their organizations increasingly similar as they try to change them"*; Streeck and Thelen (2005) name *"displacement, layering, drift, conversion, and exhaustion"*. Are these faithful readings, and does the isomorphism literature actually lack an independent ground in the cases cited?
- **Refuter:** show that conformity among frames sharing one evidence pool increased measured contact with an independent ground, or that two of the five modes leave identical traces on the same ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a final theory of institutional change; no last room (Fig 7 :6).
- Not a claim about which rules matter in any field. That part is `gap`, stated as a gap.
- Not "rules are commands" and not "institutions are structures" in the physical substrate sense. Both are bindings from a social-science frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that legitimacy, prestige or conformity settles a question. All are frames that update on opinion.
- Not that a growing rulebook is a growing reach (Fig 5 :69, Fig 6 :42-43).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was run; this is a simulated derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Per-slot instrument:** does the rule's effect register on identifiable person-situations rather than as a field average?
2. **Refusal trace:** is there an observable loss of composing standing while the rule remains in the record?
3. **Independence measure:** what is the evidence overlap among the frames whose agreement is being counted?
4. **Contact test:** did the reform change the habitat's contacts, or only the rule text?
5. **Standing falsifiers:** the four in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, written before this run.
