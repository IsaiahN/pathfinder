## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A person acts under incomplete information. Let Γ be the library the person currently holds (beliefs, habits, the rules taken as given, inherited priors). Let b be the belief, w the weighting between private history and inherited prior, a the action, and o′ what actually happens. The loop's own notation gives:

```
R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|   per slot s
```

Symbols table: *"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

Two effects must be explained, and they are not the same effect:

1. **Downward, rules shape choices.** A rule enters a person's Γ and changes Γ(b,a). It narrows what is predicted and therefore what is tried. The effect is per person-slot, not a field average.
2. **Upward, people reshape rules.** When a held rule keeps predicting wrongly, its residual *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* (Formula step 2). That is not a missing explanation, it is a wrong term. What is owed is a refusal: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"* (Fig 5 :53).

The residual R here is the gap between any proposed general theory of institutional change and the two effects it must close. Its arity is two conditions plus the cycle that joins them. Its symmetry is broken: downward is a production or sequence relation, upward is a refusal plus a mint. Its scale runs from one person's lifetime to a settled rule at field level to the open chain of fields.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two (rules to choices, choices to rules) joined in a cycle. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*; Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.
- **Symmetry:** broken. Downward is ordered (a rule precedes the choice it shapes); upward is a unary refusal (¬) plus a new term. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A refutation is the eighth operator, not a bond (Fig 12 amendment, Operators table :53-58).
- **Scale:** first order inside a person or an organization; second order when a settled rule becomes an atom for further rules (Fig 12 :104); third order across fields, where there is no final room (Fig 7 :6). The chain is dependency-ordered, not importance-ordered: Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | domain yet? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The person as frame** | individuals; Γ, b, w, a, o′ | prediction error R per slot; learning | the outcomes the person actually encounters (o′) | yes, nameable |
| **B. The institution as habitat / convention** | rules (formal and informal), organizations, enforcement, the field | compliance, legitimacy, transaction costs, reproduction of the practice | what the field's ground keeps: repeated coordination, survival, measured performance | yes, nameable |
| **C. The research and verification frame** | researchers, reviewers, journals, conferences | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement; in this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool | yes in principle, zero independence in this run |

The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*). What is in contact with domain A's residual includes family, peers, organizations, law, markets, professional certifications and measurement instruments; what contacts those includes funders, regulators and the wider ecology of fields. The list is read off the world, not invented.

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union.** Domains A and B overlap in the cycle: a person's choice is part of the habitat that reproduces the rule. Overlaps are compounds with their own loop, so they are not merged. Domain C is a checking arrangement over both. Because the split is a union, unbundle before searching (F1 below).

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Settle Domain A's per-slot residual before claiming a field-level rule changed (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).
2. Refuse before mint: formula step 2 puts REFUTED ahead of the four "what is missing" bins. *"the first four sort R by what is MISSING; the fifth by what is WRONG"* (`THE_FORMULA.md`).
3. Describe the residual in effect terms before selecting a frame: Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*.
4. Settle each first-order rule before composing a second-order theory of the field (Fig 12 :104).

## 3. Findings F1 to F11, each with a verbatim figure line and a refuter

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one hard question (decisive for census)

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.

A theory that treats "rules shape choices" and "people reshape rules" as one mechanism will build a single lever. By Fig 9 :55-56 a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. The intersection (the cycle) is a specific compound with its own loop.

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union rule + Fig 12 :121 cycle, low overlap between search theory and bond grammar).
- **Refuter:** show that one mechanism, with one ground and one currency, closes both directions without a compound at the overlap.

### F2 - Rules shape choices through a frame, per slot, never by a direct bond

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

A rule is part of Γ. It changes the prediction Γ(b,a) for a person-slot, and the world returns o′. Nothing in the loop permits a rule to write a choice directly; belief b and action a and observation o′ sit in between. The dependency chain makes the same point at a different level: the middle link (having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress) has the fewest instruments, and absence of an instrument is not evidence that the link works.

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 per-slot residual + Fig 3 dependency chain, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a case where a rule determines a choice with no belief, action or observation term in between, measured per person-slot.

### F3 - People reshape rules by refusal (¬), not by adding a description

Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 5 :54-55: *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* Operators table :53: *"this does not hold"* with the test *"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."*

Persistent flat residuals are the ranking signal for which held rule to refuse (Formula step 2, *"the residual that PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"*). The old rule stays in the record; what falls is its standing to be composed over. This is why institutional change often looks like layering, drift or conversion rather than clean replacement: the record grows while the reach falls.

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 5 fifth bin + Operators table negation + Fig 6 :42-43, different figures).
- **Refuter:** show an institution changing a rule by pure composition inside its existing closure, with no refused term and no negation operator, and with the reach not falling.

### F4 - A field cannot generate its own critique from inside its closure

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

A profession trained on one literature, scored by one metric and reviewed by one community is one closure. More search inside it visits more of the same space. The wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs, and the import carries a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8: *"source gate: provenance"*, *"shadow test: explains a gap"*).

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 closure + Fig 8 surplus, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a field that expanded the set of kinds it could express by composition alone, with no external frame and no import.

### F5 - Agreement inside a field is a mirror chain, not evidence

Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.

Mimetic isomorphism is exactly this collapse. Organizations copy one another under uncertainty, homogeneity rises, and the agreement is the pool talking. It can be detected without a new theory: check the overlap between the frames before counting their agreement. This is a *contact* diagnosis, not a truth verdict; filters never hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 room chain + Fig 2 correlationvalence, very low overlap).
- **Refuter:** show that conformity among organizations selected from one evidence pool increased contact with an independent ground rather than merely raising agreement.

### F6 - The right anchor is the outcome, because the question is constitutively about people

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

For institutional theory, legitimacy and prestige are frames: they update on what organizations think. Outcomes (repeated coordination, measured performance, survival, a court's ruling) are the anchor. North's Nobel lecture corroborates from the field: *"It is the interaction between institutions and organizations that shapes the institutional evolution of an economy. If institutions are the rules of the game, organizations and their entrepreneurs are the players."* That is world corroboration only; it does not decide the verdict.

- **Status:** single-source within the figures (Fig 2 and its restatement in the Seat Map), with world corroboration.
- **Refuter:** name an anchor for institutional change that updates when a field changes its opinion of it, and show the theory still settles questions.

### F7 - Settling is necessary and not sufficient; a growing rulebook is not a growing reach

Fig 5 :69: *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."* Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."* Fig 6 :42-43: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*

A rule can be settled (the ground paid for it) and still be unusable because of how it composes. Counting rules, citations or compliance events is counting the denominator. World corroboration: North (1991) writes that institutions *"evolve incrementally, connecting the past with the present and the future"*, and Streeck and Thelen (2005) name *"five broad modes of gradual but nevertheless transformative change that we will call displacement, layering, drift, conversion, and exhaustion."* Incremental accumulation is not the same as expansion of reach.

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 5 sufficiency amendment + Fig 12 level rule + Fig 6 reach).
- **Refuter:** show a case where the number of settled rules rose and the reach rose with it, with no refused term and no change of anchor.

### F8 - Capability and persistence are properties of actor-and-habitat; design is introduction, not subtraction

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground."*

A reform that changes only the formal rule and leaves the informal norms, enforcement and field contacts untouched has not changed contact, so it has not changed what the arrangement can do. The seat can add an actor or a rule and cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. A managed institution is therefore permanent work, not a solved state. Mahoney and Thelen (2010) corroborate: *"Layering: the introduction of new rules on top of or alongside existing ones"* and *"Drift: the changed impact of existing rules due to shifts in the environment."*

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 habitat + Fig 6 record/reach, different figures).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a reform that removed a condition from the habitat and thereby changed contact, with no new actor or rule introduced.

### F9 - The most under-measured link is "what would count as progress"

Fig 3 :25-26: *"the link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

A general theory of institutional change must therefore supply, not assume, an objective in its own terms. "More compliance" or "more legitimacy" is a frame-internal score and is disqualified by Fig 1 :24-27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

- **Status:** single-source (Fig 3), with Fig 1 as a low-overlap second figure for the disqualification of self-scores. Marked triangulated for the combined claim.
- **Refuter:** show an instrument, already returning something, that measures institutional progress on the same axis the institution scores itself, and that the two readings agree for reasons other than construction.

### F10 - A final rule set is impossible; the theory returns a shape, not a name

Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room"*. Fig 7 :30: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

A general theory that claimed to be final would be the last room, which the figures rule out definitionally. It can state the necessary shape of any account (two directions, a refusal operator, a ground, an import path, no final settlement) and can say what is unreachable with the library as it stands. It cannot name the particular rules or outcomes that settle a concrete case. That is `gap`, not failure.

- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 no last room + Fig 9 shape-not-answer + Fig 13 unreachable verdict).
- **Refuter:** produce a closed institutional theory with no outside left to import from that still settles new cases against a ground.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A rule is shown to determine a choice with no frame term (b, a, o′) between them, measured per person-slot (breaks F2, Fig 1 :19).
2. An institution changes a rule entirely inside its own closure, with no refused term, no negation, and no contact with a frame whose closure differs (breaks F3 and F4, Fig 5 :53 and Fig 6 :23).
3. Conformity among frames drawn from one evidence pool is shown to increase ground contact by the same measurement that records the conformity (breaks F5, Fig 7 :35 and Fig 2 :40).
4. The number of settled rules is shown to be the number of reachable compositions, with no refused term (breaks F7, Fig 5 :69 and Fig 6 :42-43).
5. A theory of institutional change is produced that has no outside left to import from and still settles new cases (breaks F10, Fig 7 :6).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union split (F1). A single-mechanism theory is the wrong object.
2. Make the sought-for shape an ordinary term before the run: write down the five conditions in F11 and let them accrue a residual (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65, *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"*).
3. State the mode on every claim (F11). The empirical question is `gap`, not a silent omission.
4. Build the objective link (F9) before the planning link. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
5. Use public opt-in venues (see `03_public_contact_points.csv`) and ask for a refutation, not belief. *"A failure is more useful than an endorsement."*

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives: two directions that must be unbundled, a per-slot frame between rule and choice, a refusal operator for upward change, an import path through a different closure, a ground that does not update, a habitat that can be added to but not subtracted, and no final room. What does not survive is any claim to empirical content. Which rules, in which field, settle which outcomes is `gap` with the library as it stands. After re-census: **two corrections kept visible, one declared gap, no hidden strain.** All substantive claims are `specified` or `general`; none is `grounded`.
