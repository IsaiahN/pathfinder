## What the wider literature bears on this question

1. **North corroborates the two-way loop.** Institutions are the humanly devised constraints that define the choice set; organizations and entrepreneurs are the players whose daily choices alter the rules. That is the downward and upward direction in one paragraph, arrived at from economic history rather than from the figures.
2. **DiMaggio and Powell corroborate F5 (mirror chain).** *"rational actors make their organizations increasingly similar as they try to change them"* is the room-chain collapse in organizational clothing. Under Fig 2 :40 the resulting agreement is the evidence pool talking, not two frames confirming each other.
3. **Streeck and Thelen, Mahoney and Thelen, and van der Heijden corroborate F3 and F8.** Layering, drift, conversion and displacement are the observable forms of change by addition and refusal. None of these needs a new kind of institution; all are rearrangements of what the field already holds. That matches Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"*, until an import arrives.
4. **The animal-behaviour sources corroborate the shape of a convention from outside social science.** Conventions emerge without a designer, are arbitrary, stable and efficient, and persist across changes in membership. That is Fig 11's habitat and Fig 7's selection-between-rooms at a different stratum. It does not tell us which institutional rules are good.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"*. Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As currently drafted, the claim is `specified` and its empirical part is `gap`; it does not over-claim. If a reader fires any of the four refuters, stop and narrow the claim before proceeding.
