## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question, a **shape** is the set of conditions that any general theory of institutional change must satisfy before it can name particular rules. It constrains; it does not fill. "Two directions, a refusal operator, a ground, an import path, no final room" is a shape. "Rule X caused outcome Y in field Z" is a name, and the figures do not supply it.

The README's phrase matters: the shape-not-a-name limit governs a **missing** thing, a primitive not held in Γ. Naming an atom already held (for example calling a convention a "network") is a binding from another frame, not the invention of a seventh kind. The distinction matters here because "institution", "rule" and "norm" are social-science bindings onto held vocabulary, not new substrate members. Calling an institution a habitat is a binding, and must be provenanced as such.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it"*.

Applied here: the question was first restated as a per-slot residual, not as a thesis about institutions. Only then were figure lines chosen. That order is Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

So the six-term substrate is a visible guess, not a discovery of nature's own list. This matters for institutional theory: the mapping of "rule", "norm" and "field" onto the vocabulary is a choice made by a frame, and the choice is visible precisely so a later reader can revise it. Naming one social-science term as *the* institutional equivalent of a substrate member turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why source discrimination matters here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* - one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

For institutional theory the corresponding error is naming the source of a compliance reading. High compliance can come from legitimacy, coercion, mimicry, habit, or a threat, and a single reading cannot discriminate among them. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* adds that corrections carry meaning the words do not: *"the meaning is in the unstated gap between what you did and what they show you"*. A rulebook is mostly context with some subtext and almost no pretext. It travels well and teaches badly. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a louder rule.

## 5. Answer to the question's own "shape" demand

**What would a general theory of institutional change need to explain?** Not an inventory of institutions, but the constraints on any account of the loop. From the essays and figures:

1. both directions exist and are not one mechanism (union, not partition);
2. the downward direction runs through a frame and is measured per slot;
3. the upward direction is a refusal of a held rule whose residual persists, not a fresh description;
4. new rules come from contact with a different closure, not from more search inside one;
5. a settled rule is necessary but not sufficient for further composition;
6. the ground, not agreement, settles whether the rule paid;
7. the theory has no last room, so it cannot be final.

That is a shape. It is `general` as structure and `specified` as prediction. Which rules, in which field, settle which outcomes remains `gap`.

Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers famous names to agree with this shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, let an outcome settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And *"the outputs... could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula"* (*Arrival of the Fittest*). So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the theory and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
