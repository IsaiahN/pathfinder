## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a general theory of institutional change would need to explain about rules shaping choices and people reshaping rules. Option A: treat the two directions as one mechanism and offer a single lever. Option B: treat them as a union compound with a cyclic overlap, and state the necessary conditions each direction must satisfy while declaring the empirical part a gap. What each leads to: A produces a closed-looking theory that clears one condition and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :54), and it mistakes field agreement for ground contact; B produces a checkable shape, falsifiers written in advance, and an open slot where a ground must decide.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between any proposed general theory and the two effects it must close. Arity: two conditions plus a cycle, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54; Fig 9 :57-58), not one hard question. Symmetry: broken. Downward is ordered production or sequence; upward is a unary refusal plus a mint. Scale: first order inside a person or organization, second order when a settled rule becomes an atom for further rules, third order across fields with no last room.
- **Domain 1, the person as frame.** Actors: individuals, Γ, b, w, a, o'. Currency: prediction error R per slot and learning. Anchor: the outcomes the person actually encounters. Legitimate because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :37).
- **Domain 2, the institution as habitat and convention.** Actors: formal and informal rules, organizations, enforcement, the field. Currency: compliance, legitimacy, transaction costs, reproduction of the practice. Anchor: what the field's ground keeps, repeated coordination and measured performance. The habitat is enumerated, never composed.
- **Domain 3, the research and verification frame.** Actors: researchers, reviewers, journals, conferences. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero and agreement is one witness talking.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Domains 1 and 2 overlap in the cycle, which is a compound with its own loop. Domain 3 checks both. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive per slot before claiming field change (Fig 3 :19); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle each first-order rule before composing a second-order theory (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test applied: the "institutions are structures" mapping was re-censused and retracted as a strained binding of social-science vocabulary onto physical substrate terms. Bearing moved to Fig 11 (habitat), Fig 5 (refusal) and Fig 6 (closure). No hidden strain remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape stands; the empirical content is a gap. A general theory of institutional change must explain a union compound with a cyclic overlap: rules shape choices through a frame per slot, people reshape rules by refusing a held rule whose residual persists flat, new rules come from a closure that differs, settled rules are necessary but not sufficient for further composition, agreement inside one evidence pool is a mirror chain, capability lives in actor-and-habitat with introduction but no subtraction, and there is no final room. Which rules, in which field, settle which outcomes is `gap` after re-census. All substantive claims are `specified` or `general`; none is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** the shape could be too restrictive. If a field is observed changing its own closure by composition alone, or if a rule is shown to write a choice with no frame term between them, the account excludes a real mechanism. The bounded cost is that a researcher looks for a refusal and an import and fails to find them; the unbounded cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a closed-looking theory before the shape has been checked. Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. A falsified condition is removable without touching the others; the gap is explicitly recorded so nothing has to be silently withdrawn. A sent packet can be superseded by a narrowed one. No primitive has to be invented or retracted to do it.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot instrument that resolves a rule's effect; an observed loss of composing standing with the rule still in the record; a measured evidence overlap showing that agreeing frames were independent; a reform that changed habitat contact rather than rule text; or a closed theory that still settles new cases. Any of these fires a pre-registered refuter. Prestige, popularity and a large agreement pool are not such instruments.
