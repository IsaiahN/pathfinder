# The Tether's answer: what a general theory of institutional change must explain

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structure, `gap` on empirical content. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The residual is the gap between any proposed general theory of institutional change and the two effects it must close: rules shaping choices, and people reshaping rules. Arity two plus a cycle; symmetry broken; scale from one person to a field of fields. Fig 9 :54 calls a two-condition split a union compound: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*. A theory that offers one mechanism for both directions is answering the wrong object.

## 2. The shape

1. **The two directions are a union compound with a cyclic overlap.** One lever cannot close both. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
2. **Rules shape choices through a frame, per slot.** A rule is part of Γ, changing Γ(b,a); the world returns o′; R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per person-slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A direct rule-to-choice bond is not licensed; b, a and o′ sit in between.
3. **People reshape rules by refusal.** A held rule whose residual *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* (Formula step 2) is the candidate to refuse. Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. The old rule stays in the record; what falls is its standing to be composed over (Fig 5 :54-55). Negation is unary and is not a bond (Operators table).
4. **New rules come from a closure that differs.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* More search inside one field visits more of the same space. Imports carry a source gate and a shadow test.
5. **Settling is necessary but not sufficient.** Fig 5 :69: *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."* A settled rule becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104), but a growing rulebook is not a growing reach.
6. **Capability lives in actor-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Reform by introduction is permanent work; a seat cannot subtract the habitat.
7. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The anchor is the outcome, and it must not update (Fig 2 :29).
8. **No final theory.** Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*. The account returns a shape, not a name, and stops where the instruments stop. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a theory of everything, not a final room, not a closed rule set.
- Not a claim about which rules matter in any field. That is `gap`, not an omission: no figure line governs empirical institutional content.
- Not "rules are commands". A rule is a settled term in a loop, not a command that writes a choice.
- Not that legitimacy, prestige or conformity settles anything. All three are frames that update on opinion.
- Not that more rules mean more capability. Record growth and reach growth are different quantities.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement, dataset, field experiment or historical test was run.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Per-slot discrimination:** does a rule's effect appear as a residual on identifiable person-situations, or only as a field average? An instrument that resolves the slot would fire on refuter 1.
- **Refusal trace:** can we observe a rule losing its standing to be composed over while remaining in the record, as layering, drift, conversion and displacement predict? A change that leaves no refused term fires refuter 2.
- **Independence test:** do frames in a field draw on distinct evidence? Measure the overlap before counting their agreement (Fig 8 :47 - *"Independence is one quantity read twice"*). If overlap is high, the agreement is one witness.
- **Contact test:** did a reform change contact in the habitat, or only the formal rule text? Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **No-last-room test:** produce a closed theory that still settles new cases against a ground. If one exists, F10 is refuted.

The standing falsifiers are the four in section 4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, empirical content unreached.** The mapping of "institution" and "rule" onto the framework's vocabulary is a binding from the social-science frame, not a derivation of a new kind. The six-term substrate is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books*); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The account stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, records the lineage, and calls the empirical part `gap`. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer a final theory of institutions, and it does not claim to name which rules matter. It says that a general theory must explain two directions that are not one mechanism: rules shape choices through a frame, per person and per situation, and people reshape rules by refusing a held rule whose error persists flat against the ground. New rules arrive through contact with a frame whose closure differs, not through more search inside the same field; agreement inside one evidence pool is a mirror chain, not evidence. A rule that has been settled by an outcome becomes a building block for the next turn, but settling is not enough to make it usable, and a growing rulebook is not a growing reach. Capability lives in the person-and-habitat relation, so a seat can introduce rules and cannot subtract the habitat. There is no last room. What the framework supplies is the shape any candidate answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which rules settle which outcomes stays open until a ground decides.
