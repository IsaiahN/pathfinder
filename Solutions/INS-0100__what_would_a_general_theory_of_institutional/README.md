## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not *whether* institutions matter, but what a theory must be able to explain for the two-way loop between rules and choices to close. In effect terms, a person's library Γ (beliefs, habits, inherited priors) issues a prediction Γ(b,a) for an action a; the world returns o′; the residual is R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|, measured per person-slot and never averaged across a field (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). A rule is part of Γ. It bounds what can be predicted. When a person's residual persists and does not trend down, the rule is a candidate for refusal, and a new rule is owed (Formula step 2, Fig 5 :53).

- Option A, "rules are commands that directly determine choices": leads to the prediction that change is a top-down replacement and that compliance is the measure of success.
- Option B, "rules are settled terms in an agent-and-habitat loop, changed by refusal and import, and settled only by the ground": leads to the prediction that change is a cycle with two distinct directions, that agreement inside a field adds no ground contact, and that a final rule set is impossible.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conditions both directions must satisfy, plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one direction from a failure of the other. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode for the shape, and `gap` for the empirical content (which rules, which outcomes). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional theory question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of institutional change.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
