**Subject:** Check one output about institutional change before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation, and I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a general theory of institutional change has to explain two directions that are not the same mechanism. (1) Rules shape choices through a frame, per person and per situation, never by a direct rule-to-choice bond. The framework records a residual R = |prediction - observation| for each tracked slot, and warns that averaging across slots hides the only signal that says where to look. (2) People reshape rules by refusing a held rule whose residual persists and does not trend down. Nothing is deleted from the record; what falls is that rule's standing to be built on. A rule that has been settled by an outcome becomes a building block for the next rule, but being settled is not enough to make it usable. New rules arrive through contact with a frame whose closure differs, not by more search inside the same field. Conformity inside one evidence pool is a mirror chain, not evidence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a final theory of institutions, that it names which rules matter in any particular field, that legitimacy or prestige is an anchor, or that a growing rulebook is a growing capability. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which rules, in which field, settle which outcomes is a gap that I am recording as a gap.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a rule shown to determine a choice with no belief, action or observation between them, measured per person-slot;
> 2. an institution changing a rule entirely inside its own closure, with no refused term and no contact with a different frame;
> 3. conformity among frames sharing one evidence pool shown to increase contact with an independent ground;
> 4. a closed theory of institutional change with no outside left to import from that still settles new cases.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
