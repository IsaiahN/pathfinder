**Subject:** One question about the word "before", and what would refute my answer

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **Question in effect terms.** What does "before inflation" mean in a theory where space and time themselves may have changed? The phrase orders two events, and an ordering runs on time. If the theory puts time itself in doubt, the ordering may have nothing to range over.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The phrase is not one question. It is at least three:
>
> 1. **Operational precedence.** A prior field configuration had to obtain for inflation to start. This is checkable, and it is the ordinary initial-conditions question. It survives.
> 2. **Structural priority.** A settled lower-level composition becomes an atom over which inflation composes. This needs no new primitive. It survives.
> 3. **Temporal precedence.** An epoch earlier than inflation in an ordered time. This is the sense the theory puts in doubt. I mark it as a boundary shape, written `BEFORE(???)`, and I do not fill the slot.
>
> I am explicitly **not** claiming that nothing existed before inflation. Failure to find an anchor is not proof of absence. The verdict is that the ordering has no settled referent with the instruments we have, which is a result about our reach, not about the world.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. a real instrument that returns a discriminant ordering pre-inflationary configurations with respect to inflation;
> 2. a reduction of all three senses to one dependency order with a single anchor;
> 3. a derivation of a seventh primitive, or a measurement that names the referent of `BEFORE(???)`;
> 4. a case of inflation composing with no prior settled atom.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the answer. If any holds, I retract. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
