## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A cross-scale transform is lossy by construction. Going up from a micro description throws away detail, the trip back down does not reproduce what was sent, and the gap `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))` is the honest measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold. "Essential information" must be split: the reapplicable kernel or method crosses, though the level remakes it; the particular per-slot detail and the recording do not cross, and the discarded detail reappears above the crossing as noise. Therefore the answer to the literal question is no on the detail reading and yes on the kernel reading, with the two verdicts kept separate. Irreducibility is level-relative, and a settled macro pattern becomes an atom for the next composition. The deliverable is the shape, the round-trip measurement that would close it, and a refuter written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 4

- **Statement:** a scale transform is not reversible, and the loss is a measurable quantity rather than an error.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Also Fig 4 :28-29 "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought down will not match what was sent." Also Fig 4 :31-32 "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."
- **What to check:** does the framework actually define the quantity, and is the claim stated as a rule about transforms rather than as an empirical discovery? Symbols table :77-78: "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a scale transform with `R_T = 0` over a declared domain, checked against a ground. That kills the decisive verdict.

### C2 — framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable against coarse-graining practice

- **Statement:** what crosses is the reapplicable method, not the recording; the lost detail does not vanish but reappears as noise.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Also Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Also Fig 13 :31-32 "And because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Also Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** is the kernel/recording split faithful to how coarse-graining is actually used? Gell-Mann and Lloyd 1996 split a description into effective complexity (regularities, the survivor) and an entropy term (random aspects, the loss), and describe coarse graining as averaging that reflects ignorance. If that mapping fails, the world corroboration is withdrawn, though the figure verdict stands because world sources are secondary.
- **Refuter:** show a kernel that does not survive a crossing, a recording that crosses and is reapplicable as a method, or discarded detail that vanishes without reappearing as noise while the higher level remains a distinct frame.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** irreducibility is level-relative; a settled macro pattern becomes an atom for the next composition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :103 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." Also Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom". Also Fig 4 :34 "The level a method lands on remakes it."
- **What to check:** does this dissolve the apparent contradiction between "macro patterns are irreducible at their level" and "a settled macro becomes an atom"? Under the framework both hold at different steps of the loop.
- **Refuter:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or show a settled term that cannot serve as an atom for any composition. Either breaks the level-relativity claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that macro patterns are unreal, illusory or mere shorthand.
- Not that everything is reducible from the current micro vocabulary. Composition never leaves its closure (Fig 6 :23).
- Not that information is destroyed by coarse-graining. It becomes unavailable at the macro level and reappears as noise.
- Not that strong emergence is established. Anderson 1972 is a warning about construction, not a proof of ontic novelty.
- Not universal irreducibility. Irreducibility is a fact about a level (Fig 12 :104).
- Not a catalogue of derivable patterns, and no naming of a specific atom as the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.
