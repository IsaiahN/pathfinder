## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Anderson 1972 is not a proof of ontic novelty; it is a warning about construction.** His line is that reduction does not imply construction: the ability to reduce everything to simple fundamental laws does not imply the ability to start from those laws and reconstruct the universe. That is precisely the figure claim at Fig 4 :27 (the transform between scales loses information) plus Fig 8 :32 (closure is idempotent). Cite it for the derivability limit, not as evidence that macro properties are metaphysically new.
2. **The coarse-graining literature measures the loss rather than lamenting it.** Gell-Mann and Lloyd split a description into effective complexity (the compact description of regularities, what survives) and an entropy term (the random aspects, what the coarse description cannot hold). That is a world-field analogue of the framework's kernel/recording split and of `R_T`. It corroborates the shape; by Fig 2 :40 it does not decide it. Note the independence problem: Anderson, Gell-Mann, Lloyd and Landauer all sit in the physics-of-information pool, so their agreement is partly one evidence pool. The SEP entry (philosophy) and Carroll and Parola (theoretical/complex systems) are the low-overlap witnesses.
