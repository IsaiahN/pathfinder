## DERIVATION

### Q1 — A settled macro pattern at the next level: not a promotion, a role change

- Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"
- Fig 13 :4: "the two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward" (the extract line begins "There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward").
- Fig 4 :34: "The level a method lands on remakes it."

A macro pattern that has been settled against the ground can be composed over. It then functions as an atom for the next composition. It does not thereby become micro in an absolute sense: "irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". This resolves an apparent contradiction between "macro patterns are irreducible at their level" and "a settled macro becomes an atom". Both are true at different steps of the loop. **Mode `general`, triangulated** across Fig 12 and Fig 13.

**Falsifier:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or show a settled term that cannot serve as an atom for any composition. Either breaks the level-relativity claim.

### Q2 — The order: describe, cross, measure, settle, then compose again

Dependency order from Fig 3 and Fig 9:

1. **Describe R at the membrane.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Here R is the asserted lossless crossing, described as a gap to be measured.
2. **Cross.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the method, not the recording of one derivation.
3. **Measure `R_T`.** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
4. **Settle.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."
5. **Compose over the settled result.** Fig 12 :104 (above). Only then is the next level available.

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Most practice in this area begins at step 3 (compute a macro observable from a micro simulation) without step 1. Fig 3 says that is effort above the break when the slot is undescribed. **Mode `general`, single-source** for the ordering (Fig 3), corroborated by Fig 9.

**Falsifier:** show a case where refining the micro solver repairs an undescribed macro slot. That would mean the chain is not ordered by dependency in this domain and the census must be redone.

### Q3 — Are micro and macro descriptions isomers? No. They are a many-to-one crossing.

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Carroll and Parola 2024 (world source, secondary): a many-to-one map from the micro theory to the macro theory, throwing away information, commuting with time evolution.

Isomerism requires the same atoms under different bonds. A micro description and its macro description do not share an atom set: the macro frame holds coarse variables that are compositions of micro states, not rearrangements of them. Under Fig 12 they are therefore **not isomers**. They stand in the relation Fig 4 names: a lossy transform between scales, with a method crossing up and a head start coming down. Anderson 1972 (world source, secondary) makes the same point in field terms: the ability to reduce everything to simple fundamental laws does not imply the ability to start from those laws and reconstruct the universe.

The useful contrast is with two *different* micro rules that produce the same macro pattern. Those can be functionally identical terms composed by different routes. Fig 13 :121-123: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms. That is the framework's genuine isomer-like case at the macro level, and it is the sense in which universality classes (world field) are relevant: many micro details, one macro substance. **Mode `specified` for the non-isomer verdict; `general` for the rule.**

**Falsifier:** exhibit a micro and macro description of one system with the same atom set under different bonds. That would make them isomers under Fig 12 and would collapse the membrane into a within-level composition, forcing re-census of D3.

### Q4 — The analogue that actually transfers: coarse-graining, not mere analogy

The reflex is to map "scale crossing" onto "coarse-graining in statistical mechanics". Fig 8 :50-52 warns that convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, so provenance must be recorded. The transfer is legitimate only as a **shadow test**, not as a derivation:

- Gell-Mann and Lloyd 1996 (read 2026-11-22): coarse graining "assigns to microstates probabilities obtained from the fundamental ones by a kind of averaging that reflects our ignorance"; effective complexity is the compact description of identified regularities and total information adds an entropy term for the random aspects. This is the world-field form of the kernel/recording split.
- Fig 13 :31-32: "the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise". The entropy term is where the discarded detail shows up.
- Anderson 1972 (read 2026-11-22): "at each level of complexity entirely new properties appear".

The shadow test holds: the world frame predicts a survivor (regularities, the kernel) and a loss term (entropy, noise), which is the shape the figures return. It does not decide the figure verdict. By Fig 2 :40, agreement between frames sharing a pool is not a second witness, and the statistical-mechanics sources overlap with each other in the physics-of-information pool. The independent corroboration is the SEP entry (philosophy) and Carroll and Parola (theoretical/complex systems), both read for this folder.

**Falsifier for the analogue:** a coarse-graining in which no entropy term appears and no detail reappears as noise. That would break the shadow test and remove the world-field corroboration, but because world sources are secondary it would not by itself flip the figure verdict.

## CONCLUSION

A macro pattern is not absolutely irreducible: irreducibility is level-relative, and a settled pattern becomes an atom for the next composition (Q1). The check has a fixed dependency order: describe the residual, cross with a method, measure `R_T`, settle against a ground, then compose (Q2). Micro and macro descriptions are not isomers; they are related by a many-to-one lossy transform, while different micro routes to the same macro substance are the genuine isomer-like case (Q3). The statistical-mechanics analogue passes a shadow test but carries no verdict weight (Q4).

What this rescues is the middle ground the question needs: macro patterns are not free reductions of micro rules, and they are not irreducibly mysterious either. They are a level with its own anchor, whose relation to the level below is measured by a gap rather than asserted by a yes or no.
