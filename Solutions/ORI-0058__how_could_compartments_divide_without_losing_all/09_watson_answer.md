## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what shape a division process must have if a compartment is to split while each daughter retains usable contents and at least one functional copy of the genetic information. Option A: a container story, where what matters is keeping the enclosure intact. Option B: a coupled union story, where the crossing carries a seed with nonzero fidelity, closes as a cycle, and discards enough detail to supply variation. What each leads to: A predicts a membrane that cannot open and therefore cannot divide; B predicts a checkable shape with four refuters written in advance, and leaves the historical chemistry open.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the chemistry is not derived. A compartment can divide without losing all its contents or information only if the crossing carries a seed (the recorded arrangement plus enough material) with nonzero fidelity, the inherited part survives its own use, the event closes as a cycle whose daughters become the operands of the next division, and the mandatory loss supplies the variation selection needs. The candidate physical modes are isomers over a shared atom set, differing decisively in retention. Which chemistry actually worked on early Earth is a `gap`: the figures return a shape, not a name.
