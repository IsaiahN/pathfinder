# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter for a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives the reader's check. The shape and its refuter must stand on their own.

---

**Subject:** A checkable claim about how a protocell could divide without losing its genome

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A compartment grows and must split. Two requirements pull against each other: the opening that lets it divide is the opening that can spill its contents, and a single genetic object cannot be halved into two working copies. The question is what shape of handoff can satisfy both. This is a union of at least three conditions (boundary dynamics, content retention, information replication), not one hard problem.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A division that loses nothing essential is a lossy cross-scale handoff. The parent does not pass a miniature of itself to each daughter. It passes a seed: the recorded arrangement that can regenerate the whole, plus enough material and the right conditions to use it. Three consequences follow. First, the inherited part must survive its own use, meaning it must be copied or copied by a replicase inside the compartment, not consumed. Second, the process must close as a cycle, because daughters have to become the operands of the next division. Third, the loss at the crossing is mandatory, and the detail discarded there is the variation selection needs. The compartment is a region with a boundary and a record, so the composition runs across kinds, not within one.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before writing the rest:
>
> 1. A dividing system in which each daughter regenerates the parent's full organisation with no inherited record and no external seed.
> 2. Sustained information continuity where the inherited molecule is consumed on first use and no replicase is inside the compartment.
> 3. A perfectly lossless division that still yields heritable variation, or a zero-stochasticity division that supports Darwinian evolution.
> 4. A self-reproducing compartment organised entirely within one kind, with no boundary-and-record between parent region and daughter region.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** which chemistry did it on early Earth. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not asserting that RNA was the first genome, that fatty acid vesicles were the first compartments, or that a particular laboratory system is the historical one. Those are ground questions for experiment and geology. Agreement from people who share the same evidence is one pool talking, not corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Coupled growth and division of model protocell membranes (Zhu and Szostak, JACS 2009): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2669828
> - Protocells and RNA self-replication (Joyce and Szostak, Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology 2018): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6120706
> - An optimal degree of physical and chemical heterogeneity for the origin of life? (Szostak, Phil Trans R Soc B 2011): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3158921
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
