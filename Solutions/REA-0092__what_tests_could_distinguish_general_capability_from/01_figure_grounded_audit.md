## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for a test that separates two hypotheses which look identical on ordinary evidence:

- **H_general:** the system can acquire competence in a habitat it was not prepared for, and can carry what worked to the next problem.
- **H_narrow:** the system is competent inside a closed set of familiar tasks, and its successes are compositions inside that set.

On a benchmark drawn from familiar tasks, both return high scores. The observation does not discriminate. That is the residual: *we need a differential instrument whose outcome would differ between two systems that currently produce the same reading.* It is a test-design problem, not a score-collection problem.

The framework dissolves one half of the question before answering it. Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

So "general capability possessed by an agent in isolation" has no nameable anchor and is not yet a domain. The answerable residual is *general capability as agent-and-habitat contact*, and a test must vary the habitat, not merely the item.

What an answer would look like, written down before the derivation and therefore refusable: a set of observations each of which (a) holds the system fixed and substitutes the habitat, (b) reports residual per slot rather than an average, (c) carries a dated exposure record so that a passed item cannot be both an import and a derivation, (d) is anchored to something that does not move when the system or the designer pushes on it, and (e) comes with a written failure condition. If no such observation can be built, the honest answer is `gap`, not a number.

**R, described in the sense of Fig 9 :21-22 (arity, symmetry, scale):**

- **Arity:** at least two conditions, familiar and substituted habitat, plus a comparison between them. The result is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-57). H_general and H_narrow can both be true of the same system in different habitats.
- **Symmetry:** the comparison is ordered. Novel-to-familiar transfer and familiar-to-novel transfer are different readings. This is a ⋛ threshold relation and a → sequence, not a symmetric conjunction. A single symmetric "ability score" is the flat-formula error.
- **Scale:** one task loop; one family of tasks; the membrane between a training closure and a substituted habitat. The transform across the membrane is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30).

**Partition or union.** D1/D2 is a partition boundary question: which items lie inside the system's closure? **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** perception: can the instrument present a novel task at all? vocabulary: does the system have terms for what that situation affords? objective: can it say what progress would mean there? planning: a route under incomplete information. learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it. The middle link is the one that usually breaks and has no instrument (Fig 3 :25-27). A test that measures planning while vocabulary is absent is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

## 3. Findings

### F1 The question's own subject is dissolved and replaced (decisive for framing)

Fig 11 :39 and :40, quoted above, replace "is the agent intelligent" with "what makes the agent-and-habitat capable." Fig 11 :73 adds:

> **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

A general-capability test therefore cannot be a property list about the agent. It must be an intervention on contact. Fig 11 :66 and :68-70:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**
> **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

**Reading (`general` for the shape, `specified` for AI systems):** a benchmark is a substituted habitat. Passing it proves the wiring of that habitat and says nothing about capability elsewhere. A specialist and a general system can both wire the same harness. **Refuter:** Fig 11 :72 itself supplies one: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."* If a change confined to the agent reliably changes the ground reading across substituted habitats, the agent-and-habitat claim is false and the test battery in `07` must be withdrawn.

### F2 An aggregate score is disqualified by per-slot indexing

Fig 1 :19, :20, :22, :28:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state"**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step."**

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Coverage and compression achieved are named there as non-counting quantities. A broad benchmark's coverage is exactly such a quantity.

**Reading (`general`):** two systems with the same mean can have different live slots; only the per-slot residual and its trend can separate them. **Refuter:** a demonstration that the per-slot residual curve is a deterministic function of the aggregate score for every system under test, so that no information is lost by averaging. That would collapse this finding.

### F3 More familiar items are idempotent, not evidence of generality

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

Fig 13 :115 and :118:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**
> **"still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Reading (`general`):** adding items generated by the same rule, from the same corpus, or by the same generator model spends budget without moving the wall. It can improve a leaderboard and cannot discriminate H_general from H_narrow. **Refuter:** a case where repeated search inside a declared closed set demonstrably adds a new atom, that is, produces a capability whose primitive was not available at the start and whose provenance is recorded. That would falsify closure idempotence as applied here.

### F4 Provenance is the discriminator, not a footnote

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**Reading (`specified`):** a held-out item that appeared in the training corpus is an import wearing the costume of a derivation. A dated exposure audit, a post-cutoff item or a rule-generated item with a recorded generation date is therefore part of the test, not an optional refinement. **Refuter:** a result showing that for the system under test, exposure status does not change the residual on any item, combined with a complete and independently audited training ledger. Then the provenance requirement adds no discrimination for that system.

### F5 The checker must not be able to produce what it is checking

Fig 10 :42-43:

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**
> **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."**

Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

**Reading (`general`):** human preference is not the anchor for a capability claim. It is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. The anchor is what the system does under a substituted habitat with a recorded exposure boundary. **Refuter:** show that the substituted habitat itself depends on the system's output for its scoring, so the anchor updates with the frame and panel 3 of Fig 2 degrades into panel 1.

### F6 Depth and absence must be separated before declaring a failure

Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 8 :40-41:

> **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."**

Fig 6 :27 and Fig 5's support guard (the gap must have real mass) together mean a single failed novel-task session does not license "not general." Fig 8 :39 forbids reading it as proof of absence. Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

**Reading (`specified`):** distribute independent searchers at the same reach before declaring absence; if the residual trends down, it was depth and the remedy is more search, not a new primitive. **Refuter:** a case where independent search at the same reach reduces the residual to zero on items that were previously failed, with no import. That would show the absence verdict was a budget error, not a closure verdict.

### F7 The middle link needs its own instrument

Fig 3 :25-27:

> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

**Reading (`specified`):** a transfer test that only scores final answers reads a step that may never have received its input. It must also record whether the system can state what the new situation affords and what would count as progress there. **Refuter:** a system that performs at ceiling on substituted habitats while being unable to state what counted as progress in any of them, across multiple probes. That would show the vocabulary link is not load-bearing for this question and the instrument requirement is redundant.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The differential-instrument shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An agent-only change, with no change in contact, moves the ground reading across substituted habitats (breaks Fig 11 :40 and the built-in refuter at :72).
2. Per-slot residuals are a deterministic function of the aggregate score for all systems tested, so averaging loses nothing (breaks Fig 1 :19-22).
3. Repeated search inside a declared closed set adds a new atom with recorded provenance (breaks Fig 8 :32).
4. A complete, independently audited training ledger shows that exposure status never changes the residual for any system tested (breaks F4).
5. A verifier that can reconstruct the held-out items returns the same verdict as one that cannot, across independent anchors (breaks Fig 10 :42).
6. A system reaches ceiling on substituted habitats while showing no vocabulary/objective link on multiple probes (breaks F7).

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the battery in `07` is revised. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual, not with a theory of intelligence: two systems, one reading, no discriminator. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before testing (Formula "Direction").
2. Build the battery around contact substitution (F1), per-slot curves (F2), provenance (F4), checker non-access (F5) and depth/absence separation (F6). Keep the six verdicts separate; never average them into a "generality index."
3. Treat every benchmark score as a filter for the budget and never as a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
4. Use the public opt-in venues in `03`; ask for a refutation, not agreement.
5. Send the generator, that is the residual, the refuter and the figure lines, not a recording of this derivation (Fig 4 :22-23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*).
