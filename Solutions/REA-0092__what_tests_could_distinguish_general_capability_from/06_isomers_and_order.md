# Isomers of one score, and the order a test battery must follow

**Mode: `specified` on the AI application, `general` on the bond and dependency structure.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8).

## RESIDUAL

Two systems return the same aggregate benchmark score. Is that one fact about two instances of a kind, or two different substances that the scoring notation cannot tell apart? A second question follows: in what order must the proposed observations be run, so that a later reading is not taken below a break in the dependency chain?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 Are two equal-score systems isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**

A score records ingredients and a magnitude. It does not record the bond between the system and its habitat. Under Fig 12, two systems with the same atoms and the same magnitude but different bonds are **not variants of one thing**; they are different substances whose notation cannot tell them apart. The general system is bonded to the habitat by contact that can be substituted and carried; the narrow system is bonded to a training closure by repeated exposure. Same reading, different bond, different behaviour under substitution.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a system's behaviour under substituted habitats is a deterministic function of its familiar-task score plus its atom set, for every system tested, the bond carries no additional information and the isomer claim fails. The two systems are then one substance with two spellings.

### Q2 Are they a functional group instead?

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Both systems share the loop `state -> structure -> state` that Fig 13 requires for a composition. That shared substructure fixes the kind: both are agents that map situations to results.
- The qualifier, that is which member of the group each is, is exactly what the score omits. So the weaker claim is also true: as described by a score, the two systems share a functional group and differ only in a qualifier that has not been measured.

Q1 and Q2 are compatible. The isomer claim is the stronger one and is the one worth testing: it predicts that an instrument sensitive to the bond will separate them, while an instrument sensitive only to the functional group will not.

### Q3 What order do the observations run in?

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :22-23: **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."**
- Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments"*, and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The dependency order for the battery is therefore:

1. **Perception:** can the instrument present a task that is genuinely outside the familiar closure, and can it tell that task apart from a rephrased familiar one?
2. **Vocabulary and objective:** can the system say what the new situation affords and what progress would mean there? This is the middle link and the one that needs a new instrument.
3. **Exposure record:** was the item present in training, generated after the training cutoff, or rule-generated with a dated provenance? Without this, step 1 cannot be trusted.
4. **Contact substitution and per-slot residual:** run the task family, record the residual per slot and its trend under independent searchers.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, and test whether it carries to the next substituted habitat.

Running step 4 before step 2 is the named failure: a planning score taken while the vocabulary link is broken is a reading of nothing. Running step 3 after step 4 wastes the result, because a passed item cannot be reclassified as unseen after the fact without an audited record.

### Q4 What bond is the discriminator itself?

- Operators table: → is **sequence**, ordered; ⋛ is **comparison**, ordered and threshold-like; + is symmetric.
- The battery is therefore not a conjunction of five independent tests. It is a → chain (each observation consumes the previous one) ending in a ⋛ comparison (familiar residual against substituted-habitat residual, against a threshold fixed before the run).
- Summing the five into one symmetric "generality score" would be the flat-formula error of Fig 12 :5. It would record the ingredients and lose the arrangement that carries the discrimination.

### Q5 Analogues that corroborate the structure, each from a different evidence pool

- **Psychometrics, Cattell and Horn.** Fluid intelligence is *"the ability to solve novel reasoning problems"*; crystallized intelligence is *"the application of learned procedures and knowledge"* (Wikipedia, *Fluid and crystallized intelligence*, accessed 2026-11-24). This corroborates the need to vary novelty rather than difficulty. It conflicts with Fig 11 :39 if read as a property of the agent alone; after re-census to agent-and-habitat it corroborates the test dimension and carries no verdict weight.
- **Psychometric validity.** *"Does the measure behave like the theory says a measure of that construct should behave?"* (Wikipedia, *Construct validity*, accessed 2026-11-24), with convergent and discriminant validity. This corroborates the per-slot and exposure requirements: a test that converges with training exposure fails discriminant validity against general capability.
- **Animal cognition.** Transfer experiments end with never-seen items, where *"successful sorting of these items shows that the animal has not simply learned many specific stimulus-response associations"*, and the novel object recognition test separates familiar from novel (Wikipedia, *Animal cognition*, accessed 2026-11-24). This is a genuinely different evidence pool with the same discriminator shape.
- **Benchmark engineering.** Dynamic benchmarks exist because *"potential data contamination is unavoidable"* when the benchmark is public (Chen et al., 2025). The survey proposes collision and interpretability criteria for generated items. This corroborates the dated-exposure requirement at the engineering level.
- **Mathematics.** The no-free-lunch result that *"any two algorithms are equivalent when their performance is averaged across all possible problems"* explains why a fixed suite cannot rank general capability over all tasks: the ranking depends on the distribution of problems, which is the habitat.
- **Economics.** The Lucas critique says it is *"naive to try to predict the effects of a change in economic policy entirely on the basis of relationships observed in historical data"*. A benchmark that becomes a target changes the behaviour it was measuring, which is the same structural warning as Goodhart's law.

No analogue overrules a figure. All six are corroboration from low-overlap fields.

## CONCLUSION

Two equal-score systems are best read as Fig 12 isomers: same atoms and magnitude, different bond to the habitat, different behaviour under substitution. The weaker functional-group reading also holds and is what a score alone can see. The battery must run in dependency order, with the vocabulary/objective link instrumented before transfer is measured, and it must end in an ordered comparison rather than a symmetric sum. The isomer claim is refuted if behaviour under substitution is a deterministic function of atom set and familiar score.
