# Expert verification packet: differential tests for general capability

**What this is:** a test-design claim in a form an evaluator, psychometrician or capability researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the test designs, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No system was tested and no result is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A benchmark score cannot distinguish a system that can acquire competence in an unprepared habitat from a system competent only in a familiar closure, because both can return the same reading. What separates them is a differential instrument with five properties: habitat substitution with the system held fixed; per-slot residual curves instead of an average; a dated exposure record so an import cannot pass as a derivation; independent anchors that do not share an evidence pool; and a depth-versus-absence probe before declaring failure. A sixth, earlier observation instruments whether the system can state what a new situation affords and what would count as progress there, because a transfer score taken below that broken link is a reading of nothing. The output is a shape, that is, constraints on a separating test, with six refuters written in advance. It is not a verdict about any named system.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Capability is a property of agent and habitat; a harness solve proves wiring

- **Statement:** a test that only adds items from the task family the system was prepared for cannot discriminate general from narrow competence. The habitat must be substituted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- **What to check:** is the figure line reproduced accurately, and does the inference from "capability is agent-and-habitat" to "vary the habitat, not the item" follow without adding a new premise? Does the claimed substitution actually change contact rather than merely rephrase items?
- **Refuter:** an agent-only change, with no change in contact, moves the ground reading across substituted habitats. The figures supply this themselves: Fig 11 :72: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

### C2 Score and provenance are the two halves of the discriminator

- **Statement:** per-slot reporting is required because a mean hides a live residual; dated exposure is required because a passed item may be an import rather than a derivation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** can a reader construct two score distributions with the same mean and different per-slot residuals? Can they construct a held-out item that was in fact present in training, such that the passing result is an import? If both are possible, the test-design requirement follows.
- **Refuter:** (a) per-slot residuals are a deterministic function of the aggregate score for all systems tested; or (b) a complete, independently audited training ledger shows exposure status never changes the residual for any system tested.

### C3 The checker must be outside what it checks, and depth must be separated from absence

- **Statement:** an evaluator or test frame that can reconstruct the held-out items cannot verify a capability claim from them. A single failed novel family is not proof of absence; independent search at the same reach is needed first.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 8 :40-41: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."*
- **What to check:** does the proposed two-frame design actually have low overlap, or do both frames draw on the same generator and corpus? Is the depth-versus-absence probe run before the absence verdict?
- **Refuter:** a verifier that can reconstruct the held-out items returns the same verdict as one that cannot, across independent anchors; or independent search at the same reach reduces a previously failed family to zero with no import.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict about any named model, lab or benchmark. No system was tested.
- Not that a broad benchmark is worthless. It is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the framework returns a generality score, a percentile or a named missing primitive (Fig 13 :134-135).
- Not that human agreement is the ground. Human judgment is the right anchor for "is this a good answer for a person" and only an instrument for "is this code correct" (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not that adding items to a closed task family adds evidence. Closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32).
- Not that the six refuters have been fired. They are pre-registered, not executed.
