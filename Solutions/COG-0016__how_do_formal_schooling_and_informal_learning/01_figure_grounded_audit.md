## 0. The residual in effect terms

The effect to be explained is a **variation in expectations**. A person meets a claim and a "why" question. Before hearing any answer, they already expect certain things to count: a reason of a certain form, a source of a certain standing, a kind of warrant, a threshold of sufficiency. Call that bundle the person's *evidentiary/explanatory expectation*, E.

The question asks how two learning histories bear on E:

- **Formal schooling:** deliberate, extracted from daily life, carried out by routines, with explicit criteria and a designated correct answer.
- **Informal learning:** participation in daily practice, demonstration, correction through contact, criteria largely unstated.

Stated as a bet, observation pair: Γ(b, a) predicts which answer will satisfy a person; the observation is which answer actually satisfies them. R is the gap. The residual is indexed per slot: what counts as evidence, what counts as an explanation, who may certify, how much agreement is enough, whether a rule transfers. Aggregating those slots into one "worldview score" would destroy the signal (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

**What an answer would look like, bound before the run (Formula "Direction").** Not a name such as "schooling produces analytic reasoning." A shape with four parts: (1) E is a bound, learnable term in the learner's frame; (2) each regime imports a vocabulary and a weighting onto the ground, not a new capacity; (3) each regime has a characteristic failure mode that is the mirror of the other's; (4) the two regimes form a union compound needing separate loops and low-overlap instruments. Plus the instrument that would name which part fires for a given person. That shape is what the findings below constrain. It is written down here so it can accrue a residual and be refused like any other term.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least two conditions, and they co-occur in every real person. A person is never only schooled or only informally socialised. This is a **union compound**, not a partition: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 from the union/partition discussion). Treating it as one variable with two levels is the blend failure.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric at the institutional level (one regime has pinned explicit criteria; the other has diffuse tacit criteria), but symmetric in its effect on the learner's frame, because both are **imports**: what enters Γ from outside. The symmetry claim is therefore about the *form* of the bearing, not about equal magnitude.
- **Scale:** first order inside the individual frame (belief, vocabulary, weighting); second order at the institution and the research community, where methods cross a membrane; developmental time is the loop, not a separate scale.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The learner's epistemic frame | the person, Γ (belief b, vocabulary for "because", "proof", "evidence"), weighting w | prediction versus observation, R per slot | the ground: what actually settles whether the answer works in the world | yes |
| D2 Formal schooling | institution, teacher, curriculum, answer key, credential | explicit criteria, grades, canonical form | the institutional convention for questions about the curriculum; **an instrument, not the ground**, for questions about the world | yes |
| D3 Informal learning habitat | family, peers, practitioners, physical setting | practical feedback, correction through contact, demonstrated skill | the ground through contact: whether the action works | yes |
| D4 Verification and research frame | researchers, tests, tasks, review | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | yes |

All four have nameable anchors, so all four are domains. A candidate domain without a nameable anchor would not be admitted yet.

### Partition or union

D1 is the inner frame in which both D2 and D3 are imports. D2 and D3 are **not** a partition of experience; they overlap and conflict inside one person (Scribner and Cole 1973, read 2026-11-22: *"the two are constantly intermingled"*). D4 observes D1 through D2 and D3, so it cannot simply read E off a test score; the test is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70). Result: a union compound with its own loop, not one hard question.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Perception of what the claim offers → vocabulary for what counts as evidence and explanation → the objective (what would count as progress here) → planning a response → learning and carrying. The question targets the **middle link**, which Fig 3 names as the one with no instrument: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* (Fig 3 :25-26) and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27). Effort spent on the links above the break (more content, more tests of recall) does not arrive at this one.

## 3. Findings (one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate)

### F1 — E is a bound, learnable term, not a fixed trait (D1)

The Symbols table defines the belief: *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w."* (Symbols table :20-21) And the weighting: *"w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* (Symbols table :24-26)

An expectation about what counts as evidence is exactly such a belief: formed from private history (what has counted before) and inherited prior (what the institution or community handed over), mixed by a learnable w. Two people with identical cognitive architecture diverge because their histories and weightings diverge. This is why the question is well posed at all: E is a variable with a learnable parameter, not a fixed property of the species.

Fig 5 :65: *"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* That is what §0 did with the shape, and it is also what a person does with E when a grounded correction refuses it.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Verdict:** `triangulated` across the Symbols table (b and w), Fig 5 (Direction), and Fig 11 :40 (*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*). Low overlap: a definition of belief, a rule about preferences, and a habitat law.

**Refuter:** a grounded correction that changes a person's behaviour while leaving E unchanged and unrevised, or a demonstration that w is not learnable from outcomes for this class of expectation. Either breaks F1.

---

### F2 — Both regimes import vocabulary, not new capacity (D2, D3)

Fig 6 :22: *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."* The new terms a person can use for "because", "proof", "witness", "source", "replicate" enter Γ from outside. The learner does not grow a new basic cognitive capacity; they gain access to a vocabulary and a weighting. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

World corroboration, low overlap with the framework: Scribner and Cole 1973, *"All culture groups thus far studied have demonstrated the capacity to remember, generalize, form concepts, operate with abstractions, and reason logically."* They distinguish **capacities** from **skills** and *"functional learning systems"*: the difference is in how capacities are brought to bear, not in whether they exist. Kuhn 2001 independently shows that the *criteria for justifying a claim* develop and vary among adults, which is a difference in E, not in basic reasoning capacity.

This is the strongest reason not to answer the question as "schooling makes people logical and informal learning does not." That framing imports a capacity difference the figures and the world sources both refuse.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 6 + Scribner and Cole 1973 + Kuhn 2001; three fields: framework metatheory, cross-cultural cognitive development, developmental epistemic cognition).

**Refuter:** a controlled task in which the basic capacity to reason differs by learning history after content, literacy and task familiarity are matched. That would move the difference from skill/vocabulary to capacity and break F2.

---

### F3 — The question targets the middle link, which has no instrument (D1, D4)

Fig 3 :25-26 and :27, quoted in the census. Expectations about evidence and explanation are the "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress" link. It sits between perception and planning. Perception and planning have obvious failures and tests; this link has neither.

Consequence for the question as posed: most educational measurement lands above the break (what was learned, what was recalled, what score was obtained). Those measurements do not arrive at the link that shapes E. A test that scores correct answers measures the output of the chain, not the person's standard for what would count as an answer.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Verdict:** `single-source` for the figure claim (Fig 3), with world corroboration from Scribner and Cole's observation that group differences were *"more consistent and more striking"* in the *verbal explanations* given than in the groupings themselves, which is a difference at the justificatory link rather than at the task output.

**Refuter:** an existing instrument that measures a person's standard of evidence directly, with published reliability and a ground check, would falsify the "no instrument" part of F3 (not the rest of the finding).

---

### F4 — Formal schooling's characteristic risk: the instrument becomes the anchor (D2)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Note the test: if it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor. A school's answer key is pinned against the student's view, so it passes that half of the test for questions constitutively about the curriculum. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*

But the answer key is not the ground for questions about the world. Fig 2 :37-38: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* When a learner treats the institutional certificate as what settles truth rather than what settles the curriculum, the boundary has been crossed. Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 2 :43). A grading convention that decides what counts as a correct explanation has stopped being a convention and has become an author of the outcome.

Scribner and Cole describe the matching content-side failure independently: school learners who *"know the words" but not the referents, who are limited by their empty verbal constructs.* The explicit verbal rule arrives before the empirical referent, and the learner can master the rule without the referent. That is the precise shape of an expectation that treats certification as evidence.

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* If the institution supplies both question and criterion, the learner's "I checked, it is correct" is a reconstruction, not a verification.

**Mode:** `specified` (a risk and a prediction, not a claim that all schooling does this). **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 ground rule, Fig 10 convention rule, Scribner and Cole 1973 on empty verbal constructs).

**Refuter:** show a school setting in which the criterion is explicitly declared revisable against the world, students are required to push on it, and the criterion demonstrably moves when pushed, while still functioning as a shared standard. That would show the instrument has not become the anchor and would narrow F4 to settings where criteria are sealed.

---

### F5 — Informal learning's characteristic risk: the habitat is not enumerated (D3)

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Informal learning happens in a habitat of actors, conditions and relations that no one listed. Fig 11 :52-54 warns that contact cascades and that scoping to direct contact misses the effect several steps out. So the informal learner's E is shaped by what the habitat actually contains, including things nobody intended to teach.

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The practical competence an apprentice shows is not a free-standing trait; it is the person plus the setting. Change the setting and the competence may not travel, which is what Lave and Wenger 1991 describe with *"abstract representations are meaningless unless they can be made specific to the situation at hand."*

Scribner and Cole name the matching limitation on the other side: informal learners may be limited by *"inarticulated practical constructs."* The knowledge regulates behaviour without being formulated, so it does not travel to a new case by description.

Isolation has a specific cost. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* A researcher who measures informal learning by taking a person out of the setting and giving a decontextualised task has substituted a habitat and will read wiring, not capability.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 habitat and isolation rules, Lave and Wenger 1991, Scribner and Cole 1973).

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the same practical expectation transfers unchanged across genuinely different habitats, with the second habitat enumerated and shown to lack the triggering actors. That would break the habitat-dependence part of F5.

### F6 — The two failures are symmetric, not a ranking (D2, D3)

Put F4 and F5 side by side:

| regime | bond to the ground | strength | characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| formal schooling | explicit criteria, certification | the rule can be stated and examined | words without referents; a convention deciding the outcome |
| informal learning | practical consequence in the habitat | the referent is present and corrected | inarticulated constructs; unenumerated cascade |

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Both regimes can hand over a recording instead of a generator. The school hands over the canonical answer; the habitat hands over the remembered successful occasion. Neither is a method until it can be reapplied. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

The canonical essay on tacit knowledge (read 2026-11-22) states the asymmetry directly: writing carries context and some subtext but almost never *"the pretext, the 'you had to be there' part."* That is why a compressed expert message unpacks only for a listener with the right history, and why the correction loop must run both ways.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Verdict:** `single-source` for the exact pairing of failure names (Scribner and Cole 1973), `triangulated` for the underlying symmetry (Fig 4 recording rule plus the tacit-knowledge essay plus Fig 11 contact law).

**Refuter:** a measured asymmetry in which one regime's failure is strictly larger on the same discriminating instrument across several habitats and tasks, with the habitats enumerated. That would justify a ranking and break the symmetry claim.

---

### F7 — It is a union compound, and averaging hides the live slots (D1, D4)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :19 and :22 (quoted in §0) forbid the aggregate. A single "effect of schooling" score blends at least five slots: source standing, warrant type, explanation form, sufficiency threshold, transfer scope. Scribner and Cole already warn that *"the complexity of education-task interaction prohibits any sweeping generalizations about the 'effects of schooling.'"*

Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* So a null result from one instrument is not proof that no expectation difference exists.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 union rule, Fig 1 slot rule, Scribner and Cole's interaction warning).

**Refuter:** a single scalar that predicts all five slots across persons and tasks, validated against a ground, would dissolve the compound into a partition and break F7.

---

### F8 — Shape, not a name: which expectation a person holds needs an instrument (D4)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures constrain the *form* of E and its failure modes; they do not say which person holds which E. Naming requires a ground reading. Fig 6 :32-34: an instrument is *"improved from a worse instrument already returning something"*; a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere is not available. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

This is the honest stop. The research question is answerable in principle, but the answer is not derivable from the figures. What the figures supply is the discriminating description: an instrument must separate *"the value of information is closely related to who imparted it"* (the authority-bonded expectation Scribner and Cole report for informal education) from *"a general rule"* bonding (the criterion-bonded expectation they report for schooled populations), and it must do so in the same person across both settings.

**Mode:** `general`. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 shape limit, Fig 6 instrument rule, Hard Problem of Source essay on metamerism).

**Refuter:** a figure line that named a specific expectation as the output of a given regime would break F8. None was found after the re-census recorded in §5.

---

### F9 — Researcher agreement from one instrument is one witness (D4)

Fig 2 :31-32: independence is *"how little they draw on the same evidence"* and *"is what makes agreement mean something."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* But two research groups using the same test, the same coding scheme and the same sample draw on one evidence pool. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

Fig 7 :35: *"Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* The remedy is not more agreement; it is a second room with a different closure, chosen against the residual the first surfaced. For this question that means a second instrument whose currency is not test performance: for example, observed correction behaviour in a genuine habitat, or a source-discrimination task, not another questionnaire.

**Mode:** `general`. **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 independence, Fig 8 surplus, Fig 7 selection).

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the two proposed instruments are independent in the Fig 2 sense (their errors uncorrelated, their evidence pools disjoint) would strengthen rather than break this; a demonstration that they are the same instrument would confirm the warning, not refute it. F9 is a design rule and is not falsified by an empirical result. It is marked as such.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the framework's own discipline: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The following were committed before the verdict in `07_the_tether_answer.md` was drafted. The shape claim is refuted if any of these holds:

1. **No learnable parameter.** A grounded correction changes behaviour while E stays unchanged and unrevised across repeated slots, or w does not learn from outcomes for this class of expectation. (Breaks F1.)
2. **Capacity difference.** A matched task shows a basic reasoning *capacity* difference by learning history after content, literacy and familiarity are controlled. (Breaks F2.)
3. **Instrument becomes ground, twice over.** In a setting where the criterion demonstrably moves when students push on it, students still treat it as settling truth, *and* the same pattern appears in an informal setting with no institutional criterion. (Breaks the specificity of F4.)
4. **Habitat independence.** The same practical expectation transfers unchanged into a second, enumerated habitat that lacks the triggering actors. (Breaks F5.)
5. **Single scalar suffices.** One scalar predicts source standing, warrant type, explanation form, sufficiency threshold and transfer scope across persons and tasks, validated against a ground. (Breaks F7.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask the empirical question as five slots, never as one score (F7).
2. Treat the answer key as an instrument for curriculum questions and refuse it as an anchor for world questions (F4). State which question is being asked before scoring anything.
3. Enumerate the habitat before comparing regimes; otherwise the comparison substitutes a test harness and measures wiring (F5).
4. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong: use at least two instruments with disjoint evidence pools, chosen against the residual (F9). Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
5. Send the generator, not the conclusion: the shape, the refuter and the instrument description lead; the framework comes later and only on request (Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
6. Keep the four domain verdicts separate. Never average "schooling helps" with "informal learning helps" into a single claim.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives all ten checks: E is a bound, learnable term; the two regimes import vocabulary and a weighting rather than a capacity; their failure modes are symmetric; they form a union compound; and which expectation fires in a given person needs a ground-reading instrument, not more argument. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**, one live empirical refuter set, and an honest `gap` on the direction and magnitude of the difference. The figures do not predict which regime produces more of which expectation in which population. That is a ground question. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
