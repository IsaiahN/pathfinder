# The Tether's answer on formal schooling, informal learning, and expectations about evidence and explanation

**Mode: `specified` on the domain findings, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No person, classroom or apprentice was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

People differ in what they expect to count as evidence and as explanation before they hear an answer. The question names two learning histories as the possible shapers. The effect is indexed per slot, not as one world view: Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The slots are source standing, warrant type, explanation form, sufficiency threshold and transfer scope.

The two histories co-occur in every real person, so this is a union compound: Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* It needs its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape (one verdict per domain, never averaged)

**Domain 1, the learner's frame (`general`, triangulated).** An evidentiary expectation is a bound belief formed from private history and inherited prior, mixed by a learnable weighting. Symbols table :24-26: *"w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* So E is a variable with a learnable parameter, not a fixed imprint. Fig 5 :65: *"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

**Domain 2, formal schooling (`specified`, triangulated).** It imports an explicit vocabulary and a weighting onto certification and decontextualised criteria. Fig 6 :22: *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."* No new cognitive capacity is involved. The institutional criterion is a legitimate anchor for questions constitutively about the curriculum: Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* It is not the ground for questions about the world: Fig 2 :37-38 *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* When the criterion decides what counts as a correct explanation, Fig 2 :43 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* fires. The matching content-side failure is a rule stated without its referent.

**Domain 3, informal learning (`specified`, triangulated).** It imports vocabulary through participation and contact, and bonds evidence to practical consequence. Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Because nobody enumerated it, the causal story can be several steps out, and the knowledge may remain an inarticulated practical construct. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Re-census correction retained: informal learning is **not** pure contact; a family or master maintains the habitat. Isolation substitutes one habitat for another: Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* So a decontextualised test measures wiring, not capability.

**Domain 4, the compound and the check (`general`, triangulated).** Neither regime is ranked above the other. Both can hand over a recording instead of a method, and both have a characteristic failure that is the mirror of the other's. The researcher's agreement is not a witness: Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* What is owed is at least two low-overlap instruments, chosen against the residual each other surfaces.

**Structures (`specified`).** Over a shared atom set the two regimes stand as isomers: same atoms, different ordered bond, different behaviour (`06_isomers_and_order.md`). The weaker, safer claim is that they are two members of one functional group (import terms, test, carry). Informal learning has a cycle shape; the credentialing layer on top of schooling has a chain shape. The latter reading is restricted to the assessment layer, not to every classroom.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that schooling produces a reasoning capacity that informal learning lacks. Import adds an atom, not a capacity.
- Not that informal learning is pure contact, unmaintained or universally better at grounding.
- Not that either regime is better. Both have a named, symmetric failure mode.
- Not that "the effect of schooling" is one thing. It is a union of at least five slots.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any scale settles `left(R, φ)` here.
- Not a name for what a particular person expects. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not a claim that the figure set was derived from education data. It was applied to the residual; the world sources are secondary corroboration from other people's ground readings.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay on metamerism is the model: a spectrometer, not a debate, separated sources that produced the same green reading. Here the instrument must:

1. Separate, in the same person and across both settings, an authority-bonded expectation (the value of information is tied to who imparted it), a criterion-bonded expectation (the explicit rule is the warrant), and a consequence-bonded expectation (what worked in the habitat is the warrant).
2. Show that the expectation updates under grounded correction, which is the learnability test in F1.
3. Enumerate the habitat before comparing, so the second setting is not a substituted test harness.
4. Be independent of the first instrument in the Fig 2 sense: disjoint evidence pools, uncorrelated errors.

The standing falsifiers are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and were written before this conclusion. If any fires, the shape is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set constrains the form of any answer and the shape of the instrument. It does not say which regime produces more of which expectation in which population. That is a `gap` after a genuine re-census, and it is recorded as a gap rather than filled with a plausible story. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank formal schooling against informal learning and does not claim a new reasoning capacity for either. It says that expectations about evidence and explanation are learnable beliefs, formed from history and inherited prior and mixed by a weighting that changes with outcomes; that both regimes add vocabulary and a weighting rather than a new capacity; and that they differ in the bond they put between a claim and what settles it. Schooling bonds evidence to explicit criteria and certification, so its characteristic failure is a rule that can be stated without its referent and a grading convention that ends up deciding the answer. Informal learning bonds evidence to practical consequence in a habitat, so its characteristic failure is skill that cannot be articulated or carried into a new setting. The two failures are mirror images, and the regimes co-occur, so the question is a compound with several slots rather than one effect. What the framework returns is that shape plus a description of the instrument that would name which slot fires in a given person. It does not name the person's expectation; that needs a ground reading, and none is claimed here. The refuters were written in advance, and an instrument, not more agreement, is what would close it.
