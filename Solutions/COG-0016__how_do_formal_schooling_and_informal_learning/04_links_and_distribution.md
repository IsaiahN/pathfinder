## Three findings in the wider literature that bear on the shape claim

1. **The difference is at the justificatory link, not the capacities.** Scribner and Cole found that Kpelle high schoolers and villagers produced similar groupings but *"more consistent and more striking group differences in the verbal explanations given for the groupings than in the nature of the groups per se."* That is exactly Fig 3's middle link: the terms and the "what would count as progress" standard, not perception or planning.
2. **Both regimes have a characteristic failure, and they are mirror images.** School learners can *"know the words" but not the referents*; informal learners may be limited by *"inarticulated practical constructs."* This is why the answer is a symmetric table and not a ranking.
3. **The expectation is developmental and variable, not a fixed type.** Kuhn 2001 reports that the preference for explanation over evidence *"diminishes developmentally"* and *"disappears (in favor of a preference for evidence) among highly able university undergraduates."* Note the selection caveat: "highly able" is an ability-selected group, so this is not a clean schooling effect. It corroborates learnability (F1), not the causal direction.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described an instrument sends the interior, not the claim. Lead with the five slots and the refuter.
- **Fig 2 :45** — *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape claim were true, before showing them the derivation.
- **Fig 8 :45** — *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* The surplus exists only while the frames differ. Two readers of the same packet share one evidence pool and are one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- **Fig 7 :35** — *"Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Choose the next venue against the residual the first one surfaced, not by adding more of the same audience.
- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Stage 3 sends a method (the formula with its refuter discipline), not an answer.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape and the five refuters; (2) ask what instrument would discriminate the three bonds; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, on request, the three framework links, with the note that the figure text, not the summary, is the authority.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third)

- **Stage 1:** the residual in effect terms, the shape, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record the verdict *before* any theory is shown.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only:** the three local framework links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
