## Independent domain census (written from the residual line alone)

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the variation in what counts as evidence and explanation. Arity: five distinguishable slots and two co-occurring learning conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54), not one effect with two levels. Symmetry: the institutions differ (pinned criteria versus diffuse criteria), while the bearing on the learner's frame is symmetric because both are imports. Scale: first order inside the frame, second order at the institution and research community, developmental time as the loop.
- **Domain 1, learner's frame.** Actors: person, library, belief b, weighting w. Currency: prediction versus observation per slot. Anchor: the ground. Question type: learnability of a bound term.
- **Domain 2, formal schooling.** Actors: institution, teacher, key, credential. Currency: explicit criteria. Anchor: the institutional convention for curriculum questions; an instrument for world questions. Question type: where a convention stops being one.
- **Domain 3, informal learning habitat.** Actors: family, peers, practitioners, setting. Currency: practical feedback. Anchor: the ground via contact. Question type: enumeration and substitution of habitat.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: researchers and instruments. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this run its independence is zero: one evidence pool.

Order by dependency: refuse the capacity claim before minting one (Formula step 2); describe the effect before selecting a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); enumerate the habitat before comparing regimes (Fig 11 :43); settle the first-order expectation before composing the comparison (Fig 12 :104).
