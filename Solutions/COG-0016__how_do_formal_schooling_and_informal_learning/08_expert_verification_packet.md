# Expert verification packet — the Tether on formal schooling and informal learning

**What this is:** a claim in a form an education or cognitive-development researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a person is claimed. This is a shape and a route.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Expectations about what counts as evidence and explanation are learnable beliefs, formed from private history and inherited prior and mixed by a weighting that changes with outcomes. Formal schooling and informal learning do not add different cognitive capacities; they import different vocabularies and different bonds between a claim and what settles it. Schooling bonds evidence to explicit criteria and certification, with the characteristic failure of a rule stated without its referent and a convention that decides the outcome. Informal learning bonds evidence to practical consequence in a habitat, with the characteristic failure of skill that cannot be articulated or moved to a new setting. The failures are mirror images and the regimes co-occur, so this is a union compound with several slots, not one effect. The framework returns the shape and the instrument description; it does not name what a particular person expects. Five refuters were written before the derivation.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading the Symbols table and Figure 5

- **Statement:** an evidentiary expectation is a bound, learnable term, not a fixed trait or a new capacity. The two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and weightings diverge.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table :20-21 — *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w."* Symbols table :24-26 — *"w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Fig 5 :65 — *"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*
- **Refuter:** a grounded correction changes behaviour while the expectation stays unchanged and unrevised across repeated slots, or the weighting demonstrably does not learn from outcomes for this class of expectation.

### C2 — education-facing; checkable against Scribner and Cole 1973 and Kuhn 2001

- **Statement:** the institutional criterion is an anchor only for questions constitutively about the curriculum; used as the ground for questions about the world it becomes a convention deciding the outcome. The informal habitat supplies practical consequence but cannot be enumerated in advance, so its competence may not travel. The two failures are symmetric, and no basic capacity differs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 — *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37-38 — *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 2 :43 — *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 11 :39 — *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :43 — *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 6 :22 — *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*
- **What to check:** Scribner and Cole 1973 report group differences *"more consistent and more striking"* in verbal explanations than in the groupings; they describe school learners with *"empty verbal constructs"* and informal learners with *"inarticulated practical constructs"*; and they state that *"the complexity of education-task interaction prohibits any sweeping generalizations about the 'effects of schooling.'"* Kuhn 2001 reports that the preference for explanation over evidence *"diminishes developmentally"*, with the ability-selected undergraduate caveat. Are those readings faithful?
- **Refuter:** a matched task showing a basic reasoning *capacity* difference by learning history after content, literacy and familiarity are controlled; or a demonstrated case where the criterion moves when students push on it and yet still settles truth for them, with the same pattern in an informal setting with no institutional criterion; or a practical expectation that transfers unchanged into an enumerated second habitat lacking the triggering actors.

### C3 — structural and methodological; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1, 2 and 8

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound of at least five slots, so it must be unbundled, and a particular expectation cannot be named without a ground-reading instrument. Agreement between researchers using one instrument is one witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 2 :40 — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 8 :45 — *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
- **Refuter:** a single scalar, validated against a ground, that predicts source standing, warrant type, explanation form, sufficiency threshold and transfer scope across persons and tasks, would dissolve the compound into a partition and break C3. An existing instrument that names a person's expectation with ground validation would break the "needs an instrument" half.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that schooling creates a capacity informal learning lacks. Import adds an atom, not a capacity.
- Not that informal learning is pure contact. A family or master maintains the habitat.
- Not that one regime is better. Both have a named, symmetric failure.
- Not that every classroom is a chain. The chain reading is restricted to the credentialing layer.
- Not `grounded`. No person, classroom or dataset was measured for this packet.
- Not a name for what a particular person expects. That requires an instrument, and none is reported as having fired.
- Not that the figures were derived from education data. They were applied to the residual; the education sources are secondary corroboration with their own provenance.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not an argument. It must separate, in the same person and across both settings, an authority-bonded expectation, a criterion-bonded expectation and a consequence-bonded expectation; show that the expectation updates under grounded correction; enumerate the habitat; and be independent of any first instrument in the Fig 2 sense (disjoint evidence pools, uncorrelated errors). The source essay on metamerism is the model: a spectrometer, not a debate, separated sources that produced the same reading.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Symbols table :20-26 and Fig 5 :65. Is C1 a fair reading of a learnable weighting?
2. Read Fig 2 :36-43, Fig 11 :39-43 and Fig 6 :22. Does the instrument/anchor distinction survive for the curriculum-versus-world question?
3. Check C2 against Scribner and Cole 1973 and Kuhn 2001: are the quoted findings faithful, and is the ability-selection caveat preserved?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the three bonds, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
