**Subject:** One checkable claim about how people learn what counts as evidence

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** people's expectations about what counts as evidence and what form an explanation should take behave like learnable beliefs, not fixed traits. Two learning regimes bear on them by importing vocabulary and a weighting, not by adding cognitive capacity. Formal schooling tends to bond evidence to explicit, decontextualised criteria and to certification; its characteristic failure is a rule that can be stated without its referent, and a grading convention that ends up deciding what counts as a correct explanation. Informal learning tends to bond evidence to practical consequence in a habitat; its characteristic failure is knowledge that regulates behaviour but cannot be articulated or transferred by description. The two failures are symmetric, and the two regimes co-occur in real people, so this is a compound question with its own loop, not one variable with two levels.
>
> What the framework returns is a **shape and an instrument description**, not a name for what a particular person believes. Fig 9 :32 puts it as *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all schooling produces authority-worship; that informal learning is pure contact (it has a maintainer: a family or a master); that one regime is better than the other; that any child or classroom has been measured for this; or that the framework can tell you which expectation a given person holds. Those bindings need a ground reading, and no ground reading is reported here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a grounded correction changes behaviour while the evidence standard stays unchanged and unrevised, or the learnable weighting does not learn;
> 2. a matched task shows a basic reasoning capacity difference by learning history after content, literacy and familiarity are controlled;
> 3. the practical expectation transfers unchanged into a second, enumerated habitat that lacks the triggering actors;
> 4. one scalar predicts source standing, warrant type, explanation form, sufficiency threshold and transfer scope across persons and tasks, validated against a ground;
> 5. in a setting where the criterion demonstrably moves when students push on it, students still treat it as settling truth, and the same pattern appears in an informal setting.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as a good answer to stage 1:** a named instrument that would distinguish an authority-bonded expectation from a criterion-bonded one and from a practical-consequence-bonded one in the same person across both settings, or a reason why no such instrument can be built. It is the instrument I am asking about, not my conclusion.
>

Fig 5 :65: *"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
