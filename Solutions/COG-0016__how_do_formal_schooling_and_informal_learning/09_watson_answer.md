## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the figures constrain about the bearing of two learning regimes on a person's expectations about evidence and explanation. Option A: treat it as one effect of one variable, name a direction (schooling makes people analytic, informal learning makes people practical), and answer with a capacity claim. Option B: treat it as a union compound of several slots, keep the two regimes' verdicts separate, constrain the shape and the instrument, and record the empirical direction as a gap. What each leads to: A delivers a `general`-mode verdict with no ground reading, confounds content with capacity, and spends the independence of anyone asked to endorse it; B delivers a checkable shape, five refuters written in advance, and an open slot until a discriminating instrument returns something.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The variation in what people expect to count as evidence and as explanation before hearing an answer. Arity: at least five slots (source standing, warrant type, explanation form, sufficiency threshold, transfer scope) and two co-occurring learning conditions, so a union compound, not a partition. Symmetry: asymmetric institutionally (one regime has pinned explicit criteria), symmetric in effect on the learner's frame (both are imports). Scale: first order in the person's frame, second order at the institution and research community, with developmental time as the loop.
- **Domain 1, the learner's epistemic frame.** Actors: the person, the library, belief b, vocabulary for "because" and "evidence", weighting w. Currency: prediction versus observation, R per slot. Anchor: the ground, what actually settles whether the explanation works. Question type: learnability of a bound term.
- **Domain 2, formal schooling.** Actors: institution, teacher, curriculum, answer key, credential. Currency: explicit criteria and grades. Anchor: the institutional convention for curriculum questions; an instrument, not the ground, for world questions. Question type: boundary between convention and ground.
- **Domain 3, the informal learning habitat.** Actors: family, peers, practitioners, setting. Currency: practical feedback and correction through contact. Anchor: the ground via contact. Question type: habitat enumeration and substitution.
- **Domain 4, verification and research.** Actors: researchers, tests, tasks. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive what the claim offers, acquire terms for what counts as evidence, say what would count as progress, plan a response, learn and carry (Fig 3). The question targets the middle link, which has the fewest instruments. Describe the effect before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse a capacity claim before minting one (Formula step 2).

Strain test applied: the Fig 2 anchor rule and the Fig 11 ground law are not in conflict. Fig 2 governs what may count as an anchor for a given question; Fig 11 governs what settles. The answer key is pinned as a convention, not as the ground for world questions. Re-census also retracted the earlier phrase "informal learning is pure contact": Fig 11 :4-9 separates the unmaintained and maintained modes, and a family or master maintains the habitat.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a capacity ranking and a named direction do not. E is a learnable weighted belief. Both regimes import vocabulary and a weighting rather than a capacity. Schooling bonds evidence to explicit criteria and certification, so its characteristic failure is a rule without its referent and a convention deciding the outcome. Informal learning bonds evidence to practical consequence in a habitat, so its characteristic failure is an inarticulated construct that does not travel. The failures are mirror images, the regimes co-occur, and the question is a union compound with five slots. The framework returns that shape and the instrument description, not a person's expectation, and the empirical direction and magnitude are a gap. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states five refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a discriminating description. Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the slots and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The unsupported parts (capacity language, regime ranking, pure-contact reading, chain reading applied to every classroom) are removable without touching the derivation, and `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminating instrument that separates authority-, criterion- and consequence-bonded expectations in the same person across both settings, with disjoint evidence pools, would name the slot. A matched task showing a basic capacity difference would break the import finding. A single validated scalar predicting all five slots would dissolve the compound. A practical expectation transferring into an enumerated, different habitat would narrow the habitat finding. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
