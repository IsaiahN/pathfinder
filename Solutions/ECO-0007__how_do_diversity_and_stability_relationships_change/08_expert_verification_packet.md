# Expert verification packet: diversity and stability across scales

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecosystem-ecology researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken in this run. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The "diversity-stability relationship" is not one relationship. It is a union of separate questions, one per stability metric and per scale axis. Resistance, initial resilience, recovery, and invariability are isomers over the same community atoms: same ingredients, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117). Spatial and temporal scale are different terms, not one axis (Fig 13 :76). Moving between scales is a lossy transform, so the honest report is the round trip gap `R_T`, that is, what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :27-32). Aggregating across species or sites hides the per-slot signal that often drives the result (Fig 1 :22). A species' stabilizing role is relative to the residual, not a fixed property (Fig 11 :49). The framework does not give the sign of the relation at any particular scale. What it offers is the shape, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The question is a union, and stability metrics are isomers

- **Statement:** stability metrics are not interchangeable readings of one quantity; they share atoms but differ in bond, and their scale dependence differs. So the diversity-stability question must be unbundled by metric.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- **What to check:** do resistance, initial resilience, and invariability, as you define them, have the same scale dependence? Jarillo et al. (2022) report they do not. Is the "diversity-stability relationship" in your field a bundle of these?
- **Refuter:** show that two of these metrics are related by identity, or that their diversity relations have identical sign, magnitude and scale dependence, so the bond carries no information and no unbundling is owed.

### C2. The scale transform is lossy; report the gap

- **Statement:** a diversity-stability result at one grain cannot be relabelled onto another. Carrying up and bringing down do not match. The honest report is `R_T`, not a portable coefficient.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :28-29 "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought down will not match what was sent." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** take a result at one spatial extent and one temporal window. What is lost when it is moved to another? Does your field report that loss, or only the new coefficient? Hodapp et al. (2023) state that any stability measure scales with extent and that upscaling from small experiments is complicated by this.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a reversible scale transform for the relation, with `R_T = 0`. If shown, this sub-claim fails.

### C3. Space and time are distinct, and aggregation hides the live signal

- **Statement:** spatial and temporal scale are different terms, and averaging over species or sites discards the identity-level contribution that can drive stability. A species' valence is relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** in your system, do spatial asynchrony and temporal species asynchrony enter the same way? When you aggregate to a community mean, which species or site contribution disappears? Hodapp et al. report that dominant species can but need not have stabilizing effects, and that the sign depends on community structure.
- **Refuter:** show that one scalar scale parameter suffices for both axes, or that aggregation preserves the identity-level signal exactly, or that a species has a fixed stability valence across residuals. Any one breaks this sub-claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that diversity always stabilizes or always destabilizes. The sign is left to the ground.
- Not a universal scaling exponent or a portable coefficient.
- Not that spatial and temporal scale can be pooled.
- Not that these stability metrics are redundant. They are isomers, and the difference is carried by the bond.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this run, and the derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". Candidate discriminators named in the sources read:

1. **Invariability-area relationship slope:** near 1 for near-independent local dynamics, near 0 for synchronous dynamics. Hodapp et al. report slopes below 1 in all three of their datasets.
2. **Stability-synchrony decomposition:** separates species insurance from spatial insurance.
3. **Reversibility test:** carry a relation up in scale and back down, measure `R_T`.
4. **Metric-isomer test:** check whether any two metrics have identical scale dependence.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :117, Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 4 :27-30, Fig 13 :76, Fig 1 :22, Fig 11 :49. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own definition of stability: are the metrics isomers in your practice?
3. Name one instrument in your system that would measure the round trip gap between two scales, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
