# The Tether's answer on diversity, stability, and scale

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §4), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same diversity-stability question yields positive, negative, and null answers depending on which stability metric and which observation window is used. The residual is not "what is the coefficient" but "what changes when the scale changes, and what is lost in the change". It is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-58), not one hard question: a case at two scales is not a harder case of one scale.

## 2. The shape

1. **There is no single diversity-stability relationship.** Stability is a family of isomer metrics over the same community atoms (Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."). Resistance, initial resilience, recovery, and invariability have different scale behavior. Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
2. **Spatial and temporal scale are different terms.** Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Spatial asynchrony among sites and temporal asynchrony among species are distinct mechanisms, not two labels on one axis.
3. **Moving between scales is lossy, and the honest report is the round trip gap.** Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." A coefficient fitted at one grain does not transfer to another by relabeling.
4. **Aggregation hides the per-slot signal.** Fig 1 :19 and :22: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Dominant and rare species effects are exactly what a mean erases.
5. **A species' stability valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49-51: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same species can stabilize one metric and destabilize another.
6. **The framework does not give the sign.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Whether diversity raises or lowers a given stability metric at a given scale is a question for the ground, not for the figures.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that diversity always stabilizes, and not that it always destabilizes. Both are ground questions left open.
- Not that there is a universal scaling exponent or a portable coefficient.
- Not that space and time can be pooled into one "scale" variable.
- Not that this simulation measured anything. It is a simulated derivation, checked only for internal consistency.
- Not that the stability metrics are redundant. They are isomers; the difference is carried by the bond and is decisive.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The relevant discriminators named in the sources actually read:

- **Invariability-area relationship (IAR):** the slope from local to regional invariability. A slope near 1 indicates near-independent local dynamics; a slope near 0 indicates synchrony. Hodapp et al. report slopes below 1 in all three of their datasets. This is a candidate measure of what a scale change keeps and drops.
- **Stability-synchrony decomposition:** partitioning meta-community variability into species and spatial components. Hodapp et al. find spatial asynchrony contributes more than species-level compensation in their systems, but that species and site contributions are context dependent.
- **Reversibility test:** carry a diversity-stability result up in scale and back down; measure `R_T`. If `R_T` is zero for a given relation, F2 is refuted.
- **Metric-isomer test:** check whether two stability metrics have identical scale dependence in sign and magnitude. If so, F1 is refuted.

Standing falsifiers from the pre-registered list in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3: identical sign/magnitude/scale dependence for one metric across axes; a lossless scale transform; aggregation preserving the per-slot signal; a fixed stability valence for a species.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures say what the answer must look like and what would refute it. They do not say which sign the answer has, and this run took no measurement. The essays' own rule applies: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself". What is recorded here is the lineage, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it. "Indiscernibility is not identity": a shape is not the number it constrains.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a single diversity-stability coefficient, and it does not say whether diversity stabilizes ecosystems. It says that the question is a union of separate questions, one for each stability metric and each scale axis, because resistance, recovery and invariability are isomers over the same community and space and time are different terms. It says that moving between scales is a lossy transform, so the honest report is the gap between what a coarse description holds and what it drops, not a portable number. It says that averaging species and sites hides the very individual effects that often drive the result, and that a species' stabilizing role is relative to the question being asked rather than a fixed property. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The sign stays open until the ground settles it.
