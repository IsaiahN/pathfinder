## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :27-32 (lossy transform), Fig 9 :54-58 (union), Fig 12 :117 (isomer), Fig 13 :76 (two scale axes), Fig 1 :22 (per-slot signal).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: union of metric-by-scale compounds; scale change is lossy; report `R_T`; space and time distinct; aggregation hides signal; sign open.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete ecological claim of the kind found in the literature, for example "diversity raises temporal stability of community biomass at regional extent".

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Five things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the metric** (resistance, resilience, invariability are not interchangeable, so the concrete claim must name one);
- the **axis** (spatial and temporal results cannot be pooled under one scale label);
- the **gap itself** (a concrete coefficient omits what was discarded in aggregation);
- the **per-slot contributions** (a community mean hides the dominant or rare species that drove the result);
- the **open sign** (the shorthand refuses a sign; the concrete claim supplies one, so the sign is imported, not derived).

One thing survives cleanly: the requirement to split by metric and scale and to report what the transform drops. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded as an enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the condensed shape as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
