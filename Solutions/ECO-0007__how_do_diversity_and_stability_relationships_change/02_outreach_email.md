**Subject:** Check one shape claim about diversity, stability, and scale

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the "diversity and stability relationship" is not one relationship. It is a union of separate questions, one per stability metric and per scale axis. Resistance, initial resilience, recovery, and invariability are different quantities, and the published evidence shows they scale differently. Space and time are also different axes, not one "scale" knob. So the relationship you get depends on which metric and which window you choose, and moving between scales is a lossy transform: detail is thrown away, and the trip is not reversible. The honest measure of a move between scales is the round trip gap, that is, what the coarser description cannot hold. This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement, and it does not say whether diversity raises or lowers stability at any particular scale.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that diversity always stabilizes, that it always destabilizes, that there is a universal scaling exponent, or that spatial and temporal scale can be pooled. I am not claiming this simulation has measured anything. It is a derivation checked for internal consistency against a set of figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one stability metric whose diversity relation has the same sign, magnitude, and scale dependence across spatial grain, spatial extent, and temporal window;
> 2. a reversible scale transform for the relation, with a round trip gap of zero;
> 3. aggregation across species or sites shown to preserve the identity-level signal exactly;
> 4. a species or functional group with a fixed stability valence across all metrics and scales.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

> - Editorial: Stability across spatial and temporal scales (Zelnik et al., 2023): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2023.1201269/full
