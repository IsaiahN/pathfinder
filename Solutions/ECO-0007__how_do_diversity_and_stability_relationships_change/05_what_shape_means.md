## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can fill it. Here the shape is: the question is a union of metric-by-scale compounds; a move between scales is lossy; spatial and temporal scale are different terms; aggregation hides the per-slot signal. A filled answer would be "diversity raises stability by X per unit area at extent Y". That number is not a shape and this run did not measure it.

## 2. How the shape was obtained

- *Library of Babel* uses proximity: "use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of." Here the known vocabulary was Fig 4's membrane, Fig 13's two scale states, and Fig 1's per-slot residual; the new vocabulary was grain, extent, invariability, synchrony, and `R_T`.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" This is the union claim F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, said in the essays' own vocabulary.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place". The stop here is not an answered question but a narrowed one: the sign is still open, and the instrument that would close it is named below.

## 3. What licenses a name, and why none is given here

Only two things license a name: the ground settles it, or a provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Neither happened. Specifically:

- Naming "the diversity-stability relationship" as one thing is a name where the figures return a union. The essays' bundle warning applies directly.
- Naming a sign (positive or negative) would be a name where only a shape is held. There is no figure line that fixes a sign, and no measurement in this run.
- Naming spatial scale and temporal scale as one axis is a name imposed on two distinct terms (Fig 13 :76).

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument rule: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". A stability reading that is the same under two different mechanisms (asynchrony among sites, compensatory dynamics among species) does not identify its source. The essay's remedy is an instrument: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy". In this question, the invariability-area slope and the stability-synchrony decomposition in Hodapp et al. are candidate discriminators; this run did not use them.

## 4. The two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." A list of well cited reviewers who agree with a diversity-stability coefficient is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the shape a budget to be tried and lets an instrument settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 5. Answer to the standing question

**What does "shape" mean for "how do diversity and stability relationships change across scales"?** It means a four-part constraint that any answer must satisfy and a fifth part that stays open:

1. It must be indexed by stability metric, not averaged over metrics.
2. It must treat spatial and temporal scale as distinct terms.
3. It must report the loss incurred by moving between scales, not a portable coefficient.
4. It must keep species and site level signal visible under aggregation.
5. It must leave the sign at any particular scale to the ground.

A single global coefficient violates all five. That is why the answer returned here is a shape and a refuter, not a number.
