**Question:** How do people learn to distinguish reliable signals from noise in environments with different risks and information sources?

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question as an effect

Given a stream of observations, an answer would tell a learner what to expect when a cue, a source, or a regularity is treated as bearing on an outcome. If it is a reliable signal, acting on it should pay against an outcome that does not move when the learner's confidence moves. If it is noise, acting on it should not pay. The two readings are not exclusive: the same cue can be reliable for one residual and noise for another. That is why the question is treated as a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition into reliable and unreliable sources (Fig 9 :54).

An answer would look like a rule that assigns each cue-source-situation slot a verdict, states the risk-dependent threshold, names the instrument that would settle disputed slots, and says what may be passed on to another learner. What this solution actually delivers is narrower. The figures fix the **shape** such a rule must have and they cannot name the cues, the threshold value, or a curriculum. That part is a declared `gap`, not silence (Fig 13 :134).

## Boundary

This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a ground reading. It cannot settle the empirical question it reports on. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` header and `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7.

Nothing outside this folder was changed. Figure quotes come from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` only, cited as `Fig N :line`. World sources were fetched on 2026-11-22 and are recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. The field for public contact points is judgment, decision-making, and signal detection research.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name a reliable cue, a criterion value, or a training schedule.

The reasons are figure-grounded. Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40). A verifier handed the full interior cannot verify the claim (Fig 10 :42). A room cannot add an anchor (Fig 7 :35). A verifier is denied evidence bearing only on the maker of a claim (Fig 9 :51).
