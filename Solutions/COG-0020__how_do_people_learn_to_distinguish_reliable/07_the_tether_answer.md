# The Tether's answer on reliable signals and noise

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the curriculum and the criterion value. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A learner must decide, per cue-source-risk slot, whether an observation should move a decision. The cost of treating noise as a signal and the cost of treating a signal as noise differ with the risk, and the information sources differ in how much evidence they share. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symbols table :38-40 adds that what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read. The question's "different risks and information sources" is a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, not a partition (Fig 9 :54). Verdicts are kept per slot and per domain, never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Reliability is a relation, not a carried property.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The same cue can be a signal for one residual and noise for another. What survives is a per-slot verdict against an outcome that does not move when the learner's confidence moves (Fig 2 :29-30).
2. **The split is an ordered threshold, not a symmetric class label.** Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities." The two errors have different costs, and Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Different risks change the criterion and the amount of independent checking, not the method. Detection theory's separation of sensitivity from bias and its payoff-dependent criterion are outside corroboration, not the source of the claim.
3. **Frame-internal quantities cannot settle a verdict.** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Confidence, fluency, coherence, and agreement among sources that share an evidence pool are all readings the frame produces. Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice."
4. **The usual broken link is the objective, not perception.** Fig 3 :25-27: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." More attention cannot rescue a missing objective (Fig 3 :19). The repair is to bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before looking: Fig 5 :65-66, "Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."
5. **Refit before minting, and a new source is an import.** Fig 5 :13: "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." An import needs provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13); Fig 8 :50-52 says convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them.
6. **Pass methods, not recordings.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." A list of sources that worked last time is a recording; a procedure for testing a source against a non-updating outcome is a generator. Between environments, Fig 7 :36 requires the next room to differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced; otherwise it is "one long room: motion, no progress" (Fig 7 :32).
7. **Two of the things called noise are different objects.** Fig 13 :32: "the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Symbols table :38-40 says what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read. Within-level distractors are one thing; discarded detail and out-of-reach residual are another. A learner who labels the second kind noise has turned an instrument limit into a fact about the world.
8. **A cue that stops paying is a channel failure until shown otherwise.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :25-26 names saturated metrics and pooled readings. Goodhart's law and the base-rate false positive paradox corroborate the two mechanisms from outside.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a checklist of trustworthy sources. Fig 11 :51 refuses a standing list.
- Not a universal accuracy target, a criterion number or a training schedule. Those are D5 gaps.
- Not that signal detection theory, base-rate research or Kahneman and Klein are wrong. They are used as low-overlap corroboration.
- Not that confidence is useless. It is a reading; it is disqualified as a **verdict**, not as data (Fig 1 :27).
- Not that agreement is always corrupt. Fig 2 :41 says it is "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." It becomes worthless as a verdict when the frames share an evidence pool.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at a slot was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it, and the refuters

A source-discriminating instrument, not a longer argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened 2026-11-22, says an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source" and that the color case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding questions are:

1. At a slot, does a cue's reading separate competing candidate sources, or do several sources produce the same reading?
2. Does the outcome used as the anchor remain unchanged when the learner's confidence or the reported reliability changes (Fig 2 :29-33)?
3. Do two purportedly independent sources draw on different evidence, or the same feed (Fig 2 :31-32)?
4. Does a method passed to a new habitat produce a new success, or only a replay of the old one (Fig 4 :22-25)?
5. Standing refuters written before the run (see `01` section 4): reliability survives a residual change with no re-enumeration; a frame-internal quantity settles a verdict; a state-state or structure-structure composition cannot be refused; a recording produces a new success in a changed habitat; practice fails to improve hit-false-alarm separation in a regular environment with feedback.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The curriculum and the criterion value are D5, a `gap` with no nameable anchor. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 9 :32: "→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The canonical README adds that indiscernibility is not identity: a cue set that has always paid is not proven complete.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not hand out a list of trustworthy signals. They say that reliability is a relation between a cue, a residual and a habitat, so no standing list survives a change of environment; that the signal-noise split is an ordered threshold whose costs differ between false alarms and misses, so higher risk changes the criterion and buys more independent readings rather than changing the method; that confidence, fluency and agreement within one evidence pool are frame-internal and cannot settle a verdict; that the usual learning failure is a missing objective rather than weak perception; and that what can cross to another learner is a method for testing sources, not a recording of the sources that worked last time. The framework returns the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument owed at each slot. It stops short of naming the cues or setting the number, and it reports that stop as a gap rather than silence.
