# Order, isomers and functional groups in learning to detect signals

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

A learner's cue set fails in two different risk environments and with two differently overlapping source sets. Three questions are owed: (1) which link of the dependency chain actually stops the learner, (2) are the two risk regimes isomers over one atom set or do they require different atoms, and (3) what order must a learning procedure follow so that a verdict can be trusted.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a cue, an observation, an outcome, a criterion, a cost pair, a source; states of the world and structures of evidence are not separated here because the framework's six-term substrate applies to the learning loop, not to the cue inventory. Bonds held: the seven, especially ⋛ comparison (threshold) and → sequence. No new atom is available without an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :23). Order by dependency (Fig 3), not by importance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which link breaks? (`specified`, triangulated)

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :25-27 names the middle link: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Therefore the default diagnosis for a learner who cannot separate signal from noise is not a perception deficit. It is a missing objective: the learner has no stated term for what would count as a reliable reading and no test for progress.
- Fig 5 :65-66 gives the repair: "Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else." This is the pre-registered prediction in `02_outreach_email.md`.
- **Falsifier:** if a learner given an explicit objective and a pre-registered prediction still cannot discriminate at above-chance accuracy with immediate feedback in a regular environment, the break is upstream at perception and this diagnosis is wrong.

### Q2: Are two risk regimes isomers? (`specified`, triangulated for the definition)

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Hold the atom set fixed: the same cues, observations, outcomes and cost pair. A high-risk regime and a low-risk regime differ in where the ⋛ threshold sits and how much independence is bought (Fig 2 :42). Same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. They are **isomers**.
- The two regimes are not mere spelling variants. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." A liberal criterion (more false alarms, fewer misses) and a conservative one are different substances even though the ingredient list is identical. Detection theory's sentry example is the world analogue.
- **Falsifier:** if one regime requires an atom the other does not, for example an independent instrument or an extra source that adds an atom to the closure, the relation is no longer isomerism. It is composition plus import, and Fig 6 :23 governs instead. This falsifier is kept open because the world may force a new instrument at high risk.

### Q3: Functional group across learning conditions (`specified`, single-source on the figure side)

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Across D1 to D4 the shared substructure is `cue -> criterion against a cost pair -> ground that does not update`. The qualifier names which member fills each slot: a visual cue or a testimony cue, a liberal or conservative criterion, an outcome measurement or an independent peer reading.
- This is why the four domains cannot be averaged. They share the group but not the members. A learner can fill the cue slot well and the ground slot badly.
- **Falsifier:** if a learning condition can be written without the criterion substructure, for example a pure association with no threshold and no asymmetric cost, the functional-group claim fails for that condition and it belongs to a different family.

### Q4: Order, recursion and the two currencies (`general` on the order, `specified` on the currencies)

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom". Once a cue-outcome relation is settled against the ground, it can serve as an atom in a higher rule.
- Fig 12 :107-110 records the boundary condition: where the arrangement is not recorded, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again. A rule of thumb passed without its derivation is exactly this case. This is the structural reason F9 of `01` prefers methods over recordings.
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A self-maintaining check, such as a registry that regenerates its own evidence, behaves differently across uses from a spent one-off tip. Nothing records which a term is unless the lineage does.
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes". Feedback in learning is a cycle; if the feedback is drawn from the learner's own confidence, the loop closes on itself and becomes Fig 2's panel 1 (mutual update), not an anchor.
- Fig 12 :125-126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." A learner can state a reliability rule cheaply and still be unable to afford the actions needed to check it. The budget decides how much of the cue space is reached.
- **Falsifier:** if a settled cue can serve as an atom at the next level without any record of its derivation, Fig 12 :104 holds but the boundary condition at :107-110 is false; the method-versus-recording distinction in `01` F9 would then need re-census.

### Q5: The order of a checking procedure (`general`)

1. Describe the residual at a slot in effect terms, before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 8 :53).
2. Write down the sought-for shape as an ordinary term with its refuter (Fig 5 :65-66, formula "Direction").
3. Refuse before mint: if the held cue is wrong, a refusal is owed rather than another explanation (Fig 5 :53, formula step 2).
4. Refit before inventing (Fig 5 :13).
5. Import only with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :50-52).
6. Settle against the ground; a gate is not the ground (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 5).
7. Pass up a method and send down a head start, never a recording (Fig 4 :22-25).
8. Change the room when the question changes (Fig 10 :72) and make the next room differ from the last while matching the surfaced residual (Fig 7 :36).

**Falsifier for the whole order:** a successful refutation obtained by reversing steps 1 and 2, that is by naming the frame first and fitting the residual afterwards, would not count under this derivation. Fig 9 :27-28 calls that apophenia. If such a reversed procedure reliably produced ground-settled verdicts across low-overlap frames, the order claim would be refuted.

## CONCLUSION

The learner's likely break is the objective link, not perception. Two risk regimes over one cue set are isomers: same atoms, different threshold bond, different substance. All learning conditions share a functional group with different members, which is why per-domain verdicts must not be averaged. A settled cue becomes an atom at the next level only if its derivation is recorded; otherwise every lineage pays again. Description cost and checking cost are two currencies and do not add. The order is residual first, refusal before mint, refit before invent, import with provenance, settlement by the ground, and methods across boundaries.
