## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, then stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Fig 9 :32: "→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

For this question, a shape is the form of a rule we can state before we can name the reliable cues. **"A per-slot verdict with a risk-set comparison against a non-updating ground, with only methods crossing to other learners"** is a shape. **"Trust experts, check multiple sources, and ignore unlikely claims"** is a name, and no figure line supplies it. The shape constrains: it rules out a standing reliability list (Fig 11 :51), rules out a pooled accuracy score (Fig 1 :22), rules out confidence as a measure (Fig 1 :27), and rules out treating an unreadable residual as noise (Symbols table :38-40). It does not fill the table of cues.

From the exemplar's carried mapping of *The Library of Babel* (not re-read this session): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* A narrowed search returns a constraint on the answer, not the answer.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- **Describe the residual in effect terms first.** Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Applied here: describe the learning problem as a cue-source-risk slot with two asymmetric errors, not as a general "media literacy" problem. Then the relevant outside fields appear: psychophysics, decision theory, epistemology, evaluation.
- **Use near vocabulary and recurse** (carried from *The Library of Babel*): start with the closest held terms, sensitivity, bias, base rate, criterion, and narrow by overlap. Each new term makes the next search cheaper.
- **Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else." For this question: write down what a reliable cue would predict before looking at it, so the preference can be refused by the same test as everything else. This is the formula's "Direction" step and shows up in the first contact as a pre-registered refuter.
- **Stopping** (carried from *The Library of Babel*): the search ends where the empirical question begins, at *"the finish line will also be the starting place."* That is an honest stop, not a failure.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." For this question, an outcome at the slot that does not move with the learner's confidence.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A new instrument or expert frame may add an atom; it must say where it came from and which gap it explains.

**The source problem.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened 2026-11-22, gives the decisive warning. An instrument reads green but "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." Several underlying sources can give the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." Applied here: observing that a source's predictions were right does not identify why they were right. It could be skill, a shared cause, luck, or a private feed the learner cannot see. The remedy is a discriminating instrument, not more agreement. Fig 2 :31 makes the same point in the figures' vocabulary: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."

## 4. What "shape" means specifically for signal versus noise

Four constraints, all figure-derived, form the shape.

1. **Per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Verdicts attach to a cue-source-risk slot, never to a learner-wide score.
2. **Ordered threshold.** Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities." Operators table: comparison is the test about which is larger. Treating noise as signal and signal as noise are different errors, with costs set by the risk (Fig 2 :42).
3. **Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Perception, criterion, social independence and cross-generational transfer are separate conditions with different anchors. One lever leaves the intersections standing.
4. **Method, not recording.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What a learner may pass to another learner is the checking procedure and a head start, not the list of cues that worked once.

A correct answer in this shape reads: for each slot, name the outcome that anchors it, state the two error costs, write the prediction a reliable cue should make, name an instrument that would separate competing sources of the observed regularity, and record which part of the method can cross to another learner. An incorrect answer reads: a universal checklist of trustworthy signals.

## 5. Noise is partly a bookkeeping fact

Fig 13 :32: "the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Symbols table :38-40: "what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read." So there are two things people call noise. One is the within-level distribution of distractors that signal detection theory models. The other is the detail a coarser description dropped, which is a residual relative to the instruments held. The second one is not out there waiting to be ignored; it is out of reach. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." A learner who labels the second kind "noise" has converted an instrument limit into a fact about the world. That re-census dissolves the apparent conflict between Fig 13 and detection theory (see `01` section 7, S2).

## 6. Prestige, agreement and the simulation limit

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (quoted in the exemplar's `05`, not re-read this session): "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A reliability claim backed by famous names or a large literature gets a hearing; it does not get settled. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Same essay on the status of this work (carried): "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." And: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." From *How Intelligence Actually Works* (carried): "the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." A cue set that has always paid is not thereby proven complete.
