## 3. Distribution rules: offer, do not push

1. **Fig 4 :22**, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework to someone who has not described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and the refuter.
2. **Fig 10 :42**, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
3. **Fig 9 :27**, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true *before* the links are shown.
4. **Fig 2 :40**, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Never select recipients for likely agreement and never pool later agreement as corroboration.
5. **Fig 2 :42**, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Cost here is a false verdict about a system people rely on, so spend real effort on low-overlap checkers.
6. **Seat Map**, "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A list ranked by receptiveness produces a convention, not a check.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim; (2) the four refuters, written before the run; (3) "what would you count as a failure?"; (4) only if asked, the figure links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) the formula as a reusable method, with each user writing their own refuter and mode.

## 4. Two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the per-domain shape and the pre-registered refuter. Record the verdict before showing any theory.

**Stage 2, opt in.** If they are curious, offer the primary links in section 1. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Stage 3, method only.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must name a ground and write a refuter before running it, because the canonical article warns its outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."
