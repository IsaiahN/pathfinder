## Residual in effect terms (the thing actually being decided)

A system is handed an item that presents as evidence. It predicts a label: this item tracks the ground, or this item is crafted to produce belief without tracking the ground. The observation arrives later, when the ground settles whether the claim held. `R` is the gap between that prediction and that observation, indexed **per item**, not as one global accuracy number (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :28). Persuasive misinformation is not merely false content. It is content whose surface was optimised against the same frames that will judge it, so its fluency and its agreement with an audience pool are exactly the quantities the figures disqualify as verdict evidence (Fig 2 :39 to :41). The question asks whether that discrimination can be *reliable*, which in this framework means settled against an anchor that does not update, recorded with a lineage, and refutable in advance, not merely self-consistent or widely agreed.

## What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term

Before the run, the sought-for shape is written down so it can accrue a residual like any other term: **"reliably distinguish" = per-slot `R` ranges against an anchor that does not move when the system's frames push on it; the reading is sourced, not merely fluent; independent low-overlap frames agree only at the ground; and the system reports its own unreached cases rather than certifying its completeness.** If this shape fails any refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6, it is refused, not reinterpreted.

## Headline outcome, in one line

The question is a **union compound**, not one capability: the ground-anchored classification loop, the audience-belief loop, and the system's self-certification loop have different actors, currencies and anchors, and their verdicts are never averaged. Ground-anchored per-slot discrimination is reachable **in shape** with a provenance import and a live channel; agreement-based reliability and self-certified reliability are foreclosed by the figures. No deployed system is measured here, so nothing is `grounded`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that any named detector, platform or fact-checking organisation is reliable or unreliable. That would require a ground reading this derivation does not have.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words.

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list of people selected for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40).
2. **Do not hand the recipient the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.
3. **No verdict before ground.** `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate." An unmeasured reliability claim is a specification of what to measure, not a result.

Legally: public opt-in submission channels avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM-style failure that unsolicited bulk outreach to harvested addresses creates. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
