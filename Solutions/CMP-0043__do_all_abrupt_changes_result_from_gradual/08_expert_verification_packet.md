# Expert verification packet: the Tether on abrupt change and warning

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in tipping points or complex systems can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a real system is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do all abrupt changes result from gradual loss of resilience?" is a union of conditions, not one law. In the threshold-approach condition a gradual loss of stability with slowing recovery, rising variance and rising autocorrelation is expected. In the noise, rate and shock conditions an abrupt change can occur with no change in the system's own return rate, so if a warning exists it lives in the forcing record, not in the system's state. The claim offered for checking is: "no warning was detected" is a report about the observing instrument and its aggregation choice, not proof that no gradual loss occurred. A global resilience average can hide the component that tipped, and no failed search establishes absence. The figure lines below govern that shape; the mechanism names come from the literature and are secondary.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8, 9 and 1

- **Statement:** a search that finds no warning does not prove that no warning existed, and aggregation can hide the live signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44, "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that the observing frame is a sealed room for this quantity, or that a single global average is sufficient to detect every component-level regime shift at the stated resolution. The figures themselves forbid the first; a valid demonstration would be a framework-level refutation.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 11

- **Statement:** detectability is a property of the observing arrangement, not of the system alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 6 :34, "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve." Fig 6 :32, "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
- **Refuter:** a case in which absence of a warning is proved for every possible instrument at every resolution, not merely not yet found. Also: a demonstration that the warning is intrinsic to the system and invariant under a change of observer and aggregation.

### C3: physics-facing; checkable by a researcher against the tipping literature

- **Statement:** the three named mechanisms are isomers over a shared atom set, or at minimum members of one functional group, so their difference is carried by the arrangement and is invisible in a raw time series. In the threshold cell a warning is expected; in the noise and shock branches a large perturbation can give no warning.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **What to check:** is the shared-atom reading faithful to the dynamical-systems taxonomy? Do the empirical reports support no critical slowing down before the shock-like cases, and rising variance without slowing in the stochastic cases?
- **Refuter:** one mechanism requires an atom the others do not; or all three show identical warning signatures at identical resolution, collapsing the behavioural difference that Fig 12 :117 requires; or one indicator is demonstrated with zero false negatives and zero false positives across all branches.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything, and no final answer. The union center stays a compound with its own loop.
- Not that early warnings never work. Critical slowing down is real in the threshold-approach cell.
- Not that any particular observed crash lacked a warning. That needs a ground reading of that system.
- Not that a universal warning law is disproved. It is refused for lack of settlement, not refuted at the ground.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the framework names the mechanism for a system. Mechanism names are bindings from the dynamical-systems frame, provenanced as such.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A warning indicator with zero false negatives and zero false positives across the threshold, noise, rate and shock branches at a declared resolution.
2. An instrument that discriminates a falling return rate from a constant return rate under increasing forcing variance in the same time series.
3. A component-level warning statistic recorded at the resolution of the component that tipped, with aggregation choice and round-trip gap reported.
4. Any of the five pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 firing.
