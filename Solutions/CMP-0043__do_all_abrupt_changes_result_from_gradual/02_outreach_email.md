# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 5 survive your own check. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

**Mode of the claim: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement in this package settles a real system.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about tipping points, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about early warnings for abrupt change that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do all abrupt changes result from gradual loss of resilience?" is not one question. It bundles at least two conditions: a system's own approach to a threshold, and the forcing acting on that system. A single warning indicator searched across the bundle will clear the cases where only one condition holds and leave the intersections standing. In the first condition a warning exists in principle, as slower recovery, rising variance and rising autocorrelation. In the second, an abrupt change can occur with no change in the system's own return rate, so if a warning exists it lives in the forcing record, not in the system's state.
>
> The claim I want checked is narrower than a verdict about any real system: **"no warning was detected" is a report about the observing instrument and its aggregation choice, not proof that no gradual loss occurred.** A global resilience average can hide the one component that tipped. An undetected signal is not an absent signal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:**
> - Not that abrupt change has no causes, and not that early warnings never work. Critical slowing down is real for the threshold-approach condition.
> - Not that I have measured any system. This is a shape and a refuter, not a result.
> - Not that the framework can name which condition a given system is in. It returns a constraint, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. One warning indicator with zero false negatives and zero false positives across threshold-approach, noise-driven, rate-driven and shock-driven change, at a declared resolution.
> 2. A derivation showing noise and rate mechanisms reduce to the threshold case, so the bundle is really one mechanism.
> 3. A demonstration that a single global average suffices to detect every component-level regime shift at the stated resolution.
> 4. A case where absence of detection is proved for every possible instrument at every resolution, not merely not yet found.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I will send them after you have recorded your verdict on the claim above.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the figures and formula are public. Run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage rule

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim and pre-registered refuter. Record their verdict before showing anything else.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the three public source links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground and writes their own refuter first.
