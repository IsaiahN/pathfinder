## 3. Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full framework before a recipient has described what they would count as a failure sends a recording, not a generator.
- Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A reader who has absorbed the interior before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Ask what they would expect to see under each mechanism before giving them the links.
- Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Do not rank recipients by likely agreement, and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Stage 3 sends a reusable method, which is permitted; it does not send an answer.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## 4. The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Residual, shape claim and refuter. No theory, no links. Record the verdict before anything else is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three primary source links (Scheffer 2009, Ashwin 2012, Guttal 2016) and the local figure extract path. Their later agreement is a conversation, never corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method. Each user writes their own refuter, states their own mode, and names the ground that would settle their result.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. The claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is `specified` and ungrounded; if a recipient fires any refuter in section 5 of that file, stop and withdraw the corresponding finding before continuing.
