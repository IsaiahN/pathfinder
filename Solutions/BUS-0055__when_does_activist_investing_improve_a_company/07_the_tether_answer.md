# The Tether's answer on activist investing

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is not one question. It is a union of two conditions indexed per firm and per horizon:

- C1: did the intervention change contact with a paying ground, meaning what customers actually pay for?
- C2: did reach renew, meaning did the firm's capacity change rather than only the arrangement of held atoms?

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The four cells of the union must be kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The announcement metric is not the anchor.** Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." A campaign can push the price and the reported number, so neither settles whether the company improved. The anchor is the realized payment stream at a horizon the campaign cannot push.
2. **Improvement that leaves contact unchanged is not capability change.** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Applied to firms, a buyback that lifts earnings without changing what customers pay for changes the reading, not the capability.
3. **Activist valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same fund benefits one firm and harms another. There is no standing list of good or bad campaigns (Fig 11 :51).
4. **Short-termism is a channel failure.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." When the metric becomes the target, it stops discriminating capacity from appearance.
5. **The distinction is an isomer distinction.** Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." Extract and reinvest use the same cash; the bond differs (subtraction and sequence versus production and sequence). This is why both sides can truthfully say "value".
6. **Costs cascade.** Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect". A first-order gain can carry a third-order capacity loss that direct scoping misses.
7. **Which intervention wins is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." No figure line names the winning campaign for a specific firm. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all activism is harmful, and not that it is always beneficial. The verdict is per firm and per residual.
- Not that the 2015 result (no short-term harm over five years) or the 2016 result (relative underperformance after matching) is the answer. They share an evidence pool and differ in specification; by Fig 2 :40 that is one witness, not two.
- Not that the announcement return measures value. It measures a reading the campaign can push.
- Not a derivation of a specific campaign's outcome. No firm-level ground reading was taken.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument at the firm, not a further argument. The "Hard Problem is About Source" essay (opened 2026-11-22) says an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. Here the questions are:

- Does the post-campaign payment stream change in a way that the metric the campaign pushed cannot explain? That is the contact test.
- Does the firm's reachable set of actions grow, measured by new products, new customers or new capabilities, rather than by the same atoms under a new bond? That is the reach test.
- Does any capacity loss appear at the third order, after the campaign's exit? That is the cascade test.
- Does the arrangement survive the activist's exit as a catalyst (Fig 12 :119) or vanish as a spent part?

Standing falsifiers are the seven in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached at this budget, not unreachable in principle.** The specific winning intervention needs an instrument that does not yet exist in this run, characterised in effect terms but not held. The framework says "a shape, not a name" and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity. The ecology analogue corroborates the mechanism at low overlap but names no campaign.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say activist investing is good or bad. It says the question is two conditions that must be kept separate for each firm and each horizon: did the campaign change contact with the paying ground, and did it renew the firm's capacity? The announcement pop is not the anchor because the campaign can push it. Improvement that leaves contact unchanged changes the reading, not the capability. The activist's value depends on what was broken, so there is no standing list of good campaigns. Short-termism is a failure of the measurement channel, and extracting and reinvesting the same cash are isomers, same atoms under a different bond. Which campaign wins in which firm remains an open slot. What this offers is the shape any answer must have, seven refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it.
