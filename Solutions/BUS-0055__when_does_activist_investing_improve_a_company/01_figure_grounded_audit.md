## 0. The residual under audit

**Question as effect.** When an activist buys a stake and campaigns, the observed firm outcome o' diverges from the campaign's prediction Γ(b,a). The residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is not one number. It is indexed by firm slot and by horizon (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

| what is being decided | option A leads to | option B leads to |
|---|---|---|
| whether a campaign improved the company | one averaged "value created" figure across all targets and all horizons | two conditions kept separate, each with its own ground and refuter |
| whether short-term gains were bought at the cost of long-term capacity | a rhetorical dispute between opposing studies | a measured question about contact and reach per firm |

**The shape claimed as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** Write down what an answer would look like before running: an answer has the form of a union of two conditions, C1 (contact with a paying ground changed) and C2 (reach or capacity renewed), each indexed per firm and per horizon, with an open slot for which specific intervention satisfies both in a specific firm. This shape is bound now so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- Arity: a three-place relation (activist action, firm state, market outcome) observed at two horizons. It carries a sequence bond (→, campaign before outcome) and a comparison bond (⋛, short against long), not a symmetric join.
- Symmetry: "improvement" is a threshold relative to a counterfactual, not an intrinsic property (Fig 12 :85-89, comparison is a threshold relation between two quantities). Extraction and renewal are ordered, not interchangeable: the same cash payment is a return in one arrangement and a capacity drain in another.
- Scale: single firm loop (board decisions), population of targets (campaign selection), and the time channel from campaign to exit; the transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-28).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** A domain is admitted only with a nameable anchor.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Campaign loop (the activist) | activist fund, stake, thesis, campaign actions | residual reduction per campaign; the "value gap" thesis | realized cash flows and payments, not the announcement pop | admitted |
| D2 Firm loop (board and management) | board, executives, routines, Γ | actions, earnings, retained capacity | the paying ground, customers and their payments (Fig 11 :39) | admitted |
| D3 Market habitat | customers, competitors, entrants, regulators, capital markets | survival, share, payment | customer choice and the market (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| D4 Temporal and measurement channel | metrics, reporting horizons, board information, proxy advisors | contact, percept fidelity | the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| D5 Verification frame | researchers, analysts, commenters | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement | admitted |
| X1 "Activist skill" as a free-floating property | the star activist | talent | none nameable | not a domain yet |
| X2 "Short-termism" as a single cause | the quarter | impatience | already inside D4 | absorbed |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The whole is a union of C1 (contact changed) and C2 (reach renewed). They overlap: an intervention can move contact without renewing reach, renew reach without moving contact, both, or neither. "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones" (Fig 9 :55), and "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-58).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. A campaign applied at the planning link cannot rescue a board whose perception link is broken (Fig 3 :19). "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed" (Fig 3 :29).

**Strain test applied before concluding.** The anchor rule (Fig 2 :29-30) and the capability rule (Fig 11 :39-40) govern different quantities: what settles a reading versus what changes capacity. They do not strain. The one live strain is recorded in §7.

## 3. Findings (verbatim figure lines before conclusion)

### F1. Improvement that leaves contact unchanged is not capability change (governing)

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Reading, mode `specified`: a buyback or a cost cut that raises the reported figure without changing what customers pay for changes the reading, not the firm's capability. The same move applied to a firm whose broken link is perception (Fig 3) can change contact. So the verdict is per firm. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 capability rule and Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change.", low overlap: habitat rule versus scale rule).

Refuter: a documented capability gain, measured by new customer payment rather than by the metric the campaign moved, with no change in contact with the paying ground.

### F2. The announced price pop is not the anchor (decisive against averaged verdicts)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

Reading, mode `specified`: the campaign can push the quarterly price and the reported metric, so those cannot settle whether the company improved. The anchor is the realized payment stream over a horizon the campaign cannot push. **Triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 1 frame-internal rule, low overlap).

Refuter: a case where the announcement return reverses completely within the measurement window and the long-run payment stream is unchanged; then the price was not carrying a real prediction and the anchor reading must be restated, not the verdict flipped.

### F3. Aggregating horizons is how the signal disappears

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Reading, mode `general`: a single pooled effect size across targets and across short and long horizons is the exact operation the figure forbids. The residual must stay indexed per firm and per horizon. This is why the two finance findings below can conflict and still both be true. **Single-source** for the indexing rule (Fig 1), corroborated by Fig 9 :57 for the union split.

Refuter: a demonstration that short-horizon and long-horizon residuals trend together with no cross-over under a per-slot decomposition.

### F4. Short-termism is a channel failure, not a ground change

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Reading, mode `specified`: when the earnings target decides pay, it stops being a measurement. This is the figure's own statement of the Goodhart failure (world source read 2026-11-22: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"). A campaign that wins by moving the target rather than the ground degrades the channel. **Triangulated** (Fig 10 channel rule and the world measurement source, genuinely low overlap: metatheory versus accountability studies).

Refuter: documented long-term capacity loss with the channel demonstrably intact (no metric saturation, no pooling, board information traceably grounded in customer payment).

### F5. Activist valence is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Reading, mode `general`: the same fund is a benefactor to a firm whose residual is sleepy capital allocation and a malfactor to a firm whose residual is unenumerated innovation capacity. The campaign is a relation, not a kind. Cremers et al. (2016) supply the firm-level version: the relative underperformance is "driven by firms that are more engaged in innovation and where stakeholder relationships seem more important for long-term value creation." **Triangulated** (Fig 11 valence rule and the matched-sample finance result, low overlap).

Refuter: an activist whose measured valence is invariant across two documented residuals of different kinds.

### F6. Contact cascades; the cost is three steps out

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect"

Reading, mode `specified`: a first-order cash release and a second-order board victory can have a third-order capacity effect that a direct-contact scoping misses. The DDT example is the figure's own case: the residual that mattered was three steps out. **Single-source** (Fig 11) for the cascade rule; the ecology resilience source (read 2026-11-22) independently states the same shape at low overlap: "Efforts to deliberately enhance adaptability can (unintentionally) lead to loss of resilience."

Refuter: a campaign whose full habitat enumeration, out to the cascade stopping point, shows no third-order effect on capacity.

### F7. Two frames that share a pool are one witness

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Reading, mode `general`: Brav et al. (2008), Bebchuk et al. (2015) and Cremers et al. (2016) use overlapping datasets and the same Schedule 13D event definition. Their disagreement is a genuine within-pool dispute about selection and horizon; their agreement would be one witness, not three. **Triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 8, low overlap: anchor independence versus union surplus).

Refuter: two studies with demonstrably non-overlapping samples and instruments returning the same horizon-dependent verdict.

### F8. A thesis that renames the same plan adds no atom

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Reading, mode `specified`: most campaigns propose remedies already in the board's library (payout, divestiture, board seats). These are arrangements over held atoms (Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."). A campaign that only rearranges held atoms can change substance without importing anything. Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." **Triangulated** (Fig 6 and Fig 12, low overlap).

Refuter: a campaign whose predicted gain disappears when its atoms and bonds are written out and compared with the incumbent plan.

### F9. This is a union, so one lever is insufficient

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Reading, mode `general`: announcement return, payout ratio and ROA are filters for where to spend scrutiny. They are not witnesses for whether long-term capacity changed. **Triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 3, low overlap).

Refuter: a single intervention that restores both contact and reach across all four intersection cells with no categorically different residue.

### F10. Naming the winning intervention is a gap, not a hidden answer

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

Reading, mode `general`: after a genuine re-census no figure line names which specific intervention improves which specific firm. The figures return the shape of the answer. The specific list is owed from a source-discriminating instrument at the firm, not from a further argument. A search that finds nothing is not proof of absence (Fig 8 :39). Verdict: `gap`, stated plainly.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A refuter written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A campaign produces a documented capability gain, measured by new customer payment, with no change in contact with the paying ground (breaks F1).
2. A campaign's reported value creation survives a horizon substitution in which the metric cannot be pushed and the payment stream is unchanged (breaks F2).
3. Per-slot residuals at short and long horizons trend together with no cross-over in a multi-firm sample (breaks F3).
4. Long-term capacity loss is measured with the channel demonstrably intact (breaks F4).
5. A campaign's valence is invariant across two different residuals (breaks F5).
6. Full habitat enumeration to the cascade stopping point shows no third-order capacity effect (breaks F6).
7. One intervention restores both conditions across all intersection cells with no categorically different residue (breaks F9).

Until at least one is run, nothing here is `grounded`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 7. Strains, re-census, round trip note

- **Fig 2 :29 versus Fig 2 :30.** One line names a market as an anchor, the next disqualifies anything that moves when pushed. Re-census: the anchor is the payment stream; the pushed reading is the announcement price. Different objects, no strain after the narrowing in §6.
- **Fig 6 :23 "only import moves the wall" versus Fig 12 :117 "different substance."** Re-census: Fig 6 governs the reach (what is derivable), Fig 12 governs the substance of an arrangement over held atoms. An activist can change substance without moving the reach. Different quantities, no strain.
- **The one live strain.** Fig 11 :40 makes contact necessary for capability change. The world finance evidence measures value with market-to-book and abnormal returns, which are readings of the channel, not of contact. Whether those instruments carry contact at a five-year horizon needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded, not hidden.
- **Round trip.** The condensation sent up is "sort campaigns by contact change and reach renewal"; brought back down to the Cremers matched-sample design, two things drop out: the per-slot indexing and the third-order cascade. R_T is nonzero and qualitative; Fig 4 :30 requires it be measured rather than assumed zero. See `11_audit.md`.

**Verdict of this audit.** The shape survives with narrowed wording: activist valence depends on the residual; the announcement metric is not the anchor; improvement requires a change of contact and a question about renewed reach; short-termism is a channel failure; the whole is a union; which intervention wins is a `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. No figure line was smoothed to fit.
