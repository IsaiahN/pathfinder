# Expert verification packet: the Tether on activist investing

**What this is:** a claim in a form a governance or finance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No firm-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "when does activist investing improve a company, and when does it push toward short-term gains at the expense of long-term capacity?" is a union of two conditions, indexed per firm and per horizon: did contact with a paying ground change, and did reach or capacity renew. The announcement metric is not the anchor because the campaign can push it. Improvement that does not change contact changes the reading, not the capability. The activist's valence depends on the residual, so there is no standing list of good campaigns. Extraction and reinvestment are isomers over the same atoms. Short-termism is a channel failure in which a measurement becomes a target. Which campaign wins where is a declared gap. The packet offers the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal and finance-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and the three finance studies

- **Statement:** improvement that does not change contact with the paying ground is not a capability change, and the activist's valence is relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Also Fig 11 :49 - "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Also Fig 11 :51 - "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** does the 2015 five-year result and the 2016 matched-sample result differ in a way that tracks the residual of the target firms? Cremers et al. report relative underperformance "driven by firms that are more engaged in innovation and where stakeholder relationships seem more important for long-term value creation". If the sign of the campaign's effect flips with the residual, the valence claim holds.
- **Refuter:** a campaign whose measured capability gain, measured by new customer payment, occurs with no change in contact; or an activist whose valence is invariant across two different residuals.

### C2. Measurement-facing; checkable by anyone who has used an earnings target

- **Statement:** short-termism is a channel failure, and a metric that becomes a target stops discriminating.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26 - "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 10 :47 - "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".
- **What to check:** in the campaigns you know, did the metric move before or after any change in customer payment? Goodhart's law, opened 2026-11-22, states "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure". If the metric leads and payment never follows, the channel rather than the ground moved.
- **Refuter:** a case of long-term capacity loss with the channel demonstrably intact (unsaturated metric, no pooled readings, board information traceable to payment).

### C3. Structural; checkable by writing the campaign's atoms and bonds

- **Statement:** extraction and reinvestment are isomers over the same cash, and the activist is either a catalyst that survives its exit or a spent part.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :119 - "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- **What to check:** can each campaign be written as `cash - capacity` plus `cash -> return`, or as `cash => capacity` plus `capacity -> payment`? Brav et al. (2008) report that "target prices decline upon the exit of a hedge fund only after it has been unsuccessful", which is a checkable post-exit hinge.
- **Refuter:** a campaign whose bond type cannot be recovered from disclosed actions, or whose post-exit persistence is indistinguishable from a price pop with no arrangement change.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that activism is always good or harmful.
- Not that any single study settles the question. The 2015 and 2016 papers share a dataset family by Fig 2 :40.
- Not that the announcement return measures value. It is a reading the campaign can push (Fig 2 :30).
- Not that the winning intervention is named. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this run.
- Not a claim of independence for this packet. It shares the figure evidence pool with the primary derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

One instrument at the firm that discriminates a moved metric from a changed capacity. Concretely:

1. Post-campaign payment stream that changes in a way the pushed metric cannot explain.
2. Growth in the reachable set of actions (new products, new customers, new capabilities) rather than the same atoms under a new bond.
3. A third-order capacity effect measured after the activist exits.
4. Post-exit persistence of the arrangement, distinguishing catalyst from spent part.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :40, :49, :51, Fig 10 :24, :47 and Fig 12 :117, :119, eight short passages.
2. Check C1 against one campaign you know: did the target's residual determine whether the campaign helped?
3. Check C2 against one earnings target you have seen: did the number become the goal?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a metric change from a capacity change at the firm, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
