**Subject:** One checkable claim about activist investing, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "when does activist investing improve a company, and when does it push toward short-term gains at the expense of long-term capacity?" is a union of two conditions that must be kept separate and indexed per firm and per horizon.
>
> 1. **Contact changed.** The intervention changed what the paying ground, customers and their payments, actually pays for. A change in the reported metric that the campaign itself can push is not a change of contact.
> 2. **Reach renewed.** The intervention changed what the firm can do, not just how the existing atoms are arranged and paid out.
>
> The same activist is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so there is no standing list of good or bad campaigns. Short-termism appears when the metric that was a measurement becomes the target, and the channel degrades while the underlying capacity is spent. The announcement price pop is the thing the campaign can push, so it cannot be the anchor that settles the question.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that activism is always good or always harmful; that the 2015 finding of no long-term harm settles the question; that the 2016 matched-sample finding of relative underperformance settles it the other way. Those studies share an evidence pool and a horizon choice. A pooled average across firms and horizons is exactly the operation that hides the signal.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a campaign produces a capability gain measured by new customer payment with no change in contact with the paying ground;
> 2. per-firm short and long horizon residuals trend together with no cross-over;
> 3. long-term capacity loss is measured with the reporting channel demonstrably intact;
> 4. one intervention restores both conditions across every intersection cell with no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I will send them after you record your verdict on the output above.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (local framework file): see `10_source_ledger.csv` for the path.
> - Bebchuk, Brav and Jiang, "The Long-Term Effects of Hedge Fund Activism," Columbia Law Review, 2015.
> - Cremers, Giambona, Sepe and Wang, "Hedge Fund Activism and Long-Term Firm Value," 2016.
> - MathisonSlee et al., "Fourteen propositions for resilience, fourteen years later," Ecology and Society, 2022.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
