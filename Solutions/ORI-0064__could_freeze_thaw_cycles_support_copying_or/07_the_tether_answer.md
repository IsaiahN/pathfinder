# The Tether's answer on freeze-thaw cycles and genetic molecules

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the specific prebiotic site. Not `grounded` by this derivation.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. ## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether freeze-thaw cycles can support copying **or** assembly of genetic molecules. In effect terms, dilute and chemically reluctant building blocks do not by themselves make long information-bearing polymers, and a polymer that forms does not by itself make another copy. The question bundles four conditions with different anchors: concentration, assembly, templated copying, and the environmental reality of a cycling site. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. The answer therefore has four verdicts, not one.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, concentration. Verdict: yes as a shape, triangulated.** Fig 13 :22 calls a gradient *"a difference that can be spent"*. Fig 11 :46 enumerates *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*. Ice exclusion creates a directed chemical-potential difference that can be spent on concentrating solutes in a brine vein. The figure frame licenses that shape. It does not name a molecule. Attwater et al. (2010) and Budin and Szostak (2010) report concentration factors above 200-fold from dilute starts; that is the world anchor for the lab case, not a figure verdict.

**D2, assembly. Verdict: yes as a shape, single-source for the figure structure, world-corroborated.** Fig 12 :119 defines a catalyst: *"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :123 defines a functional group: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Activated intermediates and mineral surfaces are bindings onto these structural slots. Zhang et al. (2022) and Ding et al. (2023) show activation, bridged intermediates and ligation in a single frozen mixture. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* The chemistry was imported from the chemistry frame with provenance; no primitive was added.

**D3, copying and loop closure. Verdict: conditional yes, triangulated.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 13 :31: *"each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next"*. A primer extension is a chain; a replication cycle is a loop whose product survives the thaw and becomes a template. Attwater et al. (2010) show high-fidelity RNA replication in ice with the ribozyme stabilised; Attwater et al. (2025) show exponential replication across cycles and partial self-replication of a ribozyme fragment; Salibi et al. (2023) show ribozyme assembly and propagation across vesicle populations. The condition is product retention. Where the sign is wrong, the cycle does not close: freezing ruptures vesicles, and selected RNA and DNA sequences are preferentially degraded (Root-Bernstein and Brown 2022).

**D4, the environmental regime. Verdict: `gap`, unreachable at this budget, reported as a verdict and not as silence.** No figure line returns a Hadean temperature amplitude, a cycle period or a site. Fig 6 :34: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The cited authors themselves bound the problem: Salibi et al. (2023) note that their −80 °C cooling differs from plausible environmental fluctuations, and Root-Bernstein and Brown (2022) argue that freeze-thaw cycling may have begun early but that the context has been little investigated. A source-discriminating geological instrument is owed, not a guessed pond.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "freeze-thaw caused the origin of life". No figure line governs a historical event, and no instrument in this folder read one.
- Not "cold is good for RNA". Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Cold concentrates and protects in D1/D2; cycling selectively degrades and ruptures in D3/D4.
- Not a universal cycle count, temperature, concentration factor or yield. Those are measurements at named sites and protocols.
- Not a derivation of a specific nucleotide chemistry from the six-term set. Specific chemistries are imported bindings from the chemistry frame, provenanced as such.
- Not a claim that the figures settle the lab results. The HPLC, gel and sequencing anchors belong to the experimenters. This derivation did not replicate them.
- Not a single averaged verdict across the four domains.

## 4. What would settle it, and the falsifiers

The ground here is an instrument or a measurement, not a further argument (Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*).

1. **Closure test:** isolate the product of one round and show it serving as template or catalyst in the next, across independent labs and readouts. This is the figure's cycle test at the ground.
2. **Discriminating readout:** distinguish bridged-dinucleotide, monomer-bridged-oligonucleotide and triplet-invasion routes from a single projection, the way a spectrometer distinguishes sources of green (Hard Problem is About Source).
3. **Compartment compatibility:** measure product retention and membrane integrity through the same cycles that drive activation. Ding et al. (2023) propose separating the activation environment from the replication environment; that proposal is itself a testable design.
4. **Site reconstruction:** a geological or astronomical reading that returns the cycle regime at a named setting. That closes D4.

Standing falsifiers of the shape (written before the run, repeated from `01` section 4): no product survives the thaw; no solute enrichment under any bonding-permitting regime; valence is fixed rather than residual-relative; precursor and copied pools differ only by ingredients; a state-state bond is admitted by the six-term set; any figure line is claimed to settle a chemistry verdict. If any fires, the corresponding claim is retracted in the log.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The four conditions are now describable and the shape is stable. The D4 site is not read. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap between a freezer protocol and a field cycle is `R_T`, and this folder records it qualitatively rather than pretending it is zero.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that freeze-thaw cycles caused life, and it does not name an activation chemistry. It says that the question is four questions with different anchors: concentration, assembly, templated copying, and whether a cycling site actually existed. A directed difference must be spent to concentrate dilute solutes before bonding becomes cheap; a chemical loop closes only if the product survives the thaw and re-enters as input; a catalyst is a part that survives its own use, while a spent part does not; and the valence of cold is relative to which of those four conditions is at issue. The laboratory work has measured the first three under specific protocols, with high-fidelity in-ice replication, one-pot activation and copying, and exponential cycling, while also showing the destructive side: selective degradation and vesicle rupture. The specific prebiotic site remains unreached, and the framework reports that as a verdict rather than silence. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
