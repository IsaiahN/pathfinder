# Outreach draft: check the shape and its refuter before deciding whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 1.3 are kept separate in the text. If the email collapses concentration, assembly, copying and site into a single "yes", the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one structural claim about freeze-thaw cycles before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` structurally, not a chemistry result):** freeze-thaw is a habitat condition enumerated from the world, not a primitive of the framework. What the figures license is a shape, in three parts. First, a directed difference must be spent on concentrating dilute solutes before bonding becomes cheap; the ice eutectic brine does that physical job in the experiments I have read. Second, a chemical loop closes only if the product of one round re-enters as input on the next round, so a cycle that degrades or loses its product is not a replicating cycle. Third, a catalyst survives its own use, while a spent part does not. Nothing in the figures names a nucleotide, an activating agent or a yield. Those are bindings from the chemistry literature, with provenance, not derivations.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures show a particular activation chemistry works, that a specific Hadean pond has been identified, or that the laboratory freeze-thaw protocols are automatically plausible field conditions. Those are empirical questions for an instrument or a geological reconstruction. I also am not claiming that cold is generally good for RNA: the same cycling that concentrates solutes and slows hydrolysis also degrades some sequences and ruptures vesicles. Whether it helps depends on which of the four conditions is at issue.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a controlled experiment in which the product of one freeze-thaw round demonstrably cannot serve as template or catalyst in the next, across independent labs and readouts;
> 2. a measurement showing that the relevant solutes are not enriched in the interstitial brine under any bonding-permitting regime;
> 3. evidence that the valence of a cycle is fixed rather than relative to the residual, so the same regime helps and harms nothing selectively;
> 4. a demonstration that precursor and copied pools differ only by ingredients and not by typed bond arrangement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Attwater et al. 2010, "Ice as a protocellular medium for RNA replication", Nature Communications: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1076
> - Zhang et al. 2022, "Freeze-thaw cycles enable a prebiotically plausible and continuous pathway from nucleotide activation to nonenzymatic RNA copying", PNAS: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9169909
> - Root-Bernstein and Brown 2022, Life, on freeze-thaw cycles as a selection process and the gap in apparatuses: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9605314/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> With thanks,
> [name]

## Distribution constraint

This email is one to one. It is not a campaign. Fig 7 :35 says *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A list selected for people likely to agree would manufacture the correlation that disqualifies their agreement (Fig 2 :40). A Track B reply, if one is ever authorized, is a conversation and never corroboration.
