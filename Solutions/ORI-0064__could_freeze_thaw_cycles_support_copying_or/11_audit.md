## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: a cycling condition enumerates the habitat; a directed difference is spent on concentration; a loop closes only with product retention; cold's valence is relative.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete world claim: freezing concentrates solutes above 200-fold, activation and copying occur in one frozen mixture, and product strands serve as templates in later cycles under a coupled pH/freeze-thaw regime.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **specific protocol conditions** (flash freezing in liquid nitrogen, −80 °C cooling in the vesicle work, high Mg2+, coupled pH swings) are dropped by the shorthand, so a reader could mistake a lab regime for an environmental one;
- the **sign of the loop** is held as a condition by the shorthand but is a measured, and sometimes negative, quantity in the chemistry (selective degradation, vesicle rupture);
- the **independence of the world witnesses** is lost in the shorthand, because six chemistry papers share authors and reference chains and count as one pool, not six corroborations.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a chemical loop needs product re-entry, and a directed difference must be spent on concentration before bonding becomes cheap. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no shared instrument and no field data exist; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
