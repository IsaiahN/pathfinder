## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Equivalence is contested, not given.** Leonardi's review and Chidlow et al.'s study of international business research both show that "equivalence" is used as if it were achieved by procedure. Chidlow et al. found the "technicist view of translation associated with the equivalence paradigm" dominant. This corroborates F3: a convention is doing the work of a verdict.
2. **Back-translation is a validity check, not a guarantee.** Beaton et al. and Brislin (as quoted in Chidlow et al.) both say so. This is F2 in world vocabulary.
3. **Construct bias precedes item bias.** van de Vijver and Milfont and Fischer both hold that without construct or measurement equivalence, comparisons are not fully meaningful. This is F5 and F3 in world vocabulary.
4. **Samples and languages are frames.** Henrich et al. for populations, Jackson and Lindquist for emotion semantics. These corroborate F4: what looks like a universal is often one frame generalised.
5. **A channel is not a meaning.** Shannon 1948 and the 2026 round-trip benchmark, from engineering and machine translation, are deliberately low-overlap fields. They corroborate the shape of F2 while noting the limit: a clean round trip does not prove a clean intermediate step.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. If one did, the rule is to return to the domain census, not to flip the verdict. Note also that all framework essays share an author with the figures; their agreement is internal consistency, not independent corroboration. The world sources above are from different authors and venues, and the ledger records their overlap.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the conditions and the round-trip method, not a finished conclusion about a culture.
- Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if a condition were true before giving them the article.
- Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not select recipients for likely agreement.
- Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Prefer readers in a different closure: translation theorists for the methods claim, cross-cultural methodologists for the equivalence claim, instrument builders for the anchor claim.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the five conditions and the refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what instrument would settle it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1:** the residual, the five conditions, the refuter. Ask for a verdict before any theory is shown. Record that verdict.

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** the three links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Stage 3, opt-in only:** the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names a ground, states a mode and writes a refuter before running it. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the user must bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. As drafted, the conditions are figure-consistent but unmeasured. The round-trip instrument in F2 is the first thing to build.
