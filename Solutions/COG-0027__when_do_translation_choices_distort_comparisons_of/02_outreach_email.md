# Outreach draft - validate the output before deciding about the frame

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the round-trip measurement described in F2 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` exists for the term pair you are discussing. The stage-1 output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Five conditions for a translation to distort a cross-cultural comparison, checked before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** When a concept is carried from one language-culture into another for comparison, the translation is a frame transform with a measurable round trip, not a neutral copy. I have five conditions under which that transform contributes to the observed difference rather than the concepts doing so:
>
> 1. The translation preserves the terms but changes the relation between them, so two different concept molecules are compared as one term. The test is substitution: put the translated term back and ask what was lost.
> 2. The round trip (A to B to A) has a nonzero semantic gap that was never measured. The comparison is then dead reckoning.
> 3. The translation is a declared equivalence that is allowed to decide the comparison's outcome, so a convention has quietly become a verdict. A mechanical check: if changing the translation changes the reported cultural difference, the translation is a frame, not an instrument.
> 4. The two frames draw on the same evidence (same source wording, same adjudicator, same theoretical vocabulary). They are then one frame with two names, and their agreement or difference is the pool talking.
> 5. The comparison is averaged across items, so one item whose translation broke disappears into the mean.
>
> These are written before I show you anything else. They are a shape, not a result: the framework returns the conditions an answer must satisfy, not a verdict about your concept pair.
>
> **What would refute them:** a controlled substitution that changes the relation and changes nothing on a ground-anchored task; a pair with a measured zero semantic round-trip gap that still diverges; a translation varied with no effect on the ground-anchored result; two frames sharing one pool that still produce a replicable ground-confirmed difference; a comparison shown to be a clean partition with no interaction.
>
> If one of these holds, I will retract the corresponding condition. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README (usage rule, including "a shape, not a name"): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a translation you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]

**Two-stage discipline.** Record the verdict on stage 1 before showing any theory. Once a reader has absorbed the framework's interiors, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."). Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
