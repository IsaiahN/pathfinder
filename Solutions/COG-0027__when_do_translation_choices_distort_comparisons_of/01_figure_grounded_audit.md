## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A comparison of concepts across cultures runs through a translation choice: a term, item, gloss or scale is carried from frame A into frame B so that the two can be ranged against one another. Call the result C(A,B). The residual R is the gap between C(A,B) and the comparison that would be obtained if each concept were first settled against its own cultural ground and only then composed.

- **Arity:** binary comparison plus a directional transform. R is indexed per object slot, that is per concept, per item, per bilingual adjudicator, not per study mean (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").
- **Symmetry:** the comparison bond is ordered, a threshold relation of the ⋛ kind (Operators table :47-51); the frame transform is directional; the reverse transform (back-translation) is a second, distinct operator, not the inverse.
- **Scale:** translation is a same-scale frame transform (Symbols table :79-82, `F`, "Cannot be run without a pose"), while the comparison result is a second-order composition over two settled first-order concept molecules (Fig 12 :103-104).

**Distortion** means: mass in R contributed by the transform. It is not the same as a genuine cross-cultural difference. The question "when" asks for the conditions under which the transform contributes that mass.

**Partition or union.** A union compound, not a partition. A cross-cultural comparison has at least four overlapping conditions: a genuine difference in the concepts; an artifact contributed by the transform; high overlap of frames (two names for one frame); and a break at one item slot. Fig 9 :54 "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." A single lever (a better dictionary) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** (1) Settle each concept inside its own frame; (2) describe R in effect terms before selecting an equivalence frame (Fig 9 :27-28); (3) run the transform with a declared pose and measure R_T (Fig 4 :30); (4) establish construct or measurement equivalence before comparing scores; (5) compare. Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Strain test: a figure line that needs forcing governs another quantity. Two strains were found and are recorded in section 6 rather than smoothed over.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

### F1 - The bond is the substance; a term-for-term match can change it invisibly

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Fig 12 :14 "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"

Fig 12 :95-96 "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing."

Operators table :25-27 defines identity (≡) as "two names, one referent" and gives the test: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."

A translation can preserve the apparent atoms, the dictionary glosses, while changing which relation holds between them. The comparison then treats two different molecules as one term. Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Where the two concept molecules share only a functional group, Fig 12 :124 applies: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The identity test operationalises this: substitute the translated term back into the source frame and ask what was lost. If answerable with a concrete loss, ≡ fails and the comparison is of two different substances.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for the prediction that a bond change in a given term pair will show up in the comparison.

**Verdict:** triangulated. Fig 12 (composition) and the Operators table (identity test) have low overlap: one states what a molecule is, the other gives a yes/no test for two names and one referent.

**Refuter:** a controlled term-pair substitution in which no behaviour, judgement or downstream inference changes, with a ground that would have detected such a change. Then the bond change is not load-bearing.

### F2 - Distortion is the unmeasured round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Fig 4 :31 "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"

Symbols table :75-78 defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, what the coarser description cannot hold.

In this domain the transform is `F`, the frame transform of Symbols table :79-82, and its round trip is the familiar back-translation: A to B to A. A comparison that reports C(A,B) without reporting the round trip is dead reckoning. The gap is not noise to be wished away; it is the quantity being asked about.

**Mode:** `specified`.

**Verdict:** triangulated. Fig 4's membrane narrative and the Symbols table/Formula step 6 definition of R_T are different objects (narrative vs notation) with low overlap.

**Refuter:** a term pair with zero round-trip discrepancy under a semantic, not surface, metric, that still yields divergent comparison outcomes on a ground-anchored task.

### F3 - A translation convention distorts when it decides an outcome

Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"

Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

A translation choice is a convention: a declared equivalence between two terms. It is legitimate as a convention. It becomes distorting at the moment the comparison's verdict is decided inside that convention and never ranged against anything outside it. The anchor test is mechanical: if the reported cross-cultural difference changes when the translation is changed, the translation is not a neutral instrument, it is a frame contributing to the verdict.

Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." For "is this a good translation for speakers of B", the speakers are the right anchor. For "does concept X differ between A and B", a speaker judgement is only an instrument, and Fig 2 :37-38 warns instruments "can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

**Mode:** `specified`.

**Verdict:** triangulated. Fig 10 (seat, convention) and Fig 2 (ground, anchor test) have low overlap.

**Refuter:** a study in which a deliberately varied translation leaves the ground-anchored comparison unchanged, with the variation shown to have been detected by the coders.

### F4 - High-overlap frames manufacture a difference that is not cross-cultural

Fig 8 :13 "same atoms, different names"

Fig 8 :14 "their union adds nothing"

Fig 8 :15 "two names for one frame"

Fig 8 :29 "low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"

Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice."

Fig 13 :38-39 "If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."

If the "two cultures" in a comparison share the same instrument, the same source-language wording, the same bilingual adjudicator and the same theoretical frame, they are one frame wearing two names. Their apparent difference, or their apparent agreement, is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40). Independence must be bought, and Fig 2 :42 prices it: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**Mode:** `general`.

**Verdict:** triangulated. Fig 8 (union surplus), Fig 2 (correlation valence) and Fig 13 (independence of the six members) apply the same quantity at different scales and with different objects.

**Refuter:** two frames demonstrably drawing on one evidence pool that nevertheless produce a ground-confirmed, replicable difference in the concept's behaviour.

### F5 - The residual is a union, so unbundle before comparing

Fig 9 :4-5 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Fig 9 :54 "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

A bundle of "concept X in cultures A and B" is usually a union: genuine difference plus translation artifact plus shared frame plus per-item breakage. The method is to split, name each condition and give each its own loop. What comes out is a shape (the conditions), not a verdict about a particular concept pair without a ground.

**Mode:** `general` for the method, `specified` for the claim that any given comparison is a union until shown otherwise.

**Verdict:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 9), corroborated in the world literature by van de Vijver's construct bias and by the ITC precondition guidelines, both read 2026-11-22.

**Refuter:** a comparison shown empirically to be a partition, where each condition is disjoint, each measured separately and no interaction term remains.

### F6 - Two opposed failure modes: inward refinement and outward naming

Fig 6 :29 "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."

Fig 6 :30 "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

Fig 6 :31 "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."

The inward failure is endlessly polishing target-language vocabulary for a concept the target frame cannot represent. The outward failure is importing a term under a name the other frame cannot read. Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world." An import has a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12 "source gate: provenance", Fig 6 :13 "shadow test: explains a gap").

**Mode:** `general`.

**Verdict:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 6). The two tracks are not to be read against each other; doing so is the named error.

**Refuter:** a single procedure that validly answers both the adequacy question and the availability question without one degrading the other, demonstrated on a concept pair where both were previously failing.

### F7 - Residuals must be read per slot, not as a mean difference

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Fig 5 :18 "support evaluated per slot"

A scale-level mean difference across cultures can hide the one item whose translation broke. Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state" licenses a study whose average looks clean while one term is carrying all the distortion.

**Mode:** `specified`.

**Verdict:** triangulated. Fig 1 (agent loop) and Fig 5 (mint pipeline) state the per-slot rule for different operations.

**Refuter:** a ground-anchored demonstration that item-level residuals are independent across the two cultures and that aggregate residuals predict the ground as well as the per-item set.

### F8 - Describe the residual before choosing the equivalence frame

Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"

Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."

If the equivalence frame (say, "this is an emotion term" or "this is a individualistic value") is chosen before the failure is described, any frame will appear to explain it. The discipline is to write down what failed to resolve in effect terms first.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Verdict:** triangulated. Fig 9 (leave/arrive) and Fig 8 (union surplus) agree on the order with low overlap between their subject matters.

**Refuter:** a written protocol in which the equivalence frame was committed before the residual description and the result was still corroborated by a ground the frame could not have anticipated.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A controlled term-pair substitution that changes the bond (relation, framing, scope) produces no change in any ground-anchored comparison that a detector had power to see (breaks F1).
2. A pair with a measured zero semantic round-trip gap still diverges on a ground-anchored comparison (breaks F2).
3. Varying the translation convention leaves the ground-anchored comparison invariant, with the variation independently confirmed (breaks F3).
4. Two frames demonstrably sharing one evidence pool produce a ground-confirmed, replicable difference (breaks F4).
5. A comparison shown to be a partition, with disjoint conditions and no interaction, contradicts the union claim (breaks F5).

If any fires, the condition list must be revised or withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Unbundle before comparing: genuine difference, transform artifact, shared frame, per-item break (F5).
2. Report the round trip per term and per item, using a semantic not merely surface metric (F2, F7).
3. Name the anchor per question and check that it does not move when the frames are pushed (F3).
4. Measure frame overlap and buy independence where the cost of being wrong is high (F4).
5. Apply the identity test: substitute and record what is lost (F1).
6. Keep inward and outward as two tracks (F6).
7. Describe the failure in effect terms before naming the equivalence frame (F8).
8. Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric." Agreement among coders is not that metric.
