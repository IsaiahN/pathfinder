# The Tether's answer: when translation choices distort cross-cultural concept comparisons

**Mode: `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A concept is carried from frame A into frame B by a translation choice, and the two are compared. The residual R is the part of the observed difference contributed by the transform rather than by the concepts. R is indexed per term and per item, not per study mean (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The comparison is a union compound, not one hard question: genuine difference, transform artifact, shared frame and per-item break overlap (Fig 9 :54 "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").

## 2. The shape: five conditions

1. **The relation changes while the terms look the same.** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." A term-for-term match can compare two different molecules as one. Test: substitute and record what was lost (Operators table identity test).
2. **The round trip is unmeasured.** Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Translation is the frame transform F, which needs a pose (Symbols table :82 "Cannot be run without a pose."). The gap R_T is the quantity at issue, not noise.
3. **A convention decides an outcome.** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." If changing the translation changes the reported cultural difference, the translation is a frame contributing to the verdict, not a neutral instrument. Anchor test: Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
4. **The frames share a pool.** Fig 8 :15 "two names for one frame." Same source wording, same adjudicator, same theoretical vocabulary: the apparent difference or agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
5. **The signal is averaged away.** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A clean mean is compatible with one live broken item.

Each condition is separate and falsifiable. Verdicts are not averaged across domains. Conditions 1 and 2 are `triangulated` (Fig 12 with the Operators table; Fig 4 with the Symbols table/Formula step 6). Condition 3 is `triangulated` (Fig 10 with Fig 2). Condition 4 is `triangulated` (Fig 8 with Fig 2 and Fig 13). Condition 5 is `triangulated` (Fig 1 with Fig 5). The method in section 4 is `single-source` for Fig 9, corroborated in the world literature but not derived from it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a verdict about any specific culture, language pair or concept. No such comparison was run.
- Not "translation is impossible." Jakobson's point and the figures' point are about full equivalence of code units, not about the possibility of translation.
- Not "agreement is always corrupt." Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Disqualification applies to verdicts about the world.
- Not that the six-term substrate (Fig 13) is a taxonomy of cultural concepts. That use was refused in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not grounded. No instrument was run, no participants were tested, no R_T was measured numerically.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

Not more agreement. A source-discriminating instrument, in the sense of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding instruments are:

- **Round-trip measurement with a semantic, not surface, metric**, per term and per item, plus a check that a clean round trip did not hide a bad intermediate step (the 2026 round-trip benchmark makes this caveat concrete).
- **Substitution test**: replace the translated term in the source frame and test whether behaviour, judgement and downstream inference change on a ground-anchored task.
- **Anchor perturbation**: deliberately vary the translation and ask whether the cross-cultural difference moves. If it moves, the translation is a frame.
- **Independence audit**: record how much the two frames draw on one evidence pool and buy independence where the cost of being wrong is high (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **Partition test**: show that the conditions are disjoint and non-interacting, which would break the union claim.

Standing refuters are written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and were recorded before this conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for any specific comparison.** The framework returns a kind of thing, not the answer itself (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"). What it can say is what an equivalent would have to satisfy: a measured R_T, an independent frame, a relation that survives substitution, a per-item record. It cannot name which translation is correct for a given pair without a ground. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence", but this question is not declared a gap: figure lines govern the conditions. It stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that translation is impossible, and it does not name the right equivalent for any particular culture pair. It says that a translation is a frame transform with a measurable round trip, and a cross-cultural comparison is distorted when one of five conditions holds: the relation between terms changes while the terms look the same; the round trip is never measured; a declared equivalence is allowed to decide the verdict; the two frames draw on one evidence pool; or the signal is averaged across items until one broken term disappears. Each condition has a test and a refuter written in advance. What the framework offers is the shape a trustworthy comparison must have, not a verdict about a culture. The center stays open until a ground settles it.
