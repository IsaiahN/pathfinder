## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the transform-contributed part of a cross-cultural concept difference. Arity: a binary comparison plus one directional transform, and a reverse transform that is a second operator, not an inverse. Symmetry: the comparison is ordered (a threshold relation), so operand order matters. Scale: the transform runs between two frames at one scale (F); the comparison result composes two settled concept molecules at a higher level. Indexed per term and per item, so a union with four overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54).
- **Domain 1, source frame.** Actors: speakers of A and the concept in Γ_A. Currency: intra-cultural ground contact. Anchor: A's ground. Question type: settlement before composition.
- **Domain 2, target frame.** Actors: speakers of B and the concept in Γ_B. Currency: intra-cultural ground contact. Anchor: B's ground. Question type: settlement before composition.
- **Domain 3, transform.** Actors: translator, term pair, item wording, adjudicator. Currency: R_T. Anchor: back-translation plus committee plus field test, nameable but often missing. Question type: measurement, not negotiation.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: researchers and reviewers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes. In this simulated run its independence is zero.

Order by dependency: describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle each concept before composing (Fig 3 :19); declare a pose and measure the round trip; apply the identity test; compare only after equivalence is established.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :30 (round trip), Fig 12 :5 (bond), Fig 10 :47 (convention), Fig 8 :15 (overlap), Fig 1 :19 (per-slot).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: five conditions, a required R_T, an independent frame, a pose, a per-item record.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete protocol a researcher could run: back-translate an instrument per item, compute a semantic round-trip score, vary the translation to test anchor movement, audit the overlap of the two samples, and substitute the term in the source frame.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **pose**. The shorthand says "a pose is required"; the concrete protocol must say who speaks, to whom and for what task, or F is undefined (Symbols table :82).
- the **intermediate step**. A clean final back-translation does not prove a clean intermediate translation (the 2026 round-trip benchmark, read in `04`). The shorthand's "measure R_T" silently assumed the final score was sufficient.
- the **ground for a concept-in-use**. The shorthand says "range against the ground"; the concrete protocol needs an instrument whose reading does not change with the frame, and for concepts-in-use that is harder to name than for a physical quantity. This is the live strain recorded in `01` section 6.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a term match is not an identity unless substitution loses nothing, and an averaged residual can hide a live item. Those are the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
