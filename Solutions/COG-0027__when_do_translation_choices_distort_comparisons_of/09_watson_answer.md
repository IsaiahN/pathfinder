## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, under what conditions a translation choice contributes mass to the residual of a cross-cultural concept comparison rather than the concepts doing so. Option A: treat the translation as a neutral copy and attribute the whole difference to culture. Option B: treat it as a frame transform with a measurable round trip and keep only the conditions that survive a refuter. What each leads to: A sends a verdict whose anchor moves when the translation changes, and it spends the independence of the people checking it; B sends a shape, a measurement instruction and written refuters, and leaves the specific comparison open until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The part of an observed cross-cultural concept difference contributed by the transform. Arity: binary comparison plus one directional transform, indexed per term and per item (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Symmetry: the comparison is an ordered threshold relation; the transform is directional; the reverse transform is a second operator, not an inverse. Scale: translation is a same-scale frame transform (F), while the comparison result is a second-order composition over settled first-order concepts. Fig 9 makes it a union compound: genuine difference, transform artifact, shared pool, per-item break.
- **Domain 1, source frame.** Actors: speakers of A, the concept in Γ_A, its atoms and bonds. Currency: intra-cultural ground contact. Anchor: A's ground, the behaviour of the concept in that culture. Nameable.
- **Domain 2, target frame.** Actors: speakers of B, the concept in Γ_B. Currency: intra-cultural ground contact. Anchor: B's ground. Nameable.
- **Domain 3, translation transform.** Actors: bilingual translator, term pair, item wording, adjudicator. Currency: round-trip gap R_T. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument (back-translation plus expert committee plus field test). Nameable but frequently absent, which is the hole to report.
- **Domain 4, comparison and verification.** Actors: researchers, the claim, reviewers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: a cross-cultural measurement that does not move when either frame changes its mind. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Partition or union.** Union (Fig 9 :54). A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Unbundle before searching again.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Settle each concept in its own frame; describe R in effect terms before choosing the equivalence frame; declare a pose and run F; measure R_T; apply the identity test; compare only if equivalence is established on the scale used for the scores.
- **Strain test applied.** Fig 13's six-term set is not a cultural-concept catalogue; using it as one would be a strain and was refused. The Fig 8 / Fig 13 independence readings are the same quantity at different scales, not a conflict (Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice.").

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. Overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Translation theory.** Vanessa Leonardi, "Equivalence in Translation: Between Myth and Reality", Translation Journal 4(4), 2000: "This means that in interlingual translations there is no full equivalence between code units." Predicts that a dictionary match cannot carry identity; corroborates the bond/identity verdict. Overlap: distinct author and venue, but shares the Jakobson source with translation studies generally.
- **Business / cross-language method.** Chidlow, Plakoyiannaki and Welch, Journal of International Business Studies 45(5), 2014: "We conclude by advocating that the translation process be reframed as a process of intercultural interaction, rather than a lexical transfer of meaning." Predicts that a lexical transfer decides outcomes improperly; corroborates the convention-as-verdict verdict. Overlap: independent of the framework.
- **Cross-cultural measurement.** Beaton et al., Spine 25(24), 2000: "agreement between the back translation and the original source version does not guarantee a satisfactory forward translation, because it could be incorrect; it simply assures a consistent translation." Predicts a nonzero R_T even when the round trip looks clean; corroborates the transform verdict. Overlap: independent.
- **Cultural psychology.** Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 33(2-3), 2010: "there are no obvious a priori grounds for claiming that a particular behavioral phenomenon is universal based on sampling from a single subpopulation." Predicts that a single frame generalised looks like a universal; corroborates the overlap verdict. Overlap: independent.
- **Cross-cultural method.** Fons van de Vijver, 1998: "Multilingual studies are often based on the tacit assumption that a careful translation of the instrument will lead to a full transfer of all measurement characteristics such as construct validity and reliability." Predicts that the transfer must be tested, not assumed; corroborates the convention-as-verdict verdict. Overlap: independent.
- **Cross-cultural psychology.** Milfont and Fischer, 2010: "The establishment of measurement invariance is a prerequisite for meaningful comparisons across groups." Predicts that comparison before equivalence testing is void; corroborates dependency order. Overlap: independent.
- **Cognitive science of language.** Jackson and Lindquist, Behavioral Scientist, 2020: "These insights suggest that we should be cautious of translation dictionaries that equate words such as the English love and the Turkish sevgi." - **Machine translation engineering (low-overlap field).** "Round-Trip Translation Reveals What Frontier Multilingual Benchmarks Miss", arXiv preprint, 2026: "A correct round-trip translation does not necessarily imply correct intermediate translations, which may contain awkward phrasing, inappropriate formality, register mismatches, or missing cultural nuance that disappear in the final back-translation." ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; no specific translation verdict is asserted. A translation choice distorts a cross-cultural concept comparison when (1) the relation changes while the terms look the same, (2) the round trip is unmeasured, (3) a declared equivalence decides the outcome, (4) the two frames share one evidence pool, or (5) the signal is averaged across items. The first four are `specified`; the method that produces them is `general`. The framework does not name the correct equivalent for a pair, and it does not license a verdict about any culture. This is a `candidate`, ungrounded result. The strongest single figure-grounded point is the anchor test: if the reported difference moves when the translation is pushed, the translation was a frame all along.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a measurement instruction, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the conditions and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The five conditions are removable or revisable without touching the derivation. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, no primitive has to be withdrawn, and a translation can be re-run with a different pose without discarding the data.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-item semantic round-trip measure with a declared pose; a substitution test showing no consequential loss where the relation changed; a translation perturbation that leaves the ground-anchored difference invariant; two high-overlap frames that still produce a replicable ground-confirmed difference; or a partition demonstration with no interaction. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
