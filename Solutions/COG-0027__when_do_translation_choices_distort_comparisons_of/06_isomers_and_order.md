# Isomers, the frame transform, and dependency order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Given a concept C held in frame A and a candidate equivalent E offered in frame B, decide (1) whether C and E are the same substance under a different name, or different substances whose ingredient lists look the same, and (2) in what order the operations must run for a comparison to be assessable at all.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms: the terms available in each frame's library, plus the relation holding between them. Operators: the seven bonds and negation; the unary identity test; the frame transform F; the round trip R_T. No new primitive is assumed. The anchor for each sub-question is declared below; where none is nameable, the sub-question is left open rather than answered.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a translated term the same substance?

- Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands."
- Operators table :25-27 defines ≡ as "two names, one referent", with the test: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."
- Fig 12 :95-96 "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing."
- ⇒ A dictionary match licenses ≡ only if the substitution test passes against a ground. If something is lost (a scope, a sentiment, a causal implication), the two are not one referent. They may be **isomers** (Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour.") or merely members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.").
- Refuter for Q1: a substitution with a demonstrable loss that leaves every ground-anchored inference unchanged.

### Q2 - What kind of operator is translation?

- Symbols table :79-82 defines F as "the frame transform", the map between allocentric and egocentric at one scale, and says "Cannot be run without a pose."
- It is explicitly "Distinct from T_A and T_E, which run between scales."
- ⇒ Translation between two languages at one social/cognitive scale is F, not abstraction or concretisation. It requires a declared pose: who is speaking, to whom, for what task. Without a pose the transform is not merely error-prone, it is undefined.
- The round trip is the composition of F with its reverse, and its gap is R_T (Symbols table :75-78): `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, generalised here to the frame transform. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- Refuter for Q2: a translation performed and validated with no declared pose and no loss of ground contact, demonstrated on a term where pose was previously load-bearing.

### Q3 - What order must the operations run in?

- Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
- Dependency order derived:
  1. Perceive and name the concept inside A against A's ground (Fig 3 links 1 to 2).
  2. Do the same inside B.
  3. Describe R in effect terms, per slot, before selecting an equivalence frame (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second.").
  4. Declare the pose and run F; measure R_T.
  5. Test substitution (≡) and decide isomers versus functional group.
  6. Only then compare scores, and only if equivalence has been established on the scale on which scores are reported (ITC guideline SSI-2, read 2026-11-22).
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." A better dictionary cannot rescue a missing construct equivalence.
- Refuter for Q3: a valid comparison obtained with the order reversed (translate first, describe the concept later) where the reversal is shown not to have hidden a failure.

### Q4 - Does the fisher's six-term set enumerate cultural concepts?

No. Fig 13's six terms (space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network) are a proposed minimal substrate for physical systems. Reading them as a catalogue of cultural concept kinds would be a strain, and a strained line governs another quantity. The only part of Fig 13 used in this solution is its independence condition, Fig 13 :38-39 "If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal." That applies to frames, not to a list of cultural concepts. This refusal is recorded so the analogue cannot later be smuggled in.

- Refuter for Q4: a derivation of a cultural-concept taxonomy from the six terms that does not require adding a seventh term or redefining one.

## CONCLUSION

A translated term is not the same substance merely because its ingredient list matches. The identity test (Operators table) and the isomer distinction (Fig 12) decide this. Translation itself is the frame transform F, which needs a pose and a measured round trip R_T; it is not abstraction or concretisation. The operations are strictly ordered by dependency, with residual description before frame selection and settlement before comparison. The six-term substrate is not a cultural-concept catalogue and is not used as one.

## STANDING FALSIFIER

The whole supporting derivation fails if a translation can be shown, on a ground-anchored cross-cultural task, to preserve the relation between terms (not just their names), to have a measured zero semantic round-trip gap, to require no pose, and to work identically when the dependency order is reversed. Until such a demonstration, the result stands as `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
