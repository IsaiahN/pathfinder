# The Tether's answer on unsettled claims

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the three-domain mapping, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An information system holds claim C in slot s. Γ predicts Γ(b,a); the ground returns o′; R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o′| fails to close. The question names three ways this happens: uncertain (no support), contested (frames disagree), future-dependent (the event has not arrived). Fig 9 :4 says to split rather than search: these are *"not one hard question but several well-formed ones."* Fig 9 :57 adds *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The whole is a union compound with its own loop, not one problem.

## 2. The shape

1. **Keep the residual per claim, never average it.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A corpus reliability scalar is a frame-internal quantity and cannot score the frame that produced it (Fig 1 :27).
2. **Classify the valence of agreement before using it.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. World-claims need independent frames ranged against an anchor; conventions are made by agreement. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
3. **Carry a refusal as a first-class state.** Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*; Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 6 :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."* The claim and its refusal both stay in the record, with provenance.
4. **Report lost contact instead of a guess.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*. For a future-dependent claim the honest output is that the anchor has not returned. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
5. **Evaluate sources relative to the claim.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A whitelist is a standing list. Capability is system-and-habitat (Fig 11 :40). Declare every stake that cannot be removed: Fig 10 :59, *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
6. **Describe the gap before choosing the checker.** Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
7. **Which algorithm implements this is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* No figure line selects an algorithm. That is a verdict of `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a truth score, a consensus rule, or a credibility ranking.
- Not that agreement is always disqualifying. It is efficient for a convention, but a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that a correction deletes the old claim. Nothing is deleted; the reach falls because a refusal was added (Fig 6 :42).
- Not that popularity, engagement or prestige is evidence. They are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not grounded. No measurement settles which implementation is best, and this is a simulated derivation.
- Not a claim that a source has a fixed reliability independent of the claim it is being used for.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

1. **A per-slot discriminator:** an instrument that shows a live claim-level residual surviving a change that a corpus average conceals. That would confirm F1 at the ground.
2. **A valence discriminator:** a case where a convention-claim and a world-claim are handled by the same rule, and an independent anchor shows the rule failing on one of them. That would settle whether the split is load-bearing.
3. **A refusal detector:** a system with no negation operator that nonetheless carries and composes over a refutation. That would break the operator claim.
4. **A channel test:** a claim whose ground event arrives while the channel is stale, showing whether the system reports loss of contact or a verdict.
5. **The framework's own open item:** Fig 6 :47, *"If a term and its refusal are both settled, the record holds a contradiction. Whether that is two scopes, an error in the ground, or a world that changed is not yet decidable here."* An instrument that decides among those three would close this.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is reportable now; the implementation and the source-discriminating instruments are not held. Fig 9 :40-41: *"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*, so every design cut must be recorded. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The stop is placed where the available instruments can no longer distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give an information system a truth score for an unsettled claim. It says that the claim's residual is indexed per claim, so a corpus average throws away the only signal that says where to look; that the system must classify whether a disagreement is about the world or about a convention before it uses agreement at all, because shared-pool agreement is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention; that a correction has to be carried as a first-class refusal, since a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate; and that when the observation channel goes stale the honest output is loss of contact, not a guessed truth value. Sources are judged relative to the specific claim, never from a standing whitelist, and popularity and prestige are filters for attention, not witnesses for truth. The figures return this shape and its refuters. They do not name an algorithm; that stop is a verdict, not a silence, until a ground settles it.
