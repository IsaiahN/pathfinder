## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what an information system should do with claim C in slot s when Γ(b,a) does not match a settled o′. Option A: assign one truth score and update it by the flow of opinions. Option B: keep a per-slot disposition with a classified valence of agreement, a first-class refusal, a channel check and an honest unreached report. What each leads to: A averages away the live signal, lets the anchor update on frames, and leaves the system unable to carry a correction; B sends a checkable shape with refuters written in advance and an open stop where the ground has not returned.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o′| for claim C in slot s, unresolved. Arity: three conditions (uncertain, contested, future-dependent), so a union compound, not one hard question. Symmetry: ordered, not symmetric: describe, split, refuse, settle, compose. Scale: per claim slot; compounds upward to a corpus and downward to one source percept, with a lossy transform between (Fig 4).
- **D1 Uncertain claim, single slot.** Actors: claim, library Γ, observer instrument. Currency: per-slot residual R_s and support. Anchor: the measurement or record that settles this slot. Question type: partition, is there a live slot at all.
- **D2 Contested claim, multiple frames.** Actors: frames/sources, claim, ground. Currency: agreement, correlation, residual. Anchor: the ground, never the vote. Question type: union of world-claim and convention-claim.
- **D3 Future-dependent claim, time channel.** Actors: claim, future event, current percepts, channel. Currency: contact over time. Anchor: the event when it arrives. Question type: union of claim and not-yet-arrived observation.
- **D4 The system itself.** Actors: operators mint, negation, import; record Γ; habitat. Currency: bits for description, actions for settlement. Anchor: ground contact maintained by a seat. Question type: design of the operator set and the habitat.
- **X1 Truth as internal consensus score** and **X2 Prestige or engagement as evidence.** No nameable anchor: both update on frames or are frame-internal quantities (Fig 2 :25, Fig 1 :27). Not domains yet.
- **Union, not partition.** D1/D4 and D2/D3 form two compounds; the whole is a union of three conditions plus their intersections (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: perceive, have terms, say what counts as progress, route, record and carry (Fig 3).

Strain test: Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 look opposed but govern different question types (world-claim vs convention). Re-census resolves the apparent strain; it is not a contradiction. The one live open item is Fig 6 :47, recorded below.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the single-score design does not. Keep R per claim slot; classify whether a disagreement is about the world or about a convention before using agreement; carry a refusal as a first-class non-deletable term; report lost contact and unreached states rather than a guess; judge sources relative to the residual; and treat engagement, recency and prestige as budget filters. The three conditions are a functional group with different qualifiers, the bundle is a union, and the operator order is describe, split, refuse, settle, compose. Which algorithm implements the shape is a declared `gap`. Mode `general`/`specified`, ungrounded, candidate only. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** a system built on the wrong shape would keep aggregating live claim signals into a scalar, let its ground drift with its consensus, and delete corrections instead of carrying them. The cost is not just a bad score; it is a broken channel that looks sound, which Fig 2 :34 says is coherent drift nobody notices. The cost is bounded and locatable because the refuters are written before the design, and it is avoidable by leading with the shape and its tests.
- **undoable:** yes. No claim is deleted if the shape fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The record carries provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-65). A cut that turns out wrong can be kept ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :40-41).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot discriminator showing an aggregate concealing a live failure; a world-claim settled by shared-pool agreement with no anchor that survives a change of pool; a system with no negation operator that carries a refutation; a stale channel returning a correct verdict without re-checking; an invariant source-trust ranking that stays predictive across a change of claim and habitat; or an instrument deciding among the three cases in Fig 6 :47. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
