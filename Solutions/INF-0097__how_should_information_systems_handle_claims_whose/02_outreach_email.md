**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 is stated in the order given there (residual, then refuter, then verdict). The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output about unsettled claims before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about contested and uncertain information. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mapping, not `grounded`):** an information system should not assign one truth score to a claim and update it by the flow of opinions. The gap a claim leaves is indexed per claim slot, not aggregated, and a single reliability number throws away the signal that says where to look. Three things the literature calls uncertainty, contest and future-dependence are not one hard problem. They are three conditions with different operations, and a claim that is contested and future-dependent is a specific compound, not a harder uncertain claim.
>
> The shape that survives has four parts: keep the residual for this claim rather than a corpus average; before using agreement, classify whether the disagreement is about the world or about a convention; carry a refusal as a first-class state so the record can be corrected without pretending the old term never existed; and when the observation channel goes stale, report that contact is lost rather than issuing a truth value.
>
> **What this does not claim:** that there is a single best algorithm, that a source's reliability is a fixed property independent of the claim, that engagement or prestige is evidence, or that a system can settle a claim whose ground event has not happened. The framework returns a shape, not an algorithm name.
>
> **What would refute it:** (1) a single aggregate score that preserves the live per-claim signal under a documented change of one claim; (2) a world-claim settled by shared-pool agreement with no anchor, surviving a change of the pool; (3) a system with no negation operator that nonetheless carries a refutation as a first-class non-deletable state; (4) a closed pool that settles a claim whose anchor has not returned; (5) one intervention that clears all three conditions with no different residue left; (6) a source-trust ranking that stays predictive across documented changes of claim and habitat.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong in that domain and I will retract it there. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, canonical text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this workspace
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public peer-reviewed venue in this field: https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/submit
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a claim you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
