## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*

From *The Library of Babel*, read this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- A shape: *the claim slot must carry a per-slot residual, a classified valence of agreement, a first-class refusal, and a channel check.*
- A name: *use Bayesian updating with a Beta prior and a threshold of 0.8.*

The first constrains any acceptable implementation. The second is an implementation, and no figure line picks it.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, read this session: use the vocabulary you already have that is *"closest to the words that you wish to describe"* and connect them with *"commonly recurring new terms"*; *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* The claim's gap is described before a checker is chosen.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox that by then *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."* For this question, the stop is the point where the shape is stated and the instrument that would close it is named in effect terms.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The canonical rule: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence.

The world sources in `09_watson_answer.md` are exactly such imports: Sperber on epistemic vigilance, the CIA tradecraft primer on disconfirmation, Ecker et al. on continued influence, Tetlock on calibration, and Goodhart/Strathern on metrics that become targets. Each is a binding from a different field onto the shape, recorded with provenance, not a derivation from the six-term set.

## 4. Why reading a source is not the same as naming its cause

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session, gives the mechanism with metamerism: an instrument detects green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; the source might be one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Applied here: an engagement signal, a reputation score and a source label can all read as "this claim looks settled" while their sources differ. Naming the source of that reading from the reading alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a more confident score.

## 5. Q2 - what licenses a particular state name in the slot?

Nothing in the framework picks an algorithm. The framework returns a shape. A particular algorithm is licensed only by a ground settling it, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the algorithm is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

Does this solution commit the forbidden error? It names reportable states (pending, settled, refused, lost-contact, unreached-at-this-budget). That is not the forbidden error. Those are ordinary terms bound so they can accrue a residual, which is what Fig 5 :65 requires: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."* The canonical phrase *"a shape, not a name"* governs a *missing* primitive or a missing algorithm, not the binding of a term we have just defined. It does not license claiming that any one algorithm is the answer.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A campaign that gathers prestigious names to agree with the claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, and let the ground settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
