# Expert verification packet - the Tether on unsettled claims

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on contested information can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three-domain mapping, not `grounded`.** No measurement settles the implementation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An information system should not assign one truth score to a claim and update it by the flow of opinions. The gap a claim leaves is indexed per claim slot, not aggregated; uncertainty, contest and future-dependence are three conditions with different operations, and a claim holding two of them is a specific compound. The shape that survives has four parts: keep the residual for this claim; before using agreement, classify whether the disagreement is about the world or about a convention; carry a refusal as a first-class, non-deletable state so the record can be corrected; and when the observation channel goes stale, report lost contact rather than a truth value. Sources are judged relative to the specific claim, and attention filters are never witnesses. The framework returns this shape and its refuters. It does not name an algorithm.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual is per claim slot, agreement has a valence that must be classified, and a bundle of conditions must be split before search.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 9 :4 - *"Split it rather than search."*
- **Refuter:** a single aggregate reliability score that preserves the live per-claim signal under a documented change of one slot; or a world-claim settled by shared-pool agreement with no anchor that survives a change of the pool.

### C2 - operator-level; checkable against the Operators table and Figures 5, 6 and 10

- **Statement:** correction requires a negation operator; a refusal is added to the record and the reachable set falls because something was added, not deleted; a stale channel reports loss of contact, not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*; Fig 5 :53 - *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*; Fig 6 :42 - *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*; Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :27-28 - *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*.
- **What to check:** is negation genuinely absent from the system you are evaluating, and does it therefore only accumulate? Does its audit trail delete the disputed claim, or does it retain both claim and refusal?
- **Refuter:** a system with no negation operator that carries a refutation as a first-class non-deletable state and composes over it correctly; or a channel test in which a stale channel still returns a correct verdict without re-checking the ground.

### C3 - world-facing; checkable against the wider literature

- **Statement:** the world literature, from fields with little overlap with the framework, independently supports the shape. Sperber et al. describe modulated trust rather than default acceptance; a CIA tradecraft primer recommends evaluating evidence that disconfirms hypotheses; Ecker et al. document that a correction leaves a lingering influence; Goodhart/Strathern show that a measure becomes useless as a target.
- **Sources with quotes:** Sperber et al., *Epistemic Vigilance*, Mind & Language 25(4), 2010: *"Humans depend massively on communication with others, but this leaves them open to the risk of being accidentally or intentionally misinformed."* CIA/Directorate of Intelligence, *A Tradecraft Primer*, 2009: *"evaluation of all evidence that will disconfirm rather than confirm hypotheses."* Ecker et al., Nature Reviews Psychology 1, 2022: *"exerts a lingering influence on people's reasoning after it has been corrected"*. Goodhart/Strathern: *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"*.
- **Prediction:** if the claim were wrong, these fields would be expected to support default acceptance, confirmation-seeking, clean erasure after correction, and reliable self-scoring. They do not.
- **Refuter:** a low-overlap source showing that default trust, confirmation and self-generated scores are reliable for world-claims.
- **Overlap note:** these are independent authors and fields. Their agreement is corroboration of the *shape*, not evidence that the figures are authoritative.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a truth score, consensus rule, credibility ranking or fixed source whitelist.
- Not that agreement is always disqualifying. It is efficient for a convention, but a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that a correction deletes the old claim. Fig 6 :42 says the record only grows.
- Not that any specific algorithm is the answer. Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles the implementation.
- Not a claim that this packet settles the question it reports on. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot discriminator showing a live claim-level residual that a corpus average conceals.
2. A valence discriminator showing a convention-claim and a world-claim failing under one shared rule.
3. A system without negation carrying and composing over a refutation.
4. A channel test where the ground event arrives while the channel is stale.
5. An instrument deciding among the three cases in Fig 6 :47: *"If a term and its refusal are both settled, the record holds a contradiction. Whether that is two scopes, an error in the ground, or a world that changed is not yet decidable here."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :40-41, Fig 9 :4, Fig 6 :42 and Fig 10 :24-28. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against a system you know: does it have a first-class refusal, and does it delete or retain the disputed claim?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a stale channel from a settled claim, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
