# Union, order and operator type in an unsettled-claim system

**Mode: `specified` on the mapping, `general` on the operator order.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles three conditions: uncertain, contested, future-dependent. We need to know (1) whether they are isomers, a functional group, or a partition, and (2) what order the operators must run in so the system does not build on a term it should have refused.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (recap)

Atoms held: the reportable claim states pending, settled, refused, lost-contact, unreached; the operators mint, negation, import; the bonds including comparison ⋛ and sequence →. Anchor: the ground for the claim, never the agreement about it. The three conditions are each a live residual R_s with a different missing quantity: support, independence, or arrival.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three conditions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The three conditions do not share one atom set. Uncertainty holds an ungrounded claim slot; contest holds two frames with a valence question; future-dependence holds a claim and an event that has not arrived. Their atoms differ, so they are **not isomers** under Fig 12's definition.
- They are a **functional group**: all three are "a residual that has not closed against the ground." The group says what kind (unsettled); the qualifier says which member (support, agreement, arrival). That is exactly Fig 12 :124.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if all three conditions can be written over one identical atom set and differ only in the bond between those atoms, the isomer claim fires instead and the functional-group reading fails.

### Q2 - Is the bundle a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55-58: a bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions; one lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. What remains is *"categorically different"*.
- A claim can be contested and future-dependent at once. That intersection is a specific compound with its own loop, so the bundle is a **union**, not a list.
- **Falsifier:** a documented case set in which no claim ever holds two conditions at once would make it a partition and remove the compound.

### Q3 - What order do the operators run in?

- Formula step 2 puts **REFUTED** before mint: a term held that is wrong owes a refusal, *"not an explanation"*. Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** Settlement precedes composition. A claim not settled at this level is not an atom for the next one.
- Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."** Describe the gap, then choose the checker.
- Fig 13 :136: **"It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."** Split precedes search.
- Therefore the legal order is: describe R per slot, split the union, refuse what is held and wrong, settle against the ground, then compose and route. A system that aggregates or composes first has reversed the order and will build on a term it should have refused.
- **Falsifier:** a case where composing first, then refusing, yields the same reachable set and the same ground contact as refusing first.

### Q4 - What kind of bond is agreement?

- Operators table :52: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"** and Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**.
- Agreement is not a bond at all unless a threshold and a quantity are declared. Without them it is a mirror: Fig 2 :35, **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
- With a declared threshold and an independent anchor, agreement becomes a comparison between two frame-readings ranged against that anchor, which is the structured case of Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**
- For a convention, agreement is the product: Fig 2 :41, **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**. But Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** is the guard that prevents the convention from becoming a verdict.
- **Falsifier:** a system that uses agreement as a verdict about the world with no threshold and no anchor, and whose result survives an independent re-check.

### Q5 - Does the system need a seventh state?

- Fig 12 :90: **"Six tests, and they separate the seven bonds. Seven is a count of bonds, not of operators."**
- Operators table :68: **"Seven tests separate the eight named here. Whether there are more is a question no test derived from these can answer."**
- The five reportable states proposed in `01` are not new operators. They are bindings of existing operators and outcomes: pending is support not yet arrived, settled is the ground paying, refused is negation, lost-contact is a channel failure, unreached-at-this-budget is a reach report. None is a seventh bond.
- **Falsifier:** show that one of the five states cannot be written from the existing operator set and requires a new primitive.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are a **functional group**, not isomers: same unsettled shape, different qualifiers. The bundle is a **union**, so it must be unbundled before search and its intersections given their own loops. The operator order is describe, split, refuse, settle, compose; reversing it is the named failure. Agreement is a comparison bond only when a threshold and an anchor are declared, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. No seventh operator or bond is needed.

This rescues what is defensible in a naive "truth score" design without the part that failed: the shape, the split, the refusal and the cancellation of agreement-as-evidence need no new primitive and no popularity-weighted trust list.
