**Subject:** Check one claim about potential output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an unobserved capacity ceiling cannot occupy the anchor position in a policy rule. Fig 2 defines an anchor as something that settles the question and *"does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"*, and adds *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Published potential output estimates revise, and most of the revision comes from re-estimating potential growth rather than from new data on actual output. So the estimate is a reading, not the ground. The shape that follows is: report capacity as a band, read slack per component rather than as one aggregate, and require a policy action to remain acceptable across the band and against independent slack measures.
>
> **What this is not claiming:** that potential output does not exist, that it can never be measured, or that any particular published estimate is wrong. From inside an estimating frame, a genuinely unobservable quantity and one we simply cannot resolve yet look the same. The honest report is that the slot is unreached. It is also not claiming that a consensus of forecasters settles anything. Agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a named potential output estimate that is invariant under all plausible estimation methods and all data vintages, with outturn inflation and employment shown to revise when the estimate revises;
> 2. a single aggregate gap that tracks slack in every sector, region and group with zero residual across dated episodes that include a supply shock and a demand boom;
> 3. a single observed series that is one-to-one with sustainable capacity in all states, so independent slack readings add nothing;
> 4. a vintage round trip that returns the original real-time estimate exactly, with no residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line failure is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Real Potential Gross Domestic Product, CBO series on FRED: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GDPPOT
> - Output gaps in practice: Proceed with caution, VoxEU/CEPR: https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/output-gaps-practice-proceed-caution
> - The figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
