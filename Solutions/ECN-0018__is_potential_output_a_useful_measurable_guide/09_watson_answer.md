## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about an unobserved capacity ceiling used in policy. Option A: treat a published potential output estimate as the anchor that decides the stance. Option B: treat it as a bounded, revisable input that must be read per slot, triangulated against independent observations, and ranged against outturn data. What each leads to: A gives a clean number and a hidden dependence on model assumptions, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B gives a checkable shape, written refuters and an open slot until an instrument discriminates the sources. The question as posed is a union, not a partition: the estimate can be informative and too imprecise to be decisive at the same time.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** One unobserved scalar inferred from several observed series, feeding a policy stance. Arity: many-to-one, so underdetermined and a union compound; informative and imprecise can both hold. Symmetry: candidate sources are symmetric for observed output and asymmetric for capacity utilisation, vacancies, wage growth and inflation. Scale: the published aggregate, its component detail, and its real-time-to-final revision are three levels with lossy transforms between.
- **Domain 1, framework-internal metatheory.** Actors: figures, methods, the library Γ, residual R. Currency: composition, residual, shape. Anchor: the figures as metalaws for structure, the ground for any measurement. Question type: partition, what an inferred reading can carry.
- **Domain 2, economic measurement and national accounts.** Actors: GDP and its vintages, capacity utilisation, unemployment and alternatives, vacancies, hours, wage growth, producer prices, productivity. Currency: published statistics and revision records. Anchor: outturn data and the vintage record. Question type: which series carry slack and how estimates revise.
- **Domain 3, latent-state estimation and macroeconomic theory.** Actors: potential output, NAIRU, trend/cycle decomposition, production functions, statistical filters. Currency: model-based inference. Anchor: prediction tested against Domain 2. Question type: union, one unobserved state with several observationally equivalent drivers.
- **Domain 4, policy decision and verification.** Actors: central banks, fiscal authorities, surveillance bodies, expert readers. Currency: policy stance, agreement and residual. Anchor: outturn inflation and employment, never agreement. Question type: triangulation, not negotiation.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate the habitat before choosing variables (Fig 11); read the residual per slot before aggregating (Fig 1); describe the effect before naming the ceiling (Fig 9); unbundle the union before searching (Fig 9); triangulate before acting (Fig 2, Fig 8); settle at the ground before carrying a verdict forward (Fig 5).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Both halves of the question survive, in different roles. Potential output is useful as a bounded, revisable, per-slot input that frames a policy question; it is too uncertain to rely on as a decisive anchor or a single point value. The either-or is a union that must be unbundled. The figure-derived output is a decision rule: publish a range with its vintage and revision gap, read slack per component, triangulate against low-overlap observations, accept an action only if it survives the range, and range everything against outturn inflation and employment, which are the anchor because they do not revise when a model does. No episode verdict is asserted. No figure line was forced; Fig 13 and the Fig 12 isomer analogue are retracted as strained and non-governing. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The strongest figure-grounded point is the anchor test: Fig 2 :29-30 says an anchor must not move when a frame pushes it, and the published estimates demonstrably revise.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a point estimate as a verdict instead of a shape and a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost, if the shape were wrong, is over-caution: treating an informative estimate as unusable and discarding a real signal. The union reading is precisely the guard against that.
- **undoable:** yes. No capacity value is asserted, so nothing has to be withdrawn to correct this work; only the shape could be refined. If a band were later published, it could be superseded with a new vintage, and no primitive would have to be removed. Publishing the revision record makes the correction visible rather than silent.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A numeric vintage round trip with zero residue would break F5 and Fig 4 :30; a single aggregate gap that tracks all named slots would break F2 and Fig 1 :22; a proof of structural unidentifiability would move the slot from `unreached` to `underivable` and change the instruction; a source-discriminating instrument that separates a demand boom from a fall in capacity would name the slot; a demonstration that outturn inflation and employment revise when a capacity estimate revises would break the anchor assignment in F1. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
