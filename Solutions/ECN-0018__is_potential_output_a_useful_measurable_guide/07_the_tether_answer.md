# The Tether's answer: is potential output a useful measurable guide for policy?

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a decision rule, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A policy institution observes output, inflation and unemployment directly but wants the distance to the sustainable capacity ceiling to decide how hard to push demand. The ceiling is inferred by a model from those same readings, and the inference revises when the model or the vintage changes. The question "useful or too uncertain" bundles two conditions that can both hold: the estimate is informative, and it is too unstable to be decisive. Fig 9 :57-58: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Unbundle before searching.

## 2. The shape

1. **An anchor that updates is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Potential output revises, with about two-thirds of the revision coming from re-estimating potential growth. So it cannot occupy the anchor position. The anchor is outturn inflation and employment.
2. **Report a band, not a point.** The unobserved ceiling is set-identifiable at best (identifiability source, `06` Q1). The figure shape is a bounded capacity constraint with a stated interval, vintage and revision history. A point value presented as "the" capacity ceiling is the forbidden name.
3. **Read slack per slot.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Publish capacity utilisation by industry, unemployment by group, vacancies, hours and wage growth alongside the scalar. The VoxEU study finds the aggregate only slightly correlated with other slack indicators, which is what a lost per-slot signal looks like.
4. **Triangulate; do not count heads.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Two instruments with low overlap have surplus (Fig 8 :45). A consensus of forecasters using the same model is one evidence pool wearing many names. Cost of being wrong is a policy error, so independence is worth buying (Fig 2 :42).
5. **Publish the revision as a measured round-trip gap.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Real-time median -0.7 percent to final -0.1 percent in the VoxEU sample is `R_T` made public, not an embarrassment to smooth away.
6. **The verdict is `unreached`, not `unobservable in principle` and not `settled`.** Fig 6 :27: depth and genuine absence look the same from inside the frame. A proof of structural unidentifiability would change the instruction to "replace the concept"; a source-discriminating instrument would name the slot. Neither exists in this folder.
7. **The decision rule, bound in §0 of `01`:** accept a policy action only if it remains acceptable across the published band and against the independent slack readings. The estimate is an input, not the verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

So the answer to the question as posed is: **both, at different levels.** Potential output is a useful measurable guide when it is a bounded, revisable, per-slot input; it is too uncertain to rely on as a decisive anchor or a single point value. The either-or is a union that must be unbundled.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that potential output does not exist, and not that it can never be measured. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that a particular published estimate is correct. No vintage-level computation or episode data were used here.
- Not that the framework settles whether an economy had a given amount of slack in a dated episode. That needs data and an instrument.
- Not a claim that lower potential output explains any particular slowdown, or that a specific NAIRU value is right.
- Not `grounded`, and not a theory of everything. No final room. The centre stays a compound with its own loop.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument and a vintage-level computation, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Vintage round trip:** for a named economy, carry the real-time estimate forward to the final vintage and back; report the residue `R_T` numerically. If it is zero, F5 of `01` fails.
- **Source discrimination:** find an observable that separates a demand-driven boom from a fall in capacity when output and inflation readings are identical. If none exists at any resolution, report `unreached` rather than absence.
- **Per-slot decomposition:** show that a single aggregate gap carries zero residual against industry capacity utilisation, group unemployment, vacancies, hours and wage growth across a dated set containing a supply shock and a demand boom. If it does, F2 fails.
- **Anchor test:** show that outturn inflation and employment revise when a capacity estimate revises. That would restore the estimate to anchor status and break F1.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the six pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate is a visible guess, and the decision to treat a capacity estimate as a reading rather than an anchor is likewise a visible choice, not a neutral fact. Other instruments could discriminate the sources that the current readings conflate. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Potential output is both useful and too uncertain, but not in the same role. It is useful as a bounded, revisable input: publish a range with its vintage, read slack across industries and worker groups rather than as one average, and check that the intended policy action still makes sense across the range and against independent measures such as capacity utilisation, vacancies and wage growth. It is too uncertain to be the anchor, because an anchor must not move when the model changes, and published capacity estimates revise, mostly when potential growth is re-estimated. Outturn inflation and employment are the anchor. The framework does not say capacity does not exist or can never be measured; it says the slot is unreached until an instrument can tell apart the different sources that produce the same reading. What it offers is the shape any decision-grade estimate must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the round-trip gap that must be published rather than hidden.
