## The question restated as an effect

A policy institution must decide how hard to push demand. It observes actual output, inflation, and unemployment directly. It wants the distance to the economy's capacity ceiling, potential output, to judge how much slack remains and how much inflation pressure a given stimulus would create. The decision is whether an estimated scalar can serve as the anchor the policy leans on, or only as one revisable input with a stated band. Option A, treat the estimate as decisive: a clean number, a clear rule, and a hidden dependence on model assumptions. Option B, treat it as a bounded input: a range, an explicit revision history, and a requirement to triangulate against independent measures before acting. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): a **decision-grade capacity estimate**, that is a rule mapping several low-overlap observables to a range with a stated interval, revised transparently, with the policy decision tested for sensitivity across that range.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with a diagnosis of slack.
- No bulk-send plan, and no claim that agreement among forecasters decides the question.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not the anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :29-30: an anchor is *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome"*. For potential output the anchor is outturn inflation and employment, never a head count of forecasters or the estimate itself.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
3. **Showing the framework before taking the verdict spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
