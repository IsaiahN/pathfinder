## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question is: how much information does a de Sitter horizon carry?

Stated as an effect, before any theory is named: an observer in de Sitter space is bounded by a cosmological horizon (the world source *De Sitter space*, Wikipedia, states *"there is a cosmological horizon at r=α"*). Things beyond it can never be observed. The observer wants to count what is accessible, and the horizon is the boundary of that count. Two readings are on offer, and they are not the same question:

- **Option A, geometric:** the horizon has a finite entropy proportional to its area, the de Sitter analogue of Bekenstein-Hawking entropy. The world source *Black hole thermodynamics* records the area law and the constant: *"The entropy is proportional to the logarithm of the number of microstates"* and the area relation fixed at one quarter. If this is all, the answer is a number.
- **Option B, information theoretic:** "information content" means the number of distinguishable states accessible to the observer, or the bits that would be needed to specify the horizon's state. The world source *Information content* defines the bit as the unit: *"An event with probability 100% is perfectly unsurprising and yields no information."* If this is the question, a count and a discriminator are both owed.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula step "Direction"): a shape for the information content with four properties stated in advance, (1) it is finite, (2) it is bounded by the horizon area, (3) it is counted per patch rather than averaged globally, (4) the coefficient and the microstate basis are not asserted until a ground settles them. Binding this in advance makes the preference refusable by the same test as anything else.

The residual is the gap between the horizon as presented (a finite boundary in a finite region) and a count that has not been settled at the ground. It is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: the geometric entropy claim and the accessible-information claim are two conditions, and *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57-58). Each needs its own loop.

Scale: semiclassical at the horizon area, microscopic at the Planck cells the count would need. The world source *Black hole thermodynamics* states the limit plainly: *"There is no known way to verify black hole thermodynamics"*, and that *"until 1995 no one was able to make a controlled calculation of black hole entropy based on statistical mechanics"*. D2 has no settled anchor for de Sitter space. This is recorded as a gap at the ground, not hidden by calling the analogy an anchor.

**Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* D1 and D2 overlap at the area, so this is a compound with its own loop rather than a list of two separate questions.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - The horizon is the "contained" boundary of a finite region

Fig 13 :10: **"near, far, between, contained"** (space, a state).

A horizon is the boundary of what is contained, exactly the relation this state names. De Sitter space has such a boundary, as the world source *Cosmological horizon* confirms: the event horizon *"includes all possible future observations: points outside the event horizon can never be observed."*

- **Mode:** `general`. It is a structural reading of the state "space", not a measurement.
- **Refuter:** show that a cosmological horizon is not a containment boundary for the observer (for example, that accessible information is not limited by the horizon at all).
- **Status:** `single-source` within the figures (Fig 13 :10 alone). Corroborated by world sources that share no author with the figures, so the corroboration is real but secondary.

### F2 - Finiteness is what makes the counting terminate

Fig 13 :68: **"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"**.

This is the strongest direct bearing of any figure line on this question. The horizon bounds a finite region, and the figure says finiteness in a finite region is what terminates a count. So the information content is finite, not infinite, and the reason is structural. The world source *Bekenstein bound* says the same in physics language: *"It implies that the information of a physical system, or the information necessary to perfectly describe that system, must be finite if the region of space and the energy are finite."* And *Black hole thermodynamics*: *"the number of degrees of freedom in any volume is bounded and not infinite."*

- **Mode:** `specified`. The finiteness is derived from the bounded region; the value of the bound is not claimed.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a finite-region horizon whose accessible information content is infinite or grows without bound. That would break the figure's application and the world claim together.
- **Status:** `triangulated`. Fig 13 :10 (containment) and Fig 13 :68 (termination of counting) are two different figure quantities with low overlap, and the world sources are independent of the framework author. The triangulation is between the containment boundary and the finiteness rule, not between two restatements of one sentence.

### F3 - Count per patch, never aggregate into one global number

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Symbols table :43-44: R is *"One object, region or entity being tracked. R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*

For horizon information this means the count attaches to patches of the horizon (the natural slots), not to a single global average. A global "information content of the horizon" number is a compression that throws away the only signal that says where the count lives. The world source *Information content* gives the additive structure that makes this precise: *"the total amount of information is the sum of the self-informations of the individual events."*

- **Mode:** `general`. It is a rule about how the residual is indexed, not a claim about any particular bit density.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that the relevant degrees of freedom are irreducibly global, so that a per-patch decomposition loses information that a global count keeps. If that holds, Fig 1 governs a different quantity and this finding returns to census.
- **Status:** `triangulated` within the framework between Fig 1 and the Symbols table, with the caveat that both are framework internal (one evidence pool). The information-theory source is a different field and corroborates the additivity, but does not prove the framework's per-slot rule.

- **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 9 :32 and the canonical README, two distinct sources with low overlap, both framework internal.

### F5 - There is no settled anchor for the microscopic count, so the honest verdict is a gap at the ground

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

For black holes there is a partial anchor: *Black hole thermodynamics* records that Strominger and Vafa computed the entropy of a supersymmetric black hole in string theory in 1995 and that the result agreed with the area formula. For de Sitter horizons, the world source *Holographic principle* records the harder case: dS/CFT and celestial holography are programmes, and the triangulated cases *"fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward."* There is no equivalent settled state count for the de Sitter horizon that this audit found. An observer inside the horizon has no outside vantage from which to count, and the horizon's entropy is observer dependent.

So D2 is a **gap at the ground**. The verdict is not silence. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**.

- **Mode:** `specified`, candidate.
- **Refuter:** produce a settled microscopic count for de Sitter horizon entropy that is checkable by a frame that does not share its evidence pool. That would move D2 from gap to grounded. Note the asymmetry from *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"Being 'below the union horizon' is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet."* A failure to find a count is not a proof that none exists.
- **Status:** `single-source` for the figure definition of an anchor (Fig 2), `gap` for the ground. World sources corroborate the absence of a settled count; an absence of citations is not a claim of originality.

### F6 - A search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence; underivable and unreachable are different facts

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* is explicit that a metatheory *"cannot validate itself"* and that *"You may all agree and be wrong."* Fig 8 :40-42 separates underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-budget, and issues different instructions. Applied here: the absence of a de Sitter microstate count in the sources opened this session is a report about this search, not a theorem that no count is possible. Importing an atom against a budget limit would add something not needed (Fig 8 :42-43).

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** a proof that de Sitter horizon information is underivable in principle, from a frame whose closure differs. Until then the honest label is `unreached`, not `unreachable`.
- **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 8 :39 and the architecture article's own note that several uncited claims *"a search was made and returned nothing, and Rule 3 on Figure 8 says exactly what that is worth"* (low overlap between the figure rule and the article's provenance note).

### F7 - The area law is a binding from the physics frame, not a figure derivation

It has yet to be explained."*

- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded as a framework result. As a physics result it is corroborated by world sources, but world sources are secondary and never overrule the figures.
- **Refuter:** show the figure set derives the coefficient 1/4 from its own atoms and bonds. Otherwise the binding must be labelled with its provenance, exactly as *They're Scanning Books When They Should* requires: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

## 4. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. These are committed now, before any verdict is stated.

1. **Finiteness refuter (breaks F2):** a finite-region de Sitter horizon whose accessible information content is infinite, or grows without bound, with a source that an outside frame can check. The figure application falls with it.
2. **Per-slot refuter (breaks F3):** a demonstration that the degrees of freedom are irreducibly global, so a per-patch decomposition loses information. Fig 1 would govern another quantity and F3 returns to census.
3. **Anchor refuter (moves F5):** a settled microscopic state count reproducing the area law for de Sitter horizons, checkable by a frame that does not share its evidence pool. That moves D2 from gap to grounded. A proof that no such count is possible is a different fact (underivable, not unreachable at budget) and does not fire this refuter.
4. **Substrate refuter (breaks the framework itself):** derive one of the six terms from the other five, or admit a first-order state-state or structure-structure composition that cannot be refused.
5. **Census refuter (breaks F8's replacement):** show that Fig 13 :68 governs black-hole counting only and not cosmological horizons, so F1/F2 must be re-censused.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the change is appended here, not silently erased. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.
