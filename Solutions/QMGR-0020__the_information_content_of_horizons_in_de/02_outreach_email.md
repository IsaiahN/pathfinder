**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 is intact and unchanged. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about de Sitter horizon information, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of the shape of an answer to a physics question, not a physics result. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an observer in de Sitter space is bounded by a cosmological horizon. Under the framework's six-term set, space is the state that includes the relation "contained", and a horizon is exactly such a containment boundary. A second figure rule says that finiteness in a finite region is what makes counting terminate. So the shape any answer must have is: the information content is finite, it is bounded by the horizon, and it is counted per patch of the horizon rather than as one global average. That is a shape, not a number. The framework does not yield the coefficient or a microstate basis.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have a microscopic state count for de Sitter entropy; that the area-over-four coefficient is derived by the framework (it is a binding from the physics frame, and I record it as such); or that an agreement among readers settles anything. There is no settled anchor for the microscopic count that I could find, and I record that as a gap at the ground rather than filling it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this run:**
> 1. a finite-region de Sitter horizon whose accessible information content is infinite or unbounded;
> 2. a demonstration that the relevant degrees of freedom are irreducibly global, so per-patch counting loses information;
> 3. a settled microscopic state count that disagrees with the shape, or a figure re-census that shows the finiteness rule governs black-hole horizons only and not cosmological ones.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README and THE FORMULA, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Distribution rules

- One recipient at a time. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line.
- Stage 1: shape claim, mode, and refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the answer before any theory is shown.
- Stage 2: opt-in only. If they are curious, send the three links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
- Stage 3: offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user states a ground and writes a refuter before running it.
- Never pool later agreement as corroboration. Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- If a reader returns a refutation that fires one of the five in `01` §4, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it there.
