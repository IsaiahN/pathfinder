# Expert verification packet: the Tether on de Sitter horizon information content

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at a de Sitter horizon is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a number.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The de Sitter horizon is a containment boundary of a finite region, and finiteness in a finite region is what makes counting terminate. So any answer about the horizon's information content must be finite, bounded by the horizon, and counted per patch rather than as one global average. The framework does not yield the coefficient or a microstate basis; the area-over-four law is a provenanced binding from the physics frame, not a figure derivation, and the microscopic count is recorded as a gap at the ground rather than filled with agreement. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a number. No coefficient, bit density or microstate basis is asserted as a figure result.
- Not a solution to the black-hole information paradox.
- Not that de Sitter and black-hole horizons carry identical information structure. At most they are isomers over a shared atom set, with the observer's position as the differing bond; the weaker functional-group claim is the one relied on.
- Not that the framework is validated. The framework cannot validate itself (see the architecture article's section "Frameworks that could not validate themselves").
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not corroborated by agreement. Agreement is a convention when the frames share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:
