# Isomers and order: credentialed and local arrangements of qualification

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks *how societies decided who is qualified*. Two stable historical answers recur in the sources: the credentialed-expert arrangement (academies, degrees, peer review, licensing) and the local-knower arrangement (elders, witnesses, practitioners, oral transmission). Two questions are owed: (1) are these two arrangements different substances or two spellings of the same substance, and (2) what is the dependency order among the conditions the shape requires? A correction to my own earlier reading is handled first because it changes what an isomer can be here.

- **States:** a producer of claims, a ground that refuses them, a community that acts on them.

- **Bonds available:** the seven typed bonds plus negation. The relevant ones are `+` conjunction (both conditions hold), `→` sequence (one requires the other), `⋛` comparison (a threshold between two quantities), and `¬` refusal (one term is false, not merely absent).

### Q2 — Are the two arrangements isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Over the full shared atom set `{producer, ground, community, convention, verifier seat, habitat}`, the two arrangements can be written with different bonds between producer and ground:
  - **Credentialed:** producer `→` convention `→` ground. The convention mediates contact. Peer review is a second convention on top of the first. Wikipedia's *Peer review* page describes it as *"a form of self-regulation by qualified members of a profession within the relevant field."*
  - **Local:** producer `+` habitat `→` ground. Long contact and oral transmission mediate, and the convention governs use rather than access. Wikipedia's *Traditional knowledge* page records that the knowledge is *"transmitted orally across generations"* and that *"customary law may guide who may use different kinds of knowledge at particular times and places."*
- Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour when the question changes. Under Fig 12's definition these are **isomers**, and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list that says only "society has experts."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either arrangement requires an atom the other does not (for example, a written record on one side with no counterpart on the other, or a licensing institution with no habitat equivalent), the isomer claim fails and the weaker **functional-group** claim stands instead: Fig 12 :123, **"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** In that case both are members of the kind "qualification arrangement" with different qualifiers, not isomers.
- Status: `single-source` on the figure side (the Fig 12 definition). The two world pages describe the institutions but do not establish that the atom sets are identical. That identity is our binding and is recorded as such.

### Q3 — What is the order among the conditions?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- The dependency order for a qualification decision is:
  1. **Perception / ground contact.** Is anything returning a signal from the world about this person's claims? Fig 6 :34-35: *"the question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
  2. **Vocabulary.** Does the society have terms for what a claim affords and for what would count as progress? Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** A society that can count degrees but cannot say what a degree's holder would do differently has a dictionary where it needs a grammar.
  3. **Objective.** What counts as knowledge here? Fig 3 :10 (*"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*).
  4. **Planning.** Who may check, with what ground, and from which pool? Fig 3 :12-13.
  5. **Learn and carry.** Fig 3 :15 (*"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"*). What crosses is a method or a head start, not a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :22-25).
- A credential placed at step 2 or 3 before step 1 is a term doing the work of a missing instrument. It is the Fig 3 break: effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- **Falsifier:** show a society where the objective and the checking arrangement were specified before any instrument for ground contact existed, and the arrangement still tracked the ground across changes of question. That would reverse the dependency order claimed here.

### Q4 — Catalysts, spent parts and the standing of a credential

- Fig 12 :119: **"Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A credentialing process that re-tests contact at each use is a regenerating part. A title that is never re-tested is a spent part. The record usually does not say which, so the distinction is a question for an instrument, not a definition.
- Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* A credential is standing. Where a stake cannot be removed, it must be declared (Fig 10 :59-60).
- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Peer review is an output (a verdict on a claim) re-entering as input (a credential for the next claim). The sign of that loop decides whether it closes into a mirror chain (Fig 7 :12-13, *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*) or stays open to a ground. Whether it closes is an empirical question about a given institution, not a verdict the figures return in advance.
- **Falsifier:** a credentialing record that shows the re-test at each use and whose verdicts changed when the ground changed, in which case the part regenerates and the spent-part reading is wrong for that institution.

### Q5 — What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :54-55: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- The three conditions (knower in contact, credentialing convention, checking arrangement) overlap and have different anchors. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
- Credentialing is a budget filter: it decides how much effort is spent trying a claimant. It is not a witness for whether the claimant's claims bear on the world. Conflating the two is the union treated as a partition.
- **Falsifier:** a single instrument that measures contact, convention validity and verifier independence in one currency. Then the union collapses to a partition and Q5 is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

The two recurring arrangements are isomers over the full shared atom set, if and only if the atom sets really match; otherwise they are a functional group with different qualifiers. Their dependency order runs from ground contact to vocabulary to objective to checking arrangement to carried method, and a credential installed before the instrument for contact is a term standing in for a broken link. A credential is a spent part or a regenerating part depending on whether the re-test is recorded, and that is a question for a record, not a definition. The union stays unbundled: credentialing filters the budget and never supplies the verdict.

This framing rescues what is defensible in both historical answers without inventing a seventh primitive: no wall-moving import, no ban on naming held bindings, and no standing list of qualified persons (Fig 11 :51).
