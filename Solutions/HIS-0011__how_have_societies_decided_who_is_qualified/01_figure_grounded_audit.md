## 0. The residual in effect terms

> **Question:** How have societies decided who is qualified to produce or interpret knowledge?

Stated as an effect, the question asks for the rule that separates those admitted to speak as knowers from those excluded. In effect terms this is not a query about a list of persons. It is a recurring prediction failure: a society grants the label *qualified* on the basis of a convention (a degree, a title, a lineage, a reputation), then later finds that the label did not track whether the person's claims survived contact with the world. The label persists anyway, because it is standing that outlives the occasion on which it was earned.

The residual `R` is the gap between what the credential predicts (this person's claims will bear on the ground) and what the ground returns (some do, some do not, and the credential does not discriminate). Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* The credential is a frame-internal quantity. Fig 1 :24-27 names it exactly:

> **"coverage, and compression achieved"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

So the residual's ordinary term, *qualified*, is a candidate term that must be re-grounded before it is used. Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect is: a frame whose contact with a non-updating ground can be checked, by a verifier who cannot reconstruct it, drawn from a pool the society did not author for the occasion.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run.** An answer must state (a) what the label separates, (b) what settles it, (c) who may read the settlement, and (d) what would reverse it. Anything that returns only a roster of persons or a criterion such as "a degree" fails this binding, because it names a state and cannot name a route to one (Fig 12 :111). This is the pre-registered shape of the sought-for answer, written before the findings below.

## 1. Domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** The residual is relational. It needs at least two terms (a claimant and an arrangement that tests the claim), and in practice three (producer, credentialing institution, checking frame). A single person alone is not a domain; no qualifier can be decided without something that refuses the claim.
- **Symmetry.** The ordinary label reads as symmetric: qualified people are peers, and "A is qualified" looks exchangeable with "B is qualified." The underlying relation is ordered. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* A credential-to-trust correlation is symmetric, while contact-with-ground is directed. A pooled correlation therefore cannot choose the arrow, and any answer that treats qualification as a stock that a person carries is using a symmetric proxy for an ordered relation.
- **Scale.** Three scales register: the individual knower (one slot), the institution that issues the label (a population of slots with a shared convention), and the whole society (the arrangement of seats and the habitat from which knowers are drawn). Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Qualification must be read per person and per claim, never as a societal average.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the knower in the habitat | practitioners, elders, witnesses, apprentices, local knowers | claims and whether the world keeps them | the ground, the world that refuses | yes, the habitat is enumerable (Fig 11 :43) |
| D2, the credentialing institution | academies, universities, licensing boards, journal editors, reviewers | conventions, credentials, prestige, standing | ought to be the ground; in practice often the institution's own agreement | yes, a maintained seat (Fig 10 :71-72) |
| D3, the checking arrangement | rival specialists, later generations, instruments, other fields, the public | residuals, refutations, agreement | the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35) | yes, an unauthored pool (Fig 10 :73-74) |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. All three have one: the ground for D1 and D3, and for D2 the ground that the institution either keeps reachable or replaces with its own convention. D2 is therefore a real domain, but its anchor is contestable, and the seat map's rule applies: where the anchor is contestable the arrangement reports which condition broke rather than adjudicating.

### 1.3 Partition or union

This is a **union**, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-57). A society needs knowers whose contact survives, institutions that coordinate who may act, and outside frames that can refuse. These conditions overlap; a person can be a practitioner and a credentialed member and a reviewer simultaneously. Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

A lever such as "issue more credentials" clears the region where only credentialing binds and leaves the contact and refusal conditions standing. Verdicts are therefore kept separate per domain and never averaged into one criterion of qualification.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order here is: (1) ground contact of the producer, (2) vocabulary for what a claim affords, (3) the objective, what counts as knowledge here, (4) planning the checking arrangement, (5) learning and carrying methods rather than recordings. The middle link is the one that usually breaks. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* A society that can count degrees but has no instrument for whether the degree's holder still changes contact with the ground has a working link above a broken one, and Fig 3 :19 applies: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

## 3. Findings

### F1 — "Qualified" is a label applied afterwards, not a property carried (triangulated)

Fig 11 :34-36:

> **"What is intelligence?"**
> **"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."**
> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

The question under audit has the same grammar. "Who is qualified to know?" asks for a property carried by a person. The figure's replacement question is about what makes a system capable, and Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The same substitution applies: the answerable question is not who is qualified but what arrangement keeps a knower's contact with the ground reachable, and capability is a property of knower-and-habitat, never of the knower alone.

**World corroboration.** Wikipedia, *Credentialism and degree inflation* (opened 2026-11-23): *"Credentialism is a reliance on formal qualifications or certifications to determine whether someone is permitted to undertake a task, speak as an 'expert' or work in a certain field."* The page also reports Matt Sigelman's description of the degree as *"a proxy for quality employees - a rough, rule-of-thumb screening mechanism"*, and records that the high school diploma once attested to middle-class respectability and later *"barely qualifies the graduate for menial service work"* (citing Randall Collins 2002). The label changes value while the person's contact may not. This corroborates the figure shape without settling the historical question.

**Triangulation.** Low overlap between Fig 11 (post-hoc labelling in an unmaintained habitat) and Fig 1 :26 (credibility accrued is frame-internal), two figures governing different quantities. Mark `triangulated`. The world source is a separate pool but only a proxy observation, not a ground reading of any historical society.

**Refuter.** A historical case in which the label *qualified* demonstrably preceded and caused the ground contact, with the label's content unchanged by what the ground returned and no institution maintaining it. That would make the label a carried property, not a post-hoc one, and F1 fails.

### F2 — Credibility accrued is a frame-internal quantity and cannot score the claim (single-source on the figure side, world corroborated)

Fig 1 :24-27, quoted in §0. The decisive sentence is *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A society that decides qualification by counting credentials, citations, titles or reputational standing is scoring a frame with a quantity the frame itself produces. Fig 1 :23 adds *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

This is the strongest figure line bearing on the question, and it is the one most often violated in the historical record described by the world sources. The credentialism page records that professionalization *"tends to involve establishing acceptable qualifications, a professional body or association to oversee the conduct of members of the profession and some degree of demarcation of the qualified from unqualified amateurs"* and *"creates 'a hierarchical divide between the knowledge-authorities in the professions and a deferential citizenry.'"* That is a frame producing its own score.

**Triangulation status.** `single-source` on the figure side: Fig 1 alone names *credibility accrued*. The world source is genuinely different evidence, but it reports the same shape rather than an independent measurement, so I do not upgrade this to triangulated. The *Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* essay adds the author's own formulation: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* That essay shares the framework author's pool with the figures, so it corroborates the reading, not the fact.

**Refuter.** Show a case where accrued credibility is itself a non-updating anchor for the question asked, that is, where the question is constitutively about reputation rather than about the world, so that counting credibility is legitimate (Fig 2 :36 gives the test: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*). The claim would then be wrong for that domain, though not for knowledge-production questions.

### F3 — A credential is standing that survives the outcome it was earned on (triangulated)

Fig 10 :59-61:

> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."**
> **"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."**

A credential is exactly standing that survives the outcome on which it was earned. Fig 12 :119 adds the mechanism: *"Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* A credentialing process that re-verifies contact at each use behaves like a regenerating part; a title that is never re-tested behaves like a spent part, and the record does not say which it is. Wikipedia's credentialism page names the spent-part signature: credential inflation is *"the devaluation of educational or academic credentials over time"*, and a 2014 US study found *"65% of job postings for executive secretaries and executive assistants now call for a bachelor's degree, but only 19% of those currently employed in these roles have a degree."*

**Triangulation.** Low overlap between Fig 10 :61 (stake that survives) and Fig 12 :119 (spent versus regenerating part). `triangulated` for the shape; the empirical inflation figures are one world pool, not a second witness for the mechanism.

**Refuter.** A credentialing body that re-tests ground contact at every use and whose record shows the re-test, in which case the part regenerates and the F3 failure does not apply to it. This falsifier is a design instruction: ask whether the arrangement records re-verification, not whether it calls itself a credential.

### F4 — A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (triangulated)

Fig 10 :47-49:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Fig 2 separates the two valences of agreement:

> :41 **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
> :40 **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

A credential is legitimate as a convention about who may sign a prescription or use a title. It becomes a failure the moment it is used as a verdict about whether the holder's claims bear on the world. Wikipedia's *Peer review* page (opened 2026-11-23) describes peer review as *"a form of self-regulation by qualified members of a profession within the relevant field"* and notes that *"Academic peer review requires a community of experts in a given (and often narrowly defined) academic field, who are qualified and able to perform reasonably impartial review."* That is a convention doing coordination work. The same page records the failure: *"A particular concern in peer review is 'role duality' as people are in parallel in the role of being an evaluator and being evaluated. Research illustrates that taking on both roles in parallel biases people in their role as evaluators as they engage in strategic actions to increase the chance of being evaluated positively themselves."* The convention has begun to decide an outcome.

**Triangulation.** Low overlap between Fig 10 :47 (convention-outcome collapse) and Fig 2 :40-41 (valence of agreement). `triangulated`.

**Refuter.** A case where the credential is used only as shared meaning, never to settle a claim about the world, and where lowering it would change no verdict. Then F4 does not bite; the credential stayed a convention.

### F5 — The verifier must not hold the interiors, and evidence bearing on the maker is inadmissible (triangulated)

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4, *THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md*: *"The outside frame does not know what the reports describe. It reads shape without referent, which is exactly why it can catch a failure of shape that everyone who knows the content reads straight past."* Fig 9 :51 is the admissibility rule:

> **"evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"**

Applied here: whether a knower has the right school, the right supervisor or the right pedigree is evidence about the maker, not about the residual. The fact that peer review suffers role duality is the world-side instance of this failure. Wikipedia's *Peer review* page records that reviewers are experts in the same field and that *"impartial review, especially of work in less narrowly defined or inter-disciplinary fields, may be difficult to accomplish."* That is the correlation the arrangement is meant to buy independence against, priced by Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Triangulation.** Low overlap between Fig 10 :42 (soundness from non-access) and Fig 9 :51 (admissibility rule). `triangulated`. The peer review source is corroboration about an institution, not a measurement of a verifier's independence.

**Refuter.** A documented case where a reviewer who held the claim's full interior produced a more reliable refutation than an outside frame with a different vocabulary, and where the advantage was not explained by shared evidence. That would refute the non-access rule for this domain.

### F6 — The pool from which knowers are selected must be unauthored (triangulated)

Fig 10 :73-79:

> **"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available."**
> **"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."**
> **"a lottery" / "everyone who qualifies" / "a set assembled before the question existed" / "or the habitat itself"**

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

So there is no standing catalogue of qualified persons. A society that draws its experts only from a list authored by the previous generation's credentialing body has authored its checking room. Wikipedia's *Traditional knowledge* page (opened 2026-11-23) supplies the low-overlap world instance: traditional knowledge is *"a cumulative body of knowledge, know-how, practices and representations maintained and developed by peoples with extended histories of interaction with the natural environment"* (ICSU study group, quoted on the page), it is *"transmitted orally across generations"*, and *"customary law may guide who may use different kinds of knowledge at particular times and places, and specify obligations that accompany the use of knowledge."* The page's photograph caption records that tribal colleges pass on knowledge *"through employing community Elders"*. The elders are the habitat enumerated, not a roster authored for the occasion. The page also records the counterpressure: critics *"see such demands for 'respect' as an attempt to prevent unsubstantiated beliefs from being subjected to the same scrutiny as other knowledge-claims."* Both sides of that dispute are needed: an unauthored pool is necessary, not sufficient, and it still must range against a ground (Fig 2 :45).

**Triangulation.** Low overlap between Fig 10 :73-79 (selection mechanism) and Fig 11 :43-51 (enumeration and valence). `triangulated`. The traditional knowledge page is a genuinely different field (anthropology and indigenous knowledge), so it is the strongest independent world witness in this solution.

**Refuter.** A case where the unauthored habitat pool consistently missed a refutation that the authored roster caught, with the difference explained by something other than correlation at origin. Then F6 fails for that domain.

### F7 — Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation (triangulated)

Fig 2 :39-42:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* To ask who is qualified by asking whom people already trust is to select the room for agreement, which is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**Triangulation.** Low overlap between Fig 2 :39-42 and Fig 7 :35. `triangulated`. The Dunning-Kruger result below bears on the maker's self-assessment, not on the selection rule, and is not used as a second witness here.

**Refuter.** A documented case where correspondents selected for receptiveness produced a refutation of the claim at a rate indistinguishable from a randomly drawn pool, and where their shared evidence was demonstrably low. Then selecting for agreement would not have manufactured the correlation in that case.

### F8 — A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure (triangulated)

Fig 10 :71-72:

> **"The room composition is a function of the question."**
> **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

Fig 10 :80-81: *"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

A lifetime credential is a room fixed at the question the candidate answered once. When the residual changes, the credential tracks the old question. Wikipedia's *Sociology of scientific knowledge* page (opened 2026-11-23) describes the discipline's aim: *"the objective of the researcher is to explain why one interpretation rather than another succeeds due to external social and historical circumstances."* It also records the controversy: *"Both scientists and philosophers have fiercely criticized sociological theories of scientific knowledge for ignoring the objective reality of nature."* That conflict is not a verdict about which room is right. It is evidence that the room must change with the question and that the ground cannot be replaced by the room's agreement.

**Triangulation.** Low overlap between Fig 10 :71-72 and Fig 7 :36. `triangulated`. The science-studies source is one pool and is used only to show the historical contingency of rooms, not to settle the science wars.

**Refuter.** A standing panel whose membership never changed and whose verdicts tracked the ground across several different residuals better than rotating panels. That would refute the dependence of room on question for that domain.

### F9 — A person's low self-assessment ability is a broken link, not a qualification criterion (single-source figure reading, world corroborated)

Fig 3 :25-27 names the middle link that has no instrument: having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A person who cannot distinguish good from bad performances has no instrument for the residual, so self-certification cannot close it.

**World corroboration, genuinely low overlap.** Wikipedia, *Dunning-Kruger effect* (opened 2026-11-23): the effect is *"the systematic tendency of people with low ability in a specific area to give overly positive assessments of their ability"*, and *"the metacognitive explanation holds that poor performers misjudge their abilities because they lack the ability to recognize the qualitative difference between their performances and the performances of others."* David Dunning is quoted on the same page: *"The first rule of the Dunning-Kruger club is you don't know you're a member of the Dunning-Kruger club."* The page also records the statistical explanation (regression to the mean plus the better-than-average effect) and says most researchers accept that *"regression toward the mean is a relevant statistical effect that must be taken into account when interpreting the empirical findings."* I keep the mechanism contested; the figure claim is only that self-assessment is not a ground, which does not depend on which explanation is correct.

**Triangulation status.** The figure side is `single-source` (Fig 3 plus Fig 12, which are different figures but both about vocabulary, so not independent in the way Fig 11 and Fig 1 are). The world source is a different field (human cognition) but corroborates only the shape. Mark `single-source`, with honest note that the overlap between the two figures is high.

**Refuter.** A domain in which self-assessment is demonstrated to be calibrated against a non-updating ground at the level of individual claims, so that self-certification is a usable instrument. Then F9 does not apply there.

### F10 — The union needs three loops with different anchors, not one criterion (triangulated by the census itself)

Fig 9 :54-57, quoted in §1.3. The three domains have different anchors and currencies. D1 is settled by the world that keeps a claim; D2 is a maintained seat that coordinates the label; D3 is a checking arrangement whose anchor is the ground and never agreement. Fig 9 :60 forbids converting a filter into a witness. A credential is a filter for the budget of who gets tried; it is not a witness for whether the person knows.

**Refuter.** A single instrument that settles all three domains with one currency, in which case the union collapses to a partition and this finding is wrong. No figure line supplies such an instrument, but the refuter is stated so an outside frame can look for one.

### F11 — Isomers over a fixed atom set, with a falsifier (single-source figure definition, world corroborated)

Credentialed-expert and local-knower arrangements can be written over the same atoms (producer, convention, ground, verifier seat, habitat) with a different bond between producer and ground: a credential-mediated bond in one, a long contact and oral transmission bond in the other. Under Fig 12 :117 they are then **isomers**: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If the arrangements do not share the full atom set, they are a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123): *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The full derivation and falsifier are in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Triangulation.** `single-source` on the figure side (the Fig 12 definition). The peer review and traditional knowledge pages describe two institutions that could be two instances, but the atom-set identity is our binding, not a world finding, so I do not call this triangulated.

**Refuter, written before the run.** If either arrangement requires an atom the other does not (for example, no written record on one side, or no institution on the other), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A standing list of qualified persons survives a change of residual and remains correct (breaks Fig 11 :51 and Fig 10 :72).
2. A credential is shown to be a non-updating anchor for knowledge questions, so that counting it is legitimate ground contact (breaks Fig 1 :26 and Fig 2 :33).
3. A verifier that held the interiors reliably outperformed a different-vocabulary outside frame, with the advantage not explained by shared evidence (breaks Fig 10 :42).
4. An authored roster of checkers beat an unauthored habitat pool, with the difference not explained by correlation at origin (breaks Fig 10 :73-74).
5. A measurement shows qualification is a property carried by the individual across habitats (breaks Fig 11 :40).
6. A single instrument settles the producer, convention and verification domains in one currency (breaks the union reading, Fig 9 :54-57).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and the packet is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2. They are the two figure lines that re-ground the question's ordinary term: *qualified* is a label applied afterwards, and credibility accrued is a frame-internal quantity that cannot score a claim.
2. Keep the three domain verdicts separate (F10). Do not collapse them into a universal criterion or a percentage.
3. Treat credentials as conventions (F4) and as filters for the budget (Fig 9 :60), never as verdicts about the world.
4. Ask for a refutation, not agreement (F7). Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, the shape and the refuter, not a list of names.
5. Report the literal roster as a `gap`. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* No figure line returns a standing list of qualified persons, and Fig 11 :51 refuses one in advance.
6. In outreach, ask what the recipient would count as a failure before showing them any theory (Fig 9 :27-28; Fig 10 :52).

### Strains and re-census

- **Fig 11 :36 versus the historical fact of licensing bodies.** The first pass read these as a contradiction: societies plainly do maintain qualification. Re-census resolves it. Fig 11 :36 governs the unmaintained mode (no seat, post-hoc label). Fig 10 governs the maintained mode (a seat enumerates, ranks and introduces). Different objects, so no strain after re-census. Both remain in the audit because both are needed for the answer.
- **Fig 2 :41 (agreement efficient for a convention) versus Fig 2 :40 (agreement disqualifying for a verdict).** Not strained. Same quantity, two valences, set by what the question is constitutively about (Fig 2 :36). Kept visible in F4.
- **Fig 12 :119 (spent and regenerating parts) versus F3.** The figure does not say which part a credential is. That is the live empirical question, named as a refuter in F3 rather than silently decided.
- **Fig 13 physical substrate versus social terms.** The six-term set (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network) is not remapped onto kinds of expert. Doing that would be the forced-fit error the README warns against. The figures that govern this question are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12, each at the scale of a checking arrangement.
