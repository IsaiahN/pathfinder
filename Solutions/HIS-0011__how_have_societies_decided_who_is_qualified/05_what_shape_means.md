## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* The same essay says a bundle of conflated questions must be *"search[ed] all of those problems individually"* before combining them.

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name its occupant. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is the set of conditions a qualification decision must satisfy (a knower in ground contact, a convention that stays a convention, a verifier denied the interiors, a pool not authored for the occasion), and it is **not** a roster of qualified persons or a single criterion such as a degree.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the ordinary term *qualified* was not accepted as a primitive. It was re-described in effect terms (a label separating who may speak, settled by a non-updating ground, read by a verifier who cannot reconstruct the claim), and only then were figure lines selected. That is Fig 8 :53, *"Residual first, frame second"*, applied to vocabulary.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

For this question, a historical society's credential is a binding from that society's frame onto a held slot (who may act). It is not a new primitive and it does not move a wall. Naming a specific credential as *the* answer would turn a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why a self-scored credential is the named error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered can all produce the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

A credential is a reading. It cannot comment on the source, that is, on whether the holder's contact with the ground is what produced the passage through the institution. Several paths create the same credential: long apprenticeship, institutional gatekeeping, social closure, and genuine competence. Wikipedia's credentialism page records occupational closure and the *"hierarchical divide between the knowledge-authorities in the professions and a deferential citizenry."* The appropriate response is a discriminator, not a name. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A credential that changes with the institution's opinion of itself is not that.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 5. Q2 — what licenses a particular qualification mechanism?

**Nothing in the framework licenses a particular roster or a universal credential.** The framework returns a shape. A particular mechanism is licensed only by the ground settling a term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the mechanism is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does the historical record commit the error the README forbids?** Often, yes: a reading (the credential) is treated as the source (the knower's contact). But see the correction in §6, because the error is misreading a projection as a source, not the mere act of naming a held binding.

## 6. Correction to my own §3 and §5 (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1)

I initially treated *"shape, not a name"* as a ban on naming any qualification mechanism. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in the library.

Credentials, conventions, grounds and verifier seats are not missing primitives of the framework. They are bindings from the history-of-expertise frame onto terms the framework already holds (library, bond, ground, seat, habitat). Naming one is a **binding with provenance**, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains wrong is claiming that the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"*) or that the framework itself selects which credential a society ought to use.

The §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: treating a credential reading as a report of the knower's source is still the projection error. The reason is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held atoms. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof. Someone let reputation substitute for result. Someone let the trusted name validate the untested claim."* And the operative clause: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers prestigious names to endorse the shape claim is the named collapse. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground, a historical case with a checkable record, settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a historical study and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 8. Summary of the shape for HIS-0011

The shape is a four-part separator:

1. **Post-hoc label.** Qualification is a label applied afterwards to a lineage whose claims the ground kept (Fig 11 :36); it is not a property carried across habitats (Fig 11 :40).
2. **Convention with a boundary.** The credential is a convention about who may act, efficient for shared meaning (Fig 2 :41); when it decides a claim about the world it has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
3. **Non-updating ground and denied interiors.** A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42), and evidence bearing only on the maker is inadmissible (Fig 9 :51).
4. **Unauthored pool that changes with the question.** The pool is the habitat enumerated, not a roster authored for the occasion (Fig 10 :73-74, Fig 11 :43), and it changes when the question changes (Fig 10 :72).

What the shape does not give: the name of any particular credential, the correct historical periodization, or a standing list of qualified persons. Those are bindings or gaps, and the distinction is recorded.
