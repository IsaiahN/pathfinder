## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the mismatch between a qualification label and ground contact. Arity: three terms. A claimant alone is not enough; there must be an issuer of the label and something that can refuse the claim. Symmetry: *qualified* is grammatically symmetric (peers), while the relation it reports is ordered, because a frame ranges against the ground and the ground does not range against the frame (Fig 2 :45). A correlation between label and truth cannot choose that arrow. Scale: person, institution, society. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." My boundary matches the primary's.
- **Domain 1, knower and habitat.** Actors: practitioners, elders, witnesses. Currency: claims that survive. Anchor: the ground. Question type: unmaintained, so the label is applied afterwards. Fig 11 :36 governs.
- **Domain 2, credentialing seat.** Actors: academies, boards, editors. Currency: conventions and standing. Anchor: the ground when reachable, otherwise the institution's own agreement. Question type: maintained seat. Fig 10 governs.
- **Domain 3, checking frame.** Actors: rival frames, instruments, later generations. Currency: residuals and refutations. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: selection of a room. Fig 10 and Fig 7 govern.
- **Union or partition.** Union. The three conditions overlap and have different anchors. Fig 9 :55. I did not need the primary's conclusion to reach this: the anchor test alone forces it, because a partition would require one anchor to settle all three domains and no such anchor is named in the figures.
- **Order by dependency.** Ground contact before vocabulary, vocabulary before objective, objective before checking arrangement, then carrying methods. Fig 3 :20. Refusal before mint (Formula step 2), residual described before a frame is chosen (Fig 8 :53).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. The routes differ: I derived the union from the anchor test and the domain split from the maintained/unmaintained distinction (Fig 11 versus Fig 10), while the primary derived them from the Fig 11 post-hoc-label line and the Fig 9 union rule. Low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular inside the framework. It is still one evidence pool, so no independent weight attaches.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **maintained/unmaintained distinction** (Fig 11 :36 versus Fig 10 seats), which the shorthand compresses into "post-hoc label" and which must be restored at the concrete level;
- the **convention/verdict boundary** (Fig 2 :40 versus :41), which the shorthand states as "convention" and which becomes a live test only when an institution's actual use is examined;

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a claim's ground must not update on the checker's opinion, and evidence bearing only on the maker is inadmissible. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no historical case was checked; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
