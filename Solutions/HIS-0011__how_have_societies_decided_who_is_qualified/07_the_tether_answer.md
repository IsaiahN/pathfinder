# The Tether's answer: how societies decide who is qualified to know

Mode: `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal roster of qualified persons. Not `grounded`. This is a shape and a route, not a historical result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The ordinary question asks for a list or a criterion. In effect terms the residual is the gap between a credential's prediction (this person's claims will bear on the world) and what the ground returns (some do, some do not, and the credential does not reliably discriminate). Fig 1 :26-27 names the defect: "predicates minted, and credibility accrued" and "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces" The answer cannot be a better credential scored by the same frame; it must be an arrangement in which a ground that the frame cannot move settles the claim.

The question bundles three conditions, so it is a union and not one problem with one answer (Fig 9 :54-55). Verdicts are kept separate.

## 2. The shape, with per-domain verdicts

### D1, the knower in the habitat — `specified`, triangulated

Qualified is a label applied afterwards, not a property carried. Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." The same substitution gives: a knower is called qualified after their claims survive contact, not before. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Capability is a property of knower-and-habitat, never of the knower alone (Fig 11 :39). The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43), and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51).

World corroboration: the credentialism source shows the credential behaving as a proxy whose value changes over time; the traditional-knowledge source shows knowers selected from an enumerated habitat (Elders) under customary rules of use; the Dunning-Kruger source shows why self-certification cannot settle the matter. These are three different fields, but only traditional knowledge is a genuinely low-overlap witness; the other two corroborate shapes.

### D2, the credentialing institution — `specified`, triangulated

A credential is a convention about who may act, and it fails when it decides a claim about the world. Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on (Fig 10 :61), so a credential needs a recorded re-test at each use or it becomes a spent part (Fig 12 :119).

World corroboration: peer review is described as self-regulation by qualified members (a convention) and criticized for role duality (the convention deciding an outcome). The credentialism source records occupational closure and credential devaluation.

### D3, the checking arrangement — `specified`, triangulated

A verifier must range against a ground it cannot move, must be denied the interiors of the claim, and must be drawn from a pool not authored for the occasion. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 9 :51: "evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it." Fig 10 :73-74: "A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second." Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change."

World corroboration: the role-duality finding in peer review; the traditional-knowledge page's record that critics demand the same scrutiny for such claims; the sociology-of-scientific-knowledge page's record that interpretations succeed for social and historical circumstances, with the science-wars criticism kept in view.

### The union, kept unbundled

These three domains have different anchors and currencies. Credentialing filters the budget of who gets tried (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."). It never supplies the verdict about the claim. Over a fixed atom set, the credentialed and local arrangements are isomers (Fig 12 :117), with the fixed-atom condition stated as a live constraint in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a roster. No figure line names who was qualified in a given society, and Fig 11 :51 refuses a standing list in advance. The roster is a `gap`, a verdict and not silence (Fig 13 :134).
- Not that credentials are worthless. They are legitimate conventions (Fig 2 :41) and useful budget filters (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that local practitioners are always right and credentialed specialists always wrong. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same actor flips valence when the residual changes.
- Not that science studies settles whether a historical case was justified. The discipline's own account records interpretative flexibility and a fierce criticism from scientists and philosophers. That conflict returns to the census, never flips the figure verdict.
- Not `grounded`. No historical measurement was taken and no case was adjudicated here.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. From The Hard Problem is About Source: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved the case. Here the corresponding instruments are:

1. Re-test record. For a given institution, does the credential's record show a fresh test of ground contact at each use? If yes, the part regenerates (Fig 12 :119); if no, it is spent and F3 of `01` applies.
2. Independence test. Did the verifier's verdict come from a frame whose closure differs from the claimant's, and was evidence bearing only on the maker excluded (Fig 9 :51)? A role-duality audit answers this for peer review.
3. Change-of-question test. When the residual changed, did the room change (Fig 10 :72)? A panel that stayed fixed while the question moved is the named failure.
4. Standing-list test. Did any roster of qualified persons remain correct across a change of residual (Fig 11 :51)? A single counterexample would fire refuter 1 in `01` §4.
5. Cross-habitat test. Does qualification travel with the individual across habitats (Fig 11 :40)? A controlled comparison would settle whether capability is carried or relational.

The six pre-registered refuters in `01` §4 are the standing falsifiers, written before this conclusion. If any fires, withdraw the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

Unreached, not unreachable. The three conditions are stateable as a shape, and the instruments above are nameable. No historical case was checked against them in this run, so nothing here is settled. The figures stop where the available instruments stop, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." A search that returns no roster is not a proof that no roster ever existed (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a list of qualified people. It says that qualified is a label applied afterwards to a lineage whose claims the world kept, not a property a person carries (Fig 11 :36, :40). It says the credential that issues the label is a convention about who may act, legitimate for coordination and disqualifying as a verdict about the world the moment it decides one (Fig 2 :40-41; Fig 10 :47). And it says a claim is settled only where a verifier ranges against a ground that cannot be moved by opinion, is denied the interiors of the claim, and is drawn from a pool the society did not author for the occasion (Fig 2 :29, :35; Fig 10 :42, :73-74). Those three conditions have different anchors, so they are a union and their verdicts stay separate. Credentialing filters who gets tried; it never supplies the verdict. What the framework returns is this shape, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The roster stays open until a ground settles it.
