# Expert verification packet: the Tether on who is qualified to know

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or sociologist of expertise can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal roster. Not `grounded`.** No historical case is adjudicated here. This is a shape and a route, not a result. The whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A society's decision about who is qualified to produce or interpret knowledge is a union of three conditions with different anchors, not one criterion with one answer. First, *qualified* is a label applied afterwards to a lineage whose claims survived contact with the world; it is not a property a person carries across habitats. Second, the credential that issues the label is a convention about who may act, efficient for shared meaning, and it fails only when it is used to decide a claim about the world. Third, a claim is settled only where a verifier ranges against a ground that does not update on opinion, is denied the interiors of the claim, and is drawn from a pool the society did not author for the occasion. Credentialing filters who gets tried; it never supplies the verdict. The framework returns this shape, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not return a list of qualified persons.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11

- **Statement:** *Qualified* is a post-hoc label, and credibility accrued cannot score the claim it helps produce.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :36 — *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."* Fig 11 :40 — *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 1 :26 — *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*; Fig 1 :27 — *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **Refuter:** show a standing list of qualified persons that survives a change of residual and remains correct (breaks Fig 11 :51); or show a credential that is itself a non-updating anchor for knowledge questions, so counting it is legitimate ground contact (breaks Fig 1 :26 and Fig 2 :33).

### C2 — institution-facing; checkable against the history of credentialing and peer review

- **Statement:** a credential is a convention that fails when it decides an outcome, and standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on. A credentialing process that does not re-test contact at each use is a spent part, and the record usually does not say which it is.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 — *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 2 :40 — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 10 :47 — *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :61 — *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 12 :119 — *"Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **What to check:** take one institution (a medical licensing board, a journal, an academy) and ask whether its credential is used as a verdict about the world or only as a convention about who may act. Then ask whether its records show a re-test at each use.
- **Refuter:** a credentialing body whose record shows the re-test at each use and whose verdicts changed when the ground changed, in which case the part regenerates and the spent-part reading is wrong for that institution. Also: a case where the credential is used only as shared meaning and lowering it changes no verdict, in which case C2 does not bite.

### C3 — verification-facing; checkable against the arrangement described in the literature

- **Statement:** a claim is settled only by a verifier ranging against a non-updating ground, denied the interiors of the claim, and drawn from an unauthored pool; the room must change when the question changes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 10 :42 — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 9 :51 — *"evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."* Fig 10 :73-74 — *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* Fig 10 :72 — *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."*
- **What to check:** in one documented controversy, identify the ground each side ranged against, whether the checkers held the claim's interiors, who selected them, and whether the panel changed when the question changed. The peer-review literature's *role duality* finding and the traditional-knowledge literature's customary rules of use are two places to look.
- **Refuter:** a verifier that held the interiors reliably outperformed a different-vocabulary outside frame with the advantage not explained by shared evidence; or an authored roster of checkers beat an unauthored habitat pool with the difference not explained by correlation at origin.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything. No final room; the three domains keep their own anchors (Fig 9 :54-55).
- Not a roster of qualified persons. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- Not that credentials are worthless. They are conventions (Fig 2 :41) and budget filters (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that local practitioners are always right. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- Not a verdict on any historical case. The sociology-of-scientific-knowledge literature records both interpretative flexibility and a strong criticism from scientists and philosophers; that conflict is noted, not adjudicated.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.
- Not that "shape, not a name" forbids naming a held mechanism. The README phrase governs a *missing* primitive; naming a binding is provenance. See `05` §6 and `06` Q1.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Re-test record:** does the credential's record show a fresh test of ground contact at each use?
2. **Independence test:** did the verifier's verdict come from a frame whose closure differs from the claimant's, with maker-only evidence excluded?
3. **Change-of-question test:** did the room change when the residual changed?
4. **Standing-list test:** did any roster survive a change of residual and remain correct?
5. **Cross-habitat test:** does qualification travel with the individual across habitats?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :36 and :40, Fig 1 :26-27, Fig 2 :40-41, Fig 10 :42, :47, :61, :72-74 and Fig 9 :51. Ten short passages.
2. Pick one institution from your own field and say which of C1 to C3 it fails, if any.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a credential reading from the knower's ground contact, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
