# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are in the body of the message. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about how societies decide who counts as an expert

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from the history and sociology of expertise. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** a society grants the label *qualified* on the basis of a convention (a degree, a title, a lineage, a reputation), then later finds that the label did not track whether the person's claims survived contact with the world. The label persists anyway. The question I am asking is not who the qualified people were in a given period. It is what arrangement keeps a knower's contact with a non-updating ground reachable, and who is in a position to read the result.
>
> **The shape claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the three domains below, not `grounded`):** three conditions are needed and they are a union, not a partition, so the verdicts stay separate.
>
> 1. Knowers whose contact with the world survives. The framework's Fig 11 says that where nothing maintains anything, a label like *intelligent* is applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. I am claiming the same for *qualified*: it is applied afterwards, not carried.
> 2. An institution that issues the label. This is a maintained seat. Its credential is a convention about who may act, and Fig 2 says agreement about a shared meaning is efficient for a convention. It becomes a failure only when the convention decides a claim about the world.
> 3. A checking arrangement with a ground that does not update on anyone's opinion, a pool not authored by the people being checked, and a verifier who is not shown the interiors of the claim. Fig 10 says a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that any particular credential is worthless, that credentials can be replaced by a roster of wise people, that local practitioners are always right and specialists always wrong, or that the framework returns a list of who is qualified. It returns a shape. Fig 11 says no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.
>
> **I wrote these refuters down before sending this.** The shape claim fails if any of the following holds:
>
> 1. A standing list of qualified persons survives a change of residual and remains correct.
> 2. A credential is shown to be a non-updating anchor for knowledge questions, so counting it is legitimate ground contact.
> 3. A verifier that held the interiors reliably outperformed an outside frame with a different vocabulary, with the advantage not explained by shared evidence.
> 4. An authored roster of checkers beat an unauthored habitat pool, with the difference not explained by correlation at origin.
> 5. A measurement shows qualification is a property carried by the individual across habitats.
> 6. A single instrument settles all three domains in one currency.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I am interested in whether the shape is wrong, not in whether you like it.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem in your own field you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
