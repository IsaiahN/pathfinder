## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Knowledge tied to place, identity, or long experience" is a union of three conditions with different anchors, not one question. What makes any of them testable is a four-part checking arrangement: an anchor at the place that does not update on the community's opinion; frames that keep **separate** contact with that same anchor, so agreement is triangulation and not one evidence pool talking; a refusal operator aimed at the held term whose residual stays flat and does not trend down; and a path across generations that carries methods upward and head starts downward, never a recording of one occasion mistaken for the method. Identity carries the pretext that makes compressed messages work, but it cannot settle an empirical dispute. Long experience is a check only when the experience is separate. Consensus within one group is a convention, not a verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 7 and 10

- **Statement:** the anchor for a place claim is the outcome return, which does not update on what the frame thinks; agreement inside a closed group cannot replace it. Identity is a frame property and cannot serve as the anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 10 :80 - *"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained."*
- **Refuter:** a practice persists and guides action with no ground return at all, where "who we are" settles empirical disputes and unfavourable outcomes produce no revision; or two frames with fixed-at-origin correlation and no separate experience converge on a place claim without ranging against the place, and that convergence changes the claim's truth-value.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 6 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** "revise" requires a negation term, aimed at the bound term whose residual persists and does not trend down. The record only grows; what falls is the reach. A vocabulary without negation can be corrected in behaviour and cannot carry the correction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :59 - *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"*. Fig 5 :55 - *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.
- **What to check:** does the community in question have an observable form of refusal, or does it only add new practices around a failed one? Is the target of refusal selected by a persistent flat residual, or by authority?
- **Refuter:** show a system in this domain that revises a persistent false term with no negation operator, its library only growing.

### C3 - framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by reading Figure 4 and the tacit-knowledge essay

- **Statement:** a recording of one occasion is not a reapplicable method. What crosses generations is method and head start; experience is rebuilt by each learner through contact. Codifying a practice into a database or textbook carries a round-trip gap that must be measured, not assumed zero.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 - *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **What to check:** take a codified version back to the place. What survives, and what has to be relearned on contact? *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* predicts the latter: *"Lived experience however, does not travel, so each learner rebuilds it through contact."*
- **Refuter:** show a recording of one occasion reapplied at a new place with a zero round-trip gap and no new contact loop.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that consensus is a test, or that a room becomes an anchor by adding people.
- Not that identity settles empirical disputes. It carries method and pretext; a self-updating anchor is coherent drift.
- Not that long shared experience alone makes knowledge reliable. It must be separate experience, renewed against the same ground.
- Not that formal science validates local knowledge. An outside frame adds a surplus only after the residual is described in effect terms, with a source gate and a shadow test.
- Not that a laboratory or synthetic reproduction tests capability. It proves wiring (Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*).
- Not which community or practice is correct. That is a ground question, and no ground reading was taken here.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this packet was settled by an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. Four tests are specified in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4: anchor test, independence test, refusal test, round-trip test. The relevant shape of instrument is the spectrometer in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and several sources can give the same reading. Here, an outside observer reading a practice cannot name its cause from the practice alone.
