## RESIDUAL

The source question bundles three conditions: knowledge tied to **place**, to **identity**, or to **long experience**. We need to know whether these are one object of study or a union of overlapping objects, what order the loops run in, and which analogues are load-bearing rather than decorative. A falsifier is stated at the end.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - One question or a union?

Fig 9 :54: > *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55 to :57: > *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The *Library of Babel* essay says the same from the searcher's side: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

So:

- place -> anchor loop (is the outcome return reachable and non-updating?);
- identity -> frame loop (does membership carry method without settling truth?);
- long experience -> contact and membrane loop (is experience separate and renewed, and does what crosses scales remain a method?).

A single intervention, for example "write the knowledge down," clears the place-only region and leaves the identity and experience intersections standing. That is the signature of a union.

### Q2 - Is "identity" the identity bond ≡? No, and the re-census matters

Fig 12 :65 to :66 defines identity as: > *"two names, one referent"*, symmetric, with the test > *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*

Community identity is **not** that bond. Substituting one community for another loses a history, a pretext and a set of bearings (tacit essay: *"your pretext is the history you bring to the words"*). So it fails the identity test. Re-census: community identity is a **frame property**, not a logical operator and not an anchor. Fig 2 :25 (*"pinned: it does not update on A or B"*) describes what an anchor does; identity updates with the group's history, so it is a frame. Treating it as the anchor is Fig 2's collapse 1, coherent drift.

This re-census dissolves an apparent strain between "identity matters" and "the ground decides." Triangulated (Fig 12 bond test + Fig 2 anchor test), two distinct loci.

### Q3 - What is the order of the loops?

Fig 3 :19: > *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :27: > *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Dependency order from Fig 3 :4 to :16: perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry. Applied here:

1. **Perception.** Can practitioners tell a change at the place from what was there before? If not, there is no R.
2. **Vocabulary.** Do they have terms for what the place affords? Fig 3 :7: > *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*. Without terms, no residual can be bound, so no refusal can be aimed.
3. **Objective.** Can they say what would count as progress here, in their own terms? This is the middle link with no obvious instrument (Fig 3 :24 to :27) and the one most likely to be assumed rather than measured.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Fig 3 :15: > *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"*.

Then the four-part checking arrangement adds its own order: describe the residual first (Fig 9 :27 to :28), keep the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :29), keep frames separate (Fig 2 :31), refuse the flat persistent term (Fig 5 :59), and carry the method across the membrane (Fig 4 :22). Refusal precedes minting (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2): you cannot add a correction until you have refused the term that kept being wrong.

### Q5 - Are local knowledge and formal science isomers?

Fig 12 :117: > *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Take the shared atom set of the checking arrangement: `{anchor, separately-maintained frame, refusal, method-carrying membrane}`. A local guild and an outside research group can be written over the same four atoms, differing in which bond joins them and in what is taken as the anchor's local name (outcome return vs instrument reading). Over that shared set they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance.

As the source question is normally phrased, though, they are written over different atoms (identity and place vs publication and replication), so they share little and are not isomers. The stronger isomer reading is the claim worth testing, and it is a claim about arrangement, not about which one is right.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either system can be written without an atom the other requires (a guild with no outcome anchor, or a research group with no separately maintained ground reading), the isomer claim fails and the weaker "two functional groups" reading stands. If neither can be refused without importing a new operator, the structural claim in `01` F3 fails with it.
