# The Tether's answer: how should managers respond when eradication is impossible and continued suppression has ecological side effects?

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A manager holds an established non-native actor in a maintained habitat. The frame predicts that suppression moves toward a solved state. Observation refuses both premises: the actor cannot be excluded, and the action changes non-target species and relations. R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), not averaged. The two conditions, cannot eradicate and suppression has side effects, overlap, which Fig 9 :54-57 names a union compound, and containment as habitat substitution is a third, categorically different option. More suppression clears only the region where the first condition holds alone.

## 2. The shape

1. **The manager stays in the maintained mode, but the subtractive lever is gone.** Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :32: "→ introduce, never subtract." What remains is introduction and change of contact. Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." So the answer is a maintained contact pattern with monitoring, not an endpoint.

2. **Enumerate the habitat outward before acting.** Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52 explains the side effects directly: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Per slot, never as one score: Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

3. **State the goal as a threshold comparison, not a maximum-effort rule.** Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." The world literature supplies the two quantities for the ⋛: invader density and the level of ecological change counted as unacceptable (Green and Grosholz 2021). That is a provenanced import with a shadow test, not a derivation.

4. **Re-derive valence; do not inherit a good-actor list.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated." Introduction is available as an operation (isomers and functional group, `06_isomers_and_order.md`), but the member still needs provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13).

5. **Treat containment as substitution with two silent failure modes.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."

6. **Do not improve the tool in isolation.** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A better trap, herbicide or model of the same suppression loop is not capability if the contact set is unchanged.

7. **Keep the channel to the ground reachable.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Monitoring is not reporting; it is the instrument that must still resolve the third-order effect.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a species list, a dose, a release site, or a density threshold. The framework returns a shape, not a name; those are ground readings or provenanced imports.
- Not "do nothing" in the sense of leaving the seat. Deliberate non-action with monitoring is a maintained-mode choice; the unmaintained mode is where "nothing chooses" (Fig 11 :31) and selection, not management, decides.
- Not that biological control is safe, and not that any particular actor is a benefactor to this residual.
- Not a solved state. There is no terminal condition after which work and monitoring cease.
- Not that suppression is useless. It may be a component, but in the intersection of the two conditions it is not a sufficient lever on its own.
- Not `grounded`. No local measurement, density-impact curve or cascade map was read for this folder.
- Not a licence to count agreement as evidence. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The Hard Problem is About Source (essay, opened 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; it was the spectrometer that resolved the case. Here the equivalent questions are:

- Which slots are actually live? A per-slot cascade map that distinguishes genuine suppression from redistribution, trophic release, and channel failure. A single density reading cannot do this (F2, F4 in `01`).
- What is the threshold? A local density-impact function and a recolonisation rate, which define the two operands of the ⋛. Green and Grosholz 2021 supplies the method; the local data must supply the values.
- Does the introduced actor explain this gap? A shadow test plus a valence re-derivation for this residual, with provenance (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 11 :49-51).
- Does the substituted habitat reproduce the goal? Test both invisible failure modes explicitly (Fig 11 :68-69).
- Standing falsifiers, written before the run: a manager subtracting the habitat in the maintained mode; first-order side effects fully captured by one global score; a single lever clearing both conditions; containment inheriting nothing; fixed valence across a residual change; an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; a goal with no threshold comparison and still a success criterion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figure gives the form of the answer and refuses the specific names. The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list; nothing here depends on a seventh term. The answer stops where the available instruments stop resolving the cascade, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity. Where local data exists, this shape can be checked in twenty minutes with the packet in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`; where it does not, the shape remains `candidate`.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not name a control agent, a release site, or an acceptable density, and it does not promise a solved state. It says that because the manager cannot subtract the actor from the habitat, the response must work by introduction and change of contact, and that a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a finished job. It says the ecological side effects are what happens when the habitat is scoped to direct contact, so the manager must enumerate actors, conditions and relations outward until the cascade stops mattering, per slot and never as one averaged score. It says the goal has to be stated as a threshold comparison, the way the functional-eradication literature states it, and that "eradication is the goal" is refused rather than subtracted. It says containment substitutes one habitat for another, with failure modes that stay invisible until the goal fails or the introduced actor acts, and that no actor keeps a fixed valence across a change of residual. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific choice stays open until the ground settles it.
