## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the manager's response when the target cannot be excluded and suppression damages non-target actors and relations. Option A: treat it as one hard suppression problem and optimise the existing lever. Option B: unbundle it into a union compound, state the goal as a threshold comparison, enumerate the habitat outward, work by introduction or substitution, and run the result as permanent monitored work. What each leads to: A spends effort above the break, hides the live slot in an average, and leaves the intersection of the two conditions standing; B sends a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and an instrument programme, while leaving the specific species, density and site open.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the frame's prediction that suppression moves toward a solved state and the observation that exclusion is unavailable while suppression changes non-target actors and relations. Arity: two conditions in a union compound, plus a third categorically different option (containment as substitution). Symmetry: contact is asymmetric, introduction available and exclusion not. Scale: first order at the target, second and third order in the side effects. R is indexed per slot, never aggregated.
- **Domain 1, the unmaintained habitat.** Actors: target actor, native species, predators, competitors, mutualists, plus conditions and relations. Currency: survival, what the ground keeps. Anchor: the ground, which does not update; the habitat is not the ground. No seat: nothing chooses, selection ran. This is the mode before management, not the mode the question asks about.
- **Domain 2, the maintained habitat (the garden).** Actors: the manager seat, instruments, introduced actors, the target. Currency: actions and cascade ranking. Anchor: the ground still decides; the ground does not decay, the channel does. A seat exists and acts. Question type: a union compound that must be unbundled before one loop can be run per condition.
- **Domain 3, the verification and contact frame.** Actors: expert ecologists, land managers, peer reviewers. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all frames draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54 to :58): a single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Enumerate the habitat and acquire relational terms (links 1 to 2), then say what counts as progress as a threshold comparison (link 3), then choose the lever (link 4), then name what worked and carry it (link 5). Refuse the eradication goal before minting a new one (formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28).

Strain test applied: Fig 11's instruction to introduce and never subtract strains against a problem defined by an unwanted species only if read at the actor scale. Re-census at the habitat scale resolves it: the unavailable operation is subtraction of the habitat or exclusion of the actor, and the available operations are introduction and contact change. No strain remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the specifics are not supplied. Exclusion is unavailable in the maintained mode, so the manager works by introduction and contact change, and the result is permanent monitored work rather than a solved state. The suppression side effects are cascade effects of a first-order habitat scope, so enumeration must run outward to the cascade horizon per slot. The goal is a threshold comparison, with eradication refused as a negation. Containment substitutes a habitat and carries two silent failure modes, and no actor keeps a fixed valence across a change of residual. No species, density, site or substitute habitat is named by the framework: those are imports or ground readings. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result with F9 marked `gap`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states its falsifiers before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a recipe instead of a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual and the refuter. If the shape itself is wrong, a manager could enumerate the wrong cascade horizon and still miss the third-order effect, which is why the per-slot instrument comes before the lever.
- **undoable:** yes. The specific bindings (which actor, which threshold, which substitute) are removable without touching the figure-derived shape; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A published recommendation can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. The union can be re-split if a new condition appears.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot cascade map distinguishing suppression from redistribution and trophic release would close the measurement gap; a local density-impact function and recolonisation rate would supply the operands of the comparison; a documented case in which a manager subtracted the habitat in the maintained mode would break Fig 11 :61; strictly first-order side effects fully captured by one global score would break Fig 11 :52 and Fig 1 :19; containment inheriting nothing would break Fig 11 :66 to :69; an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would fire the framework's own refuter at Fig 11 :72. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
