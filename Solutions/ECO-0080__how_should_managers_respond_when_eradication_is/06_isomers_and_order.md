# Supporting derivation: the bond form of a management goal, isomeric levers, and dependency order

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit leaves three structural questions open that are not the main verdict but bear on it:

1. Given that exclusion is unavailable, what **form** can the manager's goal take at all?
2. Are "suppress harder", "introduce a control agent" and "substitute a habitat" different instances of one lever, or different objects sharing ingredients?
3. In what **order** must the work be done so that effort is not spent on a link whose input never arrives?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in this derivation: the target actor, non-target actors, conditions, relations, the manager seat, an instrument, a threshold quantity (invader density), a damage quantity. Bonds held: the seven plus negation (¬). No new primitive is introduced. The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43). The dependency order is Fig 3's chain. No claim is made about which species occupies any atom.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What form can the goal take?

Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

Operators table :47-51 defines comparison (⋛) as "a threshold relation between two quantities", with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."

The manager's old goal, "eradicate the invader", is a subtraction (−) of the target actor from the habitat. The audit's F1 establishes that this subtraction is unavailable in the maintained mode. So the manager cannot convert the subtraction into a goal; it can only be refused. The refusal is ¬, not −: "eradication is not the goal" removes the standing of that goal to be composed over, and the record keeps it (formula step 3: "NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED").

What replaces it must be a comparison, because a goal is a comparison and the vocabulary has no other form for one. The two quantities are supplied by the import from ecology: invader density and the level of ecological change counted as unacceptable. This is exactly the structure of Green and Grosholz 2021: "suppressing invader populations below levels that cause unacceptable ecological effects" and "practitioners must define 'unacceptable' change". The figure gives the bond; the world gives the operands.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a management goal can be stated that is neither a threshold comparison nor reducible to one, and the plan still has a success criterion, the bond mapping fails. Also falsified if ⋛ cannot take density and damage as its two quantities at the ecological scale.

### Q2 - Are the three levers isomers or the same lever?

Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

All three responses to "cannot eradicate" can be written with the same ingredient list: {target actor, non-target actors, conditions, relations, manager seat, action}. The ingredient list cannot tell them apart. What differs is the bond:

| lever | dominant bond | what it writes |
|---|---|---|
| continued suppression | − (subtraction, repeated) | target density reduced in the existing habitat; side effects are the unenumerated remainder |
| introduction of a control agent | ⇒ (production) then → (sequence) | a new actor is produced in the habitat, then its cascade is waited out |
| containment or transfer | − on the original site, then substitution of a new habitat | the residual is moved; the new habitat's actors are inherited |

Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The practical consequence is decisive: "suppress harder" and "introduce an agent" are not a dose and a larger dose of the same thing. They are different substances whose ingredient lists look identical in a budget table. A manager who ranks them by cost alone is reading the flat formula and has recorded half of the plan.

The three also form a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124). The substructure "the seat acts on the habitat by changing contact" fixes the kind; the qualifier (which bond, which actor, which substitute) says which member. This matches F1: introduction is available as an operation, but the operation's member still needs provenance, a shadow test and a residual-relative valence (F5).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two responses with identical actor sets produce identical outcomes regardless of the relation between those actors, the isomer claim fails and the flat-form reading stands. If a plan's cost table fully predicts its ecological outcome, the bond carries nothing and Fig 12 does not govern this domain.

### Q3 - In what order must the work be done?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Mapped to this question, Fig 3's five links read:

1. **Perception.** Can the manager tell which actors and conditions are in contact with the residual, and tell them apart from what was there before? This is Fig 11 :44 enumeration. Without it, link 2 has no input.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the manager have terms for what each actor affords: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked? Fig 11 :47 supplies relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency); without them the habitat is a species list, not a web.
3. **The objective.** Can the manager say what would count as progress in its own terms? This is the ⋛ threshold from Q1. It is the middle link, and Fig 3 :25-26 says it is where chains usually break because it has "neither [obvious failures] and the fewest instruments".
4. **Planning.** Choose the lever (suppression, introduction, substitution) under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next site. The channel matters here (Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does").

The common failure is to buy a better lever (link 4) before the objective (link 3) has an instrument. That is effort spent above the break. The world literature corroborates this shape at low overlap: Green and Grosholz 2021 report that more than 90 per cent of surveyed managers faced invaders beyond eradication scale, but only about two per cent had identified targets for when they had done enough. That is a broken link 3 measured in the field.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a manager can show that choosing the lever before defining the objective produced no loss (same ecological outcome at lower cost across cases), the dependency claim fails for this domain.

### Q4 - The analogue that must be refused

Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A model that reproduces the target's population dynamics in a simulation has substituted a habitat. It proves the model's wiring, not that the management plan will work in the field. This is the standard analogue trap for this question: a clean in-silico success is not a capability result. Any simulated output in this folder, including this one, is subject to it.

## CONCLUSION

The manager's goal must be a comparison (⋛) because subtraction is unavailable and a goal has no other bond form. The three apparent levers are isomers under Fig 12, not doses of one thing, and they form a functional group whose member still needs provenance. The work is ordered by dependency: enumerate, acquire relational vocabulary, define the threshold, then plan, then carry; and the middle link is the one most likely to be broken. The substitution analogue must be refused as a capability result. All three claims are `specified`, not `grounded`, and each carries a falsifier written before the run.
