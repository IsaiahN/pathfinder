## 0. The claim under audit

> When the target cannot be excluded and repeated suppression damages non-target actors and relations, the manager should stop treating suppression as the lever, enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering, state the goal as a threshold comparison, select actions by introduction or substitution rather than subtraction, and run the result as permanent monitored work rather than a solved state.

The claim is a shape and a route, not a prescription. It does not name which actor to introduce, which density is "unacceptable", or which habitat to substitute. Those are ground readings and are marked `gap` in F9.

**Residual R in effect terms.** A manager (a seat) holds an established non-native actor in a habitat. The frame predicts that suppression reduces impact toward a solved state. Observation gives two refusals: the actor cannot be removed, and the action changes non-target actors and relations. R is the gap between those. Arity: two conditions in a union compound plus a third, categorically different option (containment as substitution). Symmetry: contact is asymmetric, introduction is available and exclusion is not. Scale: first order at the target, second and third order in the side effects (compare the DDT example, Fig 11 :52). R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), never averaged into one net impact score.

**Order of derivation.** Census first, then figure lines, then verdicts kept separate per domain, then the pre-registered refuter (section 3), then the recommendation (section 4). No figure line was chosen before the census was written.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The manager is in the maintained mode, and the lever set is asymmetric (decisive)

Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."

Fig 11 :31: "→ nothing chooses"

Fig 11 :58: "Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on"

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Fig 11 :32: "→ introduce, never subtract"

Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

The question's premise "eradication is impossible" is the figure's "exclusion is not [available]". The manager has not left the maintained mode; the seat still acts, but the subtractive lever is gone. What remains is introduction and alteration of contact. The honest form of success is therefore permanent work, not a solved state. The line "introduce, never subtract" is read at the scale of the habitat, not as a licence to add any actor: F5 and the three guards limit it.

**Strain test.** Does an imperative about introduction fit a problem defined by an unwanted species? Yes, and only at the habitat scale. The unavailable operation is subtraction of the habitat or exclusion of the actor; the available operation is adding actors or conditions and changing contact. No strain. Triangulation: single-source for the asymmetry rule (Fig 11 only), corroborated at the world level by García-Díaz et al. 2021 (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter.** Show a manager in the maintained mode subtracting the habitat or excluding the established actor with the same authority the figure grants for introduction, or exhibit a solved state after which monitoring and work cease. Either breaks this finding.

### F2 - Suppression side effects are cascade effects at second and third order; first-order enumeration is their cause (decisive)

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

Fig 11 :54: "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

Fig 11 :45-47: "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on" / "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain" / "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The "ecological side effects" in the question are not an accident attached to an otherwise correct method. The figure names the mechanism: the habitat was scoped to direct contact, so the effect three steps out was invisible until it acted. The remedy is enumeration outward through actors, conditions and relations until the cascade stops mattering, per slot. A single net-impact score is the error Fig 1 names.

**Strain test.** The DDT example is an instance of pesticide use, not of invasive-species suppression. Does the rule transfer? Yes: the figure states the rule generally ("contact cascades", "scoping to direct contact is how..."), and the mechanism is identical when a suppression agent drifts, bioaccumulates or releases a mesopredator. No strain at this scale. Triangulation: triangulated, Fig 11 (cascade) with Fig 1 (per-slot residual, no aggregation) at low overlap, one about habitat scope and one about measurement indexing.

**Refuter.** Show that the side effects are strictly first-order and fully captured by one global impact score with no live slot residual, or show that enumeration outward never changed a management decision in a documented case.

### F3 - The question is a union compound, not one lever (decisive for the form of the answer)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

The two stated conditions overlap: the actor cannot be excluded, and suppression has side effects. "Suppress harder" clears only the region where the first condition holds alone; in the intersection the second condition still binds. A third condition, containment as habitat substitution, is categorically different (F4). These need separate loops, not one optimisation. A cost or feasibility score that ranks levers is a filter for the budget and must never be read as the verdict.

**Strain test.** Is this a genuine union or just a hard single problem? The figure's own test is whether each rule works well on a different subset. Feasibility-of-eradication and non-target effect are answered by different instruments (removal technology versus cascade monitoring), so the split is a union. No strain. Triangulation: single-source for the union rule (Fig 9), corroborated by the structure of the world literature (functional-eradication thresholds versus non-target screening are separate literatures).

**Refuter.** Exhibit a single lever whose effect is monotone over both conditions and whose success criterion is the same instrument for both, with no intersection residue.

### F4 - Isolation and containment substitute a habitat; they do not remove one

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"

Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

A common response to "cannot eradicate" is "contain it": fence it, hold it in a refuge, move the affected species elsewhere, or run a surrogate system. The figure says this is a habitat move with two silent failure modes. What you failed to reproduce stays invisible until the goal fails; what you brought with you stays invisible until it acts. The response cannot be evaluated by asking whether the fence works, only by asking whether the substituted habitat still carries the goal.

**Strain test.** Does this govern in-situ containment as well as ex-situ transfer? The line says "isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." A fence around a population substitutes a bounded habitat for an open one, so yes. No strain. Triangulation: single-source (Fig 11). The world source on non-target effects of broad-spectrum tools and fenced areas is corroboration only and shares no framework vocabulary.

**Refuter.** Show a containment action that leaves the habitat unchanged, inherits no unchosen actors, and has no failure mode that is invisible until the goal fails.

### F5 - Valence is relative to the residual; there is no standing good-actor list

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

Fig 6 :12: "source gate: provenance"

Fig 6 :13: "shadow test: explains a gap"

Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

The obvious answer to "exclusion is unavailable" is "introduce a biological control agent". The figure gives no standing permission for that. Introduction is engineering only when the cascade was enumerated; with the cascade unenumerated it is the same move that produced cane toads and kudzu. Any actor imported from ecology (a pathogen, a competitor, a grazing regime) must pass a source gate and a shadow test, and its valence must be re-derived for this residual.

**Strain test.** Does "no standing good-actor list" forbid using a proven biocontrol agent? No. It forbids inheriting its valence from a different residual. A proven agent is a provenanced import whose shadow test must be run again here. No strain. Triangulation: triangulated across Fig 11 (valence) and Fig 6 (import gates) at low overlap, one about ecological relations and one about frame closure.

**Refuter.** Name an actor whose valence is fixed across a change of residual, or import an actor with no provenance and no shadow test and show it still closes the residual.

### F6 - Capability lives in agent-and-habitat, not in a better suppression tool

Fig 11 :39: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

Buying a more precise trap, a more selective herbicide or a better model of the same suppression loop is an improvement that does not change contact if the set of actors and relations in play is unchanged. It changes nothing however much it improves. The manager's job is to keep the channel to the ground reachable (monitoring that actually registers the third-order effect), because the ground does not decay and the channel does.

**Strain test.** Does this forbid incremental improvement? It forbids calling it capability. An incremental improvement that changes which actors are in contact is admitted; one that only refines the same lever is not. No strain. Note the framework's own falsifier: Fig 11 :72, "can still come back wrong: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim." This is recorded as a live refuter, not hidden. Triangulation: triangulated with the metalaws note (WATSON 60, local), which reads Fig 11 the same way.

**Refuter.** Produce an agent-only improvement that moved the ground, or show that the habitat was unchanged while the outcome changed.

### F7 - The objective is a comparison; refusing eradication is a negation, not a subtraction

Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

Operators table :47-51: "comparison" / "a threshold relation between two quantities" / "no" / "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."

Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"

Symbols table: R is "The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it."

The manager's goal cannot be "remove the species": that is a subtraction, and subtraction of the actor is unavailable. The goal must be a comparison between two quantities, for example, invader density and the level at which ecological change becomes unacceptable. That is the ⋛ bond. The plan for reaching it is a sequence (→), progress toward it is a subtraction (− of invader density), and the statement "eradication is not the goal" is a negation (¬), which removes the standing of that goal rather than deleting it from the record. This is why "functional eradication" in the world literature is a real structural match: it defines the goal as suppression below a damage threshold.

**Strain test.** The Operators table restricts ⋛ to two quantities. Density and damage level are two quantities at the ecological scale, so the bond types cleanly. No strain. Triangulation: triangulated, Operators table and Fig 12 at low internal overlap, plus Green and Grosholz 2021 at low field overlap (empirical marine and freshwater ecology). This is the strongest triangulation point in the package.

**Refuter.** State a management goal that is neither a threshold comparison nor reducible to one, and show that the plan still has a success criterion; or show ⋛ cannot take density and damage as its two quantities.

No figure line names the actor to introduce, the unacceptable-density threshold, the recolonisation rate, or the substitute habitat. Those are not withheld by caution; they are not figure-derivable. ## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Seat Map :287. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A manager in the maintained mode can subtract the habitat or exclude the established actor with the authority the figure grants to introduction, or a solved state exists after which monitoring and work cease (breaks F1).
2. Suppression side effects are strictly first-order and fully captured by one global impact score with no live slot residual (breaks F2).
3. A single lever is monotone over both stated conditions with one success instrument and no intersection residue (breaks F3).
4. A containment action leaves the habitat unchanged, inherits no unchosen actors and has no failure mode invisible until the goal fails (breaks F4).
5. An actor's valence is fixed across a change of residual, or an actor imported without provenance or shadow test still closes the residual (breaks F5).
6. An agent-only improvement moves the ground (the framework's own standing refuter, Fig 11 :72).
7. A management goal is stateable with no threshold comparison and the plan still has a success criterion (breaks F7).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the packet is reissued. Until a ground reading exists, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Refuse the frame "eradication is the goal" as a negation (¬), not as a subtraction. Keep it in the record but remove its standing to be composed over (formula step 2; F7).
2. State the objective as a threshold comparison (⋛): define the ecological change that counts as unacceptable and the density at which it occurs. The world literature's functional-eradication framework supplies the instrument; record it as an import with source gate and shadow test (F7, F5).
3. Enumerate the habitat outward through actors, conditions and relations until the cascade stops mattering, per slot, never as one averaged score. Put instruments on the third-order effects before choosing a lever (F2, Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").
4. Rank candidate actions by cascade, and re-derive each actor's valence for this residual. Introduce only with provenance and a shadow test (F5).
5. Treat containment as substitution with two silent failure modes, and require the substituted habitat to reproduce the goal (F4).
6. Accept permanence. The output is a maintained contact pattern with monitoring, not a solved state (F1, F6).
7. Verify by refutation from independent frames, not by agreement. Send the residual and the refuter first (F8).

## 6. Retractions and confirmations

This is the first pass for this question, so there are no earlier readings to retract. Two items are recorded as corrections of emphasis rather than retractions, so they stay visible:

1. **Correction of emphasis on F1.** A first reading of "introduce, never subtract" could be taken as a blanket endorsement of releasing any control agent. F5 narrows it: introduction is available as an operation, but each actor needs provenance, a shadow test and a residual-relative valence. The narrower form is the one carried into `07` and `08`.
2. **Correction of emphasis on F7.** A first reading could map "functional eradication" directly onto the figure's ⋛ and call it a derivation. It is not. The figure supplies the form of the goal (a comparison); the empirical literature supplies the quantities. That is a provenanced import, and the record says so.
3. **No figure quote was taken from a summary.** Every `Fig N :line` string used here is from `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and was re-read for `11_audit.md`.
4. **No ground reading was taken.** Nothing in this folder is promoted from `candidate` to `accepted`. Mode remains `specified`, with F9 marked `gap`.
