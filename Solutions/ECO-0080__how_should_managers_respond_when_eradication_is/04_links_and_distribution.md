## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Functional eradication (Green and Grosholz 2021).** This is the closest structural match. It redefines the objective as suppressing invader density below the level that causes unacceptable ecological effects in high-priority locations, and it says practitioners must first define "unacceptable" change and quantify both density-impact and density-removal relationships. In figure terms, this supplies the two quantities for the ⋛ comparison (F7) and the instrument for the threshold. It is a provenanced import with a shadow test, not a figure derivation.
2. **Non-target and cascade effects (Pearson, Clark and Hahn 2022).** Unintended outcomes were reported in 36% of cases reviewed, most arising from simple direct or density-mediated effects, and studies that assessed more interaction types reported more unintended outcomes. Their community assessment framework "systematically identifies and follows out strong linkages until they become weak", which is an independent ecological wording of Fig 11 :44 ("outward until the cascade stops mattering"). Low overlap with the figures: different authors, field, method and vocabulary.
3. **Long-term management and the "do nothing" option (García-Díaz et al. 2021).** Eradication of many species is unfeasible, so long-term plans are needed, and high-level actions include "do nothing, monitor, research, and immediate active management". This corroborates the figure's permanence claim and its refusal of a single lever. Note the honesty constraint: "do nothing" in the figure would be the unmaintained mode, where selection runs and no seat acts. A manager choosing "do nothing" as a decision still occupies the seat, so it is a maintained-mode choice with monitoring, not a retreat to pure contact.
4. **Ecosystem engineering and introduction (Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994).** The figure cites this paper directly at Fig 11 :65. Its central claim that organisms modify, maintain and create habitats through physical state changes is the ecological basis for "introduction is available". Because the figure already cites it, its agreement is high-overlap and carries little independent weight.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. The closest thing to a tension is terminological: the world literature says "functional eradication" where the figure says "exclusion is not available". Re-census resolves it: the world term names a threshold comparison, not the subtraction of the actor. Different objects, so no strain.
