## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 :29-30 (anchor test), Fig 9 :54-58 (union), Fig 10 :24-29 (channel decay) and :42 (non-access), Fig 11 :39-40 and :66-70 (habitat substitution).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: private ground, per-slot residuals, independent instruments, verifier without access, refresh or retire, provenance.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete evaluation policy, for example a canaried held-out set, a multi-metric report, a perturbation suite and a deployment outcome.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down unless the evaluator supplies them:

- the **union structure**, which a single "use a private set" policy collapses into one condition;
- the **verifier non-access condition**, which a private set published after scoring violates;
- the **channel-decay distinction**, which a saturated score reported as "capability reached" violates;
- the **habitat substitution law**, which a synthetic solve reported as capability violates.

One thing survives cleanly: a metric that moves when the system is optimized against it is not a ground. That is the residue worth sending first. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. `09_watson_answer.md` now states this.
