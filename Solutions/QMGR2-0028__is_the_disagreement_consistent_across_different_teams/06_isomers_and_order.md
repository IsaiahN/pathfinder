## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the residual a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :54-58: a bundled problem is "usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. What remains is "categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Here the conditions are: (a) shared anchor lineage, (b) shared software or photometry, (c) shared SN sample and selection, (d) a coherent systematic acting across routes. Removing one does not remove a case where another still binds.
- ⇒ The question "is the disagreement consistent?" is a **union**, not a list. A yes or no over the whole union is a category error. The operational meaning of "unbundle" is: for each condition, ask whether the case survives when that condition is removed, and keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if all four conditions reduce to one measurable overlap parameter, the union collapses to a partition and unbundling is not owed.

### Q2 - What is the order of operations?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Ordered chain for this question:
  1. perception: do two instruments return different readings? (link 1)
  2. vocabulary: do we have terms for shared evidence, covariance, and closure? (link 2)
  3. objective: what counts as a persistent residual here? (link 3)
  4. planning: design a comparison between frames whose closures differ (link 4)
  5. learn and carry: name what worked and range it against the ground (link 5)
- The break most likely to matter is between 2 and 3, exactly Fig 3 :24-27: having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress has neither obvious failures nor obvious instruments, so it attracts the least attention. The overlap audit is that missing instrument.
- Fig 2 :31-32 puts independence before persistence; Formula step 2 puts refusal before mint when a term persists flat. So the licensed order is: **independence, then persistence, then refusal or mint, then ground**. Running a mint before an independence check is effort above the break.
- **Falsifier:** a controlled comparison in which a persistence verdict changes after an overlap audit would show the order was reversed and the earlier verdict was spent on a step whose input had not arrived.

### Q3 - Are the two routes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- SH0ES and CCHP, as described in Riess et al. 2024, share a large part of the atom set: the same sky, overlapping host galaxies, the same anchor NGC 4258, the same photometry package DOLPHOT, and SNe Ia. They differ in which indicators are emphasised (Cepheids versus TRGB and JAGB), which hosts are selected, which filters and epochs are used, and which statistic defines the edge of a luminosity function.
- On Fig 12's definition they are **isomers over a shared atom set**: same ingredients, different arrangement (bond), different substance (H0 estimate). The formula that lists only the ingredients, Cepheids plus NGC 4258 plus SNe Ia, cannot tell them apart; the arrangement carries the difference. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- The weaker reading is that they share a **functional group**: a distance ladder with a geometric anchor, a stellar indicator, and a Hubble-flow rung. The group fixes what kind of thing it is; the qualifier (which indicator, which selection) says which member, and therefore which H0.
- This is a structural analogue, not a physics claim. It explains why two teams can be simultaneously faithful to the same ladder and return different numbers without either being in a different closure. It does not say which number is right.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two pipelines can be written with disjoint atom sets (no shared anchor, no shared hosts, no shared software, no shared systematics), the isomer claim fails and they are genuinely two frames, which is the condition F1 in `01` requires before agreement or disagreement can count.

### Q4 - What relation joins the two estimates?

- Operators table: ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Fig 12 :86 "a threshold relation between two quantities." The test is whether it is about which is larger, not about both being present.
- The two H0 values are therefore **compared**, not pooled. Adding them as if they were independent measurements of one quantity is the flat-formula collapse of Fig 12 :13-16: the ingredients are recorded, the arrangement is not, and nothing downstream can tell what was lost.
- Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." A covariance audit is the record that distinguishes two independently derived values from one value counted twice under two names.
- **Strain, named not hidden:** at the analysis scale the operands are numbers with error bars, so ⋛ is well typed. At the meta scale a "comparison" between two whole pipelines is a comparison between methods, not between quantities. Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom at the next level, but whether a pipeline-now-atom counts as a quantity for ⋛ needs an instrument (an explicit covariance definition), not an argument. Recorded as a strain.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a **union of overlapping conditions**, so unbundling precedes search. The licensed order is independence, persistence, refusal or mint, ground (Fig 2 :31-32; Formula step 2; Fig 3 :20-21). SH0ES and CCHP are **isomers over a shared atom set**, or at minimum members of one functional group, which is why different arrangements of the same ingredients can yield different H0 values without either team being wrong in its own frame. The two values are compared with ⋛, never pooled. The isomer reading rescues the intuition that team differences matter while refusing the unsupported step that a team label constitutes independence. The strain at the meta scale is named.
