## The question restated as an effect

The observed gap between early-universe inference of the expansion rate (Planck plus LCDM, about 67.4 km/s/Mpc) and the local distance ladder (SH0ES, about 73.0 km/s/Mpc) is roughly 5 sigma. The question asks whether that gap is *consistent*: does it survive when the measurement is repeated by different teams and through different analysis pipelines, or does it dissolve under re-analysis?

- Answer A, "yes, it is consistent": treat the residual as stable across independent frames and therefore owed a term (a coherent systematic or new physics).
- Answer B, "no, it is pipeline-dependent": treat the residual as an artifact of a shared evidence pool, where teams and pipelines that appear separate draw on the same data, anchors, samples, software, or assumptions.

What an answer would look like: a per-domain verdict on whether the disagreement persists across genuinely independent frames and does not trend down, with the conditions for a real disagreement stated before the verdict. The framework's own discipline requires the sought-for shape to be written as an ordinary term before the run, so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula step "Direction").

## Verdict in one line

The framework does not return a bare yes or no. It returns a shape: **independence comes first, then persistence, then the ground**. A disagreement counted across pipelines that share an evidence pool is the pool talking, not two witnesses. A residual that persists and does not trend down across frames whose closures differ is owed a term, and only a ground reading settles what that term is. The verdict is `specified` and open.
