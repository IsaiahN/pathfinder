# Outreach draft: check the two quantities before you decide about the claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are written down in the recipient's own terms. A stage-1 offer without a refuter is not an offer; it is a request for agreement.

---

**Subject:** Two quantities that AI assistance moves in opposite directions, and what would refute the claim

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a person works with AI assistance, two quantities move and they need not move together.
>
> 1. **Aided accuracy:** the combined person-plus-AI system's error against the ground on this task.
> 2. **Own capability:** the person's unaided error on held-out tasks after the aid is withdrawn, and whether they can still produce a new judgment rather than reproduce a recorded one.
>
> The claim is that these are two quantities, not one. A tool can improve the first while reducing the second. The framework's own wording is that the residual is indexed by task slot, not averaged, and that a system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation, because capability is a property of the person and the habitat together.
>
> **The shape claim:** assistance improves judgment without weakening own skill when it changes the person's contact with the ground and leaves a method they can reapply; it weakens own skill when it substitutes the person's task habitat with a recording to be replayed, because what was failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails. For questions constitutively about people, human judgment is the anchor and AI is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. That is a shape and a route, not a name of one best tool.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named product is good or bad, that users' confidence is evidence, that a survey showing people feel more productive settles anything, or that this derivation is `grounded`. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. Unaided capability after long answer-only assistance is indistinguishable from a no-assistance control on held-out tasks while aided accuracy improved.
> 2. An aid that supplies only a finished answer, no method and no reapplication, still improves unaided performance on genuinely new tasks.
> 3. For a person-level question, an AI instrument accurate on its measured axis but wrong on an unmeasured axis settles the question as well as human judgment.
> 4. Aided accuracy and unaided capability move in the same direction on every measured slot, with no trade-off anywhere.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula (public repo, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The worked human-AI meta-analysis: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1
> - Human-AI feedback loops and judgment bias: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02077-2
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah

## The two-stage offer, spelled out

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual (two quantities), the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the four links above. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because Fig 2 :40 says agreement from a shared evidence pool is worthless as a verdict.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

The order is structural, not polite. Once a recipient has absorbed the framework's interiors, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*). One-to-one only. No bulk send.
