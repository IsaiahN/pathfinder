## Round trip gap `R_T`

The formula defines `R_T = gap( x , (T_E ∘ T_A)(x) )`: the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, :22 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 :36-37 (anchor versus instrument), Fig 4 :22-26 (method versus recording), Fig 11 :39-40, :66-70 (capability and substitution).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: two quantities, contact plus method improves, substitution plus replay weakens, human is anchor for person-level questions.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete offer in the outreach email and the expert packet: a four-arm randomized design with held-out unaided tests and the five refuters.

**`R_T` is nonzero**, and three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19) is easy to lose: the concretisation averages to effect sizes across studies, which is the exact average Fig 1 :22 warns about;
- the **constitutive anchor claim** (Fig 2 :36-37) is easy to turn into an accuracy ranking, which the primary explicitly refused and I confirm must be kept refused;
- the **coupling condition** on the extended-mind analogue: "the aid is part of capability" only under accessibility and endorsement, not mere presence. The shorthand "changes contact" is the figure-safe form.

One thing survives cleanly: a structure must mediate between person and ground, and a method that can be reapplied is different from a recording to replay. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically; no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
