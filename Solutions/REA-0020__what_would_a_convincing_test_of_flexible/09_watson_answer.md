## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a convincing test of flexible out-of-distribution learning must look like. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is indexed per environment slot. Option A: a convincing test is a larger, harder held-out set drawn from the same generator, so generalization is a property of the model considered alone. Option B: a convincing test enumerates the deployment habitat, uses a ground that does not move, requires contact with a closure differing from training, reports per-slot residual, uses an unauthored and independent pool, and reports failure as a verdict that separates depth from absence, so generalization is a property of learner-and-habitat. What each leads to: A produces a frame-internal score that can be improved by selecting on the same instrument, and it substitutes a habitat without recording the substitution; B produces a checkable shape with written refuters and a recorded lineage, and it stops where instruments can no longer discriminate.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a learner predicts and what a changed environment returns, indexed by slot s and ordered by time. Arity: four-place (learner, action, environment, slot), so a sequence with an ordered bond and a comparison bond for "flexible". Symmetry: not symmetric, learner and environment are not interchangeable, and "out of distribution" is relative to the training closure. Scale: one learner loop, a population of environments, and the channel between training and deployment. Fig 9 names a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the learner loop.** Actors: the learner, its Γ, its training history. Currency: actions and residual reduction on training slots. Anchor: the verifiable reward or measurement in the training environment. This is a partition boundary question (which compositions cross the learner's closure).
- **Domain 2, the deployment habitat.** Actors: hospitals, camera traps, seasons, locations, users, novel games. Currency: survival of low R under shift. Anchor: the real outcome, a ground named in Fig 2 :29. Question type: a habitat enumerated, never composed.
- **Domain 3, the test instrument.** Actors: held-out sets, synthetic corruptions, leaderboards, metrics. Currency: a score. Anchor: grounded only when test conditions remain in contact with Domain 2, otherwise frame-internal (Fig 1 :27). Question type: the channel, which decays while the ground does not.
- **Domain 4, invariance and reach.** Actors: atoms (features, invariances) and bonds. Currency: reach and cost of stating a term. Anchor: settlement against Domain 2. Question type: closure and import.
- **Excluded:** "general capability as a free-floating property of the model" has no anchor independent of the habitat, so it is not a domain yet. "Robustness to synthetic corruption as the whole of OOD" is measurable but is absorbed into Domain 3.
- **Order by dependency:** describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle the anchor before the instrument (Fig 2 :29-30); run SEARCHED before INWARD and OUTWARD (Formula step 7); settle each first-order claim before composing across scales (Fig 12 :104).

That is a strain, so Fig 13 governs the honest stop here, not the learner's Γ. Re-census in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 records it. No unresolved strain remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives. A convincing test of flexible OOD learning enumerates its conditions from the deployment habitat before choosing a benchmark, uses a real outcome that does not move as anchor, requires contact with a closure differing from training, reports residual per environment, draws an unauthored and independent test pool, reports failure as a verdict separating depth from absence, and measures the round-trip gap to the deployment ground. A synthetic solve proves wiring, never capability. Option A is refused: a harder held-out set from the same generator is an instrument, not a ground, and a frame-internal score cannot settle a frame-internal question. Option B is `specified`, ungrounded and a candidate. Which particular shift will break a given system is a gap, not a silence; the framework returns a shape, and the instrument that would name the source is not held here.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims, verbatim lines and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a theory before a criterion, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The refused Option A is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A synthetic suite that prospectively predicts real deployment residual across independent shifts, a model-only change that moves the ground with contact unchanged, a method that expands reach purely by in-closure search and still passes a fresh real-shift test, or an experiment that measures depth versus absence in one score, would each break the corresponding claim. A derivation of one of Fig 13's terms from the other five, or a failure of Fig 8 :32 idempotence, would break the figures themselves. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
