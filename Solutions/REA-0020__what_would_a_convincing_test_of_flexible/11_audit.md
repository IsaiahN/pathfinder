## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between what a learner predicts and what a changed environment returns. Arity: four-place (learner, action, environment, slot), indexed by time, so an ordered sequence with a comparison bond for "flexible". Symmetry: not symmetric, learner and environment are not interchangeable; "out of distribution" is relative to a closure. Scale: learner loop, population of environments, and the lossy channel between them. Fig 9 names a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, the learner loop.** Actors: learner, Γ, training history. Currency: residual reduction. Anchor: the verifiable reward in the training environment. Question type: closure boundary.
- **Domain 2, the deployment habitat.** Actors: hospitals, camera traps, seasons, locations, games. Currency: survival of low R under shift. Anchor: the real outcome, the ground named in Fig 2 :29. Question type: enumerated habitat.
- **Domain 3, the test instrument.** Actors: held-out sets, corruptions, leaderboards. Currency: a score. Anchor: only when in contact with Domain 2, otherwise frame-internal (Fig 1 :27). Question type: channel maintenance.

- **Excluded:** model-only "general capability" (no anchor) and synthetic robustness as a whole domain (absorbed into Domain 3).

Order by dependency: describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); fix the anchor before the instrument (Fig 2 :29-30); SEARCHED before INWARD and OUTWARD (Formula step 7); settle first-order molecules before second-order compositions (Fig 12 :104).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x)), measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :68-70 (substituted habitat), Fig 6 :21-23 (import moves the wall), Fig 9 :27-28 (describe before looking) and Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: enumerate the habitat, use a movable anchor, require a differing closure, report per environment, separate depth from absence.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete test proposal, for example "evaluate on a fresh hospital, a new camera trap and a novel game, report each environment separately, and record which method imported an atom".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- which conditions count as in contact with the residual (Fig 11 :52 warns that contact cascades three steps out, but the shorthand just says "enumerate");
- what would discriminate depth from absence at the ground (Fig 6 :27 says they look the same from inside, and the shorthand names a discriminator without specifying one);
- how independence is to be bought (Fig 2 :42 prices it, but the shorthand says "independent pools" without an evidence-pool audit).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability, and per-slot reporting beats a mean. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no deployment ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
