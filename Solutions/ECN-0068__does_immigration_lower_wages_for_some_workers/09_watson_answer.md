## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the three candidate reads of an immigration inflow: lower wages for some workers, higher overall productivity, or different effects by skill group and horizon. Option A: treat the three as competing estimates of one quantity and report the winning sign. Option B: treat them as a union compound with a slot-indexed residual, keep the estimands and scales separate, and leave the signs to the ground. What each leads to: A publishes an averaged headline that can hide a live slot and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B publishes a checkable shape, four refuters written in advance, and an honest `gap` where the figures stop.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The mismatch between a one-sign headline and the measured wage outcomes on individual slots. Arity: at least four indices, education-experience cell, region, wage percentile, horizon. Symmetry: asymmetric, because a supply shock into a cell lowers the wage of competing workers and can raise the wage of complementary workers; swapping the worker kinds does not preserve the claim. Scale: local versus national, and short run (capital quasi-fixed) versus long run (capital adjusted). Fig 9 :55 makes it a union, not a list.
- **Domain 1, the labour market as habitat.** Actors: workers by education-experience cell, firms, immigrants, natives, capital, wage-setting institutions. Currency: wages, employment, hours, total factor productivity, output. Anchor: measured census, labour force and firm data that do not move when a frame changes its mind. Question type: union compound, because skill, region, percentile and horizon overlap.
- **Domain 2, the empirical research programme.** Actors: competing research teams, natural experiments, instrument designs, meta-analysts. Currency: estimates and identification checks. Anchor: reproducible data and the identification strategy. Question type: union, because the relative-wage estimand and the total-wage estimand answer different questions (JEP 2016).
- **Domain 3, verification and distribution.** Actors: expert readers, referees, editors, first-contact recipients. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the measured ground of domain 1, never agreement. Question type: partition; this domain can check a claim but cannot author the result.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the slot-indexed residual before choosing a frame; settle the estimand and the anchor before reading a sign; read slots before aggregates; measure the round trip gap; choose the frame last.

Strain test applied: Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is" was first tempted toward the horizon question. Re-census assigns it to the six-term substrate, where it belongs, and sends the horizon claim to Fig 4 and Fig 9. Two strains were named and resolved in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5; none hidden.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. They never overrule a figure. Overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Labour economics, competing-workers frame.** George J. Borjas, "The Labor Demand Curve is Downward Sloping: Reexamining the Impact of Immigration on the Labor Market", NBER Working Paper 9755 / QJE 118(4), 2003: "immigration lowers the wage of competing workers: a 10 percent increase in supply reduces wages by 3 to 4 percent." Predicts a negative sign in cells most similar to the inflow. Corroborates the slot and valence shape; conflicts with any universal-positive reading. Overlap with other sources: medium, shares the skill-cell framework with Ottaviano and Peri but uses a different identification.
- **Labour economics, complementarity frame.** Gianmarco I. P. Ottaviano and Giovanni Peri, "Rethinking the Effects of Immigration on Wages", NBER Working Paper 12497, 2006, revised 2008: "This positive effect results from averaging a positive effect on wages of U.S.-born workers with at least a high school degree and a small negative effect on wages of U.S.-born workers with no high school degree." Predicts opposite signs in different cells and a horizon dependence. Corroborates F2 and F4. Overlap: high with the JEEA 2012 journal version (same authors and design), so those two count as one witness.
- **Labour economics, journal version.** Gianmarco I. P. Ottaviano and Giovanni Peri, Journal of the European Economic Association 10(1), 2012: "we show that most of the wage effects of immigration accrue to native workers already within a decade." Predicts that the horizon index matters. Corroborates F4. Overlap: high with the NBER version above.
- **Labour economics, synthesis.** David Card, "Immigration and Inequality", NBER Working Paper 14683 / AER 99(2), 2009: "the impacts of recent immigrant inflows on the relative wages of U.S. natives are small." Predicts small relative effects, leaving distributional detail to other designs. Corroborates the separation of estimands. Overlap: medium, shares data era with Borjas.
- **Labour economics, productivity frame.** Giovanni Peri, "The Effect of Immigration on Productivity: Evidence from US States", NBER Working Paper 15507 / Review of Economics and Statistics 94(1), 2009/2012: "an increase in employment in a US state of 1% due to immigrants produced an increase in income per worker of 0.5% in that state." Predicts a positive aggregate productivity read alongside mixed wage reads. Corroborates the compatibility of F2. Overlap: low with the wage-cell studies, different dependent variable and instruments.
- **Research synthesis, method.** Christian Dustmann, Uta Schonberg and Jan Stuhler, "The Impact of Immigration: Why Do Studies Reach Such Different Results?", Journal of Economic Perspectives 30(4), 2016: "studies in the first two groups estimate different relative effects, and studies in the third group estimate the total effect of immigration on wages", and they "are not comparable". Predicts that the disagreement is an estimand split. Corroborates F1 and C2 strongly. Overlap: low, a taxonomy rather than a new estimate.
- **Research synthesis, meta-analysis.** Mette Foged, Linea Hasager and Vasil Yasenov, "The role of labor market institutions in the impact of immigration on wages and employment", Scandinavian Journal of Economics 124(1), 2022: "the mean and median effects on the relative wage are negative and significantly different from the small positive mean and median effects on the average wage." Predicts the joint aggregate-plus-loss state directly. Corroborates F2. Overlap: high with the underlying literature it pools, so it is one witness with many rows, not many witnesses.
- **Research synthesis, review.** Anthony Edo, "The Impact Of Immigration On The Labor Market", Journal of Economic Surveys 33(3), 2019: "because adjustments take time, the immediate labor market effects of unexpected (as opposed to expected) migration episodes can be detrimental." Also: "immigration can create winners and losers among native workers via changes in the wage structure." Predicts horizon dependence and slot dependence. Corroborates F3 and F4. Overlap: medium, a review of the same literature.
- **Research, later period.** Alessandro Caiumi and Giovanni Peri, "Immigration's Effect on US Wages and Employment Redux", NBER Working Paper 32389, 2024: "a positive and significant effect between +1.7 to +2.6% on wages of less educated native workers" over 2000-2019. Conflicts with the sign reported by Borjas for the earlier period. A conflict sends us back to the census: the two are different periods and different skill-mix conditions, so they are different slots, not one contradiction. Overlap: high with Ottaviano and Peri's structural family.
- **Cross-field statistics and philosophy of science.** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Simpson's Paradox", first published 2021, substantive revision 2026: "an association between two variables in a population emerges, disappears or reverses when the population is divided into subpopulations." Predicts exactly the aggregate-versus-cell reversal the figures demand we look for. Corroborates F2 from a field with low overlap with labour economics.
- **Metatheory, canonical usage.** Isaiah N, figures README, `general-agent` branch (local canonical copy read 2026-11-23): "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and "Indiscernibility is not identity." Predicts the honest stop; corroborates the `gap` verdict. Overlap: high, same author as the figures.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict on the shape. The sign conflict between Borjas (2003) and Caiumi and Peri (2024) is a conflict between world sources, and the conflict rule sends us back to the domain census rather than flipping the figure verdict: the two characterize different periods and skill mixes, which are different slots. All world sources are secondary; their agreement with the figures is not a ground reading.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures decide the shape, not the empirical sign. Any admissible answer must be slot-indexed, must unbundle the union before searching, must keep relative and total estimands apart, must treat scale changes as lossy with a measured R_T, and must name the anchor that would settle each slot. No figure line governs whether a given low-skill cell loses or gains, so the sign is a `gap` from the figures and a `specified` candidate from the world literature. This is a `general` structural result plus a `specified` reading of the literature, ungrounded and candidate. The four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 are live.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the slot-indexed shape is wrong, a single aggregate statistic will have covered every slot, and the error will be invisible to anyone reading only the headline. The framework prices this: Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable part is spending a reader's independence by sending the full frame before their verdict, which Fig 10 :42 names.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape claim is a constraint, not a measurement; a refuter can remove its standing without withdrawing any data. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already strip out every sign claim. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Cell-level exogenous variation reported separately from a general-equilibrium total effect; an instrument that discriminates a lower-tail loss from an upper-tail gain from a composition shift; the same cell before and after capital and occupation adjustment with R_T reported; or any of the four pre-registered falsifiers firing. Until one of these, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
