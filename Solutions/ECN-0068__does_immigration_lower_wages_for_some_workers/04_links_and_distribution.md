## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on the shape

1. **The estimand split is not a disagreement about facts.** Dustmann, Schonberg and Stuhler (2016) show that relative-effect and total-effect studies answer different questions. Foged, Hasager and Yasenov (2022) then measure both across the same literature and find opposite signs in the mean. That is the project's F1 and F2 from a second, non-framework direction: the bundle must be split, and the aggregate must not be used to erase the slot.
2. **The sign moves with the period.** Borjas (2003) finds negative effects on competing workers in 1960-2000 data; Caiumi and Peri (2024), using the same structural family on 2000-2019 data, find positive effects for less educated natives because the immigrant skill mix changed. This is the horizon and composition caveat. It is a reason to keep the time index, not a reason to average the two into "the answer".

Neither finding is a figure line. Both are world corroboration of the shape, and they are recorded as such in the WATSON answer.
