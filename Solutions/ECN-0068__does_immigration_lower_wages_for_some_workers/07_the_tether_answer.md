# The Tether's answer on immigration and wages

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the checkable pattern in the literature. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken here.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles three reads: a loss for some workers, a gain in aggregate productivity, and a pattern that changes across skill groups and horizons. In effect terms the residual is the mismatch between a one-sign headline and the measured outcomes on individual slots, where a slot is a cell of (skill group, region, wage percentile, horizon). The framework's answer is about the *form* that any settled answer must take, not about the sign.

## 2. The shape

1. **Read R per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A near-zero aggregate with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). So a headline gain in productivity and a loss in one cell are compatible readings, not a contradiction.
2. **Unbundle before searching.** Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Skill, region, percentile and horizon overlap; a single lever leaves the intersections standing.
3. **Keep the estimands apart.** Relative and total wage effects are different terms. As written they are members of a functional group (Fig 12 :124), not isomers (Fig 12 :117), because they do not share the same atom set. A full shared-atom restatement would make them a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1).
4. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." An inflow competes with one cell and complements another. Borjas (2003) and Ottaviano and Peri (2006) can both be reading the same actor against different residuals.
5. **Horizons are different terms.** A scale change carries a round trip gap. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Short-run losses with fixed capital and long-run neutrality or gain are not one estimate viewed twice.
6. **The ground decides the sign.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 13 :138: "narrowing gets you close while the ground decides." After a genuine re-census, no figure line returns a wage sign. The empirical sign is a `gap` from the figures and a candidate from measured data.

## 3. What the world literature supplies, and what it does not

These are secondary candidates, not figure verdicts:

- **Competition reading:** Borjas (QJE 2003) finds a 10 percent supply increase reduces wages of competing workers by 3 to 4 percent in education-experience cells.
- **Complementarity reading:** Ottaviano and Peri (NBER w12497 2006, JEEA 2012) find a positive average with a small negative effect for workers with no high school degree, and that most effects accrue within a decade.
- **Distribution reading:** Dustmann, Frattini and Preston (REStud 2013) find wage depression below the 20th percentile and slight increases above the 40th.
- **Meta reading:** Foged, Hasager and Yasenov (SJE 2022) find negative mean and median relative-wage effects against small positive average-wage effects across 1,030 estimates.
- **Productivity reading:** Peri (NBER w15507 2009) finds a 1 percent immigration-driven employment increase associated with a 0.4 to 0.5 percent income-per-worker increase; Caiumi and Peri (2024) find positive wage effects for less educated natives in 2000-2019.
- **Horizon reading:** Edo (JES 2019) notes that immediate effects of unexpected episodes can be detrimental because adjustments take time.

These disagree in sign, period and estimand. The disagreement is the phenomenon the shape predicts, not noise to be averaged away. Agreement among them is not a second witness when they draw on the same evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that immigration lowers wages in general, and not that it always raises them.
- Not that any particular skill group gains or loses by a stated magnitude. That is data work.
- Not a derivation of a wage effect from the six-term substrate. No such line exists in the figures.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here, and no ground reading of a wage dataset.
- Not a claim that the meta-analysis is an independent instrument. It pools the studies it summarises.
- Not a moral verdict on immigration. Fig 11 valence is relative to the residual, not a standing property of people.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A settled answer needs, per slot: the estimand named, the anchor named, and an instrument that would move the reading.

1. **Cell-level identification:** a design in which the inflow into an education-experience cell is plausibly exogenous (natural experiment or valid instrument), reported separately from the general-equilibrium total effect.
2. **Distribution-level instrument:** a source-discriminating reading that says whether the average moved because the lower tail lost, because the upper tail gained, or because the composition of workers changed.
3. **Horizon test:** the same cell measured before and after capital and occupation adjustment, with the round trip gap R_T reported rather than assumed zero.
4. **Standing falsifiers:** finding 1-4 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3. If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures can narrow the answer to a slot-indexed, unbundled, ground-settled form. They cannot fill the slots. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The line between a question that is underivable in principle and one that is merely hard at this budget matters: here the data exist, so the work is INWARD (sharper cell-level instruments) and OUTWARD (a frame with a different closure, for example a firm-level design rather than a cell-level one), run as two tracks and never read against each other (Formula step 7). Indiscernibility is not identity; the stop is recorded so a later reader can check it.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell us whether immigration lowers wages or raises productivity, and it does not average the two. It says the question is bundled: the residual lives in slots (skill cell, region, wage percentile, horizon), so a near-zero national average with a live loss in one cell is a legal state. It says relative and total wage effects are different estimands and must not be merged, that an inflow competes with one cell and complements another, and that short-run and long-run readings are separated by a round trip gap that has to be measured. The figures give the shape any settled answer must have and four refuters written in advance. The signs are owed to data, and until a source-discriminating instrument settles each slot, the answer stops there honestly rather than silently.
