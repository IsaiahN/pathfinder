# Expert verification packet - the Tether on immigration and wages

**What this is:** a claim in a form an immigration economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the checkable pattern reported in the literature. Not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken here, and no empirical wage sign is asserted by the framework.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The immigration-wage question as usually posed is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question. The residual is indexed by slot: skill cell, region, wage percentile and horizon. A near-zero aggregate reading with one live slot is a legal state, so "overall productivity rose" and "a group of workers lost" are compatible readings. Relative and total wage effects are different estimands, and short-run and long-run readings are separated by a round trip gap that must be measured. An inflow competes with one cell and complements another; its valence is relative to the residual. The figures supply this shape and its refuters. They supply no wage sign. The signs are owed to data and to a source-discriminating instrument per slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot, and the headline question is a union compound that must be unbundled before searching. No single sign is expected to cover all cells and horizons.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13 - "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Fig 9 :55 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **World corroboration (secondary only):** Foged, Hasager and Yasenov (SJE 2022) report negative mean and median relative-wage effects alongside small positive average-wage effects across 1,030 estimates. Dustmann, Schonberg and Stuhler (JEP 2016) show that relative and total effect studies are not comparable.
- **Refuter:** one aggregate statistic fully predicts every slot's wage trajectory with no live slot residual, or the sign and magnitude in the intersection are exactly the sum of the single-condition effects.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against the estimand literature

- **Statement:** relative and total wage effects are different terms. As currently written they share a subtraction form but not the full atom set, so under Fig 12 they are members of a functional group rather than isomers. A full shared-atom restatement makes them a genuine isomer claim.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124 - "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :125 - "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- **What to check:** is the distinction in JEP 2016 a difference of estimand rather than a disagreement about facts? Does any accepted identification strategy make relative and total effects the same quantity? If yes, C2 fails.
- **Refuter:** either estimand can be written without an atom the other requires, even after the full-set restatement; or a single strategy identifies both as the same object under every accepted assumption.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a world-facing consequence

- **Statement:** the scale change from a cell result to a headline, and from a short-run to a long-run reading, is lossy. The gap R_T must be measured, not assumed zero. The anchor is measured data, never agreement among researchers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 - "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 4 :31 - "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up." Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :31 - "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
- **What to check:** name a reading that would move one cell's estimate and say whether the headlined number is the same object brought back down. If the two are identical with no loss, C3 fails.
- **Refuter:** the same estimate applies at both horizons with no R_T, or the estimates are shown to track the preferred model rather than the measured data.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a wage sign for any skill group, region, percentile or horizon.
- Not that immigration always lowers wages or always raises productivity.
- Not a new estimate, and not a replication of any study cited.
- Not that the meta-analysis is an independent second witness; it pools the same literature (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a derivation of a labour-market result from the six-term substrate. After re-census this is a `gap` from the figures.
- Not a moral verdict on immigration. Fig 11 valence is relative to the residual, not a standing property of people.
- Not `grounded`. The whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument per slot, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. A design in which the inflow into an education-experience cell is plausibly exogenous, reported separately from the general-equilibrium total effect.
2. A reading that distinguishes a lower-tail loss from an upper-tail gain from a composition shift.
3. The same cell before and after capital and occupation adjustment, with R_T reported.
4. The four pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3, any one of which withdraws the shape claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :13, :19, :22 and Fig 9 :55 (four short passages).
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: are relative and total wage effects different estimands in the sense described, or the same object under different labels?
3. Name one instrument that would move a single cell's wage reading without moving the headline, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Later agreement is not pooled as corroboration.
