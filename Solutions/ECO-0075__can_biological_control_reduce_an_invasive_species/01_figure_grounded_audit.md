## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

An invasive species I is in contact with a habitat H and reduces a valued ecological state V (native abundance, diversity, ecosystem process rates). A manager proposes to introduce a natural enemy A so that A suppresses I. The residual R is the gap between the predicted post-release state (I suppressed, V restored) and the observed post-release state.

Three things follow before any figure is chosen:

1. **R is per slot, not global.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The target slot (I abundance) can close while slots for native species, predators, or process rates open. A single "net benefit" number would be exactly the aggregation the figures forbid.
2. **The question is a union of two conditions.** "Reduce I" and "create no new ecological risks" are overlapping conditions, not one goal with two names. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
3. **The effect is directional and asymmetric.** Introduction is available; removal of what was introduced is not guaranteed. The relation manager-to-habitat is not reversible, so any answer that treats release and recall as symmetric is already wrong.

The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term: **the set of constraints a defensible release must satisfy before the ground is consulted.** The slot for which agent, in which habitat, stays open.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two conditions joined in a union compound (reduce I AND hold every other slot within tolerance). Not one residual with two labels.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The seat can add an actor; it cannot subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :61). Contact does not consult the manager.
- **Scale:** three ranges. First order is A acting on I and I acting on V. Second order is A acting on a native predator or competitor of I, and that actor acting on a third (the cane toad case: toads poison varanid lizards, lizards no longer suppress rodents, rodents eat the crop). Broader order is the change in ecosystem process (scavenging rates, nutrient cycling). Fig 11 :52 names the depth explicitly: the DDT residual "was three steps out."

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none is named, it is not yet a domain.

- **Domain 1, the invaded habitat (ecological contact).** Actors: the invasive species, native species, conditions (season, water, terrain), relations (predation, competition, dependency), and the proposed agent. Currency: abundance and contact. Anchor: a measurable ecological state (target abundance, non-target abundance, process rate). The anchor is not yet named for any particular site, so this domain is a shape waiting for a ground reading, not a result.
- **Domain 2, the management seat (maintainer).** Actors: the regulator, the selection protocol, the agent as an import. Currency: introductions, not exclusions. Anchor: the field outcome after release, not the manager's intention or the review panel's approval. This is the maintained mode of Fig 11 only.
- **Domain 3, the assessment frame (risk assessment and verification).** Actors: risk assessors, host-range tests, models, post-release monitors. Currency: predictions and residuals, plus agreement among assessors. Anchor: the ground, never the agreement. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so their independence is low and their agreement is not corroboration.

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. The two conditions overlap in the same habitat: the agent that reduces I is the same agent whose contact may harm V, and the same native species can be both a competitor of I and prey of A. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Unbundle first, then search each condition against its own ground.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Describe the residual and enumerate the habitat before choosing an agent (Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 11 :43-44).
2. Decide what is being refused (a chemical-only regime, a do-nothing regime) before minting or importing the agent (Formula step 2).
3. Settle the first-order effect on I before reasoning about the second-order cascade (Fig 12 :104, a settled molecule becomes an atom at the next level).
4. Arrive, then let the ground eliminate candidates (Fig 9 :42). Agreement before arrival is a filter, not a verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 2. Findings F1 to F9

### F1  -  The question is a union compound and must be unbundled (governing)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

A single lever, the agent, clears the region where only "reduce I" holds and leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :56). The two conditions need separate anchors and separate readings. **Mode: general.** **Triangulated** across Fig 9 and Fig 11 (the cascade in Fig 11 supplies the second condition's mechanism; low overlap between the search figure and the habitat figure). **Refuter:** show that one instrument settles both conditions simultaneously, so there is no intersection to leave standing.

### F2  -  The agent is an import, and an import carries a provenance debt

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

A co-evolved natural enemy from I's native range is an atom from a different habitat. It cannot be derived from the invaded habitat's closure, which is precisely why the target escaped its enemies in the first place. The import is the only operator that moves the wall, so it is legitimate, but it owes a source gate and a shadow test: where did this atom come from, and which gap does it explain? **Mode: general.** **Triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 8. **Refuter:** exhibit a control agent derived entirely from the invaded habitat's own closure that still expands the reachable set; then it is not an import and the debt does not apply.

### F3  -  Risk is a function of enumeration depth, and first order is not enough (governing)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

The figure does not say that every release creates new risks. It says the failure class is the *unenumerated cascade*, and it names cane toads and kudzu as that class. The honest shape is that risk tracks how far the contact list was read before release, and the unread remainder is residual by definition. **Mode: general for the rule, specified for the named cases.** **Triangulated** across Fig 11 and Fig 6 (instruments extend what can be represented at all). **Refuter:** demonstrate a finite, complete enumeration of the contact cascade for a real habitat, so that third-order effects are provably absent; then depth stops being a standing constraint.

### F4  -  Valence is relative to the residual, not a property of the actor (governing)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A parasitoid is a benefactor to the residual "reduce I" and a malfactor to the residual "keep native V." Calling an agent beneficial, host-specific, or safe is assigning a kind where the figure says there is only a relation. This is why a specialist can still harm a non-target through apparent competition, a point the world literature confirms independently (Taylor and Snyder 2021). **Mode: general.** **Single-source** for the figure definition, with world corroboration from a low-overlap field noted but not counted as a second witness. **Refuter:** produce an actor whose valence is invariant across a change of residual in the same habitat.

### F5  -  The seat's power is asymmetric, so safety is permanent work, not a certificate

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :62 spells out the reason: "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."

This is the sharpest answer to the "without creating new risks" clause. A one-time finding of low risk cannot be kept, because the agent and its contacts persist and the habitat updates. What can be kept is a monitoring obligation. The Operators table adds a related limit: subtraction removes an operand, whereas negation refuses a claim; stopping a release is not the same as subtracting its effects. **Mode: general.** **Single-source** (Fig 11). **Refuter:** exhibit a reversible introduction in a real habitat where the actor and every contact can be exactly subtracted, so managed systems can reach a solved state.

### F6  -  The ground settles each case; agreement among assessors does not

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Regulatory approval, a high citation count, and a consensus of practitioners are filters for what gets tried, not witnesses for whether risk was avoided. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." The implementer's stake in a yes answer must be declared, not treated as evidence. **Mode: general.** **Triangulated** across Fig 2, Fig 9 and Fig 10. **Refuter:** identify an anchor for ecological risk that moves when a review panel changes its mind; then it is a frame, not a ground.

### F7  -  Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails." Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

Quarantine host-range testing and a glasshouse trial are substituted habitats. They prove that the agent attacks the intended host under those conditions. They do not prove field capability, because the contacts they failed to reproduce are invisible until the goal fails, and the contacts they added act without being chosen. This is why post-release monitoring is part of the shape, not an optional extra. **Mode: general.** **Single-source** (Fig 11). **Refuter:** show a substituted habitat that preserves every contact of the target habitat at the same scale, so the substitution is lossless.

### F8  -  The figure marks its own limits, and that limit is part of the answer

Fig 11 :71: "How to read the claims here. Enumeration and asymmetry are rules this framework imposes on itself." The line continues that "The isolation law and the capability claim fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they can still come back wrong: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."

So the figures do not return a veto on biological control. They return a discipline: enumerate, respect asymmetry, keep the ground reachable, and accept that the isolation and capability claims are themselves falsifiable. **Mode: general.** **Single-source**, stated honestly. **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moved the ground, which would break the capability claim without breaking the enumeration rule.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the exemplar's discipline, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in `07` and `09` is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** One instrument or one measurement settles both "reduce I" and "no new risks" at once, so Fig 9 :54-57 has mis-split the question.
2. **Depth refuter.** A finite, complete enumeration of the contact cascade is achievable for a real habitat, so Fig 11 :52's third-order residual is not a standing constraint.
3. **Asymmetry refuter.** An introduced actor and all its contacts can be exactly subtracted, so Fig 11 :63's "permanent work" is false.
4. **Valence refuter.** An actor's valence is invariant under a change of residual in the same habitat, so Fig 11 :49-51 fails.
5. **Import refuter.** A control agent derived from the invaded habitat's own closure expands the reachable set, so the agent is not an import (Fig 6 :23).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn and `07`, `08` and `09` are revised. Until a ground reading exists, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

Note what is *not* a refuter: a successful programme with no observed non-target effect. That is a case where the shape is satisfied, so it corroborates nothing by itself and refutes nothing.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Do not answer yes or no. Answer with the shape: two conditions, an import with provenance, enumeration to the order where the cascade stops mattering, asymmetric power, and the ground as the only settlement.
2. Unbundle before searching. Ask the reduction question and the no-new-risk question against different anchors (F1).
3. Require the source gate and shadow test for the agent, with dates and the order of derivation recorded (F2).
4. Require the contact list to be enumerated, not composed, and state the order at which monitoring stops (F3).
5. State that "safe" is a relation to a residual, not a property of a species (F4), and that safety is permanent work, not a certificate (F5).
6. Send the residual and the refuter first, through the opt-in venues in `03`. Ask what a failure would look like. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration (F6).

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the generator, meaning the residual, the enumeration rule and the refuter, not a recording of a past success.

## 6. Strain test and re-census

- **Fig 11 :43 "the habitat is enumerated, never composed" versus the loop's composition elsewhere.** Re-census: enumeration is the operation that reads actors off the world; composition is the operation that joins atoms inside a closure. Different operations. No strain.
- **Fig 11 :61 "introduce, never subtract" versus practical risk mitigation.** Re-census: asymmetry describes the seat's power over contact (you cannot remove a fact about the world); mitigation describes reducing resistance on one slot. Mitigation is possible; exact subtraction is not. Different quantities. No strain, but the limit is stated in F5.
- **Fig 9 :54 union versus Fig 11 cascade.** Both describe multiplicity, but Fig 9 governs how the question splits and Fig 11 governs how contact propagates. Low overlap, so this is triangulation, not a repeat of one claim in two vocabularies.
- **Fig 6 import versus Fig 11 introduction.** Same move at two descriptions. The figure text itself links them: import moves the wall (Fig 6) and the seat introduces actors (Fig 11). Included as corroboration between two figures with different actors, not as two independent witnesses.

After re-census: **no hidden strain.** One genuine open item is recorded, the order at which enumeration may stop, which is a ground question and not a figure question.
