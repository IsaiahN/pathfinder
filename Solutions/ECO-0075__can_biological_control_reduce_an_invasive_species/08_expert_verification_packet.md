# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on biological control risk

**What this is:** a claim in a form a biological-control researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on any programme, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "can biological control reduce an invasive species without creating new ecological risks?" is two overlapping conditions in one habitat, so it must be unbundled before search. The control agent is an import from a different habitat and owes provenance. Risk tracks the depth at which the contact cascade is enumerated before release, with first-order scoping as the named failure class. Actor valence is relative to the residual, so "host-specific and safe" is a relation, not a kind. Introduction is available while exclusion is not, so safety is permanent work with a monitoring obligation rather than a one-time certificate. A quarantine test substitutes a habitat and proves wiring, never field capability. The figures return this shape and its refuters, not a yes or a no.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9

- **Statement:** the reduction condition and the no-new-risk condition form a union compound, not a partition or a single quantity with two labels. They need separate anchors and separate readings.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **What to check:** in a system you know, does a lever that suppresses the target leave the non-target condition binding? If the two conditions are genuinely disjoint, this sub-claim is wrong.
- **Refuter:** one instrument or measurement settles both conditions at once, so there is no intersection left standing.

### C2  -  ecology-facing; checkable against the habitat you work on

- **Statement:** risk is a function of how far the contact list was read before release; the habitat is enumerated, never composed, and a synthetic test substitutes a habitat rather than reproducing it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :65 "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."
- **What to check:** in a release you know, was the residual that mattered first order, second order, or further out? Did the quarantine host-range test predict the field outcome? Suckling and Sforza 2014 report that observed ecological changes were not predicted before release in at least a few cases.
- **Refuter:** a finite, complete enumeration of the contact cascade for a real habitat, so third-order effects are provably absent; or a substituted habitat that preserves every contact of the field at the same scale.

### C3  -  ecology-facing; checkable against non-target outcomes

- **Statement:** an actor's valence is relative to the residual, and the manager's power is asymmetric, so safety is a relation and a continuing obligation, not a property and a certificate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
- **What to check:** is there a well-studied specialist agent whose non-target harm arose through apparent competition when the target became scarce? Taylor and Snyder 2021 document this pattern for relatively specialised agents. Can a failed release be exactly reversed in your system?
- **Refuter:** an actor whose valence is invariant across a change of residual in the same habitat, or a release whose actor and contacts can be exactly subtracted so a managed system can reach a solved state.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that biological control always creates new risks. The figure names a failure class, not a verdict on every release.
- Not that any named agent is safe or unsafe, and not that chemical control or inaction is better.
- Not that specialist agents are safe. Fig 11 :49-51 makes valence relative to the residual.
- Not that quarantine testing settles the question. Fig 11 :70 makes a test harness a substituted habitat.
- Not grounded. The figures themselves mark their isolation and capability claims as untested forward.
- Not a ground reading of any habitat. This packet was produced by a simulated derivation and checked only for internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating field reading, not a further argument. A fall in target abundance cannot comment on its own source. The tests are: post-release residual on slots outside the first-order list; non-target harm via apparent competition when the target becomes scarce; exact reversibility of a failed release; field failure not predicted by quarantine; and the five standing refuters in `01` §3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 11 :43-44, :52, :50-51, :61-63 and :70. Eight short passages.
2. For one release you know, write the first three contacts outward from the target and say at which order the residual actually appeared.
3. Name one case where an agent was beneficial to suppression and harmful to a non-target, or say why none exists in your system.
4. Say whether a failed release in your jurisdiction can be exactly reversed.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
