**Subject:** One checkable claim about biological control risk, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from your own work. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with a position on biological control. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` in shape, `specified` for any programme, not `grounded`):** the question "can biological control reduce an invasive species without creating new ecological risks?" is two conditions in one habitat, not one goal. Under the figures, three constraints follow before any agent is named.
>
> 1. The habitat is enumerated, never composed. You list everything in contact with the target, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. What you cannot yet perceive is the residual. The figure names the failure class directly: cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated.
> 2. An actor's valence is relative to the target of the intervention, not a property of the actor. The same natural enemy is a benefactor to the "reduce the invader" condition and a potential malfactor to the "keep native species" condition. "Host-specific and therefore safe" is a relation claim, not a kind claim.
> 3. The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor, you cannot subtract the habitat. So "no new risks" is not a certificate you earn once, it is permanent work with a monitoring obligation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that biological control always creates new risks, that any particular agent is unsafe, or that chemical control is better. The figures do not return a yes or a no. They return a shape that any defensible release must have, and they explicitly mark their own isolation and capability claims as untested forward. The agent that actually works, in the habitat you know, is something only the ground can settle.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. One instrument settles both the reduction condition and the no-new-risk condition at once.
> 2. A finite, complete enumeration of the contact cascade is achievable for a real habitat, so third-order effects are provably absent.
> 3. An introduced actor and all its contacts can be exactly subtracted, so managed ecosystems can reach a solved state.
> 4. An actor's valence is invariant under a change of the thing you are trying to fix.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
