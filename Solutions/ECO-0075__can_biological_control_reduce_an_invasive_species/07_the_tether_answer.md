# The Tether's answer on biological control and ecological risk

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on any claim about a programme, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An invasive species reduces a valued ecological state. A proposed release of a natural enemy aimed at the invader may also act on other slots in the habitat. The question bundles two conditions, reduction and no new risk, into one sentence (Fig 9 :55). The residual is per slot, not a net benefit (Fig 1 :19). This is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground, not one hard question with a single number.

## 2. The shape

1. **Unbundle before you search.** The two conditions share the same agent and the same habitat, so a single lever clears the region where only one binds and leaves the intersection standing. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Reduction and safety get separate anchors and separate readings.
2. **The agent is an import with a provenance debt.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The co-evolved enemy comes from a different habitat, which is why the invader escaped it. Import is the only operator that moves the wall, so it is legitimate, but the source gate and shadow test are owed: where did this atom come from and which gap does it explain (Fig 8 :51).
3. **Risk tracks the depth of the contact list.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 requires listing what is in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Fig 11 :52 names the failure of first-order scoping: the DDT residual "was three steps out." Fig 11 :65 names the failure class for biological control: "Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."
4. **Valence is a relation, not a kind.** Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." A natural enemy that is a benefactor to the reduction condition can be a malfactor to the native-species condition. "Host-specific and therefore safe" is not a figure-derived verdict.
5. **Safety is permanent work, not a certificate.** Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." The agent and its contacts persist, so a one-time finding of low risk cannot be kept. What can be kept is a monitoring obligation.
6. **A substituted habitat proves wiring, never capability.** Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Quarantine host-range tests and models are part of the budget filter, not the ground.
7. **The ground settles each case.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Endorsement, citation count and regulatory approval are filters for what gets tried (Fig 9 :60), not witnesses for whether new risks were avoided.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that biological control always creates new risks. The figures name a failure class, the unenumerated cascade, not a verdict on every release.
- Not that any particular agent is safe or unsafe. That is a ground reading for a named habitat.
- Not that chemical control or doing nothing is better. Those are different imports with their own cascades.
- Not that specialist agents are safe. The world source Taylor and Snyder 2021 finds no clear relationship between specialisation and safety, and Fig 11 :49-51 makes safety relational.
- Not that quarantine testing settles the question. It substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :70).
- Not grounded. The figures themselves mark their isolation and capability claims as untested forward.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A source-discriminating field reading, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the reading "target abundance fell" cannot comment on the source. The tests that would close the shape are:

- **Cascade test:** does post-release monitoring find residual on a slot that was outside the first-order contact list? A positive finding corroborates the enumeration requirement; a demonstrably complete finite enumeration would refute it.
- **Valence test:** does an agent rated beneficial for the reduction condition harm a native species through apparent competition when the target becomes scarce? Taylor and Snyder 2021 supply case material.
- **Asymmetry test:** can the agent and all its contacts be exactly subtracted after a failed release? If yes, "permanent work" is false.
- **Substitution test:** does a field outcome fail in a way the quarantine test did not predict? Suckling and Sforza 2014 note that observed ecological changes were not predicted before release in at least a few cases.
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** one instrument settling both conditions; a provably complete enumeration; exact subtraction; residual-invariant valence; a control agent derived from the invaded habitat's own closure that moves the wall.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the name.** The figures can say what a defensible release must look like. They cannot name the agent that works in a specific habitat, because that is a ground task and the canonical rule returns a shape, not a name. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The contact list is partially enumerable, the third-order residual is real, valence is relational, and the ground decides each case. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to biological control. It says the question is two conditions in one habitat, so the reduction goal and the no-new-risk goal must be read against different anchors. The control agent is an import from a different habitat and owes a record of where it came from. Risk tracks how far you enumerate the contact cascade before release: first-order scoping is the failure class the figures name when they cite cane toads and kudzu. Whether an actor helps or harms is relative to the thing you are trying to fix, so "host-specific and safe" is a relation claim, not a property of a species. And because introduction is available while exclusion is not, safety is permanent work with a monitoring obligation, not a certificate you earn once. Quarantine tests prove wiring, never field capability. What the framework offers is that shape, four written refuters, and the tests that would close it. The name of the agent that works is left to the ground.
