**Subject:** One check on a claim about inflation expectations and inflation

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in inflation expectations research and I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to read a theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `gap` on the magnitude):** the question "are inflation expectations a major cause of inflation, or mostly a response to price changes" is a forced choice over a **union compound**, not a partition. Both legs are present in the same system. Expectations are a belief held before price and wage setting, so they enter the action that produces realised inflation. The belief is also formed from past outcomes, so realised inflation feeds back into it. The coupling is a **signed cycle**, not a one-way chain, and the sign has to be measured rather than assumed.
>
> Two consequences follow from that shape. First, the question is not one hard question but several well formed ones: how expectations form, how they enter price setting, and what instrument could move one term without moving the other. Second, the coupling must be read **per slot**, by country, horizon, forecaster type and inflation regime. A single aggregate number for "major" throws away the signal that says where to look.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that any particular coefficient is large. "Major" is a magnitude word, and no figure line returns a magnitude. On this derivation that part is a `gap`, not a "no". I am also not claiming that professional forecasts, household surveys and market breakevens are the same reading of expectations. They are different instruments, and a reading that does not discriminate among them cannot settle direction.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. An exogenous, randomised shift in expectations produces no change in price or wage setting at any horizon.
> 2. Realised inflation surprises produce no update in stated expectations at any horizon.
> 3. The coupling has the same sign and magnitude in every country, horizon, forecaster type and regime, so reading it per slot buys nothing.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Inflation Expectations indicator and method (Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, accessed 2026-11-23): https://www.clevelandfed.org/our-research/indicators-and-data/inflation-expectations
> - Rudd, "Why Do We Think That Inflation Expectations Matter for Inflation? (And Should We?)", FEDS 2021-062: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/why-do-we-think-that-inflation-expectations-matter-for-Inflation-and-should-we.htm
> - Moessner, "Effects of Inflation Expectations on Inflation", National Institute Economic Review, open access: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/national-institute-economic-review/article/effects-of-inflation-expectations-on-inflation/B34CC30843D8225128777FED966367A6
> - Fair, "Inflation expectations, price equations, and Fed effects", Business Economics 59 (2024): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s11369-024-00378-y
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and the formula are in the local framework files listed in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, recorded explicitly

- **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim, the per-slot rule and the three refuters. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the verdict **before** any framework material is shown.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the four links above and the local figure text. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Nothing is sent by this agent. The user hosts and sends.
