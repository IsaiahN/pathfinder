# Expert verification packet: the Tether on inflation expectations and inflation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an inflation-expectations researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the per-slot rule, `gap` on the magnitude. Not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken in this run.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are inflation expectations a major cause of inflation, or mostly a response to price changes" is a forced choice over a union compound, not a partition. Both legs are present in the same system. Expectations are the belief `b`, held before the price and wage setting action `a`, so they enter the production of the outturn `o'`. The belief is also formed from past outcomes through the weighting `w`, so realised inflation feeds back. The coupling is a signed cycle, not a one-way chain, and the two directional readings are isomers over the shared atoms, differing in bond arrangement only. The residual must be read per slot (country, horizon, forecaster type, inflation regime), because averaging across slots removes the signal. The word "major" is a magnitude, and no figure line returns one, so it is recorded as a gap here and owed to an instrument that moves expectations exogenously.

### C2: framework-internal and notation-facing; checkable with the Symbols table

- **Statement:** expectations and realised inflation are distinct terms (`b` and `o'`), the cause leg runs `b → a ⇒ o'`, and the response leg runs `o' → b`; over the same atoms these are isomers.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that expectations are or are not a "major" cause. That magnitude is `gap` here.
- Not that either A or B alone answers the question. Both are refused as a complete answer.
- Not a coefficient, a variance share, or a universal sign.
- Not that survey expectations, professional forecasts and market breakevens are the same reading of the belief.
- Not that the world literature has settled the matter. Rudd (2021) finds the strong causal belief rests on "extremely shaky foundations"; Moessner (2025) writes there is "no consensus"; Fair (2024) finds household expectations mostly affected by current and past inflation. These are three reports from overlapping pools, not three witnesses.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". Here the projection is the expectations-inflation correlation, which can be produced by a cause path, a response path, or a common driver. The tests:

1. **Exogenous shift:** randomised information or an announcement surprise moves beliefs while costs and demand are held. Do prices follow?
2. **Surprise update:** an inflation surprise moves stated expectations by the predicted amount.
3. **Slot check:** the coupling differs by country, horizon, forecaster type and regime.
4. **Sign check:** the loop damps in anchored regimes and reinforces in high-inflation regimes.
5. **Source check:** an instrument separates a risk-premium shift from an expectations shift in market measures.
