# Expert verification packet: the Tether on atmospheric retention over billions of years

**What this is:** a claim in a form an exoplanet atmosphere researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the gap. Not `grounded`.** No frequency is claimed. This is a shape and an anchor requirement, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Atmospheric retention over billions of years is unmaintained survival under contact: what the ground kept from a cohort. It is a property of planet and habitat, not of the planet alone; it is a threshold relation between gravitational binding and incident high energy flux; it is ordered in time; and the diagnostic signal is indexed per planet, so a single averaged fraction discards what matters. The framework does **not** return a retention frequency. A frequency is a count over a sample, and only a measurement settles it. The verdict on the number is `gap` in the figure framework, with an import instruction: measure an occurrence rate over a declared sample, report non-detections as bounds, and keep the per-object record.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** retention is unmaintained survival under contact. A planet's atmosphere is not authored by a maintaining seat; what survives is what the ground kept. Replenishment is a habitat actor, not a seat, so the counted object is a flow over Gyr rather than a static envelope.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Also Fig 11 :14-15 "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept" and Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone".
- **What to check:** does the mapping survive the volcanic-replenishment case? Kite and Barnett 2020 model secondary atmosphere revival; our claim is that this is an actor introduced into the habitat, not a maintainer with a live frame.
- **Refuter:** show that the relevant persistence is authored by a seat choosing retention outcomes in the figure's sense, or that retention is a property of the planet alone across stellar types and ages.

### C2: physics-facing; checkable by an atmosphere researcher against the literature

- **Statement:** retention versus loss is a threshold, not a blend, and it is ordered by early stellar history. Two planets with the same mass, radius and volatile inventory but different irradiation histories are figure-isomers (Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour.").
- **What to check:** Ji et al. 2025 report *"its variation prevents the definition of a unique clear-cut shoreline"* and a transition zone; Pass et al. 2025 find revised XUV fluences *"2.1-3.1 times the canonical XUV scaling relation on average"* and that only the largest terrestrial planets around mid to late M dwarfs likely retained atmospheres. Is the threshold claim faithful, or is the empirical relation smooth enough that a blend is the better description?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate an additive, threshold-free retention law across the full mass and stellar-type range; or show two composition-identical planets with different histories always end in the same state at all epochs (kills the isomer claim).

### C3: framework-internal + ground-facing; the gap itself

- **Statement:** no figure line yields a frequency. The number is anchor-settled, and a non-detection bounds rather than zeros it.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** Kreidberg and Stevenson 2025 report *"no atmospheres have been definitively detected"* and list three live readings of a flat spectrum; Ih et al. 2026 report *"at most one in 10 M-star rocky planets have atmospheres at 95% CI"* from 27 non-detections. Is that a bound, as we say, or has the literature already settled a frequency for a declared sample?
- **Refuter:** produce a figure line that determines a numerical frequency without strain, or a measurement that settles `f` for a declared population. Either closes the gap and retires C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a retention fraction, exponent or critical instellation. No number is asserted.
- Not that the cosmic shoreline is a counting law. It is a threshold surface; a count needs a sample.
- Not that all rocky planets are airless. Non-detections are bounds.
- Not that planet mass alone decides retention. Habitat terms are load-bearing.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is settled by experiment.
- Not that the Tether framework is validated by these world sources. They are secondary, and most of the shoreline papers share a lineage; that overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 5. What would settle it

1. An occurrence-rate measurement over a declared planet sample, with non-detections reported as bounds and observing cost stated. This closes the D2 gap.
2. A controlled comparison of composition-identical planets with different irradiation histories, testing the isomer claim in C2.
3. A source-discriminating observation that separates a high cloud deck, a high-mean-molecular-weight atmosphere and bare rock in a flat transmission spectrum.
