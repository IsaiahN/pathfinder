## 0. The residual

State R in effect terms before choosing any figure line.

**R.** The frequency `f` with which a population of planets still holds a substantial gaseous envelope after about a gigayear or more. Arity: one reported number, but a **compound of conditions** (gravity, incident high energy flux, composition, impact history, replenishment, age). Symmetry: retention versus loss is a **threshold comparison**, not a symmetric combine of two co-present quantities. Scale: population level (many objects, a statistic), across deep time (a state), and per object (the signal lives on individual planets).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term:** a cohort survivorship curve, ordered in time, with a threshold in escape velocity versus cumulative XUV separating envelopes from bare rock, the threshold located by observation. This is the shape the search is for. Binding it lets the preference accrue a residual and be refused.

**R is not one question.** A single "how often" bundles a mechanism question (why a given planet keeps or loses gas) with a counting question (what fraction of a sample kept it). Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* This is a **union compound**, not a hard partition. Fig 9 :39: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

### F2: Atmospheric retention is unmaintained survival under contact (governing figure for D1)

Fig 11 :56:

> **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."**

Fig 11 :14-15:

> **"nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept"**

The framework's own gloss, from *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-23: *"Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground. When there is no one in the Ground Maintainer seat, the environment is the only selector."*

A planet's atmosphere has no seat choosing its persistence. It is kept or not kept by contact: escape velocity, XUV, wind, impacts, composition. So "how often do planets retain atmospheres over billions of years" is, in the figures' terms, a survivorship question: what the ground kept from a cohort. This is the strongest figure-grounded mapping in the whole question, and it carries low strain because the vocabulary is the figure's own.

**Strain, named.** Real planets can *replenish* secondary atmospheres by volcanism; Kite and Barnett 2020 report *"atmospheres should persist on worlds that start with abundant volatiles (for example, water worlds)"* and model volcanic revival. That looks like a maintainer. Re-census: the unit being counted is not a static object at an instant but a persistent gaseous *flow* over Gyr, and replenishment is an actor in the habitat, introduced by contact, not a seat authoring an outcome. Fig 11 :61-62: *"contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* The unmaintained reading survives, with the flow caveat recorded. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show that the relevant persistence is authored by a seat with a live frame that chooses retention outcomes, in the figure's sense, rather than produced by contact; or show that retention is a property of the planet alone with no habitat term.

### F4: Retention versus loss is a threshold comparison, not a blend (D1)

Fig 12 :86, with the Operators table :49 in agreement:

> **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**

The cosmic shoreline literature is a threshold object: a dividing line in escape velocity versus irradiation, with a *"transition zone"* rather than a sharp edge in the hydrodynamic models (Ji et al. 2025, opened 2026-11-23). Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Two planets with the same mass and volatile inventory but different irradiation histories are figure-isomers, not variants of one thing. Mode `specified`.

A warning on using ⋛ here. The bond is a threshold relation between quantities; the quantities at D1 are binding energy and incident energy flux. That is a clean type match. At the population level the operand is a probability, which is a different level; see `06_isomers_and_order.md` for the level separation and its strain.

**Refuter:** show retention is a continuous additive blend with no threshold behaviour at any level, or that ⋛'s operand type is violated at D1.

### F5: The answer is a distribution over per-object outcomes, not one averaged number (D1 to D2 bridge)

A single "how often" figure destroys the slot index that tells you which planets drive the answer. ### F6: A non-detection is not an absence (D2)

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 6 :32:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Kreidberg and Stevenson 2025, opened 2026-11-23, report that *"no atmospheres have been definitively detected"* on rocky exoplanets and that many plausible compositions have features *"below the current noise level."* The Ih et al. 2026 statistical framework, opened 2026-11-23, shows that even 27 bare-rock non-detections give only an upper bound: *"at most one in 10 M-star rocky planets have atmospheres at 95% CI."* These are bounds, not zeros. Treating them as zeros would be a direct violation of Fig 8 :39. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** demonstrate an instrument whose null result is constitutively a measurement of zero, not a limit, for the same population.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line, applied without strain, determines a numerical atmospheric-retention frequency or timescale for a declared population. That closes the D2 gap.
2. Retention is shown to be authored by a seat with a live frame choosing persistence outcomes in the figure's sense, rather than produced by contact. That breaks F2.
3. Retention is shown to be a property of the planet alone across stellar types and ages, with no habitat residual. That breaks F3.
4. The retention-versus-loss relation is shown to be additive with no threshold at any level. That breaks F4.
5. Two populations with different survivorship curves but the same `f` are interchangeable for every downstream question. That breaks F5.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the audit is rerun from the census. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 9. Verdict

The shape survives; the number does not exist in the figure library. Atmospheric retention over billions of years is unmaintained survival under contact, a property of planet and habitat, a threshold between binding and loss, ordered in time, and indexed per planet. The figures do not yield a retention frequency, and they forbid treating a non-detection as a zero.
