# The Tether's answer on atmospheric retention over billions of years

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the gap and on the per-domain findings. Nothing is `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a frequency `f`: the fraction of a planet cohort that still holds a substantial gaseous envelope after a gigayear or more. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :39: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). The mechanism question (why a given planet keeps or loses gas) and the counting question (what fraction did) have different anchors and must be answered separately.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **Retention is what the ground kept.** Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." A planet's atmosphere is not authored by a seat; it survives contact or does not. The framework's own gloss is that natural selection is the loop with no one maintaining the ground.
2. **Retention is a property of planet and habitat.** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Mass alone is insufficient. Incident high energy flux, composition, impacts, replenishment and stellar age are habitat actors, enumerated from the world (Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed.").
3. **It is a threshold, not a blend.** Fig 12 :86 "a threshold relation between two quantities." Binding energy versus incident energy sets the comparison. The literature calls the result a shoreline with a transition zone.
4. **It is an ordered survivorship curve, indexed per planet.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said." A single averaged fraction discards the signal that says which planets drive the answer.
5. **Time is load-bearing.** Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." "Over billions of years" bonds the time state to an energy gradient through a structure. A snapshot is the wrong object.

## 3. The gap

**The figures do not yield a frequency.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." A count over a sample is exactly that. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." So the D2 verdict is `gap` in the figure framework, with the instruction to import the measurement, not to manufacture a number. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.

This is not a claim that nobody knows anything. The world literature supplies **bounds with provenance**: Kreidberg and Stevenson 2025 report no definitive rocky atmosphere detection and many plausible spectra below the noise level; Ih et al. 2026 show that 27 bare-rock non-detections bound M-dwarf rocky atmosphere occurrence at one in ten at 95% confidence. Those are imports with samples and intervals, not figure outputs, and not ground settlements of a universal `f`.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a retention fraction, an exponent, or a critical instellation. The framework returns a shape, not a number.
- Not that all rocky planets are airless. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." A flat spectrum has at least three interpretations.
- Not that the cosmic shoreline is a law that counts planets. It is a threshold surface in parameter space; a count requires a sample.
- Not that planet mass alone decides retention. That breaks Fig 11 :39.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles any part of the shape at the ground.

## 5. What would settle it, and what would refute the shape

**The ground, not agreement.** An occurrence-rate measurement over a declared planet sample, with non-detections reported as bounds, the observing cost stated, and the per-object record kept. That closes the D2 gap. It does not need more reviews.

**Refuters, pre-registered** (from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5):

1. a figure line that determines a numerical frequency without strain;
2. a demonstration that retention is authored by a seat choosing outcomes rather than produced by contact;
3. a mass-only retention law that holds across stellar types and ages with no habitat residual;
4. an additive loss relation with no threshold at any level;
5. two different survivorship curves with the same `f` interchangeable for every downstream question.

**Strains carried forward:** replenishment versus unmaintained survival (resolved as a flow, not an object); the ⋛ operand type at the population level (named, not smoothed).

## 6. The honest stop

The shape is complete when it names the instrument that would close it. Here that instrument is an occurrence-rate survey over a declared sample, not a further argument. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round-trip gap `R_T` from the verbatim figures to the phrase "retention is survival under contact" is nonzero and is enumerated in `01` §8. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. The result is `candidate`.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a number for how often planets keep their atmospheres, and it does not claim the cosmic shoreline is a counting law. It says that retention is what the ground kept from a cohort: there is no seat choosing which planets keep their gas, so the outcome is contact with gravity, stellar radiation, composition, impacts and time. That makes retention a survivorship question, a property of planet and habitat, a threshold between binding and loss, and a curve indexed per planet rather than a single averaged fraction. The framework refuses to name the frequency because the frequency is a count and only a measurement settles it. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the kind of observation that would close the gap. The count stays open until the ground pays for it.
