# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide about the frequency

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive checking. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 shape does not hold.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about atmospheric retention, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about atmospheric retention over geological time that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** How often does a planet still hold a substantial atmosphere after a billion years or more? The number itself is a count over a sample, so only an observation settles it. What I want checked is the shape any answer to that count must have.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the gap, not `grounded`).** From the framework's habitat rule, retention is what the ground kept from a cohort. There is no seat choosing which planets keep their envelopes; the outcome is contact with escape velocity, stellar high energy radiation, composition, impacts and time. That makes retention a survivorship question, not a property of a planet alone. The framework also says the residual is indexed per object, so a single averaged fraction loses the signal, and that retention versus loss is a threshold relation, not a blend.
>
> **What the framework explicitly does not give:** the frequency. No figure line returns a retention fraction or a timescale. I report that as a `gap` in the framework, not as silence and not as a number I made up. The instruction is to import the measurement: an occurrence rate over a declared planet sample, with the non-detection treated as a bound and the observing cost stated.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. show that a figure line, without strain, determines a numerical retention frequency;
> 2. show that retention is authored by a seat choosing outcomes, rather than produced by contact;
> 3. show that retention is a property of the planet alone across stellar types and ages, with no habitat residual;
> 4. show that the loss relation is additive with no threshold at any level.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`

Write down what would refute your result before you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author's essay warns that outputs can be simulated by a language model rather than derived from the architecture. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the shape claim and the refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the figure link and the two literature links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

The order is not politeness. It is Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Once a reader holds the interior of the framework, their agreement stops being evidence.
