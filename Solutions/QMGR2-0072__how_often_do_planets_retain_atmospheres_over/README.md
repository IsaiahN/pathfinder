## The residual in effect terms

The question asks for a frequency `f`: what fraction of a population of planets still holds a substantial gaseous envelope after roughly a billion years or more. Restated as an effect, `f` is the residue of a competition between retention and loss under contact with an environment over deep time. Option A treats `f` as a number the theory can produce. Option B treats `f` as a shape plus an anchor requirement: the figures constrain what any answer must look like, while the number itself is settled only by a measurement. What each leads to: A delivers a verdict with no ground and spends the independence of the people checking it; B delivers checkable constraints, a pre-registered refuter, and an honest `gap` where the number belongs.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (THE_FORMULA, Direction):** an answer would look like a survivorship distribution over a cohort of planets, ordered in time, with a threshold separating retained envelopes from stripped rocks, and with the threshold located by observation. That shape is bound here and allowed to accrue a residual like anything else.
