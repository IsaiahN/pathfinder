# Survivorship order, threshold levels and the isomer analogue

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves three structural questions open:

1. In what order does retention unfold, and what does the order buy us?
2. At which level does the ⋛ comparison operate: a single planet, or a population?
3. Are two planets with the same mass and volatile inventory but different irradiation histories **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or merely members of one functional group?

Each is answered separately. Verdicts are not averaged.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures). Bonds held: the seven, including ⋛ comparison, → sequence and − subtraction; plus negation ¬ at D1 level. No new primitive is available without import. The population level introduces a probability, which is a derived quantity, not a seventh member.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What order does retention have?

- Fig 13 :88: *"time the ordered succession of differences."* Time is a state, and retention is composed with it.
- Fig 12 :71: sequence is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."* Early escape from a young star precedes later retention; the pre-main-sequence XUV history is a predecessor, not a co-present term. Pass et al. 2025 report that revised active lifetimes give historic XUV fluence *"2.1-3.1 times the canonical XUV scaling relation on average"*, which changes which planets are on the retained side. Order is load-bearing.
- The literature's own object is a **survivorship curve**. Rauschert 2010 writes: *"Ecologists use survivorship curves to visualize how the number of individuals in a population drops off with time."* The curve is ordered, monotone decreasing, and indexed to a cohort.
- ⇒ The answer shape is a curve over time, not a snapshot fraction. Mode `general` for the ordering, `specified` for the curve form.

**Falsifier:** if retention is time invariant at fixed instantaneous flux, so that a planet stripped at 100 Myr and one stripped at 5 Gyr are indistinguishable given the same flux, the ordering claim fails.

### Q2: Is a survivorship curve a sixth or seventh term?

- Fig 13 :43: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."* A generating set must not contain what its own members produce.
- A survivorship curve is a spectrum over time composed with a network of objects. It is a derived quantity, the kind of thing Fig 13 :124 calls *"what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*
- ⇒ No new term. The curve is a composition over held atoms. Mode `general`.

**Falsifier:** derive a retention law that requires a primitive not composable from the six; that would be a seventh and would break minimality.

### Q3: At which level does ⋛ operate?

- At D1, Fig 12 :86: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. The operands are binding energy and incident energy flux. Clean type match.
- At D2 the operand is a probability of retention for a cohort. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A population-level probability can be built from settled per-object outcomes and used as an operand at the next level.
- **Strain, named.** Whether a probability counts as a *quantity* in the sense of the Operators table at D2 is not settled by the figure. Ji et al. 2025 model a logistic loss probability and report a *"transition zone"* rather than a sharp line, which is consistent with a threshold at both levels but does not prove the type. This needs an instrument, not an argument.
- ⇒ ⋛ is licensed at D1 without strain. At D2 it is a level-relative extension, marked strained and carried forward. Mode `specified` for D1, `candidate` with named strain for D2.

**Falsifier:** show that the D2 probability cannot be derived from settled D1 outcomes, which would make the population comparison a new primitive rather than a composition.

### Q4: Are the two retention states isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Take two planets with the same mass, radius and initial volatile inventory, one around a quiet Sun-like star and one around an active late M dwarf. Same atoms. Different bond: the irradiation and time history. Different outcome: envelope retained versus stripped. On the figure's definition they are **isomers**: same composition, different arrangement, decisive behavioural difference. This is the strongest structural claim the figures support here.
- The weaker claim is that all rocky planets share a functional group, `state → gradient → state` over the six-term set, with the qualifier (which star, which epoch, which composition) deciding the member. Both claims can be stated; they are not the same claim.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two planets with identical mass, radius and volatile inventory but different irradiation histories end in the same retention state at all observed epochs, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands.

This falsifier is checkable against the literature. Ji et al. 2025 find retention *"markedly more favorable for higher-mass planets orbiting higher-mass stars"*, and Pass et al. 2025 find only the largest terrestrial planets around mid to late M dwarfs likely retained atmospheres. Those are differences in mass as well as history, so they do not yet run the clean isomer test. A controlled comparison is owed.

## CONCLUSION

Retention is a time-ordered composition, not a snapshot. Its curve is a derived spectrum over held atoms and adds no seventh term. The threshold comparison ⋛ is a clean type match at the level of one planet and a strained, level-relative extension at the population level. Planets that share composition but differ in irradiation history are figure-isomers, with a stated falsifier. None of this produces a frequency. The frequency remains the D2 gap from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.
