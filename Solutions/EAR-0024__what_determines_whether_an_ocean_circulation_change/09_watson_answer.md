## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what determines the shape of an ocean circulation response (gradual, abrupt, reversible, hysteretic) under a changing freshwater or heat forcing. Option A: treat the three adjectives as points on one dial and return one winner plus a threshold number. Option B: treat them as a union of conditions with different determinants, keep per-domain verdicts separate, and declare the specific numbers a gap. What each leads to: A asserts an ungrounded number and a false partition, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B returns a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open slot until observation settles it.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates|conflicts)

- **Physical oceanography / founding model.** Henry Stommel, "Thermohaline Convection with Two Stable Regimes of Flow", Tellus 13(2), 1961. Quote: a slight parameter change makes the circulation *"jump over to the reverse salinity circulation at c, and it would then stay there even when A was restored to its original value."* Predicts abrupt change plus hysteresis. **Corroborates** C2 and C3. One witness, the foundation of the pool.
- **Physical oceanography / intercomparison.** Stefan Rahmstorf, "Thermohaline circulation hysteresis: A model intercomparison", Geophysical Research Letters, 2005. Quote: *"all models, despite their large differences in construction, show a clear hysteresis response"* driven by *"a basin-scale positive feedback"*. Predicts hysteresis is generic and names the positive loop. **Corroborates** C2 and C3, but shares the Stommel-family evidence pool.
- **Earth system / canonical definition.** Timothy Lenton et al., "Tipping elements in the Earth's climate system", PNAS 105(6), 2008. Quote: the term covers *"reversible phase transitions, phase transitions with hysteresis effects, and bifurcations where the transition is smooth"* and models *"differ as to whether the transition is reversible or irreversible (in principle)"*; also *"Both the timescale and the magnitude of forcing are important."* Predicts a union and a rate axis. **Corroborates** C1 and the union framing directly, in the field's own words.
- **Climate tipping / mechanism.** Timothy Lenton, "Tipping points in the climate system", Weather, 2021. Quote: *"tipping points occur when reinforcing (positive) feedbacks within a system take over from stabilising (negative) feedbacks."* Predicts cycle sign as determinant. **Corroborates** C2. Same author stream as the PNAS paper, so not an independent witness.
- **Ecology / catastrophic shifts (low overlap).** Marten Scheffer et al., "Catastrophic shifts in ecosystems", Nature 413, 2001. Quote: *"smooth change can be interrupted by sudden drastic switches to a contrasting state"*; *"a loss of resilience usually paves the way for a switch to an alternative state."* Predicts threshold jumps from gradual forcing in a different field. **Corroborates** C2 at the mechanism-shape level, not for the ocean fact.
- **Ecology / spatial heterogeneity (low overlap).** Egbert van Nes and Marten Scheffer, "Implications of spatial heterogeneity for catastrophic regime shifts in ecosystems", Ecology 86(7), 2005. Quote: *"the response of the ecosystem as a whole is gradual (i.e., the average of many asynchronous small shifts) instead of catastrophic"*, yet *"the system always shows hysteresis."* Predicts aggregate-gradual / per-cell-abrupt, exactly the per-slot warning. **Corroborates** Fig 1 :19/:22 after anchor renaming per Fig 4 :20. Shares the Scheffer pool.
- **Oceanography / first GCM tipping event.** René van Westen, Michael Kliphuis and Henk Dijkstra, "Physics-based early warning signal shows that AMOC is on tipping course", Science Advances 10(6), 2024. Quote: *"about 8-Sv AMOC change due to a forcing change of 0.03 Sv"*; reanalyses are *"too short (maximum of ~100 years) at the moment to adequately estimate the distance to the FovS minimum."* Predicts abrupt jump after gradual weakening and names the owed observing instrument. **Corroborates** C2, C3 and the gap. Shares the Utrecht/CMIP pool.
- **Climate statistics / early warning.** Peter Ditlevsen and Susanne Ditlevsen, "Warning of a forthcoming collapse of the AMOC", Nature Communications 14, 2023 (Author Correction 2025). Quote: the transition is modelled as a *"critical transition through a saddle-node bifurcation"* with a 2037-2109 estimate. Predicts a threshold crossing with variance/autocorrelation warning. **Corroborates** C1 and C2 on the shape, but its specific date is not adopted; the 2025 correction and later critiques are recorded.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The three adjectives form a union of overlapping conditions with different determinants, not a partition with one winner. Continuity versus abruptness is governed by feedback cycle sign against a threshold (Fig 12 :121, :87; Fig 13 :32); reversibility by basin structure, structurally the closure and its wall (Fig 6 :14, :23; Fig 8 :32); rate is an independent third determinant (Fig 4 :30; Fig 7 :38; Fig 10 :24). Verdicts are per circulation slot and never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Observation is the anchor and model agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :29, :40). The specific threshold, critical rate and reversibility of a named circulation are a declared `gap`: unreachable at this budget, not silence. Mode `specified` on the determinants, `general` on the union shape, ungrounded and candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** a single lever applied to a union. If abruptness and reversibility are treated as one dial, a policy or an observing campaign buys the wrong instrument, and an expert's twenty minutes is spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and reversible. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. A refutation adds a term and removes nothing; the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The union, refuters and lineage are recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A sustained observing record showing a return with no reverse threshold where hysteresis was predicted; a physics-based observable that separates gradual weakening from rate-induced or noise-induced jumps; a reversal experiment across rates measuring a critical rate; or a figure re-census that reassigns the cycle-sign or closure determinant. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
