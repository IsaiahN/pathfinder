## Three things in the literature that bear directly on this solution

1. **The union is the field's own wording.** Lenton et al. 2008 list positive feedbacks, reversible phase transitions, hysteresis transitions and smooth noise-dependent bifurcations as things "tipping point" has been used to describe, and say models differ on reversibility. The union in `01` is not imposed from outside; it is the shape of the literature.
2. **A fixed threshold is not a well-defined object once rate is admitted.** Ritchie et al. and Chapman et al. show tipping without crossing a critical level and return tipping on fast reversal; van Westen et al. 2026 (Nature Climate Change, verified via public index) report the AMOC stable up to +5.5 C under slow forcing but collapsing near +2 C under fast forcing. Any outreach that quotes a single threshold number is already wrong.
3. **The anchor is an observing system, not a model vote.** Ben-Yami et al. 2024 and the short reanalysis records in van Westen et al. 2024 both point to the same owed instrument: long, sustained section measurements (SAMBA at 34°S, RAPID at 26°N). That is where a real verdict on reversibility would come from.
