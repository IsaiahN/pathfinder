# Solution EAR-0024

**Question:** What determines whether an ocean circulation change is gradual, abrupt, or reversible?

**Source:** earth systems.txt line 34

**Section:** 3. Oceans and circulation

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. Every substantive file is labelled a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending (registry maintained outside this folder)

## What is being decided, in effect terms

The question offers three adjectives as if they were competing answers to one dial. Restated as an effect: given a freshwater or heat forcing F applied to an ocean circulation cell, what determines the shape of the response trajectory, gradual (continuous, tracks a slowly moving stable branch), abrupt (a discontinuous jump), or reversible (returns to its original state on forcing removal) versus hysteretic (stays in the new state and needs a reverse forcing past a lower threshold)? The residual is R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| measured per circulation slot, never as a global average (Fig 1 :19, :22). The ground that settles a real case is observation, not model agreement.

## Verdict in one line

The three adjectives form a union of overlapping conditions with different determinants (Fig 9 :54-57), not a partition with one winner. Continuity and abruptness are governed by the sign of the dominant feedback cycle against a threshold (Fig 12 :121, Fig 12 :87); reversibility is governed by whether the system has one basin or competing basins, that is, by closure and the wall that only import moves (Fig 6 :14, :23); the rate of forcing is a third, non-reducible determinant (Fig 4 :30, Fig 7). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. No figure line returns the numerical threshold, the dominant feedback in a specific model, or whether a particular observed change is reversible. That is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by likelihood of agreement.
- No bulk-send plan.

The reason is figure-grounded, not merely legal: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention" (Seat Map :163). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes agreement worthless. Public opt-in venues are used instead.
