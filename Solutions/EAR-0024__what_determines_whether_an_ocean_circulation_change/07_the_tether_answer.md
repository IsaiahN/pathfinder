# The Tether's answer on gradual, abrupt and reversible ocean circulation change

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks what determines whether a circulation change is gradual, abrupt or reversible. That residual is multiplace (state q, forcing level F, forcing rate dF/dt, history, noise) and indexed per circulation slot, basin, depth and region (Fig 1 :19, :22). Aggregated into a single global curve, the live signal that says where to look disappears. The three adjectives are not competing answers on one dial: they are a union of overlapping conditions with different determinants (Fig 9 :55, :57).

## 2. The shape

1. **The question is a union, not a partition.** Continuity, path dependence and rate are three conditions; a case in two conditions is categorically different from either alone (Fig 9 :57). Do not answer with one adjective, and do not average the conditions into a single tipping likelihood.

2. **Gradual versus abrupt is set by feedback cycle sign against a threshold.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A stabilising loop damps the perturbation and the circulation tracks the forcing; a self-amplifying loop compounds a remainder (Fig 13 :32) and, at a comparison F ⋛ F_crit (Fig 12 :87), jumps. The ocean names are the salt-advection positive feedback and the salt-redistribution negative feedback, recorded as bindings from the ocean frame, not derived primitives.

3. **Reversible versus hysteretic is set by basin structure.** With one basin the original state is reachable and the system returns on forcing removal. With two basins the current closure cannot reach it internally; reversal is the wall-moving import (Fig 6 :14, :23; Fig 8 :32). Stommel 1961: the jumped state *"would then stay there even when A was restored to its original value."* "Import" here is a reachability shape, not a physical substance.

4. **Rate is a third, non-reducible determinant.** Fast forcing can tip without crossing a critical level and fast reversal can tip on the way back (Ritchie et al. 2023; Chapman et al. 2024). Lenton et al. 2008: *"Both the timescale and the magnitude of forcing are important."* So "the tipping point" is not one well-defined number once rate is admitted.

5. **Verdicts are per slot.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A basin-mean curve can look gradual while individual convection sites jump and fail to return (van Nes and Scheffer 2005, re-anchored per Fig 4 :20).

6. **The ground is observation.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Model intercomparison is a budget filter, not a witness: agreeing models usually share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). The owed instrument is a long, sustained observing system (RAPID at 26°N, SAMBA at 34°S, Argo, plus paleo proxies).

7. **The specific numbers are a `gap`.** No figure returns a freshwater threshold, a critical rate, the dominant feedback in a named model, or a yes/no on the reversibility of today's AMOC (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the three adjectives are mutually exclusive, and not that one wins.
- Not a numerical threshold, a critical rate, or a collapse date.
- Not that today's AMOC is or is not reversible, and not that any observed change has already tipped.
- Not that model agreement settles the case, and not that early-warning estimators (variance, autocorrelation) are reliable. van Westen et al. 2024 find them sensitive to the time window, and Ben-Yami et al. 2024 find the uncertainties too large for reliable tipping-time extrapolation.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the relevant resolution pays for any of these claims.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (canonical mapping): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Discriminator 1:** a sustained observing system long enough to measure whether the circulation returns to its prior basin after a natural perturbation. This is the RAPID/SAMBA/Argo role, and van Westen et al. 2024 explicitly say current reanalyses are too short.
- **Discriminator 2:** a physics-based observable that tracks feedback strength rather than fitted variance, for example FovS at 34°S (van Westen et al. 2024), checked against an independent product.
- **Discriminator 3:** a controlled reversal experiment (hosing then unhosing, repeating across rates) that measures the reverse threshold and the critical rate in a model whose closure differs from the CMIP pool.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the five conditions in section 4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The determinants (cycle sign, basin structure, rate axis, per-slot indexing, non-updating anchor) are held. The specific threshold, rate and reversibility are not, and the framework cannot name them because naming would mean already holding the discriminating instrument. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39); "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity: a time series that looks like a gradual weakening is not thereby shown to be one.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The three adjectives are not three answers to one question. They are a union of conditions with different determinants, and one change can be in two at once. Whether a change is gradual or abrupt is set by the sign of the dominant feedback loop against a threshold: stabilising loops track the forcing, amplifying loops jump. Whether it is reversible is set by basin structure: with one basin the system returns, with two it needs a reverse forcing past a lower threshold. The rate of forcing is a third determinant, because fast forcing can tip without crossing a critical level and fast reversal can tip on the way back. All of this must be judged per circulation cell and depth, not from a single global average. The framework returns that shape, not a numerical threshold or a yes/no on today's AMOC; only sustained observation can settle those. It states five failure conditions in advance, and it stops honestly where the instruments stop.
