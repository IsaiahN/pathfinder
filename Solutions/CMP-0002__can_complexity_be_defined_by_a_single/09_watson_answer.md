## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether one measurable quantity can define complexity across systems. Option A: collapse the readings into one scalar. Option B: keep different readings separate, each with its own currency and anchor, and count two readings as two measures only at independence. What each leads to: A gives a cheap ranking but loses where a difference lives, mixes units, and lets a frame score itself; B gives a checkable measure shape with refuters written in advance and declares a `gap` where the figures cannot name a universal index. The targeted answer is a measure shape, not a number.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The demand for one scalar across systems. Arity: at least four conditions (description cost, contact cost, arrangement, per-slot residual), so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."), not one hard question. Symmetry: the comparison between two measures is decided by independence, not by equality of output (Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."). Scale: per component, per system, per level, with recursive composition (Fig 12 :103 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible.").
- **Domain 1, representation.** Actors: library Γ, atoms, candidate term φ, declared code. Currency: bits (description length). Anchor: the code and the MDL bargain, settled against the ground. Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 5 and Fig 12.
- **Domain 2, contact and settling.** Actors: ground, instruments, actions, per-slot residual R. Currency: actions (cost of finding out whether a composition holds). Anchor: the ground, which does not update on the frame (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."). Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 2.
- **Domain 3, structure and arrangement.** Actors: atoms, bonds, molecules, networks, gradients. Currency: typed relations and arrangement. Anchor: behaviour differences that an arrangement makes (Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."). Verdict: `specified` for the isomer claim, triangulated for the figure definition, unmeasured at the ground.
- **Domain 4, verification and cross-system comparison.** Actors: measures, frames, seats. Currency: independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 8.
- **Union, not partition.** One lever clears single-condition regions and leaves intersections standing. Verdicts per domain are never averaged. **Order by dependency:** describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"); settle representation and contact before pricing arrangement; refuse before mint (Formula step 2); check independence throughout (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

Strain test applied: Fig 13's closed primitive set and Fig 12's priced arrangements govern different objects (kinds versus arrangements), so no strain after re-census. One open empirical strain remains: whether any two proposed complexity measures are genuinely independent is a question for the ground, not for the figures.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Complexity cannot be defined by a single measurable quantity. The figures govern the shape directly: a composition is priced in two currencies that do not add, and merging them is the named error; the primitive axes are not readings of one quantity and count as two only at independence; the residual is per slot and aggregation destroys the where-to-look signal; a frame cannot score itself. What the figures license is a general *form* of measurement with a per-system, provenanced binding, not a universal index. The universal scalar is a declared `gap` of the unreachable kind, not a proof of impossibility everywhere. The isomer reading is real only over a shared atom set with a changed bond; otherwise the weaker functional-group reading stands. Mode `general` on structure, `specified` on bindings, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, four refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a scalar ranking instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is importing a wrong atom across frames, which a source gate and shadow test are meant to prevent.
- **undoable:** yes. A binding can be re-provenanced, an axis can be split, a union compound can be unbundled, and a refuted condition can be withdrawn without touching the others. The framework deletes nothing: a refusal removes the standing of a term to be composed over, not the term itself. No primitive has to be discarded.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument or proof showing description cost and settling cost to be one quantity in one unit with no loss; a scalar that preserves per-slot residuals and is settled by a ground outside the measuring frame; a demonstration that two proposed measures draw on genuinely disjoint evidence yet remain redundant; or an imported primitive, with source gate and shadow test, that makes the currencies commensurable. Any one fires a pre-registered refuter and moves the verdict. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
