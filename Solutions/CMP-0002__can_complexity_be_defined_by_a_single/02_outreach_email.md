**Subject:** Check one output about measuring complexity before you decide if the theory matters

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about complexity measures. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Can one measurable quantity define complexity across systems, or must a report keep different readings separate? Option A collapses everything into one number. Option B keeps the readings apart and asks which of them are genuinely independent. What each leads to: A gives a cheap ranking but loses where a difference lives and mixes units; B says where a difference lives and what would settle it, at the cost of refusing a single global score.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain measures, `gap` on a universal index):** the figures say a composed system has at least two prices that do not add. "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them." They also say the minimum set's members "are not three readings of one quantity," and that "the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." So the shape of an admissible complexity report is: keep the currencies separate, keep readings per slot, treat two readings as two measures only when they draw on different evidence, and range each against a ground that does not move when the frame thinks. A universal scalar is `gap`, not a missing measurement to keep hunting for.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that entropy, Kolmogorov complexity, Φ, or any single index is useless for a narrow declared purpose; not that the six-term minimum set is nature's own list of complexity axes (the framework says it is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"); not that anyone who disagrees with the shape has failed to understand it. Nothing here is a ground reading. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. Show a quantity that prices description and settling in the same unit for the same system, preserving both readings with no loss.
> 2. Show two proposed complexity measures drawing on exactly the same evidence yet carrying distinct, non-redundant information.
> 3. Show a scalar that preserves the per-slot location of a live residual and is settled by a ground outside the measuring frame.
> 4. Produce an instrument, with provenance, that pays for a universal scalar across two genuinely different systems.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The formula: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a complexity question you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get (general, specified, or grounded). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
