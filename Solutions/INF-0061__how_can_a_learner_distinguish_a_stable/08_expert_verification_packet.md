# Expert verification packet - the Tether on stable pattern versus coincidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a statistical-learning researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal number. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A learner cannot distinguish a stable pattern from a coincidence with one score. The residual must be read per candidate regularity. Inside one regime a candidate is stable only when three conditions hold: its per-slot residual trends down; its description-length bargain pays, so it is not a fit to one occasion; and the ground settles it against a contact that does not move when the belief moves, after which something can compose over it. The shrink test is opposite-signed, so a held term whose residual persists and does not trend down is the one to refuse. Across a changing environment there is no standing list of stable patterns, and the ground does not decay while the channel does. The numerical threshold is a declared `gap`; the framework returns a shape and a refutable separator, never the number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 5

- **Statement:** the decision is per slot, and a coincidence is the one-occasion fit that fails the description-length bargain while a held term with a flat, persistent residual is the refusal target.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 1 :19` - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* `Fig 1 :22` - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* `Fig 5 :43-44` - *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* `Fig 5 :60` - *"The selector asks which residual is improving; this asks which one keeps being wrong and is not."*
- **What to check:** do the four quoted lines say what is claimed, and does the opposite-sign reading of the shrink test follow without forcing it?
- **Refuter:** an aggregating score separates two candidate slots that the per-slot residual cannot; or a term fitting exactly one occasion is settled and composable; or a settled, composable term keeps a residual that persists and does not trend down.

### C2 - ground-facing; checkable by a learning researcher against their own instrument

- **Statement:** stability needs a non-updating anchor and a reachable channel; a plateau in measured residual is not sufficient evidence of a stable world.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 2 :29` - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* `Fig 2 :33` - *"The anchor must not update."* `Fig 10 :24` - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; `Fig 10 :26` - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; `Fig 11 :51` - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** in a drift or concept-drift setting you know, is the stability test a held-out contact that does not update, or a quantity the learner produces itself? Does a saturated metric produce a plateau that was read as stability? Does a pattern stable under one residual stay stable under another?
- **Refuter:** a stability metric that moves with the learner's belief still settles the question; or a standing list of patterns keeps its role across a change of residual; or a saturated or pooled reading still discriminates a regime change.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation of the stop

- **Statement:** no frame is a sealed room, so failure to find recurrence is not proof of absence, and no figure line returns the numerical threshold.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 9 :44` - *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* `Fig 8 :39` - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* `Fig 9 :32` - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; `Fig 13 :134` - *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.
- **Refuter:** derive a universal stability threshold from the figure set alone; or exhibit a sealed room in which exhaustive search proves absence. Either breaks this sub-claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal confidence level, window length, p-value, CUSUM threshold or significance level. That is a declared `gap`, not a hidden number.
- Not that a low residual means stable. The shrink test is opposite-signed and ranks refusal.
- Not that a standing list of patterns survives a change of residual.
- Not that agreement among readers settles anything; by `Fig 2 :40` agreement within one evidence pool is *"disqualifying for a verdict"*.
- Not that bits and actions add into one score (`Fig 12 :125`).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator that does not update on the learner's own reading. From `Fig 2 :29`, that is the anchor condition. Concretely:

1. Does the stability test use a held-out contact, or the same data that fitted the pattern?
2. Does the residual trend down under the candidate, or persist and stay flat?
3. Does the channel still return something after the regime change, or is the plateau a saturated metric?
4. Would the same pattern keep its role after the residual changes?
5. Is there a source-discriminating instrument that separates a stable process from a one-occasion fit, rather than a single score projecting both to the same reading?
