**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the numerical threshold is reported as a `gap` and not as a number (F12 of `01`). The output must survive its own honesty conditions or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One claim about stable patterns and coincidences, with its failure conditions written first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the separator, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the number):** a learner cannot distinguish a stable pattern from a coincidence with a single score. The residual has to be read per candidate regularity, not per batch. A candidate is a stable pattern only when three separate conditions hold: (1) its description-length bargain pays, so stating it plus what it leaves unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes, and it does not merely explain one occasion; (2) the ground settles it, meaning a contact that does not move when the learner's belief moves, and something can then compose over it; (3) the channel to that ground stays reachable while the environment changes, since the ground does not decay but the channel does. A coincidence is the one-occasion fit that fails the first or second condition, or the held term whose residual persists and does not trend down. This is a shape and a separator, not a measurement, and I am not claiming a universal confidence level, window length, or p-value.
>
> **What would refute it (written before I assembled the claim):**
> 1. a term is settled and composable while its residual persists and does not trend down against a non-updating ground;
> 2. a term fitting exactly one occasion is still admitted as a stable, composable regularity;
> 3. the learner's own output, or the data used to fit the pattern, is used as the stability test;
> 4. a standing list of patterns keeps its role across a change of residual;
> 5. an aggregating score separates two candidate slots that the per-slot residual cannot separate;
> 6. exhaustive search inside one frame establishes that no stable pattern exists.
>
> If any one holds, the separator is wrong in that domain and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have the number that separates stable from coincidence for your problem. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and "unreachable" is a verdict here, not a silence. Nobody has measured the threshold in this note.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
