# The Tether's answer: stable pattern versus coincidence in a changing environment

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separator and its refuters, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal numerical threshold. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A learner predicts an observation per slot and sees an outcome. In a changing environment the same departure can be a regularity the ground will pay for, or a fit to one occasion that will not recur. The residual is the learner's inability to decide which before committing a term. `Fig 9 :4` says *"First: is there a fact here?"* and adds that disagreements which do not shrink with effort are *"not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The question is a **union of two conditions with different anchors**, not one threshold (Fig 9 :54-57). Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, term management inside one regime (`specified`, triangulated).**

1. **Read the residual per candidate regularity, not per batch.** `Fig 1 :19`: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* `Fig 1 :22`: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A coincidence in one slot must not cancel a stable pattern in another.
2. **A stable pattern pays the description-length bargain; a coincidence is the one-occasion fit that fails it.** `Fig 5 :43-44`: *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* `Fig 5 :45`: *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978"*.
3. **The shrink test is opposite-signed.** `Fig 5 :60`: *"The selector asks which residual is improving; this asks which one keeps being wrong and is not."* A held term whose residual *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* (`Fig 5 :59`) is the one to refuse; a stable assimilation is one after which residuals trend down. Note the object: this ranks refusal, not acceptance.
4. **Stable means settled and composable, two conditions.** `Fig 5 :70`: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one."* `Fig 5 :72`: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."*

**D2, regime change and habitat (`specified`, triangulated).**

5. **There is no standing list of stable patterns.** `Fig 11 :51`: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* `Fig 11 :49`: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* `Fig 10 :72`: *"When the question changes, the room should change."*
6. **The ground does not decay; the channel does.** `Fig 10 :24`: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; `Fig 10 :26`: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. A plateau in measured residual may be a saturated channel, not a stable world.
7. **A regime crossing is lossy and must be round-tripped.** `Fig 4 :30`: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

**D3, verification and outreach (`general` structural, single-source on the figure side).**

8. **Agreement is not a verdict.** `Fig 7 :35`: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* `Fig 2 :35`: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so `Fig 2 :40` applies: agreement here is *"worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.

**The separator, stated once.** A candidate regularity is called stable only when all three hold: a per-slot residual that trends down; a settlement by a ground that does not update, after which the term can be composed over; and a channel still reachable through the regime change. A coincidence is the one-occasion fit that fails the bargain, or the held term whose residual persists and does not trend down. The threshold itself is owed from a ground reading, not from the figures.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal confidence level, window length, p-value or hazard rate. `Fig 9 :32`: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* This is a declared `gap` (F12), reported as unreached, not silence.
- Not that a low residual means stable. The shrink test is opposite-signed and points at refusal (F2, S1).
- Not that a standing list of patterns survives change (F6).
- Not that the learner can certify its own completeness. `Fig 9 :44`: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*
- Not that bits and actions add into one score. `Fig 12 :125`: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement, experiment, or dataset was used by this agent.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator that does not update on what the learner thinks, not a further argument. `Fig 2 :29`: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Standing refuters from `01` section 5, any of which withdraws the separator in its domain:

1. a settled, composable term whose residual persists and does not trend down;
2. a one-occasion fit admitted as stable;
3. the learner's own output used as the stability metric;
4. a standing list of patterns surviving a change of residual;
5. an aggregate separating slots that the per-slot residual cannot;
6. search inside one closure establishing absence;
7. one additive score correctly ordering both bit-cost and action-cost cases.

Candidate instruments from the world literature are imports, not verdicts: MDL description length (Rissanen 1978), stable/reactive drift detectors with false-positive bounds (Gama et al. 2014; Bifet and Gavaldà 2007), transitional-probability learning in infants (Saffran et al. 1996, as reported by Aslin and Newport 2012), the garden of forking paths (Gelman and Loken), bee persistence sampling (Dunlap et al. 2017), and mismatch negativity as a deviance detector (Wacongne et al. 2012). Each needs a source gate and a shadow test (`Fig 8 :51`), and each is debited against the independence of its frame (`Fig 6 :24`).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** `Fig 13 :134`: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; `Fig 8 :39`: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The framework fixes the shape of the separator and the order of its tests, then stops where the instruments cannot distinguish, recording the lineage. `Fig 9 :42`: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."* The six-term set is a visible guess, not nature's own list; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number that separates a stable pattern from a coincidence, and it does not claim one. It says the decision is a union of two conditions with different anchors. Inside one regime a candidate is stable only if its per-slot residual trends down, its description-length bargain pays (so it is not a fit to one occasion), and the ground settles it so something can compose over it; the shrink test is opposite-signed, so the held term whose residual persists and stays flat is the one to refuse. Across a changing environment there is no standing list of stable patterns, and the ground does not decay while the channel can, so the learner must keep the channel reachable and round-trip any cross-regime comparison. Agreement among readers is not a verdict, and the sought-for shape must be written down and refusable before any detector is run. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, seven failure conditions written in advance, and the instruments that would close the gap. The number stays open until a ground settles it.
