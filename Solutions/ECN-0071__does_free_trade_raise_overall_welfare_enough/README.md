# Tether outreach package: ECN-0071

**Question:** Does free trade raise overall welfare enough to justify concentrated losses among workers and regions?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 96 (question number 71, section 8: Trade, globalization, and industrial policy)

**Mode:** `specified` on the structural re-framing, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the normative "enough to justify" comparison. No measurement or argument in this folder settles the interpersonal trade-off, so nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## Restating the question as an effect

Option A: aggregate welfare rises under freer trade, and the size of that aggregate surplus is "enough" to outweigh the losses concentrated in particular worker groups and regions, so policy may proceed. Option B: the aggregate and the per-slot losses are different quantities in different currencies and different slots, the figures forbid aggregating the second into the first, and "enough to justify" has no nameable anchor in the figures, so the question is a union compound that must be split rather than answered with one number. What an answer would look like is bound here as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :66): a shape plus refuters plus an open slot, not a yes/no verdict on aggregate surplus.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with a position on trade.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, in the figures' own words: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict" (Fig 7 :35) and "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make its agreement worthless, and "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong" (Fig 2 :42).
