## 0. The claim under audit

> Question 71: "Does free trade raise overall welfare enough to justify concentrated losses among workers and regions?"

Stated in effect terms:

- **Option A (the aggregate claim).** Freer trade raises total welfare. The total is large enough that the losses it leaves in particular places and occupations are acceptable. The practical consequence is that policy proceeds and the concentrated losses are treated as a price already paid.
- **Option B (the slot claim).** "Overall welfare" is an aggregate over slots. Each displaced worker and each declining region is a slot with its own live residual. The figures forbid letting an aggregate settle a live slot residual. "Enough to justify" is a comparison between two quantities that the framework does not put in one currency and for which it supplies no anchor. The practical consequence is that the question splits: measure the aggregate and the slots separately, and treat the normative comparison as unreachable here.

What an answer would look like, written down before the run (formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :66 "Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."): a shape that any answer must have, a list of refuters, and an explicit open slot. Not a single number and not a yes/no on "enough".

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** Two conditions plus many slots: one aggregate quantity and N per-slot residuals. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Symmetry.** The aggregate treats slots as interchangeable (sum or average is symmetric under permutation of workers and regions). The slot residuals are not interchangeable: they sit in named places and occupations. The question assumes these are commensurable, so that "enough" can compare them. Fig 1 :19 says no: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Scale.** First order is the direct price and production effect. Second and third order are the community, health, and identity effects that follow the job loss. Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The aggregate instrument reads first order; the residual that matters may be third order.

### One line per domain, with actor, currency and anchor

1. **Domain 1, the aggregate welfare frame.** Actors: consumers, producers, firms, the representative agent, national accounts. Currency: surplus, GDP, efficiency, a single number. Anchor claim: the market outcome, measured as total surplus. Failure of anchor status: the frame produces the metric it uses to score itself. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." An aggregate surplus that is defined inside the frame cannot be the anchor that settles the frame.
2. **Domain 2, the per-slot distributional frame.** Actors: displaced workers, their households, regions, communities, local institutions. Currency: income, employment, health, community continuity. Anchor: the ground for each slot, meaning observable outcomes for those people and places. This is a live residual domain. Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".
3. **Domain 3, the justification frame.** Actors: the polity, policymakers, ethicists. Currency: legitimacy, fairness, justice. Anchor: **none nameable in the figures.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 allows human judgment as an anchor only for "is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people". It does not supply an anchor for aggregating welfare across persons or for trading one person's loss against another's gain. So this domain is a **gap**, not a silent yes.

### Partition or union

Not a partition. It is a union of overlapping conditions: the aggregate is built out of the same people whose slot residuals it proposes to override, so the domains overlap at the persons. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever that raises the average leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The per-slot observations are upstream; the aggregate is a further transformation of them. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." If the slot link is broken by aggregation, no amount of refinement at the aggregate level recovers the lost signal. Dependency order is therefore: (2) slot ground, then (1) aggregate, with (3) a separate compound that cannot be computed from the other two because it has no anchor. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question aggregates the very signal it needs (decisive for the re-framing)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."
Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"

The question's grammar is "overall welfare" versus "concentrated losses". The first is precisely an aggregate over slots; the second is precisely a per-slot residual. Fig 1 says a global residual near zero with a live slot is a legal state, so a positive national total is compatible with a live loss in a particular region. The question then asks whether the total is "enough" to justify the slot. That is the averaging operation Fig 1 :22 disqualifies. Mode: `specified`, figure-derived, contradicted by no figure. Refuter: show a case where the framework admits that a per-slot residual is discharged by a global number alone, with no per-slot measurement and no settlement; that breaks Fig 1 :19, :22 and :28 at once.

Status: **triangulated across Fig 1 (agent architecture) and Fig 2 (anchor theory)**, two figures with different subject matter. Fig 2 :29 says an anchor must not change its answer because of what a frame thinks; an aggregate surplus computed inside the welfare frame is exactly such a frame-dependent quantity.

### F2: Hypothetical compensation is not settlement

Fig 5, settlement: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." (THE_FORMULA.md step 5; this is a formula line, not a figure line.)
Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

The standard aggregation defence is that winners could in principle compensate losers, so no one need lose. Kaldor-Hicks, as recorded in the world source, explicitly does not require compensation actually to be paid. Under the formula, a merely possible compensation never pays, so the slot residual is not settled. It remains a live `R⁺_s`. Calling it "welfare" does not change its status. Mode: `specified`. Refuter: produce a payment that actually reaches the affected slot and is accepted against that slot's ground, or show a figure line that counts counterfactual compensation as settlement; none exists.

Status: **triangulated across the formula step 5 and Fig 2 :45**. World corroboration from the Kaldor-Hicks source is secondary and shares the welfare-economics evidence pool.

### F3: The concentrated losses are a habitat cascade, not a first-order line item

Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

An aggregate surplus measured at first order does not register a residual that appears three steps out in a region. Displacement changes contact for households, schools, health and local institutions; adding a total-surplus number does not change that contact. So the concentrated losses cannot be waved away by a measure that never touches their habitat. Mode: `specified`. Refuter: show that the slot residual is fully captured by the first-order aggregate instrument, meaning no second or third-order effect remains; an empirical claim, not a figure claim.

Status: **triangulated across Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 1 (slot residual)**, two figures with low overlap. World corroboration: Autor, Dorn and Hanson (AER 2013), reported in `09_watson_answer.md`.

### F4: "Enough to justify" has no anchor, so the verdict is gap

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The comparison needs a rule for weighing one person's loss against another's gain. The figures provide an anchor for judging an answer to a person, but not an interpersonal aggregation rule, and none of the six substrate terms or eight operators supplies one. This is not silence. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." Mode: `general` on the shape of the gap, `specified` on the claim that no figure line governs. Refuter: exhibit a verbatim figure line that states a social welfare function, a distributional weight, or a rule that trades one slot's loss against another slot's gain. If none can be exhibited, the gap stands.

Status: **single-source for the anchor rule (Fig 2), triangulated with Fig 8 on the "unreachable is a verdict" instruction.** Recorded as `gap`, not as no.

### F5: The ⋛ comparison is mistyped

Operators table :49: "a threshold relation between two quantities". (This is a table line, not a Fig N :line citation.)
Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

"Overall welfare ⋛ concentrated losses" applies a threshold relation to two operands that are not two quantities of the same kind: one is a sum across slots, the other is a set of slot residuals. Under Fig 12 the arrangement matters and cannot be read off the ingredients; collapsing the set of slots into one operand has already lost the bond structure. So the "enough" comparison is a type error before it is an empirical question. Mode: `specified`. Refuter: define both operands as quantities in one currency at one scale and show a threshold between them that preserves slot identity; the burden is on the claim, not on the refusal.

Status: **single-source for the bond definition (Fig 12 and Operators table), corroborated in a different field by Simpson's paradox and ecological fallacy (world sources), which are the same aggregation failure in statistics.**

### F6: A rejected strain, recorded rather than hidden

Fig 12 :126 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

This line was tried as a direct statement that surplus-efficiency and distributional-loss are two non-addable currencies. **Retracted on strain test.** Fig 12 :126 is about bits (cost of a description) versus actions (cost of finding out whether it holds), not about welfare dimensions. Using it here would make the figure contradict itself with the wrong quantity, which the canonical README says means the domain was misidentified. The right home for the non-addability claim is F1 and F4, not Fig 12 :126. Nothing was deleted; the attempt and its refusal are recorded.

## 4. Strain test, re-census and corrections

- **Strain 1, Fig 12 :126.** Tried and rejected; see F6. Re-census moved the non-addability claim to Fig 1 and Fig 2.
- **Strain 2, Fig 2 :37.** Initially read as supplying the anchor for "justify". Re-census: it is anchor only for "is this a good answer for a person". "Justify free trade despite concentrated losses" is not that question; it aggregates across persons. Narrowed to a gap. Correction recorded here, not silently erased.
- **Strain 3, Fig 1 :13.** Initially read as claiming aggregate welfare is near zero. Re-census: the "global R" is the per-slot prediction residual, not welfare. The line supports compatibility of a healthy global measure with a live slot, not a claim about the size of trade gains. Narrowed accordingly.
- **Strain 4, "scarcity makes the question real".** Considered importing a claim that because losses are concentrated and gains diffuse, political economy supplies the missing anchor. Rejected: political economy is a frame inside the same pool, and Fig 2 :35 says "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." No import was made.
- After re-census: **one live strain (F4, the anchor gap), named not hidden; three corrections recorded.**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before any outreach, per Fig 5 :66 and Fig 5 :67 "Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A verbatim figure line is exhibited that states a social welfare function, a distributional weight, or a rule that settles one slot's loss by another slot's gain. That closes the gap in F4.
2. A figure line is exhibited under which a global residual discharges a live per-slot residual with no per-slot measurement or settlement. That breaks F1.
3. A ground reading shows that the concentrated losses are fully captured at first order, with no second or third-order habitat effect. That breaks F3.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the type error, not with a number. Ask "which slot is this residual in?" before "how big is the total?".
2. State the mode on every sentence. Do not present the re-framing as a verdict on trade policy.
3. Keep the three domains separate and never average their verdicts into one yes/no.
4. Put the gap plainly: the figures do not supply the normative anchor. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."
5. Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Send the slot residual and its refuter, then the links if the reader is curious.

## 8. Recursive derivation summary

1. **Is there one question?** No. Fig 9 :5 splits it into aggregate and per-slot conditions. Union, not partition.
2. **Which figure governs the aggregate move?** Fig 1 :19. Aggregation indexes away the live slot.
3. **Does possible compensation settle the slot?** No. Formula step 5 requires what pays.
4. **Are the losses first order?** Not necessarily. Fig 11 :52 puts the material residual three steps out.
5. **What is the "enough" operator?** ⋛, a threshold between two quantities. The operands are not two quantities of a kind. Type error (F5).
6. **Which frame holds the missing normative term?** None in the figures. Fig 2 :29 requires an anchor and none is nameable. Gap (F4).
7. **Route?** Describe the residual, split the union, leave the slot open, record the lineage. No import, no mint, no verdict beyond the shape.

## 9. Updated verdict

The shape survives: per-slot residuals are not discharged by an aggregate, compensation in principle is not settlement, and the concentrated losses are a habitat cascade whose material residual may be several steps out. The specific request for a yes/no on "enough to justify" does not survive: it is a type error at the comparison and a gap at the anchor. Strains after re-census: one live gap (F4), named; three corrections recorded above; no hidden strain.
