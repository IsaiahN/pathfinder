# Union, order and isomers in ECN-0071

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the analogy.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether one aggregate quantity is "enough" to justify a set of concentrated losses. Two structures are being compared: a sum over all slots and a set of slot-indexed residuals. This file asks three supporting questions: (1) is the problem a partition or a union, (2) what is the dependency order, and (3) under Fig 12, are the aggregate frame and the slot frame isomers, and what would falsify that.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

Fig 9 :5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The aggregate and the slot conditions overlap: the same people appear in both. The aggregate is built from them, so a case can be in both conditions at once. That is a union with an intersection, not a partition. The consequence: a single lever that raises the total leaves the intersection standing, because the slot condition still binds. Falsifier: exhibit a framing in which the aggregate and the slot losses are disjoint sets of persons, so a partition is correct; then the union claim fails. This is empirical, not figure-derived. Triangulated across Fig 9 and the *Library of Babel* essay's bundling passage, different fields but one author pool.

### Q2: Dependency order

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Order: slot ground first, aggregate second, normative comparison third and separate. The aggregate consumes the slot measurements; it cannot repair a slot link that was never measured. A policy that improves the aggregate instrument while the slot channel is broken is effort spent above the break. Falsifier: show a case where aggregate refinement alone closes a live slot residual without any slot measurement. That breaks Fig 3 :19 and Fig 1 :22 together.

### Q3: Are the two frames isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- The aggregate frame and the slot frame are built from the **same atoms**: the same persons and regions.
- They differ in the **bond**: the aggregate frame bonds them with + into one sum; the slot frame keeps them indexed, each with its own residual.
- By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance (a total versus a distribution). Reading one off the other is the flat-formula error Fig 12 names.
- The weaker claim is a **functional group**: both frames are `persons – bond – welfare reading`, with different qualifiers. Under Fig 12 :124 the group fixes the kind, not the member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either frame requires an atom the other does not, for example a representative agent needed only by the aggregate frame, or a community-continuity atom needed only by the slot frame, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. Also falsified if the bond is the same in both and only the labels differ, which would make them one frame with two names (Fig 8). Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the falsifier stated so the claim is checkable.

### Q4: What is the "enough" operator?

Operators table :49: "a threshold relation between two quantities". (Table line, not a Fig N :line citation.)
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

"Enough to justify" is a ⋛ comparison. Its left operand is a sum across slots; its right operand is a set of slot residuals. ⋛ requires two quantities; a set indexed by slot is not one quantity once the indexing is respected, and flattening it into a single number destroys the arrangement that Fig 12 :94 says carries the substance. So the comparison is mistyped before it is measured. Falsifier: define both operands as one quantity in one currency at one scale while preserving slot identity. Burden of proof is on the comparison, not on the refusal.

### Q5: Analogues from other fields

These corroborate; they do not decide.

- **Simpson's paradox** (statistics): a trend in each group reverses when groups are combined. Same structure as Fig 1 :22.
- **Ecological fallacy** (statistics and sociology): "A group-level relationship does not automatically characterize the relationship at the level of the individual." Same structure as Fig 1 :19.
- **Kaldor-Hicks** (welfare economics): possible compensation stands in for actual compensation. Same structure as formula step 5's "A gate is not the ground."
- **Prospect theory** (cognitive science): "Losses hurt more than gains feel good". The comparison is asymmetric even before the type error.

Overlap: the first two share a statistics pool, the third shares the welfare-economics pool, the fourth is low-overlap. They are witnesses to the shape, not sources of the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The problem is a union with an intersection, so it must be unbundled before search. Dependency runs from slot ground to aggregate to a separate normative comparison. The aggregate and the slot frames are isomers over the same persons under different bonds, with a stated falsifier. The "enough" comparison is mistyped under the Operators table.
