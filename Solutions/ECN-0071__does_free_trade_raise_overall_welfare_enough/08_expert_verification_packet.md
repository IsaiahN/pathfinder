# Expert verification packet: aggregate gain versus concentrated loss

**What this is:** a claim in a form an international-trade researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on "enough to justify". Not `grounded`.** No welfare measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result about trade policy.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does overall welfare rise enough to justify concentrated losses" applies a threshold comparison to two operands that are not two quantities of a kind. One is an aggregate over all slots; the other is a set of slot-indexed residuals living in particular workers and regions. The figures state that the residual is indexed by slot and not aggregated, so a national total cannot discharge a live slot without a per-slot measurement and a settlement at the ground. Possible compensation is not payment. The concentrated losses may sit two or three steps out in a community cascade that a first-order aggregate instrument never reads. The normative rule that would let one person's loss be offset by another's gain is not supplied by any figure line, so that part of the question is recorded as a gap. The packet offers the shape, three written refuters and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1, framework internal; checkable by reading Figure 1
- **Statement:** a per-slot residual is not discharged by a global total.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".
- **Refuter:** exhibit a figure line under which a global residual settles a live slot with no slot measurement and no ground payment; or show that "overall welfare" is not an aggregate over slots. Either breaks C1.

### C2, framework internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 5
- **Statement:** hypothetical compensation is not settlement, and the "enough" comparison has no anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The settlement rule in `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5 is "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." (a formula line, not a figure line).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a verbatim figure line that states a social welfare function, a distributional weight, or a rule settling one slot's loss by another's gain. That closes the gap and breaks C2.

### C3, empirical-facing; checkable by a trade or labour economist against the literature
- **Statement:** the concentrated losses form a habitat cascade whose material residual can be two or three steps out, and they are not fully read by a first-order aggregate instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
- **What to check:** local labour-market evidence on whether import exposure leaves residuals in unemployment, participation, wages and transfer payments, and whether second and third-order community and health effects remain after first-order adjustment. The published anchor is Autor, Dorn and Hanson, *American Economic Review* 103(6), 2013, which reports higher unemployment, lower participation and reduced wages in exposed local markets and sharply higher transfer payments. That is a secondary world source, not a figure line.
- **Refuter:** a ground reading showing the concentrated losses are fully captured at first order with no beyond-first-order residual. That breaks C3, and the cascade claim is withdrawn.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Slot instrument:** measure whether a named region with a positive national total still carries a live residual in employment, participation, health and community continuity. That is read at the slot.
2. **Cascade test:** does the slot residual have second and third-order effects, or is it fully captured at first order?
3. **Anchor test:** is any figure line produced that states a distributional weight or a settlement rule between slots? If yes, C2 is refuted and the gap closes.
