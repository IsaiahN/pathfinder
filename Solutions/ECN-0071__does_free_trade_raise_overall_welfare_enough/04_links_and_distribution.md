## Wider literature that bears, and what it does not do

1. **Autor, Dorn and Hanson (2013)** is the empirical anchor for the concentrated-loss slot. It is a ground reading of local labor markets, and its result is compatible with a positive national total. It corroborates F3 but does **not** settle F4, because it does not supply a normative weighting rule.
2. **Kaldor-Hicks** is the standard defence that gains "could" compensate losses. The source states the compensation need not occur. Under the formula, a hypothetical payment is not settlement. This corroborates F2 without being a figure line.
3. **Gains from trade** defines the aggregate as a sum of surpluses. That is exactly the aggregation F1 disqualifies as a way to read a live slot. It corroborates the diagnosis of the question's grammar.
4. **Prospect theory** is the low-overlap witness from human cognition. Losses loom larger than equivalent gains, so even a commensurable comparison would be weighted asymmetrically by people. It corroborates the structural asymmetry in F1 and F5, and is the least likely source to share the trade-economics evidence pool.
5. **Simpson's paradox and ecological fallacy** are low-overlap witnesses from statistics. Both are the same formal failure: a relation at the aggregate level differs from, and can reverse, the relation at the level of the units. They corroborate Fig 1 :19, :22 in a different field. They do not decide the verdict; the figures do.

Overlap declaration: sources 1 to 3 share the welfare-economics evidence pool and count as one witness, not three. Sources 4 and 5 are separate pools, so their agreement is weaker evidence of independence but still only corroboration. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was not triggered.
