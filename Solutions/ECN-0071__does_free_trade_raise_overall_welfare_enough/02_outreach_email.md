**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable output on aggregate gain versus concentrated loss

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does overall welfare rise enough to justify concentrated losses" mixes two different quantities. One is an aggregate over all people and regions. The other is a residual that lives in particular slots, a specific worker, a specific region, a specific community. The figures I am using state that the residual is indexed by slot and not aggregated, and that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. So a positive national total is fully compatible with a live loss in one place. The "enough to justify" comparison then tries to weigh a sum against a set of slots in one number, and no figure line I can find supplies an anchor for that interpersonal trade-off. I record that as a gap, not as an answer.
>
> What I am **not** claiming: that free trade is good or bad, that any particular estimate of gains or losses is right, or that a particular policy follows. I have not opened a welfare dataset in preparing this. The claim is about the shape of the question, not about the size of the numbers.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A figure line that states a rule for settling one person's loss by another person's gain, which would close the gap.
> 2. A figure line under which a global total discharges a live per-slot residual without any per-slot measurement or settlement.
> 3. A ground reading showing the concentrated losses are fully captured at first order, with no second or third-order community or health effect left over.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage protocol

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the three refuters above. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** any framework is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, send the three local links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage sends a personal email, asks for trust, or requests a show of hands.
