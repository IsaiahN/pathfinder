# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter for a public submission through the venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter survive the recipient's own check. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about "independent" H0 measurements before we discuss any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. If it fails, there is nothing else to show you.
>
> **The output (`general` law, `specified` application, not `grounded`):** when two H0 determinations draw on a common evidence pool, their agreement is not independent confirmation. Independence here is not the number of teams or instruments; it is how little the two determinations draw on the same evidence. Two analyses that share a supernova compilation, a Cepheid or TRGB zero point, or a parallax solution can never talk to each other and still agree, because the agreement is the shared pool talking. The figures state the general rule: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). The escape clause is equally important: two frames that reach the same shape by genuinely disjoint evidence do corroborate, at what Fig 8 :50 calls "corroboration at maximum independence".
>
> **What this does not say:** it does not say which H0 value is right, it does not say the tension is or is not new physics, and it does not say any named team made a mistake. It says what a given agreement is worth, and that worth is a function of evidence overlap. Settling the value is a job for an instrument, not a derivation.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A controlled case where two pipelines with measured and removed shared covariance still agree along an axis that tracks the ground.
> 2. A re-census producing a figure line in which overlapping evidence increases the information carried by agreement.
> 3. A demonstration that the shared component cannot be separated from either pipeline even with full provenance.
> 4. An anchor that both teams adjusted in order to agree, which nonetheless settled the question.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would you count as a failure of this claim? What overlap would you need to see measured before you would treat two estimates as independent? If you have a view on that, I will record it before showing you anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures folder, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it; the framework warns that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that simulated outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula". A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]
