## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what causes convergence on a conclusion C while interpretation of C remains in dispute. Option A: one general cause of consensus formation explains the convergence. Option B: a union of overlapping conditions produces the same observable, and C functions as a convention until an unmoved ground settles it. What each leads to: A invites a single-cause story and a recipient list selected for agreement, which manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40). B yields a checkable shape, per-case verdicts that are never averaged, and written refuters.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union. Order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R (the residual).** A conclusion C held in common while its interpretation stays disputed. Arity: at least two frames, one term C, an interpretation relation per frame. Symmetry: the frame-to-C bond is not recoverable from C. Scale: one belief update, a room, a chain of rooms, an institution. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Domain 1, intra-frame belief update.** Actors: a frame with belief b and weighting w. Currency: posterior revision and per-slot residual. Anchor: the frame's own residual against its ground, if any. Question type: one loop; overlaps D2 where frames copy each other's posteriors. Verdict: belief revision explains updating, not agreement.
- **Domain 2, inter-frame convergence.** Actors: rooms A, B, chain rooms t, t+1. Currency: agreement and influence. Anchor: the ground only if pinned. Question type: a **union** of three overlapping bonds, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-58). Verdict: triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 and Fig 8.
- **Domain 3, semantic or interpretive layer.** Actors: conventions, declared meanings, terms. Currency: shared meaning, not truth value. Anchor: none, because a convention makes no claim about the world (Fig 10 :46). Question type: overlaps D2; every converged C is also a semantic object. Verdict: triangulated across Fig 2 :39-41 and Fig 10 :45-47.
- **Domain 4, evidence and instrument layer.** Actors: instruments, shared data, evidence pool. Currency: evidence and independence. Anchor: the ground that does not update (Fig 2 :33, Fig 10 :24). Question type: overlaps D1 and D2. Verdict: triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 8, Fig 13.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, frame selection, carry (Fig 3 :20-23). The break is at link 3: what would count as a verdict rather than a convention. A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing.
- **Strain test applied.** "Interpretive disagreement" initially invited a Fig 12 isomer mapping. Re-census: Fig 12's atoms are framework atoms, not social interpretations. Downgraded to a named analogue, single-source and strained, and excluded from the figure verdicts.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Convergence on a shared conclusion with interpretive disagreement is a compound of three overlapping bonds plus a semantic layer, not one cause. Mutual update converges on influence; a shared pool produces agreement without independent confirmation; only ranging against an anchor that does not update makes agreement evidence. Because the conditions overlap, verdicts are per case and per slot and are never averaged. Persistent interpretive disagreement marks a convention while interpretation is unresolved and no unmoved anchor is settling the case. Fig 2 :41 calls this efficient for a convention, not a failed verdict. The dangerous transition is Fig 10 :47: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one, after which drift is coherent and invisible from inside. This is a `general` shape with `specified` per-domain verdicts, ungrounded and candidate. No single historical cause is named, and none can be named without a ground reading of that case.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape states its three bonds, its diagnostic and its refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording of this derivation before they have recorded a stage-1 verdict. Fig 2 :42 prices that: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The union call, the convention reading and the metric filter are separable from one another. A case-level ground reading can override one bond assignment without withdrawing the others, and no primitive must be removed. Fig 6 :42-43: *"the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not."* The record keeps the earlier reading visible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A non-updating anchor plus independent instruments corrected separately would convert a case from convention to verdict; a fourth convergence bond would break the union census; a scalar measure that distinguishes panel 1 from panel 3 without provenance would break C3; a case where interpretive disagreement fails to shrink under an unmoved anchor would break C2; a Figure 12 re-census that admits the symmetric-bond analogue as a derivation rather than a strained analogue would move the semantic claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
