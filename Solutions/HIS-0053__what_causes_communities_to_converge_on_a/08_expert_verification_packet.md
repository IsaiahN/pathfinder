# Expert verification packet: the Tether on convergence with interpretive disagreement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or sociologist of science, an STS scholar, or a formal epistemologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape and `specified` per domain, not `grounded`.** No historical case was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Convergence on a shared conclusion with persistent interpretive disagreement is a compound, not one phenomenon with one cause. Three bonds can produce the same observed conclusion: mutual update with no anchor, which converges on whoever had the most influence; a shared evidence pool, where frames agree without talking because the pool is talking; and separate ranging against an anchor that does not update. These overlap, so each case must be scored per slot and never averaged. While the interpretation is unresolved and no unmoved anchor is settling the case, the conclusion functions as a convention, and agreement about shared meaning is the product of a convention rather than a corruption of a verdict. It changes kind when it is used to decide an outcome, after which drift becomes coherent and invisible from inside. The packet offers the shape, the diagnostic and the refuters. It does not name the cause of any particular historical consensus.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 7

- **Statement:** the three convergence bonds are distinct and form a union, not a partition. Agreement under a shared pool is not corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :13-15 *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*
  - Fig 2 :19-21 *"they never talk, and still agree"* and *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*
  - Fig 2 :25-27 *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"* and *"both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement"*
  - Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
  - Fig 8 :47 *"Independence is one quantity read twice."*
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a fourth bond not composable from these three, or show a case where all three scores are zero yet convergence persists.

### C2: field-facing; checkable by a historian or STS scholar against a case they know

- **Statement:** persistent disagreement about interpretation is the signature of a convention while no unmoved anchor is settling the case. A convention changes kind when it decides an outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*
  - Fig 10 :46 *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*
  - Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
  - Fig 2 :34 *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **What to check:** take one case of a conclusion held in common with interpretations still in dispute. Was an anchor present that did not move? Did the interpretive disagreement shrink as ground contact increased, or did it persist? Was the conclusion later used as if it settled a verdict?
- **Refuter:** a case of convergence with interpretive disagreement while an unmoved anchor is doing the settling, where the disagreement fails to shrink as ground contact increases.

### C3: measurement-facing; checkable by anyone who works with consensus metrics

- **Statement:** a single consensus score is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. It cannot distinguish influence-driven convergence from anchor-ranged convergence without provenance.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
  - Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** Shwed and Bearman's citation modularity is legitimate as an instrument (Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*), but it reports when internal divisions stopped mattering structurally. Does it, by itself, tell you which bond operated? By the figures it cannot.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a scalar measure that distinguishes panel 1 from panel 3 with no provenance information.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a single cause of consensus, and not a general theory of consensus formation.
- Not that all consensus is a convention. The convention claim is conditional on unresolved interpretation and no settling anchor.
- Not that groupthink, shared evidence, status rivalry, or authority is the cause in any named historical case. Deciding that needs a ground reading.
- Not that boundary objects, trading zones, Aumann's theorem, information cascades, or Kuhnian paradigms were derived from the figures. They are independent corroboration, read from the sources listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not that the cited Wikipedia overviews are primary sources. For Star and Griesemer and for Galison they reproduce the primary text with page numbers; the primary PDFs were not fetched in this session.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement, archive search or experiment was performed for this packet.
- Not a final answer. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*.

## 5. What would settle it

1. An anchor that demonstrably did not move when frames pushed on it (Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."*).
2. Independent instruments corrected separately against that anchor, not two readers of one report (Fig 2 :31-32).
3. A source-discriminating instrument that distinguishes the bonds producing the same conclusion. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*
4. The lineage of each frame's conclusion, so convergent derivation can be told from adopted import (Fig 8 :50-52).
5. Either of the standing falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 firing, which would retract the shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :13-21, :25-27, :39-41 and Fig 10 :46-47. Four short passages, each under three lines.
2. Check C2 against one case you know: was the shared conclusion used as a convention or as a verdict, and did that change over time?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish an anchor-ranged conclusion from a pool-driven conclusion in that case, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
