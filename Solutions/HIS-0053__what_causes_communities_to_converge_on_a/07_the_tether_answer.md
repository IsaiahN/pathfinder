# The Tether's answer: what causes convergence with interpretive disagreement

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape and `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a diagnostic, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No historical case was measured.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

A community holds conclusion C in common while its members persistently disagree about what C means. Arity: at least two frames, one shared term, and an interpretation relation. Symmetry: the frame-to-C bond is not recoverable from C alone. Scale: one belief update, a room, a chain of rooms, an institution. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* So C is diagnosed per case and per slot, never as one field-wide consensus score.

The question as normally posed is an either/or: consensus comes from evidence, or consensus comes from social conformity. Fig 9 :54-58 refuses the either/or. It is a union of overlapping conditions, and a case in two conditions is a specific compound, not a harder case of either.

## 2. The shape: one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

### Domain 1, intra-frame belief update (`specified`, not grounded)

Each frame leans partly on its own history and partly on inherited priors; the weighting w is learnable (Symbols table :24-26). Two frames with identical architecture can diverge because their histories and w diverge. What causes their *convergence* is not a uniform w, but a shared bond to C. Verdict for this domain: the cognitive account explains belief revision, not agreement. It cannot by itself explain why C is shared. Do not let it stand in for the community verdict.

### Domain 2, inter-frame convergence (`triangulated`, specified)

Three bonds produce the same observable:

1. **No anchor, mutual update.** Fig 2 :13-15: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Convergence tracks influence.
2. **One evidence pool.** Fig 2 :19-21: *"they never talk, and still agree"* and *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*. Convergence tracks the pool.
3. **A pinned anchor.** Fig 2 :25-27: *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"* and *"both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement"*. Convergence tracks the ground.

Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* So a room that agrees has produced variation for selection, not a verdict. Verdict for this domain: the union of three bonds is the answer; a single cause is refused.

### Domain 3, the semantic layer (`triangulated`, specified)

This is where the persistence of interpretive disagreement is explained. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*

So while C's interpretation is unresolved and no unmoved anchor is settling it, C functions as a convention. The disagreement is not a defect waiting for more data; it is the signature of what C is currently doing. The danger is the seam crossing: Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*, and then Fig 2 :34 applies: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Verdict for this domain: convention until settled by a ground; flag the moment it is used as a verdict.

### Domain 4, evidence and instruments (`triangulated`, specified)

Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A modularity score or a percentage agreement can decide which cases get tried first; it cannot decide which bond produced them. Verdict for this domain: independence and a non-updating anchor are the only things that convert agreement into evidence, and they must be bought per case.

## 3. The diagnostic to run

For each case, record three scores separately, per slot:

1. **Anchor score:** is there an anchor that did not change its answer because of what any frame thought? Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
2. **Pool score:** how much do the frames draw on the same evidence? Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."*
3. **Influence score:** did the conclusion track an influence ranking rather than either of the above? Fig 2 :14-15.

Then ask what C is doing: shared meaning (convention) or claim about the world (verdict). Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Then check the dependency order of `06_isomers_and_order.md`: perception, vocabulary, objective, then frame selection. Do not book the room before stating what would count as a verdict.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single cause of consensus. The census is a union, not a partition.
- Not that all consensus is a convention. The convention reading holds only while interpretation is unresolved and no unmoved anchor is settling the case (Fig 2 :39-41).
- Not that groupthink, shared evidence, or authority is the answer in any particular historical case. Those are world-side candidates; deciding among them needs a ground reading of the case.
- Not that boundary objects or trading zones were derived from the figures. They are low-overlap corroboration only.
- Not that a consensus metric is a verdict. It is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement, archive or experiment was run in this folder.
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*; a final answer to "why consensus" would be a terminal room.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A case-level ground reading, not a further argument:

1. **Anchor test:** show that the anchor did not move when frames pushed on it. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* If it moved, panel 3 becomes panel 1.
2. **Independence test:** two or more instruments pointed at the same thing, with separately recorded corrections, not two people reading the same report. Fig 2 :31-32.
3. **Source-discriminating instrument:** an instrument that distinguishes the mechanisms that produced the same conclusion C. The *Hard Problem is About Source* example is the spectrometer: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*
4. **Provenance check:** the record of where each frame's conclusion came from. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*
5. **Standing falsifiers:** a fourth convergence bond; a case where interpretive disagreement fails to shrink under an unmoved anchor; a scalar measure that distinguishes panel 1 from panel 3 without provenance. Any one fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape says what any answer must contain: three bonds, a convention layer, a per-slot diagnostic, and a non-updating anchor if agreement is to count as evidence. It does not name which bond produced a given conclusion, because naming it would require a ground reading of that case. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the outreach form: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* *How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the epistemic form: *"indiscernibility is not identity."* The lineage is kept so the stop is checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give one cause of scientific consensus. It says that a community can converge on the same conclusion for three different reasons: frames can update on one another and end up with whoever had the most influence; they can draw on one evidence pool and agree without ever talking, which is the pool talking rather than two independent confirmations; or they can range separately against an anchor that does not move when anyone pushes on it. Only the third makes agreement evidence. Persistent disagreement about what the conclusion means is not noise outside the account: while interpretation is unresolved, the conclusion is doing the work of a convention, and agreement about shared meaning is the product of a convention rather than a corruption of a verdict. The danger starts when that convention is used to decide an outcome, because then it has quietly stopped being a convention. What this offers is the shape any answer must have, a per-case diagnostic, and refuters written in advance. It does not name the cause of any particular historical consensus, because that requires a ground reading of the case, which this derivation does not have.
