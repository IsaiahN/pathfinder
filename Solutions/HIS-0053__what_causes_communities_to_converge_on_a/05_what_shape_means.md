## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For HIS-0053, a **shape** is the narrowed form of the answer that can be stated before one can name a single historical cause. The shape here has four parts:

1. three overlapping convergence bonds (Fig 2);
2. a diagnostic that ranks anchor, pool and influence per case;
3. a semantic layer in which unresolved interpretation marks a convention rather than a failed verdict (Fig 2 :41, Fig 10 :46);
4. a per-slot unit of analysis, never an averaged consensus score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :60).

A name would be "groupthink causes consensus" or "shared evidence causes consensus." The shape refuses both because the census is a union of conditions, not a partition.

*The Library of Babel* also gives the split rule directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* That is Fig 9 :54-58 in narrative form, and it is the single most relevant essay line to this question.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrap by proximity. *"use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of."* Then *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* This is why the residual was stated as "a conclusion held in common with interpretations still in dispute" before any figure line was chosen.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is the rule for the stage-1 email.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* The paradox: *"by the time you learn the EXACT search terms to find the CORRECT book, you won't need to read it anymore, because you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name, and why this question must withhold one

Only two things license a name:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* explains why the shared conclusion alone cannot license an interpretation: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* The color case was closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* So C held in common is the reading; the mechanism that produced it is the source; the reading does not determine the source. That is precisely why interpretive disagreement can persist after convergence.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* makes the same point at the room scale: *"the room will be swayed by negotiation which is persuasion, or by coherent drift (both of you found a middle ground to agree on). The problem is that the room session only produces an outcome that you agree on, having come from diverse frames with different vantage points of the problem. It does not mean that the output was objective truth."* And later: *"All observers have equal weight, but varying interpretations, outlooks and where they focus their attention creating alternate routines."* This is the question, stated in the framework's own narrative vocabulary, before this derivation began.

## 4. The essay that most directly answers the question

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the cleanest statement of the convention/verdict distinction:

> *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources (Fig 2 :40–41)."*

and the drift mechanism:

> *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."*

and the cultural shape:

> *"Culture is why a few words can carry so much between people who share it, and why the same words fail between people who don't."*

So the answer to "what causes convergence despite interpretive disagreement" is not a single social cause. Shared pretext makes the conclusion portable and locally interpretable; it is good for coordination and bad for truth. The interpretive disagreement is what remains when pretext is shared but sources and histories are not identical. This corroborates F2 and F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, but it is an essay, not a figure line, so it cannot decide the verdict.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the fallacy to avoid: *"Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times."* and *"The group's agreement is never the verdict."* It also explains the two collapses in plain terms: *"In the first, agents update on each other until they converge on 'whoever had the most influence.' In the second, they never talk but share one source, so 'the agreement is the pool talking.' Only the ground decides."*

## 5. Two additional constraints from the essays

**Independence is a dial, not a virtue.** *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"Votes come from outcomes, not popularity."* and *"Use the success rate per try, not the total. Whatever gets tried most piles up votes."* This is Fig 9 :60 applied to consensus metrics and is why Shwed and Bearman's modularity is a filter, not a witness. *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: *"a committee regresses toward consensus, and consensus is the average, which is duller than the best member on the thing the best member is best at."*

**The choice of frame is visible, not neutral.** *They're Scanning Books*: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* and *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* Applied here: choosing to study consensus through citation networks, or through interviews, or through archival case studies, selects what can register. None is the ground.

**Provenance is the only discriminator when outputs coincide.** *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."* and *"indiscernibility is not identity."* This is Fig 8 :50-52 in narrative form. Two communities can hold the same conclusion C by entirely different bonds; only the lineage separates them.

## 6. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name" (kept visible)

I first applied *"shape, not a name"* as if it forbade naming any mechanism of consensus formation. That overstates it, and the correction is preserved rather than erased.

- The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- Nothing in this question asks for a missing primitive. All four domains have nameable actors, currencies and anchors. The schema is present; what is withheld is a single cause, because the census is a union.
- So naming "shared evidence pool" or "non-updating anchor" is not forbidden. What is forbidden is claiming that the framework *derives* which one produced a particular historical conclusion. That requires a ground reading of that case, with provenance, which this folder does not have.
- My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: the conclusion C is a reading, and the reading does not determine the source. But the reason is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held mechanisms. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

This matches the correction in the exemplar's `05_what_shape_means.md` §5b and `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1, reached independently for this question.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach and for status

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous names who agree that consensus is good is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a refuter.

**Agreement is not truth at scale.** Same essay: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."* That is Fig 2 panel 1 and Fig 7's mirror chain in one sentence.

**This derivation is a simulation.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The architecture article adds: *"the outputs generated must be seen as simulacra"* and warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* So `01` through `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the claim and must be labelled as such everywhere.

**A convention that decides an outcome is already cited in the architecture article.** Its bibliography entry for Goodhart (1975) reads: *"An instance. Figure 2's instrument clause and Figure 10's warning about a convention that decides an outcome."* That corroborates F3 of `01` from the framework's own provenance record. It is still not a measurement of any historical case.
