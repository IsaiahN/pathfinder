## 1. The residual, stated in effect terms

**R (observed).** A community holds a conclusion C in common while its members persistently disagree about what C means, which mechanism it implies, or how it should be applied. The disagreement is not noise that will wash out with more data; it survives the formation of the conclusion.

- **Arity.** At least two frames, one shared term C, and an interpretation relation from each frame to C. Three or more frames add the room-chain scale (Fig 7).
- **Symmetry.** The bond between a frame and C is not recoverable from C alone. A frame can arrive at C by mutual update, by a shared evidence pool, or by ranging against an anchor. These are different bonds with the same observed contents (Fig 8 :50-52).
- **Scale.** One belief-update step (one frame), a room (a community at one time), a chain (a community across handoffs), an institution (a convention that may decide an outcome).

**What an answer would look like.** A taxonomy of the convergence bonds, a diagnostic that tells which bond is operating in a given case, a verdict on whether C is at that moment a convention or a verdict, and refuters that would break the taxonomy. It is a shape and a route, not a name of one cause.

**Direction bound as an ordinary term (formula step 2, "Direction").** *"convergence with interpretive disagreement"* = a term C held by frames whose closures differ, where the frame-to-C bond is not recoverable from C. Binding the shape lets it accrue a residual like anything else, so the preference is refusable.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union | dependency order (Fig 3) |

**Partition or union.** The three Fig 2 panels form a union of overlapping conditions. A case can be both influence-driven and pool-driven. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* So each historical case gets its own verdict; a single average "consensus cause" is forbidden by the census.

**Order by dependency.** Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* The broken link in this field is link 3, deciding what would count as a verdict rather than a convention. Most measurement effort lands above it on agreement counts and citation modularity.

**Strain test applied during the census.** The phrase "interpretive disagreement" initially tempted a mapping onto Fig 12's isomer, same atoms and different bond. That is a strain: the "atoms" of a social interpretation are not the framework's atoms. It is kept as an analogue in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and marked single-source, not promoted to a figure verdict. No figure line was forced.

## 4. Findings F1 to F10

### F1: Convergence has three bonds, and they form a union, not one cause

Fig 2 panel 1, verbatim:

> Fig 2 :13 **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**
> Fig 2 :14-15 **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"**

Fig 2 panel 2, verbatim:

> Fig 2 :18 **"one evidence pool"**
> Fig 2 :19 **"they never talk, and still agree"**
> Fig 2 :20-21 **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

Fig 2 panel 3, verbatim:

> Fig 2 :25 **"pinned: it does not update on A or B"**
> Fig 2 :26-27 **"both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement"**

These are three different bonds producing one observable, agreement on C. Fig 9 :54 and :57-58 (quoted in §2) require the union reading. Fig 7 :35 adds: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

- **Diagnostic.** Ask, per case: does the conclusion track a non-updating anchor, a shared evidence pool, or the influence ranking? Record all three scores. Do not average them.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 panels plus Fig 7 selection rule plus Fig 9 union rule, low overlap between the anchor test and the room-chain test).
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a fourth bond that is not a composition of these three and is not a shared-pool variant; or show a case where all three scores are zero yet convergence persists.

### F2: A shared evidence pool explains why interpretation can stay open

Fig 2 :31-32: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. Fig 8 :47: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** Fig 13 :37-38: **"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."**

The mechanism: frames read the same C from the same pool. The reading does not determine the source, so the interpretation of C remains underdetermined even while C is shared. The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay gives the world-side name, metamerism: an instrument reads green and cannot comment on the source. That is a corroborating analogue, not a figure line.

- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 2, Fig 8, Fig 13 for the independence claim; the metamerism essay is secondary and shares no author with the figures but is not a figure line).
- **Refuter:** find a case where two frames with documented identical evidence draw different conclusions *and* the divergence is not explained by different histories or weightings (Symbols table: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge"*). That would break the pool explanation.

### F3: Persistent interpretive disagreement is the signature of a convention

Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."** Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**. Fig 10 :46: **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."**

So agreement about a shared meaning is not a failed verdict. It is the product of a convention, and the figures explicitly say so. The persistent disagreement is not residual noise outside the explanation; it is the evidence that C is currently doing conventional work.

The danger is the crossing of the seam. Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. When the shared conclusion is used as if it settled a verdict, it has silently changed kind, and Fig 2 :34 applies: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 valence rule and Fig 10 convention rule are low-overlap figure lines).
- **Refuter:** show a case where frames converge on C with persistent interpretive disagreement while ranging against a non-updating anchor, and the disagreement does not shrink as ground contact increases. That breaks the diagnostic as applied to panel 3.
- **Retraction of an earlier reading, kept visible:** I first wrote "all scientific consensus is a convention." That is retracted. Fig 2 :41 says the correlation is disqualifying *for a verdict* and efficient *for a convention*; it does not say every converged conclusion is a convention. The narrowed claim is: while interpretation is unresolved and no non-updating anchor is doing the settling, C functions as a convention.

### F4: Influence-driven convergence produces coherence, not evidence

Fig 2 :14-15 (quoted in F1) says what the convergence converges on. Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**. Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 11 :36: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."**

World corroboration from low-overlap fields: the information-cascade literature reports that *"Cascades can be incorrect"* and that cascades arise when agents act on observable actions rather than private signals; Janis's *"concurrence-seeking"* is the same panel in psychological vocabulary. Neither is a figure line. Neither overrules one.

- **Verdict:** `single-source` for the specific "whoever had the most influence" claim (Fig 2 only); `triangulated` for "agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 plus Fig 2 plus the cascade literature, which is secondary).
- **Refuter:** show a chain of rooms with high agreement whose ground contact increased at each handoff, measured independently of the agreement. That breaks the mirror-chain reading.

### F5: A single consensus score is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

The field's own quantitative measure, Shwed and Bearman's citation-network modularity, is a legitimate instrument: Fig 6 :32 says **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**, and they improved Latour's black box and Newman's modularity. But a falling modularity score tells you *when* internal divisions stopped mattering structurally; it does not tell you *which* bond produced the convergence or whether the anchor was pinned. Their three trajectories (spiral, cyclical, flat) are shapes, matching Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**.

- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 slot rule and Fig 9 filter rule, with the world source as corroboration only).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single scalar consensus measure that, without further information, distinguishes a pinned anchor from a shared evidence pool. By Fig 2 the two can look identical in the contents, so this refuter is designed to be hard to satisfy.

### F6: Frames must range against the ground separately, and independence is bought, not assumed

Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** Fig 2 :46: **"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."** Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**.

A consensus conference that puts representatives in a room and asks them to agree is panel 1, not panel 3. A set of instruments pointed at the same thing, each corrected separately against a non-updating ground, is panel 3. This is the practical content of the answer to the question: the cause of legitimate convergence is not the meeting, it is the shared non-updating reference plus independence between the frames ranging against it.

- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 anchor rules plus Fig 10 channel rule).
- **Refuter:** produce a case where negotiation between frames, with no anchor at all, added evidence rather than influence. Under Fig 2 :35, **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**, this would require a mirror to correct.

### F7: The world's boundary object and trading zone are corroboration, not derivation

Star and Griesemer 1989 (quote read via the Wikipedia Boundary object page, which reproduces p. 393): *"Boundary objects are objects which are both plastic enough to adapt to local needs and the constraints of the several parties employing them, yet robust enough to maintain a common identity across sites."* That is F3 in the sociology of information science vocabulary: one common identity, local interpretations.

Galison 1997 (quote read via the Wikipedia Trading zones page): *"Two groups can agree on rules of exchange even if they ascribe utterly different significance to the objects being exchanged; they may even disagree on the meaning of the exchange process itself."* That is the same shape in the history of science vocabulary: local coordination despite global disagreement.

These are genuinely low-overlap witnesses, unlike the framework essays. They corroborate the figure verdict; they do not produce it. Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. The residual was described from Fig 2 before these fields were searched.

- **Verdict:** `corroborates` in the WORLD sense; no figure verdict rests on them.
- **Refuter:** find a boundary object whose local interpretations are not genuinely different but merely differently worded. Then "common identity across sites" collapses into one frame with synonyms, and the analogue fails.

### F8: The mathematical cases split cleanly onto the figure panels

Aumann 1976 (read via the Wikipedia Aumann agreement theorem page): *"If their differing beliefs about something are common knowledge, they must in fact agree."* The conditions are a shared prior and common knowledge of posteriors. That is a formal relative of panel 3: agreement follows from a shared, non-negotiated structure, not from social pressure.

Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch 1992 (read via the Wikipedia Information cascade page): *"Information cascades occur when external information obtained from previous participants in an event overrides one's own private signal, irrespective of the correctness of the former over the latter."* That is panel 1: agents update on each other, and the correctness of the result is not part of the mechanism.

The two results do not conflict; they are different bonds. A conflict here would send us back to the census, not flip a verdict.

- **Verdict:** `corroborates` the three-bond census from mathematics and behavioural economics, both low overlap with the framework.
- **Refuter:** derive a single set of conditions under which both theorems collapse into one mechanism. That would dissolve the union and require a new census.

### F9: The psychological account is one condition among several, and its own literature says so

Janis (read via the Wikipedia Groupthink page) defined groupthink as *"the mode of thinking that persons engage in when concurrence-seeking becomes so dominant in a cohesive ingroup that it tends to override realistic appraisal of alternative courses of action."* That is Fig 2 panel 1 in psychological vocabulary. The same page reports that Park's 1990 review found *"only partial support of his hypotheses"* and that group homogeneity and insulation are better supported than cohesion.

That mixed result is not a problem for the derivation. It is exactly Fig 9 :4-5: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** The psychological condition must be split from the institutional and evidence-pool conditions, not pooled into one "conformity" cause.

- **Verdict:** `single-source` for the psychological mechanism as a figure analogue; `triangulated` for the instruction to split rather than search (Fig 9 plus the empirical review as secondary).
- **Refuter:** show that the cohesion, homogeneity and insulation findings all reduce to one variable with no loss of predictive structure. Then the split is unnecessary.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map field guide, local). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A fourth convergence bond is demonstrated that is not a composition of mutual update, shared evidence pool, and non-updating anchor.
2. A case is found where convergence persists with interpretive disagreement while an unmoved anchor is doing the settling and the disagreement fails to shrink as ground contact increases.
3. A single scalar measure is shown to distinguish panel 1 from panel 3 without any provenance information.
4. A convention is documented that continues to decide outcomes while remaining a convention with no verdict function.
5. The union call fails, that is, the three conditions are shown to be a partition with no overlapping cases and no compound residues.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the three-bond diagnostic, not with a general theory of consensus (F1).
2. Ask, for every case, whether the anchor was pinned and whether the frames were independent (F6). Record all three bond scores separately.
3. Treat a falling disagreement or modularity score as a budget filter, not a verdict (F5).
4. Name the semantic layer as a convention while interpretation is unresolved, and flag the moment a convention starts deciding outcomes (F3).
5. Send the generator, the diagnostic and the refuter, not a recording of this derivation (Fig 4 :22-25).
