**Subject:** One checkable output on consensus with interpretive disagreement

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in consensus formation: how a community can converge on a shared conclusion while its members keep disagreeing about what that conclusion means. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, not `grounded`):** convergence on one conclusion can be produced by three different bonds, and they overlap rather than partition the cases. (1) Frames update on each other with no anchor, so they converge on whoever had the most influence. (2) Frames draw on one evidence pool, so they never talk and still agree; the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other. (3) Frames range separately against an anchor that does not update. This taxonomy is from figures 2, 7 and 9 of a figure set I am testing, not from a historical dataset.
>
> The persistent interpretive disagreement is not noise outside the account. In the figures' terms, agreement about a shared meaning is the product of a convention, and a convention makes no claim about the world while a verdict does. So a conclusion stays a convention while its interpretation is unresolved and no unmoved anchor is settling it. The dangerous moment is when that convention starts deciding outcomes, because then it has quietly changed kind.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any general theory of consensus is now established; that a falling disagreement score or citation modularity tells you which bond operated; that the boundary-object and trading-zone literatures were derived from the figures (they are independent corroboration only); or that I have measured a historical case. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figure text. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. demonstrate a fourth convergence bond that is not a composition of mutual update, shared evidence pool, and non-updating anchor;
> 2. show a case of convergence with interpretive disagreement while an unmoved anchor is doing the settling, where the disagreement fails to shrink as ground contact increases;
> 3. show a single scalar consensus measure that distinguishes influence-driven convergence from anchor-ranged convergence without any provenance information.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or re-census the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
