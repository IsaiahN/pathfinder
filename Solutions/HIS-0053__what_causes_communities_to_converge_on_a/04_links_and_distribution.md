## Wider literature that bears on the question

The eight world sources above cover genuinely different closures: sociology of science (Shwed and Bearman), STS and information science (Star and Griesemer), history of science (Galison, Kuhn), mathematics and game theory (Aumann), behavioural economics and network theory (Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch), and social psychology (Janis, through Park's review). They are corroboration only. None of them outranks a figure line, and a conflict among them would send us back to the domain census rather than flip a verdict.

Two overlap warnings:

1. The Wikipedia overview pages are tertiary summaries. For Star and Griesemer and for Galison, the quoted passages reproduce the primary publications with page numbers given; the primary PDFs were not fetched in this session. The ledger records this honestly.
2. The scientific-consensus and paradigm overviews both draw on the same Kuhnian pool. They are one witness for the paradigm point, not two.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the whole framework before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the three-bond diagnostic and its refuter.
- **Fig 4 :22-25** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Offer the loop as a method after stage 1, not the conclusion as a fact.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement and do not select recipients by likely agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the pool being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see in a case of convergence with interpretive disagreement before showing any theory.
- **Fig 9 :60** *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A citation score or a rejection rate can prioritize who is tried first. It cannot decide the claim.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer first; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the three-bond diagnostic, the convention reading of persistent interpretive disagreement, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask one question: what would you count as a failure here? Record the verdict before any framework material is shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the Shwed and Bearman paper, the boundary-object and trading-zone passages, and the local verbatim figure text. Tell them the figure text is the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the eight-step loop as a reusable method. Each user writes down what would refute their own result before running it and labels the mode of what they get. This is permitted because a generator crosses; a recording does not (Fig 4 :22-25).

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. The diagnostic is not yet grounded. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, and it must be labelled as such in every message.
