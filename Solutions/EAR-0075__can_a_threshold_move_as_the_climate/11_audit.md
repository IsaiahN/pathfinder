## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the identity of the object called a threshold. Arity: two quantities are compared, so binary. Symmetry: the comparison is ordered, because "which is larger" depends on the operands; this is not a conjunction. Scale: a threshold lives in one subsystem's state space, with a lossy re-expression possible when the compared quantity changes from a level to a rate.
- **Domain 1, system dynamics.** Actors: state variables, forcings, feedbacks, noise. Currency: the comparison and the cost of measurement. Anchor: observation of the system. Question type: is the threshold a property that updates with the habitat?
- **Domain 2, decision and assessment.** Actors: assessors, managers, targets. Currency: verdicts and budgets. Anchor: a reference that does not change with what the assessors think. Question type: is the number being used as ground, and therefore required not to update?
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: outside readers. Currency: residual versus agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Its independence here is zero because all three domains use one evidence pool.

Order by dependency: describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle the ground-use question before the dynamics, because a quantity used as ground cannot move by definition; strike the decision bargain after the anchor is fixed (Fig 5 :42); keep a channel to a non-updating measurement last (Fig 10 :24).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 11 :73 and Fig 2 :29, I derived from Fig 12 :87-89 (comparison bond) and Fig 9 :57 (union). Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes within the framework, so the agreement is weak but not circular in the narrow sense. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: a threshold is a movable relation; when it moves it is not ground; the decision is a re-struck bargain; the number remains a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete world claim: the AMOC tipping point is at +2 C under fast forcing but not under slow forcing, so the threshold should be recast as a rate threshold.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the compared quantity** (level versus rate), which the shorthand compresses into one word "threshold" and which Fig 4 :30 says must be measured as a gap;

- the **source discrimination** of the warning signal, which Dakos et al. and Kefi et al. say cannot name the mechanism, and which the shorthand's phrase "threshold moves" glosses over.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: an ordered comparison relation whose operands can change, with the ground kept separate from the habitat. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no instrument was run; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed metalaw" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
