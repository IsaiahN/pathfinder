## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a threshold when the pressures around a system change. Option A: a threshold is a fixed anchor, a criterion that settles a verdict and does not change when pressures change. Option B: a threshold is a relation among changing quantities, a habitat property that moves with the system. What each leads to: A lets a number be fixed once and trusted as ground, but if the system moves that number, decisions keyed to it drift coherently and nobody notices; B makes the threshold unable to serve as ground, requires the decision to be re-struck against a measurement that does not update, and makes the threshold's movement itself a residual to re-measure.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The identity of the object named "threshold". Arity: two quantities, a state and a comparison level (or a value and a rate); Fig 12 :87 defines the bond as "a threshold relation between two quantities". Symmetry: ordered, not symmetric; Fig 12 :89 gives the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Scale: within one subsystem's state space, with a lossy re-expression possible across scales (level to rate).
- **Domain 1, system dynamics (the thing that tips).** Actors: state variables, forcings, feedbacks, noise. Currency: the comparison ⋛ and the cost of measuring it. Anchor: observation of the system. The threshold here is a habitat property and can move. Genuine domain, anchor nameable.
- **Domain 2, decision and assessment (the thing that uses the number).** Actors: risk assessors, managers, policy targets. Currency: verdicts, agreements, budgets. Anchor: a reference that does not change because of what the assessors think. The threshold here is ground use and must not move. Genuine domain, anchor nameable.
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: outside expert readers and their instruments. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero.
- **Partition or union.** Domains 1 and 2 overlap: the same number is both a description of the system and a decision target. Union compound, not partition. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Verdicts kept per domain, never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle the ground-use question before the dynamics question; strike the decision bargain after the anchor is identified; keep the channel to a non-updating measurement last (Fig 3 chain order).

Strain test: Fig 2 requires the anchor not to move, while the world literature says thresholds move. These strain only if "threshold" names the same object in both. Re-census gives two objects, a ground use and a habitat relation, so no strain after re-census (S1 in `01` §6). One live strain remains, S3: which quantity the threshold lives in needs an instrument, not an argument.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A threshold in the figures is an ordered comparison relation, so as a **system property** it can and does move when its quantities move: the habitat updates, and the rate-induced-tipping literature reports exactly that. As a **decision anchor** it must not move; a sliding target stops being an anchor and the decision drifts coherently, which is why nobody notices. The two verdicts belong to different domains of a union compound and are never averaged. On the decision side the figures license a bargain re-struck against a non-updating measurement, treat threshold estimates as budget filters rather than witnesses, and leave every numerical threshold a declared gap. The answer is therefore: yes in one sense, no in the other, and the distinction is the answer. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The strongest single corroboration is world-side and secondary: the AMOC result, where the same system collapses at +2 C under fast forcing and stays stable to +5.5 C under slow forcing.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, two senses, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a settled number or a conflated claim instead of a shape and its refuter. Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the two senses and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The conflation is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A claim already sent can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no term has to be withdrawn from the library.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The union test (showing the two senses are one quantity at one scale), an invariance test (a threshold unchanged by rate, noise, history and background state), a drift test (a moving decision criterion that still settles verdicts without coherent drift), or a measured zero round trip between level and rate descriptions would each reverse the corresponding verdict. A figure line defining ⋛ as a fixed constant or letting an anchor legitimately update would reverse the framework reading itself. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
