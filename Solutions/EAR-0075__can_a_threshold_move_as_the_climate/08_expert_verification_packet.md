**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement at any tipping element is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. ## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Threshold" does two jobs that must be kept separate. As a property of a changing system, a threshold is an ordered comparison between two quantities, so it moves when the quantities move: the habitat updates. As an anchor that settles a decision, a threshold must not move; if it moves with the pressures it judges, it stops being an anchor and the decision drifts coherently. Therefore the answer to "can a threshold move?" is: yes for the system-property sense, no for the ground-use sense, and the two verdicts are never averaged. The decision rule is a cost bargain re-struck against a measurement that does not update, not a fixed cut. Re-expressing a level threshold as a rate threshold is a change of comparison operand with a measurable round-trip gap, not a rename. The numerical threshold of any tipping element is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12, 11 and 2

- **Statement:** a threshold is an ordered comparison relation; as a habitat property it can move, as a ground it must not.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :87 — *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Fig 12 :89 — *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
  - Fig 11 :73 — *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*
  - Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*; Fig 2 :33 — *"The anchor must not update."*
- **What to check:** are these quoted correctly and is the two-object reading (habitat relation versus ground) forced by them, or is it a strain?
- **Refuter:** a figure line defining ⋛ as a fixed constant or a symmetric bond; or a figure line letting an anchor legitimately update and still count as an anchor; or a demonstration that the two senses are the same quantity in the same frame at the same scale.

### C2 — world-facing; checkable against the tipping literature

- **Statement:** the system-property sense of threshold is reported as moving in the literature, with the movement depending on rate, history, noise and background state.
- **Sources, opened 2026-11-22:**
  - Ritchie, Alkhayuon, Cox and Wieczorek, Earth System Dynamics 14, 669-683, 2023: *"the change in the forcing usually does not cause any qualitative change in the stability landscape but instead shifts its position"*.
  - van Westen, Borner and Dijkstra, Nature Climate Change 16, 1195-1206, 2026: *"such a threshold may not be meaningful because AMOC stability depends on the rate of radiative forcing change, not a set temperature"*; slow ramp stable to +5.5 C, fast ramps collapse near +2 C.
  - Feudel, Nonlinear Processes in Geophysics 30, 481-502, 2023: *"the boundaries of the basins of attraction ... start moving under time-dependent variation of intrinsic parameters or external forcing"*.
  - Global Tipping Points Report 2023, Key concepts (Lenton et al.): *"A tipping point is a type of threshold."*; hysteresis reaches *"a different tipping point"* on reversal.
- **What to check:** is this a fair reading of those papers, and does the rate-induced case really move the comparison quantity rather than merely reveal a fixed one? Note the live strain S3 in `01`: which variable the threshold lives in needs an instrument.
- **Refuter:** a measurement showing a system threshold invariant under changes of forcing rate, noise, history and background state. That would conflict with C2 and send the derivation back to the census; it would not automatically flip the figure verdict.

### C3 — decision-facing; checkable against the argument in Figures 5, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** on the decision side the figures license a bargain re-struck against a non-updating measurement, and treat a threshold estimate as a budget filter rather than a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 5 :42 — *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."*
  - Fig 2 :42 — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
  - Fig 9 :60 — *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
  - Fig 10 :24 — *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
- **What to check:** does the bargain line really govern the acceptance test rather than the definition of a threshold? It is a single figure line for that specific claim; this is declared, not upgraded.
- **Refuter:** a figure line licensing a fixed threshold as the mint acceptance rule; or a case in which a threshold estimate is legitimately the verdict rather than an input to the budget.

## 5. What would settle it

1. The union test: show the two senses are one quantity in the same frame at the same scale.
2. The invariance test: measure a threshold under changes of rate, noise, history and background state.
3. The drift test: show a moving decision criterion that still settles verdicts without coherent drift.
4. The round trip: measure `R_T` between the level and rate descriptions for one system; zero would refute F8.
5. Derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or show a direct bond where Fig 13 :75 forbids one (standing framework refuters, carried from the exemplar).
