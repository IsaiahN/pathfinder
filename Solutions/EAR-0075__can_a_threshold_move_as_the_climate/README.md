## The question restated as an effect

The question decides what kind of thing a threshold is. Option A: a threshold is a fixed anchor, a criterion that settles a verdict and does not change when the pressures around the system change. Option B: a threshold is a relation among changing quantities, a property of the system's habitat that moves as its climate, ecosystem or human pressures move. What each leads to is different. Under A, a number can be fixed in policy and trusted as ground, but if the underlying system actually moves that number, decisions anchored to it drift coherently and nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). Under B, the threshold cannot serve as ground, the decision must be re-struck against a measurement that does not update, and the movement of the threshold is itself a residual to be re-measured.

The answer is bound as an ordinary term before the run, per the formula's "Direction" step: *a threshold is a comparison relation between two quantities; it moves when the quantities move; while it moves it cannot be the anchor that settles a verdict.* That binding accrues a residual and is refusable like any other term.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not add an anchor. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
2. Selecting people for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies their agreement as a verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
3. Asking someone to trust before they check spends the only thing they contribute. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and institutional channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
