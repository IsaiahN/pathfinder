# The Tether's answer: can a threshold move?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A decision or warning system uses a threshold. The climate, ecosystem or human pressures around the system change. Does the threshold stay put or move? The residual is the identity of the object, not any number. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Each tipping element has its own threshold; one global "the threshold" hides the live slot.

## 2. The shape

1. **A threshold is an ordered comparison relation, not a constant.** Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with test Fig 12 :89: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* When one of the quantities changes, the comparison changes: the threshold moves.

2. **The question splits into two uses (a union, not a yes/no).** Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The same number is a property of the system and a ground for a decision. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).

3. **As a system property it can move.** The habitat updates while the ground does not. Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* The world literature corroborates this: the position of the threshold shifts with forcing rate, noise and history (Ritchie et al. 2023; Feudel 2023; van Westen et al. 2026; Lenton et al. 2008; Global Tipping Points Report 2023).

4. **As a decision anchor it must not move.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33-34: *"The anchor must not update."* *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* If the number a decision leans on slides with the pressures it judges, the decision has no ground and drifts without anyone seeing it.

5. **The decision rule is a bargain, not a fixed cut.** Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* What must remain fixed is the measurement (Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*), not the number.

6. **Re-expressing a threshold across scales is lossy.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* A warming level and a warming rate are isomers in the comparison operand, not two spellings of one threshold; the round-trip gap `R_T` must be reported.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "yes, all thresholds move." It says *which sense* of threshold can move, and that the other sense must not.
- Not a value for the AMOC, Greenland, the Amazon or any other tipping element. Those are world names, provenanced to their sources, not figure derivations.
- Not that early-warning signals reliably announce a threshold. Dakos et al. 2015: *"Critical slowing down indicators are not forecasting tools per se"*; the fastest approaches can leave no signal at all.
- Not that a threshold is one of the six substrate terms. That reading was retracted in `01` §6 and narrowed in `05` §5.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried quote): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Union test:** show that the system-property sense and the ground-use sense are the same quantity in the same frame at the same scale. If so, the union collapses and the shape is wrong.
- **Invariance test:** measure a threshold under changes of forcing rate, noise, history and background state. Invariance would refute the system-property claim (F3).
- **Drift test:** show a decision criterion that moves with pressures and still settles verdicts without coherent drift. That would refute F2.
- **Cross-scale round trip:** measure `R_T` between a level description and a rate description for one system. Zero would refute F8; nonzero quantifies what the coarser account drops.
- Standing refuters from `01` §4: a figure line defining ⋛ as a fixed constant or symmetric bond; a figure line letting an anchor legitimately update.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The numerical threshold, the identity of the tipping element and the responsible mechanism are not given by the figures; they belong to instruments and provenanced models. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The live strain S3 (which quantity the threshold lives in) is recorded, not hidden, and needs an instrument rather than an argument. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give you a new number for any tipping element. It says that "threshold" is doing two jobs that must be separated. As a property of a changing system, a threshold is a comparison between two quantities, so when the quantities change it moves; the rate-induced-tipping literature reports exactly that, with basin boundaries shifting and the same ocean circulation collapsing at +2 C under fast forcing but staying stable to +5.5 C under slow forcing. As the number a decision leans on, a threshold must not move: if it slides with the pressures it was meant to judge, the decision has no ground and drifts coherently, which is why nobody notices. So the answer is not yes or no but: a threshold can move, and when it does it is not an anchor. What must stay fixed is the measurement; the decision is a bargain re-struck against it, not a cut held forever. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The number stays open until the ground settles it.
