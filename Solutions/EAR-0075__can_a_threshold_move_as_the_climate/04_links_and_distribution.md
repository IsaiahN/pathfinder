## Two findings in the world literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The threshold is reported as moving in the literature itself, not inferred from the framework.** Ritchie et al. describe a moving stability landscape and threshold instability; Feudel describes moving basin boundaries; van Westen et al. show the same system collapsing at +2 C under fast forcing and remaining stable to +5.5 C under slow forcing. This corroborates the system-property sense of threshold (F3). It does not decide the verdict, which comes from the figures (F1 to F4).
2. **Observing an approaching threshold is limited exactly where movement is fastest.** Dakos et al. and Boettiger et al. record that early-warning signals are not forecasting tools per se, and that a rapid approach can leave no time for a signal. This corroborates the round-trip-gap finding (F8) and the per-slot requirement (F6). The early-warning sources share an evidence pool (Scheffer and co-authors recur), so they are one witness for this point.
