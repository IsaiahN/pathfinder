# Two senses of "threshold", union structure and cross-scale order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The single word "threshold" is used for two different things in the same sentence: the level at which a system would change, and the number a decision holds as a target. The derivation owes: (1) are these one quantity or two, (2) how are they ordered, and (3) what happens when the comparison is re-expressed at another scale (a warming level becomes a warming rate).

### Q2 — Are the two senses one quantity or two?

- Sense S (system property): a threshold is where a state crosses a comparison level under some forcing, rate, noise and history. Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* The threshold here is in the habitat, and the habitat updates.
- Sense G (ground use): a threshold is the number that settles a verdict. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
- One verb, two predicates: S updates, G must not. A single object cannot both update and not update in the same frame at the same scale.
- ⇒ They are **two senses of one relation in two domains**, a union compound (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), not two kinds of threshold and not one threshold with two moods.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a demonstration that S and G are the same quantity in the same frame at the same scale, such that one lever (fix it or float it) clears both conditions. If that holds, the union collapses and this Q is wrong.

### Q3 — What is the order between them?

- Fig 3 :19-20: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The chain is ordered by dependency.
- The anchor question comes first: if the number is being used as ground, it must not move; only after that can we ask whether the system property moves. Fixing the decision rule before deciding what the number is for is effort above the break.
- Then the decision rule: Fig 5 :42 *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."* The bargain is struck after the anchor is identified, because the cost of being wrong is priced against the ground (Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*).
- Then the measurement: Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; what must be kept reachable is the measurement, not the number.
- ⇒ Order: describe the residual (Fig 9 :27-28) → settle S/G → strike the bargain → keep the channel to a non-updating ground. Trying to answer "can it move?" before splitting S and G lands above the break.

### Q4 — What changes when a threshold is re-expressed across scales?

- The world case: van Westen et al. 2026 report a collapse near +2 C under fast forcing and stability to +5.5 C under slow forcing, and propose converting the warming threshold to a warming-rate threshold.
- Under Fig 4 :22-26 only methods cross up and only head starts come down; a recording of one occasion does not become knowledge by being carried upward. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- A level description and a rate description of the same system are not interchangeable names. The transform drops the forcing path and the history, which are exactly what determine the outcome. The round trip gap `R_T` is nonzero and must be reported.
- Are they isomers? Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The level and rate descriptions share the atom set (state, forcing, comparison) but differ in which quantity the comparison bond attaches to. They are **isomers in the comparison operand**, not the same threshold written two ways. That is why the two give different collapse temperatures and why the gap is load-bearing.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that for a given system the level and rate descriptions are losslessly intertranslatable, so `R_T = 0` and the isomer claim fails. `11_audit.md` records `R_T` qualitatively as nonzero.

### Q5 — Is there an ordering analogue elsewhere in the figures?

Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* The lesson used here is not that a threshold is a structure in the six-term set; it is that **a composition needs an intermediate term of the opposite kind**. A threshold used as ground is a state of the decision frame; a moving threshold is a relation in the habitat. They cannot be the same term without an intervening change of role. Fig 13 :4-5: *"the two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward."* Recasting a level as a rate is such a change of role, which is why it is a lossy crossing and not a rename.

I record the limit: no figure line assigns "threshold" to one of the six substrate terms. Doing so would be the overreach corrected in `05` §5 and retracted in `01` §6.

## CONCLUSION

The two senses of "threshold" are a union compound with different predicates (updates versus must-not-update) and different anchors (measurement versus ground). They are ordered: split before answering, settle the anchor before the dynamics, strike the bargain after the anchor, keep the measurement reachable last. A level threshold and a rate threshold are isomers in the comparison operand; re-expressing one as the other carries a round-trip gap that must be reported, not assumed away. The 2026 AMOC result is a world instance of that isomorphism-plus-gap, corroborating the system-property sense while leaving the figure verdict to the figures.
