# Expert verification packet - the Tether on organizing and fragmentation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of collective action can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The grievance is not the explanatory unit. It is the residual the loop is trying to close, an operand with no anchor of its own, and "similar grievances" is a pooled reading that discards the per-group signal. Organizing and fragmenting are isomers: the same atoms under a different bond are different substances. Capability is a property of the group and its habitat, never of the group alone, so effort that does not change contact changes nothing. A repertoire cannot renew from inside its own closure; it needs contact with a differing closure. Internal agreement is variation, not a verdict. Two conditions, maintained contact and renewed reach, form a union and must be kept separate. The framework returns that shape, not the name of the institution or tactic that wins.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12

- **Statement:** capability is group-and-habitat, and the organize/fragment difference is a bond difference, not an ingredient difference.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is the bond claim doing work the ingredients cannot do? If a list of grievance, members, resources and opportunities fully predicts the outcome, the bond term is free and the claim fails.
- **Refuter:** two groups with the same atoms and independently measured identical habitat arrangement differ in outcome, and the difference is fully accounted for by an internal variable while contact is held fixed.

### C2 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** reach renews only on contact with a differing closure; fragmentation is a channel failure of pooled readings and lost contact; the two conditions form a union, not one lever.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **What to check:** is the union real? Do contact failure and reach failure dissociate in the cases you know, or is one really a special case of the other? Is the "similar grievances" label ever a pooled reading in your data?
- **Refuter:** a group renews its repertoire purely by internal composition with a ground-verified new atom and no contact with a differing closure; or a single intervention restores both conditions with no categorically different residue; or a pooled reading preserves the per-group signal on held-out outcomes.

### C3 - world-facing; checkable against the collective-action literature

- **Statement:** the standard causes (grievance severity, political opportunity, resources, selective incentives, leadership) are bindings from one evidence pool, not figure terms. A genuine conflict exists in that pool between Olson (large diffuse groups fail) and Ostrom (bounded communities self-organize), and the figures resolve it as a scale question, not by picking a winner.
- **What to check:** read Olson 1965 and Ostrom 1990 side by side. Is the apparent contradiction better explained by scale and boundary conditions than by one theory being wrong? Are Centola and Macy's multiple-reinforcement requirement and the quorum result from collective animal behaviour genuinely low-overlap witnesses for the mechanism shape?
- **Refuter:** a source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-group organizing is fully predicted by averaged grievance severity with no contact or reach variable. That would return the derivation to the census and could flip the verdict.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no final room. The specific case stays a compound with its own loop.
- Not that political opportunity, resources or grievance severity is the answer. Those are world bindings, admitted as secondary corroboration only.
- Not that a cohesive group is an organized group. Agreement is variation, not a verdict.
- Not that the framework licenses naming the winning institution, leader or tactic. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an outcome measurement.
- Not a claim that this packet's simulated derivation is an outside check. It is internally consistent with the figures and no more.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Contact Test:** outcome tracks an independently measured change in access to the paying ground, with atoms held fixed.
2. **Import Test:** a verified new tactic appears only after contact with a differing closure.
3. **Slot Test:** a per-group reading outperforms the pooled reading on held-out outcomes.
4. **Union Test:** the two conditions fail independently and leave a categorically different residue when both fail.

Plus the three refuters in section 2.
