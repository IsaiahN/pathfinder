## 1. The residual in effect terms

R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s. Here:

- **s, the slot:** a group facing a grievance. Not a person, not a category of grievance.
- **Γ, the library:** the group's shared repertoire, its beliefs about what action will redress the harm, its terms for what the situation affords.
- **b, the belief:** how far the group leans on its own history versus inherited repertoire (Symbols table :24).
- **a, the action:** collective action, or its absence.
- **o′, the observation:** whether the harm actually ceased, the resource was actually secured, the outcome actually changed.
- **R:** the gap between predicted and actual redress, indexed per group. *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19). *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22).

**Anchor check.** A nameable anchor exists. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The anchor here is the material outcome: did the harm stop, did the resource get secured, did the policy change. The state or institution that may grant it is in the habitat, not the ground; a ground listed as a party has become a frame (Fig 2 :47). So this is a well-formed question, not yet a `gap`.

**Option A and Option B** are stated in `README.md`. The sought-for shape is bound there as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"), so it can accrue a residual like anything else and be refusable.

## 2. Domain census

### 2.1 Arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** a relation among group library Γ, action a, and outcome o′, indexed by slot and ordered by time. At least three operands plus an ordering. It carries an ordered bond (Fig 12 :30, sequence: organize then redress) and a comparison bond (Fig 12 :87, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*: is the outcome enough). It is not a flat conjunction.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. The ground does not update on the group's opinion (Fig 2 :25). The label similar grievance is an identity or threshold claim over atoms; enough participation is a threshold over quantities. Neither is mere conjunction.
- **Scale:** the slot is a group, but the ranking of "collective" exists only at a larger scale (Fig 4 :5, the population). The membrane between individual action and group action is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30). The residual is intrinsically cross-scale.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - Capability is a property of group-and-habitat, never of the group alone (decisive)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The question asks why groups with *similar* grievances differ in outcome. The figure says the difference cannot be located in the group considered alone. Training leaders, articulating the grievance more clearly, or increasing internal cohesion are improvements to the agent in isolation unless they change contact. Mode `specified` for the group application, `general` for the structural claim.

**Refuter:** a change in organizing outcome fully accounted for by a within-group variable while contact with the paying ground is held fixed and independently measured.

### F2 - The grievance is an operand, not an actor: X1 has no anchor

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The grievance is R, the gap the loop is trying to close. It is not an actor with its own currency and anchor. Severity without a nameable anchor is not a domain (census rule). World sources that treat grievance severity as the cause (see WORLD: political opportunity, resource mobilization) are recordings of a frame's vocabulary, not a figure term. After re-census they are re-mapped as operands and carry zero verdict weight until provenanced.

**Refuter:** show a grievance variable that settles the organizing outcome without reference to any outcome measurement, with its own anchor independent of the group's.

### F3 - Organization and fragmentation are isomers, not degrees

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Groups with the same grievance atoms under a different internal bond are *different substances*, not weaker and stronger versions of one thing. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Naming the grievance names the state; it does not give the route to organization. This is why the question's own framing (the grievance is shared, so why the different result?) is backwards against the figure: shared ingredients are exactly what the bond does not record.

Fig 12 :119 names the **catalyst**: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Durable organizations are catalyst-like: a part that survives its own use. A one-off mobilization is a spent part. Nothing in the ingredient list tells them apart.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that bond structure is irrelevant once atoms and habitat are held fixed, that is, two groups differing only in arrangement with identical measured organizing outcomes.

### F4 - Fragmentation is a closure failure, not a missing ingredient

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."** Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

A group's existing repertoire of contention cannot generate a new tactic from inside itself. Renewal requires an import from a differing closure, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :12-13). A fragmented group can search forever inside a finite reach (Fig 13 :118). This is the D3 condition, kept separate from D1/D2/D4.

**Refuter:** a group that renews its repertoire purely by internal composition, with a new atom verified against the ground and no contact with a differing closure.

### F5 - Fragmentation is also a channel failure, and the question's own wording is the main pooled reading

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*.

The phrase "groups facing similar grievances" is itself a pooled reading. It aggregates across slots and discards the per-slot residual that says where to look (Fig 1 :19, :22). Per Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The residual must be characterized per group before the venue is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).

**Refuter:** a verifier that aggregates across groups and still predicts the per-slot organizing outcome at the ground, with the aggregation shown not to discard the live slot.

### F6 - Union, not partition: unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The answer is two conditions kept separate: contact maintained and reach renewed. A single lever (more grievance articulation, more funding, better leadership) is effort above the break (Fig 3 :29-30) and clears only the single-condition cases. The intersection is a specific compound, not a harder instance of either.

**Refuter:** an intervention restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue, or a demonstrated partition with one residual per group and no overlap.

### F7 - Internal agreement is variation, not a verdict

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

A cohesive group that agrees about its grievance has produced variation inside one room. It has not produced a verdict. Progress per handoff requires both a faithful bracket and the ground's pressure (Fig 7 :37-39). Fragmentation can therefore be a mirror chain: same closure handed along, increasing agreement, no new evidence.

**Refuter:** a group whose internal agreement is shown to move the ground on an independently measured outcome, with agreement and outcome drawn from different evidence pools.

### F8 - Political opportunity and resources are world bindings, not figure terms

World sources (WORLD section) name political opportunity, resources, and selective incentives as the causes. These are bindings from the collective-action frame onto figure atoms, not figure lines. Fig 6 :23 governs: the bound moves only on contact with a differing closure. Fig 8 :50-52: *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*

They are admitted as corroboration after re-mapping, with no verdict weight where the mapping is asserted rather than shown. The conflict between Olson (large groups fail) and Ostrom (communities self-organize) is a scale conflict, returned to the census as a membrane question (Fig 4), not used to flip the verdict.

**Refuter:** a source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing per-slot organizing fully predicted by averaged grievance severity with no contact or reach variable.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat map discipline: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Two groups with identical grievance atoms and independently measured identical habitat arrangement differ in organizing outcome, and the difference is fully accounted for by a within-group variable with no change in contact (breaks F1, Fig 11 :40).
2. A group renews its repertoire purely by internal composition, with a ground-verified new atom and no contact with a differing closure (breaks F4, Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32).
3. A verifier that aggregates across groups still predicts the per-slot organizing outcome, with the aggregation shown to preserve the live slot (breaks F5, Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. The "similar grievance" class is demonstrated to be a partition with one residual per group and no overlapping conditions (breaks F6, Fig 9 :55).
5. Bond structure is shown irrelevant once atoms and habitat are held fixed (breaks F3, Fig 12 :5, :117).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Grievance sufficient?** No. It is an operand R, not an actor with an anchor (Fig 11 :43; world: political opportunity, resource mobilization).
2. **Difference in the group alone?** No. Capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39-40).
3. **Difference in the bond?** Yes. Same atoms, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :5, :117). This is why the phrase similar grievances is the wrong explanatory unit.
4. **How does reach renew?** Only on contact with a differing closure (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32).
5. **What breaks the channel?** Pooled readings and lost contact (Fig 10 :24, :26). The question's own wording is the main pooled reading.
6. **One lever or two?** Two, a union of contact and reach (Fig 9 :54-57). Unbundle before searching.
7. **Route to a name?** None claimed. The framework returns a shape; the specific institution or tactic is a `gap` closed by the ground, not by argument (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: **organizing is a bond-and-contact phenomenon, not a grievance-severity phenomenon. Fragmentation is what happens when contact fails, when reach cannot renew from a differing closure, or when a pooled reading hides the live slot. The two conditions form a union and must be kept separate.** Everything that names a specific mechanism as *the* answer is refused as a world binding without a source gate. Strains after re-census: **none unresolved**. Mode `specified`; the instrument that would close the gap is characterized but not held.
