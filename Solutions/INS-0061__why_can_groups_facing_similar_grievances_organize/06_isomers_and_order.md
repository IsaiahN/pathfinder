# Isomers, catalysts and order in the organizing question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks why the same grievance atoms yield organization in one setting and fragmentation in another. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12's structural forms, what kind of difference is this, and (2) what order does the chain have, and where does it stop.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What kind of difference is organize versus fragment?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- A fragmented group and an organized group with the same grievance, the same members and the same outward target are **isomers**. They are not one thing at two strengths. The difference is carried entirely by the bond, which the ingredient list does not record.
- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* The grievance is a state. Organization is a route. Naming more precisely the state does not supply the route.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** two groups equal in atoms and habitat but differing only in arrangement show identical organizing outcomes at the ground. Then the isomer claim fails and the grievance-ingredient reading stands instead.

### Q2 - Is there a catalyst form?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A durable organization has a part that survives its own use: a repertoire, a rule, a record that can be used again. A single protest that spends its energy is a spent part. Both can have identical ingredient lists on the day.
- The world literature touches this directly: Ostrom's design principles for long-enduring institutions and Centola and Macy's mechanism of credibility through repeated reinforcement (both read, see `09_watson_answer.md`). These are secondary corroboration for the *shape*, not the source of the figure claim.
- **Falsifier:** a persisted organization in which no part survives its own use, verified by observing the part consumed on first use and never recovered.

### Q3 - What is the functional group?

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Across the cases, the shared substructure is: a residual is registered per slot, a repertoire is available, a bond connects members, and the outcome is ranged against a ground. The qualifier (which institution, which leader, which tactic) says which member of the group. The group says what kind of thing to look for; it does not name the winner.
- This is the canonical shape. It is why the answer can say what any organizing case must have while remaining silent on which specific case will occur.

### Q4 - What order does the chain have, and where does it stop?

- Fig 3 :4-17 gives the dependency order: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- A group that perceives the harm and has terms for it but cannot say what would count as progress has stopped at the middle link. Fig 3 :26-27: that link *"has neither"* an obvious failure nor an obvious test, and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading of the group. It is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). So outcome data from a group whose objective link is missing cannot be used as evidence about its planning capacity.
- Order matters. Fig 12 :30 defines `→` as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, with the test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Organize → redress is not interchangeable with redress → organize. A flat `+` formula would lose that, and the bond term would be uncharged (Fig 12 :43, *"one bond type costs nothing"*).

### Q5 - Where does a refusal fit?

- Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Negation is unary, not a bond (Fig 12 :134-136).
- A group that refuses a tactic, an institution or its own prior strategy is carrying a ¬ term. A vocabulary with only bonds can add terms and cannot record that one of them is refused. It will keep composing over a term that should have lost its standing to be composed over (Fig 5 amendment, Fig 6 :41-43).
- This is a real failure mode in organizational memory: the repertoire accumulates and nothing is crossed out, so the group keeps re-trying a refused path. It is a channel and record problem (Fig 10 :24), not a missing-ingredient problem. Mark `specified`; no measurement here.

### Q6 - What order is the union?

- The two conditions are contact maintained and reach renewed. They are a union, not a chain: each can fail independently, and the intersection is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-57).
- Dependency ordering applies *within* each condition, not between them. Effort spent sharpening the objective of a group that has already lost contact is effort above the break (Fig 3 :29-30).
- The comparison at the ground is a threshold, ⋛, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48). "Did the harm stop?" is a comparison, not a conjunction. This is why a grievance can be acknowledged and still unresolved.

## CONCLUSION

Organize versus fragment is an **isomer** difference under Fig 12, with a **catalyst** form for durability and a **functional group** that fixes the kind of answer without naming the winner. The dependency chain stops most often at the middle link, where a group has terms for the harm but no instrument for what counts as progress. The union of contact and reach is not a sequence, and refusal is a unary operation that a bond-only vocabulary cannot record. This rescues what is defensible in the world literature (the same atoms under different arrangements, the need for reinforcement, the possibility of durable local rules) without naming any of them as the framework's answer.
