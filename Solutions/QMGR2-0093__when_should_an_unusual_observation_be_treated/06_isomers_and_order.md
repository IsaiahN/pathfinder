# Union, order and analogues in the anomaly decision

**Mode: `specified` on the checkable structure, `general` on the ordering claims.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

`01` establishes that the anomaly decision is a union of conditions, not a two-way partition. Three things remain owed: (1) the exact structure of the union; (2) the order in which the available moves must be tried, because an out-of-order check can spend budget or import the wrong atom; (3) whether "fluctuation" and "discovery" are different *substances* over the same atoms (Fig 12's isomer question) or merely members of one family, and what analogue from another field can and cannot corroborate.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a per-slot residual R⁺_s, a prediction Γ(b,a), a scan (how many slots were visited), a stopping rule, a candidate term φ with cost |φ| and leftover left(R,φ), an instrument channel, and a ground. Bonds held: → sequence, ⇒ production, − subtraction, ⋛ comparison, ¬ negation (Operators table). No new primitive is available without an OUTWARD import and its source gate.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the structure of the union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific"
- Fig 9 :58: "compound."

The five bins of Fig 5 are not five boxes that can be checked independently in any order. Support (is there live mass?) gates everything; novelty and reachability decide whether a mint is even available; the bargain prices it; persistence decides whether to keep looking; the channel audit decides whether the residual is about the world at all. Where two conditions bind at once (for example a large residual that is also mis-attached), clearing one lever leaves the compound standing. **The correct structure is a compound with its own loop and its own ground**, not a list. Concordant with *The Library of Babel*: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

### Q2 - What is the order of operations?

Three orders are licensed by three separate figure passages and they must not be collapsed:

1. **Within the decision chain (Fig 3):** perception, then vocabulary, then objective, then planning, then learn and carry. Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
2. **Within the search for a term (Formula step 7):** SEARCHED first, because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer; then INWARD (extend an instrument already returning something); then OUTWARD (import a primitive from a frame whose closure differs). Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."
3. **Within the routing of the residual (Formula step 2):** refuse before mint when the residual says a held term is false. Fig 5 :56: "Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain." An anomaly that refutes a held regularity must be allowed to remove that term's standing to be composed over, not just add a positive explanation on top of it.

**Why order matters here:** importing an atom before SEARCHED is run adds a primitive that was not needed; Fig 6 :24 "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Minting before a refusal is available produces an accumulating library of epicycles: Fig 12 Operators amendment, "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Measuring significance before declaring the scan and stopping rule produces a frame-internal score: Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

### Q3 - Are fluctuation and discovery isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

As the question is usually written, "fluctuation" and "discovery" are described in different vocabularies (a p-value versus a physical mechanism), so they share no atoms and are not isomers. Under the figure census of this folder, both can be written over the same full atom set: {R⁺_s, Γ, scan, stopping rule, φ, channel, ground}. What differs is the *arrangement*, that is, which atom is allowed to move:

- **Fluctuation:** R⁺_s is allowed to fall as data accumulate; Γ, the channel and the ground stay fixed. The bond between R and the scan is a comparison (⋛) against a declared stopping threshold; the candidate φ is not admitted.
- **Discovery:** R⁺_s is allowed to persist; Γ is changed by admitting φ (⇒ production) or by refusing a held term (¬); the ground settles the change. The scan and stopping rule stay fixed.

Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour. Under Fig 12 :117 that is an **isomer claim**, and it is the stronger, testable form: an anomaly class is determined by the arrangement of the decision atoms, not by how loud the residual is. If the bond assignments are ignored, the two collapse into the same flat formula, which is Fig 12's "collapse 6: the flat formula", "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".

The weaker reading is that both are members of a **functional group**: any live residual under a declared scan and stopping rule, with the qualifier "admitted" or "not admitted". Fig 12 :124: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." This is safer but less informative.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either class can be written without an atom the other requires (for example a discovery with no ground contact, or a fluctuation with no declared scan), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives. Equivalently, if a single bond assignment with a fixed threshold correctly distinguishes every case in a controlled benchmark, the arrangement does not carry the information and the isomer claim is dead.

### Q4 - The cross-field analogue and where it strains

*They're Scanning Books* (opened 2026-11-23) gives: "Discovery under consequence = a random walk plus a model that can be surprised. ... Evolution is one implementation: variation is the walk, death is the recognizer ... Science is another: experiment is the walk, theory is the model, and the anomaly-chaser is the recognizer."

Mapping: random walk to the scan (sample many slots); model to Γ(b,a); surprise to R⁺_s > 0; recognizer to the disposition to invest in a persistent, compressible violation rather than discard it as noise. This is a low-overlap corroboration of F1, F3, F4 and F6: a walk with no model has no anomalies, only noise.

**Where it strains.** The essay says "The recognizer can only register an anomaly it has the primitives to represent." The figures say something stronger and structural: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom" (Fig 6 :21) and "no search, however long, adds an atom" (Fig 8 :32). The essay's "new dimension of the space" (an OUTWARD import of an axis) is not a mint and is not licensed merely by an anomaly the frame cannot represent; Fig 6 requires a source gate and a shadow test, and prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives. So the analogue corroborates the *recognizer* half of the decision but must not be read as licensing imports that the figures forbid. This strain is named, not smoothed.

## CONCLUSION

The union is a compound with its own loop. The order is fixed in three distinct layers: decision chain (Fig 3), search (Formula step 7), and routing (Formula step 2, refuse before mint). Fluctuation and discovery are isomers over the full decision atom set if and only if the arrangement (which atom is allowed to move) carries the discriminating information; otherwise they are only a functional group, and the falsifier above decides which.

What survives from this derivation into `07` and `08`: the decision form is a structure, not a cutoff; the order of checks is part of the claim; and the isomer test supplies a concrete, falsifiable experiment (vary the bond assignment at fixed residual magnitude and see which class the benchmark assigns).
