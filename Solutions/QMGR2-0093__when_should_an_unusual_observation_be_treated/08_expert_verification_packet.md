# Expert verification packet - the Tether on anomaly assessment

**What this is:** a methodological claim in a form a statistician, methodologist or experimental physicist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the decision form, `specified` on the testable sub-claims. Not `grounded`.** No experiment or dataset is claimed to have settled the rule. This is a shape and a route.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An unusual observation is a live per-slot residual (something happened in one tracked slot that the prediction missed). The question "discovery or fluctuation?" is the wrong partition: it bundles at least five bins, one of which (a held term is false) owes a refusal rather than a new explanation. A candidate discovery should be admitted only when a declared code prices it below the confusion it removes, the residual persists instead of trending down under accumulation, the field's closure has been searched before any new atom is imported, a channel audit has failed to make the reading disappear, and an independent low-overlap ground contact confirms it. A significance threshold, a trials correction or a Bayes factor is a **budget filter** for how much follow-up to buy, not a verdict. What is offered is the form any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 5, Fig 9 and Fig 1

- **Statement:** the discovery/fluctuation binary is a false partition; the decision is a union of five bins, and the significance of a scanned winner is a frame-internal score.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :10 "sort the gap into four kinds"; Fig 5 :50 "AMENDMENT · the gap has a fifth kind, and the pipeline has no branch for it."; Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"; Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a closed two-way criterion that correctly handles all five bins, including the refusal bin, without a union split; or show a frame computing an unbiased external measure of its own post-hoc selection effect from the same data that selected it. Either breaks C1.

### C2 - framework-internal and benchmark-facing; checkable by constructing a declared code

- **Statement:** magnitude is not sufficient. A candidate is admitted only if cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R) under a declared code, and only if the residual persists and does not trend down. A term that explains one occasion perfectly is rejected by the rule.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :42 "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."; Fig 5 :44 "fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."; Fig 5 :18 "seeks the gap that is large and compressible"; Fig 5 :60 "already runs, read with the opposite sign." The formula gives the inequality and the persistent-and-non-trending ranking.
- **What to check:** under a declared code on a benchmark with known injected signals and null fluctuations at matched residual magnitudes, does cost plus leftover separate the two, and does the residual persist only for the injected signals? Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn (Psychological Science, 2011) and the ASA statement (2016) are secondary corroboration of the multiplicity and threshold problems, not of this inequality.
- **Refuter:** a controlled benchmark in which cost plus leftover separates signals from fluctuations *worse* than a fixed threshold at the same budget, or a documented case where the residual trends down under accumulation while a real discovery is missed under a declared stopping rule. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - ground-facing; checkable against any standard verification chain

- **Statement:** a threshold is a filter and the ground is the verdict; search precedes import; a channel audit is a precondition of promotion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."; Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."; Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."; Fig 6 :24 "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."; Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
- **What to check:** in a real detection chain, was the sigma level paired with independent instruments and pipelines and a channel audit, or was it treated as the decision? GW150914 (two detectors, two pipelines, estimated background, 5.1 sigma) is a positive instance; the OPERA 2012 timing fault (a 60 ns early arrival traced to a GPS/fibre connection and clock) is the mis-attached instance. Each is one case, not a frequency estimate.
- **Refuter:** a domain where a threshold with no independent ground contact sets the truth of the decision; or a post-mortem showing that importing a new atom beat distributed search inside a closure that already contained the answer; or a case where an anomaly survived every independent channel audit and was still a pure instrument artifact at every resolution. Any one breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a new significance test, and not a replacement for p-values, Bayes factors, FDR control or registered reports. The rule needs those filters; it only denies them the verdict.
- Not a numeric threshold. No sigma or p-value is asserted as the answer.
- Not a verdict on any particular anomaly, and not a claim that a large residual is a discovery.
- Not a claim that expert agreement settles a methodological question. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not a claim that the six-term substrate governs anomaly decisions. It does not; the bearing figures are Fig 5, Fig 9, Fig 1, Fig 2 and the Formula routing table.
- Not `grounded`. The figure lines decide the shape; the world sources corroborate the problem statement and the need for independent checks.
- Not a ground reading. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A controlled benchmark at matched residual magnitudes testing C2, with the scan and stopping rule declared in advance.
2. An arrangement test on the isomer model of `06` Q3: vary which atom is allowed to move and see whether the class assignment changes.
3. A channel test routing an anomaly to "mis-attached" when a single instrument parameter is varied.
4. The six pre-registered refuters in `01` §4, written before these sub-claims were assembled.
5. Independent replication of the methodology claim by a frame with low overlap, corrected separately against the ground (Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it.").

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 5 :10-14, :42-44, :50-53, Fig 9 :54 and :60, and Fig 1 :19 and :27. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own field: does a two-way discovery/fluctuation decision lose anything that the five bins keep? Name the loss or name the equivalence.
3. Check C2 against a benchmark you know: would cost plus leftover beat your current threshold, and would the persistence test fire at the right time?
4. Check C3 against one real detection or retraction you know: was the threshold paired with independent ground contact and a channel audit?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
