# The Tether's answer: when an unusual observation may be treated as a discovery

**Mode: `general` on the decision form, `specified` on the testable sub-claims. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No instrument or dataset was run for this question.

## 1. The residual it answers

An observer predicts Γ(b,a) per slot s and receives o'. An unusual observation is a live per-slot residual R⁺_s > 0. The question asks when that residual may be promoted to a candidate new term (a discovery) and when it stays inside the current library (a fluctuation). The options and what each leads to are stated in `README.md` §0: a wrong mint imports an atom that costs more than starting from primitives; a missed signal leaves the reach smaller than it could be.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

### Domain 1, the inference frame (verdict: triangulated)

1. **Start per slot, and record the scan.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A global goodness of fit cannot veto a single live slot, and a post-hoc winner cannot be scored by the same frame that selected it (Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."). State how many slots were searched and the stopping rule before reporting the residual.
2. **Sort before judging.** Fig 5 :10 "sort the gap into four kinds", with the fifth kind added at Fig 5 :50 and :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". The binary discovery/fluctuation is a false partition (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").
3. **Price the candidate, do not apply a cutoff as the verdict.** Fig 5 :42 "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold." A candidate is admitted only if cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R) under a declared code. Fig 5 :44 "fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." Fig 5 :18 "seeks the gap that is large and compressible." Magnitude alone is not sufficient.
4. **Check persistence.** Formula step 2: the residual that "PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN" is the one to point a term at; it is the shrinking test read with the opposite sign. A fluctuation trends down as data accumulate.
5. **Search before importing.** Formula step 7 orders SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD. Fig 6 :24 "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." If the candidate is already in the field's closure, the remedy is more independent search, not a new term.
6. **Keep a refusal available.** Fig 5 :53 owes a negation when a held term is false; the Operators table says "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Do not answer every anomaly by adding another positive explanation.

### Domain 2, the significance frame (verdict: filter only, zero verdict weight)

A p-value, a sigma level, a trials correction or a Bayes factor is a **budget filter**: it decides how much independent follow-up to buy and which cuts to keep ranked and reversible. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The frame cannot settle its own selection effect (Fig 1 :27), so the post-hoc significance of a scanned winner is not external evidence. This verdict is not an averaging with Domain 1; it is a separate office assignment.

### Domain 3, the ground frame (verdict: single-source in the figures, corroborated across fields)

The verdict is the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks.", Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."). Independence is "how little they draw on the same evidence" (Fig 2 :31); two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames, while two people reading the same report are one. The world record corroborates the structure of this check: the GW150914 detection paired a 5.1 sigma threshold with two detectors and two independent pipelines, while the OPERA 60 ns anomaly disappeared when the timing channel was repaired. These are **single cases**, not frequency estimates, and they corroborate but do not derive the rule.

### Domain 4, the announcement frame (verdict: not a domain, zero verdict weight)

No nameable, independent anchor exists here. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Prestige can fund the test of a claim, never settle it. This frame makes conventions (a journal's sigma policy), not verdicts.

### The answer in one line

Treat an unusual observation as a **possible discovery** when it is a live per-slot residual that (i) survives an unbundling into the figure's five bins, (ii) is not already reachable by independent search inside the current closure, (iii) admits a candidate whose stated cost plus leftover is less than the confusion it removes, (iv) persists and does not trend down under accumulation, (v) survives a channel audit, and (vi) is confirmed by a low-overlap ground contact. A threshold decides how much to spend on steps (iv) to (vi), not whether the answer is yes.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a new significance test, not a replacement for p-values, Bayes factors or FDR control. Those are filters and the rule needs them; it only refuses to let them be the verdict.
- Not a numeric threshold. No sigma, p-value or Bayes factor is asserted as the answer.
- Not a verdict on any particular anomaly. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not a claim that a large residual is a discovery. Fig 5 :44 rejects "explains one occasion perfectly".
- Not a claim that agreement among experts settles anything. Fig 2 :35 and :40.
- Not a claim that the six-term substrate governs anomaly decisions. Re-census in `01` §6 assigns that question to Fig 5, Fig 9 and the Formula, not Fig 13.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by an instrument run for this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating test, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Benchmark test (F3, F4):** inject known signals and null fluctuations at matched residual magnitudes under a declared scan and stopping rule. Does cost-plus-leftover separate them, and does the residual persist only for the injected signals? A controlled result here would move the rule toward `grounded`.
2. **Arrangement test (Q3 of `06`):** vary the bond assignment in the isomer model (which atom is allowed to move) and see whether the class assignment changes at fixed residual magnitude. If no bond assignment carries the information, the isomer claim falls and only the functional-group claim stands.
3. **Channel test (F9):** construct an anomaly whose apparent signal disappears when a specific instrument parameter is varied, and confirm that the rule routes it to "mis-attached" rather than to a mint.
4. **Standing refuters:** the six refuters in `01` §4, written before the conclusions. Any one firing withdraws a finding.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books*: "The recognizer can only register an anomaly it has the primitives to represent." An anomaly along an axis the field cannot encode is invisible to every discoverer at once, and "no search, however long, adds an atom" (Fig 8 :32). The framework can say that something is missing in effect terms, and it can say what a candidate would have to look like, but it cannot name the missing primitive without already holding it (Fig 13, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"). The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not give a number for when an anomaly becomes a discovery. They give the shape of the decision. An unusual observation is a live residual in one tracked slot, so it must be read per slot, with the scan and stopping rule declared. Before judging it, sort it into the figure's five bins, because "discovery or fluctuation" is a bundle, not a binary. Then check four things: whether the candidate can be built from what the field already holds, whether stating it plus what it leaves unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes, whether the residual persists instead of trending down, and whether an independent instrument confirms it after a channel audit. A sigma or p-value is a filter that decides how much follow-up to buy, never the verdict, because the ground alone settles it. If the residual shows a held belief is false, the vocabulary must be able to refuse that belief instead of adding another explanation. What this offers is the form any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it, with no claim to have settled any particular anomaly.

## 7. Verdict fields

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. A wrong mint imports an atom that Fig 6 :24 prices above starting from primitives and can shrink the reach; a missed discovery leaves a live residual unfollowed. Sending a recording instead of a checkable rule also spends the independence of outside readers. All of these are recoverable if the record keeps the lineage and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes, in the record. "NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED" (Formula step 3). A false promotion is corrected by adding a refusal, not by silently erasing it; a missed signal is recovered by more independent search. The reach and the community's independence take time to rebuild, but no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled benchmark result, an arrangement test, a channel test, or any of the six pre-registered refuters in `01` §4. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
