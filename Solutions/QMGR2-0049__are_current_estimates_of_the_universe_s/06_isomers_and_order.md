# Comparison, settled molecules, and the two-camp union

**Mode: `specified` on the world-facing claims, `general` on the structure.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question "are age estimates consistent across independent methods?" hides three structural questions that need a derivation: (1) what kind of bond is "consistent"? (2) at what order is an age a composed term? (3) are the two camps isomers, convergent derivations, or something else? Answering these decides whether agreement can be counted as a witness.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the figure's sense: measurement slots (CMB map, ladder rung, GW strain, stellar age), a model (ΛCDM), and a derived scale (H0). Bonds held: ⋛ comparison, → sequence, ≡ identity, ⇒ production. No new physical primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What kind of bond is "consistent"?

- Fig 12 :87 and Operators table :49: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** defines comparison ⋛, with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?" (Operators table :51).
- "Consistent with" is a threshold against quoted error bars, not identity. Two ages of 13.797 and 13.801 Gyr are consistent under Planck's 0.023 Gyr error; the same pair would be wildly inconsistent under a 0.001 Gyr error. Fig 12 :99-101: "A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more," so the comparison is priced by the number of alternatives the error scale admits.
- ⇒ "Consistent" is ⋛, asymmetric in evidence provenance and scale-dependent. It is **not** ≡ ("two names, one referent"), so agreement does not license saying the two estimates are the same object.
- **Falsifier:** a case where two estimates are declared consistent with no declared error scale, or where consistency is treated as identity. If that is standard practice, Q1 is wrong about the bond.

### Q2: At what order is an age a composed term?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**
- H0 is a settled molecule: a composition of a measurement and ΛCDM that has paid against the ground many times. Age is composed over it: a first composition (measurement → H0) produces the atom; a second composition (H0 → age, via the Friedmann integral) produces the term. Age is therefore **second order**, not a direct reading.
- Turner (2024) confirms in world terms: age-redshift uncertainty is "dominated by the uncertainty in the Hubble constant," with Ωm, w and Ωk far less important. So the atom H0 carries almost all the error that the age term inherits.
- The transform is lossy, so the round trip must be measured: Fig 4 :30, "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." `11_audit.md` records the gap R_T qualitatively.
- **Falsifier:** a direct, model-independent age measurement. That would demote age to first order and break Q2.

### Q3: Are the two camps isomers, or convergent derivations, or neither?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- As the camps are actually built, early-universe inference uses the CMB and sound horizon; late-universe inference uses geometric distances, Cepheids/TRGB/JAGB and supernovae. They do **not** share the same atoms arranged differently, so under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two different molecules whose outputs are compared by ⋛ at a threshold.
- Within the early camp, Planck CMB and BAO do share an atom, the sound horizon, but they reach it by different routes. Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Whether this is genuine corroboration depends on independence, and Fig 8 :51-52 warns that "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Here the record shows a shared calibration, so the corroboration is at low, not maximum, independence.
- Within the late camp, Freedman (2024) reports three indicators on the same galaxies; two agree and "the Cepheids are an outlier right now." That is a within-camp bond failure, not a cross-camp isomer claim.
- **Falsifier:** if both camps could be written over one shared atom set differing only in which bond mediates which quantity, the isomer claim would stand and the "two different molecules" reading would fail. No source read here supports that.

### Q4: What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :54-57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The conditions are C1 (within-camp consistency) and C2 (cross-camp consistency). This is a union, not a partition.
- Dependency order (Fig 3 :20): perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. The disagreement sits at vocabulary/objective, that is, at the step that maps a measurement to H0 and then declares what would count as a consistent age. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The shared calibration is exactly that link.
- A single lever (average all estimates, or adopt one camp) clears C1 and leaves C2, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :55).
- **Falsifier:** a single lever that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition and falsify Q4.

### Q5: What closes the union, and can it be named?

- Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall" is import. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Composing harder inside ΛCDM cannot close a gap about the scale that ΛCDM supplies.
- Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name." So the answer is: an import is owed, characterized in effect terms as something that changes the normalization without breaking the observed shape of the expansion history. Verde et al. (2024) note that proposed fixes fall into three classes (early-time physics, global modification of the model's assumptions, or unmodeled systematics) and that "None of these three options has the consensus of the community." The framework selects none of them.
- **Falsifier:** a figure-derived term naming the specific import. Under the canonical limit this cannot happen; if it did, the limit claim would be false.

## CONCLUSION

"Consistent" is a scale-dependent ⋛ comparison, not identity (Q1). An age is a second-order term composed over the settled molecule H0, so it inherits H0's error and a round-trip gap (Q2). The two camps are neither isomers nor maximum-independence corroboration; they are different molecules with a shared calibration, so their internal agreement is a convention (Q3). The question is a union of within-camp and cross-camp conditions, ordered by dependency through the vocabulary/objective link (Q4). An import is owed but cannot be named (Q5). Each conclusion carries its falsifier above.
