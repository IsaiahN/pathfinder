# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly. The offer collapses on contact if Stage 1 does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One output to check: are "independent" age-of-the-universe estimates actually independent?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the world-facing claims, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`):**
>
> "The age of the universe" is not read off a clock. It is inferred: a measurement (a CMB map, a Cepheid distance, a gravitational-wave signal, an old star) is passed through a model, usually flat ΛCDM, to give H0 and then an age. So the question "are estimates consistent across independent methods?" is really a question about the independence of the estimating frames.
>
> Three things follow, and they are the output I want checked:
>
> 1. Agreement among methods that share a model and a calibration is not two witnesses. Planck CMB, BAO and supernovae under ΛCDM all give an age near 13.79 to 13.80 Gyr, but BAO's physical scale is set by the same sound horizon the CMB fixes. Their agreement is excellent for a common convention and carries no verdict weight about the world.
> 2. The methods that do not share that pool, early-universe inference versus the local distance ladder (and, at lower precision, standard sirens and old stars), do not agree. Their implied H0 values sit near 67 and 73 km/s/Mpc, which under the same densities imply ages near 13.8 and 12.9 Gyr. That gap has not shrunk with a decade of better data.
> 3. Because the gap persists while each camp is internally coherent, it is a union of two different questions, not one hard question. A single best-fit age clears the within-camp question and leaves the across-camp one standing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that new physics is required, that ΛCDM is wrong, that any collaboration made an error, or that the framework can name the missing thing. It returns a shape: an import is owed, something that moves the boundary between the two camps, but which import is not derivable from what is held. I am also not offering a `grounded` age.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. Two genuinely low-overlap methods shown to share no evidence and yet to agree on H0, hence age, within the precision needed.
> 2. The early versus late gap falling below 2σ under more data and systematic reanalysis, with no new model term.
> 3. A direct, model-independent measurement of cosmic age.
> 4. A single lever that restores both within-camp and across-camp consistency and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> If any one holds, the output is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I will not send them until you have recorded a verdict on the output above.
> - Planck Collaboration 2020, "Planck 2018 results. VI. Cosmological parameters": https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2020/09/aa33910-18/T1.html
> - Riess et al. 2022, SH0ES local H0: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/ac5c5b
> - Verde, Schöneberg and Gil-Marín 2024, "A Tale of Many H0": https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-astro-052622-033813
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula and figures are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name / role]
