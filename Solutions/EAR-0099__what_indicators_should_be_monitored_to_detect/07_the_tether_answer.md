# The Tether's answer: which indicators to monitor for early practical action

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s. A model predicts the earth system; the earth system returns readings; the gap is indexed by component and region, never averaged. The practical question is which readings o′ make that gap visible at a time when an action a can still change the outcome. That time condition is a comparison between a detected change and a decision boundary, not a property of an indicator alone (Fig 12 :85-87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*).

It is a **union** compound, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape: eight conditions

1. **Per slot, not aggregated.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A global mean can be near zero while one region carries the live signal. Monitor components and regions; keep a global index only as a summary, never as the warning.

2. **Directed, not just ordered.** Fig 13 :20 gives *"ordering, so more and less can be said"* (spectrum); Fig 13 :22 gives *"a difference that can be spent"* (gradient). Fig 13 :46-47: **"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."** A level tells you where things stand; a directed component tells you which way they are moving and how much headroom remains. Fig 13 :75-76 adds that a gradient over space and one over time are different terms, so spatial pattern and time trend are both potentially needed. The world literature names the directed statistics: variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, skewness (Scheffer 2009; Ditlevsen 2023). Those names are bindings from another frame, not figure derivations.

3. **Anchored to a ground measurement.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Indicators are ranged against that anchor separately. They do not negotiate with each other (Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

4. **Thresholded with a lead time.** "Early enough" is a comparison to a decision boundary tied to a goal. Operators table :45: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."* Without a comparison and a boundary, an indicator is a reading, not a warning.

5. **Channel instrumented.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**; Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**; Fig 10 :28: **"you have lost touch"**. In all four failure modes the internal instruments stay green. Monitor the channel itself: staleness, dropped transients, saturation limits, pooling across a change. Seat Map :285: *"Every instrument points inward unless one is built to point out."*

6. **Habitat enumerated, cascades included.** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** Read the list off the world and outward until the cascade stops mattering; re-enumerate when the residual changes.

7. **Independent frames, not more versions of one.** Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Satellite, in situ, archive and local knowledge are independent only if they draw on different evidence. Tipping errors are high cost, so buy independence.

8. **An instrument on the middle link.** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :25-26 says the link between having terms for what a situation offers and saying what counts as progress *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* This is where monitoring programmes usually stop.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- No final indicator list, no numeric thresholds, no claim to have named the right variable. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* That is a `gap`, stated plainly (F9 in `01`).
- No claim that critical slowing down, variance or autocorrelation ARE the figures' Gradient. They are world-frame bindings with their own provenance and their own validity conditions.
- No claim that a single global index is enough, and no claim that more indices automatically help.
- No claim that interdependence of earth-system processes (Stockholm Resilience Centre 2026) means the observing frames may overlap. Different objects.
- No claim of `grounded` status. No warning was tested against a held-out case where action changed the outcome.
- No claim that the framework is the only valid account of monitoring. That would be a uniqueness claim over a census never run, and a frame cannot certify its own limit.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An instrument and a held-out case, not agreement.

1. **Directed-component test:** does a statistic that orders values only (a level) give equal lead time to a directed statistic? If yes, condition 2 falls.
2. **Channel test:** do channel diagnostics stay green while warnings fail across regimes? If yes, the standard channel model in condition 5 is wrong.
3. **Cascade test:** does a panel with no second-order measurement surface a third-order residual? If yes, condition 6 is not necessary.
4. **Independence test:** do two frames sharing one evidence source add real corroboration weight? If yes, condition 7 falls.
5. **Action test (the decisive one):** in a held-out transition, does acting at the warning time change the outcome relative to acting at the baseline time? That is what pays for the shape. Nothing in this folder establishes it.
6. **Standing refuters** from `01` section 4, written before this answer: global aggregate wins; level-only indicator wins; green channel with systematic failure; fixed panel catches cascades; shared evidence corroborates; pure-description indicator works.

A source-discriminating instrument matters too. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"* and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Rising variance may be critical slowing down, an observation-density artifact or a trend. The discriminator is an instrument, not a further argument.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The shape stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing: the figure set constrains form, the world frame supplies candidates, and the ground pays or does not. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The lineage is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv` and checked by an independent second seat in `11_audit.md`, which shares this evidence pool and therefore corroborates nothing.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not name an indicator list. They constrain the shape any early-warning list must have. Monitoring must be indexed per component and region, because a global average hides the live signal. A reading must carry a directed component, not just an ordering, because only a difference that can be spent is a warning. Every indicator is ranged against a ground measurement and compared to a decision boundary with a lead time, because "early enough" is a threshold relation. The channel to the ground must itself be instrumented: stale readings, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled populations all fail silently while internal instruments report green. The habitat is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, the way DDT reached eagles three steps from the sprayed field. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and it comes from different evidence, not more versions of one retrieval. Finally, the least instrumented link is not the sensor but the leap from a reading to what would count as progress. What this offers is the shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific variables and thresholds stay open until the ground settles them.
