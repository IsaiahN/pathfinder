# Union, order and isomers in an indicator set

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question "which indicators, early enough for practical action" could be one problem or a bundle. If it is a bundle, a single lever will clear the region where only one condition holds and leave the intersections standing. This file resolves three things: (1) is the residual a union or a partition, (2) what is the dependency order, (3) are different indicator sets over the same earth-system atoms isomers, and what would falsify that.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (kept from `01` section 1)

Atoms held: the four domains D1 observation channel, D2 model frame, D3 earth-system habitat, D4 decision frame. Conditions held: P1 per-slot, P2 directed, P3 anchored, P4 thresholded, P5 channel-maintained, P6 habitat-enumerated, P7 independent, P8 instrumented middle link. No new primitive is available without import. Union, not partition, because all eight must hold at once for a warning to be actionable.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the residual a union or a partition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

"Early enough for practical action" is the intersection of at least two conditions: a detectable change (D1/D2/D3) and a decision window still open (D4). A better sensor clears the first and leaves the second standing. A lower policy threshold clears the second and leaves an undetectable change standing. Neither is a harder version of the other. They are a specific compound (Fig 9 :58 *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*).

- **Result:** union with at least two loops. P1 to P8 are not a checklist; the product structure matters (Formula step 3: *"gated by a product; any factor at zero forces inertness"*).
- **Falsifier:** one intervention that restores all eight conditions with no categorically different residue left over. If that is demonstrated, the union reading is wrong.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 gives five ordered links:

```
1 perception      can we read the system at all                 D1
2 vocabulary      what is in contact with the residual          D3
3 objective       what would count as progress (a threshold)    D4
4 planning        a route to action under incomplete information D4
5 learn and carry name what worked, verify, carry to next problem
```

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :25-26: the middle link *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

For earth-system monitoring, the break is usually between perception and objective: instruments exist and are counted, response plans exist, but the term that connects a reading to "this is progress / this is the point of no return" is thin. Most monitoring investment lands at perception because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). A programme can add sensors for a decade without moving the actionable-warning time at all.

- **Result:** order is perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → carry. Effort must be applied at the first broken link, found by measurement rather than assumed (Fig 3 :29).
- **Falsifier:** a programme that improves actionable lead time while its objective/vocabulary link tests as absent. If that is repeatable, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q3 - Are indicator sets over the same earth-system atoms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :92: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"*; Operators table :59: *"Symmetry decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*

Take the same atom set: atmosphere, ocean, cryosphere, land, biosphere, human land use (the habitat enumerated in Fig 11 :46-48). Two monitoring programmes that measure all six but arrange them differently (one indexes per region and compares variance to a local threshold; another pools globally and compares the mean to a global threshold) are not variants of one programme. Under Fig 12 :117 they are different substances with the same ingredients, because the arrangement (the bond) is not recoverable from the ingredient list. Their behavioural difference (one warns, one does not) is exactly what the figure says is *"invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Two programmes that share only the functional form `state → directed statistic → threshold` but no atoms are not isomers. They share a **functional group**: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"* (Fig 12 :124). This is the correct weaker claim when the atom sets differ.

- **Result:** the isomer claim is real when the atom set is held fixed and only the arrangement changes. It is `specified`, not `grounded`, because no programme comparison was run.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two programmes over a fixed atom set with different arrangements behave identically on held-out transitions (same lead time, same false alarms), the isomer claim fails and they are one substance under two spellings. If two programmes with different atom sets behave identically because they share only the functional group, the stronger claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - The cascade analogue

Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Carson, Silent Spring, 1962; the mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade (Fig 11 :54).

This is an order claim about the indicator, not just a warning about completeness. A first-order indicator panel (measure the sprayed field) is not a weaker version of a third-order panel (measure the top predator). The third-order residual is categorically different: it did not exist as a reading at first order. That is the same structure as Fig 9 :57-58, where the residue after a lever is *"smaller and also categorically different."*

- **Result:** cascade enumeration is an ordering operation on the habitat, not a quantity that can be traded off against sensor precision.
- **Falsifier:** a first-order indicator that surfaces a third-order residual without any second-order measurement in the chain. If that is common, the cascade ordering is not necessary.

### Q5 - What does the "functional group" look like in the world frame?

Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen 2023 construct an AMOC fingerprint as SST in the subpolar gyre minus twice the global mean SST anomaly (their Figure 1 and Methods). That is a composite: a regional reading bonded to a correction for a global trend, calibrated against direct measurements. It is a **settled molecule becoming an atom** for the next comparison (Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**): the fingerprint then serves as the operand whose variance and autocorrelation are compared to a threshold.

This is a binding from the world frame, not a figure derivation. It is included because it shows the figure's composition rule operating in a real monitoring chain, with its own calibration provenance. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

- **Falsifier:** the fingerprint is shown to be a raw global-mean reading with no independent regional content. Then it is not a composed term and the illustration fails.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union of conditions with a dependency order, not a list and not a single hard problem. The order runs perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → carry, and the usual break sits at the vocabulary/objective link, which has the fewest instruments. Two indicator programmes over the same earth-system atoms but with different arrangements are isomers in Fig 12's sense, with a stated falsifier; programmes with different atoms share only a functional group. Cascade enumeration is an ordering operation, not a precision tradeoff. A real world-frame example (the AMOC SST fingerprint) shows a settled composite becoming an atom for a threshold comparison. Nothing here is measured in this folder; all of it is `specified`.
