**Subject:** Check one shape claim about early-warning indicators before you decide about the framework

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about earth-system monitoring. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which indicators should be monitored early enough for practical action" is not one metric. It is a conjunction of eight conditions, and the figures I work from constrain the shape of each one. In effect terms:
>
> 1. Residual is indexed per component and region, not aggregated. A global mean can be near zero while one region carries the live signal.
> 2. A reading that only orders values is not enough. A directed component (a trend, a rate, a change in variance or autocorrelation) is what can be spent as a warning.
> 3. Every indicator is ranged against a ground measurement, not against other indicators.
> 4. "Early enough" is a threshold relation between the detected change and a decision boundary with a lead time. An indicator without a boundary is a reading, not a warning.
> 5. The channel to the ground must itself be instrumented. The named failure modes are stale readings, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled populations. In all four, internal instruments stay green.
> 6. The indicator list is enumerated from contact with the residual and extended outward until the cascade stops mattering. The figure's own example is DDT reaching eagles three steps out from the target.
> 7. Independence comes from different evidence, not from counting more versions of one measurement. High-cost errors justify buying more independence.
> 8. The least instrumented link is not the sensor. It is the link between having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress. Most effort lands above that break because that is where the measurements already exist.
>
> This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement, and not a final indicator list. The specific quantities (variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, spatial pattern, an SST fingerprint for a particular circulation) are bindings from the published literature onto that shape. They are offered with provenance, not as things the framework derived.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the final set of indicators, that this is the only valid monitoring framework, that any threshold number is settled, or that agreement with this shape would count as evidence for it.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a global aggregate that gives equal or better warning at equal false-alarm cost;
> 2. a level-only indicator with no directed component and no decision boundary that gives equal lead time;
> 3. channel diagnostics that stay green while warnings fail systematically;
> 4. a fixed panel with no cascade enumeration that still detects third-order effects;
> 5. two indicators drawing on one evidence source that add real corroboration weight.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, that condition drops out of the shape and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

> - Warning of a forthcoming collapse of the AMOC, Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen, Nature Communications 14, 2023: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39810-w
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local figures set. Run it on a monitoring problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
