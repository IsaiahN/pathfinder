# The Tether's answer: persistent world models that update

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question joins two conditions: persistence (a model held across time and changing circumstance) and updating (the model changed so it tracks the world when the world changes). It is a union compound, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* An answer that averages the two into one "yes" hides the fact that one is nearly free and the other is conditional on an external office.

## 2. The shape, with verdicts kept separate

1. **Persistence: YES, structurally.** Fig 6 :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."* THE_FORMULA step 4 stamps every admitted term with origin and sequence. The caveat is a boundary: *"Inside a run the arrangement persists and nothing is re-earned; across a boundary that drops it, everything is"* (Fig 12 :107 to :110). A model can be held. What is held is a record, not a guarantee of correctness.
2. **Updating: CONDITIONAL, not a property of the model alone.** Three things are required. (a) A refusal: Fig 5 :53, *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*, and the Operators table, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* (b) A live channel: Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* (c) An office outside the loop: Fig 11 :40, *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* THE_FORMULA step 8 assigns channel maintenance to a seat, not to the loop. **Triangulated** across three documents.
3. **Self-certification: NO.** A system cannot tell from inside whether its channel is open. Fig 8 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* Fig 10 :27 to :28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."* The correct internal output is a loss-of-contact report, not a freshness claim. **Triangulated.**
4. **The change discriminator: GAP.** Fig 6 :47: *"Whether that is two scopes, an error in the ground, or a world that changed is not yet decidable here."* The framework can register a residual and can say something changed; it cannot say from inside what changed. `single-source`, explicitly open. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."
5. **A model is a shape, not a copy.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The canonical README says the framework returns *"a shape, not a name."* The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap's objection, *"This is why world models will not work. You cannot recreate our reality,"* is recorded as a live conflict with a copy theory and is not smoothed over.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a complete model of the world. No claim that any system's readings determine their own source (the metamerism limit).
- Not that persistence implies correctness. The record grows; the reach can fall. They are isomers, not synonyms.
- Not that a model can certify its own freshness. A confidence readout is not a ground reading.
- Not that the three-way change discriminator is solved. Fig 6 :47 is open.
- Not a new architecture, not an implementation, and not `grounded`. No measurement is claimed.
- Not that "more data," "bigger context" or "more parameters" closes any of the above. Those are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating test, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Channel test:** a system's model is probed after a documented change in the environment, with the change measured independently of the system. Does the model's prediction track the changed ground, and is contact measured rather than asserted? This would fire on F3 and F4.
- **Negation test:** can the system refuse a held term, and does its reach demonstrably fall when it does, while the record still holds the term and its refusal? This fires on F2.
- **Boundary test:** carry the model across a boundary that drops the record. Does the system re-derive, or does the stored arrangement survive unchanged? This fires on F1.
- **Discriminator test:** an instrument inside the frame that distinguishes "world changed" from "held term is wrong" from "two scopes." None is known here; Fig 6 :47 says it is not decidable from inside. This would close the gap.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a system tracking a changed ground with no external seat; a refusal written with only the seven bonds; an internal discrimination of the three cases; a stored model surviving a record-dropping boundary; a confidence readout that discriminates a dead channel from a perfect prediction.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no in one word, because the question is two conditions. Persistence it grants structurally: what is recorded only grows, so a model can be held, with the caveat that a boundary dropping the record makes everything be paid again. Updating it makes conditional: it needs a refusal of a held term (the one unary operator, not a richer vocabulary), a live channel to an unmoving ground, and a seat outside the loop that keeps that channel open, because the ground does not decay, the channel does. A system cannot certify from inside that its channel is alive; a perfect prediction and a dead channel look the same. And when a term and its refusal are both settled, whether the world changed or the system was wrong is explicitly not decidable from inside the closure. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the open item named rather than hidden. The center stays open until a ground reading settles it.
