## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

**R:** Can a system hold a model of the world across time *and* change that model so it tracks the world when circumstances change?

- **Arity:** two conditions, persistence and updating. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A single lever that clears persistence leaves updating standing, and the reverse.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Updating has a direction, it is the refusal of something held. The ground does not update on the model. Fig 12's ordered bonds (`→` sequence, `⇒` production) and its unary negation carry direction; the three symmetric bonds (`+`, `∥`, `≡`) do not.
- **Scale:** persistence is a within-run property of the record; updating is a reach property that crosses from the frame to the ground and back. Two scales, a lossy transform between them (Fig 4 :27 to :32).

**What an answer would look like, bound before the run:** a record that only grows, paired with a reach that can fall, kept in contact with an unmoving ground by an office outside the loop. Binding this shape as an ordinary term follows Fig 5 :65: *"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."*

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Persistence is structurally cheap, but it is record persistence only (D1)

Fig 6 :42:

> **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."**

THE_FORMULA step 4: `Γ ← Γ ∪ {φ}`, stamped with origin and seq. Fig 12 :107 to :110:

> **"Inside a run the arrangement persists and nothing is re-earned; across a boundary that drops it, everything is."**

So a system can hold a model persistently. That is nearly free: nothing is ever deleted. But the figure attaches a boundary condition. Persistence is guaranteed only across boundaries that carry the record. A system whose weights hold a fact but whose record does not will pay again after any boundary that drops the record. *"Which boundary matters is the one the record does not cross"* (Fig 12 :109). Persistence is therefore easy to claim and easy to overclaim.

**Verdict: triangulated** across Fig 6 :42, Fig 12 :107 to :110 and THE_FORMULA step 4 (low overlap between the reach law, the substrate boundary caveat and the loop rule).

**Refuter:** demonstrate a stored model that survives a boundary that drops its record with no re-derivation. That breaks Fig 12 :107 to :110.

### F2 - Updating is a change of reach and requires negation, not a richer vocabulary (D2)

Fig 5 :53:

> **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* And Fig 6 :42 again: the reach can fall because something was **added**, a refusing term, not because anything was deleted.

Updating a model is not "add more facts." It is: identify a held term that keeps being wrong (Fig 5 :58 to :60, the persistent-and-flat residual), write a term that refuses it, and let the reach fall. A system with only the seven binary bonds can compose indefinitely and still return nothing new it can retract (Fig 5 :49). The fifth bin sorts by what is **wrong**, not by what is missing.

**Verdict: triangulated** across Fig 5 :53, the Operators table and Fig 12 :140.

**Refuter:** exhibit a system that writes a refusal of a held term using only the seven binary bonds and no unary operator. That breaks the arity distinction in the Operators table :70 to :74.

### F3 - Updating is not a property of the model alone; it needs a live channel and an office that keeps it (D2)

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :29, *"→ keep the anchor reachable."* Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

And Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* THE_FORMULA step 8 states the division of labour: *"Nothing here maintains the ground. The ground does not decay; the channel does, and that is a seat's office, not the loop's."*

A world model that "updates as circumstances change" is therefore a claim about agent-and-habitat. The stale case is named in Fig 10 :25 to :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. The fix is restoring the reading, not adjudicating what it would have said. There is no amount of internal improvement that substitutes for an external channel.

**Verdict: triangulated** across Fig 10 :24 to :29, Fig 11 :39 to :40 and THE_FORMULA step 8 (framework-internal laws from three separate documents).

**Refuter:** a system whose model demonstrably tracks a changed ground with no external seat maintaining contact, where contact is measured, not asserted. That breaks Fig 11 :40. Note that a readout of the system's own confidence is not such a measurement (F4).

### F4 - The system cannot certify that it is updating (D3)

Fig 8 :44:

> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."**

Fig 10 :27 to :28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."* Seat Map :285: *"Every instrument points inward unless one is built to point out."* THE_FORMULA closure note: the loop stops when R stops arriving, *"which is either a perfect prediction or a closed channel, and from inside those look the same."*

So the honest report of a dead channel is *"contact has been lost"*, not *"the world is unchanged."* A model's own statement that it is up to date is exactly the output F4 disqualifies. This is why the two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md` asks for a verdict from an outside frame before showing any theory.

**Verdict: triangulated** across Fig 8 :44, Fig 10 :27 to :28, THE_FORMULA closure note and Seat Map :285.

**Refuter:** a system that discriminates a dead channel from a perfect prediction using only its own internal readings. That breaks the symmetry named in the closure note.

### F5 - The decisive discriminator is an open item, and this is a gap (D4)

Fig 6 :47, verbatim:

> **"Open. If a term and its refusal are both settled, the record holds a contradiction. Whether that is two scopes, an error in the ground, or a world that changed is not yet decidable here."**

This figure names the exact question the user asks ("updates as circumstances change") and declares it undecidable from inside the closure. A residual that fires because the ground returned a different value can mean three things: two scopes were conflated, an instrument erred, or the world changed. The record holds both the term and its refusal; nothing in the closure says which reading applies. Fig 13 :134 to :138 confirms the limit: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* but *"It cannot say which primitive is missing."*

**Verdict: `gap` for the discriminator.** One figure names it and leaves it open. `single-source`. This is not a refusal to answer; it is the answer the framework gives: the system can register that something changed and cannot say, from inside, what changed. An outside seat with a different closure is required. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

**Refuter:** a derivation from the figures that closes the three-way split, or a demonstration that an instrument inside the frame can discriminate the cases. Until one is run, the gap stands.

### F6 - The question is a union, so verdicts must be kept separate

Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

And Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Persistence and updating overlap (a record is needed to know what was refused) but they are not the same condition. A single "yes" averaged over both hides the asymmetry: persistence is structural and cheap, updating is conditional and expensive. Fig 1 :22 warns what averaging costs: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Verdict: `specified`, single-source for the structural reading** (Fig 9), corroborated by the world sources on concept drift and digital twins (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter:** demonstrate that one mechanism closes both conditions with the same instrument and the same anchor. That would unbundle nothing and would invalidate the two-domain split.

### F7 - A "model of the world" is a shape, not a copy, and one world source conflicts here

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The canonical figures README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* So the Tether can characterise what any persistent updating model must be; it cannot name a complete model of the world.

A genuine conflict must be recorded. Isaiah N, *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026-11-19), writes: **"This is why world models will not work. You cannot recreate our reality. It is combinatorically impossible. The best you could do is take traces of patterns."** This conflicts with a strong "yes, a faithful persistent world model is buildable" reading. Per the conflict rule, the conflict sends us back to the census rather than flipping the verdict. Re-census: the essay targets *recreating* reality (a copy), while the figures license a shape that tracks the ground through a channel. As the same essay says: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* The essay therefore corroborates F4 while conflicting with the strong reading of F7. It shares an author and evidence pool with the figures, so it is weak corroboration on F4 and a live objection on F7. Both are kept visible.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 9 :32 and the canonical README, with a declared world-source conflict on the strong reading.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a system holds a complete model whose readings determine their own source. That would defeat the shape limit. The metamerism argument in *The Hard Problem is About Source* is the standing obstacle: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A system is shown to keep a model whose reach tracks a changed ground with no external seat maintaining contact, where contact is measured (breaks F3).
2. A system writes a refusal of a held term using only the seven binary bonds and no unary operator (breaks F2).
3. A system discriminates, from inside its own closure, "the world changed" from "a held term is wrong" (closes F5).
4. A stored model survives a boundary that drops its record with no re-derivation (breaks F1).
5. A model's own confidence reading is shown to discriminate a dead channel from a perfect prediction (breaks F4).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. This refuter is carried verbatim into `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Answer the question as two verdicts, never one. Persistence: yes, structurally. Updating: conditional on negation, a live channel and a seat. Say the gap on the change discriminator plainly.
2. State the mode on every claim (F4 and THE_FORMULA mode rule).
3. Do not report a model's self-report of freshness as evidence. Bring an outside frame and a ground reading (Fig 10 :42).
4. Keep the shape claim and the refuter in front of the links. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, not the conclusion.
5. Treat the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap's "world models will not work" as a live objection to the strong reading, not as a problem to smooth over. Publish it alongside the answer.
