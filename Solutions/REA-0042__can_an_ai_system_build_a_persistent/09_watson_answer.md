## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a model that is both held across time and revised as circumstances change. Option A: answer yes, on the grounds that systems already store and update internal representations. Option B: split the question, grant persistence structurally, make updating conditional on a refusal, a channel and a seat, and report the change-discriminator as open. What each leads to: A averages two conditions and certifies a channel from inside, which F4 disqualifies; B gives separate checkable verdicts, a pre-registered refuter and a named open item, at the cost of not delivering a single word answer.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**Two verdicts, never averaged.** Persistence: yes, structurally, with a boundary caveat. Updating: conditional on a unary refusal, a live channel and a seat that maintains it; not a property of the model alone. Self-certification: no. The discriminator "world changed versus I was wrong versus two scopes" is an open gap at Fig 6 :47, and is reported as a gap. The model is a shape, not a copy. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest objection to the strong reading is published alongside it: *"This is why world models will not work. You cannot recreate our reality."*

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters, the open gap and the conflicting source before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is certifying a channel from inside and reporting a silent dead channel as a correct model. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One measured outside check is worth more than many agreeing witnesses.
- **undoable:** yes. The three conditions are separable. A channel test, a negation test and a boundary test can be run without withdrawing the shape. Every cut is kept ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41). No primitive has to be withdrawn if a refuter fires; a refusing term is added and the record keeps both.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A channel test showing a model tracking an independently measured environmental change; a negation test showing a refusal with fall of reach; a boundary test showing no re-derivation; or an instrument closing the three-way split at Fig 6 :47 would move the verdict. Prestige, benchmark scores and later agreement are not such instruments.
