# Expert verification packet - persistent world models that update

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in world models, continual learning or machine-learning theory can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is two conditions, not one. Persistence is structural: what is recorded only grows, so a model can be held, with a boundary caveat. Updating is not a property of the model alone. It requires a refusal of a held term (negation, the one unary operator), a live channel to an unmoving ground, and a seat outside the loop that keeps that channel reachable. The system cannot certify its own channel from inside, and whether a change was in the world or in its own held terms is explicitly open in the source figures. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the open item named rather than hidden.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 6

- **Statement:** record and reach are two quantities. The record grows; the reach can fall. Persistence and updating must not be averaged into one verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :42 - **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."** Also Fig 9 :57 - **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system in which adding a refusal leaves the reach unchanged or increases it while the record grows; or demonstrate that one instrument with one anchor closes both conditions. That breaks the two-quantity reading.

### C2 - framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading the Operators table and Figure 10

- **Statement:** updating requires three things that a better model in isolation does not supply: a unary refusal, a live channel, and an outside office that maintains it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :58 - **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** Fig 10 :24 - **"the ground does not decay, the channel does."** Fig 11 :40 - **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
- **What to check (field-facing):** is it true in your system that a held term can be refused, that the channel to the world is a measured quantity, and that improving the model alone leaves the contact unchanged? Compare with the catastrophic-forgetting result: *"maintain expertise on tasks which they have not experienced for a long time"* (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017). That result mitigates the tension; it does not derive the three requirements.
- **Refuter:** a system whose model tracks a changed ground with no external seat maintaining contact, where contact is measured; or a refusal written with only the seven binary bonds and no unary operator.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 6 and Figure 8

- **Statement:** a system cannot certify from inside that it is tracking a changing world, and the three-way split (two scopes, error in the ground, world changed) is open. This is reported as a gap, not hidden.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :44 - **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."** Fig 6 :47 - **"Whether that is two scopes, an error in the ground, or a world that changed is not yet decidable here."** Fig 10 :27 to :28 - **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."**
- **Refuter:** a system that discriminates a dead channel from a perfect prediction using only its own internal readings; or an instrument inside the frame that closes the three-way split. The latter would close the declared gap and is the single most valuable result someone could return.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a complete model of the world. The framework returns a shape, not a name (canonical README), and *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that persistence implies correctness. Same atoms under a different refusal arrangement are isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"* (Fig 12 :117).
- Not that a confidence readout or a self-report of freshness is evidence. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
- Not that the change discriminator is solved. It is open at Fig 6 :47.
- Not an architecture, not an implementation, not `grounded`, and not a claim that more data or parameters close anything (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict"*).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Channel test:** probe the model after an independently measured change in the environment. Does the prediction track the changed ground, and is contact measured rather than asserted?
2. **Negation test:** does the system refuse a held term, does its reach fall, and does the record still contain both the term and its refusal?
3. **Boundary test:** carry the model across a boundary that drops the record. Does it re-derive, or does the stored arrangement survive?
4. **Discriminator test:** an instrument that distinguishes "world changed" from "held term wrong" from "two scopes." None is known here.
5. **Standing refuters:** any of the five in section 2 and in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :42 and :47, Fig 8 :44, Fig 10 :24, Fig 11 :40 and the Operators table negation entry. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own system: is the channel a measured quantity, and can a held term be refused at all?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a dead channel from a perfect prediction, or say why none is possible. This is the live question.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement after seeing the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
