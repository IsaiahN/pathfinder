**Subject:** Check one claim about persistent world models before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can an AI system build a persistent model of the world that updates as circumstances change?" is two conditions, not one. The figures I am working from give two different, non-averaged verdicts:
>
> 1. **Persistence is structural.** What is recorded only grows. A model can be held across time. The caveat is a boundary: where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup; across a boundary that drops the record, everything is paid again. Fig 6 :42 and Fig 12 :107 to :110.
> 2. **Updating is not a property of the model alone.** It needs a refusal of a held term (negation, the one unary operator), a live channel to a ground, and a seat outside the loop that keeps that channel reachable. *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24), and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves"* (Fig 11 :40).
>
> I am also reporting an honest gap. Fig 6 :47 says that when a term and its refusal are both settled, whether that is two scopes, an error in the ground, or a world that changed *"is not yet decidable here."* So the framework can say that something changed and cannot say, from inside, what changed. I am not smoothing that over.
>
> **What this is not claiming:** not that a model can be a complete copy of reality. One of the sources I am using says directly, *"This is why world models will not work. You cannot recreate our reality. It is combinatorically impossible. The best you could do is take traces of patterns."* I am publishing that objection alongside the claim. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Not `grounded`: no measurement is claimed.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written before this message):
> 1. A system keeps a model whose reach tracks a changed ground with no external seat maintaining contact, with contact measured.
> 2. A system writes a refusal of a held term using only the seven binary bonds and no unary operator.
> 3. A system discriminates from inside its own closure "the world changed" from "a held term is wrong."
> 4. A stored model survives a boundary that drops its record with no re-derivation.
>

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the two verdicts, the open gap and the refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. **Record that verdict before showing any theory.**
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure text and the literature links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because an outside frame that has absorbed the framework has joined the frame it was there to check (Fig 2 :40, Seat Map :163).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The honest "cannot certify from inside" result (F4) is part of that output, so an objection to it is a useful failure rather than something to defend.
