# Expert verification packet - the Tether on cross-scale climate governance

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate governance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rule, explicitly not `grounded`.** No measurement of a governance arrangement is claimed.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A compound, cascading Earth-system risk is one problem with three levels, not three problems added together. Any arrangement that coordinates the response must run the same decision loop at local, national and international scale, with only actors, currency and anchor changing; must pass reapplicable methods upward and head starts downward, measuring the round-trip gap on each; must keep the Earth-system measurement as the anchor at every level rather than an agreement count; must buy genuine independence between decision centres; must enumerate the contact habitat out to the third-order cascade; and must have a move that refuses a failing term. The figures do not name or rank a governance kind. Polycentric, adaptive, multilevel and subsidiarity-based arrangements are named as provenanced imports from the literature, not as figure results.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Union compound, one kernel at three sizes

- **Statement:** the three levels are levels of one loop over a union compound, not a partition into three problems. A case in two levels is a specific compound with its own loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57-58, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 4 :19-20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
- **What to check:** does the risk in your domain actually cross levels through an intersection (a local adaptation shifting risk nationally, a national decision changing local exposure), rather than being three separable residuals? Can the same decision loop be restated at each level with only actors, currency and anchor changed?
- **Refuter:** show that the residuals are separable, or that a governance form that closes its residual at one level has no restatement at another with only those three changes.

### C2 - Ground anchor and independence

- **Statement:** the anchor at each level is the Earth-system measurement, and multiple centres only corroborate when they draw on different evidence. Agreement counts cannot settle the question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :31-32, "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." and "Independence is what makes agreement mean something." Fig 10 :47, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- **What to check:** in a coordination process you know, what actually changed the verdict: a measurement, or the number of signatories and frameworks? Do the reviewing bodies use different instruments, or the same assessment?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a coordination success where the anchor moved in response to what the frames thought, or where two bodies reading the same assessment counted as independent corroboration against the measurement.

### C3 - Membrane direction, habitat scope, refusal

- **Statement:** methods travel up and head starts travel down, each with a measured round-trip gap; the habitat is enumerated to the third-order cascade; and the vocabulary needs a refusal move.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :30, "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 11 :44, "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52-53, "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Operators table :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- **What to check:** name one cross-scale transfer you know and say whether what moved was a method or a replayed blueprint. Name a governance actor that mattered three steps out in a risk you have studied. Can the arrangement withdraw a failing instrument?
- **Refuter:** show a fixed sector list handling a third-order cascade as well as an enumerated habitat; show a nominal reorganisation with no contact change reducing the residual; or show a cross-scale transfer with a zero round-trip gap on a named instrument.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a best governance kind, and not a ranking of polycentric against adaptive against subsidiarity.
- Not that polycentricity was derived from the figures. Ostrom, Folke et al., Biermann et al. and the EU Treaty are world imports with provenance and a shadow test.
- Not that more institutions help. More rooms reading the same report are one long room.
- Not a single hierarchy and not a flat partnership of equal peers.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is reported here.
- Not a final governance recipe. Fig 7 names "the limit: no last room".

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating measurement against the Earth-system anchor. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and several sources can give the same reading. A report saying "risk reduced" is the same projection: real exposure reduction, a boundary change, or a metric that stopped registering a slow-onset hazard can all produce it. The instrument that discriminates those cases, not a further argument, would name which arrangement closed the residual. For the shape claim, the four refuters in section 2 are the settling tests.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55 and :57-58, and Fig 4 :19-20 and :22 (four short passages).
2. Check C1 against one risk you know: is it a union compound across levels, or three separable problems?
3. Check C2: in that same case, did the verdict follow a measurement or an agreement count? Did the reviewers share one evidence pool?
4. Name one cross-scale transfer and classify it as method or recording. Say what the round-trip gap would be and how you would measure it.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Later agreement is not pooled as corroboration.
