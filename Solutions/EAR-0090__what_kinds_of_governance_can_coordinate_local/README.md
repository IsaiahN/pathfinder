**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive shape claims, `general` on the structural constraint. No named governance kind is claimed to be `grounded` by this derivation; nothing here settles which institution actually works.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending (set by the registry, not by this folder).

**Boundary.** All work stayed inside this folder. No framework file, registry, question file, or other solution was read for the purpose of editing or changed. Figure text was read from the local canonical extract only.

## What an answer would look like

Stated in effect terms and bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): an answer is a predicate `coordinates(R, G)`, where `R` is the cross-scale residual (compound, cascading Earth-system risk) and `G` ranges over governance arrangements. The question asks for the `G` that satisfy the predicate. The derivation returns the **shape** such a `G` must have, keeps the named candidates as provenanced imports, and records a gap where the figures cannot rank institutions.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, and no purchased or scraped contact list.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that one named governance kind is the answer. The figures return a shape, not a name, and the question as literally worded stays open at the level of named institutions.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not the ground.** Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list of people selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :31, "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
3. **Do not spend the checker's independence.** Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
