# Levels, order and analogues: why three scales are one loop

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the bond definitions.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for "kinds of governance" that coordinate three levels. Two supporting questions are owed. (1) Are local, national and international three things to be composed, or three sizes of one thing? (2) If different named institutions satisfy the same shape, what is the relation between them, and what order binds the levels?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three levels three institutions or three sizes of one loop?

Fig 4 :19-20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :16-17: "one agent" and "the step-by-step loop, and the thing that names what it found." Fig 4 :4-5: "the population" and "many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce."

So a city council, a national ministry and a treaty body are **the same kernel at three sizes** if they each run the same loop and range against the same anchor. They are not three institutions to be stacked. Treating them as three separate problems is the Fig 9 union error (F1 in `01`).

**Falsifier, written here:** exhibit two levels whose decision loops have no shared kernel under any restatement that changes only actors, currency and anchor, and which nonetheless close the same residual. That would break Fig 4's scale rule.

**Verdict:** three sizes, one kernel. `general` structural, triangulated (Fig 4 plus the Ostrom and Folke world sources, which are low-overlap with the figure).

### Q2 - What is the bond between the levels?

Not `+`. Under conjunction, "both hold at once" (Fig 12 :57) and the bond is symmetric: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No" (Fig 12 :59). Local and national action are not interchangeable in this residual; swapping them changes the outcome.

Yes `→`. Fig 12 :71-72, "A then B; B needs A to have happened." A national standard that arrives before any local method exists has nothing to compress and becomes a recording. Fig 3 :20, "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The dependency order is: ground reachable, then vocabulary at the level, then objective, then coordination plan.

But the relation is not a single chain. Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." That is two directed paths with different currencies, so it is a cycle with two legs rather than one sequence. Fig 12 :121, "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

**Verdict:** the levels form a two-legged cycle of ordered bonds, not a flat conjunction and not a one-way hierarchy. `specified`, single-source for the figure definition of the two paths.

### Q3 - Are different governance kinds isomers or members of a functional group?

Fig 12 :117, "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124, "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- As the world literature names them, polycentric governance, adaptive co-management and subsidiarity-based multilevel governance share a **functional group**: multiple decision centres at multiple scales ranging against a ground. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member. They are **not** isomers on that description, because their atom sets differ (markets and networks in one, bridging organisations and social memory in another, legal competence in the third).
- The stronger claim becomes available if both are written over the full shared atom set {anchor, loop, membrane, habitat, independence, refusal}: two arrangements with the same atoms, differing only in which bond connects which pair, would be **isomers**, "same atoms, different bond, different substance." That is a checkable structural claim, not yet measured.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one arrangement can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. The world sources as read do require different atoms, so the functional-group reading is the one that survives this pass.

**Verdict:** `specified`, functional group confirmed at the level of named kinds; isomer claim held open over the shared atom set.

### Q4 - Where does progress live in such a system?

Fig 12 :112, "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

A governance inventory that lists institutions (states) but has no sequence, no comparison against the anchor and no subtraction of residual cannot say whether coordination improved. IPCC AR6 SYR C.6 adds the instrument side: "Clear goals, coordination across multiple policy domains, and inclusive governance processes facilitate effective climate action." Monitoring and evaluation is the comparison; the residual is the subtraction.

And the refusal move is required. Operators table :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A system that can add frameworks but not withdraw a failing one has no way to make the reach fall except by accident.

**Verdict:** `general` structural for the need for sequence, comparison, subtraction and negation; `specified` for the claim that this particular governance vocabulary lacks the refusal move until one is added.

## CONCLUSION

The three levels are three sizes of one decision loop, joined by a two-legged cycle (methods up, head starts down) with a measured round-trip gap. They are a union compound, not a partition. Different named governance kinds satisfy the same functional group and are isomers only over a shared atom set, a claim held open with a stated falsifier. Progress requires a sequence, a comparison against the Earth-system anchor, a subtraction of residual, and a refusal move. Nothing here names a best kind.
