## 0. The question under audit, stated in effect terms

> "What kinds of governance can coordinate local, national, and international responses to Earth-system risks?"
>
> Source: `earth systems.txt` line 118. Section 9, Human infrastructure, society, and adaptation.

**Option A:** answer with a list of named governance kinds (polycentric, adaptive co-management, multilevel governance, subsidiarity-based unions, treaty regimes) and treat the list as the result.

**Option B:** derive the shape any coordinating kind must have, keep the named list as provenanced imports with a source gate and a shadow test, and state the gap where the figures cannot rank institutions.

**What each leads to.** A hands over a name where the figures return only a shape, and it spends the independence of the people asked to check it. B hands over a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and an honest stop. The derivation takes B. This is not a preference for modesty. It follows from Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself", and from Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** Formula step "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape it seeks is a preference, not a term. So the answer predicate is written down now: `coordinates(R, G)` holds when the arrangement `G` keeps the Earth-system anchor reachable at all three scales and closes the cross-scale residual `R` without substituting agreement for measurement. Written down, the preference can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Residual R

**In effect terms.** Earth-system risks are compound and cascading. They originate in emissions and land use that no local authority bounds, they land in impacts that no single ministry sees end to end, and they are governed by international agreements that cannot implement themselves. The gap is between the structure of the risk (compound, cascading, crossing administrative boundaries) and the scope of authority of any one governance frame.

- **Arity:** three levels named in the question (local, national, international), but not three independent residuals. This is one compound with three conditions.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. Coordination is directional. Methods move up, head starts move down, and the levels are not interchangeable peers.
- **Scale:** first order inside each level, second order at the interfaces between levels, third order at the cascade where a local adaptation shifts risk to a neighbour or to a future generation.

**Name the anchor.** The anchor is the Earth-system measurement itself: emissions, concentrations, temperature, exposure, loss, ecological state. It does not update on what any governance frame thinks. Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :36, "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." The question is constitutively about whether risk fell, so a measurement is the right anchor and an agreement count is not.

A ministry, a city council and a treaty body differ in actors and currency; if they also differ in anchor, they have stopped being levels of one system and become drifting frames.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - The risk is a union compound, not three separate problems

Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57-58, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

World corroboration (secondary): IPCC AR6 SYR headline B.2, "Climatic and non-climatic risks will increasingly interact, creating compound and cascading risks that are more complex and difficult to manage". IPCC AR6 WGII headline B.5, "Multiple climate hazards will occur simultaneously, and multiple climatic and non-climatic risks will interact, resulting in compounding overall risk and risks cascading across sectors and regions."

So the "three levels" in the question are not three slots to be solved and added. A case in two levels is a specific compound with its own loop. A governance arrangement that only sums local plus national plus international action leaves the intersections standing.

**Refuter:** show that the three residuals are separable, that no adaptation at one level shifts risk to another level or to a later time, and that a case in two levels is just a harder case of one. The IPCC statements above would then have to be wrong.

**Status:** triangulated. Low overlap between Fig 9 (composition logic) and IPCC risk assessment (empirical assessment). The world source corroborates; it does not decide.

### F2 - The kernel is the same at all three levels; only actors, currency and anchor change

Fig 4 :19-20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :21, "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."

This is the figure-derived content of "multilevel" or "nested" governance. It is not a claim that one institution rules over the others, nor that each level is independent. It is the claim that the same perceive, decide, act, check-against-the-ground loop runs at each size, with different actors and different currency, and that what passes between sizes is filtered and directional.

World corroboration: Ostrom's Nobel lecture defines the central term in effect terms, "'Polycentric' connotes many centers of decision making that are formally independent of each other. Whether they actually function independently, or instead constitute an interdependent system of relations, is an empirical question in particular cases." Folke et al. 2005: "Such governance connects individuals, organizations, agencies, and institutions at multiple organizational levels."

**Refuter:** exhibit a governance form that closes its residual at one level whose kernel cannot be restated at another level with only actors, currency and anchor changed, and no loss of prediction. Or exhibit a membrane with no direction, where everything crosses both ways with equal cost.

**Status:** triangulated. Low overlap between Fig 4's scale membrane and two independent literatures (institutional economics, resilience ecology). The names are imports; the kernel constraint is figure-derived.

### F3 - Coordination is directional: methods cross up, head starts cross down

Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :23-24, "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." and "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once." Fig 4 :25, "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

Applied here: local experimentation produces reusable methods (what worked in one ecosystem under one hazard) and these should travel upward. National and international levels should send down head starts (finance, models, standards, information) that make the next local search cheaper. A uniform blueprint sent downward is a recording. It produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one, which is exactly the maladaptation failure the surrounding questions in section 9 are about.

World corroboration: Ostrom 2010 World Bank paper, "A polycentric approach has the main advantage of encouraging experimental efforts at multiple levels, leading to the development of methods for assessing the benefits and costs of particular strategies adopted in one type of ecosystem and compared to results obtained in other ecosystems." EU Treaty on European Union Article 5(3) (subsidiarity): "the objectives of the proposed action cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States, either at central level or at regional and local level, but can rather, by reason of the scale or effects of the proposed action, be better achieved at Union level." Subsidiarity is a world import that passes the shadow test for this figure: it says act at the level that lowers the search cost, not at the highest level by default.

**Refuter:** show a transferred recording (a fixed blueprint substituting for local prediction) that lowers local search cost without replacing local search, or show a locally reapplicable method that cannot cross upward at all. Either breaks the method/recording distinction this finding rests on.

**Status:** triangulated for the shape (Fig 4 plus Ostrom plus EU law), single-source for the word "recording" (Fig 4 only). The strain noted between this figure and binding international law is handled in section 6.

### F4 - Keep the ground reachable at every level; agreement is not the anchor

Fig 2 :29 (quoted in section 1). Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :29, "keep the anchor reachable." Fig 10 :47, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

A coordination process that reports success by counting agreements signed, frameworks adopted or meetings held has substituted a convention for the ground. The failure is not laziness; it is a category error. The question is constitutively about risk, so only a measurement settles it. Biermann et al. 2010 names scale as a crosscutting research theme precisely because the architecture question keeps separating from the outcome question.

**Refuter:** name an institution whose answer changes when the measurement changes in the direction that would make it a better answer, and show that agreement counts played no role in its verdict. Or show a coordination success that reduced risk where the anchor moved in response to what the frames thought. The second would break Fig 2's anchor rule.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10 are low-overlap figures, plus IPCC C.6's emphasis on monitoring and evaluation).

### F5 - Independence between decision centres is load-bearing, not decorative

Fig 2 :31-32, "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." and "Independence is what makes agreement mean something." Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 8 :45, "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :47-48, "Independence is one quantity read twice." and "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

For Earth-system risks the cost of being wrong is high, so the figure says buy independence in proportion. Two national agencies reading the same assessment and reaching the same conclusion are one witness with two names. Two instruments (a satellite record and a community observation network) pointed at the same exposure are two frames. This is the structural reason a single global modelling frame cannot be the whole coordination system, and also the reason that adding more bodies that share one evidence pool does not help.

**Refuter:** show that two bodies drawing on the same assessment and report count as independent corroboration for this question, or that multiplying bodies with one shared pool reduces the residual against the measurement.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 8 and the Ostrom Nobel lecture's explicit "empirical question" clause. The clause is important: polycentricity does not guarantee independence, it makes independence possible and measurable.

### F6 - Scope must follow the contact cascade, not an administrative boundary

Fig 11 :43-44, "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." and "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :49-51, "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." and "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :52-53, "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

The figure's own worked case is an Earth-system case. A governance mandate scoped to first-order contact misses the third-order effect. This connects the coordination question to the neighbouring question about adaptation shifting risk to neighbours or future generations (EAR-0089). The habitat must be enumerated for each residual; it cannot be composed from a fixed list of sectors.

**Refuter:** exhibit a standing checklist of actor types, fixed before the risk was known, that handles a three-step cascade as well as an enumerated habitat does, with the same measurement anchor.

**Status:** triangulated. Fig 11 (habitat enumeration) and IPCC B.5 (cross-sector cascades) are different kinds of evidence and low overlap.

### F7 - Capability is agent-and-habitat; restructuring without contact change does nothing

Fig 11 :39-40, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." and "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

A reorganisation that renames agencies, merges departments or adds a coordination summit without changing who measures what, whose decision reaches which exposure, or what the finance pays for, has changed the agent and not the contact. It cannot reduce the residual. This is the figure-grounded reason that "which kind of governance" cannot be answered by naming a structure: the same structure has different capability in different habitats.

**Refuter:** exhibit a purely nominal reorganisation that changes no measurement contact, no decision path to exposure and no finance flow, yet measurably reduces the risk residual. This would refute the capability claim at the ground.

**Status:** single-source for the figure claim (Fig 11), with the IPCC WGII D.2 point about finance and decision support tools as weak corroboration. The exemplar's own note applies: this figure rule "fits cases already known and has not been tested forward."

### F8 - Every cross-scale transfer carries a measured round-trip gap

Fig 4 :27-30, "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." and "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 4 :31-32, "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."

When a local method is abstracted into a national guideline and concretised back into a local decision, something is dropped. `R_T = gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x))`. Treating "a national strategy summarises local experience" as lossless is the dead-reckoning error. The honest report names the dropped constraints and measures them where an instrument exists.

**Refuter:** exhibit a cross-scale transfer with a zero round-trip gap on a named instrument, or show that the abstraction and concretisation transforms compose to identity for this domain.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 round trip plus the Symbols table definition of `R_T`, plus `THE_FORMULA.md` step 6). Not measured numerically here.

### F9 - The figures return a shape, not a name: the literal question stays partly open

Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134-135, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The canonical figures README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and stops at the limit of the instruments, recording the lineage.

Therefore the answer to "what kinds" at the level of named institutions is a **gap after re-census**, not a silence and not a failure. What is closed is the shape: nested, ground-anchored, method-up and head-start-down, independent centres, enumerated habitat, with a refusal move. The names (polycentric, adaptive co-management, multilevel governance, subsidiarity) come from world sources and enter as imports with a source gate and a shadow test: Fig 6 :12-13, "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap." Fig 6 :23, "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that names a governance kind, or show that the six-term substrate plus the operators determines a unique institutional form. Until one of these fires, the gap stands.

**Status:** triangulated for the limit (Fig 9 and Fig 13 and the canonical README). The specific ranking of named kinds is `gap`, explicitly.

### F10 - A governance vocabulary that can only accumulate cannot correct

Operators table :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 12 :112, "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

Coordination systems whose only move is to add another treaty, agency, fund or framework cannot withdraw a failing one. Fig 6 :42-43: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." A refusal is a legitimate move and must be available. This is why the packet in `08` asks what would falsify the shape, not whether the reader endorses it.

**Refuter:** show an accumulating-only governance vocabulary that can remove the standing of a failed arrangement without adding a refusal term, and that does so against the measurement anchor.

**Status:** single-source for the framework claim (Operators table and Fig 12 share an author and evidence pool; counted as one witness).

### F11 - More rooms do not help if they are mirrors

Fig 7 :35, "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36, "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

A chain of review panels that all read the same assessment is one long room. The next room must be selected against the residual the last one surfaced, and drawn from what is available, not hand-picked for likely agreement.

**Refuter:** show a chain of replicating committees whose agreement reduced the residual against an independent measurement, without introducing a new frame or instrument.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 2, different figures, low conceptual overlap at this level).

## 5. Strain test and re-census

A figure line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. Three candidate strains were found and re-censused.

**Strain 1, Fig 4 :22 versus binding international law.** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." International law sometimes transfers binding rules downward, which looks like a recording, not a head start. Re-census: Fig 4 governs the *kind* of thing that crosses (a reapplicable method versus a replay of one occasion), not the *direction of legal authority*. EU subsidiarity, as a world source, says act at the level that can best achieve the objective, which is a head-start rule when it lowers local search cost. So no strain after re-census. The live empirical question is whether a given transfer is a method or a recording, and that requires an `R_T` measurement, not an argument.

**Strain 2, Fig 11 :61-62 versus real exclusion.** "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Governance can revoke a permit or demolish a sea wall, so exclusion seems available. Re-census: Fig 11 :62 says why: "because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." You can remove an actor and you cannot subtract the contact relations that made it consequential. An exclusion that changes no contact changes nothing (F7). No strain after re-census; the asymmetry is about the habitat's contact graph, not about legal power.

**Strain 3, Fig 2 :29 versus markets and review bodies as anchors.** Fig 2 :29 lists "a market, a patient outcome" as anchors, yet markets move when actors push on them. Re-census: Fig 2 :36-37 resolves it: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." A market is an anchor for a question about price formation and only an instrument for a question about physical risk. For this question the anchor is the Earth-system measurement. No strain after re-census; a scope note recorded.

**After re-census: no hidden strain.** One item remains live and named: whether particular cross-scale transfers are methods or recordings is a ground question, not a figure question, and is reported as such in `07` and `09`.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single governance frame at one level can close the cross-scale residual while ranging against the ground, with no cross-level method transfer and no head start received (breaks F2 and F3, and Fig 6's closure rule).
2. A fixed actor list assembled before the risk, without enumerating the contact cascade, handles a third-order risk shift as well as an enumerated habitat (breaks F6 and Fig 11 :43-53).
3. Two bodies drawing on the same assessment and report count as two independent witnesses for a verdict about the world (breaks F5 and Fig 2 :31-32).
4. A nominal reorganisation that changes no measurement contact, decision path to exposure or finance flow measurably reduces the residual (breaks F7 and Fig 11 :40).
5. A cross-scale transfer is shown to have a zero round-trip gap on a named instrument (breaks F8, or rather confirms that this particular transfer is lossless and removes the caveat for it).
6. A figure line names a governance kind, or the substrate plus operators determines a unique institutional form (closes the gap in F9 in favour of Option A).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and the pre-registered refuter, not with a list of named institutions (F9, Fig 9 :32).
2. Treat levels as levels of one loop with a common anchor, not as separate problems (F1, F2).
3. Build the upward path for methods and the downward path for head starts, and measure the round-trip gap on each (F3, F8).
4. Keep the Earth-system measurement reachable at each level; refuse success reports based on agreement counts (F4, F11).
5. Enumerate the habitat for each specific risk out to the third-order cascade, and keep a refusal move available (F6, F10).
6. Import names from the literature with provenance and a shadow test; never present a world import as a figure derivation (F9, Fig 6 :12-13, :23).
