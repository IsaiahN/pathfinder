## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the answer

The figures README gives the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134-135, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And on method: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."

So for EAR-0090 a **shape** is the narrowed form of a coordinating arrangement that can be stated without picking a named institution. "Nested decision loop with one anchor, methods up, head starts down, independent centres, enumerated habitat" is a shape. "Polycentric governance, subsidiarity-based, with an Earth System Governance architecture" is a name. The shape constrains the name; it does not produce it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. Describe the effect rather than the cause, matching Fig 9 :30-31, "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- Fig 6 :30, "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- Fig 9 :27-28, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." In this question that matters acutely: if you start with "polycentric governance" you will find polycentric governance everywhere.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place" and the answer is composed through the process of locating it.

## 4. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name"

I initially wrote the limit as if it forbade naming any governance kind. That was an overstatement.

- The canonical phrase governs **a missing thing**: "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The subject is a primitive not held in the library.
- The named governance kinds are not missing primitives. They are already-held concepts in the world literature. Selecting one and declaring what it means is a binding from an outside frame, which is an import with provenance, not the invention of a new primitive.
- What remains forbidden is claiming the framework *derived* the name, or that the framework can rank named kinds. Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134-135 still govern that.

Both the original claim and the correction are kept visible. The operative rule for this question is: name freely with provenance, never as a figure result.

## 5. Why "source" matters for governance coordination

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. A colour instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source" because several underlying causes can give the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the cases.

A governance report that says "risk reduced" is the same kind of projection. Several arrangements can produce that reading: real exposure reduction, a boundary change that moved the exposed population out of the counted area, or a metric that stopped registering a slow-onset hazard. An instrument that cannot discriminate these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy. The shape claim therefore demands that the anchor be the Earth-system measurement, at each level, with the discriminating instrument named. Agreement among report authors cannot substitute for it.

## 6. Prestige is not action, and agreement is not the ground

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, section "Prestige Is Not Action": "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A campaign that gathers well-known governance names to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried against a measurement, and let the measurement settle it. Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
