# Expert verification packet: the Tether on education and machine-produced demonstrations

**What this is:** a claim in a form an assessment researcher or educational measurement specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on any specific design. Not `grounded`.** This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When a machine can produce the task education has been using as evidence of understanding, the reading no longer discriminates its source: a learner who understands and a machine that produces a passing product give the same observable reading. Effective change is therefore in the unit of evidence, not in a format: a per-learner trajectory, a defense in the learner's own words and a situated judgment under conditions the tool cannot pre-solve, with the product kept as one component. The objective link in the institutional chain must be repaired before adding detection instruments. Capability lives in the agent-and-habitat arrangement, so a machine that performs retrieval and correction for the learner improves output without improving capability, while a machine the learner must critique creates contact. The tool is a provenanced import from a differing closure, and the model's agreement with the learner is one evidence pool, not two witnesses. Old and new arrangements are isomers over a fixed atom set. No figure line returns a format, weight, percentage or tool policy; that is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal and source-facing; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the figure on the ground channel

- **Statement:** a produced artifact is an instrument, not the anchor, for a question about a person. When two sources can produce the same reading, the reading cannot settle the question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does."
- **What to check:** is the distinction between a legitimate anchor and an instrument correctly applied here? Does the product reading actually fail to discriminate source in the cases you know?
- **Refuter:** a machine-produced demonstration that discriminates the learner's capability at the same product reading with no additional evidence; or evidence that the product channel did not decay when machine task performance became available.

### C2: Checkable against the learning literature and Figure 11

- **Statement:** capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Introducing a tool that performs retrieval and correction for the learner changes the artifact but not the capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- **What to check:** is the contact prediction consistent with the testing effect and desirable-difficulties evidence? Does a revised assessment that removes the learner's struggle improve performance while leaving delayed retention unchanged?
- **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the learner's ground without changing contact. That is the standing refuter the figures themselves name for the capability claim (Fig 11 :71-72).

- **Refuter:** one anchor that settles all three conditions; a new design requiring an atom not derivable from the held set; or a figure line that directly governs an assessment format, weight, percentage or tool policy. The last would close the gap verdict.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ban on machines. Exclusion is not available (Fig 11 :61).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and a local ground pilot, not agreement. From the Hard Problem is About Source: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Concretely:

1. **Discriminator test:** does the proposed arrangement separate a learner who understands from a machine-produced passing product at the same product reading?
2. **Contact test:** does it increase retrieval, correction and situated judgment after the tool is removed and in a reproduced habitat?
3. **Objective test:** can the institution state and instrument what counts as progress in understanding?
4. **Import test:** what gap does the machine explain, what is its provenance, and is its independence debited?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :36-38, Fig 10 :24, Fig 11 :39-40, :61, :70, Fig 9 :32, :55 and Fig 12 :117. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 against a case you know: did a task product ever fail to tell you which source produced it?

4. Name one instrument that would discriminate source in your field, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
