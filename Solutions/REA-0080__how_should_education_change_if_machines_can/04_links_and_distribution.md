## What the wider literature adds, and what it does not

1. **The validity framing is the load-bearing point.** Crompton and Burke say the problem is validity before integrity, and Nikolic and Basta Nikolic define the assessment validity gap as a misalignment between what a format measures and what outcomes require. Both agree with the figure-derived finding that a product is an instrument, not an anchor. They are one broad assessment-research pool, not two independent witnesses, because they cite many of the same sources (Messick, Liang, Bearman).
2. **The four-pillar paper is a design instrument, not a validated result.** It explicitly says its tools are not psychometrically validated and proposes a quasi-experimental pilot with inter-rater reliability targets. That is exactly the honest stop this solution records: no figure and no paper returns a format or a weight; a ground study is owed.
3. **Oral defense is not an unqualified fix.** The same paper records accessibility and equity concerns for students with speech or hearing impairments, anxiety disorders, non-native speakers and fully online programs, and proposes written viva, asynchronous video and extended portfolio as alternatives of unknown equivalence. This supports the gap verdict.
4. **The testing effect and desirable difficulties are the genuinely low-overlap witnesses.** Roediger and Karpicke, and the Bjork summary, come from cognitive psychology, not assessment research. They corroborate the figure-derived contact claim: retrieval and correction are the learning event, so a tool that performs them for the learner changes capability even when the artifact improves.
5. **Campbell's law is a second low-overlap witness, from evaluation research.** It says a quantitative indicator loses value as a status signal once it becomes the goal. It corroborates the channel-decay finding: the product metric was useful while it estimated capability, and stops being useful when it becomes the target of machine assistance.
6. **The essays share the framework author's evidence pool with the figures.** Their agreement with the figure verdict is expected and carries little independent weight. They are used here for the canonical reading of "shape" and for the simulated-derivation warning, not as outside witnesses.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule (return to the census) was not triggered. The one apparent tension, that testing is both a measurement and a learning intervention, was re-censused as two currencies in `01` section 4, strain S3.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework before a reader has described the residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
