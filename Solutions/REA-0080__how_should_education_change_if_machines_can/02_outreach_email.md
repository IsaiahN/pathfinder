# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send, no automation, no purchased list.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 1 and 5 are checked against the figures. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about assessment and machine-produced work

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a machine can produce the task that education has been using as evidence of understanding, the reading no longer discriminates its source. Two different sources, a learner who understands and a machine that produces a passing product, create the same observable reading. The ground, the person's actual capability, has not moved. The channel, product quality as a proxy, has become pooled and saturated. From the six-term substrate and the ground rule it follows that this is a channel failure, not a claim that essays or exams are worthless and not a reason to ban a tool.
>
> The framework's rule is that an anchor must be legitimate for the question and must not update on what any frame thinks. For a question constitutively about a person, human judgment is the legitimate anchor; a produced artifact is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on the axes they do not measure. The instrument here cannot comment on which source produced the reading.
>
> **What the derivation says education should change:** the unit of evidence, not the format. A per-learner trajectory that can be checked: successive attempts, a defense in the learner's own words, and a situated judgment under conditions the tool cannot pre-solve. The product stays as one component, not the verdict. The learner must still do the retrieval, the struggle and the correction, because capability lives in the agent-and-habitat arrangement, and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. A machine may be introduced as an actor the learner has to critique, with its provenance recorded; a machine that removes the learner's contact improves output without improving capability.
>
> **What the derivation explicitly does not say:** which format to adopt, what percentage of the grade to assign to it, whether oral defense is better than a portfolio, which tool to permit, or that any of this has been measured. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Those choices are a `gap` in my write-up, reported as unreached rather than filled in. I am not asking you to trust the framework or to endorse it.
>
> **What would refute the output. Written down before this message:**
> 1. A machine-produced demonstration that still discriminates the learner's capability at the same product reading, with no additional evidence.
> 2. A checkable reading of capability that needs no differing closure and no non-updating anchor.
> 3. An agent-only improvement that moves the learner's ground without changing contact.
> 4. Evidence that the product channel did not decay when machine task performance became available.
> 5. A figure line that directly governs an assessment format, a weight, a percentage or a tool policy.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape claim. Would any of these be where you would look first?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked four-pillar assessment framework, open access: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2026.1841682/full
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

| the move | figure-grounded reason |

| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |

| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |

## Stage 2, only after a verdict is recorded

If the reader returns a verdict on stage 1, record it before showing any theory. Only then offer the three links and note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: the shared evidence pool would make it one witness talking twice (Fig 2 :40). Stage 3 offers the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode before running it.
