## Q1 - Are two teams with the same members and safety score isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Two teams with the same headcount, the same skills and the same safety score have the same atoms. If one composes them as a repeated agreement sequence and the other as a network in which dissent reaches a differing closure, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the safety score. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you have, it is how you connect it", but stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the teams share only a pattern (both report errors upward) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A score comparison that finds no difference between two teams cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bonds have to be recorded. Fig 8 :50-51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two teams with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in learning with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D3), which moves the explanation out of composition.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the team application. World corroboration is Edmondson's field-guide note that the same design and resources produced a 3.9 safety score in one team against 5.0 and 5.9 in two others, and that *"a member said its leader did not want to know when things went badly"*. That is a practitioner summary of one qualitative case, not a measurement here.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a team facing a change in what pays:

1. **Perception.** Can it read the ground and the risk of speaking up at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its Γ have terms for what speaking up affords in the changed situation: error, near miss, dissent, idea, deliberation, execution? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem.

C1 (contact) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (reach) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the team names what worked but never imports a new atom. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55). The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The safety survey instruments link 1 well; links 2 and 5 have almost none, so budget piles up at 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a team whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Edmondson 1999 team safety** (Field Guide summary read 2026-11-22) | a team-level belief that interpersonal risk taking is safe | Γ and habitat; Fig 11 :39 team-and-habitat | The causal direction: the summary records *"the design is cross-sectional and single-company, so nothing here is causal"* |
| **Frazier et al. 2017 meta-analysis** (Field Guide summary) | antecedents and outcomes across 136 samples | one witness from one pool; Fig 2 :31 | It cannot separate a channel that reaches the ground from an agreement room; both produce high safety scores |
| **Eldor, Hodor and Cappelli 2023 inverted-U** (PSNet summary) | performance rises then declines with safety | matches Fig 11 :49 valence flip at the performance stratum | It supplies a curve shape and no instrument that discriminates the two conditions; collective accountability is named as a buffer but not specified as a bond |
| **Cangiano 2026 dual pathway** (full text) | enabling pathway plus regulatory loss: filtering loss, effort withdrawal, norm erosion | matches Fig 7 :36 and Fig 10 :26 at the behaviour stratum | It is a conceptual framework with seven propositions, not a ground measurement; it shares the safety evidence pool |
| **Cohesiveness / groupthink** | liking and agreement in the group | Fig 7 :12-13 agreement compounds, evidence does not; Fig 11 :50 valence | The field-guide summary of Edmondson explicitly separates safety from cohesiveness; using them as synonyms is the error the figures name |
| **Aviation crew resource management and 14 CFR 121.542** | calibrated challenge at defined moments, nonessential conversation barred in critical phases | Fig 11 :49 valence relative to residual, applied at an engineering anchor | It is an instance from a different closure, not this team's ground; it cannot transfer semantics, only the shape (Fig 9 :30-31) |
| **Animal collective learning** (Collet et al. 2023; Toyokawa et al. 2019) | individual learning, learning about others, complementarity; conformity under uncertainty | Fig 11 :40, Fig 7 :12-13 at a different stratum | It does not measure psychological safety, organisations or innovation; it corroborates mechanism shape only |
| **Luck / temporary halo** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame. Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the low-overlap fields corroborate mechanism, not the team-level fact.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the team-level claim, as the habitat operand under D2 (Fig 11 :39);
- the confidence that voice and error reporting matter, as C1 and the channel instruments (Fig 10 :24-29);
- the nonlinear performance result, as the valence flip when the residual or phase changes (Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 12 :119);
- the warning about cohesiveness and groupthink, as the mirror room (Fig 7 :12-13, :35-36);
- the need for challenge at defined moments, as the import valve (Fig 6 :23) plus phase-dependent valence (Fig 11 :49).

It cannot generate the *number* at which safety becomes too high, the *list* of phases in which challenge is valuable, or the *name* of the intervention that restores both conditions. That is the `gap` recorded in F11 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two teams with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two teams sharing only a `state → voice → ground` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :123. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard safety explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a number or a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the amount gap is declared rather than filled. The valence flip is triangulated for the rule and single-source for the phase schedule, and the phase schedule is not promoted to a figure line.
