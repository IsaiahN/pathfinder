# Expert verification packet - the Tether on psychological safety and team learning

**What this is:** a claim in a form a team researcher or empirically minded practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No prospective team measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Psychological safety does not have a single main effect on learning and innovation. It helps when two conditions hold, and those conditions have different failure modes with different instruments. First, the team's channel to whatever actually pays it stays reachable: its readings of errors, near misses and decisions are not stale, saturated or pooled into an organisation-wide average. Second, its reach keeps moving outward through contact with people or situations whose ways of working differ from its own, so it is not simply re-running the same successful routine with more enthusiasm. Safety becomes a suppressor of challenge when the shared belief turns into "we agree and no one challenges", because a team that hands itself the same room compounds agreement while evidence does not. The same voice act can also change sign when the work moves from deciding what is wrong to carrying out the decision, so the useful amount of openness is phase-dependent rather than maximal. The anchor is the error corrected, the decision revised and the idea adopted, measured outside the team's own account; the safety survey is an instrument, not an anchor. The claim is a shape, not a number, a programme or a phase schedule.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal, with one team-facing check

- **Statement:** capability is a property of team-and-habitat, so learning cannot be located in safer individuals alone; the anchor is the ground, not the survey; quick loss is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
  - Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Edmondson 1999 is described in the Field Guide summary as cross-sectional and single-company, so *"nothing here is causal"*. Does any later study actually measure the ground rather than a second self-report? A "no" is a real gap in the evidence, not a refutation of the figure rule.
- **Refuter:** an individual-level increase in felt safety that changes error reporting or decision correction while team-habitat contact is measured as unchanged; or a team that loses learning while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure measure high and non-declining. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus the challenge question

- **Statement:** internal agreement is not evidence; a team that keeps handing itself the same room stops travelling; renewed reach requires contact with a closure that differs, because composing inside a closure cannot add an atom.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 7 :13 - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 7 :35 - *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
  - Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
  - Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Cangiano 2026 writes that *"high psychological safety combined with low accountability produces a comfort zone rather than a learning zone"*, and the Field Guide summary of Edmondson 1999 says the construct *"is not group cohesiveness, which can suppress disagreement to the point of groupthink"*. Does the field actually distinguish safety from agreement in its measures, or do the items blur them? A blur would explain the second clause of the question without any new theory.
- **Refuter:** a team with high safety and high internal agreement that keeps producing ground-verified new readings with no differing room and no import; or a demonstration that a team renewed its reach purely by internal composition with no new atom. Either breaks idempotence and C2 with it.

### C3 - Structure, order and the valence flip

- **Statement:** the union of conditions must be unbundled before a lever is chosen; two teams with identical atoms but different bonds are isomers, so a headcount plus a score cannot predict learning; the value of voice changes with the residual and the task phase.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*; Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
  - Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** 14 CFR 121.542(b) treats *"engaging in nonessential conversations within the cockpit"* as not required during critical phases, while crew resource management trains challenge at defined moments. Is the phase distinction real in your setting, and can you name the instrument that marks the phase? A "no" leaves the shape intact but removes the phase application.
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; two teams with identical atoms and identical bonds whose learning diverges with no difference in contact or imported atoms; or a voice act whose value is invariant across a documented change of residual and phase.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a main effect, not a theory of everything about organisations, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not a number. No claim about what safety score is optimal or where a curve turns.
- Not a phase schedule. The figures give the valence rule; the phase distinction is single-source.
- Not that safety, leaders, culture or talent are irrelevant. They are Γ and habitat; the claim is that Γ alone does not settle learning.
- Not that any specific programme is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the safety literature independently corroborates this. Edmondson, Frazier and Cangiano share one evidence pool. The low-overlap sources from social learning, animal behaviour and aviation corroborate the mechanism shape, not the team-level fact.
- Not that this packet, produced with an LLM, is submittable or publishable as authored work. See the AI-policy note in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-team study that measures C1 and C2 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, and reports which condition failed before the decline. Concretely:

1. **Channel test:** does the team's reading of the ground degrade while the ground keeps paying someone? Indicators: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
2. **Fidelity and pressure test:** are bracket fidelity and ground pressure both nonzero at each handoff? Fig 7 :37-39 says zero either stops the chain.
3. **Import test:** did any new atom enter the team's reach from a closure that differs? If not, C2 is refuted.
4. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a reachable reporting channel from an agreement room from a survey artifact when all three produce the same score? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24 and :26, Fig 7 :13 and :36, Fig 8 :32, Fig 6 :23, Fig 9 :57 and Fig 12 :117. Nine short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of team learning: is there a documented case of high safety and high agreement that kept producing ground-verified new readings with no import and no differing room? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a reporting channel from an agreement room using observable team data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
