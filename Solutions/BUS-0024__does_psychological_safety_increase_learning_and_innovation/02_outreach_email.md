# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no ground measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

**Subject:** Check one claim about when speaking up helps before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about psychological safety and team learning that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** psychological safety does not have a single main effect on learning and innovation. It helps when two conditions hold, and those conditions have different failure modes.
>
> 1. The team's channel to whatever actually pays it stays reachable, meaning its readings of errors, near misses and decisions are not stale, saturated or pooled into an organisation-wide average.
> 2. The team's reach keeps moving outward through contact with people or situations whose ways of working differ from its own, so it is not simply re-running the same successful routine with more enthusiasm.
>
> The claim is that these are two different failures with two different instruments, not two degrees of one problem. The reason the second clause of the question matters is structural: when safety becomes "we agree and no one challenges", the team keeps handing itself the same room. Agreement then compounds while evidence does not. The same voice act can also change sign when the task moves from working out what is wrong to carrying out the decision, so the right amount of openness is phase-dependent, not maximal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the right amount of safety, that more voice is always better voice, that any particular team is a case in point, that this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of learning and innovation. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. an individual-level increase in felt safety changes error reporting while the team's contact with the ground measures as unchanged;
> 2. a team's safety rises while both its reading fidelity and the ground's pressure on it measure high and non-declining, and error correction still declines;
> 3. a team with high safety and high internal agreement keeps producing ground-verified new readings with no differing room and no new input from outside;
> 4. a team produces ground-verified new ideas purely inside its own closure, with no contact with a frame that differs from it;
> 5. the value of the same voice act stays the same across a documented change of task phase.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers: the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or a channel the venue has published for opt-in use. Record the source and date.
- A reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Fig 10 :59).
- The AOM publishing page states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* This packet is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation. Do not submit it as authored work. A human researcher who runs the check owns and writes the resulting article, with disclosure under the venue's policy.
