# The Tether's answer: evidence for uncredited and erased contributions

**Mode: `specified` on the claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No historical case was settled here.

## 1. The residual it answers

A contribution was made and its attribution is absent or was reassigned. The question is not "who deserved credit" but "what kind of evidence could still name the route". The published artefact is invariant under relabelling of its producer; several routes can print the same byline. The residual is indexed per contribution, never aggregated into a field-wide score (Fig 1 :19). It is a union compound: the artefact and the credit assigned to it are two conditions, and clearing one leaves the other binding.

## 2. The shape

1. **The artefact does not determine its producer.** Fig 13 :129: "The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage." And Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term." So the evidence that reveals a contribution is lineage, not a closer reading of the result.

2. **The lineage must cross a closure boundary.** Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." A second historian reading the same institutional file is one witness. A dated letter held elsewhere, a lab notebook in a different collection, an instrument log, a patent filing or a rival's citation are low-overlap lines. Agreement among them means something; agreement inside one archive does not.

3. **Convergent derivation and taken credit are content-identical.** Fig 8 :50-51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". This is why a content-only argument about who "must" have done the work cannot settle attribution.

4. **The source gate is what erasure removes.** Fig 6 :12: "source gate: provenance". Reattribution requires reattaching a source, not constructing a more plausible narrative. The 2023 Franklin reattribution worked exactly this way: an unpublished manuscript and a colleague's letter found in an archive, not a new analysis of the published paper.

5. **Credit decisions carry their own provenance and remain corrigible.** Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom." Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record." A well-documented authorship committee is not therefore immune to correction.

6. **Where no trace survives, the verdict is `unreachable`.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway." Absence of found evidence is never proof the contribution did not happen.

7. **The method cannot name a person before the ground settles.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." It can say what a sufficient trace would look like: dated, independent of the crediting frame, and capable of changing the attribution. It cannot supply the name from the shape alone.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that every erased contribution can be recovered. Many routes leave no trace. The answer gives a shape and a stop, not a promise.
- Not that a famous name is evidence. Prestige is a budget for trying a claim, not a settlement of it.
- Not that a single archive is enough. One evidence pool, however large, is one witness.
- Not that a well-provenanced credit ruling is correct. It is corrigible (Fig 10 :65).
- Not that "no trace found" means "no contribution". That is the forbidden inference (Fig 8 :39).
- Not `grounded`. No specific attribution was settled by a ground reading in this run.

## 4. What would settle a specific case - the ground

1. **A surviving trace with a source gate:** a dated document held outside the crediting institution, with its custody chain recorded. Checkable by anyone who can read it.
2. **An independent second line:** a different closure holding a different trace that was not copied from the first. If the two descend from one unrecorded original, they are one witness.
3. **An instrument that discriminates sources:** where a trace survives but is below the current distinction horizon, a better instrument can close the case. This works only if a trace exists; the lab-grown diamond case is the positive type, the colour mixture the negative type.
4. **The ruling's own lineage:** the dated record of how the earlier credit decision was reached, so the error can be located rather than merely felt.

A falsifier written before the run: a contributor recovered from the artefact alone with no lineage and no instrument reading a trace; or a content-only discriminator between convergence and adoption; or two declared-independent lines shown to share one unrecorded original with the attribution still standing; or a trace-free case settled by a better instrument (which would refute that case's classification).

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for names.** The framework can characterise the kind of evidence that would be sufficient. Whether such evidence survives in any particular case is a ground question. The essay *How Intelligence Actually Works* calls the limit a "union horizon" and warns that being below it "is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet". Indiscernibility is not identity, and the record of the route is what keeps the stop checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of wrongly credited scientists. It says something narrower and more useful: a published result does not record its own route, so the artefact cannot tell you who made it. Convergence and copied credit look identical in the contents; only a provenanced record separates them. The evidence that can reveal a contribution is lineage material, a dated trace held in a closure different from the one that assigned the credit, checked against a second independent line rather than a room of agreeing historians. Where that trace is missing, the honest report is "unreachable", not "no contribution existed". What this offers is the shape of sufficient evidence, the order in which to read it, refuters written in advance, and a recorded stop at the limit of what the surviving traces can distinguish.
