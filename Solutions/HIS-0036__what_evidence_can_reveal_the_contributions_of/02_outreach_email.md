# Outreach draft: check the shape of the evidence before deciding about the case

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used or listed.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a specific case has been worked through the enumeration in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6. The output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would count as proof of a contribution the published record left out?

> Hello,
>
> I am working on a narrow question in the history of science: what kind of evidence can reveal the contribution of a researcher whose name was left off a result or erased from the later account? I am not asking you to agree that any particular person was wronged, and I am not asking you to read a theory first.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a published result does not record its own route. The route, and who took it, lives in lineage material: dated documents, correspondence, notebooks, instrument records, custody chains. The only evidence that can restore attribution is lineage held in a closure different from the one that assigned the credit. A second historian reading the same institutional file is not a second witness. This is a shape, not a list of names, and it may be wrong.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a case where the contributor is recovered from the artefact alone, with no external record and no instrument reading a surviving trace;
> 2. a content-only test that reliably separates independent derivation from adopted credit with no provenance record;
> 3. two "independent" lines of evidence shown to descend from one unrecorded original, with the attribution still standing;
> 4. a trace-free case settled by a better instrument (that would refute my classification of that case, not the shape).
>
> I wrote these down before doing the case work. If any one fires, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source links, with the note that the figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> If you want to reuse the method: enumerate everything in contact with the contribution before choosing an archive; demand a source gate for every reattribution; declare where two witnesses share an original; and report "unreachable" rather than "did not happen" when no trace is found. Write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state whether your finding is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`.

## Stage 1 / stage 2 boundary

Stage 1 is the text above, without the links. Record the recipient's verdict on the shape and refuter *before* sending anything else. Stage 2 is opt-in: if they are curious, send the three links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Their later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness). Stage 3, if asked, offers the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Legal and practical.** One-to-one only. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line. Public institutional channels and opt-in submission venues only. An opt-out line is implicit in "no reply is needed" and can be made explicit if the venue expects it.
