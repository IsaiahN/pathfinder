## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **The artefact is not the route.** The essay *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: "A written sentence is a lossy compression of the reasoning that produced it... the text is the residue that survived being written down." This is Fig 13's lineage claim stated for authorship. It means a bibliography is a poor instrument for attribution: it records outputs, not the generator.
2. **Provenance loss has a mechanism.** The essay *How Intelligence Actually Works*: a video frame and a photograph "are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which." The essay names "provenance loss" and adds that "very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it." It also gives the positive case: lab-grown versus mined diamonds become distinguishable when "isotope ratios genuinely differ, so if you have a tool that can perceive the trace, you regain distinction."
3. **Convergence versus copying needs a record.** The essay *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "I could prove I had not copied her because my paper trail was different, and my vocabulary was different." This is Fig 8 :50-51 applied to a real dispute.
4. **Prestige corrupts the credit anchor.** Same essay: "someone let prestige stand in for proof... The prestige loop confirms itself. The high-prestige person is believed because they are high-prestige. The action is assumed to work because the person is believed. The ground is never consulted." Read beside the Matthew and Matilda effect sources, this explains why D2's anchor updates.
5. **Source misattribution is not only institutional.** The source-monitoring source shows the same failure in individual memory, including cryptomnesia. Oral testimony from a participant is therefore a line of evidence with a documented failure mode, not a privileged anchor.
6. **A real recovery case is documentary, not rhetorical.** The Live Science report and the Franklin page both describe the 2023 reattribution as resting on an unpublished manuscript, a colleague's letter, and seminar notes held in an archive. No new experiment on the 1953 paper was required.
7. **A public re-examination can overturn a settled reading.** The Science History Institute homepage reports new work on Photo 49 revising the standard Photo 51 story. This is a live example of INWARD evidence (a different surviving trace re-read with better attention), not an agreement among historians.

Overlap warning: the Live Science article, the Franklin Wikipedia page and the Science History Institute item all draw on the same 2023 Comfort and Cobb work and the same archival cluster. They are **one evidence pool**, not three independent witnesses. The Matthew and Matilda effect pages likewise share the Merton / Rossiter sociology pool.
