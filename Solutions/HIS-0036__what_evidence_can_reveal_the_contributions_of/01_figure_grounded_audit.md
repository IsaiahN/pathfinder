## 0. The question under audit

> What evidence can reveal the contributions of researchers whose work was uncredited or erased?

Stated as an effect: research work happened along a particular route, and the record naming the route-taker is absent or was reassigned. What remains that could still name the route? The answer is bound as an ordinary term before the search: **lineage-bearing evidence held in a closure different from the one that assigned the credit**. Binding it makes the preferred shape refusable (Formula, "Direction").

---

## 1. Residual R, in effect terms

- **Arity:** the residual is indexed per contribution, never aggregated into "the history of science". Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A single erased contributor is one live slot even when the public record of a field looks complete.
- **Symmetry:** the published artefact is invariant under relabelling of its producer. Green is green whether it came from one light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered (essay, *The Hard Problem is About Source*). The route is not invariant; the route is what attribution names.
- **Scale:** a dated trace at the bench, a custody chain in an archive, a credit decision in a field's canon. These are different scales, and Fig 4 requires a lossy transform plus a measured round trip between them.
- **Resource direction:** inward (is a surviving trace readable with a better instrument?) and outward (does another closure already hold the trace?) are opposed and must not be read against each other (Fig 6 :28-31).

- **Partition or union.** D1 and D2 are a **union compound**, not one question: a paper and the credit assigned to it are two conditions, and clearing one leaves the other binding (Fig 9 :54-58). D3 and D4 are separate loops.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Establish a surviving trace in D3 before attempting attribution in D2. Describe the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). D1 alone cannot settle D2, because the artefact does not carry its own route.
- **No nameable anchor means no domain.** All four have a nameable anchor. D2's anchor is compromised, which is a finding, not a reason to delete the domain.

Strain test applied before concluding: Fig 2 calls an anchor something that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29). D2's credit anchor plainly does change with what frames think (a famous name gets the credit). That is not a strain on Fig 2; it is Fig 2's named collapse 1, mutual update. The correct move is to re-census D2 as a *failure* to be diagnosed, not to soften the anchor definition.

## 4. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - The artefact does not carry its own route (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 13 :127: "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."
Fig 13 :128: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."
Fig 13 :129: "The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."
Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."

A published result is an artefact. Its author line is a label attached to the artefact, not a property recoverable from it. Erasure removes the lineage while leaving the artefact intact. So reading the paper harder cannot settle who made it. The evidence that can reveal the contribution is the surviving lineage itself.

Triangulation: Fig 12 :117 supplies the same claim from composition, "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 8 :50-51 supplies it from union/provenance. Three figures with different subjects, so `triangulated`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the contributor is recoverable from the artefact alone with no external record and no instrument reading a surviving trace. Under the figures this cannot happen for a route, only for a published name already printed on the artefact.

### F2 - Independent discovery and taken credit look identical in the contents (triangulated)

Fig 8 :50-51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

This is the exact structure of an attribution dispute. Two researchers arrive at the same result. One is credited. The contents cannot say whether the second derived it independently or used the first's data. Only the record of where each came from separates the cases. The essay *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* reports the author's own instance: *"I could prove I had not copied her because my paper trail was different, and my vocabulary was different."*

**Refuter:** a content-only test that reliably separates independent derivation from adopted credit with no provenance and no instrument. If one exists, F2 falls.

### F3 - The credit anchor has been made to update (single-source on the figure, corroborated from a different field)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

In D2 the thing that is supposed to settle "who contributed" is reputation, which updates on reputation. That is Fig 2's collapse 1. World sources from sociology and history of science name the same mechanism: the Matthew effect (*"eminent scientists will often get more credit than a comparatively unknown researcher, even if their work is similar"*) and the Matilda effect (*"work is consequently attributed to their male colleagues"*). These are secondary corroboration from a different field; they do not decide the verdict.

**Refuter:** a credit process whose answer demonstrably does not move when the reputation of the claimant changes, measured across at least two independent archives.

### F4 - The source gate is what erasure removes (triangulated)

Fig 6 :12: "source gate: provenance"
Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall"

An imported contribution is legitimate only when its provenance gate is passed. Erasure strips that gate. What restores it is not a new argument about who *deserved* credit but the reattachment of a source: a dated letter, a seminar note, a lab notebook, a custody record. The 2023 reattribution of Rosalind Franklin's role is a real case: reporters describe how historians *"dug into the archives at the University of Cambridge's Churchill College and found an unpublished article for Time magazine"* plus a second document, *"a letter written by Pauline Cowan"*. The evidence was lineage material held outside the crediting frame, not a re-reading of the 1953 paper.

**Refuter:** show a restored attribution that passed no source gate and rested on plausibility alone. Under Fig 6 that is an unpriced import.

### F5 - Artefact-only and archive-only searches fail in opposite ways (triangulated)

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

- SEARCHED: reading the published corpus again adds no trace that was never in it. The essay *How Intelligence Actually Works* puts the case sharply: a video frame and a photograph *"are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which."*
- INWARD: a better instrument can read a surviving trace. The same essay contrasts lab-grown and mined diamonds, where *"isotope ratios genuinely differ, so if you have a tool that can perceive the trace, you regain distinction."*
- OUTWARD: a different closure may already hold the trace. This is what the Churchill College archive was.

These are three distinct instructions (Formula step 7), not one. Where none returns a trace, the honest report is `unreachable` at this budget, not "no contribution existed".

**Refuter:** a proof that absence of a found trace entails absence of a contribution. Fig 8 :39 forbids it, and the essay adds that *"'No trace exists' can never be confirmed by better instruments."*

### F6 - Independence decides whether two witnesses are one (triangulated)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

Ten historians who all read the same institutional archive are one witness with ten names. A lab notebook, a personal letter held elsewhere, and a patent filing are three closures: low overlap, so agreement between them means something. Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*

**Refuter:** two lines of evidence shown to be independent that in fact descend from one original document, with the dependency unrecorded. Overlap declarations in `10_source_ledger.csv` exist to catch this.

### F7 - A credit ruling carries provenance, and provenance does not make it right (single-source, Figure 10)

Fig 10 :62: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."
Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."

A well-documented committee decision about authorship is still corrigible. The lineage of the *decision* must be recorded too, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it. This is why a reattribution needs both the trace of the work and the trace of the earlier credit ruling.

**Refuter:** a case where a perfectly documented credit decision was therefore immune to correction. That contradicts Fig 10 :65.

### F8 - Enumeration, not pruning (single-source, Figure 11)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

The evidence habitat for an erased contribution includes the obvious (papers, bylines) and the second-order (the supervisor's correspondence, the funding application, the instrument log, the departmental minute, the conference abstract, the thesis acknowledgment, the rival's citation). Contact cascades. Scoping to first-order contact is how a contribution three steps out stays invisible. Introduction is available, exclusion is not: you can add a line of evidence, you cannot subtract a fact about the world.

**Refuter:** a complete evidence census assembled by pruning to likely authors before enumeration. Under Fig 11 that is an authored room, not a habitat.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A contributor is recovered from the artefact alone, with no lineage and no instrument reading a surviving trace, in a case where the recovery is later settled at the ground.
2. A content-only discriminator reliably separates independent derivation from adopted credit without any provenance record.
3. Two lines of evidence declared independent are shown to descend from a single unrecorded original document, and the attribution still stands after that discovery.
4. A trace-free case is settled by a better instrument, which would show the "no trace" classification was a budget artifact rather than a genuine absence (this refutes the *case classification*, not the shape).
5. A credit ruling is shown to be immune to correction because it was well documented.

A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Written before the run, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual, not with a list of famous erased scientists. The list selects for agreement and manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
2. Enumerate the evidence habitat outward from the contribution before choosing which archive to search (Fig 11 :43-44).
3. Demand a source gate for every claimed reattribution (Fig 6 :12), and declare overlap between witnesses (Fig 2 :31).
4. Keep INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks: better instruments on surviving traces, and contact with other closures that may already hold them.
5. Where no trace is found, report `unreachable`. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39).
6. Send the generator (the enumeration method plus the refuter), not a recording of which names were restored. Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives and is `triangulated`: lineage-bearing evidence held in a closure different from the one that assigned credit is what can reveal an erased contribution; the artefact alone cannot. The credit anchor in D2 is a live collapse, not a neutral referee. Where no surviving trace is found, the report is `unreachable`, not absence. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**; the one live open item is whether a specific case has a trace, which is a ground question and is explicitly not claimed here.
