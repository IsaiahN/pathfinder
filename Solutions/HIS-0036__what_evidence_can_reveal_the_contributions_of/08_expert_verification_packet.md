# Expert verification packet: what evidence can reveal an erased contribution?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science, archivist or provenance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No particular attribution was settled by a ground reading in this run. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A published result does not record the route that produced it, so the artefact cannot determine its contributor. Convergent derivation and adopted credit are content-identical; only a provenanced record separates them. The evidence that can reveal an erased contribution is lineage material, a dated trace held in a closure different from the one that assigned credit, checked against a second independent line and with the earlier credit ruling's own provenance recorded. Where no trace survives, the report is `unreachable`, not "no contribution existed". What is offered is the shape of sufficient evidence, the order of reading, refuters written in advance, and a stop at the limit of what the traces can distinguish.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13 and 8

- **Statement:** the artefact does not carry its own route; the lineage does.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :129 - "The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage." Also Fig 13 :130 - "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term." And Fig 12 :117 - "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** a case where the contributor is recovered from the artefact alone, with no external record and no instrument reading a surviving trace, later settled at the ground.

### C2 - Attribution-facing; checkable by a historian against a real dispute

- **Statement:** convergence and adopted credit cannot be separated by content; only the record separates them; agreement inside one evidence pool is one witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50-51 - "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 2 :31 - "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 7 :35 - "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **What to check:** take any well-documented dispute (the Franklin case is in `04_links_and_distribution.md`). Are the documents that changed the attribution dated and held outside the institution that assigned credit? Were the two key documents independent, or did one descend from the other? If the latter, the witness count falls to one.
- **Refuter:** a content-only discriminator that reliably separates independent derivation from adopted credit with no provenance; or two lines declared independent shown to share one unrecorded original, with the attribution still standing.

### C3 - Methodological; checkable against the figures and against a case record

- **Statement:** the source gate is what erasure removes; the credit ruling carries its own provenance and remains corrigible; no trace found is `unreachable`, never an absence proof.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :12 - "source gate: provenance". Fig 10 :62 - "A ruling carries provenance like an atom." Fig 10 :65 - "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record." Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** does the case record include the provenance of the earlier credit decision as well as the new trace? Is "no evidence found" being reported as "no contribution"?
- **Refuter:** a well-documented credit ruling shown to be immune to correction; or a proof that absence of a found trace entails absence of a contribution.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of wrongly credited scientists, and no claim about any named case being settled.
- Not that every erased contribution is recoverable. Many routes leave no trace.
- Not that a famous name, a large archive, or a unanimous field is evidence of attribution.
- Not that a well-provenanced credit decision is correct. It is corrigible.
- Not that "no trace found" means "no contribution". That inference is refused.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument or a ground reading in this run.
- Not that the shape names a person. It names the kind of evidence that would be sufficient.

## 5. What would settle a specific case

1. A dated trace with a recorded custody chain, held outside the institution that assigned credit.
2. A second independent line from a different closure, not descended from the first.
3. An instrument that discriminates sources where a trace survives but is below the current distinction horizon.
4. The provenance of the earlier credit ruling, so the error can be located.

A reported failure is more useful than an endorsement. Refuters are listed with each sub-claim and repeated in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :129-130 and Fig 8 :50-51, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one dispute you know: were the decisive documents independent of the crediting archive?
3. Name one instrument or record type that would discriminate two routes in that dispute, or say why none is available.
4. Say whether "no trace found" is being treated as "no contribution" anywhere in the case record.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
