# Isomers, convergence and the order of reading evidence

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The artefact left behind by a contribution does not say which route produced it. Two questions are owed: (1) under what structure do two different contributions count as the same or different "substance", and (2) in what order must the evidence be read so that a refusal precedes a new attribution.

Atoms held for this derivation: a dated trace, a custody chain, a published artefact, a credit ruling, a reputation signal. Bonds held: sequence (→), identity (≡), subtraction (−), comparison (⋛), negation (¬, unary, not a bond). No new primitive is available without import. The anchor is the dated trace, not the reputation signal.

## DERIVATION

Dated documents, custody chains and instrument logs are already held terms in the historian's library. Naming a **class** of evidence is a binding onto held terms, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains forbidden is naming the **person** before the ground settles it.

I initially applied the ban to evidence classes. That was an overstatement. Retracted as applied to classes; kept as applied to persons. Triangulated (README + Fig 13 :135).

### Q2 - Are two contributions isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Two researchers can produce the same published formula, the same data table, the same byline block. Under Fig 12 that is **not** evidence that they made the same contribution. Same atoms under a different arrangement are a different substance, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond, which the formula does not record. In this domain the "bond" is the route: who measured, who interpreted, who wrote, who decided the claim was ready.

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Two contributions that share only a functional group (for example, both are "the person who produced the X-ray image") are weaker than isomers: they share a role, not an atom set. The difference between "isomer" and "functional group" matters because it tells you how strong the attribution claim can be.

- **Strong claim (isomer):** both routes span the same full atom set (same data, same instruments, same problem), arranged differently. The artefact cannot distinguish them; the lineage can.
- **Weak claim (functional group):** the two contributions share a role but not the same atoms. This licenses only a role claim ("she produced the critical measurement"), not a full authorship claim.

### Q3 - Convergence and copying are content-identical

Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
Fig 8 :50-51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

So a dispute of the form "A and B published the same thing, so B must have used A" is underdetermined by the contents. Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms. The converse also holds: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance. Only the record separates convergence from adoption.

The real-world counterpart is the Franklin case: the 2023 archival report did not reanalyse the X-ray photograph and conclude who deserved credit. It found a different record (an unpublished manuscript and a letter) that described collaboration. The record settled what the artefacts could not.

### Q4 - What order is the evidence read in?

Formula step 2 sorts the gap before minting. The relevant bins here:

- **Held:** the artefact and its byline are what was published. Nothing owes.
- **Novel:** a trace exists that the published record does not mention. Extend perception; aim the probe at the archive that holds it.
- **Broken, rebinding:** the byline is mis-attached. Refit the attribution to the trace; do not invent a new story.
- **Mechanism broken:** the credit process itself updates on reputation. A term is owed, and it is a refusal of the byline's standing to settle the question.
- **Refuted:** a term held is false, specifically "the published author line names the producer". What is owed is a refusal, not an explanation.

Negation is a unary operator, not a bond (Operators table :70-78). It acts on one operand: it refuses the claim "byline = producer" while retaining the artefact. This matters because a vocabulary with subtraction but no negation could say only that the byline was *missing* something, not that it was *wrong*. The refusal must come before minting a new name, or the new name is built on a standing that was already refused.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3):** (1) refuse the byline's authority where the anchor is corrupted; (2) enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43); (3) test a surviving trace against an independent closure (Fig 2 :31); (4) only then attribute. Steps 3 and 4 are distinct; a trace found inside the same archive that produced the byline is a re-reading, not a witness.

### Q5 - Instrument, budget and absence are three different things

Fig 8 :40-43 separates underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-this-budget. The essays add a distinction horizon (one observer cannot discriminate) and a union horizon (no available observer with any available instrument can).

- **Distinction horizon:** a specific archive or instrument cannot separate two routes. INWARD is the remedy: a better reading of a surviving trace.
- **Union horizon in principle:** no trace exists; no instrument can recover a route. Underivable. Import from another closure is the only move that changes the wall (Fig 6 :14).
- **Unreachable at this budget:** the trace exists in a closure not yet contacted. OUTWARD is the remedy. Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

These three give different instructions and must not be collapsed into "there is no evidence".

### Q6 - The round trip from figures to a case has a measurable gap

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

1. **Sent up (x):** Fig 13 :127-130 (the record is the proof), Fig 8 :50-51 (contents do not separate convergence from adoption), Fig 2 :31 (independence).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "look for an external dated record".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim that a specific letter proves a specific contribution.

`R_T` is nonzero: the shorthand drops the requirement that the record cross a closure boundary, the corrigibility of the earlier credit ruling (Fig 10 :65), and the difference between a trace and a budget-limited search. These are enumerated in `11_audit.md`. The shorthand is a generator, not a verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Two contributions that publish the same artefact can be **isomers** over a shared atom set (strong claim) or share only a **functional group** (weak claim); the artefact cannot decide between them. Convergence and adopted credit are content-identical, so only a provenanced record separates them. The evidence must be read in a fixed order: refuse the byline's standing where its anchor updates on reputation, enumerate the habitat, test a trace in a different closure, then attribute. Distinction horizon, union horizon in principle and unreachable-at-budget are three different verdicts with different remedies.

## FALSIFIER (written before the run)

The derivation fails if any of the following holds:

1. A content-only test separates convergent derivation from adopted credit with no provenance and no instrument reading a trace.
2. A trace held inside the same archive that produced the byline is shown to have the same witness value as one held in a different closure (this kills the independence requirement).
3. A case classified as "no trace exists" is settled by a better instrument, showing the classification was a budget artifact.
4. A new attribution is successfully mounted without first refusing the standing of the byline it contradicts.
