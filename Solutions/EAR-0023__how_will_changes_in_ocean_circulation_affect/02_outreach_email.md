# Outreach draft — check one shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are stated exactly as written, and until the recipient understands that no regional number is being claimed.

---

**Subject:** Can you break this shape claim about far-field ocean impacts?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a change in a basin-scale ocean flow produces a mismatch in distant land regions that a frame predicting a region from local conditions alone cannot close. From that it follows that any answer must be indexed by region and by medium, because a global mean can stay near zero while one region carries a live signal, and that rainfall, temperature and ecosystems are three overlapping conditions with separate grounds, not one averaged problem. The effect is carried by a network, so the pathway description cannot be derived from local predictors; it has to come from a frame that already holds it. The pathway is an ordered cascade, ocean to atmosphere to land surface to biota, which makes the ecosystem effect third order, so the same circulation strength under a different arrangement is a different outcome. Observations, not model agreement, settle each slot.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving a sign, a size or a date for any named place. The regional forecast is declared as a gap, with the instruments that would close it named below. I am not saying AMOC collapse will cool Europe, or that any one model result is the answer. Model ensembles share an evidence pool, so their agreement is not corroboration. I am not claiming the atmospheric bridge is a new primitive; it is the network structure the framework already holds, imported with its provenance recorded.
>
> **What would break the shape claim:**
> 1. one lever clears the rainfall, temperature and ecosystem residuals together, with no categorically different residue left over;
> 2. a local-only frame reproduces a documented far-field response with no pathway term;
> 3. a global mean predicts the per-slot residuals with no region-specific term;
> 4. the pathway vocabulary measures high and non-declining while remote predictions still fail;
> 5. two pathway arrangements with the same forcing give identical regional outcomes, with no detectable difference of arrangement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The local extract is at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project folder; the public architecture page and the corpus are below.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is offered as a method, not as a conclusion. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Stage 2, opt-in only

Send the links only after the stage-1 verdict is recorded. Once a recipient has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*). Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
