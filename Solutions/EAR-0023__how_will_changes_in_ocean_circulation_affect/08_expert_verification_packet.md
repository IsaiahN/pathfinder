# Expert verification packet — the Tether on far-field ocean circulation impacts

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ocean circulation or climate researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the shape, `general` for structural facts. Not `grounded`.** The regional forecast is a declared `gap`. No measurement is claimed by this packet.

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** It is checked for internal consistency against the figures, not validated against the world. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A change in basin-scale ocean circulation produces a residual in distant land regions that a local-only frame cannot close. The answer must therefore be given per region and per medium (rainfall, temperature, ecosystems), because a global average discards the live signal and the three media are a union of overlapping conditions with separate grounds. The effect is carried by a network, so the pathway description is an import from a frame whose closure differs, not something derivable from local predictors. The pathway is an ordered cascade from ocean to atmosphere to land surface to biota, which makes the ecosystem effect third order; the same circulation strength under a different pathway arrangement is a different outcome. Observations, not model agreement, settle each slot. The regional sign, size and timing is a `gap`, with named instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 9

- **Statement:** the answer is indexed by region and medium; the three media form a union, not one averaged problem.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :55 — *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Refuter:** one lever clears rainfall, temperature and ecosystem residuals together with no categorically different residue; or per-slot residuals are fully predicted by a global mean with no region-specific term. Either falsifies C1.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable against the teleconnection and attribution literature

- **Statement:** the remote response is carried by a network and is underivable from a local-only frame; the pathway is an import from a frame whose closure differs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :25 — *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*. Fig 6 :23 — *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :53 — *"Residual first, frame second."*
- **What to check:** is the atmospheric bridge a genuine carrier of remote influence (Alexander et al. 2002; Liu et al. 2007), and can a local-only prediction reproduce a documented far-field response? If a local frame does reproduce it, the import is unnecessary.
- **Refuter:** the remote response is fully derivable from local predictors with no pathway atom, across a documented circulation change. That falsifies C2.

### C3 — framework-internal plus observation-facing

- **Statement:** the pathway is an ordered cascade three steps out; the middle dependency link is the one to instrument; observations, not agreement, settle each slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 — *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 11 :52 — *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **Refuter:** two pathway arrangements with the same forcing produce identical per-slot residuals with no detectable bond difference; or the remote ecosystem residual is fully accounted for by direct contact alone; or a change in model consensus with no new observation settles a regional claim. Any of these falsifies C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a regional number, a date or a sign. No named place is assigned a forecast.
- Not the headline that AMOC collapse will cool Europe, or any single world-source result. The world sources dispute timing and magnitude among themselves; the figures do not arbitrate that dispute.
- Not that model agreement corroborates the shape. Ensembles share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a derivation of atmospheric teleconnection physics from the six-term substrate. It is a provenanced import.
- Not a claim that the atmospheric bridge is a seventh primitive. It is the network structure already held.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A pathway-resolving attribution that separates circulation-driven from radiative-forcing-driven change in a named region.
2. A sustained direct transport record long enough to distinguish a trend from decadal variability (RAPID, OSNAP, Argo).
3. A third-order ecological series at a remote site with a stated threshold.
4. Any of the pre-registered refuters firing, which would break the shape rather than close the gap.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 9 :55 (three short passages). Do per-slot residuals survive a global mean in your field?
2. Read Fig 13 :25, Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :53. Is the import of a pathway frame a fair description of how teleconnection physics entered the picture, or is the pathway already local?
3. Read Fig 11 :52. Name one remote ecosystem effect in your own system that is three steps from the direct forcing, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
