## Three things in the literature that bear directly on this shape

1. **The fingerprint is a projection, not a source reading.** Caesar et al. identify a circulation change through a sea-surface-temperature pattern and calibrate it with models. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay) says an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*. The fingerprint is a reading; the pathway arrangement that produced a given regional outcome is the source, and a source-discriminating observation is what is owed. This is why the package refuses to name a regional sign from a global index.
2. **The pathway is a genuine import with a source gate.** Alexander et al. and Liu establish that remote influence travels through an atmospheric bridge. That is a frame whose closure differs from a local-predictor frame (Fig 6 :23). It is cited with provenance, not smuggled in as an analogy. The shadow test is that it explains the remote gap; the source gate is the published record.
3. **The world sources disagree about timing, and that is not a figure conflict.** The Met Office reports the IPCC assessment of medium confidence that collapse will not occur this century; Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen estimate a mid-century tipping. Fig 2 :40 says agreement within a shared evidence pool is not corroboration, and the canonical rule says a disagreement that strains a reading sends it back to the census. Both were returned to the census. The figure verdict stays at shape level and is not flipped by either.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27:** *"describe it before you go looking"*. Ask what they would expect to see in a far-field slot if the shape claim were true, before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :13:** *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Ten agreeing readers are one room, not ten witnesses.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) what would they count as a failure, recorded before any theory; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. The recipient checks a per-region, per-medium expectation without holding the framework's interior. This is Fig 10 :52 (*"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*) and Fig 2 :45 (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** only if curious, read the theory that produced it. The verdict on stage 1 must be recorded first; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. This is permitted (Fig 4 :22), but each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it (Fig 5 :62-63: the sought-for shape is a preference, not a term, so the mechanism cannot see it).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. It is a shape, not a regional number. If a recipient asks for a forecast, the honest answer is the declared `gap` in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §2.7 and the instruments in §4.
