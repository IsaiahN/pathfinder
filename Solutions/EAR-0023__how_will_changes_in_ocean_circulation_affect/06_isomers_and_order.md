# Cascade order, union structure and the limit of a local frame

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

A change in a basin-scale ocean flow produces a mismatch in remote land slots. Three questions are owed: (1) what is the order of the chain that carries the effect, and is that order part of the answer; (2) is the three-medium question a partition or a union; (3) can a frame containing only local predictors derive the remote response, or is an import from a differing closure required?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What is the order of the chain, and is the order part of the answer?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Operators table, `→`: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, non-symmetric.
- Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*

The chain ocean → atmosphere → land surface → biota alternates states and structures. The circulation is a flow (energy + gradient), the bridge is a network, and each land response is a cross-kind molecule. The order is **part of the term**, not a notation choice: a different arrangement of the same atoms is a different substance under Fig 12. This is why a single scalar "circulation strength" cannot carry the answer. Two states with the same strength but different pathway arrangements are isomers in the figure's sense.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two pathway arrangements with the same forcing produce the same per-slot residuals with no detectable bond difference, the arrangement is free and this finding fails. Triangulated (Fig 12 bond definition and Operators `→` ordering).

### Q2 — Partition or union for rainfall, temperature and ecosystems?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Rainfall and temperature can move in opposite directions in the same region; an ecosystem response can follow the rainfall tail with a delay and a threshold. The intersection is a compound with its own loop and its own ground. Treating the three as one averaged problem is the error Fig 1 :22 names: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Falsifier:** one intervention clears all three residuals with no categorically different residue. That collapses the union and fails the finding. Triangulated (Fig 9 union rule and Fig 1 per-slot rule, low overlap).

### Q3 — Can a local frame derive the remote response?

- Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
- Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*
- Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

A frame whose library contains only local predictors is closed with respect to a remote effect: searching it harder adds no pathway atom. The needed atom is reached by OUTWARD import, by describing the residual in effect terms and finding the frame whose closure predicts its shape. That frame is atmospheric teleconnection physics, which already holds the bridge. The import has a source gate and a shadow test (recorded in the source ledger); it adds no new substrate primitive (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).

The distinction between underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget is live here. The pathway is not underivable in principle; instruments exist (RAPID, OSNAP, Argo, reanalysis). The current limit is short records and contested pathways, so this is closer to unreachable at this budget. The remedy is SEARCHED first, INWARD second, and an import only where the closure genuinely differs.

**Falsifier:** the remote response is fully derivable from a local-only frame with no imported pathway atom, across a documented circulation change. Single-source for the closure law (Fig 6/Fig 8, one framework cluster); the physical mapping is world corroboration, not a figure line.

### Q4 — How far out does the habitat go?

- Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Direct-contact enumeration (ocean adjacent coast) is first order. The rainfall and temperature response is second order. The ecosystem response is third order, which is exactly the phrase "far from the ocean". The same circulation change can be a benefactor to one slot and a malfactor to another, so there is no standing list of good or bad changes. The habitat is enumerated from the world, never composed.

**Falsifier:** a documented case where the remote ecosystem residual is fully accounted for by direct contact alone, with no second- or third-order term. Triangulated (Fig 11 cascade rule and its own DDT precedent).

## Analogy check (living-system test)

The nearest living analogue is the cascade of a xenobiotic through a food web to a top predator, which the figure itself records (DDT to eagles). Actors: a substance introduced at one level; currency: concentration multiplied at each transfer; anchor: the eggshell-thinning measurement that settled it. The mapping is structural, not visual: what travels is influence through contact, and the residual that matters is three steps out. The second analogue is a signal in a network: the same source signal produces different downstream readings depending on the path, which is Fig 12's isomer claim. No analogue is claimed between ocean currents and any particular wave train; the figures govern the relation, not the physics.

## CONCLUSION

The chain that carries a far-field ocean effect is an **ordered, cross-kind cascade**, and the order is part of the substance (Fig 12 :117). The three media form a **union** of overlapping conditions, each with its own ground (Fig 9 :55-57). A **local-only frame cannot derive the remote response**; the pathway atom is an import from a frame whose closure differs, found by describing the residual first (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :53). The habitat extends **three steps out**, so direct-contact scoping misses the ecosystem effect (Fig 11 :52). The empirical sign, size and timing remain a declared `gap` (Fig 13 :134).

What survives is a shape and a route. What does not survive is any claim that the figures name a particular region's outcome or that agreement among like frames closes the question.
