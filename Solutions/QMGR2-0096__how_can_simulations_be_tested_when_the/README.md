## What this package decides, in effect terms

A simulation is a library Γ of physical laws, initial conditions and a numerical scheme. It generates predictions that can be compared with observations of the universe we can actually see. The laboratory recreation of the early universe is not available. The question is what counts as a test when the direct experiment is missing.

Two options and what they lead to:

- **Option A:** treat internal consistency, visual fidelity, resolution, or agreement with other simulations as the test. This leads to a frame scoring itself with quantities it also produces, and to a room of agreeing frames that share one evidence pool. It produces a convention, not a ground verdict.
- **Option B:** treat the observed universe as the anchor; range each prediction against it separately; buy independence between probes; commit predictions before observing; use laboratory analogues only for wiring; record what was unreachable and what would reverse the verdict. This leads to a checkable shape with a stated refuter and an honest stop.

The package derives Option B and states the refuter that would break it.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **Agreement is not a ground.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Adding more simulators who share initial conditions and tuning data raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention. Agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 must see the residual and the shape claim, not the framework's interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
