## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- "the selected observable is stable under the interaction, robust, and redundantly recorded" is a shape.
- "the preferred basis is position" is a name.

The distinction matters because the formal question is basis-agnostic. A shape narrows the space of candidates without pretending the formalism selected one.

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- The residual was described in effect terms in `01` section 0 before any figure line was selected: a basis-democratic formalism against a basis-specific outcome. That order is required by Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place" and the paradox is that by then you have "already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50), with a source gate and shadow test.

For the preferred basis, a name would require an instrument that discriminates among candidate couplings and records, plus the outcome reading. The SEP decoherence entry states the analogous physical condition: what states are preferred "will depend on the details of the interaction". That dependence is the ground-shaped constraint; it is not a name.

## 4. Why "all bases equivalent" is not the same as "no basis selected"

*Library of Babel* names the bundle failure directly: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" The preferred basis question is such a bundle (F6 of `01`). Two claims must be kept apart:

- Equivalence of bases as descriptions of one state: identity, "two names, one referent" (Operators table :25).
- Selection of an observable by a physical coupling: a bond to an external frame, settled by an outcome (F1, F2, F4 of `01`).

A shape answer keeps both and refuses the inference from the first to the denial of the second. The inferred term is the one with the persistent residual (F7 of `01`).

## 5. The low-overlap analogue: metamerism

From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (as mapped in the exemplar, not re-fetched here): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". This session opened an independent source in color science: Wikipedia's Metamerism (color) page states "Metamerism is a perceived matching of colors with different (nonmatching) spectral power distributions." Different spectral combinations give the same receptor response and the same colour sensation.

The analogy is structural and low-overlap: one reading does not determine the source. Applied here, the state's equivalence over bases is one reading, and it does not determine which observable the apparatus actually records. The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the colour case. Here the corresponding instrument would have to discriminate among candidate pointer observables and their redundancy in the environment. The analogy corroborates the shape-not-a-name limit; it does not decide the physics, and Fig 2 :35 says "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

## 6. Prestige is not action, and this audit is a simulation

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A list of famous endorsements cannot settle which basis is preferred.

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01`, `06`, `07` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 7. Consequence for this question

The honest shape answer is: a measurement singles out an observable through contact with an external frame, the observable is stable and redundant for that coupling, and which one it is awaits a ground reading. The framework returns the kind of thing, not the name. This is `general` at the structural level and `specified` as applied to measurement, ungrounded throughout.

### Falsifier for this shape reading

If a single system under fixed conditions yields definite outcomes in two incompatible bases, or if the state alone determines the basis with no external contact, then the shape collapses to a name or to nothing, and sections 1 to 7 are withdrawn. Written before the run; see `01` section 4.
