**Subject:** One checkable claim about the preferred basis, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the preferred basis problem that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the observable a measurement singles out is not readable off the quantum state. It is fixed by how that system is coupled to something outside it, namely the apparatus and the environment, and it is settled only by the recorded outcome. The formalism itself cannot pick a basis: it treats all bases as equivalent descriptions of one state. That equivalence is a relabelling, not a physical preference. What the measurement selects is a stable, robust, redundantly recorded observable, and which one that is depends on the system and the coupling, not on one universal basis.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names position, or any observable, as *the* preferred basis. It returns a shape, not a name. Decoherence is often said to have solved this problem. I am claiming only that it selects a stable set from within one frame; it does not by itself account for a definite outcome. That is a separation of questions, not a dismissal of the result.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. One system, identical preparation and coupling, returning definite outcomes in two incompatible bases with no change in conditions.
> 2. A derivation inside unitary quantum mechanics alone, with no environment, apparatus, cut or added record, yielding a unique preferred basis and definite outcomes.
> 3. A rule that recovers the measurement basis from the state vector alone, with no external contact.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the derivation and the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** state the residual in effect terms, write down what shape an answer would have as an ordinary term, and write the refuter before the run. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
