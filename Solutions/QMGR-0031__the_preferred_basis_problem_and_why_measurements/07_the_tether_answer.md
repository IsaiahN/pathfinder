# The Tether's answer on the preferred basis problem

**Mode: `specified` on the answer, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The formalism treats all bases as equivalent descriptions of one state. Measurement returns a definite outcome in one basis. The gap is between a basis-democratic formalism and a basis-specific outcome. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: "whether any stable basis exists", "which observable is selected", and "why one definite outcome" overlap and need separate loops (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"). Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

## 2. The shape

1. **The preference is in the bond, not the operand.** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." The state does not carry its own measurement basis. The basis is supplied by a coupling to something outside the system.
2. **The formalism is a closed frame; selection enters as an import.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Unitary evolution plus the state cannot pick an observable. The apparatus and environment form the differing closure. Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
3. **Basis equivalence is identity, not physical preference.** Operators table :25 "two names, one referent". Changing basis is a relabelling; selecting an observable is a physical coupling. The inference from the first to a denial of the second is the term that keeps failing (Fig 5 :59 "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at").
4. **The selected observable is per system and per coupling.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." There is no universal preferred basis. What is stable depends on the interaction. Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
5. **The outcome is the only anchor.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Interpretive agreement and decrees do not settle it. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
6. **The answer is a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The shape is: stable under the interaction, robust, redundantly recorded. Which observable satisfies that is a ground question. No figure line governs the name after re-census; that is a `gap`, reported plainly, not a silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that position, or any observable, is *the* preferred basis by framework decree. That is an import needing provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6, Fig 8).
- Not that decoherence alone solves the problem. It suppresses interference and selects a stable set; it does not, by itself, deliver a definite outcome. That is the union split of section 1.
- Not a new primitive, not a seventh term, and not a derivation of one basis from the Hamiltonian alone.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles `left(R,φ)`.
- Not a verdict from agreement. A consensus of interpretations is a convention, not a witness (Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention").

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

An instrument that discriminates among candidate pointer observables and their redundancy, plus the recorded outcome. The three refuters written in advance (`01` section 4) double as the ground tests:

- **Ground test:** one system, identical preparation and coupling, definite outcomes in two incompatible bases with no change in conditions would break the shape.
- **Closure test:** a derivation inside unitary quantum mechanics alone, with no environment, apparatus, cut or added record, yielding a unique basis and definite outcomes would break the import claim.
- **Contact test:** a rule recovering the basis from the state vector alone would break the bond-level claim.
- **Dependency test:** an instrument at the outcome link (Fig 3) that resolves outcomes without added interpretation would close the union's hardest link.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set gives the kind of thing to look for and the constraints it must satisfy, then stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." The name of the observable is a gap after a genuine re-census; a ground reading, not another argument, would close it. The canonical README's "a shape, not a name" governs here, with the scope correction from `05` section 3: the limit applies to what the figures cannot derive, not to what a provenanced import can report.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a preferred basis, and it does not claim that decoherence alone solves the problem. It says that the quantum state does not carry its own measurement basis: the basis is a bond-level fact supplied by coupling to something outside the system, selected from a frame whose closure differs, and settled only by the recorded outcome. All bases are equivalent as descriptions of one state, which is identity, not physical preference, so that equivalence cannot be used to deny that a measurement selects an observable. What is selected is per system and per coupling: stable under the interaction, robust, and redundantly recorded. That is the shape any candidate answer must have. The three refuters were written in advance, and the name of the observable awaits a ground reading. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the center stays open until the ground settles it.
