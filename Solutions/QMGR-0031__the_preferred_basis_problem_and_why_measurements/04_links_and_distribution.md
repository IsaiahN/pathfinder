## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the framework before a recipient has described a residual sends the source, not the characterisation. Lead with the refuter and the shape.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the verdict; (3) only then the derivation and links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. Their stage-1 verdict is recorded before any framework material is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted** (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."). The method is: describe the residual, bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, write the refuter first, state the mode.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** The formula's Direction section says a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question". Each user writes their own refuter before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. Its refuters are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
