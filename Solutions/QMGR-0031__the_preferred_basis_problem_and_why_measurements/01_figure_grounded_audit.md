## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A quantum state admits infinitely many expansions; no term in the unitary formalism privileges one basis over another. A measurement nevertheless returns a definite outcome in one particular basis, typically a quasi-classical, localised one. The residual R is the gap between a basis-democratic formalism and a basis-specific outcome.

- **Arity.** At least two conditions: (i) why any definite basis at all, and (ii) why this observable. These are not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Symmetry.** The formalism has a symmetry over bases; the outcome breaks it. Fig 8 describes the frame-transform limit in general terms; here the break is not in the state but in the contact. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The state cannot be that anchor, because it does not change under a relabelling.
- **Scale.** First order inside the system (no basis is preferred). Second order at system plus apparatus plus environment (a basis is selected). The residual lives at the crossing, not inside either frame.

## 3. Findings

Each finding states its mode, a verbatim figure line read from the local extract before concluding, a refuter, and its triangulation status. Verdicts are not averaged across domains.

### F1. The basis is a bond-level fact, not an ingredient-level fact (decisive, `general` → `specified`)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Operators table :4: "The bond carries information the operands do not, so a term that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."

The state vector names its ingredients (amplitudes over a chosen basis). The arrangement, namely which observable is actually coupled to a readout, is not recoverable from those ingredients. So "which basis" cannot be read off the state. It must be supplied by something that contacts the state. This is the whole shape of the answer: the preference lives in the bond, not the operand.

- **Mode:** `general` for the structural claim (ingredients never determine arrangement), `specified` for its application to measurement.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a rule that recovers the measurement basis from the state vector alone, with no reference to a coupling, apparatus or environment. If that succeeds, F1 falls.
- **Status: triangulated**, two figure passages with low overlap (Fig 12 composition and Operators table notation), corroborated by Zurek's abstract below (different field).

### F2. The formalism is a closed frame; the selection is an import (`general` structural, `specified` application)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall"
Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

Unitary evolution plus the Hilbert-space state is a closed frame with respect to basis selection: composing inside it never leaves it, so no amount of searching the formalism adds the missing observable. The environment and apparatus form a frame whose closure differs. The basis arrives as an import from that contact, with provenance, not as a theorem of the closed formalism.

- **Mode:** `general` for closure/idempotence, `specified` for "the environment is that frame here".
- **Refuter:** derive a unique preferred basis from unitary dynamics alone, with no environment, cut or coupling assumption. That would add an atom from inside the closure and break Fig 6.
- **Status: triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (different figures, different mechanisms).

### F3. Basis equivalence is identity (relabelling), not isomerism (`general`)

Operators table :25: "two names, one referent"
Operators table :28: "a definitional identity is not a recipe at all"

Under the identity bond ≡, two descriptions of one state in different bases are two names for one referent. Substituting one for the other loses nothing. This is a decisive distinction: the formalism's basis democracy is an equivalence over *descriptions*, which does not imply that all observables are *physically realised* or equally available for readout. Conflating passive relabelling with active selection is the category error that generates much of the puzzle.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** show that two basis descriptions of the same state differ in physical predictions with the same coupling. Then they are isomers (Fig 12 :117), not identities, and this finding falls.
- **Status: triangulated** (Operators table identity definition plus Fig 12 bond meaning). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

### F4. The ground, not agreement, settles which basis (`general`)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"

The recorded outcome is the anchor: it does not change when an interpreter changes their reading. Interpretive consensus about the measurement problem is not such an anchor. Nor is decreeing that position is the preferred basis. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** produce a case where the recorded outcome changes with the interpreter's framework while all physical conditions are held fixed, or show a convention that decides an outcome without ceasing to be a convention (Seat Map :163). The dependency order (Fig 3) would also fall if "which observable" could be settled before "whether any stable basis exists".
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 10 declared-meaning rule).

### F5. The residual is per slot, not global (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

There is no single "preferred basis of the universe". Which observable is stable depends on which system and which coupling. Summing over systems produces the false impression of one universal basis.

- **Mode:** `general`, with the `specified` application that pointer states are system and coupling dependent.
- **Refuter:** exhibit one basis preferred for all systems under all couplings, independent of the interaction Hamiltonian.
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 per-slot rule plus the SEP decoherence statement that preferred states depend on interaction details).

### F6. "Whether" precedes "which": the bundle must be split first (`general`)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

The question as posed bundles (i) why a definite basis at all with (ii) why this observable and (iii) why one definite outcome. The union has its own loop. Asking "why position?" before asking "why any stable basis?" is working above the break. The answer returned is a kind of thing (a stable, robust, redundantly recorded observable), not a name.

- **Mode:** `general` for the unbundling rule, `specified` for claiming this question is a union.
- **Refuter:** show the three sub-questions collapse to one under a single principle, so no split is owed.
- **Status: single-source for the union claim (Fig 9), corroborated by the SEP decoherence entry which separates the preferred-basis question from the problem of outcomes.**

### F7. The term to refuse is the inference, not the symmetry (`specified`)

Fig 5 :59: "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"
Fig 5 :67: "The direction claim is a LIMIT rather than a step"
Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"

The held term with a flat residual is not "the state is basis-independent" (that is true and settled). It is "basis-independence of the formalism implies no observable is physically selected." That inference keeps failing against every measurement. It owes a negation (¬), not a new explanation. The sought-for shape (a selected observable) is held as a preference, not a term, so the mechanism cannot see it; writing it down as an ordinary term, as this audit does, makes it refusable.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** show that the inference's residual trends down under more search inside the formalism, or that a new explanatory term, not a refusal, closes it.
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 5 fifth-kind amendment and Operators table negation).

### F8. Decoherence from within standard quantum mechanics is one frame, not two (`specified`, with a live strain)

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

Decoherence is derived from the same quantum formalism that generated the puzzle. By itself it is one frame reasoning about its own closure. Its result gains independent force only when the environment is treated as a frame whose closure differs and the outcome is read against the ground. This is a strain to record, not to hide: the literature itself says decoherence explains why we do not see superpositions of outcomes but not why we see outcomes at all. That is exactly the union split of F6.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** produce a within-formalism derivation of definite outcomes (not merely suppressed interference) with no interpretation, hidden variable, collapse or external record added. That would collapse the union and remove the strain.
- **Status: triangulated** for the independence rule (Fig 2 + Fig 8); the physics claim is single-source and explicitly corroborated by the SEP decoherence entry, which is secondary.

### F9. The figure that is often stretched here does not govern this quantity (re-census, `general`)

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

An earlier reading of this question tried to use the six-term substrate to name the preferred basis (position as Space, energy as Energy, and so on). That is a strain. Fig 13 governs composition of the six substrate terms; it does not rank observables inside a Hilbert space. Re-census sends the line back to its own quantity: it can say what composes with what, not which observable a pointer couples to. No figure line governs the *name* of the preferred basis.

- **Mode:** `general` (a limit on what the figure licenses).
- **Refuter:** derive a unique observable basis from the six-term set with no further physical input. Until then, naming one is an import requiring a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6, Fig 8).
- **Status: gap for the name; figure-governed for the shape.** "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." The honest report is that the figure set returns a shape, and the name awaits a ground reading.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is F1 to F6 above, stated as: "the observable a measurement singles out is fixed by coupling to an external frame, is stable and per-system, and is settled only by an outcome reading; the figures return its shape, not its name."

It is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Ground refutation.** A single physical system, under identical preparation and coupling, yields definite outcomes in two incompatible bases on repeated trials with no change in conditions. This breaks F4 and F5.
2. **Closure refutation.** A derivation inside unitary quantum mechanics alone, with no environment, apparatus, cut or added record, yields a unique preferred basis and definite outcomes. This breaks F2 and F8.
3. **Independence refutation.** The selection is shown to be fully determined by the state vector alone, with no external contact, so F1's bond-level claim is false.
4. **Name-refutation.** A figure-grounded derivation names the preferred observable without a ground reading or a provenanced import. This would break F9 and the shape-not-a-name limit.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction is written into the log below. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: the basis is a bond-level, contact-selected fact, not readable from the state. This is the part the figures decide and the part a physicist can check.
2. State the mode on every sentence (F7, F10). The answer is `specified` and a shape.
3. Split the bundle before answering (F6): "whether stable basis" and "why this observable" and "why one outcome" get separate loops and separate grounds.
4. Do not name position, or any observable, as the framework's answer. Report the shape: stable under the interaction, robust, redundantly recorded. Any name is an import with provenance and a shadow test (F9).
5. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Offer the method of describing the residual and writing a refuter; do not push the framework's interior before a verdict is recorded.
