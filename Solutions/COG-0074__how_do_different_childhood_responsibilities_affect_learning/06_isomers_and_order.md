# Are learning, independence and cooperation isomers? And what bond orders a responsibility?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). It is a supporting derivation: it does not answer which responsibility does what, it says what kind of thing the three outcomes are and what order binds them.

## RESIDUAL

The question names three outcomes as if they were three effects of one lever. Two things are owed: (1) are learning, independence and cooperation **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or a **functional group**, and (2) what bond type orders a responsibility into an outcome? If the three outcomes are the same quantity under three names, the per-slot claim in `01` F2 is wrong and a single score is legitimate.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available in the description: the child (a system with a library), the responsibility (a habitat change: actors, conditions, relations), and feedback (what arrives back). Bonds available: Fig 12's seven, plus negation. No new primitive is introduced. The three outcome labels are not atoms; they are candidate molecules to be typed by their bond.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three outcomes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Under a **fixed child-and-habitat atom set**, varying only the arrangement of the responsibility (who directs it, what feedback returns, whether the support is tapered), the three outcomes can be read as **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different developmental substance. This is the strong form.
- As the question actually writes them, the three outcomes have different currencies (a new term learned; action without external direction; contribution to a shared outcome). On the exemplar's analogous test, when the record wrote GR and QM with mostly different atoms they were **not** isomers but members of a functional group. Here the three outcomes also fail the strict isomer test as written: they do not share an identical atom set, and what differs is partly *which* atoms are involved, not only the bond.
- So the honest reading is: the three outcomes are a **functional group** under the qualifier "responsibility-driven capability" (Fig 12 :124: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"). The strong isomer claim is a **checkable structural hypothesis** only when the atom set is held fixed and the arrangement varied.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if each outcome can be produced with an atom set that neither of the others requires (for example cooperation requires peers that learning never needs, and learning requires an assessment that cooperation never needs), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands. This is likely, so the strong form is presented as a hypothesis, not a finding. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 :117, :124); single-source for the application.

### Q2 - What bond orders each outcome?

Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Reading each slot as a bond type:

| slot | candidate bond | figure test | why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| learning | production `⇒` (Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before") | is there a B before A fires? No | learning mints a term that was not in the library |
| independence | subtraction `−` (Fig 12 :82 "A with B removed or absent") | is the second operand's absence the point? Yes | independence is the removal of external direction, not the addition of a trait |
| cooperation | conjunction `+` (Fig 12 :57 "both hold at once") or comparison `⋛` | swap operands; does meaning change? No (for `+`) | cooperation holds self and other as both present, or compares contributions at a threshold |

- The three slots are **not one bond type**. A vocabulary that has only `+` (the flat formula of the exemplar) can say that all three are present and cannot say that one is a production, one a subtraction and one a conjunction. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- This **supports F2** from a second direction: a global maturity score is the flat formula, recording that the three ingredients are present and losing the arrangement that makes them different substances.
- **Falsifier:** if all three slots reduce to the same bond under a correct description (for example each is a production of a new term), the per-slot separation is weakened and the flat score becomes defensible. Mark `specified`, unmeasured. Triangulated (Fig 12 bond table + the per-slot rule in Fig 1).

### Q3 - What order binds a responsibility to an outcome?

- A responsibility is a **plan**, hence a sequence `→` (Fig 12 :71 "A then B; B needs A to have happened"), only if the child can perceive the affordance and form the plan. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- A goal is a **comparison** `⋛` (Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"): "doing it well enough" is a threshold, not a substance.
- Progress is a **subtraction** `−` (Fig 12 :112): how far along, measured against a prior state.
- So the order is: affordance -> plan (sequence) -> goal (comparison) -> progress (subtraction) -> a settled term that can become an atom for the next responsibility (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"). This is Fig 3's chain written in Fig 12's notation, and it is the structural form of the zone of proximal development (a world frame, provenanced as such, not derived).
- **Falsifier:** if a child can produce the outcome with no goal-comparison step (purely by imitation with no threshold), the sequence claim fails and the chain must be re-censused. Mark `specified`. Single-source for the figure mapping; the ZPD page is secondary corroboration.

### Q4 - Do responsibility types differ as isomers?

- Chores, caregiving and self-care share the child atom but differ in the habitat atoms they introduce (a sibling, a resource, a deadline) and in the feedback currency (gratitude, a kept routine, self-efficacy).
- Fig 12 :98: "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation." Under the framework's own rule, same atoms with a different arrangement are a different substance, so two responsibility types with the same atom inventory but a different arrangement should be expected to differ behaviourally.
- But **shared atoms are not the signature of convergence** (Fig 8 :50-52): two frames can hold the same atom by separate routes. Whether two responsibility types are genuine isomers or merely share a name needs a ground comparison, not an argument.
- **Falsifier:** if two responsibility types produce identical profiles across all three slots under every arrangement tested, the isomer claim fails and they are one thing under two names.

## CONCLUSION

The three outcomes are a **functional group** under "responsibility-driven capability" as the question writes them, and a **candidate isomer set** only when the atom set is held fixed and the arrangement varied. They are ordered by different bonds (production, subtraction, conjunction or comparison), which is a second, independent reason to refuse a single maturity score. A responsibility reaches an outcome only through the chain affordance -> sequence -> comparison -> subtraction -> settled term, and the order is dependency order, not importance order. Every claim here is `specified`; none is `grounded`. The strong isomer forms carry falsifiers written above and are presented as hypotheses, not findings.
