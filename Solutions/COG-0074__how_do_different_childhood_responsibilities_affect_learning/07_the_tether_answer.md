# The Tether's answer on childhood responsibilities

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` where one slot has a figure line plus a low-overlap world frame. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A childhood responsibility changes what a child contacts: new actors (a sibling, a household routine), conditions (a deadline, a resource), and relations (someone depends on this being done). The question asks how that change registers in three separate slots. R is indexed per slot, not as one score (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The three outcomes form a union compound with D2, not three independent questions (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound.").

## 2. The shape

1. **The effect is through contact, not through the child in isolation.** Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A responsibility with no real consequence, no feedback and no dependency changes nothing. This is necessary, not sufficient: a heavy care load can change contact and still cut into study time.
2. **Read each slot separately.** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Learning (a new term), independence (external direction removed) and cooperation (self and other both held) are different bonds (Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."), so one maturity score is the flat formula that loses the arrangement.
3. **The dependency chain gates the effect.** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A responsibility builds skill only if the child can perceive the affordance, has terms for it, can say what counts as doing it well, can plan, and can carry what worked. More responsibility above a broken link arrives where it cannot be used (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").
4. **No responsibility has a fixed valence.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." The same care duty can feed cooperation and starve learning, or feed both if structured as a learning task.
5. **Arrangement beats ingredients.** Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Which responsibilities, in what order, with what feedback and tapered support, change the substance. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` for the isomer and functional-group derivation with falsifiers.
6. **What crosses scales is a method.** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A responsibility hands the child a reusable method (plan, coordinate, name what worked), not a recording of one success.
7. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." It cannot say which responsibility produces which effect size. That is `unreached`, not an answer, and not a silence (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that chores, caregiving or paid work cause any particular outcome. Those are bindings from the world frame, provenanced as such, not figure derivations.
- Not that more responsibility is always better, or that any responsibility is inherently good. Valence is relative to the residual (F4).
- Not that the three outcomes can be added into one score. They are different bonds, and a global score is the metamerism error (`05` §4).
- Not a developmental theory, and not a replacement for developmental science. The low-overlap world frames (self-determination theory, zone of proximal development, cooperative breeding, parental care) corroborate the shape; they do not get their authority from it.
- Not `grounded`. No per-slot measurement settles `left(R,φ)`.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A per-slot instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Contact test:** describe a responsibility by which actors, currency and anchor it changes, then measure whether a slot moves when contact is held constant versus changed. Refuter 1 in `01` §5 fires if a no-contact-change duty still moves a slot.
- **Slot discriminator:** show that separate slot scores carry signal a single global score loses. Refuter 2 fires if a global score is lossless.
- **Chain test:** identify a broken link (no affordance perception, no terms, no goal) and show that adding responsibility does not produce competent action. Refuter 3 fires if it does.
- **Valence test:** show a responsibility with a fixed, residual-independent valence across all slots and arrangements. Refuter 4 fires if one exists.
- **Ground reading:** a longitudinal or experimental design, ideally a registered report (the venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`), that settles a specific mapping with the refuter written first.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached at this budget, not unreachable in principle.** The six-term set and the figure rules are "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. Fig 8 :40-43 separates underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-this-budget, and importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed. So the stop here is a budget stop: the shape is stated, the discriminating instruments are named, and the lineage is recorded. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer which childhood responsibility builds which trait, and it does not claim the final word on development. It says that a responsibility acts only by changing what a child contacts, so a duty that changes nothing about contact changes nothing; that learning, independence and cooperation are three different bonds and must be read separately, never folded into one maturity score; that a responsibility builds skill only when the child can perceive the task, name it, set a standard and carry what worked; that no responsibility is inherently good or bad, because its valence depends on which residual it meets; and that the arrangement of responsibilities matters as much as their ingredients. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the per-slot instruments that would close it. The specific effect sizes stay open until the ground settles them.
