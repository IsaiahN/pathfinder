# Expert verification packet - the Tether on childhood responsibilities

**What this is:** a claim in a form a developmental scientist or education researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the slot-level application. Not `grounded`.** No per-slot measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A childhood responsibility (a chore, caring for a sibling, managing a resource) is a change to what a child contacts: new actors, conditions and relations. Its effect lives in the child-and-habitat system, not in the child in isolation, so a duty that changes no contact changes nothing. The effect must be read in three separate slots, because learning (a new term), independence (external direction removed) and cooperation (self and other both held) are different bonds, not one quantity. A responsibility builds skill only through an intact dependency chain: perceive the affordance, have terms for it, say what counts as doing it well, plan, carry what worked. No responsibility has a fixed valence; the same duty can feed one slot and starve another. The framework does not name which responsibility produces which effect size. It offers the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close each slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and contact-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11
- **Statement:** contact is necessary. A responsibility that changes no contact changes nothing; capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, not the agent alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Supporting: Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." and Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
- **What to check:** is "responsibility" correctly described as a habitat change (actors, conditions, relations), and is the necessity claim distinguished from the sufficiency claim we retracted? A heavy care load changes contact and can still harm a slot; that is not a counterexample to necessity.
- **Refuter:** a responsibility that demonstrably changes no contact (no new actor, condition, relation or feedback) still changes one of the three slots; or the same responsibility has a fixed, residual-independent valence across all slots and arrangements.

### C2 - per-slot; checkable by a developmental scientist against measurement practice
- **Statement:** the three outcomes are different bonds and must be reported separately. Learning reads as production, independence as subtraction, cooperation as conjunction or comparison.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- **What to check:** is a single global "maturity" or "self-reliance" score lossless with respect to the three slots? In measurement terms, does the aggregate carry all the between-slot signal? If it does, this sub-claim fails.
- **Refuter:** a single global score predicts each slot as well as separate scores with no loss of signal; or all three slots reduce to the same bond under a correct description.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a world-facing test; checkable by an education researcher
- **Statement:** an intact dependency chain gates the effect; more responsibility above a broken link cannot produce competent action. This is the figure form of the zone of proximal development (a world frame, used as corroboration, not as a derivation).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- **What to check:** can the child perceive the affordance, name what the task affords, state a standard for doing it well, plan, and carry what worked? If a link is absent, is adding responsibility the wrong intervention? The ZPD page (opened 2026-11-22, in the ledger) says responsibility within reach with tapered support builds skill; treat that as corroboration at low overlap, not as the source of the claim.
- **Refuter:** adding responsibility above a demonstrably broken link still produces competent action; or a child produces the outcome with no goal-comparison step at all, which would force a re-census of the chain.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that chores, caregiving or paid work cause any particular outcome. No effect size is asserted.
- Not that more responsibility is always better, or that any responsibility is inherently good.
- Not that the three outcomes can be added into one score. That is the flat-formula and metamerism error (`05` §4).
- Not a developmental theory and not a replacement for developmental science. Four low-overlap world frames were opened (self-determination theory, zone of proximal development, cooperative breeding, parental care); all four are Wikipedia summaries, so their agreement is partly one pool, and none outranks the figures or vice versa in this packet's reasoning.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is asserted as having settled anything.
- Not a claim that the strong isomer version in `06_isomers_and_order.md` holds. That version carries its own falsifier and is presented as a hypothesis.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot instrument, not a further argument.

1. **Contact test:** hold contact constant versus changed, and see whether a slot moves. If a no-change duty moves a slot, C1 fails.
2. **Slot discriminator:** show that separate slot scores carry signal a global score loses. If the global score is lossless, C2 fails.
3. **Chain test:** identify a broken link and show that adding responsibility does not produce competent action. If it does, C3 fails.
4. **Valence test:** show a responsibility with a fixed, residual-independent valence across all slots and arrangements. If one exists, C1's second clause fails.
5. **Ground reading:** a registered report (venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`) settling a specific mapping with the refuter written first.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :40 and :49-50, Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 12 :111-112, Fig 3 :19-20 and :27. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against measurement practice: is a single maturity score lossless with respect to the three slots?
3. Check C3 against a case you know: a child given a responsibility above their current capability. Did the support structure exist, and was it tapered?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the three slots, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
