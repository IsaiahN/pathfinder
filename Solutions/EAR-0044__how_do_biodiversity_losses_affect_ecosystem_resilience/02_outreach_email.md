**Subject:** One checkable claim about biodiversity, resilience, carbon and recovery

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a decision maker wants to know what is removed when diversity declines, and what follows for three outcomes that are measured differently. Resilience is the variance and persistence of a function through change. Carbon storage is a conserved stock in biomass and soil. Recovery is an ordered trajectory after a perturbation. These have different currencies and different anchors, so a single "effect of biodiversity loss" cannot settle any of them.
>
> **The shape claim:** diversity acts as the set of rooms available for selection. A disturbance surfaces a residual, and the system keeps a function only if some remaining room has a different response and is selected against that residual. What matters is independence of responses, not the number of names in the list. Many species with the same response are one room wearing many names. Carbon follows a different route: several species can compose the same carbon-accumulating substance, but the sign and magnitude of the effect are stock-specific and scale-specific. Recovery is a substituted habitat with unchosen actors, not a replay of what was there.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any figure returns a percentage; that a diversity index is a cause rather than a reading; that more species always means more resilience; that a fast carbon sink is a resilient ecosystem; that restoration returns a previous state. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Where a number exists, it is a measurement from a ground, not a derivation.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message was sent):**
> 1. Holding response diversity and independence constant, a loss of richness changes a function with no change in the availability of any room.
> 2. A single lever is shown to clear all three outcomes' intersections, so the three outcomes form a partition rather than a union.
> 3. A carbon stock composes within one kind, with no cross-kind structure between the states it relates.
> 4. A restored system is shown to be a genuine removal of habitat rather than a substitution, with no unchosen actors inherited.
> 5. The frame grading an intervention can correct the anchor it is ranged against.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding domain verdict is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
