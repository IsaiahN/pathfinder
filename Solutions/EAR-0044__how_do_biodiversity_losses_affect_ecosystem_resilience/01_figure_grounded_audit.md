## 0. The question restated as an effect

Question: "How do biodiversity losses affect ecosystem resilience, carbon storage, and recovery after disturbance?"

Stated in effect terms, a decision maker wants to know what is removed when diversity declines and what follows for three outcomes that are measured differently:

- **Resilience:** does the system keep delivering a function while conditions change? Currency: variance and persistence of a flux. Anchor: a measured function through a perturbation.
- **Carbon storage:** how much carbon is held in biomass and soil? Currency: mass of carbon, a conserved quantity in a finite region. Anchor: a measured inventory.
- **Recovery after disturbance:** how does the system return after a perturbation? Currency: an ordered trajectory through time. Anchor: a measured return to a reference state.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a *separator*, namely a set of conditions that says for each outcome and each slot whether the effect of losing diversity is felt, plus a refuter and an instrument that would settle each branch. A single global percentage is *not* the sought-for object; it is the shape the question appears to ask for but which the figures refuse.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 The residual R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three outcomes (resilience, carbon, recovery) crossed with many slots (species, functional groups, trophic positions) and many disturbance regimes. It is not a one-argument function of a richness number.
- **Symmetry:** the verb "affect" is symmetric in form, while every causal claim here is ordered. A correlation between a diversity index and an outcome cannot choose the arrow. Operators table :15 tests symmetry by swapping operands; :33 defines the ordered sequence bond. Fig 2 :39 names "Correlation has valence" and Fig 2 :40 makes shared-pool agreement "disqualifying for a verdict".
- **Scale:** per slot first (Fig 1 :19), across scales through the membrane (Fig 4 :22). A landscape-level richness number and a plot-level response are different readings, not the same reading at two resolutions.

A lever that clears the region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56).

## 3. Findings F1 to F12

### F1: The question is a union, not one effect size

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

The residual is three outcomes with different currencies and anchors. A pooled "biodiversity loss reduces ecosystem function by X percent" is a filter for the budget, not a witness for any of the three verdicts. World corroboration: Ives and Carpenter 2007 in *Science* write that "different types of stability describing different properties of ecosystems lead to multiple diversity-stability relationships." Moffett et al. 2026 in *Nature Ecology & Evolution* find that "regulating services, including water quality and pollination, often exhibit linear relationships" while "hazard regulation appear relatively insensitive to biodiversity, instead depending on a few functionally unique foundational species". Different services, different verdicts, in one synthesis.

**Verdict per domain: `general` structural split, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 2 and the two world sources (low overlap between the stability literature and the 2026 service synthesis).**

### F2: The effect registers per slot, not as a plot average

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Symbols table :43-44: R is "measured per slot, never as a single global number."

A species count averages over the very variation that carries the effect. Mori, Furukawa and Sasaki 2013 in *Biological Reviews* state that "Traditional diversity measures (e.g. 'species richness') may not adequately capture the aspects of biodiversity most relevant to ecosystem stability and functionality", and define "response diversity, describing the variation of responses to environmental change among species of a particular community." That is a per-slot reading. Moffett et al. 2026 add that blue carbon sequestration is "exceptionally sensitive" while hazard regulation is not, which is a verdict difference between slots, not a global constant.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 + Symbols table + two independent ecology sources).**

### F3: Diversity loss is a change of contact, not a subtraction of items

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :47: "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Three corrections follow, and they are kept visible rather than folded into the headline:

- C1. "Loss" is not subtraction from a habitat. Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." A cleared forest replaced by pasture is a substituted habitat with unchosen actors, not a forest with fewer items.
- C2. The seat's power is asymmetric. Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Restoration adds; it does not restore a previous substrate by decree.
- C3. Valence is relative to the residual. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A species that is a benefactor for carbon may be a malfactor for recovery; no standing list of good species survives a change of outcome (Fig 11 :51).

Fig 11 :52 warns that contact cascades: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles". The effect of losing a pollinator can be three steps out from the measured function.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 internal set plus the habitat enumeration in Mori et al. 2013 and Moffett et al. 2026).**

### F4: Resilience is selection between rooms, not a stock of species

Fig 7 :33: "→ variation inside, selection between". Fig 7 :24-25: "low ρ between rooms; the next room is picked against the residual the last one surfaced". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

A disturbance surfaces a residual: a changed temperature, a new pathogen, a altered fire regime. Resilience is whether some remaining room (some species or functional group with a different response) can be selected against that residual and keep the function paid. Losing response diversity removes candidate rooms. If every remaining room has the same response ("one long room"), selection has nothing to choose and the function fails. Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 11 :39 closes the triangulation: capability is agent-and-habitat.

World corroboration: Yachi and Loreau 1999 in *PNAS* state that "biodiversity insures ecosystems against declines in their functioning because many species provide greater guarantees that some will maintain functioning even if others fail", and identify a "buffering effect, i.e., a reduction in the temporal variance of productivity". Oliver et al. 2015 in *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* conclude that "biodiversity, encompassing variation from within species to across landscapes, may be crucial for the longer-term resilience of ecosystem functions and the services that they underpin." McCann 2000 in *Nature* says "Both theory and empirical evidence agree that we should expect declines in diversity to accelerate the simplification of ecological communities." A simplified community is a chain of mirrors.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig 11, with three independent ecology sources at low overlap to the framework.**

### F5: The active parameter is independence, not head count

Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 8 :49: "completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :31-32: "two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence" / "is what makes agreement mean something." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not."

Ten species with the same drought response are one frame wearing ten names. The insurance effect runs on asynchronicity, which Yachi and Loreau 1999 name explicitly: "The strength of these insurance effects is determined by three factors: (i) the way ecosystem productivity is determined by individual species responses to environmental fluctuations, (ii) the degree of asynchronicity of these responses, and (iii) the detailed form of these responses." Hong and Page 2004 in *PNAS*, a genuinely low-overlap mathematical witness from a different field, find that "their relatively greater ability is more than offset by their lack of problem-solving diversity": selecting the best individual performers can produce a worse team because they have converged. Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." The frame is response diversity, not richness.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 8 + Fig 2 + a theoretical ecology source + a mathematical social-science source at very low overlap).**

### F6: Carbon storage is a composed stock; no figure returns its sign or magnitude

Fig 13 :14 names energy as a state, "conserved, finite, directionless alone." A carbon inventory has that shape: a finite conserved stock whose direction comes from a gradient, not from the stock itself. Fig 13 :22: gradient is "a difference that can be spent." Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A carbon number and a diversity number are in different currencies; adding them into one index is the error Fig 12 names.

What the figures do give is a shape. Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 13 :123: "Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms". Multiple species can compose the same carbon-accumulating function, so losing one route need not lose the substance until the routes all fail. Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A persistent canopy or a soil mycorrhizal network is a part that survives its own use, and spent and regenerating parts behave differently.

What the figures do not give is a sign or a percentage. That is a declared `gap`, not silence: Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

World sources conflict here, and the conflict was returned to the census, not used to flip the verdict:

- Chen et al. 2023 in *Nature*: "greater tree diversity is associated with higher soil carbon and nitrogen accumulation, validating inferences from biodiversity-manipulation experiments." Measured stock: organic and mineral **soil** carbon and nitrogen, decadal accrual, Canadian National Forest Inventory.
- Sullivan et al. 2017 in *Scientific Reports*: "Diversity-carbon relationships among all plots at 1 ha scale across the tropics are absent, and within continents are either weak (Asia) or absent (Amazonia, Africa)." Measured stock: **aboveground biomass** carbon, 360 plots, pan-tropical.

Re-census: different currencies (soil accrual versus aboveground stock), different scales (decadal change versus 1 ha snapshot), different domains. No contradiction at the figure level; the union reading is strengthened. Moffett et al. 2026 refine it further: oceanic "blue carbon" sequestration is "exceptionally sensitive" while other services are not. The verdict is per stock and per scale, never averaged.

**Verdict: `specified` on shape, triangulated (Fig 13 + Fig 12 + three ecology sources); `gap` on a universal sign or magnitude.**

### F7: Recovery is habitat substitution with two silent failure modes

Fig 13 :12 defines time as "the ordered succession of differences." Recovery is an ordered trajectory, not a return to an unchanged state. Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce," / "and what you brought with you". Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

Fig 7 :36 supplies the selection rule: "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." A recovered ecosystem is not the old one replayed; it is a different room selected against the residual the disturbance left. Moffett et al. 2026 give a sharp case: hazard regulation depends on "a few functionally unique foundational species whose persistence nonetheless is interdependent on the wider community." That is Fig 12 :123, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Keystone species are a functional group reading, not a richness reading. Lose the group and recovery does not follow from the number of remaining species.

Carbon and recovery can diverge: a fast-growing monoculture can accumulate carbon and have almost no capacity to select a new room after a novel pest. The verdicts are kept separate, never averaged.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 7 + Fig 12 + the 2026 synthesis).**

### F8: The channel to the ground decays, not the ground

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients," / "saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

A richness index or a satellite greenness index is an instrument, not an anchor. It can saturate when species are added redundantly, and it can pool readings from different functional groups. The anchor for resilience is a measured function through a perturbation; for carbon it is an inventory with declared pools and dates; for recovery it is a measured trajectory against a declared reference. If the metric updates on the management decision it is meant to grade (for example, a target defined as "percent of native species planted"), it has become another frame and Fig 2 :34 predicts coherent drift: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 + Fig 2).**

### F9: "Affect" is symmetric; the causal direction is ordered

Operators table :15 tests a symmetric bond by "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Operators table :33 defines the ordered bond by "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 makes shared-pool agreement disqualifying for a verdict.

Observed correlations between diversity and ecosystem function are symmetric readings. The claim "loss of diversity causes loss of resilience" is ordered. A correlation cannot choose the arrow unless the frame imports a disturbance or an intervention from a closure that does not share the measurement pool. That import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13), and Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".

**Verdict: `specified`, single-source on the figure side for the bond distinction, triangulated with Fig 2; the ground test is owed.**

### F10: Prestige and size of evidence pool are not witnesses

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A large meta-analysis is budget and a source of hypotheses; it is not a second witness if it draws on the same plot network and the same response variables. The ledger records overlap so a pool is not mistaken for corroboration.

**Verdict: `general` methodological, triangulated (Fig 7 + Fig 2 + the essay).**

### F11: No figure names a species or a percentage

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The canonical figures README states the limit as "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The output is the separator: which conditions must hold for each outcome, and which instrument would settle each branch. A universal "X percent loss of diversity causes Y percent loss of function" is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not an unmeasured number we could guess.

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 9 + Fig 13 + canonical README).**

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat discipline, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Room test.** Holding response diversity and independence constant, a loss of richness changes a function with no change in the availability of any room (breaks Fig 7 :33-36 and Fig 8 :48).
2. **Union test.** A single lever is demonstrated to clear all three outcomes' intersections, showing the three outcomes form a partition rather than a union (breaks Fig 9 :55-56).
3. **Composition test.** A carbon stock is shown to compose within one kind, with no cross-kind structure between the states it relates (breaks Fig 13 :74).
4. **Habitat test.** A restored or substituted system is shown to be a genuine removal of habitat rather than a substitution, with no unchosen actors inherited (breaks Fig 11 :66-67).
5. **Anchor test.** The same frame that grades a diversity intervention is shown to be able to correct the anchor it is ranged against (breaks Fig 2 :33-35).
6. **Currency test.** A single number is shown to price both the description of a community and the actions needed to settle its function, so that the two currencies of Fig 12 :125 do add (breaks the two-currency reading).

If any fires, the corresponding domain verdict is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Search / inward / outward, kept distinct

- **SEARCHED:** is the needed discriminator already in the ecological literature and unfound by us? Yes, partly. Response diversity, asynchronicity and functional groups are already named instruments (Mori et al. 2013; Yachi and Loreau 1999). More search inside this closure would refine which instrument to use; it will not produce a universal percentage.
- **INWARD:** extend the instrument from one already returning something. Portfolio variance of a measured flux through a perturbation is an extension of an existing measurement, not a new sensor invented from a description (Fig 6 :32).
- **OUTWARD:** import a frame whose closure differs and predicts the shape. Hong and Page 2004 (mathematical group composition) and Ashby's requisite variety (cybernetics) are two such low-overlap frames. Both need a source gate and a shadow test, and both are debited against independence. They corroborate shape, not the ecological fact.

Outward and inward were not read against each other (Fig 6 :31).
