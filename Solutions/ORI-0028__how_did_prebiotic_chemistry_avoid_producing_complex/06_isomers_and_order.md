# Isomers, order, and the shape of the mixture

**Mode: `general` on the structures, `specified` on any binding to a named molecule, `gap` on the pathway. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL (stated in effect terms before any line is chosen)

The question asks how prebiotic chemistry "avoided" complex mixtures dominated by unwanted compounds. Before any chemistry is named, three things are owed:

1. What is a mixture, in the figure's own vocabulary? It is an arrangement surplus over a closed set of atoms.
2. What could narrow it? The figure names structures (isomer, catalyst, cycle, settlement) that are already available, without importing a seventh term.
3. What is the order of those structures? Dependency runs from what is composable, to what reacts under ground contact, to what persists and can be composed over, and only then to what gets called harmful.

`R` by arity, symmetry, scale: per species, not a global mixture statistic (Fig 1 :19, :22); symmetry multiplies spellings while leaving the term count low (Fig 12 :92); first order inside the molecular closure, second order at the habitat and its cycles (Fig 4).

## Q1. The mixture is the expected surplus, not an accident

Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

Fig 13 :113 "The array is unbounded and the reach is not."

Fig 13 :117-118 "a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."

Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."

A feedstock of small molecules fixes a closed set of atom kinds. The bonds available over those atoms generate an unbounded space of arrangements. Chemistry explores that space. A tar is what the exploration looks like from inside the flask. The Miller-Urey record is the ground-level witness: after a week the trap solution was "a deep red and turbid" organic matter (Wikipedia, Miller-Urey experiment, ledger row 5). So the answer to "how did it avoid producing them" starts with a refusal of the premise: it did not avoid them. The mixture is the default. The narrow subset is the puzzle.

**Mode:** `general`, structural. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 13, Fig 6 and Fig 8, three low-overlap arguments (minimum set, closure operator, idempotence theorem), plus one independent world observation.

## Q2. Isomerism is a bond-level fact, not a naming convenience

Fig 12 :92 "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :97-98 "Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"

Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."

Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings but one term. Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms. Either way the ingredient list records half the substance. The bond carries the rest. The figure's own historical check is Liebig and Wöhler's silver salts (1824 to 1826) and Berzelius's naming of isomerism in 1830. The world literature supplies a prebiotic instance: Islam, Bučar and Powner (2017) report that "only the aminal of glyceraldehyde separates from the equilibrating mixture," beating the thermodynamically favoured isomerisation into dihydroxyacetone (ledger row 1). That is Fig 12 :117 at the bench: same atoms, different arrangement, different fate.

**Mode:** `general` for the figure rule, `specified` for the glyceraldehyde binding. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 definition, Fig 13 historical restatement, independent chemistry paper).

## Q3. Order, symmetry, and why the mixture is larger than the vocabulary

Fig 12 :116 "Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write."

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."

Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Operators table :59 "Symmetry decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." The table gives seven binary bonds plus negation, a unary operator. Negation is not a bond: it joins nothing, and a vocabulary without it can only accumulate (Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"). This matters for the question because "unwanted" is a refusal, not an extra ingredient. The figure's grammar lets the mixture be described as an arrangement surplus without adding a atom to the world (Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world.").

The four structures the figure names are exactly the narrowing structures the chemistry needs:

1. **Isomer separation.** Same atoms, different bond, different substance. A compound that crystallises out of an equilibrating mixture is a Fig 12 :117 event.
2. **Catalyst or surviving part.** A part that survives its own use persists across cycles. Mineral surfaces, borate complexing and sequestering agents are world-side instances, not figure derivations.
3. **Cycle.** The output re-enters as input. A closing loop can amplify whatever the ground keeps. Thermochemical oscillation and wet-dry cycling are world-side instances.
4. **Functional group.** Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome." The group fixes the kind; the qualifier picks the member.

**Mode:** `general` for the four structures, `specified` for any one of them bound to a molecule. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 12, Fig 5 and the Operators table; the specific bindings are world-source only.

## Q4. Settlement, composability, and the level boundary

Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

Fig 12 :106 "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."

Fig 5 :70 "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."

Persistence alone does not make a product available to the next level. It must be settled, and settlement is not sufficient for usability. A product that merely survives hydrolysis or dilution has been kept by the ground, but whether it can be composed over is a separate structural fact. Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." The round trip from molecular persistence to habitat cycling is lossy; the gap is measured, not assumed zero (full treatment in `11_audit.md`).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 12, Fig 5 and Fig 4. **Refuter:** show a narrowing mechanism that is not one of the four structures, or show that a settled product cannot become an atom for a later composition. Either sends us back to the census.

## Q5. Read per species, not per mixture

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The phrase "a mixture dominated by unwanted compounds" invites a global statistic. The figure forbids it. A global Mass Index can rise while individual species change in opposite directions (Asche et al. 2021, ledger row 3). The live signal is the per-species residual. This is a method constraint on the whole derivation, not an extra finding about chemistry.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source (Fig 1), corroborated by one world measurement, not triangulated across two figure lines. **Refuter:** a re-census admitting a global mixture residual as the operative quantity.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim in this file is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The arrangement space of the held atoms is shown to be finite and exhaustible. That breaks Q1 and the whole surplus reading.
2. A narrowing mechanism is demonstrated that is not isomer separation, a surviving part, a closing loop, or a functional group, and that is not a ground settlement. That breaks Q3.
3. A molecule is shown to carry "harm" intrinsically, independent of a residual. That breaks the refusal in the residual section.
4. A figure line is found that names a specific selecting molecule or setting. That closes the gap and supersedes this derivation.
5. A ground measurement shows prebiotic mixtures were dominated by life-useful compounds with no differential persistence. That breaks Q1 and Q4 together.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## CONCLUSION

The mixture is not a failure of prebiotic chemistry; it is the structural default of a closed atom set with unbounded arrangements. What the figures license is a **shape**: narrowing by isomer separation, by a part that survives its own use, by a closing loop, and by settlement that makes a product composable over; all of it read per species and decided by ground contact in an unmaintained habitat. What the figures do not license is a **name**: no selecting molecule, no host setting, no claim that one of the world-side mechanisms dominated. The specific pathway stays a `gap`, reported as such. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").
