> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the reframing, `gap` on the chemical pathway):** the question asks how prebiotic chemistry "avoided" complex mixtures dominated by unwanted or harmful compounds. Three things follow from the figure set before any chemistry is named:
>
> 1. The mixture is the default, not the accident. One figure states: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." A small feedstock gives a closed set of atom kinds and an unbounded space of arrangements. Chemistry explores the arrangements; tars are what that exploration looks like from inside.
> 2. There was no planner. Another figure states: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Selectivity was differential persistence under ground contact, not forward-looking avoidance.
> 3. "Harmful" is frame-relative. "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A compound is not harmful in itself; it is harmful relative to a residual (a living system) that did not yet exist. So the question is retrospective, and the honest first move is a refusal of its framing, not a new story about a selector.
>
> The narrowing that did happen is described by structures the framework already names: isomer separation ("Same atoms, different bond, different substance"), a part that survives its own use, and a loop whose output re-enters as input. What I am **not** claiming is that the framework names the molecule or the setting that did the selecting. It returns a shape, not a name. The specific pathway is recorded as a `gap`, not filled in.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**

> 2. a demonstration that "harm" is an intrinsic molecular property, independent of any residual;
> 3. a demonstration that the arrangement space of a small feedstock is finite and exhaustible;
> 4. a measurement showing prebiotic mixtures were dominated by life-useful compounds without any selective persistence.
