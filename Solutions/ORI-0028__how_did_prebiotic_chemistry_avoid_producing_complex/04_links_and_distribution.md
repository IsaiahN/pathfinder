## Wider literature that bears, read or discovered this session

1. **The tar is real and named.** Ball and Brindley (2025) and the Miller-Urey record agree that abiotic chemistry produces dead-end tars. This is F1 at the ground.
2. **Selective purification is a laboratory fact.** Islam et al. (2017) show a compound that accumulates and purifies specific sugars from a complex mixture and separates one isomer from an equilibrating partner. This is F4's isomer structure made observable.
3. **Selection without a selector is an experimental design.** Asche et al. (2021) recursively dilute non-robust compounds and observe which species persist. This is F2 and F5.
4. **The reviewed field treats messy mixtures as the native context.** The MDPI 2026 review (discovered via search snippet, article page 403 this session, therefore not cited as a source) states in its abstract: "Rather than dismissing messy mixtures as irrelevant noise, it is more accurate to treat them as the native context in which concentration mechanisms, environmental cycling, and selective persistence could enable the accumulation and survival of RNA related molecules." This agrees with the figure verdict but is recorded as an unopened lead only.

**Overlap declaration.** The three primary research sources are independent of one another (different institutions, methods and venues: UCL chemistry, ANU/Leeds mathematical modelling, Glasgow systems chemistry). The two Wikipedia pages are reference overviews and share a pool with general knowledge, so their agreement with the figure verdict is weak corroboration. No source here shares an author with the figures, so the world support is genuinely independent of the framework, unlike the exemplar's author-written essays.
