# Outreach draft — check the separator before deciding which tool should lead

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live type question in `01` §7 (the catalyst binding) and the live strain in `11_audit.md` are stated in the note rather than hidden. The stage-1 output must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether monetary or fiscal policy should lead

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with a conclusion. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-slot claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`):** "stabilize the economy" is not one residual. It is a vector of per-slot gaps: inflation, real activity, credit conditions, financial stability, distribution and expectations. The figures require the residual to stay indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19 says "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated," and Fig 1 :22 says "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single "main tool" verdict answers the average, and the average is not a slot where a policy acts.
>
> What follows from that is a separator, not a ranking. For each slot you have to name an anchor that does not change its answer when the institution pushing on it changes its mind, then check the reach of each instrument in the current regime. Monetary reach is direct on prices and expectations and can saturate at an effective lower bound. Fiscal reach is direct on activity and distribution and is carried through a slower legislative channel. Which instrument carries a slot flips when the residual changes, because Fig 11 :51 says "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A supply-shock slot has no demand-side instrument at all, so the owed thing is an import from a frame with supply-side reach, not a harder mix of the same two tools.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that monetary policy is always the main tool, that fiscal policy should always be larger, that any particular multiplier is established by this derivation, or that a numerical split follows. The framework gives a shape and a route, not a ratio. It also does not claim that the standard "monetary versus fiscal" framing is a partition. Fig 9 :4-5 says disagreements where each rule keeps working well on a different subset "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The question is a bundled union, and the interesting cases are the intersections.
>
> **What would refute the separator (written before I ran this):**
> 1. A figure line or a ground-visible instrument that returns one standing priority order over the two tools, holding across the inflation, activity, credit, stability and distribution slots.
> 2. A measured regime change after which the valence of both tools is unchanged on every live slot.
> 3. A case where the monetary channel is unsaturated at the effective lower bound, the activity residual persists for a documented non-channel reason, and fiscal cannot reach the slot either.
> 4. A target series that stays a non-updating anchor while acting as the binding constraint through a full regime change.
> 5. A supply shock whose price and activity gaps both close under demand management alone, with no supply-side instrument imported.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers, local canonical extract: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule, local copy: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Romer and Romer 2007/2010, NBER Working Paper 13264: https://www.nber.org/papers/w13264
> - Goodhart's law, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law
> - Liquidity trap, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_trap
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a stabilization problem you know well and can check against a real outcome. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the essay cited in the packet says outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, framework second | Fig 10 :52 — "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A reader checks the separator without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 — "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter before the conclusion | Fig 5 :65 — "The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM." The ranking preference is bound and testable. |
| Mode stated on the output | `THE_FORMULA.md` — "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." Nothing here is called `grounded`. |
| The numerical split withheld | Fig 9 :32 — "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 — "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." |
| Union before ranking | Fig 9 :4-5 and :55-57 — split a bundled union rather than searching for one lever. |
| Two low-overlap world links offered, not pushed | Fig 8 :48 — independence decides whether agreement means anything. The economics and control-engineering sources are separate pools. |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask each reader to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." |

## Stage order (must not be collapsed)

1. **Stage 1:** send the per-slot separator and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure text, the canonical rule and the world links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. A method crosses the membrane; a recording of this answer does not (Fig 4 :25-26).

No reply to stage 1 is required, and no follow-up is sent to a non-responder. This is one-to-one contact only.
