# Expert verification packet — the Tether on monetary versus fiscal policy

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary economist or fiscal-policy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the separator, `gap` on any numerical split. Not `grounded`.** No multiplier, lag length or optimal mix is claimed.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Stabilize the economy" is not one residual. It is a vector of per-slot gaps, inflation, real activity, credit, financial stability, distribution and expectations, and the figures require it to stay indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22). A standing "main tool" verdict answers an average that is not a place where a policy acts. What survives is a separator: for each slot, name a non-updating anchor, check the reach of each instrument in the current regime, and compose. Monetary reach is direct on prices and expectations and can saturate at an effective lower bound; fiscal reach is direct on activity and distribution and is carried through a slower channel with its own debt cycle. Which instrument carries a slot flips when the residual changes, because no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51). A pure supply-shock slot has no demand-side instrument and needs an import with a source gate and shadow test. Monetary and fiscal are two frames whose union has a surplus only when their closures differ and a residual is described first. Rules are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. No ratio or standing priority order is returned; that is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** the residual must stay indexed per slot, and no standing ranking of instruments is figure-licensed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 — "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :51 — "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :49 — "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** do those four lines actually govern this object, or do they govern a different scale? If they govern a different scale, the re-census rule in the canonical README applies and this sub-claim must be narrowed.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument whose valence is invariant to the residual across a measured regime change, or a figure line that returns one standing priority order across all slots.

### C2 — cross-frame; checkable by a monetary economist against the effective lower bound

- **Statement:** a saturated monetary channel moves the stalled real-activity slot to another instrument or to an import; it is not a reason to refine the rate further on the same axis.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 — "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26 — "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 6 :29 — "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it." Fig 11 :40 — "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** is the mapping from the effective lower bound to a saturated actuator faithful, or is the lower bound a constraint on one nominal variable while other monetary instruments still have reach? The latter is the main live objection and is recorded in `01` F4 as a refuter, not hidden.
- **World corroboration, secondary:** Keynes (1936), quoted on the opened "Liquidity trap" page: "In this event the monetary authority would have lost effective control over the rate of interest." Åström and Rundqwist style control engineering, via the opened "Integral windup" page: "the position of a valve cannot be any more open than fully open and also cannot be closed any more than fully closed."
- **Refuter:** a measured case at the effective lower bound in which the monetary channel is unsaturated, the activity residual persists for a documented non-channel reason, and fiscal cannot reach the slot either.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a world-facing half

- **Statement:** the two cores are isomers over the shared atom set, with a functional-group fallback, and the union surplus depends on independence of evidence rather than agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 — "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123 — "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 8 :48 — "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 8 :50 — "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"
- **What to check:** is the shared atom set enumerated correctly, and do the two cores share enough atoms for the isomer reading? If the atom set is narrow, the functional-group claim is the one that survives. For the evidence half: do monetary and fiscal analyses in the case at hand draw on different evidence, or the same forecast and data vintage?
- **Refuter:** one core can be written without an atom the other requires (kills the isomer claim, leaves the functional-group claim); or both frames draw on disjoint evidence and still produce no surplus on any described slot.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that monetary policy is always the main tool, and not that fiscal policy should always be larger.
- Not a multiplier, a lag length, a debt threshold, a target rate or an optimal mix. Nothing here is settled by measurement.
- Not that the monetary-first and fiscal-first readings are a partition. They are a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55-57).
- Not a new primitive. Monetary and fiscal instruments are already held atoms; naming them is a binding, not a wall-moving import (Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom.").
- Not that credibility is settled to be a catalyst in Fig 12 :119's sense. That is a live type question: an instrument that discriminates regenerated credibility from a replayed recording is still owed.
- Not that the six-term physical substrate of Fig 13 directly governs these instruments. That first-pass mapping was retracted in `01` §7.
- Not `grounded`. Status is `candidate` until the ground pays.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From the opened *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source," and the case was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here:

1. An instrument that distinguishes a demand-contraction reading, a supply-shock reading and a transmission failure when the same aggregate outcome is observed.
2. An anchor test: a target series that remains non-updating while binding across a full regime change.
3. A channel test at the effective lower bound that separates saturation from other failure modes.
4. A cross-cycle test separating coupled from separable instrument loops.
5. A catalyst test separating regenerated credibility from a replayed success.
6. The five pre-registered refuters in `01` §5.
