# The Tether's answer on monetary versus fiscal policy

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural separator, `gap` on any numerical split or standing ranking. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which instrument should lead. The live residual is not one gap called "the economy." It is a vector of per-slot gaps: inflation, real activity, credit, financial stability, distribution and expectations. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A ranking of instruments is a ranking over a scalar that does not exist at the level where policy acts.

Fig 9 :4-5 forces the split: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The monetary-first rule and the fiscal-first rule are each well formed on a different subset. The question is a bundled union whose intersections are the interesting cases (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").

## 2. The shape

1. **Keep the residual indexed per slot.** Every slot needs a named anchor that does not change its answer when the institution pushing on it changes its mind (Fig 2 :29, :33). A measured outcome is the anchor; a committee's target is a convention.
2. **No standing ranking.** Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). A rate increase is the right actor for an inflation slot with anchored expectations and the wrong actor for a demand collapse at the effective lower bound. Fig 10 :72 adds: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."
3. **Reach is a property of instrument and habitat.** Fig 11 :39-40: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" and "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Monetary reach is direct on prices and expectations; fiscal reach is direct on activity and distribution. Neither is "main" in the abstract.
4. **At a saturated channel, move the stalled slot.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does," and :26 names "saturated metrics" among the failure modes. At an effective lower bound the rate actuator has hit the bottom of its scale. Refining it further is "refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it" (Fig 6 :29). The real-activity slot then has to be carried by fiscal policy or by an imported supply-side instrument with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).
5. **The union has a surplus only where closures differ and a residual is described.** Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." If both analyses read the same forecast and data vintage, their agreement is one witness wearing two names.
6. **Rules are filters, not witnesses.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." An inflation target or a fiscal rule coordinates expectations; it does not settle which instrument carried a slot.
7. **A supply-shock slot needs an import.** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Demand management cannot close both the price gap and the activity gap after a pure supply shock. The owed thing is a primitive from a frame with supply-side reach, not a heavier mix of the same two tools.
8. **No number is returned.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Any percentage split would be a binding from the economics frame, a candidate until the ground settles it. It is not prohibited; it is ungrounded.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that monetary policy is always the main tool, and not that fiscal policy should always be larger. Both are instances of the refused standing form.
- Not that the either/or is a partition. It is a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55-57).
- Not a multiplier, a lag length, a debt threshold or an optimal mix. No measurement was taken.
- Not that "the economy" is one slot whose residual can be averaged (Fig 1 :22).
- Not a theory of everything and not a final answer. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room."
- Not that the six-term physical substrate (Fig 13) directly governs fiscal and monetary instruments. That first-pass mapping was retracted in `01` §7. The composition analysis was re-censused to Fig 4, Fig 8 and Fig 12.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an outcome.

## 5. What would settle it, and the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): "instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy" and the case was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Concretely:

- **Per-slot discriminator:** an instrument that distinguishes a demand-contraction reading from a supply-shock reading and from a transmission failure when the same aggregate outcome is observed. Until then, the source is named from a projection, which is the metamerism error of `05` §4.
- **Anchor test:** a target series that remains non-updating while used as the binding constraint across a full regime change, shown with an instrument that discriminates source from reading (Fig 2 :33-34).
- **Channel test:** a measured case at the effective lower bound in which the monetary channel is demonstrably unsaturated and the activity residual still fails to close for a documented non-channel reason.
- **Cross-cycle test:** a regime in which the fiscal loop's sign is independent of the monetary loop's state, which would break the coupling claim of `06` Q3.
- **Catalyst test:** an instrument that discriminates regenerated credibility from a replayed recording of one success, which would clear the live type question of `06` Q4.
- **Standing refuters from `01` §5:** derive a figure line or ground-visible instrument that returns one standing priority order across all slots; show invariant valence across a measured regime change; close both supply-shock gaps under demand management alone with no imported instrument.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The separator stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish. The canonical README says the framework returns a shape, not a name, and "records the lineage so the stop is honest"; "Indiscernibility is not identity." The missing object here is a source-discriminating per-slot instrument, and its shape is stated in §5. It is not named, because naming it would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :135). The live catalyst type question is recorded rather than smoothed over. Every correction to my first reading is visible in `01` §7. A second seat re-censused the residual and agreed, but it shares this evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between monetary and fiscal policy. It says that "stabilize the economy" is not one residual but a vector of per-slot gaps, and that a single "main tool" verdict answers an average that is not a place where policy acts. Each slot needs a non-updating anchor and a check of which instrument actually reaches it in the current regime. Since valence is relative to the residual, no standing list of good tools survives a change of regime: the rate channel can saturate at an effective lower bound, the budget channel is slower and carries its own debt cycle, and a pure supply shock needs an imported supply-side instrument rather than a louder mix of the same two. Monetary and fiscal are two frames whose union produces a surplus only when they draw on genuinely different evidence and a residual is described first. Rules are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. What the framework returns is this shape, five refuters written in advance, and the names of the instruments that would close it. The numerical split stays open until the ground settles it.
