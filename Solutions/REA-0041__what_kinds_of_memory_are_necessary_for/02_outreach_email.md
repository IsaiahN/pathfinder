**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One memory claim to check before you read the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about memory systems. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** From a figure-derived derivation, flexible intelligence needs at least two memories that are kept as *different quantities*: a **record** that only grows, and a **reach** (what can actually be composed and brought to bear now) that is derived and can fall when a held term is refuted. Counting stored terms is not a measure of reach. On top of that split, three load-bearing distinctions follow: methods that can be reapplied cross between an individual and a population, while recordings of one occasion do not; a term must be settled by the ground before it can be composed over, but settling is not sufficient for usability; and the residual that says *where* attention should go must be kept per item or slot, not averaged into one global score.
>
> **How they should be organized:** by dependency, not by importance. The "name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem" link comes last and consumes the links above it (having terms for what a situation affords, and a stated objective). Keep the lineage with each term, because the route is not recoverable from the artefact. And keep the channel to the ground reachable: stale percepts and pooled readings look like a working memory right up to the point where contact is lost.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a settled cognitive taxonomy, that episodic/semantic/procedural labels *are* the figures' distinctions, or that a synthetic memory benchmark demonstrates capability. Those labels are outside corroboration at best. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a memory whose reachable set is identical to its stored set under every refutation, where stored-term count is a sufficient measure of what it can bring to bear;
> 2. an occasion-record carried across scales that is reapplicable without loss, or a cross-scale round trip with zero gap;
> 3. a global error average that preserves the location of a live slot as well as a per-slot record;
> 4. an agent-only memory change that moves the ground, or a synthetic benchmark that establishes capability rather than wiring.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - Concrete derivation narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a memory problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — Isaiah
