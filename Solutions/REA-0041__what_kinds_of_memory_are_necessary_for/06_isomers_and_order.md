# Order and structure of the memory functions

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The five memory functions in `01` are a *union of overlapping conditions*, not a list. Two questions are owed: (1) in what **order** do they depend on one another, and (2) are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or something else? A falsifier is stated before the derivation.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held from the figure set: record (append-only Γ), reach (derived, can fall), method (reapplicable generator), instance (one-occasion record), settled status, per-slot residual, lineage. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What is the order? (Fig 3 dependency chain)

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** The five links, in dependency order:

1. **Perception** — register the residual per slot (Fig 1 :19). Averaging destroys the signal (Fig 1 :22).
2. **Vocabulary** — have terms for what things afford (Fig 3 :7). This is the first half of the middle link.
3. **Objective** — say what would count as progress in the system's own terms (Fig 3 :10). This is the second half of the middle link, and Fig 3 :25–27 says it is where chains usually break because it has no instrument.
4. **Carry** — *"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"* (Fig 3 :15). This is memory proper, and it consumes links 1–3.
5. **Maintain contact** — *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24), *"keep the anchor reachable"* (Fig 10 :29). This is the seat's office that keeps the carried terms ranged against a ground.

**Consequence:** a proposal that begins with storage architecture (link 4) before the objective link is instrumented is optimizing above the break. A reading below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22). This is why "how should memory be organized" is answered with a dependency order first and a storage format second.

### Q2 — Why a union, not a partition (Fig 9)

Fig 9 :54–55: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

A stored term can simultaneously be a record (D1), settled (F4), reachable (D2), per-slot (F5) and carried (F6). These are conditions that overlap, not kinds that exclude one another. A single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersections standing: compressing the store does not fix the reach, and verifying a term does not make it usable (Fig 5 :69). So the memory functions must be unbundled before searching for a checker; otherwise any field picked will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27–28).

**Contrast with a genuine partition:** record versus reach *is* a partition of quantities, because Fig 6 :43 says *"the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not."* They cannot be the same quantity. The union reading applies across the five functions, not within that pair.

### Q3 — Are the memory functions isomers? (Fig 12)

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- **As the functions are usually written** (record, reach, method, settled, per-slot, lineage) they do **not** share the same atom set, so by Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are members of a **functional group**: each is a `record→reach`-shaped relation with a different qualifier doing the work. Fig 12 :124 fits: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **The stronger version:** if every function is written over one shared atom set, the differences being the bond and the slot each occupies, then they **are** isomers: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* That is the claim worth testing, and it is the one an expert can refute.

### Q4 — Order across scales (Fig 4, Fig 12)

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

A method that has been settled at one scale becomes an atom at the next; an occasion-record does not cross. This is why the organization is recursive rather than flat, and why the round trip carries a loss: Fig 4 :30 — *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

### Q5 — Analogues from low-overlap fields (secondary only)

These corroborate; they do not decide.

- **AI / computer science.** The neural Turing machine has *"a neural network controller coupled to external memory resources, which it interacts with through attentional mechanisms"* (Wikipedia, Graves et al. 2014). That is a structural echo of the record/controller split, but the source also notes implementations are *"not sufficiently stable for production use,"* so it is a live engineering question, not a settled result.
- **Human cognition.** Episodic memory is *"the memory of everyday events... that can be explicitly stated or conjured"* and semantic memory stores *"the 'gist' of experience, an abstract structure that applies to a wide variety of experiential objects"* (Wikipedia). These rhyme with instance versus method, but the semantic-memory page records that the strict separate-systems reading is contested. Overlap between the two Wikipedia pages is moderate and is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- **Animal behaviour.** Scrub jays *"can recall the what, where, and when (WWW) of caching episodes and use this, alongside knowledge of perishability rates, to inform cache recovery strategies at varying intervals"* (Worsfold, Clayton and Cheke, 2025, *Learning & Behavior*). This is per-slot residual memory used flexibly, the closest non-human witness to F5.
- **Business.** Organizational memory distinguishes knowledge, which is *"interpretative and predictive,"* from documented information: *"Once knowledge is documented, it reverts to being information"* (Wikipedia). A direct echo of method versus recording.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

The isomer claim (Q3, stronger version) and the dependency order (Q1) fail together if either of the following is shown:

1. The five memory functions can be written over the full shared atom set with the **same** bond and the **same** slot, producing the same behaviour. Then they are not isomers and not even a functional group; they are one thing with five names.
2. The carry link can be improved while the objective link remains unmeasured, and the improvement is demonstrably not a re-description of the same residual. Then Fig 3's dependency order does not bind memory.

If either fires, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are withdrawn in that part. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.
