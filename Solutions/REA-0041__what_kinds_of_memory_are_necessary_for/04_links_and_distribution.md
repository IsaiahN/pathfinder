## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Outputs are not generators.** *They're Scanning Books* says a corpus contains *"the problems evolution already solved and someone wrote down. It does not contain the capacity to solve new ones, because that capacity lives in the living process, the mind generating, not in the text it generated."* This is the world-side twin of Fig 4 :25's *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* It is the strongest reason to store methods rather than occasion-records. Overlap: framework author, so it corroborates the figure verdict but is not independent of it.
2. **The two-system picture is contested.** The semantic-memory page records that Howard and Kahana rejected Tulving's separate-systems account, finding semantic cues strongest when episodic cues were strong. The figures do **not** depend on that taxonomy, so this is not a conflict with the figure verdict. It is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv` and `09_watson_answer.md` as a limit on how strongly the world sources may be read.
