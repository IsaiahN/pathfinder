# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two-question split in §5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is understood. If the recipient reads "public trust" as the anchor for the failure question, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about institutional self-correction before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "a democratic institution corrects its own failure" is not a literal description of one frame checking itself. It is a four-condition shape, and the conditions form a union, not a list:
>
> 1. an anchor the institution cannot move, which for the failure question is the outcome the institution was meant to change, not an approval rating;
> 2. a live, unsullied channel to that anchor, with residuals tracked per group rather than folded into one national average;
> 3. outside checking frames that range against the anchor separately and keep their independence, because a frame that shares the institution's evidence pool cannot correct it;
> 4. a vocabulary that can refuse a held false term and renew its reach from a frame whose closure differs, rather than only adding new programs.
>
> I am **not** claiming that public trust is irrelevant. I am claiming the question contains two loops: "did the failure get fixed?" (anchor: the outcome) and "is this answer good for these people?" (anchor: their judgment). Using one anchor for both is the failure mode, not the fix. I am also not naming a constitutional reform as the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a trust reading that settles whether the outcome was fixed while held fixed against changing opinions;
> 2. a body that corrects a held error while holding both the criterion and the interior, with no outside frame able to do the same;
> 3. a live slot residual corrected with only an aggregate instrument and no per-slot reading introduced;
> 4. a vocabulary with no refusal operator that corrects by additions alone and the residual does not recur;
> 5. reach renewed by internal composition alone, with no import and no contact with a differing closure;
> 6. one lever that restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue;
> 7. a correction that succeeds after the channel is demonstrably dead, attributed to messaging rather than a restored reading.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked derivation and the six-term substrate: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## What is deliberately not in the message

- No request to trust the framework, no request to endorse it, no "bombshell" and no "true answer."
- No personal emails, no recipient list ranked by likely agreement, no bulk send.
- No claim that more civil society or more deliberation is sufficient. Correlated frames are "one frame wearing two names" (Fig 8 :48-49), and Berman's Weimar case is recorded as a real conflict in `01` §3 F7.
