## 0. The claim under audit

The question is restated in effect terms before any figure line is chosen. An institution holds a library Γ of rules, routines and models. It bets, acts and observes: R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s (Symbols table, "R", per-slot indexing). A failure is a live slot residual: something happened there that the institution's model missed. Correction means changing the reach, by refusing a held false term or minting a new one, then settling it against the ground.

"Before public trust collapses" is a timing condition on the channel. Trust is not treated here as an independent cause. It is the reading that the frames are still ranging against an anchor that has not moved; when that reading dies, "R never arrives" (Fig 10 :23) and no correction can be routed.

Two options are bound as ordinary terms before the run, per the Formula's "Direction" section:

- **Option A:** the conditions are essentially internal (better leadership, better communication, a stronger mandate, higher trust as a goal to be managed).
- **Option B:** the conditions are structural properties of a checking arrangement, an unmovable anchor, independent frames, an open channel, and a vocabulary able to refuse.

What each leads to: A optimises a channel reading and risks turning the anchor into a frame (Fig 2 :34). B keeps the verdict separate from the budget and makes the correction checkable from outside.

**X1 is not a domain yet.** An anchor is "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." (Fig 2 :29) and "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." (Fig 2 :30). A trust rating moves when pushed by messaging, so it is a reading of the channel, not the anchor. This is a re-census finding, not a dismissal: §5 scopes it.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The four admitted conditions overlap; a case in two of them is categorically different, not twice as hard (Fig 9 :57). A single lever clears the region where one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** D3 channel open (if R never arrives there is nothing to route) then D1 perception and vocabulary then D2 triangulation against the anchor then D4 refusal and renewal. Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Trust is not the anchor for the failure question (decisive for the census)

Fig 2 :29 and :30 (quoted above) define an anchor by non-updating. Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

If "restore public trust" is made the objective of the correction loop, the objective updates on the opinions of the frames it is supposed to check. That is panel 1, the mutual-update collapse, arrived at by optimising a number. The outcome the institution failed to deliver is the anchor for the question "did it correct the failure?" That outcome does not change because citizens approve of it.

**Mode `general`** for the anchor definition, `specified` for the institutional binding. **Refuter:** show a case where a trust reading settles whether the underlying outcome was fixed, with the trust reading held fixed while frames change their minds. If that holds, trust is a legitimate anchor for the failure question and this finding is withdrawn. This refuter is narrowed in §5, not erased.

### F2 - "Self-correction" is a shape, not a literal description

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A frame that holds its own interior and its own criterion is a frame checking itself; the correction it reports is agreement with its own picture.

The honest reading: an institution cannot literally self-correct in the strong sense. It can be corrected by an arrangement in which it is one frame among others and an anchor it cannot move. Federalist No. 51 (Hamilton or Madison, 1788, Yale Avalon Project) states the same structural requirement in political language: "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition. The interest of the man must be connected with the constitutional rights of the place." and "you must first enable the government to control the governed; and in the next place oblige it to control itself."

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 :35 and Fig 10 :42 (low overlap: alignment geometry vs verifier access). **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** a documented case where a body produced a correct refusal of its own held error while holding both the criterion and the interior, and where an outside frame could not have produced the same refusal.

### F3 - A live slot disappears into the national average

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A national approval statistic can be near zero while one group's slot carries positive mass. That is a legal state under the figures (a global R near zero with one live slot is a live signal, not an inert one, Fig 1 :20). A correction policy aimed at the average spends the budget where no residual is live.

**Single-source** for the figure mechanism (Fig 1), corroborated at the institutional level by political efficacy research (Campbell, Gurin and Miller via *Political efficacy*, 2026): the sense that "individual action does have, or can have, an impact upon the political process" is measured per citizen, and low efficacy concentrates among losing parties. **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** a live slot residual corrected while only an aggregate metric existed and no per-slot instrument was introduced.

### F4 - The unmeasured middle link breaks first

Fig 3 :25-27: "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

An institution can measure its approval and its outputs while lacking a term for what the failing situation affords and what would count as progress on it. Effort aimed at communication or leadership lands above the break (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

World corroboration: Goodhart's law, "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" (Strathern 1997, via Wikipedia, 2026); Goodhart's original, "Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes." The political efficacy entry adds that "elections typically restore political trust, this restorative effect is significantly dampened for voters who supported losing parties." An election is a real ground reading, but its channel is partial by slot.

**Triangulated** across Fig 3 and the audit/metrics literature (different fields). **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** show a case where the vocabulary/objective link had no instrument and correction still occurred at that link, not merely a better output upstream of it.

### F5 - Correction requires a refusal, not only a new program

Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 12 :140: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."

Adding a program to an institution that cannot repeal a failed term increases the library without changing the reach. Nothing is deleted; what falls is the standing of the refused term to be composed over. Fig 6 amendment (line 42): "the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."

World corroboration: Acemoglu and Robinson (*Why Nations Fail*, 2012, via Wikipedia, 2026) argue that "only pluralistic political institutions can guarantee that the owners of existing monopolies, using their economic power, will not be able to block the introduction of new technologies", and that inclusive institutions let "creative destruction" proceed. An incumbent that cannot be refused blocks the correction. Warren Bass's review adds a caveat worth keeping: "the definition of extractive and inclusive institution is vague in a way that cannot be utilized in policymaking."

**Triangulated** across Fig 5 amendment, Operators table, and political economy (low overlap). **Mode `general`** for the operator, `specified` for institutional application. **Refuter:** a documented correction in which no held term lost its standing to be composed over, only new terms were added, and the residual did not recur.

### F6 - Reach renewal needs a frame whose closure differs

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: closure is idempotent (quoted above). Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

A closed institutional culture can search indefinitely and add no atom. Renewal requires contact with a different closure, with a source gate and a shadow test: the residual must be described before the frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").

World corroboration: Janis's groupthink prevention list (via Wikipedia, 2026) includes "The organization should set up several independent groups, working on the same problem", outside experts who can be questioned, and a devil's advocate. Federalist 51 calls these "auxiliary precautions." Resilience research (Gunderson and Holling via *Socio-ecological system*, 2026) says "Adaptability is the capacity of a social-ecological system to learn and adjust to both internal and external processes. Transformability is the capacity of a system to transform into a completely new system, when ecological, economic, or social structures make the current system unsustainable. Adaptability and transformability are prerequisites for resilience." The Everglades case is one where "a few feedbacks between the ecological system and the social system exist, and the SES is unable to innovate and adapt."

**Triangulated** across Fig 6, Fig 8, group psychology and resilience ecology (very low overlap). **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** renewal of reach by internal composition alone, with no import and no contact with a differing closure, confirmed by a ground reading.

### F7 - Independence decays with use and must be refreshed

Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along;" and "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 2 :40 (quoted above). The seat map's shelf life states that "Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated," because "no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."

A checking body that has been calibrated by the institution it checks is more replaceable, not less. A 2011 study of 163 countries (via *Democratic backsliding*, 2026) concluded that "established independent judiciaries are successful at preventing democracies from drifting to authoritarianism, but that states with newly formed courts 'are positively associated with regime collapses in both democracies and non-democracies.'" That is a cost-of-wrong priced rotation problem: Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**Conflict handled honestly:** Sheri Berman (1997, 2021, via the same entry) argues that "A robust civil society helped scuttle the twentieth century's most critical democratic experiment, Weimar Germany." This conflicts with a naive "more frames is better" reading. It was returned to the census: valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."), and correlated frames are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49), so a dense but highly correlated civil society lowers independence rather than raising it. The conflict did not flip the verdict; it refined the condition from "more frames" to "more independent frames." Similarly, Park (1990) and Kramer (1998) contest Janis's cohesion claims, so groupthink is used only for the structural prevention list, not as a firm causal witness.

**Triangulated** across Fig 7, Fig 2, seat-map shelf life and comparative politics. **Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** a chain of same-closure rooms whose later rooms added ground-verified evidence rather than agreement, with independence measured as non-declining.

### F8 - The condition set is a union, so one lever leaves a residue

Fig 9 :54-55 (quoted in the census), Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A reform that fixes the open channel does not fix a vocabulary with no refusal term; the residue becomes a specific compound. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Approval ratings and democracy indices are filters for where to look; they are not the verdict on whether the failure was corrected.

**Mode `general`** for the union rule, `specified` for this question. **Refuter:** a single intervention that restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue left behind.

### F9 - The speed condition is a budget condition, not a motivational one

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 9 :45: "Describing the residual is the expensive part. Leaving, looking up and recognising are one step each, and cheap only in that order."

Correction arrives before collapse when the residual is cheap enough to describe and the correction affordable within the channel's remaining life. When it does not, the failure is silent: Fig 10 :25-28 names "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" and "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." Democratic backsliding is described as occurring "slowly, in barely visible steps" (Levitsky and Ziblatt, via *Democratic backsliding*, 2026) and "incremental rather than revolutionary"; the slowness is exactly what keeps the internal instruments green.

**Mode `specified`.** **Refuter:** a correction that succeeded after the channel was demonstrably dead (saturated metric, pooled reading, lost contact) with restoration attributed to messaging rather than to a restored reading.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A trust reading settles whether the underlying outcome was fixed while held fixed against changing frame opinions (kills F1 and the channel/anchor split).
2. A body corrects a held error while holding both the criterion and the interior, with no outside frame able to produce the same refusal (kills F2).
3. A live slot residual is corrected with only an aggregate instrument and no per-slot reading introduced (kills F3).
4. A vocabulary with no refusal operator corrects a failure by additions alone, and the residual does not recur (kills F5).
5. Reach is renewed by internal composition alone, with no import and no contact with a differing closure, confirmed against the ground (kills F6).
6. A single lever restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue (kills F8).
7. Correction succeeds after the channel is demonstrably dead, with restoration attributed to messaging rather than a restored reading (kills F9).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

### Strain and re-census: Fig 2 :36 versus Fig 2 :33

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." The essay's fuller wording is "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." This strains F1 as first written, which said simply "trust is never the anchor."

**Resolution by re-census, not by softening.** The question contains two questions joined into a union compound:

- Q-failure: "did the institution correct the failure?" Its anchor is the outcome. Trust is an instrument, and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37).
- Q-trust: "is this answer good for these people, and do they still regard the institution as answerable to them?" Here the question is constitutively about people, so public judgment is a legitimate anchor under Fig 2 :36.

Treating Q-trust's anchor as Q-failure's anchor is the mutual-update collapse; treating public judgment as irrelevant to Q-trust is to answer a question about people with an instrument that cannot measure it. Both questions need their own loop, and they meet as a compound. This is Fig 9 :54 applied to the question itself. The strain is resolved by scoping, and the scoping is itself a finding, not an escape.

### Other strains

- **"Buy independence" vs "independence cannot be manufactured."** Fig 2 :42 says buy it in proportion to the cost of being wrong; the seat map says a closure reached from inside is the closure you already had. Not strained: buying means rotating or replacing seats and funding separate experience, not calibrating the same seat into independence.
- **Berman's civil society finding vs "more frames."** Returned to census; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51) and correlated frames are one frame (Fig 8 :48-49). Resolved by changing "more frames" to "more independent frames."
- **Janis contested by Park and Kramer.** Recorded as a live weakness of one world source; the figure verdict does not rest on it.
- **National approval as a "filter."** Fig 9 :60 forbids letting a filter hand a verdict. Approval is admissible evidence about Q-trust only, never about Q-failure.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Split the question into Q-failure and Q-trust before searching either (Fig 9 :54). Do not let one anchor serve both.
2. Lead with the shape: an unmovable anchor, a live per-slot channel, outside frames with bought independence, and a vocabulary that can refuse.
3. State mode on every sentence. No sentence here is `grounded`.
4. Treat approval and democracy indices as filters for the budget, never as witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
5. Ask a checker what they would count as a failure before offering any framework material (Fig 10 :42).
6. Record which reform is a provenanced binding, and never present it as what the figures returned.

**Strains after re-census: one scoped tension (Fig 2 :36 vs :33), resolved by splitting the question; no hidden strains.** The result is internally consistent, which is not the same as true.
