# WATSON answer: conditions for democratic self-correction before trust collapse

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No institution was measured.

## QUESTION

Under what conditions can democratic institutions correct their own failures before public trust collapses? Restated as an effect: when does a live slot residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| get corrected (a term refused, the reach changed) before the channel to the ground fails? The question is a union of two loops: Q-failure, with the outcome as anchor, and Q-trust, with the people's judgment as a legitimate anchor because the question is constitutively about them (Fig 2 :36). Option A treats trust as the objective to manage; it turns the anchor into a frame and produces coherent drift. Option B keeps the two anchors separate and states four structural conditions with refuters.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: multi-slot, indexed per issue and per group, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: correction is a threshold comparison plus a refusal, not symmetric agreement; "corrected" is relative to the ground. Scale: citizen, institution, population of checking seats, with a lossy membrane between scales (Fig 4).
- **D3 Ground and channel.** Actors: outcomes, metrics, election counts, audits, published records. Currency: contact, fresh or stale. Anchor: the ground, which does not update. Status: admitted, and first in dependency order because if R never arrives there is nothing to route.
- **D1 Institution loop.** Actors: officials, Γ of rules and routines, observation o'. Currency: residual reduction per slot, mint or refuse. Anchor: the outcomes the institution is meant to change. Status: admitted.
- **D2 Outside frames.** Actors: citizens, press, courts, opposition, audit offices. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Status: admitted.
- **D4 Refusal and reach.** Actors: held terms, negation, imports from differing closures. Currency: reach, not library size. Anchor: settlement against the ground. Status: admitted.
- **X1 Public trust as a free-floating cause.** Actors: "the public mood." Currency: approval. Anchor: none nameable, since it moves when pushed. Status: **not a domain yet**; it is a channel reading, scoped in the re-census.
- **X2 National consensus as sole cause.** Absorbed into D2 as a convention; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.

Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: D3 channel, D1 perception and vocabulary, D2 triangulation, D4 refusal and renewal. Strain test: Fig 2 :36 versus Fig 2 :33 was resolved by splitting Q-failure from Q-trust, a re-census recorded in `01` §5.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates or conflicts)

World sources corroborate only. Their agreement is not a second witness where they share an evidence pool.

- **Constitutional design.** Hamilton or Madison, Federalist No. 51, Yale Law Library Avalon Project, 1788, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition." Prediction: independent seats with opposing interests check one another. **Corroborates** D2. Overlap with the other political sources: moderate, but different era and method.
- **Metascience and audit.** Goodhart's law, Wikipedia, 2026, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" (Strathern 1997); also Campbell's law, "The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures". Prediction: a channel metric saturates when used as a target. **Corroborates** D3 and Fig 9 :60.
- **Comparative politics.** Democratic backsliding, Wikipedia, 2026, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "slowly, in barely visible steps" (Levitsky and Ziblatt); the 163-country judicial independence study found established independent judiciaries prevent drift while newly formed courts are "positively associated with regime collapses in both democracies and non-democracies." Prediction: silent incremental erosion and the need for established independence. **Corroborates** D2 and D3. Berman's Weimar finding **conflicts** with a naive more-frames claim; returned to census and resolved as "more independent frames" (Fig 11 :51, Fig 8 :48-49).
- **Political economy.** Daron Acemoglu and James Robinson, *Why Nations Fail*, Crown, 2012, via Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "only pluralistic political institutions can guarantee that the owners of existing monopolies, using their economic power, will not be able to block the introduction of new technologies". Prediction: incumbents block renewal unless they can be refused. **Corroborates** D4. Bass's review, "the definition of extractive and inclusive institution is vague in a way that cannot be utilized in policymaking", is recorded as a limit on the binding.
- **Cognitive psychology.** Ziva Kunda and successors, motivated reasoning, via Wikipedia, 2026, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "Political reasoning involves the goal of identity protection or maintaining status within an affinity group united by shared values." Prediction: shared identity acts as shared evidence pool, producing the common-cause collapse. **Corroborates** D2. Pennycook and Rand (2019, "Lazy, not biased") is a competing account, noted not hidden.
- **Organizational psychology.** Irving Janis and successors, groupthink, via Wikipedia, 2026, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "concurrence-seeking becomes so dominant in a cohesive ingroup that it tends to override realistic appraisal of alternative courses of action"; prevention includes "several independent groups, working on the same problem". Prediction: independent groups and a devil's advocate supply D4. **Corroborates** D4 structurally. Park (1990) and Kramer (1998) contest the causal claims, so only the structural prevention list is used.
- **Mathematical logic.** Kurt Godel, incompleteness theorems, via Wikipedia, 2026, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "no such system can demonstrate its own consistency" and "a consistency proof of F in F would give us no clue as to whether F is consistent". Prediction: no frame certifies its own correction from inside. **Corroborates** D2 from a genuinely low-overlap field.
- **Resilience ecology.** Gunderson and Holling, adaptive cycle and panarchy, via Wikipedia, 2026, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "Adaptability and transformability are prerequisites for resilience." Prediction: renewal requires transformability, not just within-phase improvement; the Everglades case has "a few feedbacks between the ecological system and the social system" and "is unable to innovate and adapt". **Corroborates** D4 at the structure level, different anchor.
- **Public opinion.** Campbell, Gurin and Miller, political efficacy, via Wikipedia, 2026, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "citizens' trust in their ability to change the government"; "elections typically restore political trust, this restorative effect is significantly dampened for voters who supported losing parties". Prediction: channel restoration is partial by slot. **Corroborates** D1 and D3.
- **Framework essays (canonical only, not witnesses).** Isaiah N, five essays listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`, opened 2026-11-23. Key quote from *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." Overlap with the figures is maximal (same author), so they carry no independent weight. They corroborate the simulated status and the shape-not-a-name limit.

No world source overrules a figure verdict. The one genuine conflict (Berman) was returned to the census and changed the condition from "more frames" to "more independent frames." The political sources share a broad field, so the three political entries count as one witness, not three.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any specific institutional binding remains a `gap`. A democratic institution cannot literally correct itself; it can be corrected inside an arrangement with an unmovable outcome anchor, a live per-slot channel, outside frames with bought and refreshed independence, and a vocabulary able to refuse and renew its reach from a differing closure. The four conditions are a union with a dependency order, never averaged. Approval and democracy indices are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. Q-trust has its own legitimate anchor but cannot lend it to Q-failure. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A body failing D4 (no refusal) would be given more transparency or more participation, and a body failing D3 (dead channel) would be told to import more checks. Each mistake spends the independence of the people asked to verify, which Fig 2 :42 prices. The cost is bounded because the verdicts are kept per domain and the refuters are written in advance.
- **Undoable:** yes. No held term is deleted when a refutation lands; what falls is its standing to be composed over, so the reach falls because something was added. The shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the seven pre-registered falsifiers in `01` §4; a source-discriminating instrument that shows a trust reading settling the failure question; evidence that a channel metric still discriminates after being used as a target while the failure recurs; a documented correction by additions alone with no recurrence; or a re-census that admits one lever restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue. Until one fires, the verdict stays `candidate`.
