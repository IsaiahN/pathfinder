# Expert verification packet: the Tether on democratic self-correction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a political scientist or institutional scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question splits into two loops that must not share an anchor: Q-failure ("did the institution correct the outcome it failed to deliver?", anchor: the outcome) and Q-trust ("is this answer good for these people?", anchor: their judgment). Correction before trust collapse requires four conditions in a union, not a checklist: an anchor the institution cannot move; a live, per-slot channel (trust is a reading of that channel, not the ground); outside checking frames with independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong; and a vocabulary able to refuse a held false term and renew its reach from a differing closure. The conditions are ordered by dependency: channel, then per-slot vocabulary, then outside frames, then refusal and renewal. The framework names no specific reform; Federalist-style checks, a devil's advocate and judicial independence are provenanced bindings from other fields, not figure derivations.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Q-failure/Q-trust split and the channel (checkable by anyone who works with trust data)
- **Statement:** an approval reading cannot settle whether the outcome was fixed, and a channel that saturates or pools readings fails silently while internal instruments stay green.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" and Fig 10 :26 "saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did a trust or approval number change while the underlying outcome did not, or stay high while it did? Is the metric still discriminating after being used as a target (Goodhart's law)?
- **Refuter:** a trust reading that settles whether the outcome was fixed while held fixed against changing frame opinions, or a correction that succeeds after the channel is demonstrably dead with restoration attributed only to messaging.

### C2 - No literal self-correction; outside frames with bought independence (checkable against institutional design)
- **Statement:** a body holding both the criterion and its own interior cannot correct itself; correction requires frames that range against the anchor separately, do not share the body's evidence pool, and are refreshed by rotation rather than calibration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **What to check:** were the checking bodies able to produce a refusal the institution could not produce, from an evidence pool it did not author? Did repeated use of the same checker reduce its independence? Note the conflicting evidence: Berman's Weimar case (1997, 2021) and Park's (1990) and Kramer's (1998) criticisms of groupthink. More frames is not the claim; more independent frames is.
- **Refuter:** a body that corrects a held error while holding both criterion and interior, with no outside frame able to produce the same refusal, or a chain of same-closure rooms whose later rooms added ground-verified evidence rather than agreement with independence measured as non-declining.

### C3 - Refusal and union structure (framework-internal, checkable by reading the figures)
- **Statement:** a vocabulary that can only accumulate cannot carry a correction; the conditions form a union with a dependency order, so a single lever leaves a categorically different residue.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **What to check:** in the case you know, did any held term lose its standing to be composed over, or did correction consist only of additions? After the reform that worked, was the remaining problem a different kind of thing (requiring a different loop) or the same problem less intense?
- **Refuter:** a vocabulary with no refusal operator that corrects by additions alone with no recurrence, or one intervention that restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a democracy can literally check itself.
- Not that public trust is the anchor for Q-failure. It is a legitimate anchor only for Q-trust.
- Not that more civil society, more deliberation or more transparency suffices. Correlated frames are "one frame wearing two names" (Fig 8 :48-49).
- Not a named reform, agency, law or electoral system. That is a `gap` after re-census (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.
- Not a claim that this simulated derivation is more than an internal-consistency check.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Per-slot outcome readings that survive averaging and show whether the live residual moved.
2. A channel metric that still discriminates after being used as a target.
3. A test of whether the checker can refuse what the institution cannot, from an unauthored evidence pool.
4. A test of whether a held term lost standing to be composed over, not merely a new program appearing.
5. The seven falsifiers in `01` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :35, :42; Fig 10 :24, :26, :42; Fig 9 :55, :57. Nine short passages.
2. Name one real case where a trust number and an outcome diverged. Does the two-question split describe it?
3. Identify the checking body in that case. Could it refuse what the institution could not, from evidence the institution did not author?
4. Say whether the correction was a refusal or only an addition.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
