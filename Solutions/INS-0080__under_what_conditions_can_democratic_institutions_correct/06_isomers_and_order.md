# Isomers, order and reframing: the union of correction conditions

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit returns four conditions in a union. Two supporting questions remain: (1) do the low-overlap analogues from political theory, organization psychology, resilience ecology and law name the *same* structure or merely similar ones, and (2) in what order must the conditions be satisfied, so that effort is not spent above a break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: ground, channel, per-slot residual, outside frame, refusal, import, anchor. Bonds held: sequence (→), comparison (⋛), production (⇒), conjunction (+), and negation (¬, not a bond). No new primitive is available without a provenanced import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - The four conditions are a union, not a checklist

- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- So a case with a dead channel and a refusal-less vocabulary is not two instances of one failure; it is a compound with its own loop. A lever that restores the channel leaves the refusal condition binding.
- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one intervention restores all four conditions and no categorically different residue remains, the union reading is refuted.

### Q2 - Dependency order (Fig 3)

- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Order: D3 channel (if "R never arrives," Fig 10 :23, there is nothing to route) → D1 perception and vocabulary (per-slot residuals, a term for what the situation affords) → D2 outside frames ranging against the anchor → D4 refusal and reach renewal.
- A communication campaign lands at D3 or above; it cannot rescue a broken D1 vocabulary link. A new oversight body lands at D2 and cannot rescue a dead channel.

**Falsifier:** a documented correction achieved in reverse order, with D4 refusal first, D1 vocabulary still uninstrumented, and the residual not recurring.

### Q3 - Are the analogues isomers or only functional analogues?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Candidates:

| analogue | atoms held | bond | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Federalist 51 "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition" | separate seats, opposing interests, no shared appointment | → sequence plus ⋛ comparison | **isomer candidate**: same atoms (independent frames, anchor) arranged as mutual checking |
| Janis devil's advocate / "several independent groups, working on the same problem" | an inside frame and an outside frame that differs | → sequence (the advocate speaks before the decision) | functional group, not full isomer: the advocate is often authored by the group, so independence is weaker |
| Resilience "Adaptability and transformability are prerequisites for resilience" | habitat, contact, renewal | ⇒ production (adaptability produces transformability) | analogous at the structure level; the ground is an ecosystem, a different anchor, so not an isomer of the institution in the strict sense |
| Judicial independence (163-country study) | a court that cannot be moved by the executive | ⋛ comparison against evidence | **isomer candidate** at D2, with the same atom arrangement as Federalist checks but different currency |

- Strict isomer claim: two institutions are isomers if they hold the same atoms (ground, channel, per-slot residual, outside frame, refusal, import) and differ only in which bond mediates which pair. This is checkable but unmeasured. Mode `general` for the definition, `specified` for the candidate list.
- Weaker claim: all four analogues share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the `separate frames + unmovable reference` substructure, with different qualifiers. This is what the evidence actually supports.
- Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." So the devil's advocate that helps one residual can harm another; the list cannot be fixed once and for all.

**Falsifier:** show two analogues with identical atom sets and identical bonds that nevertheless behave differently against the ground. That would break the isomer definition's applicability here.

### Q4 - Reframing: a convention that decides an outcome

- Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- Seat Map :163 "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."
- A norm of mutual toleration is a convention when it only sets the meaning of disagreement. It becomes a verdict the moment it fixes who wins a specific contested outcome. The 2019 V-Dem finding that informing partisans that opponents are more committed to democratic norms than they thought increased their own commitment is evidence about Q-trust (shared meaning), not evidence about Q-failure (who was right).
- Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Polling and expert consensus are filters, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).

**Falsifier:** a convention that determines a contested outcome while remaining checkable against an anchor neither party can move. If such a case exists, the convention/verdict distinction needs re-census.

### Q5 - Round trip and the cost of the shape

- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Fig 9 :45 "Describing the residual is the expensive part. Leaving, looking up and recognising are one step each, and cheap only in that order."
- The four-condition shape is cheap to state (low bits) and expensive to test (high actions: each requires a ground reading). Committing the refuters before the run is what makes the price visible.
- Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The condensation from the figures to "four conditions" loses the union structure and the Q-failure/Q-trust split; `11_audit.md` enumerates R_T.

**Falsifier:** a ground reading in which all four conditions measure high and non-declining while the failure recurs. That would drop the shape rather than refine it.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions are a union with a fixed dependency order, not a menu. The cross-field analogues support a functional-group claim and two tentative isomer claims, both `specified` and unmeasured. The convention/verdict distinction and the two-currency price are the parts most easily lost in condensation, so both are carried explicitly into `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.
