# Structure, order and analogues: claims and isomers over financial primitives

**Mode: `general` on the figure definitions, `specified` on the finance mapping.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes that credit is a minted arrangement over a closed generator, that loss cascades, and that distribution is per slot. Two questions remain owed: (1) are two financial arrangements over the same underlying claims **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what **order** do the three domains stand in, and which bond does each step use?

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Which bond does lending use: sequence or production?

- Operators table :37 "A produces B; B did not exist before" (production, ⇒, ordered). Operators table :33 "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." (sequence, →, ordered).
- The Bank of England source reports that the deposit appears when the loan is made; before the loan, that deposit did not exist. So the transition is production, not mere sequence: `loan ⇒ deposit`, not `loan → deposit`.
- Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." A production bond is what lets a new operand enter rather than revealing one that was waiting.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if deposits must exist before any loan can be made, the production reading fails and the sequence reading stands. This is an empirical question about the monetary system, not a matter of notation.
- Triangulated: Operators table (bond definition, framework-internal) plus the Bank of England record (institutional fact). Low overlap between the two pools.

### Q2. Are a held mortgage pool and a tranched version of the same pool isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :118 to :119 record the chemical precedent: "A + B + C", "B + A + C", "C + B + A", "A + C + B", "one substance", "this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term". The point is that arrangement, not ingredient list, decides substance.
- A pool of mortgages held to maturity and the same pool sliced into senior, mezzanine and junior tranches share every underlying atom (the same promises, the same collateral). They differ in the bond: a single holder versus a priority ordering (⋛ comparison and − subtraction of loss from the bottom up). Under default, their behaviour differs decisively. That is an isomer claim in the figure's own sense, not a metaphor.
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Risk, credit and power share a functional group: each is a per-slot allocation over a created claim. The qualifier (which quantity) says which member.
- **Falsifier:** if tranching an identical pool changed no allocation of loss and no behaviour under default, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.
- Mode `general` for the figure definition, `specified` for the pool binding. Triangulated across Fig 12 (bond) and Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." (recursive generator), low overlap between the two figures' roles.

### Q3. What order do the three domains stand in?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- D1 (credit creation) produces the operand for D2 (risk placement); D2 produces the operand for D3 (standing to set terms). This is a dependency order, not a ranking of importance. A reform aimed at D3 while D1 is broken spends effort above the break.
- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The least instrumented link is the middle one: the bond between a created claim and the social slot it reaches. HMDA and CRA data begin to instrument it, but only for mortgages and only in the United States.
- **Falsifier:** if power could accumulate with no credit claim and no risk placement to compose over, the dependency order fails and the domains are a partition rather than an ordered union.

### Q4. What does the cascade compose over?

- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"
- A loan settles (is repaid or defaults) and becomes an atom: a record in a pool. The pool settles and becomes an atom: a tranche. The tranche settles and becomes an atom: a pension entitlement. Each level is irreducible at its own level and composable at the next. This is why a first-order enumeration of counterparties misses the loss that lands three steps out (Fig 11 :52).
- Fig 13 :118 "still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach." More levels of securitisation do not add a primitive. They add arrangements over the same settled claims.
- **Falsifier:** if a settled claim could not serve as an operand for a further claim, the cascade composition fails and the third-order loss finding in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F4 is withdrawn.

### Q5. Analogues, and their limits

- **Ecology (Fig 11 :52):** DDT reaching eagles is the figure's own analogue for first-order scoping failure. It corroborates the mechanism shape, not the financial facts. It is in the figure, so it is not an outside witness.
- **Statistics (Simpson's paradox):** "a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined." This is a low-overlap analogue for Fig 1 :19-22. It is a different field from both the framework and finance, so it is a genuine outside witness for the shape, but it says nothing about which financial slots matter.
- **Regulation (Goodhart's law):** "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." A low-overlap analogue for Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." It shares an economics pool with the rating-agency source, so the two together are one witness, not two.
- **Limit of analogy:** Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" and :31 "rather than the cause". Each analogue is used for the shape it predicts, never as a name for the financial cause.

## CONCLUSION

Lending is a production bond (`loan ⇒ deposit`) on the institutional record, with a stated empirical falsifier. A held pool and a tranched pool are isomers under Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different bond, decisive difference in behaviour under default, with a falsifier. The three domains stand in a dependency order D1 to D2 to D3, not a partition, and the middle link is the least instrumented. Settled claims become operands at the next level, which is why loss reaches third-order slots without any new primitive. Analogues from ecology, statistics and regulation corroborate the mechanism shape but never name the financial cause.
