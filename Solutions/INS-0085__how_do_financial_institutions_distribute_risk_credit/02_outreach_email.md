# Outreach draft: check one shape before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three domain findings and their refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the recipient's own terms. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape about how credit, risk and power are distributed

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about financial institutions. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (general on shape, specified on the institutional mapping, not grounded):** the framework separates three questions that are usually bundled. Credit is created inside a bank's own library of claims when a loan is made, so a financial instrument is a new arrangement over old primitives, not a new primitive. Risk does not stop at the direct counterparty; it cascades, and the loss that matters can land three steps from the lending decision. A rating or score stops being a measure once it decides an outcome and the party it rates pays for it. In each case the distribution has to be read per household, firm and neighbourhood, because an aggregate average erases the live signal.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names a specific policy as the cause. It returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming the figures give the numerical distribution, and I am not claiming a normative verdict about whether that distribution is just. The data have to settle those.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. the whole distribution is captured by one aggregate statistic with no live per-slot residual;
> 2. losses stop at first-order counterparties, with no cascade into third-order slots;
> 3. a rating keeps its discriminating power after becoming a regulatory threshold and is unaffected by who pays for it;
> 4. a financial instrument needs a genuinely new primitive, not just a new arrangement;
> 5. an institution's standing to set terms grows with no change in its contact with any other closure.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. Public figure text with line numbers: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures. The money-creation result it leans on is the Bank of England Quarterly Bulletin article, "Money creation in the modern economy", 2014.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
