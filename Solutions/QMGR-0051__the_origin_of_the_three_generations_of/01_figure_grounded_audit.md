## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for the origin of two observed facts:

1. **Count.** There are three generations (families) of each fermion species, and apparently only three.
2. **Pattern.** Their masses are strongly hierarchical and their mixings differ between quarks and leptons.

The Standard Model (SM) accommodates both but derives neither. It takes the number of generations as an input and the nine charged-fermion Yukawa couplings (plus neutrino parameters) as free parameters. Chan and Tsou write: "the Standard Model as conventionally formulated offers no explanation at all either for the existence of the 3 fermion generations in the first place, nor yet for their striking mass and mixing patterns, but takes instead all these features just as fundamental inputs." Singh states the same residual in one sentence: "The origin of the three fermion generations and their highly hierarchical mass spectra remains one of the most profound puzzles in particle physics."

So, in effect terms:

> R = {measured N_gen = 3, measured mass and mixing pattern} minus {what the SM derives about either}.

**What an answer would look like (bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, per Formula step "Direction").** An answer would be a mechanism with two parts: (i) a repetition operation that fixes the number of copies, and (ii) an ordering operation that fixes their masses and mixings. It would be stated as a shape, would be indexed by generation slot, and would make a prediction that a ground (an experiment) could refuse. Binding this before the search makes it refusable by the same test as everything else.

**A caution on the word "origin."** The count is already settled at the ground. The ATLAS Collaboration reports a Z invisible width "in agreement with the most precise determination from LEP and the Standard Model prediction based on three neutrino generations." So "why three" is a demand for an explanation of a measured integer, not a request to measure it. No figure line produces an integer; the figures can only constrain the shape of the explanation and point to the ground that settles any candidate. That distinction governs the whole audit.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least two conditions are bundled in the question: (a) why three copies, (b) why the masses are hierarchical. A third, the CKM versus PMNS mixing contrast, overlaps both. So R is not one hard question. Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Symmetry.** The three generations carry identical SM gauge quantum numbers. Under a symmetric bond, such copies are the same term with several spellings: Fig 12 :92, "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term". The mass hierarchy breaks that symmetry, so the distinguishing operation is an ordering or comparison, not a new kind of thing. Mark `specified`: this is a structural reading, not a derived number.
- **Scale.** The masses are electroweak-scale quantities (Yukawa couplings to the Higgs), while candidate explanations live at a higher scale (flavour-symmetry breaking, unification, anthropic selection). A claim crossing that boundary is governed by Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A candidate high-scale explanation must be carried down as a method or head start, not as a recording of one fit.

**Partition or union.** The bundled question is a union: count, hierarchy and mixing overlap. The first move is to split it into well-formed questions (Fig 9 :4-5, :54). The relation between frame A and frame B is also a union in Fig 8's sense: two frames together may reach what neither reaches alone, but only where the closures differ and a residual has been described first. Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain runs: (1) perception: measure the masses and the count (done, ground-visible); (2) vocabulary: say what would count as an explanation, namely a repetition operation plus an ordering operation; (3) objective: a shape that fixes both; (4) planning: which frame's closure predicts that shape; (5) learn and carry: record provenance and a refuter. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance: Fig 3 :20, "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Link (2) is the one that usually breaks: Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." That, not a shortage of models, is why the puzzle persists.

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** Domain D has an anchor only because experiment exists (domain A). A "flavour democracy" frame that predicts no number and no ratio has no anchor and is not yet a domain.

### 1.3 Strain test applied before concluding

The obvious temptation is to map generations onto Fig 12's **isomer**: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." That strains, because generations share the same atoms *and* the same bonds and differ only in the numerical value of a Yukawa coupling. Under the strain rule, the line governs another quantity, so we re-census: generations belong to domain A as parameterised copies, not to Fig 12's composition domain. No figure line governs the specific content question "which symmetry fixes the integer 3". That is recorded as a `gap` at the content level in F9 and §8, not hidden by a loose analogy.

### F3 - A generation cannot be a seventh primitive (single-source, strong)

Note the direction: this constrains the *shape* of an explanation, it does not yield the integer 3. **Mode:** `specified`, structural. **Refuter:** derive a seventh primitive from the six, or show the six-term set is not closed.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the world search and the conclusion)

Per Fig 5 :65 and the Formula "Direction" discipline, and before reading the physics sources in §2 of `09_watson_answer.md`, the shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A generation is shown to be a new primitive not reducible to a repetition or an import over held terms (breaks F3 and Fig 13 :116).
2. Count and hierarchy are shown to be one condition that shrinks with single-track effort (breaks F1 and Fig 9 :4-5).
3. The count or the masses are shown to be settled by agreement among frames rather than by a measurement (breaks F5 and Fig 2 :29).
4. A candidate explanation is admitted into the answer without provenance and a shadow test (breaks F6 and Fig 6 :12-13).
5. The analysis averages across generation slots and still retains the ordering residual (breaks F2 and Fig 1 :19).
6. A figure line is produced whose verbatim text yields the integer 3 or a mass ratio (breaks F9 and dissolves the content-level gap).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or the content verdict is revised. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.
