# Copy structure, order and analogues for the generation question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question imports three intuitive ideas that need separate derivation before they are used: (1) what kind of thing a generation is, a new atom, a copy, or an arrangement; (2) what "hierarchy" means as an operation rather than a list of numbers; (3) whether any analogue from chemistry or elsewhere licenses the word "origin" here. Each is owed a figure line or an honest gap.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What kind of thing is a generation?

- Fig 13 :116: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."
- A generation cannot therefore be a seventh primitive. Two readings remain: a copy (same term, different parameter) or an import (a new frame supplies the repetition mechanism).
- Fig 12 :92: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges." Three generations under the gauge symmetry are three spellings of one gauge term. That is the copy reading.
- The mass hierarchy means the spellings are not interchangeable. Under Fig 12 :92 the spellings would be one term, so the distinguishing operation is not a symmetric bond inside the gauge frame. It is an ordering supplied from outside that frame.
- **Conclusion:** a generation is a parameterised copy, not an atom and not an isomer. The repetition operation is an import candidate. **Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the copy reading (Fig 12), triangulated with Fig 13 for the no-new-kind half.

### Q2 - Are the three generations isomers? FALSIFIER SUPPLIED

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- The three generations share the same atoms **and** the same bonds; they differ in the numerical values of Yukawa couplings. The bond is not different, so the definition fails.
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Generations are members of one kind, not a substructure that fixes an outcome. Also fails.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a candidate model writes two generations with genuinely different bond structure over the held atom set rather than different parameter values, the copy reading fails and the isomer reading must be re-tested. Until then the isomer word is refused here.
- **Conclusion:** no. This is the strain recorded as F9 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, resolved by re-census into a content-level gap.

### Q3 - What does "hierarchy" mean as an operation?

- Fig 12 :87, Operators table: comparison (⋛) is "a threshold relation between two quantities", with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"
- A mass hierarchy is a comparison over slots: m3 > m2 > m1 for each charged species. Under the Operators table this is `⋛` applied repeatedly across three slot-quantities. That is a `specified` structural reading, not a derivation of the ratios.
- Fig 13 :31: "And because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." This is the shape that Froggatt-Nielsen-like hierarchical mechanisms resemble: a small parameter amplified by repeated steps. But the figure line does **not** derive the observed ratios, and naming that mechanism is an import. Kept as a shape constraint only.
- **Falsifier:** an observation that the mass ratios are not ordered by a single small parameter across species would break the comparison-over-slots reading and leave only the bare per-slot data.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Dependency order here: measure the count and masses (perception, done) -> say what would count as an explanation (vocabulary, the live break) -> fix the shape (objective) -> choose a frame (planning) -> record provenance and refuter (carry).
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". The measured count, once settled, becomes an atom at the next level: any candidate mechanism composes over it. It is not free to redefine it.
- **Conclusion:** the order is measurement first, then shape, then frame. Not model first.

### Q5 - Are there useful analogues from other domains?

- Chemistry's isomerism is the source of Fig 12's word, but the figure itself records the caution: "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties" (Fig 12 :97). The framework borrows the term and prices it; it does not transfer chemical facts to particle physics. So chemistry is a naming analogue only, and Q2 shows it does not fit.
- *The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies a better analogue: metamerism, where one green reading can come from one light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." That is the exact situation with the three masses: several mechanisms reproduce the same reading. The analogue governs the need for a discriminator, not the content of the answer.
- **Falsifier for the analogue:** if a single mechanism made predictions that no other candidate could reproduce, the metamerism analogue would cease to govern and the discriminator would be unnecessary.

## CONCLUSION

A generation is a parameterised copy under a gauge symmetry, not a new primitive and not an isomer. The hierarchy is a comparison over slots, with a figure-shaped resemblance to amplified remainders but no figure-derived ratios. The union must be searched in dependency order: measurement, then the missing criterion of what counts as an explanation, then a frame. The one genuine analogue is metamerism from instrument theory, and it says the reading does not determine the source. No content answer about the integer 3 or the mass ratios is derived here; that remains the `gap` recorded in F9.
