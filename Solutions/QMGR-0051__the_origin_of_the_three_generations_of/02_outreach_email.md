# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before deciding about any model

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are checked against the verbatim figure text. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the three-generation question, before any model

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded reading of the question: why are there three generations of fermions, and why is their mass hierarchy what it is. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a model first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the Standard Model fits three copies and their masses but derives neither. The question bundles at least two conditions, the integer count and the mass/mixing pattern, and Fig 9's rule is to split a bundle before searching it. The count itself is already a ground reading: the Z invisible width agrees with the Standard Model prediction based on three neutrino generations. So "why three" is a request for an explanation of a measured integer, not a new measurement.
>
> **The output:** the figures cannot produce that integer or those masses. What they constrain is the shape of any explanation. Generations cannot be a seventh primitive, because the six-term set is closed and no amount of arranging produces a seventh. So an explanation must be a repetition operation over held terms plus an ordering operation, reported per generation slot, never as an average. Any candidate from outside the Standard Model is an import and carries two gates: provenance, and a gap it explains. Only a measurement settles which candidate, if any, is right.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures predict three, that any particular flavour symmetry, algebra or anthropic argument is the answer, or that this is a theory of everything. Those are bindings from physics onto the framework's vocabulary, and until provenance is shown they carry zero evidentiary weight. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A chain of agreeing model papers is motion, not progress; the ground decides.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a generation is shown to be a new primitive not reducible to repetition or import over held terms;
> 2. the count and the hierarchy are shown to be one condition that shrinks with single-track effort;
> 3. the count or the masses are shown to be settled by agreement among frames rather than by measurement;
> 4. a candidate is admitted with no provenance and no gap it explains;
> 5. averaging over generation slots leaves the ordering residual unchanged.
>
> I wrote these down before looking at candidate models. If any one holds, the shape claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** what would you count as a failure of this shape claim? And is there an instrument or criterion, rather than another model, that would discriminate between candidate explanations? That link, knowing what would count as progress, is the one I think is missing.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text and formula (local framework files, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The puzzle stated in the literature: https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0303010
> - The measured anchor (Z invisible width): https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02789
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an unrefusable output is not a result. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - with thanks
