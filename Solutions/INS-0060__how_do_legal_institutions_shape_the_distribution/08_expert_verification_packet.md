# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on legal institutions and power

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labour-law or power researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a specific jurisdiction is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Power is not a substance that legal institutions divide among four containers. It has to be indexed per party and per relation, and legal institutions move it by changing the habitat in which the parties act: which actors, conditions and relations are in contact with the residual, and which instruments carry a rule from text to outcome. Law itself is a grammar that makes a dispute statable; it does not decide who is right. A right on paper is a description priced in bits, enforcement is actions priced at the ground, and the two do not add, so calling a right with no enforcement "partial power" is a category error. State, employers, workers and citizens are the atoms; legal rules are the typed bonds, so two regimes with the same parties but different bonds differ in substance. What the framework does not supply is a named office for a given residual; it returns the shape of one and leaves the slot open.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  Description and enforcement are two currencies that do not add

- **Statement:** for a named right and a named relation, the text change (bits) and the action spent to enforce it (actions) must be reported as two readings, never summed into one power score.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125  -  *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126  -  *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :130  -  *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **What to check:** open any jurisdiction where a right exists on paper but is not realised in a named sector. Is a single "rights score" actually predicting outcomes there, or does it conceal a paid description and an unpaid action? The labour-law record lists such cases (Angola and Benin, both with statutes that are poorly enforced).
- **Refuter:** a valid common currency in which the two readings can be added and that predicts per-slot outcomes as well as the two held apart. If produced, C1 is false.
- **Status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 10, low overlap: composition currencies vs channel decay).**

### C2  -  Law authors conventions that make disputes statable; it does not author verdicts about the world

- **Statement:** a legal rule defines what counts as a case (a convention), and the ground decides what happened. A convention allowed to determine the world-facing outcome has ceased to be a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :46  -  *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."* Fig 10 :47  -  *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 10 :17  -  *"it makes the bug statable"*. Fig 12 :115  -  *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."*
- **What to check:** is the recorded judicial reason best read as a reusable typed bond for later cases (a convention) or as a claim about the judge's motive (a verdict about the world)? Legal realism says reasons may be post hoc rationalisations; that is why this packet claims only the former. Does your own field's evidence support the narrower reading?
- **Refuter:** a legal convention that settles a factual dispute by declaration, with no ground reading, and that remains a legitimate convention rather than an authored verdict. If found, C2's split fails.
- **Status: `general` for the role, `specified` for the failure, triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 12).**

### C3  -  The checker must stand outside the case it decides, and the particular office is a `gap`

- **Statement:** an outside checker ranges against a record neither party can move. The framework can name that kind of thing but cannot name the office; any named office would be an import owing a source gate and a shadow test.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42  -  *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 2 :35  -  *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 9 :32  -  *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134  -  *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.
- **What to check:** in a dispute where the state is a party or the author of the rule, does the checking institution stand in a closure that differs from both parties, and can neither party move its record? The Factories Act 1833's inspectors are the field's own early example; the ILO's weak enforcement is the counter-example. Note the qualification from experimental economics: third-party punishment occurs, but *"second-party punishments are consistently higher than third-party punishments"*, so position is not severity.
- **Refuter:** a named office whose import is shown to add no atom (so it is not really an import), plus a ground reading showing it closes the slot without standing outside the case. That would break the non-access requirement. Alternatively, show a case where an internal checker correctly settled a dispute against a movable record.
- **Status: `specified`, triangulated for the position requirement (Fig 10 and Fig 2). The particular office is reported as `gap`, unreached not silence.**

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of state, employers, workers and citizens, and no scalar "power index" (Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that law is irrelevant. It sets the grammar and the habitat; what is refused is that changing the text alone moves the ground (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that rights and enforcement are the same quantity measured twice (Fig 12 :125-130).
- Not that a specific inspectorate, ombudsperson, court or commission is the answer. That slot is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that a convention is false. It has no truth value (Fig 10 :46).
- Not a derivation of one legal family from another and no new primitive or seventh kind (Fig 13's physical substrate does not govern labour law; see `01` R2).
- Not that the recorded reason reveals a judge's motive; only that it is a bond later courts can use (`01` R3).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a jurisdiction-level measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot instrument, not a further argument:

1. **Currency-discrimination test:** for a named right and relation, measure the text change and the action spent, then test whether their sum predicts the outcome better than either reading held apart (Fig 12 :125-130).
2. **Contact test:** find a legal change that moves outcomes with no change of actor set, condition set, relation set or instrument (Fig 11 :40).
3. **Room test:** track two consecutive decisions on the same residual and measure whether the closure changed and the second matched the residual the first surfaced (Fig 7 :36).
4. **Standing falsifiers:** a lossless scalar power index, a commensurable currency for rights and enforcement, a frame expanding by internal composition alone, an unchanged-closure precedent chain with ground-verified novelty, or a residual-independent ranking of the four parties.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :125-130, Fig 10 :46-47 and :42, Fig 2 :35 and :40-41, Fig 11 :39-40 and :49-51, Fig 9 :32. These are short passages.
2. Check C1 against one jurisdiction you know: is there a right on paper whose enforcement is a separate, unpaid bill? Say what instrument would measure the bill.
3. Check C2 against the recorded reason in one leading case: is it best read as a reusable bond, and does treating it as a motive claim change anything?
4. Check C3: name the checker in a dispute where the state was a party, and say whether neither party could move its record.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
