## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table: *"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it."*). Here:

- **b** is what each of the four parties believes the legal order entitles it to and what it expects the others to do. A statutory right lives in b as a prediction, not as an outcome. The labour-law record calls employment standards *"social norms ... for the minimum socially acceptable conditions under which employees or contractors are allowed to work"*, which is a statement about the normative frame, not a measurement of workplace conditions.
- **a** is what a party does: legislate, regulate, inspect, litigate, organise, bargain, strike, vote, comply, exit.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the party's library predicts will follow, where Γ is the legal-institutional library of statutes, precedents, procedures, rights, duties, contracts and remedies.
- **o′** is what the ground actually returns: a ruling, a sanction, a wage, a working condition, an inspection finding, a collective agreement, a repeal or a new statute.
- **s** is the party slot and the pairwise relation. There are four named parties (state, employers, workers, citizens) and at least four live relations (state/employer, employer/worker, state/citizen, union/employer). R is indexed per slot and per relation.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)

The phenomenon to explain is not a level of power but **the mechanism by which a change in the legal library moves the per-slot gap**. Call the candidate quantity `ΔR_s`: the change in the gap on slot s following a change in legal institutions. Observed: a statute can close the gap sharply on one relation (for example wage floors) while leaving the same nominal right inert on another (for example the same right for a worker in the unregulated informal sector). The labour-law record states this directly: in Angola *"There are laws that regulate working conditions but they are not well enforced even in the formal sector"*, and in Benin *"Owing to a shortage of inspectors, the labor code is enforced poorly and only in the so-called formal sector."* A frame that predicts total power moves with the text of the law has a residual here.

State the residual as: *why does a change in the legal library reduce the gap between what a party is entitled to expect and what the ground returns on some slots and relations and not on others, and what would have to be present for it to reach the slots it misses?* This is an effect description. It names no theory, no "hegemony", no "class power", no "legal enforcement". Those are candidate names withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a pair of conditions whose conjunction moves `ΔR_s` on a named slot, plus an instrument that discriminates a change in the description (rights on paper) from a change in the actions (settled outcomes). Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 5 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names candidates directly: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* A court ruling, an inspection finding, a wage record and an enacted statute are counters: they do not move because a scholar or a party thinks differently about them. Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Expert opinion about "how much power" a group has moves when pushed, so it is an instrument at best. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* This question is about people and institutions, so Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment *"the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people"*, and only *"an instrument for is this code correct"*. The verdict therefore reports a shape and a checkable route, never a rank of who is stronger. Viable anchors here: realized outcomes per slot, recorded per relation, with provenance.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a four-place distribution over named parties and a separate distribution over their pairwise relations, ordered by time (a right is announced, then complied with, then enforced or not). It carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) and a **comparison** bond for "distribution" (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87; Operators table :49). It is not symmetric: the state and a worker are not interchangeable, and swapping them changes the term.
- **Symmetry.** "Distribution of power" is a threshold comparison relative to other parties, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :85-89). A gain by one party is not the subtraction of an equal quantity from another, so the tempting zero-sum arithmetic is a flat-formula error: the arrangement is not recoverable from the quantities (Fig 12 :5, :94).
- **Scale.** Four scales are in view: the individual employment relation (one worker, one employer), the firm or workplace, the sector or labour market, and the polity. The membrane between them is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The kernel that does not change with size is the loop; what changes is the actor set, the currency and the anchor (Fig 4 :19-20). The transform between scales is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The rule-making frame** (legislature, regulator, constitution) | legislators, regulators, standard setters | the description: statutes, rights, procedures, definitions (bits) | yes: the enacted text, the official record, a counter (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| **D2 The adjudicative and inspection frame** | courts, labour inspectorates, agencies, arbitrators | the actions: rulings, sanctions, remedies, inspections | yes: the decision, the sanction record, the arbitration award | admitted |

| **D4 The civic and political frame** | citizens, movements, parties, interest groups | voice, vote, mobilisation, public pressure, agenda change | yes: elections, enacted policy, recorded mobilisation | admitted |
| **D5 The cross-scale channel** (the membrane between D1 to D4) | the instruments and metrics that carry a rule from text to outcome | contact with the ground; `R_T` | yes: the round trip measured, "take it and measure the gap" (Fig 4 :30) | admitted, as the compound of D1 and D2 |

X1 fails the anchor test and is therefore excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that domination is unreal; it is a refusal to treat a name as a measurement. *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing"* (Fig 11 :40) applies to the measurable relations, not to an unmeasurable essence. X2 fails because a legislature and a court are different frames: different actors, different currency, different ground, with a lossy transform between (Fig 10 :68).

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D2 overlap: legislation supplies the grammar that courts and inspectors apply, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D2 and D3 overlap: enforcement happens inside the workplace, so that overlap is a second compound. D3 and D4 overlap: workplace outcomes feed civic mobilisation and policy change, so that overlap is a third. D5 is the compound of D1 and D2 across the membrane. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: (C1) a change in the description (the rights, the definitions, the procedure), (C2) a change in the actions that reach the ground (inspection, adjudication, enforcement, bargaining, organising), and (C3) a change in the habitat (which actors, conditions and relations are in contact with the residual). A regime that changes only C1 and a regime that changes only C2 are not different degrees of one reform; they are different compounds needing different instruments. The labour-law record supplies the empirical version: the Factories Act 1833 *"provided for inspectors to enforce the law"* and is described as *"rigorous enforcement of the law by an independent governmental body"*. That is C2 added to C1, not C1 restated. Conversely, the ILO *"is limited to gathering evidence and reporting on member states' progress, relying on publicity to create pressure to reform"*, which is a change of C1 and C4-type contact without a change of C2 in the registering state.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the party read the legal signal at all, and can the inspector read the workplace?) to 2 vocabulary (does Γ have terms for what the situation affords, and can a party say what a right is?) to 3 objective (can the party say what would count as progress, a goal is a comparison, ⋛) to 4 planning (organise, bargain, litigate, draft) to 5 learn and carry (precedent, institutional memory, carried method). The middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. Fig 12 :111 says that a vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one, and :115 says *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Most institutional-reform effort lands above the break, where the measurements already exist (number of statutes passed, number of cases filed), because that is where the instruments are.

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about legal institutions, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1  -  Power must be indexed per slot and per relation, never aggregated

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1 to D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap: per-slot error signal vs union/partition split).**

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"** (Fig 1 :13)
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"** (Fig 9 :57)

A national index of "worker power", "state capacity" or "employer freedom" aggregates across slots and throws away the only signal that says where a legal change failed. A global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13), so an aggregate can be stable while the employer/worker relation is on fire. The question as posed invites the aggregate answer; the figures forbid it.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a single scalar per jurisdiction predicts, without loss, the per-relation outcomes of at least two independent legal changes that move different relations in opposite directions. Under Fig 1 :22 that should be impossible; if observed, F1 is false.

**World bearing (secondary only):** Dahl is recorded as viewing power as *"relations among people"*, and the power overview notes that political power *"may also be exerted through diffuse means (such as institutions)"*. Both are relational framings, which corroborate per-slot indexing. The labour-law page lists dozens of national regimes side by side, which is a mapping aid, not a measurement of any slot. Mechanism design, a low-overlap field, defines institutions as rules *"that produce good outcomes according to some predefined metric"*: the metric is the thing being designed, which is a warning that a single metric is a design choice, not a discovery (corroborates F1, does not settle it).

### F2  -  Legal institutions work by changing the habitat, not by changing the actors

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low overlap: habitat vs membrane).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"** (Fig 11 :45)
> **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"** (Fig 11 :46)
> **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"** (Fig 11 :47)

The mechanism by which law "shapes" power is not that it alters the character of the state, employers, workers or citizens. It is that it enumerates and introduces actors, conditions and relations into the habitat: who may be a party, who may act, what instruments each may use, what relations (employment, membership, citizenship, bargaining) are recognised, and what cascades follow. A law that grants a right but does not change anyone's contact with the ground changes nothing about `ΔR_s`.

Fig 11 :61 gives the asymmetry: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* The continuation, :62, is *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* A legal system can introduce a union, an inspectorate, a cause of action or a protected category. It cannot subtract the employment relation, market dependence or household need by declaring them away. Fig 11 :32 states the rule: *"→ introduce, never subtract"*. A managed legal order is permanent work, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63-64).

The world corroborates at a different stratum. Institutional economics *"focuses on understanding the role of the evolutionary process and the role of institutions in shaping economic behavior"* and *"views markets as a result of the complex interaction of these various institutions (e.g. individuals, firms, states, social norms)"*. Commons is recorded as holding that *"the economy is a web of relationships between people with diverging interests"*. The Journal of Institutional Economics describes itself as *" devoted to the study of the nature, role and evolution of institutions in the economy, including firms, states, markets, money, households and other vital institutions and organisations"*. These are habitat and relation claims from an economics pool, not figure derivations; they corroborate the shape, marked single-source for the institutional claim.

**Refuter:** a legal change that demonstrably alters realized outcomes on a named slot while leaving every actor's contact with the ground unchanged, with no new actor, condition or relation introduced, and with the same instrument set. If it survives, F2's mechanism is not the cause.

### F3  -  Law is a grammar that makes disputes statable, not a verdict about the world

**Mode: `general` for the convention role, `specified` for the failure. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 12, low overlap: seat/convention vs bond/grammar).**

> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** (Fig 10 :18)
> **"→ install what can be violated"** (Fig 10 :19)
> **"the convention does not fix the bug"** (Fig 10 :16)
> **"it makes the bug statable"** (Fig 10 :17)
> **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."** (Fig 10 :46)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** (Fig 10 :49)
> **"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."** (Fig 12 :115)
> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** (Fig 12 :111)

The legitimate function of legal institutions is to author conventions: definitions of employment, procedures for bringing a claim, standards for evidence, the vocabulary in which a grievance becomes a case. That is the grammar. It makes a wrong statable. It does not settle whether the wrong occurred. When a convention is allowed to decide an outcome by declaration, for example a legal fiction that fixes who prevails regardless of what the ground returns, it has stopped being a convention and has become an authored verdict, which is the named output-layer failure.

Fig 2 :41 must be read with this. *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. In law, shared meaning is genuinely the product: the words have to mean the same thing to a judge, an employer and a worker or the convention fails. But Fig 2 :43 adds *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The same agreement is the product at the convention layer and a corruption at the verdict layer. This is the apparent contradiction that section 6 re-census dissolves.

The labour-law page supplies the field's own version: employment standards are *"social norms (in some cases also technical standards) for the minimum socially acceptable conditions"*, and the definition of employment turns on *"the level of control the master has on them"*. A definition is the convention; whether control was in fact exercised is for the ground.

**Refuter:** a legal convention that settles a world-facing factual dispute by declaration, with no ground reading, and that remains a legitimate convention under the figures rather than an authored verdict. If such a case survives, F3's convention/verdict split fails.

### F4  -  Rights on paper and enforcement in the world are two currencies that do not add

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D5. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 10, low overlap: composition currencies vs channel decay).**

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** (Fig 12 :125)
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** (Fig 12 :126)
> **"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"** (Fig 12 :127)
> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."** (Fig 12 :130)
> **"A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."** (Fig 12 :130)
> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)
> **"you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :28)
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"** (Fig 10 :29)

A legal right is a description: cheap to state, priced in bits. Enforcement is actions: expensive to obtain, priced in finding out whether the right holds. A jurisdiction can have a complete bill of rights on paper (low bits cost) and zero enforcement (no actions paid), and the two do not average into "partial power". The distribution of actual power tracks the actions. This is the framework's sharpest claim on this question, and it is why the answer to "how do legal institutions shape power" is: by changing the description more cheaply than they change the actions, and by deciding whether the channel between them stays reachable.

The named decay modes map directly. A statute whose conditions no longer match the workplace is a stale percept. A right that exists only in a sector the inspectorate never visits is a dropped transient. A compliance score that saturates is a saturated metric. A national average that hides the informal sector is a pooled reading. All four are recorded in the labour-law page: Angola has *"laws that regulate working conditions but they are not well enforced even in the formal sector"* and *"occupational health and safety standards are poorly enforced"*; Benin's code is *"enforced poorly and only in the so-called formal sector"*; the ILO's enforcement is weak enough that scholars propose importing WTO sanctions because *"contravening WTO rules ... opens a country to retaliation through trade sanctions"*. Legal realism corroborates from a different frame: Holmes is quoted as *"The prophecies of what the courts will do in fact, and nothing more pretentious, are what I mean by the law"*, and the article says realists focus on *"the law as it actually exists in practice, rather than how it exists in books."*

**Refuter:** a demonstration that de jure rights and de facto enforcement are commensurable and can be validly summed into one power score that predicts the ground per slot as well as the two readings held apart. Under Fig 12 :125-130 the two cannot add; if the sum works, F4 is false.

### F5  -  Legal categories enter a frame by import from a differing closure, not by internal search

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 8, low overlap: closure mechanics vs union/provenance).**

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)
> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."** (Fig 6 :24)
> **"Cast it in effect terms."** (Fig 6 :30)
> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** (Fig 8 :45)
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."** (Fig 8 :50)
> **"Only the record of where each came from separates them"** (Fig 8 :52)

New legal categories (a new cause of action, a protected category, the concept of collective bargaining as a human right) do not arise by searching inside the existing legal library; they arrive from a frame whose closure differs: another jurisdiction, a social movement, a treaty body, a crisis. The labour-law record shows the import explicitly: the ILO was founded on the principle that *"labour is not a commodity"*, and the 1948 Declaration and core conventions classify eight conventions as core with compliance obligatory even without ratification. Canada's Supreme Court is recorded as reviewing *"the rationale for regarding collective bargaining as a human right"* in 2007, and Article 23 of the Universal Declaration identifies organising trade unions as a fundamental human right. Each is a term crossing a membrane.

Provenance matters. Two jurisdictions can hold the same nominal right, one by convergent derivation and one by adopted import. The contents look identical; only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52). A comparison that ignores provenance will call them the same institution, which is the error named in *The Library of Babel* as mistaking a shape for the route that produced it.

**Refuter:** a legal doctrine that demonstrably expands a frame's reach purely by composing inside its own closure, with no contact with a differing closure, and that passes the ground test across a change of habitat. That breaks idempotence (Fig 8 :32) and F5 with it.

### F6  -  Precedent handed along is a room chain, not a verdict

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 2, low overlap: selection between rooms vs anchor triangulation).**

> **"same closure, handed along;"** (Fig 7 :12)
> **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :13)
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** (Fig 7 :36)
> **"booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."** (Fig 7 :42)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)

A line of precedent that keeps handing along the same closure produces motion without progress. What makes a chain of decisions progress is that the next room differs from the last and matches the residual the last one surfaced, with the ground voting between rooms. Labour law supplies the instance: the Factories Act 1833 added inspectors, which is a new room, not a restatement of the 1802 Act; the ILO'score-convention regime is another; the ECJ decisions in Laval and The Rosella are recorded as changing the closure by which posted workers' rights are read. A committee that reviews the same evidence with the same vocabulary and reaches the same conclusion is one long room.

**Refuter:** a line of precedent that produces ground-verified new outcomes across two handoffs without any change of closure or instrument between them. Under Fig 7 :36 that should be impossible; if observed, F6 is false.

### F7  -  No standing list of powerful actors: valence is relative to the residual

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1 to D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations vs composition structures).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"The arrangement is the substance."** (Fig 12 :94)

The same institution can empower workers in one dispute and empower the state in another. A court that recognises a collective-bargaining right is a benefactor to the worker residual and a malfactor to a state residual that preferred no such right; a court that enforces a strict strike-notice rule reverses both. There is no residual-independent ranking of state, employers, workers and citizens. What can be ranked, per slot and per residual, is the arrangement of bonds. The power overview corroborates from political science: Gene Sharp is recorded as holding that *"power is not monolithic; that is, it does not derive from some intrinsic quality of those who are in power"*, and Dahl's relational definition points the same way. Clegg's circuits of power are recorded as meeting at *"obligatory passage points", which are channels for empowerment or disempowerment"*, which is a bond-location claim rather than an actor-essence claim.

**Refuter:** a stable, residual-independent ordering of the four actors' power that holds across legal regimes, measurement methods and dispute types and that predicts per-slot outcomes as well as the relational reading. Under Fig 11 :49-51 it should not exist; if produced, F7 is false.

### F8  -  The four parties are atoms; legal institutions supply the bonds; same parties, different bonds, different distribution

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 11, low overlap: bond structure vs habitat relations).**

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."** (concise form, Fig 12 :117)

State, employers, workers and citizens are the atoms. Legal institutions supply the typed relations between them: a right is a bond, a duty is a bond, a contract is a bond, a procedure is a bond, a franchise is a bond. Two jurisdictions with the same four parties but different bonds are isomers: the composition formula is the same and the behaviour is different. This is the direct structural answer to "how": by setting the bonds, not by replacing the actors.

A weaker and safer reading is the **functional group**: both regimes may share the `state → rule → citizen` shape (a goal is a comparison, Fig 12 :112), with different atoms in the slots, so they are members of one kind rather than strict isomers. `06_isomers_and_order.md` works both readings and states the falsifier for each.

The low-overlap corroborator is chemistry. The isomer page states: *"isomers are molecules or polyatomic ions with an identical molecular formula – that is, the same number of atoms of each element – but distinct arrangements of atoms in space."* It adds: *"Structural isomers have the same number of atoms of each element (hence the same molecular formula), but the atoms are connected in distinct ways."* And: *"Isomers do not necessarily share similar chemical or physical properties."* That is Fig 12 :117 corroborated from a field with no stake in law, subject to the usual caution that an analogue is not a measurement of the institutional case.

**Refuter:** two legal regimes with identical actors and identical typed bonds but demonstrably different per-slot power distributions that survive a ground reading. Under Fig 12 :5 the bond is the rest of the information; if nothing is left over, F8 is false.

### F9  -  A legal rule no real occupant can satisfy is an idealisation that licenses invalid inferences

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D1. Status: single-source (Fig 10).**

> **"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."** (Fig 10 :53)
> **"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."** (Fig 10 :54)
> **"An idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support"** (Fig 10 :56)
> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** (Fig 10 :18)

A legal standard that no actual party can meet is an idealisation. Models built on it support inferences no real position supports, and those inferences are drawn by whoever finds them useful. This is a concrete mechanism by which formal legal institutions mis-shape power: the "reasonable employer", the "informed worker", the "independent contractor" who in fact depends on one client, or a procedure that requires a complaint within a window no uninformed party could know about. Fig 10 :54 is explicit that the rule describes no position, and :56 is explicit about who will use the resulting inferences.

**Refuter:** a legal standard that no actual party can meet and that nonetheless produces correct, ground-verified outcomes for real parties across repeated cases, with the same inference pattern. If it survives, F9's idealisation claim fails.

### F10  -  Process counts are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D5. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 1, low overlap: filters vs frame-internal quantities).**

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)
> **"coverage, and compression achieved"** (Fig 1 :25)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)

Counts of statutes passed, rights enumerated, cases filed, training sessions delivered and compliance scores are frame-internal quantities that the legal system itself produces. They can rank where to spend the next enforcement budget. They cannot settle whether power moved on a slot. The labour-law page records both kinds: the Factories Act 1833 and ILO core conventions are ground-visible events, while a national count of "laws regulating working conditions" is not. Third-party punishment, a genuinely low-overlap experimental source, adds a qualification worth keeping: *"second-party punishments are consistently higher than third-party punishments for dictators that choose to share less than half of their endowment"*, so the mere presence of a third-party enforcer is not a measure of how much enforcement occurs. Position matters, severity is a separate quantity.

**Refuter:** a frame-internal process metric that, by itself and without a ground reading, correctly settles a dispute about the actual distribution of power on a named slot across two jurisdictions. Under Fig 9 :60 it cannot; if it does, F10 is false.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single scalar per jurisdiction predicts, without loss, the per-relation outcomes of two legal changes that move different relations in opposite directions (breaks Fig 1 :19, :22).
2. A legal change alters realized outcomes on a named slot while every actor's contact with the ground, actor set, condition set and relation set stays unchanged, with the same instruments (breaks Fig 11 :39, :40).
3. De jure rights and de facto enforcement are shown to be commensurable in one valid currency that predicts the ground as well as the two readings held apart (breaks Fig 12 :125-130).
4. A legal frame expands its reach purely by internal composition with no contact with a differing closure and passes the ground test across a habitat change (breaks Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32).
5. A line of precedent yields ground-verified new outcomes across two handoffs with no change of closure or instrument between them (breaks Fig 7 :36).
6. A stable, residual-independent ordering of state, employers, workers and citizens predicts per-slot outcomes across regimes and dispute types (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused from the residual line. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Re-census, strains, and a declared gap

### R1  -  Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 are not in conflict

Fig 2 :40 is *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; :41 is *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. The apparent contradiction dissolves on re-census: :41 governs the convention layer (shared meaning, which law needs to function) and :40 governs the verdict layer (claims about world-facing outcomes). This is also Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Different objects, so no strain after re-census. F3 carries the split.

### R2  -  Fig 13's six-term substrate does not govern labour law

The physical six (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network) cannot be mapped onto statutes, employers and rights without calling a right a kind of spectrum and an inspector a kind of gradient. That is a strain, so Fig 13 is re-censused to the honest-stop role only: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* (Fig 13 :115) and *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* (Fig 13 :134). The cross-kind composition rule (Fig 13 :74-75) is used only as a caution against flattening different strata, never as a physical reduction of law.

### R3  -  The legal-realism conflict, returned to the census and kept narrow

Legal realism asserts that *"the facts and law identified in a judge's opinion are not the actual reasons for why the judgment was reached"* and that judges *"reason backwards after having reached a conclusion"*. This **conflicts** with F3 as first drafted, which treated the recorded reason as the bond that later courts can use. Re-census narrows F3: the recorded reason is the typed bond that is available for later composition (a convention), not a claim about the judge's motive (a verdict about the world). The conflict sends the finding back to the census; it does not flip the verdict. The narrower claim is what `08_expert_verification_packet.md` asks an expert to check.

### R4  -  Which specific institution closes a slot is a `gap`, not a silence

No figure line names an ombudsperson, an inspectorate, a court, a commission, a union or any other concrete checker for a given residual. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* (Fig 13 :135). The third-party punishment source warns that a checker's position is not its severity: *"second-party punishments are consistently higher than third-party punishments"*. So the shape owed is "an outside checker, standing in a frame whose closure differs, able to range against a record neither party can move", and the particular office is left open. Any import of a named office would need a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :50-52) and would be debited against independence (Fig 6 :24). This is a `gap`, reported as unreached.

### R5  -  An overstatement retracted and preserved

F2 was first drafted as "legal institutions cannot change the actors at all". That overstates Fig 11 :39-40. The figures say an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, not that actors are immutable. An actor can learn, and a party's Γ can grow; the claim is that changing the label of an actor without changing contact does not settle the residual. The corrected form is in F2 above. The earlier wording is preserved here rather than erased.

## 7. Recommendation

1. State the shape, not a ranking: per-slot residuals and the conditions that move them, with the four parties kept as separate slots and the pairwise relations kept separate (F1, F7).
2. Separate the currencies: report rights on paper and realized outcomes as two readings, never as one score (F4).
3. Present law as the grammar and the ground as the arbiter; never let a convention decide a world-facing verdict (F3).
4. Name the channel explicitly: which instrument carries a rule from text to outcome on this slot, and what is its measured round-trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :30)?
5. Keep provenance for every right compared: convergent derivation and adopted import look alike in the contents (F5).
6. Use counts to allocate the enforcement budget, never to declare that power moved (F10).
7. Figure 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, the shape and the refuter, not a conclusion about who won.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is "power" a measurable quantity to distribute?** No. It has no anchor independent of the outcomes (Fig 2 :29); the residual is per slot (Fig 1 :19). The answer is a shape about relations, not an allocation.
2. **What does law actually change?** The habitat: actors, conditions, relations (Fig 11 :43-47). Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62).
3. **Is a right the same kind of thing as its enforcement?** No. Bits and actions are two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125-130). The channel can fail while the ground does not decay (Fig 10 :24).
4. **Is the law a verdict?** No. It is a convention that makes a wrong statable (Fig 10 :16-19, :46); a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
5. **How do new rights enter?** By import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), with provenance distinguishing convergence from borrowing (Fig 8 :50-52).
6. **Does agreement among lawyers or courts settle it?** No. Agreement about shared meaning is the convention product (Fig 2 :41); agreement about world-facing power is a correlation that disqualifies a verdict when the evidence pool is shared (Fig 2 :40).
7. **Can the loop name the checker?** No. It returns a kind of thing, never a name (Fig 9 :32); the particular office is a `gap` (Fig 13 :134) and would need a source gate and shadow test if imported.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives in ten per-domain claims, all `specified` or `general`, none `grounded`. What does not survive is the aggregate "power" score, the conflation of rights with enforcement, the treatment of a convention as a verdict, any standing ranking of the four parties, and any named checking institution imported without provenance. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**; one structural strain (Fig 13, R2) sent to the honest-stop role, one apparent contradiction (Fig 2 :40/:41, R1) dissolved by layer, one world conflict (legal realism, R3) narrowed rather than flipped, and one `gap` (R4) reported as unreached.
