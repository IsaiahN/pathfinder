## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not *how much power* each of the four parties holds, but the **shape of the mechanism by which legal institutions move the per-slot gap between what a party can predict from the legal order and what the ground actually returns**. In effect terms:

- **b** is each party's belief about what the legal order entitles it to and what it expects the others to do (a right on paper is a belief, not an outcome).
- **a** is what the party does: legislate, regulate, litigate, organise, bargain, strike, vote, comply, or exit.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the party's library predicts will follow, where Γ is the legal-institutional library of statutes, precedents, procedures, rights, duties, contracts and remedies.
- **o′** is what actually happens: a ruling, a sanction, a wage, a working condition, an enforcement action, a changed law.
- **s** is the slot. R is indexed per party and per relation (state/employer, employer/worker, state/citizen, union/employer) and never averaged into one national "power" number (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

- Option A, "legal institutions distribute a substance called power among four parties": leads to the prediction that power is a measurable stock that can be split and shifted by changing the law's text.
- Option B, "legal institutions set the bonds and the habitat in which the four parties act": leads to the prediction that changing the text moves outcomes only when it changes contact with the ground, and that the four party-slots and their relations must be measured separately (Fig 11 :39, Fig 11 :40, Fig 12 :5).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape stating (i) which things legal institutions can change cheaply (the description, in bits), (ii) which things they must change dearly (the actions that settle an outcome), (iii) which of the four slots and pairwise relations each change reaches, and (iv) an instrument that would discriminate a change of rights on paper from a change of realized outcomes. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the pre-registered refuter in `01` can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of law and power.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40), while *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). Law is largely a convention, so agreement about what a word means is the product; the same agreement used as evidence about world-facing power is the corruption. The two verdicts are kept apart.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
