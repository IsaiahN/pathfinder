# The Tether's answer on legal institutions and the distribution of power

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The four parties named in the question have different expectations from the legal order (b), take different actions (a), and their library Γ predicts different outcomes; the ground returns o′, which is a ruling, a wage, a sanction, a working condition, or a repeal. R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is indexed per party and per relation, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question asks how legal institutions move this per-slot gap. The answer is a union of conditions, not one mechanism: a change in the description (C1), a change in the actions that reach the ground (C2), and a change in the habitat of actors, conditions and relations (C3) are different compounds with different grounds (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 2. The shape

1. **Power is not a substance to be divided.** No anchor exists for "power" independent of the outcomes (Fig 2 :29), so the answer is a per-slot shape, not an allocation and not a rank of who is stronger. A national power index aggregates away the live signal (Fig 1 :22).
2. **Legal institutions work on the habitat, not on the actors.** Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39); an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing (Fig 11 :40). Law enumerates and introduces actors, conditions and relations (Fig 11 :43-47). Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62), so a legal order that declares a relation away has only changed the description.
3. **Law is a grammar, not a verdict.** It makes a wrong statable (Fig 10 :16-19) and authors conventions that make no claim about the world (Fig 10 :46). A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47). This is why the same agreement about a shared meaning is the product in a convention (Fig 2 :41) and a disqualifying correlation when used as evidence about world-facing power (Fig 2 :40).
4. **Rights on paper and enforcement in the world are two readings in different units.** Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds (Fig 12 :125-126). They do not add, and summing them is the named error (Fig 12 :130). A bill of rights with no enforcement is not "partial power"; it is one currency paid and the other not. The channel fails while the ground does not decay (Fig 10 :24), so the measurable object is the round-trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :30).
5. **The parties are atoms; legal institutions supply the bonds.** Same atoms, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117). Two regimes with the same four parties but different typed relations are isomers in the strong reading, members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123) in the cautious reading. "Distribution" is the comparison bond ⋛ (Fig 12 :87), not a subtraction of one party's gain from another's loss.
6. **New legal categories arrive from a differing closure.** Mint composes inside the closure and cannot add an atom (Fig 6 :21); the wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23). Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, and only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52). Provenance is part of the answer, not documentation of it.
7. **Precedent is a room chain, not a verdict.** Same closure handed along gives agreement compounding while evidence does not (Fig 7 :12-13); progress requires the next room to differ and match the surfaced residual, with the ground voting (Fig 7 :35-36). A court that reviews the same record in the same vocabulary has produced one long room (Fig 7 :42).
8. **No standing list of powerful actors.** Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51). The same court can empower workers in one dispute and the state in another. What persists is the arrangement, not an actor's essence (Fig 12 :94).
9. **Impossible legal standards are idealisations.** A rule no real occupant can meet describes no position (Fig 10 :54), and the inferences it licenses will be drawn by whoever finds them useful (Fig 10 :56). This is a concrete mechanism by which formal law mis-shapes power.
10. **Process counts filter the budget, never the verdict** (Fig 9 :60). Statutes passed, cases filed and rights enumerated are frame-internal quantities a legal system scores itself with (Fig 1 :25-27).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a rank of state, employers, workers and citizens. The four are slots, not a ladder.
- Not "law does not matter". Law matters as the grammar and the habitat setter; what is refused is the claim that changing the text alone moves the ground.
- Not that rights and enforcement are the same thing measured twice. They are two currencies and do not add.
- Not that any named office (inspectorate, ombudsperson, court, commission) is the answer for a given residual. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134), reported as unreached.
- Not a derivation of one legal family from another, and no new primitive or seventh kind.
- Not a claim that a convention is false. A convention has no truth value (Fig 10 :46); it can only fail by deciding an outcome.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a specific jurisdiction settles `left(R,φ)` here.
- Not a ToE of institutions. There is no last room (Fig 7 :6); a final account of law and power would be the terminal room the figures rule out.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A per-slot instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Currency-discrimination test:** for a named right and a named relation, measure both the text change (bits) and the action spent, and test whether their sum predicts the outcome better than either held apart. Under Fig 12 :125-130 it should not.
- **Contact test:** find a legal change that moves outcomes with no change of actor set, condition set, relation set or instrument. Under Fig 11 :40 it should not exist.
- **Room test:** track two consecutive decisions on the same residual and measure whether the closure changed between them and whether the second matched the residual the first surfaced (Fig 7 :36).
- **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** a lossless scalar power index; a commensurable currency for rights and enforcement; a frame expanding by internal composition alone; a precedent chain with ground-verified novelty and unchanged closure; a residual-independent ranking of the four parties.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Which specific checker closes which slot is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134-135). The framework can characterise the kind of thing required, standing outside the case and ranging against a record neither party can move, but it cannot name the office without an import that has a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13). The third-party punishment evidence adds a qualification worth keeping: *"second-party punishments are consistently higher than third-party punishments"*, so the borrowed claim is about the checker's position, not its severity. The answer also stops at the limit of the instruments: Fig 10 :52 says *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 4 :30 requires the round-trip gap to be measured rather than assumed zero. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank the state, employers, workers and citizens by power, and it does not claim a final theory of law. It says that power has to be indexed per party and per relation, never folded into one national score; that legal institutions act by changing the habitat of actors, conditions and relations, so a change of text that does not change contact changes nothing; that law is a grammar making disputes statable rather than a verdict about who is right; and that a right on paper (a description priced in bits) and its enforcement (actions priced at the ground) are two readings that do not add. The four parties are the atoms and legal rules supply the bonds, so two regimes with the same parties but different bonds are isomers in the strong reading and share a functional group in the cautious one. New legal categories enter from a frame whose closure differs, precedent progresses only when the next decision differs from the last and the ground votes, and no standing list of powerful actors survives a change of residual. The framework returns the shape any candidate answer must have and a falsifier written in advance; the particular institution that would close a given slot remains open until the ground settles it.
