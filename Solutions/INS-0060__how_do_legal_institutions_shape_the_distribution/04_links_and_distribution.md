## What the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The description/actions split is the field's own distinction, not just a figure claim.** Law in action is defined against law in the statutes and cases; legal realism is defined as *"the law as it actually exists in practice, rather than how it exists in books"*; the labour-law page records statutes that exist but are *"not well enforced even in the formal sector"*. Three different frames converge on the same two readings. They are one broad evidence pool in the sense that they are all law-facing, so their agreement is weak corroboration, not three independent witnesses.
2. **The four-party cast is the field's own cast.** Labour law is defined as mediating workers, employing entities, trade unions and government; collective bargaining is the employer/employee negotiation with a union representative; the power overview treats power as relational and diffuse-through-institutions. This is a framing corroboration of the domain census. It does not settle which bond moves which slot.
3. **Institutions are rules that produce outcomes under a metric.** Mechanism design defines institutions as constructed rules that produce good outcomes according to a predefined metric. That is a low-overlap corroboration of the arrangement claim, and a warning: the metric is authored by the designer, so it is a convention and must not be smuggled in as the verdict (Fig 10 :46).
4. **Same actors, different bonds, different behaviour.** The chemistry isomer page supplies the low-overlap analogue: same molecular formula, distinct atomic arrangements, different properties. It corroborates Fig 12 :117's structure, not the institutional fact. Fig 13's physical substrate is *not* being applied to law (see `01` R2).
5. **A genuine conflict was triggered and returned to the census.** Legal realism says a judge's written reasons may be post hoc rationalisations. That conflicts with treating the recorded reason as the usable bond. `01` R3 narrows the claim to the recorded bond that later courts can compose over, not the judge's motive. The verdict did not flip.
6. **A qualification was kept rather than smoothed.** Third-party punishment is real, but second-party punishment is often stronger, and observers are lenient toward their own group. The claimed shape is about the *position* of an outside checker, not about severity or impartiality by construction.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**  -  *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a full institutional theory before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**  -  *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**  -  *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.

But **Fig 2 :40**  -  *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Never pool later agreement as corroboration that power moved.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record it; (3) then the sources, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
