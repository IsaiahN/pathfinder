# Expert verification packet - the Tether on urban ecological communities

**What this is:** a claim in a form an urban ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the list. Not `grounded`.** No city measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A city substitutes a habitat rather than removing one, so its community inherits actors nobody chose. The habitat is enumerated per residual and the contact cascade is followed outward, because the effect that matters can be three steps out. Valence is relative to the residual, so no standing list of beneficial and harmful urban species survives a change of question. The ecological integrity verdict and the human wellbeing verdict have different anchors and must stay as two rows, never one score. Over a fixed habitat atom set the urban and non-urban communities are isomers; if the atom set changes they are members of one functional group. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not the species list or a canopy target.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** substitution, enumeration and cascade. An urban habitat is substituted, not removed; actors are read off the world and indexed per residual; contacts are followed outward until the cascade stops mattering.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** do these four lines say what the claim says, and is the substitution reading strained when applied to an urban land-use change?
- **Refuter:** a case where the habitat set is unchanged, one species property changes in isolation, and the community changes measurably; or a standing winner list whose valence survives a measured change of residual; or a strictly first-order urban effect with a verified empty second-order enumeration.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against public urban ecology data

- **Statement:** a first-order building change moves hydraulics, then morphology and contaminants, then biota, and only then human outcomes. This is Fig 11 :52-53 applied: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** Walsh et al. 2005 (*Journal of the North American Benthological Society* 24(3):706-723, EPA HERO record) report that the urban stream syndrome has *"a flashier hydrograph, elevated concentrations of nutrients and contaminants, altered channel morphology, and reduced biotic richness, with increased dominance of tolerant species"* and that *"most impacts can be ascribed to a few major large-scale sources, primarily urban stormwater runoff delivered to streams by hydraulically efficient drainage systems."* Is that a faithful three-step cascade? Flies, Lim and Douglas 2022 (*Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* 10:1032022) report greenspaces acting *"as a mixing ground for native and introduced species and between human and non-human animals"*, which is the human-facing second order. Is that faithful?
- **Refuter:** a published catchment-scale enumeration showing no residual at second order for a built change whose first-order effect is measured; or a demonstration that the human outcome is independent of the enumerated intermediates.

### C3 - two-anchor and isomorphism claim; framework-internal with a world check

- **Statement:** the people verdict and the community verdict have different anchors; over a fixed habitat atom set the two communities are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* Fig 2 :38 *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they"* Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** is human judgment rightly an anchor for the people question and only an instrument for the ecology question? Blumstein, Mikula and Tryjanowski 2026 (UCLA Newsroom, originally The Conversation, 2026-04-10) report that *"Cities select for smart individuals and species because that's what it takes to survive"* and that urban bird songs converge under traffic noise. Does that support the isomer reading (same species, different arrangement and behaviour) over the functional-group reading?
- **Refuter:** a single measurement settling both rows with invariant valence across residuals; or an urban community requiring an atom the rural community lacks that cannot be expressed as a value of a shared condition category, which demotes the claim to functional group.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of urban winners or losers, and not a canopy or biodiversity target number.
- Not a single urban-nature score combining ecology and wellbeing.
- Not "cities destroy habitat." The claim is substitution (Fig 11 :66).
- Not that residents' judgments are irrelevant to ecology. They are instruments there, not the anchor (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not that a manager can never remove an organism. The claim is that contact cannot be subtracted without an introduction (Fig 11 :61-62); this is live interpretive strain S1 in `01`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measuring a city.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does a per-city monitoring record discriminate habitat substitution from regional-pool filtering?
2. Does the cascade reach the measured human outcome at second or third order with intermediates enumerated?
3. Do the ecological and human verdicts stay separable under a change of residual?
4. Does the fixed-atom condition for the isomer claim hold for the community being examined?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-44, :49, :51, :61-63, :66 and Fig 2 :36-38. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against a catchment or monitoring dataset you know: are the intermediates in the right order?
3. Name one instrument that separates substitution from filtering in your city, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
