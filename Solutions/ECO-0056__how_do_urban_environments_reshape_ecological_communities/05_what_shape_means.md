## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, *"a city substitutes a habitat and the contact cascade must be enumerated per residual"* is a shape. *"Pigeons, rats and plane trees are the urban winners"* is a name, and a snapshot name at that.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on vocabulary by proximity: use the words you already have that are closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with them, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is the essay form of Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The urban question is exactly such a bundle.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email leads with refuters.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line *"will also be the starting place"*, and by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* For this question the place you stop is the habitat enumeration you can hand to someone else to redo.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A per-city monitoring record that discriminates among candidate mechanisms would settle a name.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator requirement: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* An instrument that cannot distinguish sources *"demonstrates its own inadequacy."* Applied here: a city-wide species richness reading cannot tell you whether substitution, cascade, or introduction drove it. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.

## 4. The carried-over correction from the exemplar, applied here

The exemplar's `05` section 5b corrected an overstatement of the "shape, not a name" rule. I adopt that correction and apply it here. The README phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the library. In an urban ecology question, the actors we name (vectors, canopy layers, impervious surface, ornamental plants) are read off the habitat and are already available in the ecology frame (Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*). Naming one is a binding from that frame, to be recorded as provenance. What remains forbidden is inventing a seventh mechanism and claiming it moves the wall (Fig 6 :22: *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure"*; adding an already-held atom adds none).

So `01` F2 does not say "never name an urban actor." It says the list is enumerated not composed, valence is relative to the residual, and any binding must carry its provenance.

## 5. Answer to the shape question for ECO-0056

**What licenses a particular species list or canopy number for a city?** Nothing in the figures. The framework returns a shape. A particular list is licensed only by a ground reading that settles a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does the question invite the forbidden error?** Yes. The ordinary reading of "how do urban environments reshape communities" asks for a roster of changes and a verdict on people. That is a source read off a projection: a richness index, a canopy fraction, a survey score. By *The Hard Problem is About Source*, the reading does not determine the source. The honest form is the shape plus the refuters and a declared gap.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A campaign that gathers well known urban ecologists to agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried against public monitoring data, and let the ground settle it.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on. No city was measured for this solution.

## 8. One-sentence shape

A city is a substituted habitat whose contacts must be enumerated outward per residual, whose human and ecological verdicts have different anchors, and whose actor list stays a binding until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.
