# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01` section 3 and the three sub-claims in `08` have been checked against the verbatim figure text. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about urban communities, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of how urban environments reshape ecological communities and their relationships with people. I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a city does not remove a habitat, it substitutes one. The new habitat comes with actors nobody selected, so the useful question is what is in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Two consequences follow for the people half: the ecological integrity verdict and the human wellbeing verdict have different anchors, so they cannot be averaged into one score; and valence is relative to the residual, so there is no standing list of good and bad urban species. This is a shape and a procedure, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can tell you which species will win in your city, what fraction of canopy is optimal, or what one number expresses a community's relationship with people. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Any actor I name is an observation from the ecology frame, provenanced as such, not a primitive the framework derived. No survey of residents is corroboration of an ecological claim, and no ecological metric is a verdict about people.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an urban community fully characterized by a fixed species list, with the habitat composition rule broken;
> 2. a standing list of beneficial and harmful urban actors that keeps its valence across a change of residual, with the valence measured;
> 3. a measured urban effect that is strictly first-order, with the second-order contacts enumerated and empty;
> 4. a single measurement that settles the ecological and the human verdicts on one invariant axis;
> 5. a manager permanently subtracting a contact relation with no introduction and no ongoing work.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figure text and derivation are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cite as `Fig N :line`.

> - Worked narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on an urban problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, explicit

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because Fig 2 :40 says *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground and refuter.
