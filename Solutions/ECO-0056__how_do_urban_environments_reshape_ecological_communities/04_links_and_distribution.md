## Wider literature that bears on the residual

1. **Habitat substitution rather than absence.** Grimm et al. 2008 and the BES novel-ecosystems digest are one broad ecology pool, not two independent witnesses. They corroborate F1 and F4 at the mechanism-shape level.
2. **Cascade and the third step out.** Walsh et al. 2005 supplies the best low-overlap example within ecology: a built condition (impervious surface) moves hydraulics, which moves morphology and contaminants, which moves biota. It corroborates F3. Note that Grimm et al. also discusses impervious cover, so the two papers partly share a pool.
3. **Behavioural reshaping and homogenization.** Blumstein et al. 2026 is genuinely low overlap with the framework and with the management literature. It corroborates F2 and F6 and supplies the warning that convergence of behaviour across cities is not the same as a standing winner list.
4. **Two anchors, services and disservices.** Flies et al. 2022 is the closest published match for the people half. It corroborates F5 and F7 and states that the mechanisms remain unclear, which supports the declared gap rather than an invented answer.
5. **Novelty contested.** The BES digest records disagreement about thresholds and irreversibility in the novel-ecosystem concept. That disagreement is evidence for keeping the list as a gap, not for flipping the verdict.

Overlap declaration: the four ecology sources (Grimm, Walsh, BES digest, Flies) draw on overlapping review literature and count as roughly one and a half witnesses, not four. The framework essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent witness weight.
