## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks how a city changes (a) which organisms live where, (b) how those organisms relate to one another, and (c) how those relations bear on people. Stated as an effect: something about the built environment changes the contact surface on which species arrive, persist, fail, and interact, and it also changes the readings people take from that surface (food, shade, disease, nuisance, wellbeing, belonging).

Two candidate answers are being decided:

- **Option A:** a city has a characteristic community, and one can list which species win and state one value for the human relationship.
- **Option B:** a city substitutes a habitat; the community and the human relationship are a union of overlapping conditions indexed per place and per residual; the framework returns the shape of the answer and a refuter, not the list.

What each leads to: A delivers a standing list that fails as soon as the residual changes and averages two different anchors into one number. B delivers an enumerable habitat, a cascade rule, per-slot verdicts, and a declared gap where no figure governs the specific list.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): *a rule that, given a place and a residual, tells you what to enumerate, how far the contact cascade runs, which anchor settles each domain, and what would refute the result.* This binding is written down here so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least two conditions (ecological community dynamics, human relationship) bundled into one question, so a union candidate (Fig 9 :54). Symmetry: the relations in the question are directed and valence-bearing, so a symmetric join cannot represent "benefit to one residual, harm to another" (Fig 11 :49-50). Scale: the effect is per place and per species slot, cascades to second and third order, and reaches a human institutional scale that changes the anchor (Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :52-54, Fig 10 :68).

One line per domain. A domain needs actors, a currency, and a nameable anchor; no anchor means it is not yet a domain.

- **D1, the urban habitat and the ecological community.** Actors: species (native, introduced, commensal, vector), and the conditions and relations among them: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* (Fig 11 :46) and *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"* (Fig 11 :47). Currency: contact, arrival, and persistence (Fig 11 :56). Anchor: the ground, meaning what survives and reproduces under measurement, experiment, or long-term monitoring, and *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29). Nameable, so it is a domain.
- **D2, the human seat and the people relationship.** Actors: residents, planners, managers, instruments, institutions. Currency: introductions, services and disservices, wellbeing readings. Anchor: two different ones, held apart. *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* (Fig 2 :36) and *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* (Fig 2 :37). For the ecological integrity part, *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct"* (Fig 2 :38). Nameable, so it is a domain, with an internal seam.
- **D3, the verification frame.** Actors: urban ecologists, sensors, plots, datasets, peer reviewers. Currency: residuals and settled terms. Anchor: the ground, never agreement: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* (Fig 2 :45). Nameable, so it is a domain. In this simulated run D3 draws on the same evidence pool as D1 and D2, so its independence is low and its agreement is not a witness.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A green roof can settle a microclimate slot and a human-shade slot at once; the same tree can be a service to one resident and a disservice to another. The intersections are categorically different cases (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), so they need their own loop.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*).** Enumerate the habitat and its contact surface first (D1); then arrival, persistence, and the cascade (D1); then the seat's acts and readings (D2); then verification (D3). The chain's weakest link is vocabulary/objective: we have terms for species and for "green space" but no shared instrument for what counts as progress in a "relationship with people." Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Strain test applied before findings: Fig 11 speaks of habitats and species, Fig 2 of anchors and frames, Fig 9 of union and partition. They govern different quantities, so there is no strain yet. One live strain appears at F4 and is named rather than smoothed.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The urban environment substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :66:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :67:

> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

Fig 11 :68-69:

> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

The effect term is substitution. A city replaces soil with impervious surface, seasons with heat island and artificial light, and a regional species pool with a filtered one, then inherits actors nobody selected: pigeons, rats, mosquitoes, ornamental plants, pets. The two silent failures the figure names are exactly the ones urban ecology reports: the conditions a species needed but the city did not reproduce, and the actors that arrived with the new habitat and only became visible when they acted.

World corroboration (secondary, opened 2026-11-23): the British Ecological Society digest *"Novel ecosystems: the new normal?"* defines a novel ecosystem as a system whose abiotic, biotic and social components *"by virtue of human influence, differ from those that prevailed historically, having a tendency to self-organize"* (Hobbs, Higgs and Hall 2013, as quoted by BES 2024). Grimm et al. 2008 write that *"Because cities so radically transform landscapes, creating new and less functional ecosystems in the process, they were 'shunned' by ecologists during the 20th Century."* Both predict substitution, not absence.

**Refuter:** a case where an urban environment leaves the habitat set identical and changes only a property of one species in isolation, with measured community change. That would break the substitution claim at the ground.

### F2 - The habitat is enumerated, never composed; there is no standing list of good urban species (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :48:

> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

So the answer to "which species and relations" is a procedure, not a roster. A tree is a benefactor for shade and carbon and sometimes a malfactor for water demand, allergen load, or pest habitat. The list must be re-read for each residual.

World corroboration: Blumstein, Mikula and Tryjanowski 2026 report that urban animals in Los Angeles, Lima, Lagos and Lahore behave alike despite different evolutionary histories, a process they call behavioral homogenization, and that cities select for bolder animals and for birds that sing *"louder, start singing earlier in the morning or at higher frequencies to avoid getting drowned out by low-frequency traffic noise."* The same species is reshaped differently by different residuals, which is what Fig 11 :49-50 predicts; the homogenization itself is a convergence warning, not a standing-winner list.

**Refuter:** a standing list of urban "winners" and "losers" that keeps its valence across a change of residual (shade provision, disease risk, native biodiversity, and resident preference), with the valence measured. That breaks Fig 11 :51.

### F3 - Contact cascades; a first-order habitat scoping misses the residual that matters (triangulated)

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The city is a cascade machine. Impervious surface changes runoff; runoff changes channel morphology and contaminant load; those change benthic taxa and fish; those change anglers, bird predators, and human exposure.

World corroboration: Walsh et al. 2005, *"The urban stream syndrome: current knowledge and the search for a cure"*, report symptoms that include *"a flashier hydrograph, elevated concentrations of nutrients and contaminants, altered channel morphology, and reduced biotic richness, with increased dominance of tolerant species"* and attribute most impacts to *"urban stormwater runoff delivered to streams by hydraulically efficient drainage systems."* Flies, Lim and Douglas 2022 add that urban greenspaces can provide disservices *"as a mixing ground for native and introduced species and between human and non-human animals"* and that *"These novel urban interactions may change disease patterns and adaptation by vector species and their pathogens."* Both are second and third order effects of a first-order built change.

**Refuter:** a measured urban effect that is strictly first-order for every residual in the census, with a verified enumeration of second-order contacts that return no residual. That would make the cascade clause unnecessary.

### F4 - Introduction is available, exclusion is not; management is permanent work (triangulated, one strain named)

Fig 11 :61-63:

> **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
> **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Fig 11 :65:

> **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**

Urban green infrastructure, predator introduction, shading, and drainage retrofit are all introductions. Their failure mode is the one the figure names: the cascade was not enumerated.

**Strain S1, named not hidden.** A city manager plainly can remove a bird, cull a mosquito population, or pave over a vacant lot. Read literally, "exclusion is not" seems false. Re-census: the figure is about subtracting the *habitat* and therefore the contact, not about removing an individual organism. The manager who removes rats must add a sealed bin, a competitor, or a building design; the contact surface was altered by introduction. The strain dissolves into a narrower claim: exclusion of contact is unavailable, exclusion of individuals is an introduction in disguise. This remains a live interpretive point, kept visible in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `11_audit.md`.

World corroboration: Walsh et al. 2005 call for *"widespread application of innovative approaches to drainage design"* and note that *"The mechanisms driving the syndrome are complex and interactive"*, which matches permanent work rather than a solved state. BES 2024 notes that *"We are still poor at predicting which non-natives will become invasive"*, which is the unenumerated-cascade failure.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated case where a manager permanently subtracts a contact relation without adding any substitute actor or condition, and the community stays at the target state without further work.

### F5 - Two anchors, not one; the community verdict and the people verdict cannot be averaged (triangulated)

Fig 2 :36-38:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**
> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they"**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 10 :36:

> **"the only seat standing in a second frame"**

The question contains two constitutive anchors. For "relationship with people," human judgment (wellbeing, perceived safety, cultural value) is the right anchor. For "ecological community," it is only an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. A single "urban nature score" that blends species richness with resident satisfaction lets an instrument decide an ecological verdict and a human preference decide a biological one.

World corroboration: Flies, Lim and Douglas 2022 open with *"The character, design and biodiversity of urban ecosystems have both beneficial and negative impacts on human health and wellbeing"* and note that *"the ways in which urban ecosystems influence human health remain unclear"*. Their editorial reports that the same greenspace can be recreation by day and a place perceived as dangerous at night, and that wellbeing in two cities was *"most strongly influenced by availability of basic infrastructure"* before environmental qualities mattered. Services and disservices share one site but not one anchor.

**Refuter:** a single measurement that settles both the ecological integrity verdict and the human wellbeing verdict on the same axis, with a demonstrated invariance of that axis to the residual. Without that, the two verdicts stay separate.

### F6 - Capability is agent-and-habitat; improving a species in isolation changes nothing (triangulated)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Transposed without strain: a "climate-ready tree" planted into an unreproduced soil and water habitat does not change contact and therefore does not change community persistence. The lever is the habitat configuration, not an isolated property of one organism.

World corroboration: Blumstein et al. 2026 report that *"Cities select for smart individuals and species because that's what it takes to survive"* and that losing behavioral diversity *"leaves populations less able to respond to future changes"*, a habitat-contact reading of capability. The same source notes urban animals may *"concentrate on garbage bins or rubbish dumps"* and eat *"a potentially unhealthy diet"*, so an individual advantage in one slot (easy calories) can be a population cost in another.

**Refuter:** a measured community-level improvement produced by changing only an intrinsic property of one species while the habitat contact surface is held constant.

### F7 - The question is a union, not a partition; one lever leaves the intersections standing (single-source on the figure side, corroborated)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A tree-planting program clears the shade slot and part of the biodiversity slot. It leaves standing the intersection where a dense green corridor also moves vectors, imports non-natives, or is perceived as unsafe. Those are not harder shade cases; they are different compounds.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated partition, meaning one condition whose satisfaction is exactly the satisfaction of the other across all measured residuals, so the union has no intersection.

### F8 - Verdicts per slot; no city-wide average (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

A city average of canopy cover or species richness discards the live slot where a particular neighborhood, species, or vector is failing. The human relation likewise differs by group, as the Frontiers editorial records for children, residents of informal neighborhoods, and different communities.

**Refuter:** a case where a global city metric predicts every per-slot outcome with no residual left over. That would make aggregation lossless and F8 false.

### F9 - What survives: a shape, a cascade rule, and a declared gap (verdict)

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

No figure line returns the list of species that will win in a given city, the optimal canopy percentage, or one number for "relationship with people." The framework returns the shape: enumerate the habitat, follow the cascade outward, keep verdicts per slot and per anchor, introduce with a shadow test, and write the refuter first. The specific list is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not an invitation to invent one. *"A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* (Fig 8 :39)

**Refuter for the gap itself:** a figure line, checked verbatim in `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, that names a species set, a canopy fraction, or a single human-nature index. Until such a line is produced, the gap stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An urban community is fully characterized by composing a fixed species list with no residual, such that *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed"* (Fig 11 :43) is violated.
2. A standing list of beneficial and harmful urban actors keeps its valence across a change of residual, measured (breaks Fig 11 :51).
3. All measured urban ecological effects are strictly first-order, with a verified second-order enumeration returning nothing (breaks Fig 11 :52-53).
4. A single measurement settles the ecological and the human verdicts on one invariant axis (breaks the two-anchor reading, Fig 2 :36-38).
5. A manager permanently subtracts a contact relation with no introduction and no ongoing work (breaks Fig 11 :61-63).
6. A figure line is produced that names the species list, the canopy fraction, or the human-nature index (closes the declared gap).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the audit is rewritten. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F3: substitution and cascade. They are the strongest figure-grounded points and they match the urban ecology literature without needing a new primitive.
2. Keep the two anchors of F5 separate in every artifact, including the outreach email. Do not publish a combined score.
3. Enumerate per place and per residual (F2, F8). Ask which species and conditions are in contact with the particular residual before naming any actor.
4. Treat every green infrastructure proposal as an introduction with a shadow test: state what gap it explains and what cascade it was not enumerated for (F4).
5. Publish the gap as a verdict (F9). Say plainly that no figure line returns the list.
6. Send the generator, not a recording: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* (Fig 4 :25)
