## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the order of the enumeration? (decisive, figure-derived)

Fig 11 :43-48 gives the order explicitly:

- :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- :45 *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*
- :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
- :47 *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*
- :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

So the order is: residual first, then direct contacts, then second-order contacts, then outward until the cascade stops mattering, then read the new residual. This is Fig 3 :20's dependency order applied to a habitat: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

The practical consequence for this question: starting from a species list and asking what the city did to it is effort above the break. The residual is not in the species list; it is in the contact surface. Walsh et al. 2005 make the same point in empirical terms: the built condition is at the top of a chain whose biological symptom is three steps down.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a complete direct-contact enumeration predicts all measured second and third order effects with no new residual, then the "outward until the cascade stops mattering" clause adds nothing and is withdrawn.

### Q2 - Are urban and non-urban communities isomers? (general on the analogue, specified on the condition)

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Two community readings are possible over the same regional pool:

- **Strong form (isomer).** Hold the habitat atom set fixed: the same regional species pool, the same conditions of light, water, temperature, season and terrain as categories, and the same relation types. The urban and non-urban communities differ only in the arrangement, meaning which actor occupies which relation and which condition binds which actor. Then they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a species-count formula and decisive in behaviour, which is exactly what Blumstein et al. 2026 report when urban and rural members of the same species behave differently.
- **Weak form (functional group).** If the urban habitat introduces actors or conditions genuinely absent from the rural list (impervious surface as a condition, a commensal as an actor), the two communities do not share the full atom set. They are then two members of a functional group: both are *habitat with actors, conditions and relations*, with different members in the slots. Fig 12 :123: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The honest claim is the strong form **conditioned** on a fixed atom set, with the weak form as the standing fallback. This mirrors the exemplar's treatment of GR and QM, where the isomer claim holds over the full shared set and fails as the theories are actually written.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if an urban community requires an atom that the rural community demonstrably does not have and that cannot be expressed as a value of a shared condition category, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 - What is the order of the people half? (specified, two anchors)

Fig 2 :36-37:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**

Fig 2 :38:

> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they"**

The people half is not a fifth step appended to the ecological enumeration. It is a second track with a different anchor that must be run in parallel and kept separate:

1. Ecological track: enumerate habitat and cascade, settle with a ground that does not update on residents' opinions.
2. Human track: ask what residents would count as a failure, settle with human judgment because the question is constitutively about people.
3. No averaging. Keep the verdicts as two rows. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* And :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

Flies et al. 2022 supply the empirical reason: the same greenspace can be a service and a disservice, and the mechanisms linking biodiversity to wellbeing remain unclear. Two rows, not one score.

**Falsifier:** a single instrument whose readings settle both rows with invariant valence across residuals. If one exists, the two-track rule is unnecessary.

### Q4 - Substitution versus composition (structure, specified)

Fig 11 :66:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 13 :74:

> **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

These govern different objects, so there is no strain after re-census. Fig 13 :74 governs composition of terms inside the six-term substrate. Fig 11 :66 governs what happens to a habitat when a residual is moved: you do not subtract the old one, you get a new one with inherited actors. An urban environment is therefore a substitution event, not a melt of the rural community into a new compound by composition. The bond diagram that would describe it is a change of arrangement over a (partly) new atom set, which is why Q2 needs the fixed-atom condition.

**Falsifier:** a demonstration that the urban community is derivable purely by composing the rural atoms with no substitution event, with the new conditions shown to be old atoms under different names and no residue left over.

### Q5 - The order of the union (single-source on the figure side)

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :57:

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The city is a union of the ecological contact condition and the human judgment condition. Unbundling does not mean solving one and then the other; it means giving each its own loop and then treating the intersections (vector habitat in a beloved park, a disservice that is also a service) as specific compounds with their own residual. A single lever, such as planting more trees, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

## CONCLUSION

The order is residual, direct contact, cascade outward, new residual; the people half runs as a separate track with its own anchor; and the urban and non-urban communities are isomers only over a fixed habitat atom set, otherwise members of one functional group. All three claims are structural and reversible, need no new primitive, and carry written falsifiers.

This rescues what is defensible in the ordinary question without inventing a roster: the *relation* between the city and the community is an arrangement over habitat atoms and a cascade, not a list of winners and not a new kind of thing.
