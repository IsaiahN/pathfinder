# The Tether's answer: how urban environments reshape ecological communities and their relationships with people

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain findings, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the list. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No city was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A city changes which organisms live where, how they relate to one another, and what those relations mean for people. The question bundles at least two conditions with different anchors, so it is a union compound, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). Its own loop and its own ground are owed for each condition.

## 2. The shape

1. **The urban environment substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* The new habitat comes with actors nobody chose (Fig 11 :67), and its two silent failures are what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-69). This is the central mechanism.
2. **The habitat is enumerated, never composed, and re-read per residual.** Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Actors, conditions and relations are read off the world (Fig 11 :45-48), not invented. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51), so there is no standing list of good and bad urban species.
3. **Contact cascades beyond the first order.** Fig 11 :52-53 uses DDT reaching eagles as the case where *"the residual that mattered was three steps out."* In cities, a built condition (impervious surface, heat island, artificial light) moves hydraulics, behaviour and disease vectors before it moves human wellbeing. A first-order scoping is how the relevant residual gets missed.
4. **Introduction is available; exclusion of contact is not.** Fig 11 :61-63: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Green infrastructure, drainage retrofit, and predator introduction are all introductions; their failure mode is an unenumerated cascade (Fig 11 :65). A manager can remove individuals, but cannot subtract a contact relation without adding a substitute.
5. **The ecological verdict and the human verdict have different anchors.** Fig 2 :36-37: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* For ecological integrity that judgment is only an instrument (Fig 2 :38), and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. The two verdicts are two rows, never one score (Fig 12 :125, :130).
6. **Over a fixed habitat atom set, urban and non-urban communities are isomers.** Fig 12 :117: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If the atom set changes, the weaker functional-group reading holds (Fig 12 :123). This is an arrangement claim, not a new primitive.
7. **The specific answer is a declared gap.** No figure line returns the winning species, an optimal canopy percentage, or a single value for the human relationship. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* A search that finds nothing here is not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a roster of urban winners and losers. A standing list is refused by Fig 11 :51.
- Not a canopy fraction, a biodiversity target number, or a single urban-nature index. Those are names where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that human judgment is irrelevant to ecology. It is an instrument, not the ecological anchor (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not that cities destroy habitat. They substitute one (Fig 11 :66).
- Not that a city is a single problem. It is a union (Fig 9 :54-57).
- Not a verdict on any real city. No measurement was taken; nothing here is `grounded`.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the questions are:

- Does a per-city monitoring record discriminate substitution (new actors inherited with the habitat) from simple filtering of the regional pool? An instrument that separates those names the mechanism.
- Does the cascade reach the measured human outcome at second or third order, with the intermediate contacts enumerated? This is the test Walsh et al. 2005 pose for urban streams at catchment scale.
- Do the ecological and human verdicts stay separable under a change of residual, or does one instrument settle both with invariant valence? If the latter, the two-anchor rule is falsified.
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4: a composable fixed species list, a valence-preserving standing list, a strictly first-order effect with empty second-order enumeration, one invariant axis for both anchors, or a permanent subtraction of contact with no introduction.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The habitat list is read off the world and the instruments are local and per city. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Public monitoring datasets and published experiments are the worse instruments already returning something; they are where the enumeration starts. The answer stops at the limit of what those instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the urban winners and does not give a canopy target. It says a city substitutes a habitat rather than removing one, so the new community inherits actors nobody chose, and the right question is what is in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Valence is relative to the residual, so there is no standing list of good and bad urban species. The ecological integrity verdict and the human wellbeing verdict have different anchors and must be kept as two rows, never averaged. Green infrastructure is an introduction whose cascade must be enumerated, and management is permanent work. What the framework offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and a declared gap where no figure line returns the list. The specific city stays open until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.
