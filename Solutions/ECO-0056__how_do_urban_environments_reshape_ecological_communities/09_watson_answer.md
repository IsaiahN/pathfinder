## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a bundled question: a city changes which organisms live where, how they relate, and what that means for people. Option A: return the roster of changes and one value for the human relationship. Option B: return the shape (substitution, enumeration, cascade, per-slot valences, two anchors) and a declared gap where no figure governs the list. What each leads to: A delivers a standing list that fails on a change of residual and an average of two different anchors; B delivers an enumerable habitat, a cascade rule, per-domain verdicts kept separate, and refuters written in advance.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The bundle of changes in community composition, interaction and human relation produced by a built environment. Arity: at least two conditions, so a union candidate (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). Symmetry: relations are directed and valence-bearing, so a symmetric join cannot express benefactor to one residual and malfactor to another (Fig 11 :49-50). Scale: per place and per species slot, cascading to second and third order, reaching a human institutional scale that changes the anchor (Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :52-54, Fig 10 :68).
- **Domain 1, the urban habitat and the ecological community.** Actors: species, conditions and relations, enumerated as *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* (Fig 11 :46) and *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"* (Fig 11 :47). Currency: contact, arrival and persistence (Fig 11 :56). Anchor: the ground, what survives and reproduces under measurement or experiment, and *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and the urban ecology sources with low overlap.
- **Domain 2, the human seat and the people relationship.** Actors: residents, planners, managers, institutions. Currency: introductions, services and disservices, wellbeing readings. Anchor: two, held apart. *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* (Fig 2 :37); *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct"* (Fig 2 :38) for ecology. Verdict: `general` on the split, `specified` that the anchors differ, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10 with the world source on services and disservices.
- **Domain 3, the verification frame.** Actors: urban ecologists, sensors, plots, datasets, reviewers. Currency: residuals and settled terms. Anchor: the ground, never agreement: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* (Fig 2 :45). Verdict: `general` structural; in this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is low and the verdict is internal consistency only.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* One lever (more trees) clears a single-condition region and leaves the intersections (vector habitat, disservices, perceived safety) standing.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Enumerate habitat contact, then arrival and cascade, then the seat's acts and readings, then verification. Weakest link is vocabulary/objective, where no instrument says what counts as progress in a relationship with people (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test: Fig 11 governs habitats, Fig 2 governs anchors, Fig 9 governs union and partition. Different quantities, no strain at census. One interpretive strain (S1, exclusion of individuals versus exclusion of contact) is named in `01` F4 and kept live.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the roster does not. A city substitutes a habitat, inheriting actors nobody chose. The community is read by enumerating contacts outward from the residual, with valence relative to that residual, so no standing winner list holds. Contact cascades beyond the first order. The ecological and human verdicts have different anchors and are kept as two rows. Over a fixed habitat atom set the urban and non-urban communities are isomers; with a changed atom set they are members of one functional group. The species list, canopy fraction and single human-nature index are a declared `gap`, which is a verdict and not silence. Live strain S1 (exclusion of individuals versus exclusion of contact) is narrowed and kept visible. Verdict status: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape carries explicit refuters and per-domain verdicts, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a roster or a combined score instead of a generator and a refuter. Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The forbidden pieces (the roster, the combined score, the claim that cities destroy habitat) are removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-city monitoring record that discriminates habitat substitution from regional-pool filtering, or a catchment enumeration showing a strictly first-order effect with no residual at second order, would move the mechanism verdict. A single instrument settling both ecological and human verdicts with invariant valence would break the two-anchor rule. A figure line naming the species list or canopy fraction would close the gap. A demonstration that a manager permanently subtracts a contact relation with no introduction would break Fig 11 :61. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
