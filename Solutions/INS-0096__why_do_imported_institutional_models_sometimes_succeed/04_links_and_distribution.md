## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The transfer field already distinguishes transfer from learning and from diffusion.** The Policy transfer page notes that the literature since Dolowitz and Marsh 2000 focuses on who transfers, what, from and to where, the degrees of and constraints on transfer, and its success once transferred. It also names feedback and the "indigenization" of policies as they are adapted to context. This corroborates the union reading and the import-seam condition: what travels is not identical to what lands.

2. **Compatibility and local meaning decide adoption, not the innovation's intrinsic quality.** Rogers' ACCORD model lists compatibility (fit with existing values, patterns of behaviour or tools) alongside relative advantage, and the Los Molinos case shows a boiled-water campaign failing because boiled water carried a stigma of illness locally. Seeing Like a State makes the same point at state scale: legible standards erase the local knowledge that made a practice work. These are low-overlap corroborators for the habitat and valence conditions. They are secondary; the figures decide.

3. **Path dependence and transmission bias explain why identical text diverges.** The path dependence page reports legal traditions in Polish courts persisting almost a century after formal law was unified. Dual inheritance theory reports prestige, success, similarity and conformist biases in what gets copied, and says social learning pays off only in moderately changing environments. Together they corroborate the weighting-and-history condition from a field far from institutional economics. One witness, not several: the Wikipedia institutional pages all draw on the same canon.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :25** *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the separator and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four-condition separator and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* and Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the separator without holding the framework interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict is recorded before any theory is shown and is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per case.** A frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (Formula, "Direction"), so each user writes down what would refute their result before running it.
