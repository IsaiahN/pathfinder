## 0. The residual, in effect terms

An institutional model M (a written rule set, an agency form, a court procedure, a welfare design, an electoral rule) is developed in source setting S and installed in target setting T. The text of M is the same. The residual differs:

> R_T = |Gamma_T(b_T, a) - o'_T|

where b_T is the target's inherited belief and weighting, a is the act of enacting the model, and o'_T is what actually happens. R_T goes near zero in some target slots and stays large in others.

- **Arity:** at least four place (model, target slot, enactment action, observation). R is indexed per target slot, never averaged across countries (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- **Symmetry:** the written form is exchangeable across targets (a list of clauses can be copied), the outcome is not. The symmetry is broken by habitat, weighting, anchor and channel. That is the isomer condition (Fig 12 :117).
- **Scale:** step (one official applying a rule), cycle (one agency), level (one polity), population (cross-polity comparison), lineage (the history that made the belief). There is a lossy membrane between scales (Fig 4 :27).
- **Ground:** the realized outcome at the slot, not expert agreement about the design. An anchor is *"whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* (Fig 2 :29).

**Direction bound before the run.** The sought-for answer shape is a *separator*: a set of conditions, each independently able to fail, stated in effect terms. Written down here so it can accrue a residual like any other term (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-66). The preference for a single factor is thereby made refusable.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The transferred object is a convention, not a ground (governs D1)

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*. Fig 10 :19: *"install what can be violated"*. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

A statute or an agency chart declares a meaning. It becomes a constraint only when something can violate it and a ground can refuse it. The "model" that is copied is almost always the ingredient list (the clauses, the org chart, the procedure), while the bonds (who may refuse whom, what evidence can move a decision, which contact the rule keeps reachable) are the half that is not recorded. *"The arrangement is the substance"* (Fig 12 :94).

**Refuter:** a written rule that constrains outcomes in a target with no interpretation channel and no reachable ground, for which the outcome still tracks the text. That would break this finding.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 convention reading and Fig 12 notation, low overlap).

### F2 - What crosses the membrane decides most of the outcome (governs D2)

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 4 :26: *"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."*

A successful transfer carries a generator: a reapplicable method or a head start that makes the target's own search cheaper. A failed transfer carries a recording of one occasion, the source's history with the method deleted. This is the first and most general separator.

Fig 12 :107-109 completes the mechanism: *"where the arrangement is / recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays / again."* Where the target does not hold the record of *why* the source rule worked, every lineage pays the derivation again. Best-practice guides and study tours carry context and subtext but almost never the pretext (see `05`, Decoding Tacit Knowledge).

**Refuter:** a documented case where only the verbatim text and the one-occasion story crossed, the bonds were not recorded, no head start was supplied, and the target still reproduced the source outcome on a non-updating ground across more than one slot.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 and Fig 12, different figures and different quantities).

### F3 - The landing habitat decides what the method becomes (governs D3)

Fig 4 :34-35: *"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

This is the figure-grounded reason the same model succeeds in one setting and fails in another. The unit of success is the model-and-habitat, never the model. Copying the text while leaving the habitat alone is an improvement to the agent in isolation.

Fig 11 :66-69 names the two silent failure modes of a transplant: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* and *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* The two failures: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*. A test harness or a pilot enclave is a substituted habitat: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability"* (Fig 11 :70).

**Refuter:** identical outcomes across a habitat substitution that changes actors, conditions, relations and valence, with the same recorded arrangement and the same channel.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 landing rule and Fig 11 capability claim, genuinely different figures).

### F4 - Valence is relative to the target residual, not carried by the design (governs D3)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

An independent court, a tax authority, a competitive electoral rule or a decentralised budget is not a fixed good. Its effect depends on which residual it is ranged against in that setting. This is why a list of "best practices" cannot be a verdict: it is a standing list of good actors, which Fig 11 :51 refuses. The import must be enumerated against the *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*, *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*, and *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"* of the target (Fig 11 :45-47), not composed from the source's category list.

**Refuter:** a named institution whose effect on the same residual is identical in two habitats that differ in the actors, conditions and relations enumerated around that residual.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 valence and Fig 9 residual-first, low overlap).

### F5 - Identical architecture diverges through history and weighting (governs D4)

Symbols table :25-26: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* The weighting w is *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior"* (Symbols :23-24). Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Two target settings that receive the identical written model still diverge, because each ranges it against a different private history and a different prior. This is the structural reason parallel adoption produces non-parallel outcomes. The world literature calls it path dependence and pretext; the figures call it w and closure.

**Refuter:** two targets matched on history, prior, habitat contact, recorded arrangement and channel fidelity, with a persistent unexplained residual.

**Status:** triangulated (Symbols table and Fig 6, different objects).

### F6 - Success must be anchored, or it is coherent drift (governs D5)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients,"* and *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33-34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

A target's reported "success" is only evidence if the reference does not update on the institution's own report. Where the compliance metric is produced by the institution it grades, the metric is a frame and the reading is mutual update, not triangulation. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 12 Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* - a design with no seat able to refuse it cannot be corrected, only accumulated.

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. If the imported rule fixes the outcome it was supposed to measure, it has become a verdict wearing a convention's clothes.

**Refuter:** outcomes that continue to track an independently measured ground after the institution's own reporting channel is demonstrably saturated.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2, with the Operators table on refusal, low overlap).

### F7 - The import needs a source gate and a shadow test, and it is debited (governs D2 and D6)

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* (said of IMPORT). Fig 6 :24: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

The source's success is an import, not a proof. It needs provenance (where did the claimed success come from, who measured it, what was the ground) and a shadow test (does the imported atom explain a gap the target already had). Without provenance, a convergent derivation and an adopted import are indistinguishable in the contents. Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 6 :51-52). Buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong is Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Refuter:** a transfer whose success is fully accounted for by an independently measured source ground, with provenance and a shadow test supplied, and whose independence cost is zero.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 8, the two import figures, plus Fig 2 on independence pricing).

### F8 - The comparison frame cannot manufacture corroboration (governs D6)

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A panel of reformers who all read the same best-practice literature is one evidence pool wearing many names. Agreement among them is efficient for a convention (a shared meaning about what "good governance" is) and disqualifying for a verdict about whether the model worked. A cross-country success rate is a filter for where to spend attention, not a witness for a cause.

**Refuter:** two genuinely low-overlap frames (different ground contact, different evidence pool) that independently converge on the same per-slot verdict and whose agreement survives an anchor test.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 2, different figures).

### F9 - A success reading does not determine its source (the metamerism finding)

From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23, `10_source_ledger.csv`): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the green reading. Fig 13 :128: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."*

Observing that a model "works" in S and "works" in T does not identify what produced the reading. Several different mechanisms (a method crossing, a habitat that would have produced the same outcome anyway, a saturated success metric) give the same observed success. Naming the cause as "the design quality" from the reading alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a better narrative.

**Refuter:** an instrument that discriminates among the candidate sources of a success reading in a target and shows the recorded arrangement, not contact or measurement, produced it.

**Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 13 :128 plus the canonical re-census rule), with a low-overlap world corroborator from perception science. Marked single-source, not upgraded.

### F10 - Isomers, not a single scale (governs structure)

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two target settings that nominally receive the same model are isomers over a shared atom set if they hold the same institutional atoms (a court, a budget rule, an accountability seat) arranged with different bonds (who can refuse whom, what evidence travels). They are members of one functional group if they share only the arrangement kind (for example, a separated accountability function) while filling it with different atoms. Which reading applies is an empirical question, not a figure verdict. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Refuter:** two nominal copies of the model with identical atoms and identical ordered bonds in the two targets that still diverge, which would kill the isomer read.

**Status:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with physics and chemistry analogues as corroboration only.

### F11 - The universal recipe is a gap, not a silence

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 13 :136: *"What it can do is narrow. It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line returns a transfer-success percentage, a universal sequence of reforms, or the identity of the one factor that decides success. That is a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reached after a genuine re-census of all 13 figures and both tables. Unreachable is reported, not filled. The gap is not an invitation to invent a number.

**Refuter:** a figure line that does select a transfer recipe or success fraction would close this gap; none was found after the census in section 2.

**Status:** `gap`, reported plainly.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat reasoning rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim (the four-condition separator) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Matched-pair failure:** two target settings with the same recorded arrangement, the same habitat contact, the same history and weighting, and the same non-updating channel still diverge persistently per slot.
2. **Habitat-indifference:** a transferred model produces identical outcomes across a habitat substitution that changes actors, conditions, relations and valence.
3. **No-channel constraint:** a written rule constrains outcomes with no interpretation channel and no reachable ground.
4. **Single-score prediction:** a source-independent measure predicts all per-slot transfer outcomes from one score, with no residual left after the four conditions are removed.
5. **Source-discriminating result:** an instrument shows that the recorded arrangement, not contact or measurement, produced the success reading.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the separator is rebuilt. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator, not with a theory of institutions (F1 to F6). The four conditions are checkable from public data.
2. State the mode on every sentence. The verdict is `specified`, the structural shape is `general`, the recipe is `gap` (F11).
3. Put provenance and the shadow test before any claim that the source's success transfers (F7). A success story with no record is a recording, not a generator.
4. Enumerate the target habitat before naming the frame: actors, conditions, relations, then the cascade outward (Fig 11 :43-48). Do not import a category list.
5. Keep a seat able to refuse the imported convention when the ground turns against it (Fig 10 :47; Operators :58).
6. Use a cross-country success rate only as a budget filter, never as a witness (Fig 9 :60).
7. Send the generator (the residual, the separator and the refuter), not a recording of this derivation (Fig 4 :25).
