# Solution INS-0096: imported institutional models

**Question:** Why do imported institutional models sometimes succeed and sometimes fail when applied in different political and social settings?

**Source:** institutional questions.txt line 117

**Section:** 10. Institutional change, adaptation, and research

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain conditions, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal transfer recipe or success percentage, not `grounded`. This folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## What is being decided, in effect terms

An institutional model M is built in a source setting S and installed in a target setting T. The written form is the same, the outcome is not. Restated as an effect: why does R_T = |Gamma_T(b_T, a) - o'_T| go near zero in some target slots and stay large in others when the text of M does not change?

- Option A: success and failure are noise around a portable design quality, so one score or one checklist predicts the outcome.
- Option B: the transfer is a union of conditions with different anchors. The effect belongs to the imported arrangement and the target habitat-and-channel together, verdicts are per slot, and no figure line returns a universal success fraction.

What each leads to: A produces a single "institutional fit" rating that averages away the live per-slot signal (Fig 1 :19, :22). B produces a four-condition separator, a pre-registered refuter, and an honest gap where the discriminating instrument is missing.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a *separator*, a set of conditions each of which can fail independently, stated in effect terms so a comparativist can check it without holding the framework's interiors. The sought-for shape is made refusable by writing it down here (Formula, "Direction").
