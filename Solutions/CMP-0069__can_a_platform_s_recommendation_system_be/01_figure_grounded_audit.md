## 0. The question under audit

> "Can a platform's recommendation system be modeled as part of the social network rather than as an external influence?"
>
> Source: complexity science.txt line 84, section 7, Epidemics, information, and social dynamics.

**Effect restatement.** A modeler must draw a boundary around a system that spreads information. The recommender system either (A) composes with users and ties as an actor whose state changes with what it observes, or (B) is a fixed input applied from outside, whose state is given and does not change with what it affects. The residual is the failed prediction in the slots where A and B diverge: recommender-mediated exposures, the tie changes those exposures cause, and the retraining that consumes those ties.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"): the answer we are looking for is *a boundary test with one exception*. Written down and bound, it can accrue a residual and be refused like anything else. The test is: does the recommender's state update on the behavior it affects? The exception is: if it does not, it can serve as an external anchor.

## 2. Residual and domain census

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** The residual is the failed prediction of information spread on recommender-mediated slots. Arity: two conditions compose, organic social transmission and algorithmically mediated exposure, so this is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Symmetry: the question's own wording is asymmetric. "External influence" is a one-way production or sequence (→ or ⇒ in the Operators table), while "part of the network" is a symmetric composition among peers (+). The dichotomy therefore smuggles a bond choice, not just a location choice. Scale: the recommender is a population-level selector acting on individual exposures, and individual exposures aggregate back into its ranking, so there is a membrane with a lossy round trip between two sizes (Fig 4 :30).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

- **Domain 1, the modeling convention.** Actors: the modeler, the graph, the label "inside" or "outside". Currency: descriptive convenience, coded as a boundary declaration. Anchor: **none nameable yet**. A modeling label is a convention, not something that settles a question. By Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A label fails this. No nameable anchor means this is not yet a ground-bearing domain. It is governed by Fig 10 :18 and Fig 9 :27-28, and its verdict is `general`.
- **Domain 2, the contact and habitat frame.** Actors: users, content items, the recommender system, platform instrumentation. Currency: exposure and attention, that is, what acts on what and what it acts on. Anchor: the observed cascades and randomized exposure tests, which are nameable and do not move when the modeler relabels a component. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 6). The recommender is in contact with the residual, so it belongs in the enumerated habitat.
- **Domain 3, the two-frame and closure frame.** Actors: the social-network model frame and the recommender model frame. Currency: predictions, and the independence between the two frames. Anchor: the same cascade ground, ranged against both frames separately (Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 plus Fig 8 plus Fig 2). The two frames have different closures; a learning recommender is a frame, not a pinned anchor.

**Partition or union.** The inside/outside question presumes a partition (one or the other). Fig 9 :4 says *"First: is there a fact here?"* and the compound splits into two conditions that compose, so the honest reading is a **union**. Unbundle before searching: model organic transmission and recommender-mediated exposure as two frames meeting at a described residual. Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Domain 1 first: settle the boundary as a convention and describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Domain 2 second: enumerate the habitat from contact (Fig 11 :44). Domain 3 third: decide closure, import, and whether the two frames can be triangulated independently (Fig 6 :23, Fig 2 :31). This is the dependency order, not an order of importance. Link 3 to 4 of Fig 3 is the relevant break: having terms for "inside" and "outside" is a vocabulary, not an objective for predicting spread.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - "Inside versus outside" is a declared boundary, not a ground fact (general, triangulated)

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*, followed at :19 by *"→ install what can be violated"*. Fig 4 :21: *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*

Labeling the recommender "external" declares a meaning. It becomes a ground fact only if a test can violate it. Fig 4 says the boundary between sizes is a filter with a direction, not a wall, so a categorical inside/outside wall is the wrong object. Two figures with low overlap (a seat rule about conventions and a membrane rule about crossings) give the same verdict.

**Refuter:** show that switching the label changes what the ground returns on the residual slots while everything else is held fixed. If the label is load-bearing for prediction, it is a ground fact and this finding falls.

### F2 - The recommender is an actor in the habitat (specified, triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :61-62: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*

The recommender acts on what users see, and what users see acts back on the recommender's training data. It is in contact with the spread residual. The habitat is read off contact, so the recommender is in it. Calling it "external" is an attempt to subtract an actor from the habitat, which the figure says is unavailable. Triangulated across Fig 11 (habitat enumeration) and Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Low overlap: one figure governs where actors come from, the other governs what moves a boundary.

**Refuter:** a controlled experiment showing recommender exposure has no effect on the spread residual, so it has no contact and is not in the habitat. This is the honest way the external model could win.

### F3 - A recommender that updates on users is a frame, not an anchor (specified, triangulated)

Fig 2 :25: *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"*. Fig 2 :4: *"collapse 1: mutual update"*. Fig 2 :14-15: *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

The anchor is the thing that settles the question and does not change because a frame thinks something. A real recommender retrains on the behavior it induces. That is mutual update, collapse 1. Treating it as a fixed external force while it updates on users is exactly the coherent drift the figure warns about: nobody notices because the drift is smooth. Triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor and collapse rules) and Fig 6 :23 (the bound moves only on contact with a differing closure), which are different figures with different jobs.

**Refuter:** show the recommender's ranking is invariant to the user behavior it affects, that is, it is genuinely pinned. Then the external model is licensed and this finding reverses. This is the exception the answer carries.

### F4 - It is a compound to unbundle, not a dichotomy to choose (general, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4: *"First: is there a fact here?"* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

Organic transmission and algorithmically mediated exposure are two conditions, each with its own closure. Fig 9 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. So the question "which one is it?" is malformed. Describe the residual in effect terms first; only then choose a frame. Triangulated with Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

**Refuter:** show the two conditions draw on the same underlying evidence completely, so their union surplus is zero (Fig 8 :13: *"their union adds nothing"*). Then one frame suffices and the compound reading falls.

### F5 - The two frames must be independent and ranged against one ground (general, triangulated)

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

If the network model and the recommender model are built from the same logs and the same feature pipeline, their agreement is the pool talking, not corroboration. They must be ranged against the ground separately. Fig 6 :12 *"source gate: provenance"* and Fig 6 :13 *"shadow test: explains a gap"* give the import discipline if one frame borrows a term from the other.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that the two frames cannot be ranged against a common ground at all, for example that no shared measurement exists for recommender-mediated exposure. Then triangulation is impossible and the two-frame verdict is `gap`, not `specified`.

### F6 - No new primitive is owed to put the recommender inside (specified, triangulated)

Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*

Adding the recommender to the model is re-enumerating a habitat, not minting a seventh primitive or importing an atom that moves a wall. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A model change that leaves contact unchanged is not a capability change. Triangulated across Fig 6 (closure) and Fig 11 (habitat), low overlap.

**Refuter:** show that representing the recommender inside the network requires a primitive not derivable from the six-term substrate or the existing network vocabulary, so an import that moves the wall really is owed.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (the exemplar's Seat Map citation, 2026-11-22). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A controlled experiment shows recommender exposure is independent of user behavior and network state, so the recommender is a pinned anchor (Fig 2 :25) and the external model predicts the residual slots as well as the co-evolution model.
2. A controlled experiment shows the recommender has no contact with the spread residual, so it is not in the habitat (Fig 11 :44).
3. A re-census shows the two frames have identical closures, so the union surplus is zero (Fig 8 :13) and there is no compound to unbundle.
4. Representing the recommender as internal requires a new primitive that moves the wall (Fig 6 :25).

If any fires, the criterion-and-exception answer is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the boundary test, not with "inside" or "outside": does the recommender update on the behavior it affects? Repair the question before answering it (F1, F4).
2. Enumerate the recommender as an actor in the habitat of the spread residual (F2). This is the default modeling move.
3. Reserve the external model for the pinned case, and state that exception explicitly (F3). Most deployed recommenders retrain, so the exception is narrow.
4. Range the network frame and the recommender frame against the same ground separately, and keep their evidence pools independent (F5).
5. Offer the two-stage check: stage 1 the shape and its refuter, stage 2 the links only if the reader opts in. Fig 4 :21: a filter with a direction, not a wall.

## 7. Strains, re-census, and round trip

- **Fig 4 "not a wall" versus the question's hard inside/outside wall.** Re-census: Fig 4 governs the boundary between sizes. The question's dichotomy governs a modelling convention (Domain 1). Different objects, so no strain after re-census.
- **Fig 2 "anchor must not update" versus real recommenders that update.** Not a contradiction. It is the criterion that decides which modeling option is licensed. It generates the exception rather than breaking the verdict.
- **Fig 11 habitat versus Fig 6 closure.** Both say contact matters. Re-census keeps them separate: Fig 11 says where actors come from, Fig 6 says what moves a frame's wall. Their agreement is weak corroboration across two figures with low overlap, still one framework.
- **After re-census: no hidden strains.** The one live limit is that no ground reading exists, reported as `gap` for effect sizes rather than smoothed into a number.

**Round trip gap R_T.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 6 and Fig 4 :30 require the lossy condensation to be bracketed: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Sent up (x): the verbatim figure set on membranes, habitat, anchor, and closure. Abstracted (T_A): "recommender in habitat versus external pinned anchor". Brought back down (T_E): a concrete platform model of cascade prediction. Three constraints do not survive the trip: (i) the membrane's direction, that is what crosses up and what comes down; (ii) the independence requirement between the two frames; (iii) the anchor's non-updating condition. One thing survives cleanly: an actor in contact with the residual belongs in the habitat. R_T is therefore nonzero and recorded qualitatively as that enumerated set of dropped constraints. It was not measured numerically because no ground reading exists. Treating a condensed summary as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

**Status:** the shape survives the audit; the specific modeling claim is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Strained after re-census: none hidden.
