## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 (local extract): *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, read this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name a platform-specific instance. It constrains; it does not fill. For CMP-0069 the shape is **a boundary test with one exception**: an actor in contact with the spread residual belongs in the modeled habitat, and the external model is licensed only where the actor is pinned. Naming a particular recommender architecture as "the answer" would be a name, not a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained for this question

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have *"closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of"*, then look for *"commonly recurring new terms"* that overlap or correlate, and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. So do not begin with "recommender system" as a named object. Begin with the effect: exposure changes, tie changes, and retraining that consumes the ties.
- From *The Library of Babel* on steering: *"The books that you are returning in your searches need to be tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search."* This is Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- From *The Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox that by then you have *"already composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the vocabulary bootstrap starts with "something changes what users see", moves through "that something is in contact with exposure and ties", and ends at "the deciding property is whether its state updates on the behavior it affects". The search stops when the question and the boundary test point at the same thing.

## 3. What licenses a name, and why this question does not need one

Only two things license a name, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The canonical README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test: Fig 6 :12 *"source gate: provenance"* and :13 *"shadow test: explains a gap"*.

The five world sources in `04_links_and_distribution.md` are exactly such provenanced imports. They name a co-evolution loop, but they are secondary corroboration of the shape, not the source of the verdict.

**Correction to my own first reading.** I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming the recommender as part of the habitat. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase governs a *missing* primitive, one not held in Γ. The recommender is not a framework primitive at all; it is an actor enumerated from contact (Fig 11 :43-44). Placing it in the habitat is not inventing a seventh term and not an import that moves a wall (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*). What remains forbidden is naming a particular platform mechanism as *the* answer before a ground settles it. Both the narrowed form and the original overstatement are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 4. Why the source question matters here

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session, gives the mechanism: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. It was *"proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: observing a cascade and observing that exposure was algorithmically mediated is a projection. Several mechanisms can produce the same cascade shape: organic homophily, algorithmic amplification, a common external event, or a mix. Naming the recommender as the source from the cascade reading alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, which in this field means a randomized or otherwise exogenous variation in the recommender's signal. That is also why the pinned-anchor exception in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F3 is a real exception: only a genuinely exogenous signal separates source from projection.

## 5. Distinction the shape must preserve

The shape must keep three things apart that the question's wording blends:

- **A boundary convention** (is the component inside the model or outside it). Declared meaning, not grounded (Fig 10 :18).
- **A contact fact** (does the component act on the residual). Ground-visible, enumerable (Fig 11 :44).
- **An anchor property** (does the component's state update on what it affects). Decides which modeling option is licensed (Fig 2 :25).

Collapsing these into one "inside or outside" choice is what makes the question look like a dichotomy. A shape keeps them as three separate terms.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A list of well-known researchers who agree with the boundary is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to be tried: a check against a platform's own data.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim, it cannot settle anything it reports on, and it must be labelled as such. This is stated again in `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md`.
