## What the wider literature contributes

The five world sources were chosen for low overlap with each other and with the framework. They come from five different research groups in network simulation, causal inference, machine-learning ethics, software quality, and web science. None cites the figures.

- **Direct corroboration of the modeling question.** Koley and Digrajkar name the exact dichotomy in the question: prior simulators either freeze the topology or *"treat recommendations as exogenous inputs"*. Their contribution is a closed loop in which *"recommendations alter content exposure, which changes tie formation, which updates the graph the GAT trains on."* This is the world-side version of Fig 11 habitat enumeration.
- **The anchor test.** Krauth, Wang and Jordan show that a recommender *"influences the user behavioral data that is subsequently used to update the recommender itself"*. That is Fig 2 collapse 1 in the language of causal inference. Their CAFL method is an attempt to reason about intervention distributions, which is the ground-facing move.
- **The co-evolution claim.** Jiang et al. and Fabbri et al. independently model the loop in which user decisions change the system's future input. They are two more witnesses that the recommender's state is not pinned.
- **Context of use.** Khritankov: *"We study systems in their context of use, because both learning algorithms and user interactions are important."* This is Fig 11 :39 in an engineering field: capability is a property of agent-and-habitat.
- **The legitimate external case.** The news-recommender experiments located in the Journal of Communication (abstract read, full text access-limited) treat a recommender as a designed choice environment in preregistered experiments. That is the pinned-anchor exception, not a refutation of the habitat claim. It is recorded as abstract-level and carries less weight.

No world source contradicts the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Because the five sources are independent groups, their agreement is stronger than the exemplar's author-overlapping essays. It is still corroboration only: the verdict comes from the figures.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the shape and its refuter first. The framework interior comes later, and only if asked for.
- **Fig 4 :21** *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* The boundary between the reader's frame and ours is a filter, so decide what crosses: methods and head starts, not recordings of a conclusion.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before sending the article.
- **Fig 2 :31** *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Do not select recipients for likely agreement. A list chosen for receptiveness is one frame wearing many names.
- **Fig 2 :42** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a wrong boundary that hides a feedback loop, so the check is worth a real independent reader.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the output with its mode; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figure text, not the summary, is the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** The residual, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. No framework. The reader checks the output against their own ground.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, the figure text and the independent world sources. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded and is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :35, and Fig 2 :31 on independence).
3. **Stage 3.** The formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. That is what the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 is for. If it fires, narrow the claim and do not send.
