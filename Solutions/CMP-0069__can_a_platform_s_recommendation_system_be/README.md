## The question restated as an effect

A modeler of information spread on a platform must decide where to put the recommender system. Option A: inside the network, as an actor whose state co-evolves with ties and content. Option B: outside the network, as a fixed forcing term applied to it from a separate closure. What each leads to: A admits the recommender into the enumerated habitat and exposes the feedback loop between exposure, tie formation, and retraining; B treats the recommender as a pinned anchor and hides that loop, so predictions fail in exactly the slots where users and the recommender co-adapt. The ordinary term we bind for the run is *the test that decides the boundary*: does the recommender's state update on the behavior it affects? An answer would look like a criterion plus an exception, not a yes or no.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Five world sources from five research groups are several witnesses; five pages quoting one review are one witness.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So stage 1 carries the residual and its refuter, and the framework appears only if the reader opts in after recording a verdict.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in submission venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
