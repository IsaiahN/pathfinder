## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question names one driver and three receivers. In effect terms:

> A condition in contact with a habitat (sea ice) is being removed faster than the frames that depend on it can re-fit. What is the reach of that removal, and in which currency does each receiver register it?

Stated as a residual `R`: the gap between what the current vocabulary predicts for the Arctic system and what the observations are returning. It is not a single global number.

**Arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22 names these as the three descriptors of `R`).**

- **Arity:** one driver, three receiving domains, plus two feedbacks (human activity to emissions; ice-albedo to the driver itself). Structurally a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Ice loss is a benefactor to some actors (shipping, some boreal fish) and a malfactor to others (ice-obligate species, subsistence communities). Valence is not carried by the actor. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Scale:** first-order contact, then second- and third-order cascade (Fig 11 :52-54), and a cross-scale transform from a local surface condition to hemispheric circulation (Fig 4 membrane). The effect is indexed by slot, not aggregated. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66).** An acceptable answer must give, for each receiving domain: actors in contact, the currency of the effect, a nameable anchor, a figure line governing the shape, and a refuter. A candidate that gives only an averaged "impact of sea-ice loss" fails this shape and is refused before any search.

## 2. Findings, with verbatim figure lines quoted before concluding

### F1 - The question must be split; an averaged impact is disallowed (governs the whole residual)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The three receivers have different currencies (biomass, energy, cost/meaning). There is no common unit in which an "average Arctic impact" would be a quantity. A single lever (ice removal) clears single-condition regions and leaves the intersections, so a compound answer is owed.

**Strain test:** applying Fig 9's split to D0 itself would strain, because D0 is one measured condition, not a bundle. Re-census: the split governs the *three receiving domains*, not the driver. No strain after re-census.

### F2 - D0 is ground-visible; the framework does not derive it (upstream link)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

The satellite passive-microwave record is an anchor for D0. It is not figure-derived; it is read off the world. Meier et al. (NOAA ARC 2025, Sea Ice essay, opened 2026-11-22) report the lowest annual maximum extent in the 47-year satellite record in March 2025, the 10th lowest minimum in September 2025, and *"There is almost no old (>4 years) multi-year ice remaining in the Arctic"*. NSIDC's Sea Ice Index states: *"The monthly Sea Ice Index provides a quick look at Arctic-wide changes in sea ice. It is a source for consistently processed ice extent and concentration images and data values since 1979."*

**Mode:** `grounded` for the observed trend, from the satellite anchor. The figures supply only the rule that an anchor must not update (Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*).

**Overlap warning:** the NOAA Sea Ice essay is authored by NSIDC staff and explicitly uses the NSIDC Sea Ice Index as its data source. These are one measurement lineage, not two independent witnesses. They are recorded as high overlap in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

### F3 - D1 is a contact cascade, not a list of species (governs ecosystems)

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
Fig 11 :52-54: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
Fig 11 :49-50: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* / *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

The ecosystem verdict is therefore a shape: enumerate the habitat, follow contact outward, expect second- and third-order effects, and refuse any standing list of "good" or "bad" species. The world returns the names. Frey et al. (NOAA ARC 2025, Primary Productivity essay) report that *"higher primary productivity may enhance carbon availability to food webs in some cases, important negative impacts to ecosystem health also result from trophic mismatches that stymie the movement of carbon through the food chain as well as the increasing prevalence of harmful algal blooms"* and that *"sea-ice obligate species at higher trophic levels (e.g., Arctic cod, Pacific walruses, and several species of ice seals) can be severely impacted by declining sea ice regardless of food availability."* Wise et al. (NOAA ARC 2025, Borealization essay) report that *"Warming temperatures, declining sea ice, and shifting productivity in the Chukchi and northern Bering Seas drive ecosystem changes with significant implications for fisheries, food security, and Indigenous subsistence"*, with roughly one third of boreal taxa increasing and one third of Arctic taxa declining.

This is not one direction. Productivity rises in eight of nine regions while ice-obligate species lose habitat and boreal taxa replace Arctic taxa. That is exactly the valence-relativity Fig 11 :49-50 predicts, and it is why no averaged ecosystem verdict is admitted.

**Strain test:** Fig 11 governs actors, conditions and relations in a habitat. Applying it to D2 (atmospheric circulation) would strain, because pressure and gradients are not habitat actors in the same sense. Re-census sends D2 to Fig 13 and Fig 12. No strain remains.

### F4 - D2 is a gradient-driven flow with a closing feedback (governs weather)

Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

Sea ice loss changes a state (energy absorbed at the surface) and a structure (the albedo gradient and the ocean-atmosphere heat-flux gradient). A flow composes across those kinds, and the ice-albedo loop is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes. Meier et al.: *"Its high albedo due to its bright, near-white surface reduces the absorption of solar energy, and as a physical barrier sea ice modulates the heat and moisture transfer between the atmosphere and ocean."* Ballinger et al. (NOAA ARC 2025, Surface Air Temperature essay) report that *"In autumn and winter, when Arctic Amplification is strongest, Arctic Ocean heat accumulated from increased solar energy absorption amid summer sea-ice losses ... is released to the overlying atmosphere, driving warmer air temperatures"* and that amplified Arctic warming has *"impacts extending to societies in lower latitudes"*.

**Triangulation status:** the mechanism (gradient composes a flow; loop sign governs closure) is triangulated across Fig 13 and Fig 12, two figures with low overlap in subject matter (substrate composition versus bond topology). The **magnitude of any midlatitude teleconnection is `specified`, not `grounded`, and is contested**. Ballinger et al. cite a literature of "divergent consensuses" on Arctic amplification and midlatitude severe winter weather (Cohen et al. 2020, as cited in that essay). I did not open Cohen et al.; I attribute the characterization only to the NOAA essay's own framing and do not treat it as a corroborating source.

**Strain test:** Fig 12 :121 describes a cycle. Applying it to D1's trophic cascade would strain, because a food chain is a sequence/network, not a closed loop by construction. Re-census leaves the cycle with D2 and gives D1 the network and dependency relations of Fig 11. No strain after re-census.

### F5 - D3 is valenced, asymmetric, and permanent work (governs human activity)

Fig 11 :49-50 as above (valence relative to the residual).
Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
Fig 2 :36-37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

Shipping and access increase; food security and subsistence practices are disrupted. Meier et al.: *"Sea ice plays a key role in polar ecosystems, providing an essential habitat for marine life and is an essential component of life and culture in Indigenous communities of the North."* And: *"Changing sea ice extent and thickness is allowing increased marine traffic and prompting reevaluations of national security concerns."* Wise et al. report effects on *"North Pacific commercial fisheries, Arctic food security, and Indigenous subsistence practices."*

The human domain has a legitimate human anchor (Fig 2 :36-37), so its cost verdicts are not settled by the figures alone. The figures govern the *shape*: asymmetric valence, introduction without exclusion, permanent management. They do not set the value of a livelihood.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The union-and-cascade answer is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The three receiving domains can be written as one residual with one currency and one anchor such that no slot is hidden by aggregation (breaks F1 and Fig 1 :22).
2. A downstream change is observed that is first-order, attributable to a single lever, and has no second- or third-order contact anywhere in its habitat (breaks F3 and Fig 11 :52-54).
3. The ice-albedo loop is shown, at the relevant scale, to be net damping rather than amplifying, so the signed loop does not close as a positive feedback (breaks F4 and Fig 12 :121).
4. A controlled attribution result shows zero contribution of sea-ice loss to the midlatitude circulation response, in which case the weather-domain magnitude verdict is withdrawn (F4).
5. A domain is found for which no anchor can be named after a genuine enumeration (breaks the census rule; that domain is then not a domain yet).

If any fires, the affected domain verdict is withdrawn and re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled a framework-produced `grounded` forecast.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Split the question into D1, D2 and D3 before any search. Keep the verdicts separate and never average them (F1).
2. Treat D0 as the anchor and the upstream link, measured by the satellite record, not derived from the figures (F2).
3. For D1, enumerate the habitat outward and report the cascade and the valence relativity; never publish a species list as the answer (F3).
4. For D2, state the cross-kind flow and the signed loop; mark the midlatitude magnitude `specified` and contested (F4).
5. For D3, state the asymmetry and permanent work; let human judgment anchor value claims (F5).
6. Lead outreach with the shape and the refuter, not the theory (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*). Use only the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
7. Record the lineage: what was unreachable, what was imported (the world measurements), and what would reverse it (Section 3).
