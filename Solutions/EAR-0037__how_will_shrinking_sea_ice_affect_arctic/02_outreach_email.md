**Subject:** Three small claims about sea-ice loss, and what would break each one

> Hello [name],
>
> I am writing about one question with three parts: how shrinking sea ice affects Arctic ecosystems, weather, and human activity. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not a numeric forecast).** This is not one question with one average answer. It is one measured driver and three receiving domains, each with its own currency and its own way of being settled. The driver is the sea-ice condition itself, which is measured directly by satellite since 1979. The three receivers behave differently:
>
> 1. **Ecosystems.** The effect is a contact cascade. You enumerate what is in contact with the ice, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. The rule in the framework I work from is explicit that a first-order list can miss a third-order effect, which is how a pesticide reached eagles through a food chain. It also says an actor's valence is relative to the effect being tracked, not a property the actor carries. So a rise in primary productivity and a loss of ice-obligate species are not a contradiction; they are two slots.
> 2. **Weather.** The mechanism is a cross-kind composition: a changed energy state and a changed albedo/heat-flux gradient compose a flow, and the ice-albedo loop is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes. I am **not** claiming a settled magnitude for any midlatitude teleconnection. The assessment I read describes those consensuses as divergent.
> 3. **Human activity.** The effect is asymmetric and valenced. Shipping and access grow while subsistence, food security, and cultural continuity are stressed. The shape is permanent work rather than a solved state. I am not assigning a number to any of it.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not a single averaged "impact of sea-ice loss"; not a numeric forecast; not that the framework derives sea-ice decline (that comes from the satellite anchor); not that the midlatitude weather link is settled; not that any species or industry is uniformly good or bad. Averages across unlike currencies hide the live signal.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these before writing the rest.**
> 1. The three receivers can be written as one residual with one currency and one anchor, with no slot hidden by aggregation.
> 2. A downstream change is observed that is first-order, attributable to one lever, and has no second- or third-order contact anywhere in its habitat.
> 3. The ice-albedo loop is net damping at the relevant scale rather than amplifying.
> 4. Controlled attribution shows zero contribution of sea-ice loss to the midlatitude circulation response.
>
> If any one holds, the relevant domain verdict is withdrawn and re-censused. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - NOAA Arctic Report Card 2025, Sea Ice essay (Meier et al.): https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/sea-ice-2025/
> - NOAA Arctic Report Card 2025, Primary Productivity essay (Frey et al.): https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2025/arctic-ocean-primary-productivity-the-response-of-marine-algae-to-climate-warming-and-sea-ice-decline-2025/
> - IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 2 (Parmesan et al.): https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-2/
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
