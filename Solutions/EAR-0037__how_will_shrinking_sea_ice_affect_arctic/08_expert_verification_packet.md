# Expert verification packet - the Tether on shrinking sea ice

**What this is:** a claim in a form an Arctic scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shapes, `grounded` on the observed sea-ice record and on quoted within-domain directions. Not a numeric forecast.**

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How will shrinking sea ice affect Arctic ecosystems, weather, and human activity?" is a union of one measured driver and three receiving domains, not one question. Each receiver has its own currency and anchor, so the verdicts must be kept separate and never averaged. Ecosystems register the driver as a contact cascade with second- and third-order reach and valence relative to the residual, which is why higher primary productivity and losses of ice-obligate species are both expected. Weather registers it as a cross-kind composition, an energy state and an albedo/heat-flux gradient composing a flow, closed by a signed ice-albedo loop; the framework does not set the magnitude of any midlatitude teleconnection. Human activity registers it asymmetrically, with access growing and subsistence and cultural continuity stressed, and with value anchored in human judgment. The figures return these shapes and written refuters; the measurements return the names and numbers.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Ecosystem reach and valence (checkable against marine-ecology monitoring)

- **Statement:** the ecosystem effect is a contact cascade, not a species list; valence is relative to the residual, so co-occurring increases and losses are expected rather than contradictory.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44 - *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-54 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** in a system you know, does enumerating only direct contacts miss a second- or third-order effect? Can one actor be a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another? Does the NOAA primary-productivity finding (productivity up, ice-obligate species down) fit the shape better than a single-direction claim?
- **Refuter:** a downstream change that is first-order, attributable to one lever, and has no second- or third-order contact anywhere in its habitat. Then C1 fails.

### C2 - Weather mechanism without a magnitude claim (checkable against attribution literature)

- **Statement:** the weather domain is a cross-kind flow plus a signed loop; no midlatitude magnitude is asserted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :74 - *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 13 :76 - *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **What to check:** is the albedo/heat-flux change a gradient over a state, composing a flow, rather than a direct state-state bond? Is the ice-albedo loop genuinely a cycle that a causal chain cannot write? Does the opened assessment (Ballinger et al.) actually describe divergent consensuses on the midlatitude response, so that withholding a magnitude is honest?
- **Refuter:** a controlled attribution result showing zero sea-ice contribution to the midlatitude circulation response removes the domain's magnitude verdict; a demonstration that the albedo loop is net damping at the relevant scale breaks the cycle claim. Both are live.

### C3 - Human asymmetry and the anchor for value (checkable against records and judgment)

- **Statement:** the human effect is asymmetric and valenced, requires permanent management, and its value claims are anchored in human judgment, not in the figures.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 - *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63 - *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Fig 2 :36-37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
- **What to check:** do the opened sources support both sides (more marine traffic and national-security reevaluation in Meier et al.; stressed food security and Indigenous subsistence in Meier et al. and Wise et al.)? Is a managed Arctic system a maintained, ongoing activity rather than a solved state? Is a value judgment about a fishery or a community appropriately settled by human judgment rather than by a figure?
- **Refuter:** a domain in which no anchor can be named after genuine enumeration breaks the census rule; a case where the supposedly asymmetric introduction is actually reversible by exclusion breaks the Fig 11 :61 reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not one averaged "impact of sea-ice loss", and not a numeric forecast from the figures.
- Not that the framework derives the observed sea-ice decline; that comes from the satellite anchor.
- Not that any species or industry is uniformly good or bad.
- Not that the midlatitude weather magnitude is settled. It is `specified` and contested.
- Not that the figures settle what a livelihood or a cultural practice is worth. Fig 2 :36-37 puts that anchor in human judgment.
- Not `grounded` as a whole; individual rows are `grounded`, the shapes are `specified`.
- Not a complete answer. Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 5. What would settle it

1. A contact census that finds or fails to find the predicted second- and third-order ecosystem reach, with attribution separating ice loss from warming and borealization.
2. Controlled attribution of the midlatitude circulation response, plus an instrument resolving the sign of the ice-albedo loop.
3. Transit records, food-security studies and community records for the human domain, with human judgment anchoring value.
4. The five standing falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` Section 3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-44, :49, :52-54 and Fig 13 :74, :76 and Fig 12 :121 (six short passages).
2. Name one second- or third-order effect in your domain that a direct-contact list would miss, or say there is none.
3. Say whether the midlatitude magnitude is presently settled in your field. If it is, C2's withheld magnitude is wrong and should be withdrawn.
4. Name the anchor you would use for a cost or value claim in D3, and say whether it is human judgment, a record, or both.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
