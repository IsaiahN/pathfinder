# The Tether's answer on shrinking sea ice

**Mode: `specified` on the figure-derived shapes, `grounded` on the observed record and on measured within-domain directions quoted from the public sources. Not a numeric forecast. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## 1. The residual it answers

One driver, the changing sea-ice condition, is measured directly. Three receivers, ecosystems, weather and human activity, register it in different currencies. The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :55: *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single averaged impact would hide a live slot: Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

## 2. The shape, domain by domain

**D0, the driver (upstream, not derived).** The satellite passive-microwave record since 1979 is an anchor: Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Meier et al. report the lowest March maximum extent in the 47-year record in 2025, the 10th lowest September minimum, and more than 95% loss of ice older than four years since the 1980s. This is `grounded` from the measurement, not from the figures.

**D1, ecosystems (contact cascade).** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-54: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* The shape is a cascade with second- and third-order reach, and with valence relative to the residual: Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The world returns mixed directions: primary productivity up in eight of nine regions (Frey et al.) while ice-obligate species are severely impacted regardless of food availability (Frey et al.) and Arctic taxa decline as boreal taxa move north (Wise et al.). No single ecosystem direction is asserted.

**D2, weather (cross-kind flow plus signed loop).** Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Ice loss changes an energy state and an albedo/heat-flux gradient; these compose a flow, and the ice-albedo loop is a cycle whose sign governs closure. The mechanism is figure-governed. The **magnitude of a midlatitude teleconnection is `specified` and contested**, not settled: Ballinger et al. describe divergent consensuses on midlatitude severe winter weather. The figures do not hand us that number.

**D3, human activity (asymmetric, valenced, permanent work).** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Shipping, access and security reevaluation grow (Meier et al.) while food security, subsistence and cultural continuity are stressed (Meier et al.; Wise et al.). Value claims are anchored in human judgment: Fig 2 :36-37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not one averaged impact, and not a numeric forecast of any downstream quantity produced by the figures.
- Not that the framework derives sea-ice decline. That comes from the satellite anchor and the authors who maintain it.
- Not that any species or industry is uniformly good or bad. Valence is relative to the residual.
- Not that the midlatitude weather link has a settled magnitude. It is `specified`, contested, and owed to attribution studies.
- Not `grounded` as a whole. Individual rows are `grounded`; the shapes are `specified`.
- Not a complete answer. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 4. What would settle it, and the honest stop

The figures return the shape and the refuter, not the numbers. What would settle each domain is a ground reading:

1. **D1:** a long-term contact-census that either finds or fails to find the predicted second- and third-order reach, with attribution that separates sea-ice loss from warming and borealization.
2. **D2:** controlled attribution experiments that quantify the sea-ice contribution to circulation, plus an instrument that resolves the sign and strength of the ice-albedo loop at the relevant scale.
3. **D3:** maritime transit records, food-security studies and community records, with human judgment as the anchor for value claims (Fig 2 :36-37).
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01` Section 3:** one currency and one anchor for all three receivers; a first-order effect with no cascade; a net-damping albedo loop; zero sea-ice contribution to midlatitude circulation; a domain with no nameable anchor.

The stop is honest: *unreached*, not *unreachable*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* More composition inside the figure closure will not produce the numbers; new ground contact is owed, and the lineage records exactly what was imported. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 5. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Shrinking sea ice is one measured driver with three different receivers, so the honest answer is three shapes, not one averaged impact. Ecosystems register it as a contact cascade: enumerate what touches the ice and then what touches that, because first-order lists miss third-order effects, and a rise in productivity can coexist with the loss of ice-obligate species. Weather registers it as a cross-kind flow, energy state plus albedo gradient, closed by a feedback loop whose sign decides whether it amplifies; the framework does not settle the size of any midlatitude teleconnection. Human activity registers it asymmetrically: shipping and access grow while food security, subsistence and culture are stressed, and value judgments are anchored in human judgment rather than in the figures. Each shape has a written refuter. The figures give the form and the stop; the measurements give the names and numbers.

## 6. Verdict summary

- **D0:** `grounded`, from the satellite anchor. Decline in extent, age and thickness is observed.
- **D1:** `specified` shape (cascade, valence relativity), triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 1; directions `grounded` from NOAA essays, with high overlap declared.
- **D2:** `specified` shape (cross-kind flow, signed cycle), triangulated across Fig 13 and Fig 12; midlatitude magnitude contested and ungrounded.
- **D3:** `specified` shape (asymmetry, permanent work), triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 2; costs `grounded` where records exist, valued by human judgment.
- **Whole:** a shape and a route, not a result. No domain verdict is averaged with another.
