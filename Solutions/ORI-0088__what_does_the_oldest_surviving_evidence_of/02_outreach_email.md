**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive a reader's check. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

**Subject:** One claim about the oldest evidence of life, checked before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** The oldest surviving evidence of life is a reading in the present of an ancient rock: a laminated structure, an isotopic fractionation, a microfossil. What does that reading license, and what does it leave open?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The reading establishes a **lower bound** (life, or a biological-grade organisation, was present by that date and in that setting) and it **constrains the shape** of any acceptable origin account (the account must produce that signature by then). It does not establish the source process, the exact timing of the origin, whether there was one origin or several, or the identity of the first organism. A reading is a projection, and a projection cannot comment on its source. Several sources can give the same reading, so the source is underdetermined by the reading alone.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming the oldest rock marks the moment life began. The fossil is a bound, not the origin; the last universal common ancestor is not the first life. I am not claiming that biogenicity is settled for every famous specimen: the 3.7 Ga Isua structures were reported as stromatolites in 2016 and reinterpreted as deformation structures in 2018. I am not claiming that no abiotic process can ever be excluded: for a specific feature, abiotic causes must be eliminated before the feature counts as a biosignature.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. An instrument that discriminates the origin process from the ancient reading alone, with a published demonstration, would close the gap I am reporting as open.
> 2. A closed argument eliminating all plausible abiotic causes for a specific feature would move that feature from contested to established (it would not fill in the origin).
> 3. A demonstration that every claimed oldest instance is demonstrably abiotic would collapse the lower bound.
> 4. A measurement that fixes the exact origin time from a single rock reading would refute the bound-only claim.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is a method, not an answer. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]
