# The Tether's answer on the oldest surviving evidence of life

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural limit, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken in this derivation. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The residual it answers

The reading of an ancient rock and the source process that produced it are not the same object. The question asks which conclusions the reading licenses. The gap is a many-to-one, lossy map: several sources can give the same reading, so the source is underdetermined by the reading alone. Per Fig 9 :54, the question is a union that must be unbundled before search, not one hard problem.

## 2. The shape

1. **It establishes a lower bound.** Biological-grade organisation was present by the date of the oldest accepted signature, in that setting. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The dated rock is such an anchor. The literature phrases it conditionally: *"Unless some unknown abiotic process exists..., our results provide evidence for the emergence of life on Earth by at least 3,800 Myr before present"* (Mojzsis et al. 1996, quoted in the Wikipedia *Isua Greenstone Belt* article). A bound is a legitimate `specified` result.
2. **It constrains the shape of any origin account.** Whatever happened must have been capable of producing the observed signature by then, in that environment. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* This is a filter on candidate stories, not a verdict about them.
3. **It does not establish the source.** A projection cannot comment on its source (*The Hard Problem is About Source*). One carbon-isotope reading is consistent with biological fixation, Fischer-Tropsch synthesis, metamorphic redistribution or abiogenic precipitation. The Wikipedia *Earliest known life forms* article states: *"abiotic processes can fractionate isotopes and produce similar signatures to biotic processes."*
4. **It does not establish the exact origin time.** The fossil is a floor, not the event. *"LUCA is not thought to be the first life on Earth, but rather the only type of organism of its time to still have living descendants"* (Wikipedia). The NASA *Ask an Astrobiologist* FAQ: *"We may have lost too much information over the course of evolutionary history to ever know exactly what LUCA looked like."*
5. **It does not establish one origin rather than several**, nor the identity of the first organism. Those are separate conditions in the union (Fig 9 :54).
6. **Biogenicity itself is decided by elimination, not resemblance.** Wikipedia *Biosignature*: *"A biosignature is a phenomenon that can be explained by biological processes where all possible abiotic causes of this phenomenon have been eliminated."* The 2016 Isua claim was reassessed in 2018: *"Here we report new research that shows a non-biological, post-depositional origin for the structures"* (Allwood et al., *Nature* 563).
7. **The gap is `unreached at this budget`, not `unreachable in principle`.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The remedy is an instrument, not another argument.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the oldest rock is the origin event.
- Not that a named pathway (RNA world, metabolism first, vents, clay, panspermia) is *the* source. Those are bindings from other frames, provenanced as such, not derivations (Fig 6 :22).
- Not that biogenicity is settled for every famous specimen. The Isua history is the counterexample.
- Not that no abiotic cause can ever be excluded. The *Biosignature* criterion says the opposite: exclusion is the work.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a new measurement.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; the case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here:

- **Biogenicity test:** for a named feature, state the environmental model and enumerate the abiotic causes eliminated. The 2016/2018 Isua sequence is the worked example: a better instrument changed the reading.
- **Age test:** radiometric dating of the hosting unit, with the metamorphic overprint stated (Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*).
- **Source test (still open):** an instrument that returns different readings for biological and abiotic pathways starting from the same ancient signature. None appears in the sources read. When one exists, D2 becomes a domain with a nameable anchor.
- **Standing refuters:** the five items in `01` §4 and the falsifier in `05` §7, written before this conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *How Intelligence Actually Works* calls the analogous state a union horizon: *"Being 'below the union horizon' is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet."* The six-term set and the field's vocabulary are visible choices, not nature's own list (*They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible"*). The answer stops at the limit of available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The oldest surviving evidence of life establishes a lower bound, not an origin story. It tells us that biological-grade organisation was present by a certain date in a certain setting, and it constrains the shape of any account of how life began: that account must be capable of producing the observed signature by then. It does not tell us when life actually began, which pathway produced the signature, whether there was one origin or several, or what the first organism was. The reason is structural: a rock signature is a reading, and a reading cannot comment on its source. Several processes can produce the same light carbon or the same lamination. The 2016 report of stromatolites in the 3.7 Ga Isua rocks and the 2018 reinterpretation of those same structures as deformation are not a contradiction to be smoothed over; they are the evidence that the map from source to reading is lossy. What the Tether offers is the shape of what the evidence licenses, the four questions it does not answer, and refuters written in advance. The source slot stays open until an instrument can discriminate among the candidate processes, and a search that has not found that instrument is not a proof that none exists.

It cannot settle the question it reports on. A failure is more useful than an endorsement; send the refuter, not the conclusion.
