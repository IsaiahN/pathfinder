# Solution ORI-0088

**Question:** What does the oldest surviving evidence of life actually establish, and what does it leave unknown?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 120

**Section:** 9. Evidence, experiments, and competing explanations

**Mode:** `specified` on the shape claim (a lower bound plus a constraint), `general` on the structural limit (a reading cannot determine its source). Nothing here is `grounded`: this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual in effect terms:** A biosignature is a reading in the present of an ancient rock. The question asks which conclusions the reading licenses and which it leaves open. Option A treats the oldest evidence as establishing when and how life began. Option B treats it as setting a dated lower bound and constraining the shape of any origin account, while the source process remains unreached until a discriminating instrument arrives. A sends a verdict that inverts a lossy projection and spends the independence of the reader. B sends a checkable shape, a stated limit, and written refuters.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation settled the question. It is a simulation, not a ground reading.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Adding agreement does not add an anchor. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35)
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* (Seat Map :163, local file) Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42)

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
