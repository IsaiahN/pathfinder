# The Tether's answer: when political institutions channel conflict, and when they intensify it

**Mode: `specified` on the five conditions and the room-chain condition, `general` on the bare shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No political conflict was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each contending party slot `s`, `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` is the gap between what that party expected from the political order and what it actually got. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The question asks why `R_s` sometimes falls through an institution and sometimes grows because of it. It is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"), so the verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged into an "institutional quality" score.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the contending frames.** Fig 2 :18-21 gives the intensification mechanism: "one evidence pool", "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." **Verdict:** parties whose evidence is independent can produce a settlement that means something; parties sharing one pool produce a reading that looks like agreement while `R_s` is untouched. Mode `specified`. Triangulated, Fig 2 and Fig 8. Refuter: shared-pool settlement that settles each slot at the ground and stays settled.

**D2, the institution as anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it." **Verdict:** an institution channels conflict when it settles without updating on pressure and is not counted as a party; it intensifies when it becomes a frame and converges on whoever has most influence (Fig 2 :13-15). Mode `specified`. Triangulated, Fig 2 and Fig 10. Refuter: an institution that changes under pressure and the conflict still settles per slot.

**D3, the contact channel.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch". **Verdict:** channeling requires readings that resolve each group; intensification begins when the institution's metrics saturate or pool distinct groups and the live slot disappears into the average. Mode `specified`. Triangulated, Fig 1 and Fig 10. Refuter: an aggregate-only institution that still reduces each party's residual separately and sustainably.

**D4, the habitat and the compound.** Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." **Verdict:** no institution is peaceful in itself. Its sign depends on the live residual, and a compound conflict must be unbundled before any lever is applied. Mode `specified`. Triangulated, Fig 11 and Fig 9. Refuter: valence invariant across two changes of residual, or one lever that clears the intersections of a compound conflict.

**D5, the chain across repeated disputes.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :32: "one long room: motion, no progress." Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom." **Verdict:** a standing institution that meets a changed conflict with an unchanged composition is a mirror chain. Channeling across time needs selection between differing arenas with both fidelity and ground pressure nonzero. Mode `specified`. Triangulated, Fig 7 and Fig 10. Refuter: a fixed institution with high, non-declining fidelity and pressure across three disputes while residuals fall.

**The keeper.** Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." **Verdict:** a keeper who benefits from the answer is disqualified on the reading; a procedural rule that decides a contested outcome has ceased to be a neutral convention. Mode `specified`. Triangulated, Fig 10 and Fig 2. Refuter: an uncheckable institution with an interested keeper whose readings still settle each slot.

**Naming the institution.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". **Status: `gap`, `unreached`.** No figure line governs the choice of a named institution for a named conflict after a genuine re-census. The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, characterized in effect terms and not held. This is a verdict, not an omission.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any constitution, court, election, federal arrangement or power-sharing deal is peaceful in itself. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51).
- Not that "democracy causes peace." The field's own record says "theorists disagree about the direction of causality" (Wikipedia, Democratic peace theory), which is Fig 9 :30-31 applied to a famous correlation.
- Not that aggregate peace scores are verdicts. They are admissible as filters for the budget, never as witnesses for a per-slot verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that consensus is corruption. Agreement about shared meaning is the product (Fig 2 :41); only agreement about the world from a shared evidence pool is disqualified (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that declaring a process fair grounds it. Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- Not that the answer is a theory of everything, a final institution, or a last room. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement of a political conflict.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; it was resolved "not by argument", but "by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Per-slot residuals.** Does `R_s` fall and stay down for every contending group, not just in a national average? This is the primary ground.
2. **Pressure test.** Does the institution's ruling fail to move when one side pushes, and does it move when the evidence changes? A metric that cannot discriminate those two is a saturated metric.
3. **Source test.** Do the parties' decisive readings come from separate evidence, with the record showing convergence rather than a shared pool?
4. **Intersection test.** After the lever, is there a remaining compound in the intersections that is categorically different from the single-condition cases?
5. **Handoff test.** Did the next arena differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced, with both bracket fidelity and ground pressure nonzero?
6. **Stake test.** Is the keeper's interest declared and can the convention be checked by someone outside the frame?

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 apply: if any of the eight fires, the corresponding condition is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The five conditions are a guess at the axes along which a political conflict can be settled. "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible" (*They're Scanning Books*). Another field's minimum set need not be these five. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Indiscernibility is not identity; an institution that looks peaceful on a saturated metric has not been shown to be peaceful.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a peaceful institution and does not claim a final arrangement. It says that a conflict must be read per contending group, never as a national average; that an institution channels it only while its answer does not move when a party pushes and it is not counted as a party to the dispute; that the two sides' evidence must be independent, because agreement from one pool is the pool talking; that a compound conflict over territory, identity and procedure must be unbundled before any single lever is applied; and that across repeated disputes the next arena must differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced. It adds that the keeper's stake must be declared and a rule that decides a contested outcome has stopped being a neutral convention. What it offers is the shape any candidate institution must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the tests that would close the open slot. Which institution actually works, in which conflict, stays open until the ground settles it.
