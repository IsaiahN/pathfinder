# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the residual and the refuter below survive a reader's check. The offer collapses on contact if the first stage does not hold.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about conflict institutions, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a political institution channels a conflict peacefully only when five conditions hold together, and not when any one of them fails.
>
> 1. Its answer does not move when a party pushes on it. If it tracks current pressure, it has become another party to the dispute and the readings drift together.
> 2. It is not a third party splitting the difference in a vote. Each side is corrected separately by the same thing, which cannot be moved by either.
> 3. The conflict is read per contending group, not as a national average. A pooled index hides the live group.
> 4. The two sides draw on genuinely different evidence. If they share one evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking, not corroboration.
> 5. The conflict is unbundled when it is a compound of territory, identity and procedure. One lever clears the single-condition cases and leaves the intersections standing.
>
> Across repeated disputes there is a sixth thing: the next arena must differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced. Otherwise the handoffs are motion without progress.
>
> This is a shape, not a name. I am deliberately not saying which institution satisfies the conditions in your case. That slot stays open until a measurement closes it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any constitution, court, election, federal arrangement or power-sharing deal is peaceful in itself. The same institution can be a benefactor to one conflict and a malfactor to another. I am not claiming that an aggregate peace score is a verdict, that declaring a process fair grounds it, or that consensus among people who read the same sources is corroboration.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this message:**
> 1. an institution that changes its ruling under one side's pressure and the conflict still settles per group at the ground;
> 2. an institution with only aggregate readings that reduces each group's residual separately and sustainably;
> 3. parties with near-total shared evidence whose settlement nevertheless settles each group and stays settled;
> 4. one lever that settles all conditions of a compound conflict with nothing left in the intersections;
> 5. a fixed institution whose handoffs keep both fidelity and ground pressure high across three disputes while residuals fall.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding condition is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the derivation and the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I will send the shape and this refuter first and nothing else until you have recorded a verdict on them.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the loop is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Distribution rule

Send to a public submission venue or to one person at a time. Never select recipients by how likely they are to agree: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention" (Seat Map :163). Record the stage-1 verdict before showing any theory, and never pool a later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
