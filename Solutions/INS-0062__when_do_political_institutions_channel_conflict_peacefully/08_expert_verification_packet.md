# Expert verification packet: the Tether on conflict institutions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conflict-management researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the bare shape. Not `grounded`.** No political conflict was measured and no institution was evaluated against an outcome. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A political institution channels a conflict peacefully when five conditions hold together and not when any one fails: its answer does not move when a party pushes on it; it is not a third party splitting the difference in a vote; the conflict is read per contending group rather than as a national average; the two sides draw on genuinely different evidence; and a compound conflict is unbundled before a lever is applied. Across repeated disputes there is a sixth condition: the next arena must differ from the last and match the residual the previous one surfaced. The keeper's stake must be declared, and a rule that decides a contested outcome has stopped being a neutral convention. The framework returns this shape, not the name of an institution. Which institution satisfies it, in which conflict, is left open until a ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** a non-updating anchor is required for a verdict, and the institution cannot be a party or a tie-breaker.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."
- **Refuter:** an institution that changes its ruling under one party's pressure and the conflict nevertheless settles per group at the ground, with each group's residual falling and staying down. Show the per-group series, not an aggregate. If that holds, C1 is wrong.

### C2 - field-facing, checkable by a conflict-management researcher against their own cases

- **Statement:** per-slot reading, independent evidence and unbundling are necessary; a national peace index, a shared evidence pool, and a single lever on a compound conflict each hide the live slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- **What to check:** take one conflict you know well. List the contending groups separately. Does the institution's own reporting resolve them separately, or does it publish one national score? Where did each side's decisive evidence come from? After the intervention, is there a residue involving two or more conditions at once that is categorically different from the single-condition cases?
- **Refuter:** any one of: (a) an aggregate-only institution that reduces each group's residual separately and sustainably; (b) parties with near-total shared evidence whose settlement settles each group and stays settled; (c) one lever that clears all conditions of a compound conflict with nothing left in the intersections. If any holds, C2 is wrong for that case and the condition is withdrawn.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with one live boundary

- **Statement:** across repeated disputes, the room chain requires the next arena to differ from the last and match the residual surfaced; and a procedural convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- **The live boundary:** mutual vetoes and power-sharing rules are conventions between frames, which is admissible under Fig 2 :41. The moment such a rule decides the contested outcome rather than setting the rule under which it is later settled, it becomes a verdict and fails C1. This is a boundary that must be checked empirically, not a verbal distinction.
- **Refuter:** a fixed institution, unchanged in composition, whose fidelity and ground pressure both stay high and non-declining across three disputes while per-slot residuals trend down; or a convention that decides a contested outcome and settles it at the ground without ceasing to be a convention. If either holds, C3 is wrong.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything, not a final institution, not a last room.
- Not that democracy, federalism, courts, elections, mediation or consociation is peaceful in itself. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.").
- Not that "democracy causes peace." The field's own record reports disagreement about causal direction; a correlation is a filter for where to look, not a witness for the verdict.
- Not that aggregate scores are meaningless. They are admissible for the budget and inadmissible for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that consensus is corruption. Agreement about shared meaning is the product (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that declaring a process fair grounds it: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it" (Fig 10 :49).
- Not that the named institution can be derived. The slot is a declared `gap`, `unreached`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing was measured.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In priority order:

1. Per-group residual series before and after the institution's action, with each group listed separately.
2. A pressure test that discriminates "the institution did not move under pressure" from "the metric saturated".
3. A source record for each side's decisive evidence, showing convergence or a shared pool.
4. An intersection test after a lever on a compound conflict.
5. Handoff records across at least three disputes, with fidelity and ground pressure measured.
6. A declared stake for every keeper and a checkable convention.

A source-discriminating instrument that closes the open slot would improve the answer; it would not change the shape. A refutation of C1, C2 or C3 would narrow or withdraw the condition.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :34 and :46, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :56 and Fig 7 :35-36. Six short passages.
2. Name one case where an institution changed under pressure and the conflict still settled. If you can, C1 fails.
3. Name one compound conflict where a single lever cleared the intersections. If you can, C2 fails.
4. Name one standing institution whose composition did not change across three disputes while the conflict did, and say whether its residuals fell. This checks C3.
5. Say what you would count as a failure. Send that, not an endorsement. A failure is more useful than agreement.
