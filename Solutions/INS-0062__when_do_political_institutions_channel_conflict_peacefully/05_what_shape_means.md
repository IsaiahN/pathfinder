## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For INS-0062 a **shape** is the set of conditions an institution must satisfy for a conflict to be channeled peacefully, stated before any institution is named. The shape is:

`anchor that does not update + per-slot channel + independent frames + unbundled compound + room that differs from the last + declared stakes`

That is a constraint. "Hold an election" is a name. The first narrows the search; the second fills a slot without a ground.

The Library of Babel contains the question's own warning about this exact case: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" That is F5's union in a different vocabulary, and it is why the shape here is a conjunction and never a single lever.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrap vocabulary by proximity. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Applied here: start with "neutral", "fair", "representative", then let the figures replace those with "does not update", "per slot", "low overlap", "unbundled".
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Do not ask "is this institution democratic?" Ask "does its answer move when one side pushes?"
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." The five refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` are exactly such a description: each says what you would see if a condition failed.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place", and the paradox is that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it." For this question the stop is the open slot in F10, not an institution.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." A name is licensed when per-slot residuals fall and stay down under that institution, measured against the parties' actual experience, not when the institution looks fair.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence. A constitution borrowed from another country is an import; its source and its shadow test must be recorded.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the limit: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The five conditions in this folder are a similar guess at the axes along which a political conflict can be settled. "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." Recording the conditions makes the choice auditable; it does not make it true.

## 4. Why naming an institution from an aggregate reading is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." It was resolved "not by argument", but "by the arrival of a better instrument."

Applied here: a national peace index, an election result, or a reported absence of violence is a reading. It does not disclose the source. Several institutional arrangements can produce the same reading. Naming one as *the* cause from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (F10), not another argument.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* names the political version of the same error directly: in a room, "the room will be swayed by negotiation which is persuasion, or by coherent drift (both of you found a middle ground to agree on)." And: "The problem is that the room session only produces an outcome that you agree on... It does not mean that the output was objective truth." A settlement that everyone accepts is a reading, not a source.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* identifies the one safeguard that is actually available in this domain: "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move." That sentence is the essays' own example of an anchor, and it is exactly Fig 2 :24-27. It also states the drift: "Context, subtext and pretext decide what a message means to you. None of them decides whether it is true. Only the ground does that."

## 5. Question answered: what licenses a particular institution here?

**Nothing in the framework.** The framework returns a shape. A particular institution is licensed only by per-slot settlement against the ground, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* adds the operational version: "A group is only worth having while its members stay different" and "The group's agreement is never the verdict." It also warns against choosing the checker: "a carefully chosen room is still a chosen room." That is Fig 10 :73-74 ("A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned"). The same essay notes that the aggregate is wrong for per-slot readings: results should be kept "per kind of situation" because "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 5b. Correction to my own §1 to §4 (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`)

I initially wrote "the framework returns a shape, not a name" as though it forbade naming any institution at all. That overstates the rule. The canonical phrase governs "a **missing** thing": a primitive or instrument not held in Γ. It does not forbid selecting among things already held. Elections, courts and constitutions are already in the conflict-management library. Selecting one is a binding from that library onto the five conditions, not the invention of a seventh condition.

What remains wrong in any specific binding is different:

- claiming the binding moves the wall, when Fig 6 says "IMPORT adds an atom" and a binding onto a held term adds none;
- claiming the framework *derives* the name, when it returns only the shape;
- reading the source of a green reading off the reading itself, which is the metamerism error of §4.

So the correct statement is narrower: **naming an institution is permitted, provenance is required, and derivation is not claimed.** Both the original claim and this correction are recorded so neither is quietly lost. This is the same correction the exemplar makes in its own §5b, reached here from the residual line rather than copied.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not a verdict.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A famous peace formula, a prestigious journal, or a well-cited index can get a claim tried. None of them can decide whether it is true. The essay adds the arithmetic: "You may all agree and be wrong." That is Fig 2 :40.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *Arrival of the Fittest* is blunter: it "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula," and "Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any political institution and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 7. The boundary in one sentence

A shape says what an institution must *do* to a conflict: not move under pressure, read each group separately, keep the parties' evidence independent, split the compound, change the room when the conflict changes, and declare the keeper's stake. Which institution does those things, in which conflict, is a name that only a ground can license. Until then the slot stays open, and the open slot is the honest answer rather than an omission.
