## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Social psychology, low overlap with institutional theory.** Realistic conflict theory reports that "contact with an outgroup is insufficient, by itself, to reduce negative attitudes" and that "positive relations can only be restored if superordinate goals are in place." This corroborates the shape of F1: a shared anchor that does not move with either party, not mere contact between parties. It also supplies Billig's warning that "the experimenters themselves constitute a third group, and one that is arguably the most powerful of the three, and that they in fact become the outgroup", which is F2's ground-becomes-a-frame failure at the small-group scale. Different stratum, same shape. One witness, not two.
2. **Law and social psychology, moderate overlap.** Procedural justice is "the idea of fairness in the processes that resolve disputes and allocate resources", and Tyler's finding that giving members a voice "is sometimes enough for a process to be viewed as fair" is a mechanism for how a non-updating anchor acquires legitimacy. Rawls's "pure procedural justice", where "there is no criterion for what constitutes a just outcome other than the procedure itself", is the exact convention-versus-anchor boundary of F2 and F9. Corroborates the shape; does not supply a name.
3. **Human cognition, low overlap.** Motivated reasoning describes political reasoning as serving "the goal of identity protection or maintaining status within an affinity group united by shared values" and reports that "motivated reasoning is qualitatively distinct from reasoning when people do not have a strong emotional stake in the conclusions reached." This corroborates F4's source-independence requirement and Fig 10 :59's "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." It is a cognitive-stratum witness, not a political-institution measurement.
4. **Political science, some overlap with the question's own field.** Consociationalism's own central claim is Lijphart's that "The essential characteristic of consociational democracy is not so much any particular institutional arrangement as the deliberate joint effort by the elites to stabilize the system." That matches F7's "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone": the design is not the cause in isolation. Its critics report that arrangements can be "temporarily freezing conflicts but not resolving them" and that "the very act of forming a multiethnic coalition generates intraethnic competition", which are F6's motion-without-progress and F1's mutual-update collapse at the institutional stratum. This source is inside the question's own field, so it is the least independent witness in the set.
5. **International relations, inside the question's field.** Democratic peace theory's own record states that "theorists disagree about the direction of causality" and that omitted variables may explain the correlation better. That is Fig 9 :30-31 ("your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause") applied to a famous institutional correlation. It corroborates the refusal to name an institution from a correlation, and it is not independent evidence for the shape.

No world source conflicts with a figure verdict strongly enough to trigger a re-census. Where a source looked like a conflict (consociational "success" versus F7's "no standing list of peaceful institutions"), re-census showed it was a different object: the source reports short-term settlement, the figure governs valence across a change of residual. That strain is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7, not hidden.

## A practical constraint, not a verdict

The Journal of Peace Research guidelines state that "Use of generative AI must be disclosed at first submission" and that authors are "fully responsible for the content of their manuscript, even those parts produced by an AI tool." This whole package is a simulated derivation. If any part of it is ever submitted there, that fact must be disclosed. The rule is a venue constraint and says nothing about whether the derivation is correct. Fig 11 :60 puts it plainly: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A submitted claim enters a habitat with rules that were not authored for it.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual written per contending group, the five conditions plus the room-chain condition, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer before showing any theory.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the reader is curious, send the derivation and three source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. The reader's stage-1 verdict is already on record and is never re-read in light of the theory.

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user must state their own ground, write their own refuter before running it, and label the mode of the result. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The slot naming a particular institution is deliberately open; anyone who fills it without a ground measurement has added a binding, not a derivation.
