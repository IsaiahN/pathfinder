# Is "channeling" and "intensifying" an isomer claim? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No institution was measured.

## RESIDUAL

The raw question pairs two outcomes: the same broad class of object, a political institution, is observed sometimes channeling conflict peacefully and sometimes intensifying it. Two questions are owed:

1. Are these two arrangements of the *same* atoms, so that Fig 12's **isomer** description applies?
2. Is there an order among the five conditions, or can they be permuted freely?

A third, smaller question follows: which of the four structures Fig 12 names (isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group) is actually doing work here?

Atoms in the institutional library (all already held; no new primitive is introduced): court, election, legislature, constitution, media, security force, civil society, external guarantor. Bonds in the operator set: `+` conjunction, `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison, `¬` negation. Ground: per-slot residual `R_s` measured against the parties' experience. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are channeling and intensifying isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." Fig 12 :97: "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."

**Reading.** Take `{election, court, constitution, security force}` as the ingredient set. Two arrangements are possible:

- **A:** `constitution → election → court ⋛ ground`, with the court non-updating and the election read per group. The bond between court and ground is `⋛` comparison against something neither party moves.
- **B:** `election + security force → court`, with the court updating on the election result and counted as one of the parties. The bond between court and parties is `+` conjunction, so the court is inside the frame, and the comparison bond is absent.

Same atoms, different bonds, opposite behaviour at the ground. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not variants of one institution. This is why a catalogue that records only "has elections, has courts" records half the case. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12's definition plus Fig 2's anchor geometry, which supplies the reason the bond matters). Refuter in the falsifier block below.

### Q2 - Which structure is the functional group?

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

**Reading.** The five conditions C1 through C5 are a **functional group**. Their presence fixes the kind of thing an institution is: an anchor with a maintained channel. The particular arrangement, court, commission, or mediated assembly, is the qualifier that says which member. This is the structural reason the answer can return a shape and refuse a name without contradiction: the group is derivable, the member is not.

Fig 12 :124 is a useful test here: a functional group's presence fixes the outcome, but the qualifier says which member. Consociationalism's own central claim, that "The essential characteristic of consociational democracy is not so much any particular institutional arrangement as the deliberate joint effort by the elites to stabilize the system" (Lijphart, via the essay record), is the same distinction in the field's own vocabulary: the joint effort is the group, the arrangement is the qualifier. **Corroboration from inside the field's own pool, one witness, not independent.**

### Q3 - Cycle: the sign of the loop decides whether it closes

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

**Reading.** Conflict institutions are cyclic: a ruling changes the parties' beliefs, which change the next ruling. There are two signs:

- A **retaliation cycle** re-enters with the same sign. Each ruling increases the next grievance. The loop closes and intensifies.
- A **reconciliation cycle** re-enters with the opposite sign. Each ruling reduces the next grievance. The loop closes and channels.

A one-off chain, for instance a single mediation followed by no further contact, cannot close by construction. That is why "we held one peace conference" is not a channel; it is a chain of length one. *The Hard Problem is About Source* reports the same two signs at the interpersonal scale: "Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices." *How a Society of Agents Would Work* reports the remedy: "Votes come from outcomes, not popularity." The ground votes on the cycle; the parties' popularity does not.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated at the small-group scale by an essay that shares the author and evidence pool, so weak corroboration.

### Q4 - Catalyst: does the institution survive its own use?

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

**Reading.** An independent court that rules on a dispute and remains able to rule again is a catalyst. A commission that is captured after its first ruling is a spent part that looks like the same institution. The ordinary institutional description records neither, so nothing downstream can tell them apart. Fig 12 :8-16 is the flat-formula failure: the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not, "and nothing signals that anything was lost."

This is also why F6 (the room chain) matters. A standing institution that survives its own use but never differs from the last room is a catalyst with no selection: it persists without progress. Fig 7 :32: "one long room: motion, no progress."

### Q5 - Is there an order among C1 through C5?

The operator set distinguishes ordered bonds from symmetric ones. `+` is symmetric (Fig 12 :55-59: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."); `→` and `⋛` are ordered (Fig 12 :68-74, :85-89). The five conditions are not combined by a single symmetric `+`; they are held in a dependency order.

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: refuse before mint. Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The order that follows:

1. **Describe the residual per slot** (Fig 1 :19), before choosing any frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. **Unbundle the compound** (Fig 9 :54-57), before applying a lever. Otherwise the intersections survive.
3. **Declare the keeper's stake** (Fig 10 :59-60), before accepting a ruling. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway.
4. **Verify non-updating and independence** (Fig 2 :29-32, Fig 8 :47-49), before calling the result a verdict. Agreement from one pool is not corroboration.
5. **Select the next room against the residual** (Fig 7 :36), after the current handoff, not before.

**Why the order is not cosmetic.** Reversing steps 1 and 2 is the apophenia failure: pick the frame (say, a particular constitutional model), then find a conflict it seems to fit. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Reversing 3 and 4 lets a captured institution pass as an anchor because its procedure looks clean.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 3 (dependency order), Fig 9 (unbundle before search) and Fig 10 (declare the stake), three figures with low overlap.

## AN analogue that is refused

A naive analogue would be the "balance of powers": two symmetric operands held together by `+`, so that swapping them changes nothing. That is the flat-formula reading Fig 12 warns about. The figures use `⋛` for the comparison that decides a threshold, and `⋛` is ordered (Fig 12 :85-89: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."). Political "balance" that merely places two powers side by side is a conjunction, not a comparison; it records the ingredients and not the threshold. It is declined as a derivation, not asserted.

## CONCLUSION

Channeling and intensifying are **isomers** over the same institutional atom set: same ingredients, different bonds, opposite behaviour at the ground. The five conditions form a **functional group** whose presence fixes the kind; the named institution is the qualifier, which is why the framework can return the group and withhold the name. The two outcomes are sustained by **cycles** of opposite sign, and a standing institution is a **catalyst** whose survival across uses must be checked, not assumed. The conditions are **ordered** by dependency, not combined by a symmetric conjunction. A "balance of powers" is declined as a flat-formula analogue because it uses `+` where the question needs `⋛`.

This rescues what is checkable in the raw question without inventing a new primitive or naming a winner: the difference between peace and intensification is carried by the bond, not by the ingredient list.

## FALSIFIER, written before the conclusion (also in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4)

1. **Isomer falsifier.** Two institutional arrangements with the same atoms and the same bond, measured at the ground, produce opposite per-slot outcomes. The difference would then be carried by something other than the bond, and Fig 12 :117 would not govern this case.
2. **Functional-group falsifier.** An institution satisfies all five conditions and per-slot residuals still fail to fall. The group would not fix the outcome, and Fig 12 :123 would be strained.
3. **Cycle-sign falsifier.** A ruling that re-enters with the same sign reduces the next grievance and stays reduced. The loop sign would not decide whether it closes.
4. **Order falsifier.** A permutation of the five steps, including choosing the institution before describing the residual and before declaring the stake, yields the same per-slot result across three cases. The conditions would then be a symmetric `+` set, not a dependency order.

If any fires, the corresponding structural claim is withdrawn and the raw question is re-censused rather than fitted.
