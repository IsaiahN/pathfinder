## 0. The residual in effect terms

Let `s` index a contending party. `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` is the gap between what party `s` expected from the political order before an action and what it actually got. The question asks which institutional arrangements keep `R_s` arriving through a channel to something that settles it, and which arrangements stop `R_s` from arriving or turn the institution itself into another party.

- **Arity.** More than two operands: at least two contending frames plus the institution, usually several parties, a media field and an external environment. This is not a two-body question.
- **Symmetry.** Ordered and comparative. "Peacefully" is a threshold relative to a baseline (Fig 12 :87, comparison ⋛), not a symmetric join of the parties. "Channel" is a sequence; a goal is a comparison and progress is a subtraction (Fig 12 :112). A vocabulary without those bonds can name a state called peace and cannot name a route to one (Fig 12 :111).
- **Scale.** Three scales: the single dispute, the institution that receives it, and the chain of institutions across repeated disputes. The membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30), so a method that works at one scale is not a recording that transfers to another.
- **The governing constraint.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A national "conflict index" that pools contending groups is exactly the averaging that hides the live slot. Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"):** "an institution channels a conflict peacefully" = a conjunction of five conditions, C1 through C5 below, with the particular institution that satisfies them left as an open slot. This written shape is refusable by the same test as any other term.

**Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. Peaceful channeling needs C1 through C5 together; each is a condition that can hold without the others, and the intersections are categorically different from any single condition. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The middle link is where political institutions usually break: having procedures is not the same as being able to say what counts as progress, and that link has the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :24-27). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A peaceful channel needs an anchor that does not update on the parties (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 2 :24-27 shows the geometry: "pinned: it does not update on A or B" and both parties range against it. Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** An institution channels a conflict peacefully when the parties treat its ruling, count or settlement as something that settles the dispute and does not move when they push on it. It intensifies the conflict when the institution tracks the parties' current pressure: then it has become another frame, and the drift looks like responsiveness from inside while being the mutual-update collapse from outside.

**Refuter.** Show a case in which the institution changed its ruling in direct response to one party's pressure and the conflict nevertheless resolved on the ground, with `R_s` trending down per slot. That would break Fig 2 :33-34 at the ground and would be decisive against C1.

Triangulated: Fig 2 (the alignment geometry) and Fig 10 (the seat that keeps the anchor reachable) are two figures with low overlap. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

### F2 - The institution is not a third party and not a tie-breaker (triangulated)

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it." Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** A court or commission that is counted as one side in the dispute, that splits the difference as a vote tie-breaker, or whose authority is negotiated between the parties is a frame. Negotiation among the parties can produce a convention, but a convention is not an anchor for a verdict. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."

This distinction is the one most often missed in institutional design: constitutional rules are conventions, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 2 :43). The same line is stated in Fig 10 :47, where a convention that decides an outcome is a seat-level failure.

**Refuter.** A ruling that both parties accepted as fair which also changed the material outcome, with per-slot residuals subsequently falling and no new atom imported. If a convention can settle a contested fact without ceasing to be a convention, C1 and C2 merge and this finding fails.

### F3 - The residual must be read per contending slot, never pooled (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 10 :26 names the decay mechanism: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** A peace index, an aggregate vote share or a national "tension score" is a saturated metric. The live slot, the one group whose `R_s` is positive and not trending down, disappears into the average. Intensification begins where the institution's instruments stop resolving the slot that is actually live. Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." Fig 10 :29: "keep the anchor reachable."

**Refuter.** An institution whose only available readings are aggregate, and which nonetheless reduces each party's residual separately and sustainably. That would break the per-slot rule at the ground.

Triangulated: Fig 1 (slot indexing) and Fig 10 (channel decay) name the same fact from different directions, with low overlap between an agent loop and a checking seat.

### F4 - Low overlap between the parties is what makes their agreement mean anything (triangulated)

Fig 2 :18-21 gives the common-cause collapse: "one evidence pool", "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence" Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 8 :49: two frames that overlap almost completely are "one frame wearing two names". Fig 8 :50: "two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** Two conditions pull apart here. If the parties draw on one evidence pool, their apparent agreement is the pool talking, which can look like peace while no residual has been resolved. If they draw on genuinely different evidence and still arrive at the same settlement, that is corroboration at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50), and the union of their frames has a surplus neither had alone (Fig 8 :45). An institution that forces the parties onto a single approved narrative produces the first kind and calls it consensus.

**Refuter.** A settlement between parties with documented near-total evidence overlap that nonetheless settles each slot against the ground and stays settled. That would break the independence condition as a necessary condition.

Strain noted: Fig 8 :50 says shared atoms are not the signature of collapse, so an institution must not be judged by how much the parties' *conclusions* overlap. It must be judged by where their *evidence* comes from. This is a strain only if "consensus" is read as agreement in content; re-census resolves it by reading the quantity as source independence. Recorded, not hidden (§7).

### F5 - A compound conflict must be unbundled before a lever is applied (triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** A conflict over territory plus identity plus procedure is a union, not one hard conflict. An election clears the procedure region and leaves the identity-territory intersection standing. The residue is not a harder election problem; it is a specific compound that needs its own loop. Where the institution applies one rule to the whole bundle, the intersections survive and become the live slots from F3.

**Refuter.** One institutional lever that settles all three conditions at the ground in a documented compound conflict, with no residue in the intersections. That would break the union reading of F5.

### F6 - Across repeated disputes, the room must differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced (triangulated)

Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along;" and "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :32: "one long room: motion, no progress." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :37-39 gives the two factors: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:", "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff", and "the ground's pressure on the room." Fig 7 :40: "the room is summoned by the residual rather than composed."

Fig 10 :71: "the room composition is a function of the question." Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** A standing institution that handles conflict after conflict with the same closure is a room chain of mirrors. The handoffs produce motion, not progress: each venue agrees with the last, agreement compounds, evidence does not. Peaceful channeling across time requires the next arena to be selected against the residual the previous one surfaced, with both bracket fidelity and ground pressure nonzero. Intensification is what happens when a fixed institution meets a changed conflict; the persistence of the institution is only the symptom, the mismatch is the failure.

**Refuter.** A fixed institution, with unchanged composition, whose handoffs show both bracket fidelity and ground pressure staying high and non-declining while per-slot residuals trend down across three disputes. That would break the room-chain condition.

### F7 - No standing list of peaceful institutions; valence is relative to the residual (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** The question "which institution is peaceful?" has no answer independent of a residual. Federalism, courts, elections, consociation and mediation are actors whose sign depends on which slot is live. Reforming an institution in isolation, without changing its contact with the parties and the ground, changes nothing however much it improves. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

**Refuter.** An institution whose valence is invariant across two documented changes of residual, verified at the ground. That would break the relativity claim and would allow a standing list of peaceful designs.

### F8 - Moving a conflict to another venue substitutes a habitat, it does not remove one (single-source on the isolation law, triangulated with the habitat enumeration)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** Sending a conflict to a different institution, a different country's model, or a judicial rather than a legislative arena does not remove the field of contact. It replaces it, and the new field brings unchosen actors. The two failure modes are silent until they act. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A constitution copied as a recording, rather than carried as a method with its round trip measured, is the dead-reckoning failure of Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

**Refuter.** A venue substitution that reproduces all goal-required contact and introduces no unchosen actor, verified after the fact. That would break the isolation law as applied to political institutions.

### F9 - Declaring a process fair does not ground it, and an undeclared stake is modelled anyway (triangulated)

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."

**Reading, mode `specified`.** A court with a reputation to protect, a mediator funded by one side, or an electoral commission whose members benefit from the answer is disqualified on the reading even if its procedure is impeccable. The stake cannot be removed, only declared. A rule that nobody can check becomes a constant authored by whoever holds the seat, and calling the process "fair" does not make it so. Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." An institution is a legitimate anchor only for questions constitutively about what it measures; "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37-38).

**Refuter.** An institution whose keeper benefits from the outcome, whose procedure is uncheckable, and whose readings nevertheless settle each slot at the ground. That would break the disqualification rule at the ground.

### F10 - Which named institution works is a gap, not a hidden answer (unreachable verdict)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 6 :40: "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."

**Reading, mode `specified`, status `gap`.** After a genuine re-census there is no figure line that names which institution satisfies C1 through C5 in a given conflict. The figure set returns the shape of the thing: a non-updating anchor, a per-slot channel, independent frames, an unbundled conflict, and a room that differs from the last one. It does not return a name. The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, characterised in effect terms but not held. This is `unreached`, not `unreachable in principle`, and not a silence.

**Refuter.** A figure line that, after re-census, directly governs the selection of a named institution for a named conflict. If such a line exists, this gap was a search failure (Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound?") and should be closed by SEARCHED before INWARD or OUTWARD.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The whole shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor failure:** an institution changes its answer under one party's pressure and the conflict nonetheless settles per slot at the ground, with `R_s` falling and staying down (breaks F1).
2. **Aggregation success:** an institution with only pooled, aggregate readings reduces each party's residual separately and sustainably (breaks F3).
3. **Overlap success:** parties with near-total shared evidence reach a settlement that settles each slot at the ground and stays settled (breaks F4).
4. **Union failure:** one lever settles all conditions of a documented compound conflict with no residue in the intersections (breaks F5).
5. **Room failure:** a fixed institution, with unchanged composition, keeps both bracket fidelity and ground pressure high and non-declining across three disputes while per-slot residuals trend down (breaks F6).
6. **Standing list:** an institution's valence is invariant across two documented changes of residual (breaks F7).
7. **Convention success:** a convention that decides a contested outcome settles it at the ground without ceasing to be a convention (breaks F2 and F9).
8. **Figure-line existence:** a figure line is found that names the institution for a given conflict after the re-census recorded in F10 (closes the gap; this would improve the answer, not destroy it).

If any of 1 through 7 fires, the corresponding condition is withdrawn and the conjunction C1 through C5 is rewritten. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual, not with a preferred institution. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
2. Keep the five conditions separate and never average them into an "institutional quality" score. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A ranking of institutions is a filter for where to look, not a witness for what channels conflict.
3. Measure per contending slot. Any report that starts with a national average is already inside F3's failure.
4. Separate conventions from anchors in the write-up. A rule can be a convention, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
5. Record the room selection across disputes. The relevant observation is not "did this institution work once" but "did the next room differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced" (Fig 7 :36).
6. Declare the stake of every keeper before asking for an outside verdict (Fig 10 :60).
7. Send the generator, the shape and the refuter, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

### SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD, kept distinct

- **SEARCHED.** Every relevant figure line was grepped from the verbatim extract and checked against the census. No line names a specific institution for a specific conflict. The gap in F10 is a genuine search result, not an assumption.
- **INWARD.** The missing instrument is sharpened in our own terms: a per-slot reading that discriminates "the institution did not move under pressure" from "the institution looked unmoved because the metric saturated." This is an instrument question, not another argument. An instrument is improved from a worse one already returning something (Fig 6 :32-34), so the starting point is existing per-group conflict data, not a designed-from-scratch "peace index".
- **OUTWARD.** The candidate frame is conflict management research and peace science, which already hold instruments for settlement, mediation and reconciliation. Described in effect terms first (Fig 9 :27-31), then looked up. Each source is provenanced in `10_source_ledger.csv`, its evidence pool declared, and its import debited against independence. World sources corroborate the shape; they do not supply the name and never overrule a figure.

### Updated verdict

The conjunction survives, each condition kept separate and never averaged: a non-updating anchor (F1), a ground that is not a party or tie-breaker (F2), a per-slot channel (F3), independent frames (F4), an unbundled compound (F5), a room chain that selects against the residual (F6), valence relative to the residual (F7), substitution rather than removal when venues change (F8), and declared stakes with checkable conventions (F9). Which named institution satisfies the conjunction in a given conflict is a declared `gap` (F10), `unreached`, not a silence. Strains after re-census: one live, the convention/anchor boundary, named in §7. Mode `specified` on all conditions, `general` on the bare shape, nothing `grounded`.

## 7. Strains and re-census

- **Convention versus anchor (live, not smoothed).** Fig 2 :41 calls agreement about shared meaning "efficient for a convention", while Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47 say a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. Consociational power sharing gives each group a mutual veto, which looks like a convention deciding outcomes. Re-census: a veto is a convention between frames, and it is admissible as a convention; it becomes a verdict, and fails C2, the moment it decides the contested outcome rather than setting the rule under which the outcome is later settled. The strain is in the boundary, not in the figures. It is kept live because a real institution can sit on either side of it, and the check is empirical, not verbal.
- **Shared atoms versus shared evidence (resolved by re-census).** Fig 8 :50 says two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, which is corroboration at maximum independence. Read as "same conclusions", this strains against Fig 2 :40's disqualification of shared-pool agreement. Re-census: the quantity in Fig 2 is source overlap, while the quantity in Fig 8 :50 is convergent derivation from separate sources. Different objects. No contradiction.
- **Aggregate metrics (resolved by re-census).** Fig 1 :22 disqualifies averaging live slots, while Fig 9 :60 permits filters for the budget. A national conflict index is admissible for deciding where to look and inadmissible as a witness for a per-slot verdict. Different offices, not a contradiction.
- **Room composition (resolved by re-census).** Fig 7 :36 requires the next room to differ from the last, while institutional stability is often treated as a virtue. Re-census: Fig 10 :72 makes the room a function of the question, so a fixed room is a failure only when the question has moved. Where the question has not moved, the same room is not a mirror chain. The residual decides, not the design.
- **After re-census: one live strain, one boundary that must be checked empirically, and three resolved readings. Nothing was forced to fit.**
