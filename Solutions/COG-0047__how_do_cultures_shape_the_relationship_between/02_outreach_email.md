# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the crowd-pleasing mappings in section 3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have been kept out of the message. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one described result about culture, emotion and the body before you decide about the framework

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific result I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the result does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Across cultures, the same class of bodily events, a racing heart or a churning stomach, is bound to different emotions and different explanations. What fails to resolve is the mapping: which bodily signal becomes which emotion, and which account is offered for it. I am asking what a culture changes, not cataloguing more emotions.
>
> **The result (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain verdicts, not `grounded`).** A culture is not a new kind of mind. It is a weighting of inherited prior against private history, plus a library of available emotion concepts and a habitat of actors and conditions in contact with the person. Two people with identical architecture can diverge because their histories and their weightings diverge. So a culture changes the weight, the vocabulary and the habitat. It does not add a new primitive to the underlying capacity, and it does not rewrite the loop.
>
> The relation itself is ordered: bodily sensation leads to emotion leads to explanation. The body is the ground-facing slot, emotion is a binding of that signal to a concept, and explanation is a composition above it. Which bond holds is not readable off the three things by themselves, so I leave the bond open. The three pairwise links are separate slots and must be checked separately; averaging them into "culture shapes emotion" throws away the signal.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that a culture determines a specific emotion. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so I do not say which feeling a culture will produce. Not that all cultures are two independent witnesses. Agreement is evidence only when they draw on different evidence; if both learned the same taxonomy from the same source, that is one witness. Not that transmission installs a fixed answer. What crosses generations is a method or a head start, not a recording.
>
> **What would refute this shape.** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. A culture whose members share the underlying capacity but whose body-emotion-explanation relations cannot be described by any setting of weight, vocabulary or habitat. That would require a new primitive.
> 2. An instrument showing the body-to-emotion mapping is an identity fixed by physiology alone, with no role for concept availability or prior.
> 3. Two cultural frames proved to share the same evidence pool still being counted as two witnesses, or independent frames with the same weight and vocabulary reliably producing different relations.
> 4. A figure line that names a specific emotion or explanation from the base set.
>
> If any holds, I retract the result. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the formula file. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an output contrived to fit the formula is worthless. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> --- Isaiah
