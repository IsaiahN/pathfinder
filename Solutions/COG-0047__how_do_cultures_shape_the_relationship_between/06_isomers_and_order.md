# Bond order, isomers and the open operator item

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim says the relation is an ordered cross-kind composition with an open bond. Three questions are owed: (1) what order the three relata sit in, (2) whether two cultures that differ only in which bond holds are **isomers** under Fig 12, and (3) whether the open ∥ and ≡ operator item decides the emotion/bodily relation.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What is the order of the three relata?

- Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." The rule is cross-kind alternation; it forbids a flat same-kind triple.
- Bodily sensation is the ground-facing slot (D1 has a measurable anchor). Emotion is a binding of that signal to a concept. Explanation is a composition above emotion.
- The order is therefore B leads to E leads to X, a sequence, not a symmetric set. Fig 12 defines sequence (→) as ordered: "A then B; B needs A to have happened."
- Reversal test: if we write E leads to B, we claim the concept produces the bodily signal. That is possible as a top-down predictive effect, but it is a different molecule with a different bond, not a relabelling of the same one. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **Falsifier:** an experiment showing the same bond holds in both directions for the same participants under the same concept availability would collapse the ordering into a symmetric bond and break this derivation.

### Q2 — Are two cultures isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- If two cultures share the full capacity (the same six-term set) and the same relata (B, E, X) but bind them with different bonds or different weightings, they are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different lived substance. This is the strong claim worth testing.
- If two cultures share only a bare motif (say, a body signal is named at all) but differ in the atoms available in Gamma, they are not isomers. They are members of the same **functional group**, the motif "body is bound to a feeling and accounted for," with different members in the slots. The weaker claim is much easier to defend and much less interesting.
- The distinction is checkable: list the atoms each culture actually uses for B, E and X. If the lists match and only the bond or w differs, the isomer claim stands. If the lists differ, it falls to the functional-group claim.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a culture can be shown to lack an atom another requires for the same relation (for example, no concept slot for interoception at all), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives.

### Q3 — Does the open operator item decide the emotion/bodily bond?

- Operators table :79: "An unresolved disagreement about what two of these operators mean is a visible open item and not a closing note." The disagreement is recorded against ∥ (disjunction, "either suffices; not both required") and ≡ (identity, "two names, one referent").
- Fig 12 :90: "Six tests, and they separate the seven bonds."
- In some cultural vocabularies an emotion word and a bodily word appear to function as two names for one referent (≡). In others they behave as alternatives where either suffices (∥). The framework explicitly says this is unresolved, so this derivation may not choose.
- This is a genuine `gap`, not a strain to be smoothed. The world literature corroborates variability in emotion categorization ("the boundaries and domains of these concepts are categorized differently by all cultures", from the Emotion article fetched this session) but supplies no bond name.
- **Falsifier:** a resolved canonical reading of ∥ or ≡ that maps cleanly onto an emotion/bodily vocabulary would close the gap. Until it fires, the slot stays open.

### Q4 — What is the dependency order, and where does culture act?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."
- Links: interoceptive perception (1) leads to emotion vocabulary (2) leads to what counts as a good explanation (3) leads to regulation (4) leads to carrying across generations (5).
- Fig 3 :24: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The vocabulary/objective link (2 to 3) has no clean instrument, which is exactly why culture operates there and why effort spent cataloguing more emotion words cannot rescue it.
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Measuring interoception more precisely (link 1) cannot fix a missing account of what counts as a good explanation (link 3).
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that directly measures what counts as a good explanation across cultures, with the same reliability as heartbeat detection, would move the break and change where culture is predicted to act.

## CONCLUSION

The relata are ordered B to E to X, with B ground-facing and X a second-order composition. Two cultures that share the full capacity and differ only in bond or weighting are Fig 12 isomers; cultures that share only a motif are a functional group. The specific emotion/bodily bond is not decided, because the Operators table records ∥ and ≡ as an open item, so that sub-question is a `gap`. Culture acts at the dependency chain's weakest link, the vocabulary-to-objective step, not at the perception step. This rescues what is defensible in the question without naming a specific emotion, adding a primitive, or pretending the open item is settled.

This framing needs no new term, no import that moves the wall, and no resolution of the operator disagreement. What remains open is recorded, not hidden.
