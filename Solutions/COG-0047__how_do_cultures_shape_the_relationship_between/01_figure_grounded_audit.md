## 0. The question under audit

> How do cultures shape the relationship between emotion, bodily sensation, and explanation?

Restated as an effect: across cultures, the same class of bodily events is bound to different emotions and different explanations. We must decide what culture changes (the primitives, the arrangement, or only the weighting) and what stays fixed.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term so it can be refused: an ordered arrangement in which one relatum is ground-facing, one is a binding, one is a composition above it, plus an account of what a culture modifies. This is the direction-of-question discipline of Fig 5 :62 to :66.

## 1. Residual, stated before any figure is chosen

**R (effect terms).** The covariance of (bodily sensation B, emotion E, explanation X) with culture C. Something registers: the same interoceptive state can be experienced and explained differently in different cultural settings. Nothing in the question's own vocabulary tells us whether culture changes the capacity, the arrangement, or the prior weight.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** three relata plus one modulating frame. A ternary composition with a fourth parameter, not a binary relation.
- **Symmetry:** the relata are ordered, not interchangeable. B is closer to the body's ground; E is a binding of B to a concept; X is a composition over E. Treating the triple as a flat symmetric set would lose the order B leads to E leads to X. Fig 13 :75 blocks a direct join of same-kind terms, so the order is structural, not stylistic.
- **Scale:** first order inside one person's loop (B to E), second order at the explanatory level (E to X), third at the cultural or historical scale where many persons share a library and inherited priors (Fig 4 "the population"). The membrane rule matters: what crosses scales is a method or head start, not a recording (Fig 4 :22).

**Per-slot, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Here the slots are the three pairwise relations (B-E, E-X, B-X). An average "culture shapes emotion" would throw away the only signal that says which link is being changed.

## 4. Findings F1 to F7

### F1 Culture is a weighting and a habitat, not a seventh primitive (decisive for the shape)

Symbols table :24-26: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."

Fig 13 :116: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Two persons with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge. A culture is exactly a shared history plus an inherited prior, and a habitat of actors, conditions and relations in contact with the residual. So culture changes (a) the weighting between private bodily history and inherited conceptual prior, (b) the library Gamma of available terms, and (c) the enumerated habitat. It does not add a primitive to the six-term substrate, and it does not rewrite the loop.

**Verdict per domain:** D4 `general` structural, triangulated across the Symbols table, Fig 11 and Fig 13 (low overlap: a weighting definition, a habitat rule and a minimality rule). D2 and D3 inherit the same w term.

**Refuter:** a population whose members share the six-term capacity but whose B-E-X relations cannot be described by any setting of w, Gamma or habitat would require a seventh primitive and break the shape. Also broken if a person's w were shown to be fixed by physiology alone, with no learned prior.

### F2 Bodily sensation is ground-facing; emotion is a binding, not a readout

Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

The body's interoceptive state is the ground-facing slot (D1 has a measurable anchor). Emotion is a frame binding that signal to a concept under a weighting. The relation is ordered: B leads to E. It is not a symmetric label swap and not an identity read off the signal, because which bond holds is not recoverable from the operands. This matches the world literature: Barrett is quoted in the Emotion article as saying emotions "are not triggered; you create them" and emerge from "the physical properties of your body" plus "your culture and upbringing, which provide that environment"; and interoception is described as mapping "the physiological status of all tissues in the body" into "subjective feeling states that are critical for human emotion and self-awareness."

**Verdict per domain:** D1 `grounded` for the existence of the measurable signal (triangulated: Fig 2 anchor rule plus the interoception source), D2 `specified` ungrounded for the binding (triangulated: Symbols table w plus the Barrett quote, overlapping but independent field).

**Refuter:** an instrument showing the body-to-emotion mapping is a fixed identity determined by physiology alone, with no concept availability or prior involvement, would collapse the order and break the claim. A demo in which two persons with identical interoceptive signals and identical available concepts reliably produce different emotions would also break the determinacy of the binding.

### F3 The question is a union compound, not one relation

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The three pairwise relations are separate slots: B-E (binding), E-X (composition into an account), B-X (the direct bodily explanation, for example "my stomach hurts because I am anxious"). Averaging them into "culture shapes emotion" discards the signal that says which link moves. The compound needs its own loop.

**Verdict:** `general`, single-source for the union rule (Fig 9), corroborated by the method rule in Fig 1.

**Refuter:** show that all three pairwise relations share one closure and one anchor and that one lever moves them together. Then it is one loop and the unbundling is wrong.

### F4 Cross-cultural agreement is evidence only at low overlap

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 13 :38-39: "Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."

Two cultural frames count as two witnesses only if they do not draw on the same evidence. If both learned the same emotion taxonomy from the same textbooks or the same global media, their agreement is one pool talking. Conversely, convergent derivation from genuinely separate evidence is corroboration at maximum independence; Fig 8 :50, "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents." Only the record of where each came from separates them.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 13 and Fig 8.

**Refuter:** a study showing two cultural samples share the relevant evidence source would reduce them to one witness, and any agreement between them must not be counted twice. This is a check on the checking arrangement, not a reversal of the shape.

### F5 Transmission carries a head start, not a fixed answer

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Saying "culture shapes the relationship" is defensible only if culture changes the closure Gamma and the prior, which makes the next person's search cheaper, not if it installs a finished mapping that cannot be reapplied. A practice that does not change anyone's contact with the ground changes nothing however elaborate it is. The essay "How Intelligence Actually Works" adds "Diversity is a natural hedge," which is why multiple frames are useful here: "changes in diet, or having an assortment of different perspectives in a brainstorming session can prevent outcomes from drifting further than they would with groupthink."

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 11 and the essay.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a cultural emotion practice transmitted as an immutable replay that cannot be reapplied in a new context and that still changes the B-E-X relation. That would show transmission of a recording, not a method, and break this reading.

### F6 The framework returns a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 5 :62: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term." Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."

The figures license the claim that the relation must be an ordered cross-kind composition with an open bond and a weighted prior. They do not license naming which emotion or which explanation a given culture produces. The figures README states the limit as "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"; Fig 13 adds that the framework "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 5 and the canonical README.

**Refuter:** a figure line that selects a specific emotion or explanation from the six-term set would break the limit. None was found.

### F7 A live open item bears directly on the E-B relation

Operators table :79: "An unresolved disagreement about what two of these operators mean is a visible open item and not a closing note." Operators table :75: "identity has no partner meaning two names, two referents."

The open item is recorded against the disjunction bond (∥) and the identity bond (≡). This is not an aside for this question. In some cultural vocabularies an emotion word and a bodily word may function as "two names, one referent" (identity), while in others the relation is "either suffices; not both required" (disjunction). The framework explicitly says the disagreement is unresolved, so it may not be settled here. This is recorded as a gap, not smoothed into a verdict.

**Verdict:** `general` that the item is open; `gap` on which of ∥ or ≡ governs a given culture's E-B relation. Single-source (Operators table). The world literature corroborates variability in emotion categorization (the Emotion article: "the boundaries and domains of these concepts are categorized differently by all cultures") but does not choose a bond.

**Refuter:** a resolved canonical reading of ∥ or ≡ that maps cleanly onto an emotion/bodily vocabulary would close this gap.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. A culture is found whose members have the same six-term capacity but whose B-E-X relations cannot be described by any setting of w, Gamma or habitat. That requires a seventh primitive (breaks F1).
2. An instrument shows the body-to-emotion mapping is an identity fixed by physiology alone, independent of concept availability and prior (breaks F2).
3. Two cultural frames proved to draw on the same evidence pool are still treated as two independent witnesses, or independent frames with the same w and Gamma reliably produce different relations (breaks F4 or F2 determinacy).
4. A figure line is found that names a specific emotion or explanation from the six-term set (breaks F6).
5. The union unbundling fails: all three pairwise relations share one closure and one anchor and move under one lever (breaks F3).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is culture a primitive?** No. It is w plus habitat plus shared Gamma (Symbols table, Fig 11, Fig 13).
2. **Is the triple flat?** No. Ordered B to E to X; no same-kind direct joins (Fig 13 :75).
3. **Does the body fix the emotion?** No. The bond is not recoverable from the operands (Fig 12 :46); valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
4. **Are two cultures two witnesses?** Only at low overlap (Fig 2 :31, :40).
5. **Is the union one question?** No. Unbundle the three pairwise relations (Fig 9 :54).
6. **Can we name the emotion or explanation?** No. Shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32); the shape is made an ordinary term so it can be refused (Fig 5 :62, :65).
7. **What settles it?** A source-discriminating experiment, not agreement; the ground does the settling (Formula step 5, Fig 2 :36-37).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: culture changes the weighting, the available library and the habitat, not the primitives; the relation is an ordered cross-kind composition with an open bond; the three pairwise relations form a union compound that must be checked separately; and two cultures count as two witnesses only at low evidence overlap. Strains after re-census: none hidden. One genuine gap is recorded openly, the unresolved ∥ and ≡ semantics and which one governs a culture's E-B relation.
