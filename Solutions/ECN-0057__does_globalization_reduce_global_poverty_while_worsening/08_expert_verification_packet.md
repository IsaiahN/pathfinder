# Expert verification packet: globalization, poverty, inequality

**What this is:** a claim in a form a development economist or distributional statistician can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the slot reading, not `grounded`.** No causal estimate is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The "while" in the question is a conjunction of two slots, not one net welfare scalar. A global headcount against an absolute line is one reading; inequality within a country is a set of readings at another scale. A global total can fall while a national slot stays live, and one actor can carry opposite valence for the two residuals. The global-to-national transform is lossy, and the within-country spread is what it discards. No figure line determines how much of either trend globalization caused, so that magnitude is recorded as `gap`. What the framework offers is a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot; aggregation over slots discards a live signal; the question is a union compound and must be unbundled before search.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- **Refuter:** show that one global scalar with no live per-slot component fully accounts for both trends, or that the two readings are one quantity under two names.

### C2. Framework-internal but economics-facing, checkable by anyone who has read a poverty or inequality series

- **Statement:** an actor's valence is relative to the residual. The same actor can be a benefactor for the headcount residual and a malfactor for the spread residual. This is the structural reason the word "while" is legal, and it explains why no fixed sign for globalization can be derived from the two readings.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check:** is this consistent with the fact that countries with similar exposure to global markets have very different inequality paths? The OWID Economic Inequality page reports exactly that variation and attributes part of it to national institutions and policy choice.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an economic actor whose valence is intrinsic, surviving a change of residual, or show the valence law fails in a documented trade episode.

### C3. Framework-internal derivation with a measurement consequence

- **Statement:** the global-to-national transform is lossy and `R_T` is nonzero. The comparator in the question is a conjunction, not ⋛; ⋛ belongs one level down at the poverty line. Headcount and Gini are in different currencies and must not be summed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Operators table :51 defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.
- **Refuter:** show a global-to-national aggregation preserving the full within-country distribution with `R_T = 0`, or exhibit a ⋛ reading joining headcount and Gini without changing the bond's operands or scale.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that globalization reduced global poverty, by any amount.
- Not that globalization worsened inequality within any named country, by any amount.
- Not that the two trends are a paradox requiring a single net verdict, nor that they can be averaged into one.
- Not that globalization has a fixed sign for either residual.
- Not that ⋛ joins poverty and inequality. That reading was tried, strained and retracted; it is preserved in `01` F7 and `06` Q5.
- Not `grounded`. No dataset was run, no estimate was recomputed, and no source-discriminating instrument was applied.
- No claim that agreement among economists or volume of publication settles any of this (Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :13).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Threshold Test:** in a given country and year, does the headcount change when the absolute line moves while the relative line is held, and vice versa? That separates the two slots at the ground.
2. **Round-trip test:** carry a national distribution up to a global headcount and back and enumerate, or quantify, what fails to return.
3. **Source test:** a regime or border discontinuity changing trade exposure while national institutions are held fixed, read against both slots separately. That is the missing D3 anchor.
4. **Standing refuters:** one scalar accounts for both trends; the two readings are one quantity; an unmoving instrument settles causation; an actor has intrinsic valence; `R_T = 0`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 11 :49 and :50, Fig 4 :27 and :30. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against the cross-country inequality record: do similar levels of market exposure produce different national inequality paths?
3. Check C3 against what a global headcount actually discards about a national distribution.
4. Name one instrument that would separate the trade contribution from growth and institutional change, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
