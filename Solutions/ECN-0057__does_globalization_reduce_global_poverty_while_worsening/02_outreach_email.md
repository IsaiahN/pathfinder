**Subject:** Two readings of one question, and what would refute the way I have split them

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as I receive it:** does globalization reduce global poverty while worsening inequality within some countries? Most answers treat the "while" as a paradox to be balanced into one net verdict.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the slot reading, not `grounded`):** those are two slots, not one number. A global headcount against an absolute line is one reading; inequality within a country is a set of readings at another scale. A global total can fall while one national slot stays live. The framework states this directly: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19) and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"* (Fig 1 :22). It also says one actor can carry opposite signs for two different residuals: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another"* (Fig 11 :50). So "poverty down while inequality up" is a legal two-slot state, not a contradiction and not a net welfare score. The cross-scale step is lossy: the global aggregate cannot hold the within-country spread, and the gap between the two descriptions is the thing worth measuring.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that the framework determines how much poverty fell, or how much inequality rose, or how much of either globalization caused. No figure line contains a trade elasticity or a Gini trajectory. I record that part as `gap`. World data speak to it, but they speak as measurement, not as a verdict handed to the framework. I am also not claiming the comparator is a threshold relation between the two quantities. I tried that reading and retracted it after a strain test; the "while" is a conjunction of two slot residuals.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. one global scalar with no live per-slot component fully accounts for both trends;
> 2. the headcount and the within-country spread are shown to be the same quantity under two names;
> 3. a measurement that does not move when a frame changes its mind settles globalization's causal contribution to both;
> 4. an actor is found whose valence is intrinsic rather than residual-relative;
> 5. the global-to-national transform preserves the full within-country distribution with zero round trip gap.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and the formula are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.

> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
