I redid the census from the residual line alone, before comparing my result with the primary's census. WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry, scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

Symmetry: the slots are not interchangeable; a threshold over a pooled distribution and a spread inside one distribution are ordered, not symmetric. - **Domain 2, within-country spread.** Actors: households inside one country, distributional statistics. Currency: income shares and ordering. Anchor: the declared statistic for a country and year. Nameable.

Union, not partition: the two slots overlap because both are read from household surveys. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." ## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`: what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires measuring it rather than assuming zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the two per-country household distributions with their within-country spreads and their survey definitions.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** one global headcount against an international absolute line, built by lining up surveys across countries (the PIP handbook describes this step).
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** read the global claim back onto a particular national slot to decide whether a country's poverty problem is a headcount problem or a distribution problem.

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three things the national description holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **within-country spread**, which is exactly the second residual in the question;
- the **choice of poverty line**, since the international line is an average of the poorest countries' national lines and cannot represent a rich country's line (the higher-poverty-line essay documents the $3 versus $30 divergence);
- the **income-versus-consumption distinction**, which the OWID poverty page says changes the picture at the bottom and top of a distribution.

One thing survives cleanly: a count of people below a fixed threshold, and the fact that a global total can fall while a national spread worsens. That residue is the shape worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no cross-scale ground reading was run; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating a global headcount as a compressed but equivalent national distribution would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
