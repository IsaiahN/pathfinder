# The Tether's answer: Does globalization reduce global poverty while worsening inequality within some countries?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the slot-level prediction, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the empirical question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

One process is said to move two quantities in opposite directions at once. The residual is the structure of that "while": two slots at two scales, one actor, and no declared instrument for the causal step. Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** The question is a union compound (Fig 9 :55, :57), not one scalar verdict.

## 2. The shape

1. **Two readings, not one net score.** A global headcount against an absolute line and a within-country spread are different slots. A global total can fall while one national slot stays live; Fig 1 :13 **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**. Averaging them destroys the signal (Fig 1 :22).
2. **One actor, two valences.** Fig 11 :49 **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :50 **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** Globalization can register as benefactor for the headcount residual and malfactor for the spread residual. That is a relation, not a contradiction and not a fixed property.
3. **The cross-scale transform is lossy.** Fig 4 :27 **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."** Aggregating national distributions into a global count discards the within-country spread. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip gap `R_T` to be measured, not assumed zero. The gap is the honest report of what global aggregation gave up.
4. **Anchors differ by slot.** D1 and D2 have constitutive anchors (a declared line and count; a declared distributional statistic). Fig 2 :29 **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** D3, the causal attribution, has none yet. Fig 2 :31 and Fig 13 :38 require independence between frames, and studies drawing on the same surveys are one frame wearing many names.
5. **Measurement before causation.** Fig 3 :20 **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** A causal verdict made before the measures are fixed is a reading taken below the break.
6. **The "while" is a conjunction, not a comparison bond.** The ⋛ reading was tried and retracted (see `01` F7 and `06` Q5). ⋛ governs a threshold inside D1, where income crosses the line. Headcount and Gini are in different currencies (Fig 12 :125 **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**) and must not be summed.
7. **The causal magnitude is `gap`.** Fig 8 :39 **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134 **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** No figure line contains a trade elasticity or a counterfactual Gini. The framework decides the shape; world measurement speaks to the magnitudes as secondary evidence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that globalization *does* reduce poverty by any amount, and not that it *does* worsen inequality by any amount. Those are empirical claims with a different ground.
- Not that the two trends are a paradox to be resolved or a tradeoff to be balanced into one welfare index. Averaging is the forbidden move.
- Not that globalization is intrinsically beneficial or harmful. Valence is relative to the residual.
- Not that ⋛ joins poverty and inequality; that reading is retracted and preserved in the log.
- Not `grounded`. No dataset was run and no source-discriminating instrument was applied in this simulation.
- Not a ranking of experts by likely agreement, and not a claim that more studies agreeing settles anything (Fig 7 :13).

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another review. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading that cannot distinguish sources *"demonstrates its own inadequacy"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the green-light case. Here:

- **Threshold Test:** for a given country and year, does the headcount change when the absolute line moves while the relative line is held, and vice versa? That separates the two slots at the ground.
- **Round-trip test:** carry a national distribution up to a global headcount and back; enumerate and, if possible, quantify what fails to return (`R_T`).
- **Source test:** a natural experiment or border/regime discontinuity that changes trade exposure while holding national institutions fixed, read against both slots separately. That is the missing D3 anchor.
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` §5: one global scalar accounts for both trends; the two readings are one quantity; an unmoving instrument settles causation; an actor has intrinsic valence; `R_T = 0`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The causal magnitude is unreached by the figures and by the instruments used here, not declared impossible in principle. The framework stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :44 *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* The shape is triangulated; the magnitudes are a `gap` recorded in plain sight. Prestige or volume of agreement is not an instrument (Fig 9 :60 **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**).

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not return a verdict on whether globalization helps or hurts. It says the question is two questions wearing one "while". A global poverty headcount is one slot, inequality inside a country is another, and a global total can fall while a national slot stays live; averaging them destroys the only signal that tells you where to look. One actor can carry opposite valences for two different residuals, so "poverty down, inequality up" is a legal two-slot state rather than a paradox. The step from national distributions to a global count is lossy, and what it drops is the within-country spread, so the round trip gap is the thing to measure. The framework refuses to name how much of either trend globalization caused; that part is `gap` until a source-discriminating instrument answers it. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
