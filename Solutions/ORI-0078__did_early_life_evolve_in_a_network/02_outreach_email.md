# Outreach draft — check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 claim below is a shape with a refuter, not a historical finding. If it is presented as "the figures prove early life was a network", the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** one checkable shape about gene sharing, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** in the framework's six-term substrate, "network" is one of three irreducible structures: it names the fact that entities relate and that influence travels. A line of descent is the degenerate case of a graph: one bond type, no cross-links. Gene sharing between communities is an import bond, and the framework's closure rule says import is the only operator that moves the wall. Reading a lineage as a closed set of genes, a community cannot acquire a gene that was not already in its closure except by contact with a community whose closure differs. Notice that this says "novel to the lineage" is not "new to the world".
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures tell us what early life actually did. They do not. The historical question is a `gap` for this derivation, settled only by sequence data, instruments and the record, not by a metatheory. I am also not claiming that two communities holding the same gene proves transfer: shared ancestry and later import look identical in the contents, and only the dated provenance separates them. The tree of life sentence in one figure governs cross-scale transforms; I use it as an analogue with a declared strain, not as evidence about genealogy.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a community acquiring a gene absent from its closure with no contact with a differing closure;
> 2. deriving "entities relate" from the other five structures;
> 3. a global tree statistic that preserves a transfer signal that aggregation would erase;
> 4. an instrument reading of the deep-time record returning cleanly separated lineages with no import at the relevant scale.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and formula are here. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. In this framework a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the architecture's own write-up warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula". A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
