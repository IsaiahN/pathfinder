# Graph versus chain: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` for the structural derivation, `general` for the bond rule. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading** (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

If a "network of gene-sharing communities" and "cleanly separated lineages" are both expressible in the framework, are they different *kinds* of thing, or the same kind under different bonds? And what order, if any, does the network impose that a chain does not? The question matters because a claim that they are incomparable kinds would make the empirical question meaningless, while a claim that they are one substance under two spellings would dissolve it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six (Space, Time, Energy states; Spectrum, Gradient, Network structures). Bonds held: the seven, including sequence (→), production (⇒) and identity (≡). Negation is available but is not a bond. A settled gene, once purchased by the ground, becomes an atom at the next level by Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". No new primitive is introduced by this derivation.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is a lineage a different kind of object from a network? No.

Fig 12 :48 "A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."
Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."

A chain is explicitly the degenerate case of a bonded arrangement, not a new kind of object. So Option L (cleanly separated descent) is a graph with a single bond type, vertical sequence, and no cross-links. Option N adds a second bond type between communities: import of heritable material. The two are not different categories. They are different arrangements over the same bond vocabulary. The framework therefore does not refuse either as a possible composition; it only forbids calling the arrangement "the ingredients".

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a chain that requires a bond type unavailable to a graph, or a graph that requires a new primitive beyond Network plus the seven bonds. Either would make the options incomparable and this derivation wrong.

### Q2 — Are different gene trees isomers? Yes, at the right level.

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
Fig 13 :123 "Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms"

Take a fixed set of genes as atoms at the level where each has settled against the ground (Fig 12 :104). Two different gene trees over that same set use different sequence bonds and different branch arrangements. By Fig 12 :117 they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Two communities holding the same gene may also be the same atom reached by different routes (Fig 13 :121), which is why contents alone cannot tell convergence from import (Fig 8 :51).

**Falsifier:** show that two discordant gene trees over the same gene set differ in their atoms rather than in their bonds, for example by demonstrating that the genes themselves are different under any settled description. Then the isomer claim fails and the disagreement is about inventory, not arrangement.

### Q3 — What order does the network add? Crossability, not sequence.

Operators table: "→ sequence A then B; B needs A to have happened" is ordered. A network does not replace this order inside a lineage; it adds edges between lineages. The relevant asymmetry is therefore not temporal priority but whether a node's closure can be extended from a peer. Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

A vocabulary that lists genes can say what a community contains. It cannot say how a gene arrived. That is the bond, and the bond is what the question is about.

### Q4 — One tree or many, and the round-trip gap

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

If each gene tree is a settled molecule, composing them into a single universal tree is an abstraction step: many detailed routes are compressed into one hierarchy. Bringing that universal tree back down to particular genes cannot recover the discarded routes. The round-trip gap R_T is nonzero. The honest report is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, not an identity claim. This is the structural counterpart of the pattern-pluralism result in the HGT literature: no single hierarchy is the necessary result of the mechanisms.

**Falsifier:** produce a composition of many gene trees into one universal tree that preserves every per-gene route on return, so that R_T is zero. Under Fig 4 this would require the transform to be invertible, which the figure denies.

## ANALOGUE STATUS

The "tree of life" sentence (Fig 10 :70) is used here only to support the weaker statement that a tree can be an outside view of a lossier relation. It is recorded as a declared-strain analogue in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F3 and carries no verdict weight. No biological phylogeny conclusion is drawn from it.

## CONCLUSION

A lineage is the degenerate case of a network, not a rival kind. Gene trees over a shared gene set are isomers: same atoms under different bonds. The network adds crossability between closures, not a new temporal order. Compressing many gene trees into one universal tree has a nonzero round-trip gap. All four claims are `specified` structural results and none reports what early life actually did.
