## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union compound with two opposite answers. Non-fixedness is composable: a fixed continuum is a derived remainder, a settled geometry can become an atom for the next level, and the background is a habitat that has been mistaken for a ground. Term-absence is not composable: space and time are two of the six indivisible primitives the loop assumes throughout, and the habitat can only be substituted, never subtracted. No program is named. The result is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The literal "no spacetime" reading is refused, not answered, because the refusal is a negation and negation is not a bond.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a theory before the residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the split, the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The program binding is removable without touching the substrate, and the two verdicts can be corrected independently if one of the four refuters fires. `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already separates them. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A formally agreed definition plus a source-discriminating instrument would sharpen the predicate; an observable that distinguishes derived geometry from a fixed one would name the slot; a composing quantum system with no space and no time term, or a derivation of the continuum from the other five as an indivisible seventh, would break the shape. A measurement showing quantum theory requires a fixed background in principle would break condition A. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige, consensus and the number of people who agree are not such instruments.
