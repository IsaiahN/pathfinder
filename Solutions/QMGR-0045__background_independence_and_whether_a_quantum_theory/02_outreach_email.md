**Subject:** Check one structural claim about background independence before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, stated as an effect:** can a quantum theory be formulated without a fixed spacetime? Under the six-term substrate I am using, this is two conditions in one bundle, and they have opposite answers.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. *No fixed background* is composable. A fixed smooth continuum is a derived remainder, not a primitive. A settled arrangement can serve as an atom for whatever composes over it, but it cannot be promoted to an unchanging given. Space and time are retained; the fixed four-dimensional continuum is not.
> 2. *No spacetime term at all* is not composable. Space (adjacency, near, far, between, contained) and time (the ordered succession of differences) are two of the six primitives the loop assumes throughout. Removing them leaves a system with four of six terms, which the framework says does not function. The habitat can be substituted; it cannot be subtracted.
>
> So the answer is yes to the question as physicists usually mean it, and no to a literal reading. The two must not be averaged into one verdict.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a particular program (loop quantum gravity, causal dynamical triangulations, causal sets, or any other) is the answer. The figures return a shape, not a name. I am not claiming a measurement at the Planck scale settles this, and I am not claiming background independence has a settled definition. Both are open in the literature.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. deriving any one of the six terms from the other five;
> 2. exhibiting a composing quantum system with no space term and no time term that still settles against a ground;
> 3. deriving the continuum from the six and then showing it is an indivisible seventh member;
> 4. a measurement showing that quantum theory requires a fixed background in principle.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README and citation rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture (Medium): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — Isaiah
