# Expert verification packet: compression and "what matters"

**What this is:** a claim in a form an information theorist, compression engineer, statistician or machine-learning researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the cited laws, `gap` on a universal relevance measure, not `grounded`.** No measurement at a receiving check is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Compression preserves what matters only relative to a bound relevance term imported from the use frame and scored at a ground that does not move. The compression ratio is a filter for what to try, never a witness for whether preservation happened. A lossy transform is licensed only with a measured round-trip gap at the level where the receiving check reads. The information that tends to be lost is the typed arrangement between the parts, not the ingredient inventory: two representations with the same atoms and different bonds are isomers whose bit counts cannot distinguish them. "Who determines what matters" is an anchor question: the ground of the use is the legitimate anchor because the question is constitutively about that use; a designer-chosen distortion measure allowed to decide the outcome is a convention that has stopped being a convention. The particular relevance variable is not supplied by the framework.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 The compression ratio is a filter, not a verdict; the gap must be measured

- **Statement:** a quantity the compressor produces cannot settle whether what mattered survived. The transform must be reported with its round-trip gap at the reading level.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27, **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** Fig 9 :60, **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** Fig 4 :31-32, **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**
- **What to check:** does the claim survive on a real codec and a declared perception or task check? Take a rate-distortion or rate-distortion-perception curve and ask whether the bit count alone would have predicted the check's verdict.
- **Refuter:** a compressor whose ratio predicts the receiving check's verdict across two independent applications with low overlap; or a lossy transform whose round trip reproduces the source at the reading level with a measured zero gap, twice. Then C1 falls.

### C2 The carrier of what matters is the arrangement, and isomer pairs are the test

- **Statement:** a flat representation that keeps ingredients and drops the typed relation loses the substance while the count reports success. Two arrangements over the same atoms with different bonds are isomers under Fig 12.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5, **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :94, **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :117, **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- **What to check:** construct two representations with identical atom sets and different arrangements (for example two factorisations or two tokenisations with equal bit cost). Does the receiving check distinguish them? Is the difference invisible in the ingredient count?
- **Refuter:** two such arrangements that behave identically on the ground under a declared distortion measure and two independent uses. Then the isomer claim fails and the weaker relevance-term claim remains.

### C3 The criterion is an anchor question, not a coding question

- **Statement:** the ground of the use decides what matters, and it must not update when frames push on it. A designer-chosen distortion measure is a convention; if it decides the outcome it is disqualified.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :36, **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 10 :47, **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
- **What to check:** pick a distortion measure in your field and ask whether its answer changes when two independent application frames push on it. Ask who chose it and on what evidence. The rate-distortion reference states that *"The notion of distortion is a subject of on-going discussion."* and that the measure *"should preferably be modeled on human perception and perhaps aesthetics"*; that is an acknowledged import, not a theorem.
- **Refuter:** a criterion that moves under pressure from frames and is still accepted as settling the question, reproduced twice; or a single measure that prices bits and establishment-actions in the same units with a ranking invariant to a change in the ground's refusal rate (Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that semantic information has been defined here, or that Shannon coding settles meaning.
- Not that the round trip is generally zero, or that a final room exists (Fig 7 :6, **"the limit: no last room"**).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Round trip:** send a compressed representation back up and compare at the reading level. Report the gap.
2. **Isomer test:** equal atoms, different bonds, measure the receiving check (Fig 12 :117).
3. **Anchor test:** push on the criterion from two independent frames; if it moves, it was never the anchor (Fig 2 :30).
4. **Two-currency test:** price bits and establishment-actions separately; do not sum them (Fig 12 :125-126).
5. **Gap closure:** produce a verbatim figure line naming a universal relevance primitive. That closes the declared `gap`.
