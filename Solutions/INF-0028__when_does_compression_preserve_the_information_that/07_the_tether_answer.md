# The Tether's answer: when compression preserves what matters

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the cited laws, `gap` on a universal relevance measure, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## 1. The residual it answers

A transform X to T reduces a representation. The user asks two things that must stay separate: under what condition does what survives include what is needed, and who fixes "needed". Read as an effect, preservation happens when the far end's check still passes after the transform, scored at a ground that does not move. It fails when the compressor scores itself by its own savings. Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** The whole question is a union compound of transform, criterion, ground and relevance; Fig 9 :5 says **"Split it rather than search."**

## 2. The shape, per domain, verdicts not averaged

**D1, coding and reconstruction.** Compression is lossy by design and the loss is a measurement, not an error to hide. Fig 4 :27: **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."** Fig 4 :31-32: **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."** A preservation claim without its round trip R_T is dead reckoning.

**D2, the compressor's frame.** The acceptance test is an MDL bargain: Fig 5 :43-44, **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."** But the minimisation runs inside a hypothesis class, and Fig 6 :21 says **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** So "shortest" means best in this class, not what matters. The structural content is the bond: Fig 12 :94 **"The arrangement is the substance."** A flat description keeps the ingredients and loses the relation, and **"nothing signals that anything was lost"** (Fig 12 :16).

**D3, the ground.** "Who determines what matters" is answered here, and only here. Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** The question is constitutively about the use at the far end, so that use's ground is the anchor. The compressor is a frame and cannot be the anchor. A designer-chosen distortion measure is a convention, and Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**

**D4, relevance and contact.** "Matters" must be bound as an ordinary term: Fig 5 :65-66, **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."** It is then imported from the frame that holds the use, with a source gate and a shadow test, because Fig 6 :23 says **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** The particular variable is supplied by the application, not by the figures.

**Gap.** No figure names a primitive "importance" or fixes a universal relevance measure. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. That is a plain `gap`, not a silence and not a failure.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a compression ratio is worthless. It is a budget filter (Fig 9 :60) and disqualified only as a verdict.
- Not that any distortion function, perceptual metric or relevance variable is the framework's answer. Those are imports with provenance.
- Not that "semantic information" has been defined, or that Shannon coding settles meaning. The source and destination structure is a frame; meaning is a contact question.
- Not that the round trip can be driven to zero in general. The gap is the honest report of what was given up.
- Not grounded. No measurement at the receiving check has been run here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument at the receiving end, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* states the general case: an instrument that reads the same thing for several sources *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **The round-trip test:** send the representation back up and compare at the level where the check reads. Report the gap, do not assume zero.
- **The two-curve test:** hold bits constant or hold distortion constant and show that the arrangement, not the count, changes the receiving check (Fig 12 :117 isomer test).
- **The anchor test:** push on the criterion from two independent frames. If its answer moves, it was never the anchor (Fig 2 :30).
- **The independence test:** have two frames with different evidence pools bind the relevance term separately. If they converge without shared data, that is corroboration at high independence (Fig 8 :50); if they share a pool, it is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Standing refuters: a compression ratio that predicts the ground's verdict across two independent applications; a zero measured round trip; isomer pairs that behave identically; a movable criterion accepted as an anchor; a verbatim figure line naming a universal importance primitive.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The relevance term for any actual application must be imported from that application, and the framework cannot name it in advance (Fig 13 :135). Different applications will bind different terms. *They're Scanning Books* records the reason: *"Every instrument is a crystallized hypothesis about what is worth measuring, and the hypothesis comes from human awareness."* The answer stops at the limit of the instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Compression preserves what matters only relative to a relevance term that has been stated, bound and tested, not relative to the number of bits it saved. The bit ratio is a budget filter and is disqualified as a verdict, because a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces. A lossy transform is licensed only when it carries its round-trip gap, measured where the receiving check reads; the gap is the honest report of what was given up, not an error to be hidden. What tends to be lost is the arrangement between the parts, and two representations with the same ingredients but different bonds are different things whose bit counts cannot tell them apart. As for who determines what matters: not the compressor, and not a designer-chosen distortion measure allowed to decide the outcome, but the ground of the use at the far end, which must not move when frames push on it. The particular relevance variable is an import from the application, provenanced as such, and the framework refuses to name a universal one. That is a shape and a set of refuters, not a settled answer.
