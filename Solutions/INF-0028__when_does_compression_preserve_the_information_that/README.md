## The residual in effect terms

A transform X to T throws away detail. If what survives still lets the far end pass the check the far end actually runs, preservation happened. If the compressor scores itself by how much it saved, the signal can look perfectly compressed while the check at the far end fails. The question has two parts that must not be averaged: (1) what structural condition makes the surviving representation carry what is needed, and (2) who or what fixes the criterion "needed". Part 2 is an anchor question, not a coding question, and the figures decide it differently from the way rate-distortion theory states it.

## What an answer would look like

Bind "what matters" as an ordinary term before running the derivation (THE_FORMULA.md, "Direction"): a relevance variable that predicts something, accrues a residual, and can be refused. The sought-for answer then has this shape: preservation is relative to a declared relevance term; the lossy transform is bracketed by a measured round-trip gap; the criterion of mattering is imported from the frame that holds the use, provenanced as such; and the anchor that settles whether preservation happened is the ground, which must not update when frames push on it. A specific distortion function is not supplied by the figures. That member stays a `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a particular distortion measure is the figure's answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: (1) Fig 1 :26-27 disqualifies a quantity a frame also produces as its own verdict; (2) Fig 2 :40 makes agreement inside one evidence pool worthless as a verdict; (3) Fig 10 :42 says a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it. The first contact therefore carries the residual and the refuter, not the framework.
