**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the competition/innovation framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about competition and innovation. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does competition encourage innovation, or does temporary market power fund research?" is posed as an either/or. In effect terms it is a union of two conditions with different anchors, and the effect has to be indexed per firm-slot, not averaged. Competition is a habitat relation whose valence is relative to the residual: the same rival pressure helps a neck-and-neck firm that can take the next step and hurts a laggard that cannot recover the cost of catching up. A sheltered rent is a spendable difference that can pay the action cost of a search, but only while it crosses as a head start that makes the next search cheaper, not as a recording of one past success that blocks the next one. The sign flip is a threshold comparison, not a blend.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal optimal competition intensity; that a firm's own R&D figure or a concentration ratio settles anything; that a monopolist cannot innovate (it can import from science, suppliers and users); that the framework names the turning point. The figures return a shape, not a name. The number is owed to an instrument, and I do not have it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a measurement in which the same firm-slot's innovation rate moves in one direction across all distance-to-frontier classes;
> 2. a demonstration that pressure and shelter form a partition with an empty intersection;
> 3. a permanent, non-revisable rent that keeps making successive searches cheaper across several cycles, with no entry threat and no other differing frame in contact;
> 4. a larger R&D budget that reliably produces new settled terms while the live-residual, reachability and novelty guards all stay at zero.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - NBER WP 9269, Aghion et al., *Competition and Innovation: An Inverted U Relationship*: https://www.nber.org/papers/w9269
> - NBER WP 19987, Aghion, Bechtold, Cassar and Herz, *The Causal Effects of Competition on Innovation: Experimental Evidence*: https://www.nber.org/papers/w19987
> - Brockhurst et al. 2014, *Running with the Red Queen* ( Proceedings of the Royal Society B, open access ): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4240979/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
