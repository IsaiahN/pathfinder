## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does competition encourage firms to innovate, or can temporary market power fund research?" is posed as an either/or. In effect terms it is a union of two conditions with different anchors, and the effect is indexed per firm-slot, never averaged. Competition is a habitat relation whose valence is relative to the residual: the same pressure helps a neck-and-neck firm that can take the next step and hurts a laggard that cannot recover the catch-up cost. A sheltered rent is a spendable difference that can pay the action cost of a search, but only while it crosses as a head start that makes the next search cheaper, not as a recording of one past success that blocks the next one. The sign flip is a threshold comparison, not a blend. No figure line returns the intensity at which the sign flips; that number is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus empirically checkable; the per-slot valence claim
- **Statement:** no standing verdict assigns a fixed sign to competition or market power. The effect is per slot and relative to distance-to-frontier and to the kind of slack.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 — *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 — *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51 — *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :47 — *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*. Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** within one market, does the sign of the competition effect flip across distance-to-frontier classes? The experimental literature (Aghion, Bechtold, Cassar and Herz, NBER WP 19987) reports exactly this flip in laboratory R&D choices.
- **Refuter:** a measurement in which the same firm-slot's innovation rate moves in one direction across all distance-to-frontier classes. If it fires, C1 and the shape claim fall.

### C2 — framework-internal; the union and threshold claim
- **Statement:** pressure and shelter are overlapping conditions in a union, not a partition, and the sign flip is an ordered comparison, not a symmetric blend.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 — *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 12 :87 — *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (also Operators :49). Fig 12 :89 — *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- **What to check:** are there real slots where shelter and pressure coexist (rent funded under a rival threat)? If yes, the intersection is non-empty and the partition reading fails. The inverted U in Aghion et al. 2002/2005 is the econometric signature of two opposing mechanisms sharing one axis.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that the conditions form a partition with an empty intersection, or a monotone single-signed effect across the whole range with no threshold.

### C3 — framework-internal; the head-start boundary and the budget guard
- **Statement:** a rent funds innovation only while it is spent as a head start on a repeatable search; a larger R&D budget is a guard, not a mint.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :24 — *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."* Fig 4 :25 — *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 5 :33 — *"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."* Fig 5 :48 — *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*
- **What to check:** track a sheltered rent across several innovation cycles. Does it keep producing new settled terms, or does it become a defence of one past success? Is the innovation outcome read by an instrument independent of the firm's own R&D report (Fig 1 :24-27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*)?
- **Refuter:** a permanent, non-revisable rent that keeps making successive searches cheaper across several cycles with no entry threat and no other differing frame in contact; or a larger budget that reliably yields new settled terms while the live-residual, reachability and novelty guards all stay at zero.
