# Isomers and order: pressure and shelter over a fixed atom set

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `general` on the bond vocabulary, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Can the two options in the question be written over one atom set, and if so what is their structural relation: a bond difference (isomers), an arrangement difference (functional group), or a union with its own loop? A second question: what is the dependency order of the chain from pressure through slack to a settled innovation?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states); Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. The economic bindings used here are onto held atoms: competitive pressure is a relation in the network (Fig 11 :47); a rent is a spendable difference, a gradient (Fig 13 :22, :45-47); the research method is the intervening structure; the market is the anchor (Fig 2 :29-30). ## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are pressure and shelter isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Both conditions can be written over the same atoms: firm (an instance of the network), rival relation (network), rent (gradient over the market state), research method (structure), market (state), new term (settled molecule). Same atom set, different bond arrangement, different behaviour: under pressure the gradient is spent on escape; under shelter the gradient is spent on a planned search.
- ⇒ Over the full shared atom set they are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition. This is a real checkable structural claim, not an analogy: the two share every atom and differ only in which structure mediates which gradient.
- **Weaker form:** as the question writes them, "competition" and "market power" share only the market and the firm, so by Fig 12's definition they are not isomers but members of a **functional group**: Fig 12 :124, **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The group is "spendable difference routed through a research method to a settled term"; the qualifier is "pressured" or "sheltered."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either condition can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Concretely: if sheltered research can proceed with no network relation to any rival, or pressured research with no spendable gradient, the fixed-atom condition is broken. This is a live strain, not a hidden one (the sheltered firm still has suppliers, users and science, so its network atom is not empty; see F7 of `01`).

### Q2 — What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

Ordered chain for this question:

1. **Perception.** Read R at the slot. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Without this, every later step uses a pooled signal.
2. **Vocabulary.** Terms for pressure, slack, distance to frontier and head start. Fig 3 :7: **"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"**.
3. **Objective.** Say what counts as progress in the slot. Fig 3 :10: **"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"**.
4. **Planning.** Choose the R&D action under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Fig 3 :15: **"name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem"**.

The policy question as posed tries to set step 4 (how much competition, how much protection) before steps 1 to 3 exist. That is work above the break: Fig 3 :27, **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

### Q3 — What is the union's order, and can it be unbundled?

- Fig 9 :54-56: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** A bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions; one lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- Unbundled: (a) pressure-only slots, (b) shelter-only slots, (c) the intersection, where a sheltered rent is being spent under a rival threat. Case (c) is categorically different: Fig 9 :57, **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- Fig 9 :59: **"Which is the second-order value of a big lever, and usually the larger one: not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."** Unbundling is worth doing even if no policy changes, because it makes the residual statable.
- The union is second-order: each first-order firm innovation, once settled against the ground, becomes an atom for the industry-level composition (Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**). The industry-level question, which kinds of firm survive, composes over settled firm-level innovations. This is recursion, not a new primitive.

### Q4 — Analogues and their limits

- **Red Queen / character displacement (low overlap).** Brockhurst et al. 2014 report that competition among competitors tends toward character displacement and weakens selection, while antagonistic interactions with differing counterparts sustain change. This is the Fig 8 :47-49 analogue at work: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** Rivals with the same closure are one frame wearing two names; rivals or antagonists with differing closures provide imports. **Limit:** an ecological analogue about allele frequencies is not a claim about patent races. Used for mechanism shape only.
- **Membrane direction (transfer strain).** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** A temporary rent is a head start only while it funds a repeatable method; a rent spent on defending one past success is a recording. **Limit:** whether a given rent actually crossed as a method is an empirical question requiring the discriminator described in `05` section 4.
- **Threshold glyph (transfer strain).** Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. The inverted U is read as ⋛, an ordered comparison. **Limit:** the operators table governs formal bonds, not regression functional forms; this is a binding, marked `specified`.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, pressured and sheltered research are **isomers** (same atoms, different bond arrangement, different behaviour). As the question actually writes them they are a **functional group** with two qualifiers. Their union is a second-order compound whose order is: perceive per slot, build vocabulary, state the objective, plan, carry. Unbundling the union is valuable because it makes the residue describable even before a lever is chosen. The isomer claim's falsifier is the loss of an atom from either condition; it is live and recorded, not waived.
