## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether the Tether endorses Option A (competition encourages firms to innovate), Option B (temporary market power provides the profits and stability needed for research), or a third reading, for the question at `Questions/economics questions.txt` line 65. What each leads to: A sends a universal pro-competition verdict that cannot account for the laggard whose catch-up rents are removed; B sends a universal pro-rent verdict that cannot account for the head start becoming a recording that blocks the next search, and scores the firm with a quantity the firm also produces; the third reading sends a per-slot shape, two verdicts kept separate, four refuters written in advance, and an explicit `gap` for the number. The figures decide, the world sources corroborate only.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The change in the arrival rate of new settled terms at a firm-slot when the habitat condition changes. Arity: two conditions plus their intersection, so a union compound, not a dichotomy. Symmetry: the governing bond is comparison, an ordered threshold relation, not a symmetric join. Scale: first order at the firm-slot, second order at industry composition. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- **Domain 1, the firm's innovation loop.** Actors: R&D team, budget, researchers, library Γ. Currency: new settled terms priced in bits and actions. Anchor: realized market purchases and profits, which do not move when the firm reports its own R&D. Question type: partition of what composes inside closure(Γ) and what must be imported.
- **Domain 2, the market habitat.** Actors: rivals, entrants, customers, suppliers, regulators as a second-order actor. Currency: profit, rent, market share, survival. Anchor: realized transactions, a market that does not change its answer because a frame thinks something (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."). Competition is a relation here, not an actor: Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency". Question type: union, since one firm can be pressured in one slot and sheltered in another.
- **Domain 3, the policy and antitrust frame.** Actors: regulators, courts, complainants, public affairs. Currency: legal permission, concentration indices, remedies. Anchor: consumer welfare and independently measured innovation. Failure genus: Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Question type: a binding onto the habitat, not a new primitive.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive R per slot, build a vocabulary for pressure and slack, state the objective, plan the R&D action, name what worked and carry it. Setting an intensity before the vocabulary exists is work above the break (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

Strain test applied: Fig 6 (imports move the wall) versus the claim that shelter alone funds innovation. Re-census resolves it: Fig 6 governs where a primitive comes from, while shelter funds the action cost of searching an already-held closure (Fig 12 :125-127). ## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate and never overrule. Fields were chosen for low overlap.

- **Economics, observational.** Aghion, Bloom, Blundell, Griffith and Howitt, "Competition and Innovation: An Inverted U Relationship," NBER WP 9269 (2002), QJE 120(2), 2005. Quote: "This paper investigates the relationship between product market competition (PMC) and innovation. A growth model is developed in which competition may increase the incremental profit from innovating; on the other hand, competition may also reduce innovation incentives for laggards." Prediction: opposing mechanisms on one axis, sign flip by position. Corroborates the union and threshold verdict. Overlap: high with the other economics sources, one witness.
- **Economics, experiment.** Aghion, Bechtold, Cassar and Herz, "The Causal Effects of Competition on Innovation: Experimental Evidence," NBER WP 19987 (2014), JLEO 34(2), 2018. Quote: "First, we find that increased competition leads to a significant increase in R&D investments by neck-and-neck firms. Second, increased competition decreases R&D investments by firms that are lagging behind, in particular if the time horizon is short." Prediction: valence flips with distance to frontier and horizon. Corroborates the per-slot valence verdict at the strongest available ground level. Overlap: same economics pool, different instrument.
- **Economics, theory.** Kenneth Arrow, "Economic Welfare and the Allocation of Resources for Invention," NBER chapter (1962). Quote (indexed excerpt): "an incentive to invent can exist even under perfect competition in the product markets though not, of course, in the 'market' for the information contained in the invention." Prediction: neither option is universally right; the market for knowledge is the structural problem. Corroborates the refusal of both universal verdicts.
- **Economics / history of thought.** Schumpeter, "Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy" (1942), as quoted in the Wikipedia "Creative destruction" entry opened this session. Quote: "successful innovation is normally a source of temporary market power, eroding the profits and position of old firms, yet ultimately succumbing to the pressure of new inventions commercialised by competing entrants." Prediction: rent is a phase of a cycle. Corroborates the head-start boundary.
- **Evolutionary biology (low overlap).** Brockhurst, Chapman, King, Mank, Paterson and Hurst, "Running with the Red Queen: the role of biotic conflicts in evolution," Proc R Soc B 281(1797), 2014, PMC4240979. Quote: "Competition is generally unlikely to drive perpetual evolutionary change of this kind. Coevolution of competitors tends towards character displacement, and thus weakens the intensity of the biotic interaction and the strength of selection over time." Prediction: similar rivals tend to differentiate and the arms race weakens; differing antagonists sustain change. **Conflicts** with the naive competition-always-accelerates reading. The conflict rule was triggered and sent back to the census; it narrowed the claim to differing closures and did not flip the figure verdict.
- **Evolution education (low overlap, definitional).** UC Museum of Paleontology, Understanding Evolution, "Red Queen hypothesis" glossary. Quote: "The idea that, in order for a species to maintain a particular niche in an ecosystem and its fitness relative to other species, that species must be constantly undergoing adaptive evolution because the organisms with which it is coevolving are themselves undergoing adaptive evolution." Prediction: relative position, not absolute output, is maintained. Corroborates the relative-valence framing.

Overlap declaration: the four economics sources are one evidence pool with several instruments, not four witnesses. The two evolution sources are a genuinely lower-overlap pool. The framework essays share the figures' author and evidence pool completely and are not independent witnesses. No world source overrules a figure line.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Neither Option A nor Option B survives as a universal claim. The figure verdict is a shape: the effect is indexed per firm-slot and never averaged; competition is a habitat relation with residual-relative valence; pressure and shelter are a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors; the sign flip is a threshold comparison; a rent is a spendable difference that funds research only while it is a head start and not a recording; rivals are one import source among several; the turning point is a declared `gap` that is a verdict and not a silence. Over the shared atom set, pressured and sheltered research are isomers (same atoms, different bond, different behaviour). Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the number, not `grounded`, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, carries four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a policy conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second cost is policy: treating a frame-internal concentration or R&D figure as an anchor could justify a rule that fixes an outcome instead of keeping a channel to the ground.
- **undoable:** yes. The specific bindings (which condition dominates in which slot) are separable from the shape and can be withdrawn without touching the derivation. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet; no primitive has to be withdrawn. The head-start boundary is revisable as new instruments read the source of a rent.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminator that reads innovation independently of the firm's own reporting and shows a single-signed effect across all distance-to-frontier classes would break the valence claim; an empty intersection would break the union; a permanent rent that keeps funding new searches across cycles with no differing frame would break the head-start boundary; a budget variation yielding settled terms with all three guards at zero would break the mint claim; a figure re-census that admits a numeric intensity would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
