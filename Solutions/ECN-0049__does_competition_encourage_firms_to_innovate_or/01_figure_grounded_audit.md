## 0. The residual under audit

> Does competition encourage firms to innovate, or can temporary market power provide the profits and stability needed for research?

**Stated in effect terms.** R is the gap between what a firm's library predicts a market will return and what the market actually returns at a slot: R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|. The question asks which habitat condition reduces that gap at a given firm-slot and which increases it. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A pooled answer of the form "competition helps innovation by X percent" has already thrown away the signal, because the sign of the effect depends on the slot (a frontier firm versus a laggard, a slack-rich incumbent versus a cash-constrained entrant).

**The two options are not answers to one question.** They are two conditions with different anchors: rival pressure is a relation in the habitat (Fig 11 :47), while a sheltered rent is a spendable difference that funds the action cost of a search (Fig 13 :22, :46-47). Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** The compound has its own loop. That is the first structural result, before any world evidence is read.

**What an answer must look like (bound before the run, Formula "Direction").** A shape with four properties: (i) per-slot verdicts, never a pooled average; (ii) union, not partition; (iii) valence relative to the residual, so the same condition helps one slot and harms another; (iv) the head-start / recording boundary that makes the word "temporary" load-bearing. Plus an instrument: an external reading of innovation that does not move when a firm reports its own R&D spending. The numerical turning point is not in this list, because no figure line returns a number.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two conditions (rival pressure; sheltered rent), and their intersection, so a **union compound**, not a binary choice. Symmetry: the relevant bond is comparison ⋛, **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** (Fig 12 :87; Operators :49), which is ordered, not a symmetric join, so "more competition" cannot be treated as a symmetric mixture. Scale: first order at the firm-slot (a firm mints a new term), second order at industry composition (which kinds of firm survive, Fig 8 :26-28, Fig 11 :26-27).
- **Domain 1, the firm's innovation loop.** Actors: the R&D team, the budget, the researchers, the firm's library Γ. Currency: new settled terms (a patent, a process, a product) priced in both bits and actions (Fig 12 :125-127). Anchor: realized market purchases and profits, which do not change because the firm reports a larger R&D figure. Question type: partition (what composes inside closure(Γ) and what has to be imported).
- **Domain 2, the market habitat.** Actors: rivals, entrants, substitutes, customers, suppliers, regulators as a second-order actor. Currency: profit, rent, market share, survival. Anchor: realized transactions; **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :29-30). Competition is not an actor here; it is a relation: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"** (Fig 11 :47). Question type: union (pressure and shelter overlap in the same habitat and the same firm can be in both).
- **Domain 3, the policy / antitrust frame.** Actors: regulators, courts, complainants, firms' public affairs. Currency: legal permission, concentration indices, remedies. Anchor: consumer welfare and independently measured innovation, never the concentration index alone if that index moves when firms change their own reporting. Failure genus: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47). Question type: a binding from the policy frame onto the habitat, not a new primitive.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** (1) perceive: measure R per firm-slot; (2) vocabulary: have terms for pressure, slack, distance to frontier and head start; (3) objective: say what counts as progress in that slot; (4) plan: choose the R&D action under incomplete information; (5) learn and carry: name what worked, settle it, carry the method. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** A policy that sets an intensity before step 2 is effort spent above the break.

**Partition or union.** Union. The same firm can face strong rival pressure in one product slot and enjoy sheltered rents in another; the two conditions overlap and a single lever (max competition, or max protection) clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :54-56). No averaging of the two verdicts.

**Strain test applied up front.** The apparent conflict is between Fig 6 :23 (**"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**) and the Schumpeterian claim that a sheltered incumbent can still innovate. Re-census: Fig 6 governs where a new *primitive* comes from, while shelter funds the *action cost* of searching an already-held closure (Fig 12 :125-127). Different quantities, no strain. The two are kept as separate findings (F4 and F7) and the resolution is recorded in section 8.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 — Competition is a habitat relation with residual-relative valence (triangulated)

Fig 11 :47: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**. Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A firm's competitor is not a "good" or "bad" actor. For a neck-and-neck firm with a reachable next step, a rival's pressure is a benefactor to the residual: it makes the predicted return fail and forces a mint. For a laggard several steps behind, the same rival is a malfactor: it removes the rents that would pay the action cost of a catch-up search and shortens the horizon over which a sunk R&D cost could be recovered. The figure refuses a standing list, so the verdict must be stated per slot.

**Refuter:** a figure line that assigns a fixed valence to competition or to market power independent of the residual, or a measurement in which the same slot's innovation rate moves in one direction for all distance-to-frontier classes. The second is the ground-level refuter; it is not met by the sources opened here.

**Status: `triangulated`**, Fig 11 (valence and habitat relations) with the experimental evidence in `10_source_ledger.csv` (Aghion, Bechtold, Cassar and Herz 2014/2018), which draws the same sign flip from a different instrument (laboratory R&D choices, not the six-term census). Low overlap: economics experiment versus metatheory.

### F2 — The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (triangulated)

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

"Competition or market power" is posed as an either/or. Under the census it is a union: pressure and shelter can both be present in the same firm, in different product slots or at different moments of a single innovation cycle (a sheltered rent funds the search; a rival's move decides whether the resulting term is settled). A policy that maximises competition clears the pressure-only region and leaves the shelter-only and intersection regions; a policy that grants permanent protection does the reverse. Neither lever settles the compound.

Fig 9 :4-5 adds the first diagnostic: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** The century-old Arrow/Schumpeter dispute has exactly this shape: each rule works on a different subset of firms.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the two conditions are a partition, so that every firm-slot falls under exactly one and the intersection is empty. A single clean case of a firm simultaneously sheltered in one slot and pressured in another defeats the refuter.

**Status: `triangulated`**, Fig 9 with Aghion et al. 2002/2005 (an inverted U is the signature of two opposing mechanisms sharing one axis, not the victory of one).

### F3 — The sign flip is a threshold reading, not a blend (single-source on the figure side)

Fig 12 :87 and Operators :49: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. Fig 12 :89: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."** Fig 12 :89-90 (Operator test) identifies ⋛ as the ordered comparison of two quantities. Operators :51: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**.

An inverted-U relationship between competitive pressure and innovation is what a threshold bond looks like when the two operands are the pressure on the slot and the rent available to it. Below the threshold, increasing pressure raises the incremental profit from escaping the pack; above it, increasing pressure removes the slack that pays for the step. The effect is not an average of two linear effects; it is an ordered comparison whose sign changes at a crossing. A symmetric bond `+` would merge the two operands and return only their sum, which is precisely the pooled "competition helps somewhat" answer the figures refuse.

**Refuter:** a monotone, single-signed effect across the whole observed range with no threshold, or a finding that the comparison bond is the wrong glyph for the relationship (e.g. that the two quantities are additive independent causes). The figure side is single-source (Fig 12 / Operators are one bond definition used twice), so weight is deliberately lower than F1 and F2.

**Status: `single-source`** on the figure side, corroborated at the world level by the Aghion 2005 inverted U and the 2014 experiment. Agreement from the economics pool is one witness, not two independent ones.

### F4 — "Temporary" is the head-start / recording boundary (triangulated)

Fig 4 :22-26:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**
> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

A temporary monopoly rent is the market's version of a head start: it makes the next search cheaper by paying the action cost of R&D. It is legitimate under the figure only while it crosses as a head start, that is, while it is spent on a search that could produce a new settled term. It becomes a recording when the rent is defended as a replay of one past success (the original invention) and used to block the next search. Fig 4 :25 is the exact failure name. Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** extends the same boundary into policy: a protection originally granted as a neutral rule becomes a verdict when it fixes who wins.

World corroboration: the Wikipedia entry on creative destruction states that **"successful innovation is normally a source of temporary market power, eroding the profits and position of old firms, yet ultimately succumbing to the pressure of new inventions commercialised by competing entrants."** That is the cycle, and it is temporary by construction. The ITIF and MPRA entries found in search (not opened, so not cited in the ledger) use the same "temporary monopoly" phrasing; they are recorded as search-only in `04` and carry no weight.

**Refuter:** a case in which a permanent, non-revisable rent continues to make next searches cheaper without any rival pressure or entry threat, sustained over multiple innovation cycles. Or a figure re-census that permits a recording to cross the membrane as a method.

**Status: `triangulated`**, Fig 4 with Fig 10 (two different figures, the membrane direction and the convention verdict) plus the world description, low overlap between the membrane and the antitrust frame.

### F5 — Profits and stability are a spendable difference, not a primitive (triangulated)

Fig 13 :22: **"a difference that can be spent"**. Fig 13 :45-47:

> **"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."**

The phrase "profits and stability needed for research" describes a gradient. Profit above the competitive norm is a difference; directedness is what makes it spendable on R&D rather than merely a static distribution. Stability is the absence of a rapidly decaying channel (Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**), so the firm can keep a reading of its ground long enough to pay the action cost. Neither is a sixth/seventh primitive: both are compositions of held states and structures (Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**). Naming them as new fundamentals would breach the set's minimality.

Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** Transferred to this domain (see the strain note in section 8), a rent cannot act on a research outcome without a structure between: the budget and the research method are that structure. Money alone is not an innovation; money routed through a search method is.

**Refuter:** an instance in which a cash transfer produces a new settled term with no search method, no changed vocabulary and no changed contact. Or a derivation of "profit" as one of the six primitives.

**Status: `triangulated`**, Fig 13 :22/:45-47/:74-75 with Fig 12 :125-127 (two currencies, bits and actions, which do not add). The word "stability" is re-grounded as channel maintenance, not as a market state; this is a binding, not a figure name.

### F6 — Self-reported inputs are not anchors (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30 (quoted in the census) and Fig 1 :24-27:

> **"coverage, and compression achieved"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

A firm's R&D expenditure, its patent count and its own concentration measure are frame-internal if they are used by the same firm to justify the rent that funds them. Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** What is owed is an external reading: a patent grant at a patent office, a realized purchase, a measured total-factor residual, an independent replication. Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

This is the live methodological defect in much of the competition-innovation literature: competition is often proxied by concentration, and concentration and R&D are both reported by the same firms whose behaviour is in question. The world sources used here (Aghion 2005 patenting at the US patent office; Aghion et al. 2014 laboratory choices with ex post rents set by the experimenter) are better anchored than a self-report, and that is why they are weighted.

**Refuter:** an instrument that moves only when the firm's own claim moves, shown to discriminate the residual. Or a figure line permitting a frame to be its own anchor.

**Status: `triangulated`**, Fig 1 with Fig 2, plus the two world instruments.

### F7 — Rivals are one source of imports, but not the only one (single-source, with a live limit)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :32: **"no search, however long, adds an atom."** A firm with no rivals can still import from science, suppliers, users and regulators, all of which are frames whose closures differ. So the figures do **not** imply that a monopolist cannot innovate. What they do imply is that permanent isolation from every differing closure removes the surplus that a union of frames provides (Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**), and that a sheltered firm must actively maintain those other contacts or its reach decays.

Fig 8 :47-49: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** and **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** Rivals who imitate the same template are a mirror chain (Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**), not a source of new atoms. So "more competitors" is not a monotone proxy for "more differing closures."

**Refuter:** a single closed firm, with no contact with any differing frame over multiple cycles, that mints a new primitive settled by the ground. This would break Fig 6 :23 and is the strongest available ground-level test in this folder.

**Status: `single-source`** on the figure side (Fig 6 / Fig 8 are closely related figures, one evidence pool), with the caveat that the transfer from "frames" to "firms" is a binding and must be recorded as such. The world corroboration is the Red Queen result in section 4: competition among competitors tends to character displacement, which weakens the interaction, whereas antagonistic (differing) interactions sustain change.

### F8 — Growth in the innovation budget is not growth in the reach (single-source)

Fig 5 :48: **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."** Fig 5 :49: **"Machinery firing is not the milestone. A running system can mint and still return nothing new, which is exactly what the third guard exists to record."** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: **"A library that grows is not a reach that grows."**

A larger R&D budget is support for a mint (Fig 5 :20-21: **"is there anything to explain? the gap has real mass somewhere"**), not a guarantee of one. The three guards are a product (Fig 5 :33: **"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."**): without a live slot residual, reachability and novelty, extra profit buys more motion in an already-swept region. This is the figure-side reason why "market power provides the stability needed for research" can be true without entailing "market power produces more innovation."

**Refuter:** a case in which a larger budget, with no change in residual, reachability or novelty guards, reliably produces new settled terms at the ground.

**Status: `single-source`**, Fig 5 and Formula are one source; kept separate from F5 so the funding claim and the mint claim are not averaged.

### F9 — No figure line returns the optimal intensity (declared `gap`)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

After a genuine re-census there is no figure line that returns the competition intensity at which the sign flips, the right patent term, the correct concentration threshold, or a universal policy. The figures constrain the shape (per-slot, union, valence-relative, head start not recording, threshold) and stop. The number is owed to an instrument: a natural experiment or a randomized policy variation with an independent innovation outcome. This is `unreachable-at-this-budget` and also `shape-not-a-name`, not a failure of the search.

**Refuter:** a figure line that names a numeric intensity or a specific institutional design. None was found after re-census; the gap stands.

**Status: `gap`**, triangulated across Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134 and Fig 8 :39 (three separate limit statements, low overlap).

## 4. World evidence read this session (secondary only)

Figures decide; these sources corroborate and were opened in full. Fields were chosen for low overlap in accordance with the WATSON instruction.

- **Economics, observational.** Aghion, Bloom, Blundell, Griffith and Howitt, *Competition and Innovation: An Inverted U Relationship*, NBER WP 9269 (2002), QJE 120(2), 2005. Verbatim: **"This paper investigates the relationship between product market competition (PMC) and innovation. A growth model is developed in which competition may increase the incremental profit from innovating; on the other hand, competition may also reduce innovation incentives for laggards."** Prediction: a threshold, with opposite signs by position. Corroborates F2 and F3.
- **Economics, experimental.** Aghion, Bechtold, Cassar and Herz, *The Causal Effects of Competition on Innovation: Experimental Evidence*, NBER WP 19987 (2014), JLEO 34(2), 2018. Verbatim: **"First, we find that increased competition leads to a significant increase in R&D investments by neck-and-neck firms. Second, increased competition decreases R&D investments by firms that are lagging behind, in particular if the time horizon is short."** Prediction: valence flips with distance to frontier and with horizon. Corroborates F1 directly; it is the cleanest ground-level support available and is not treated as a second witness for the economics pool as a whole.
- **Economics, theory.** Kenneth Arrow, *Economic Welfare and the Allocation of Resources for Invention*, NBER chapter (1962). Verbatim (via the NBER chapter record and the Springer/DuckDuckGo indexed excerpt): **"an incentive to invent can exist even under perfect competition in the product markets though not, of course, in the 'market' for the information contained in the invention."** Prediction: competition is not incompatible with inventive incentive; the market for knowledge is the problem. Corroborates the refusal of Option A as a universal and of Option B as a universal.
- **Economics / intellectual property.** Schumpeter, *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy* (1942), as quoted in the Wikipedia "Creative destruction" entry opened this session: **"This process of Creative Destruction is the essential fact about capitalism. It is what capitalism consists in and what every capitalist concern has got to live in."** The same entry: **"successful innovation is normally a source of temporary market power, eroding the profits and position of old firms, yet ultimately succumbing to the pressure of new inventions commercialised by competing entrants."** Prediction: rent is a phase of a cycle, not a permanent state. Corroborates F4.
- **Evolutionary biology (genuinely low overlap).** Brockhurst, Chapman, King, Mank, Paterson and Hurst, *Running with the Red Queen: the role of biotic conflicts in evolution*, Proceedings of the Royal Society B 281(1797), 2014, PMC4240979. Verbatim: **"Competition is generally unlikely to drive perpetual evolutionary change of this kind. Coevolution of competitors tends towards character displacement, and thus weakens the intensity of the biotic interaction and the strength of selection over time."** And: **"By contrast, RQ dynamics are more readily observed in models of antagonistic coevolution whereby the strength of selection acting on each species is roughly symmetrical."** Prediction: pure competition among similar rivals tends to niche differentiation and a weakening arms race; conflict with a differing antagonist sustains change. Corroborates F7 and F1 at the level of mechanism shape, and **conflicts** with the naive "competition always accelerates innovation" reading. The conflict rule was triggered and sent back to the census: it did not flip the figure verdict, it narrowed the claim to differing closures (F7), which the figures already required.
- **Evolution education (low overlap, definitional).** UC Museum of Paleontology, Understanding Evolution, "Red Queen hypothesis" glossary: **"The idea that, in order for a species to maintain a particular niche in an ecosystem and its fitness relative to other species, that species must be constantly undergoing adaptive evolution because the organisms with which it is coevolving are themselves undergoing adaptive evolution."** Prediction: relative position, not absolute output, is what is maintained. Corroborates the per-slot, relative-verbatim framing of F1.

Overlap declaration: the four economics sources share the competition-innovation evidence pool and count as one witness with several instruments; the two evolution sources are a second, genuinely low-overlap pool. No world source overrules a figure line. The conflict from Brockhurst et al. was resolved by re-census, not by flipping the verdict.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by seat reasoning: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line assigns a fixed valence to competition or to market power independent of the residual (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).
2. A measurement shows the same firm-slot's innovation rate is monotone and single-signed in competitive pressure across all distance-to-frontier classes (breaks F1/F3 at the ground).
3. The two conditions are shown to be a partition with an empty intersection (breaks Fig 9 :54-57).
4. A permanent, non-revisable rent continues to make successive searches cheaper across multiple innovation cycles with no entry threat and no differing frame in contact (breaks Fig 4 :22-26 and Fig 6 :23).
5. A larger R&D budget reliably produces new settled terms while the support, reachability and novelty guards all remain at zero (breaks Fig 5 :33 and Formula step 3).
6. A figure re-census admits a numeric competition intensity or a universal institutional design (closes the F9 gap).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the whole packet is re-derived. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union and the per-slot rule, not with an answer to the either/or (F1, F2).
2. State the threshold reading, but do not name the turning point (F3, F9).
3. Treat "temporary" as the whole policy variable: head start that makes the next search cheaper versus recording that blocks it (F4).
4. Demand an anchor outside the firm's own reporting before any concentration or R&D figure enters a verdict (F6).
5. Keep rival pressure and other imports separate; do not reduce "competition" to "number of competitors" (F7).
6. Send the generator, the shape claim and this refuter, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
