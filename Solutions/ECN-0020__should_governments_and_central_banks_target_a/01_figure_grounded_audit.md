## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A monetary authority must name a quantity and a threshold. The three options are not three numbers on one scale; they are three different couplings of an observable to the ground:

- **Low positive inflation (say 2 percent).** Leaves a positive gradient in the price level and headroom for nominal rates to fall before the zero bound. Its failure mode is inflation bias and the erosion of purchasing power.
- **Zero inflation.** Sets that gradient to zero. Its failure mode is that the stabilizing move (cut nominal rates) runs out of room, and small deflationary errors are not made up.
- **A different goal.** A price-level path, a nominal-income path, or an average-inflation rule. These change the slot, that is, which quantity is compared and whether past errors are corrected rather than treated as bygones.

What is owed is not a ranking of these by popularity but the shape any of them must have for the ground to settle it.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry, and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency, and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: three coupled conditions (price stability, real activity, debt and expectation dynamics), so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Symmetry: a target is a threshold comparison of an observed quantity against an intended value, not a symmetric join; the Operators table :48 defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Scale: first order at the policy instrument and price index, second order at the regime and expectation geometry.
- **Domain 1, the measurement frame.** Actors: statistical agencies, the CPI or HICP basket, index weights. Currency: measured price change. Anchor: the collected prices, a counter that does not change because a frame thinks about it. This anchor is legitimate only for the question "what did the basket do", not for "is the basket the right basket" (Fig 2 :36-38).
- **Domain 2, the policy frame.** Actors: the central bank, the government, the committee, the reaction function. Currency: the target comparison and the instrument setting. Anchor: realized outcomes (employment, output, default rates), which do not move because the bank announces a number. Without such a nameable anchor this domain is a frame, not a ground.
- **Domain 3, the expectation and contract habitat.** Actors: households, firms, lenders, borrowers, wage bargainers. Currency: expectations, indexation, contract duration. Anchor: observed behavior (whether spending is postponed, whether wages are indexed), the ground for the question "did the target change contact".
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: outside researchers and public submission venues. Currency: residuals and checkable records. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so the agreement between them is the pool talking, not corroboration.

**Partition or union.** Union of Domains 2 and 3 around R; Domain 1 is a partition boundary (it fixes what the number is a number of). A single inflation lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (can we measure a price change) is already working in Domain 1. Vocabulary (do we have terms for what a target affords: make-up, bygones, headroom) is partial. The objective link (what counts as progress, i.e. the target itself) is the disputed and under-instrumented link. Planning follows the objective. Learn and carry comes last. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Strain test applied before findings: an early reading tried to make Fig 13 :75 directly forbid zero inflation. That strains the line, which governs composition of the six primitives and not a policy threshold. Re-census sends that claim to a different domain and it returns as a `specified` structural prediction in F6, triangulated with world evidence. No figure line is pressed into service.

## 3. Findings

### F1: A target is a comparison over a subtraction, not a thing (structural, `general`)

Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

So "what should the target be" decomposes into two checkable parts: which quantity is compared, and what threshold and direction of travel define progress. The three options differ mainly in the first part. Low positive and zero inflation share the quantity (a rate); the third option changes the quantity (a level or a nominal path).

**Refuter:** show a target that states a goal without any comparison, or a progress report without a subtraction. If such a target works, the bond analysis is wrong for this domain.

### F2: A single headline rate hides the live residual (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step."*

A consumer-price index is an average. Debtors, creditors, renters, and fixed-income households sit in different slots. A 2 percent headline can coexist with a live residual in a specific slot (a debt-overhang slot, a food and energy slot). The figures require the residual to be read per slot before a lever is applied.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that the distributional residuals move one-for-one with the headline index, so no slot carries positive mass when the headline is on target.

### F3: The real anchor is outcomes; the nominal target is a convention (triangulated, `general` + `specified`)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

A 2 percent or 0 percent number is exactly something that changes when frames (markets, committees, expectations) think about it: it is a convention. It can coordinate expectations, which is useful, but it is not the anchor. The anchor for "should we do this" is what happens in the world (employment, defaults, output), which does not move because a number is announced. Fig 2 :36-38 adds the legitimacy test: human judgment is the right anchor for a question constitutively about people, an instrument for a question like "is the code correct"; instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* A price index is such an instrument.

This is not an argument against having a target. It is a warning against treating the target as the thing settled. Fig 10 :19: *"install what can be violated"*: a real convention is one that can be missed and corrected. A target defined so it cannot fail is a constant the seat authored.

**Refuter:** exhibit a nominal target whose answer does not change when agents revise expectations, or a measure that is itself the outcome rather than an instrument. Until then the target stays a convention and the verdict stays with outcomes.

### F4: The 2 percent consensus is a convention, not corroboration (triangulated, `general`)

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The fact that many central banks adopted 2 percent (a point recorded in the world sources) makes it an efficient convention. It does not make it right. The same evidence pool (central banker forums, the same meta-analyses) produces the agreement, so the agreement is the pool talking. Because the cost of a wrong regime is high, Fig 2 :42 requires buying independence rather than quoting consensus.

**Refuter:** show two or more low-overlap frames (for example a debt-contract frame and a labor-market frame) arriving at the same number from separate evidence. That would be convergent derivation and would carry weight; the current consensus does not.

### F5: Valence is residual-relative, so there is no universally correct target (triangulated, `general`)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another"* and Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Positive inflation is a benefactor to a debt-deflation residual (it reduces the real debt burden) and a malfactor to an inflation-bias residual (it erodes real balances and is regressive in ways the slot census would show). Zero inflation is a benefactor to a credibility residual and a malfactor to a liquidity-trap residual. Therefore "which target" cannot be answered in the abstract. It is answered relative to the live residual, which is why this document reports a conditional shape rather than a single recommendation.

**Refuter:** exhibit a target whose valence is positive across all residuals in the census, or show that the residual set is empty. Absent that, any unconditional "2 is best" or "zero is best" is a verdict without a domain.

### F6: Zero inflation removes the directedness the stabilizer needs (single-source figure + world, `specified`)

Fig 13 :46: *"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

An inflation rate is a gradient over time (a difference that can be spent by moving nominal rates). A target of exactly zero spends that gradient down to nothing at the boundary; the sign of the correction loop is then one-sided, and the stabilizing move has no room. A low positive target is a structural way of preserving headroom, i.e. preserving directedness in both directions. This is a `specified` prediction from the figure's definition of a gradient, not a figure prohibition on zero. World evidence on the zero lower bound and liquidity traps corroborates it (see `09_watson_answer.md`, WORLD section); the two have low overlap (a structural definition versus macro-financial episodes), so the agreement is weak corroboration rather than proof.

**Refuter:** exhibit a stable regime at zero inflation in which the sign of the stabilizer never flips and no residual requires a downward nominal move. Such a case would break the prediction.

### F7: Rate targeting and level targeting are isomers, not variants (single-source figure, `specified`)

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Inflation-rate targeting and price-level targeting use the same measured atoms (a price index and time) but different bonds: a rate is a first difference, a level is a cumulated path. Under a symmetric bond they would be one term; they are not, because the bond (difference versus cumulation, bygones versus make-up) changes the substance. A level rule can name a route back after a miss; a rate rule names only the current state and lets bygones be bygones. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Refuter:** show that a rate rule and a level rule are observationally identical after a miss, i.e. that make-up is not a behavioral difference. Then they are one term and the isomer claim fails.

### F8: The target is the link where chains break (triangulated, `general`)

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

Price measurement (perception) has obvious tests and is well instrumented. Planning (the reaction function) has obvious tests. The middle link, having terms for what the situation affords and saying what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention. The target question lives exactly there. Effort spent refining the index (above the break) arrives at a step that cannot use it if the objective link is unset.

**Refuter:** produce an instrument that decides the target choice directly (not a forecast, not a loss function supplied by the same frame) and show the chain no longer breaks at the objective.

### F9: Changing the number without changing contact changes nothing (triangulated, `specified`)

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A central bank is not made more capable by rewriting its target statement if the habitat (contract indexation, wage-setting, expectation formation, the zero-bound institutional setting) is unchanged. The target works only in the habitat that supports it. This generalizes F3: declaring a meaning does not ground it, and an agent-only change does not move the ground.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where changing only the announced number altered realized outcomes with no change in contracts, expectations, or instrument constraints.

### F10: No terminal target (triangulated, `general`)

Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room"* and Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Every monetary regime is a handoff (New Zealand's 0 to 2 percent band, the ECB's "below but close to 2", the Fed's 2020 average-inflation rule, the ECB's 2021 symmetric 2 percent are all revisions in the record). A regime that declares itself final would be a last room, which the figures rule out. The verdict is always `candidate`, and the stop is recorded so a later reader can find the error.

**Refuter:** show a regime that needs no revision under any future residual. That would be a last room.

### F11: The direction limit: the loop cannot pick the number (structural, `general`)

Fig 5 :62: *"holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term"* and Fig 5 :63: *"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"*. THE_FORMULA "Direction" states: the loop *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* The remedy is a discipline: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

So the honest output of this audit is not "the target should be 2". It is a bound term: a good target has to be an anchor-free convention, slot-specific, comparison-shaped, headroom-preserving, habitat-coupled, and revisable. That shape can now accrue a residual like anything else, which is what the refuters below do.

**Refuter:** derive a specific number from mechanism checks alone, without importing a preference. That would mean the direction was a term after all.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A specific target number is derived from mechanism checks alone, with no preference imported, so the direction of the question turns out to be a term (breaks F11).
2. A nominal target is exhibited whose answer does not move when agents revise expectations, making it a genuine anchor (breaks F3).
3. A target is exhibited with positive valence across all residuals in the census (breaks F5).
4. A stable zero-inflation regime is exhibited in which the stabilizer's sign never flips and no downward nominal move is owed (breaks F6).
5. A rate rule and a level rule are shown observationally identical after a miss, so make-up is not a behavioral difference (breaks F7).

If any fires, the conditional shape is wrong in that branch and the recommendation is withdrawn for that branch. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Report the shape, not a universal number (F11, F5). The answer to "2, zero, or different" is: the number is downstream of the slot and the live residual.
2. Treat the target as a falsifiable convention and keep outcomes as the anchor (F3). Install what can be violated.
3. Read the residual per slot before choosing the lever (F2), and unbundle the union rather than applying one lever to three conditions (Fig 9 :54).
4. If the live residual is debt deflation or a liquidity trap, low positive inflation with headroom is the figure-consistent branch (F6). If it is inflation bias and credibility, a rule-shaped commitment is the figure-consistent branch (F3, F9). If the problem is a union, a single rate target is a single lever and a level or nominal-path goal is the re-census (F7).
5. Lead outreach with the shape and the refuter. Send a generator, not a recording (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).

### Round-trip note

The shorthand "targets should be shaped, not chosen" is a lossy condensation of the figure set. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* What the shorthand drops is the conditional structure (which residual, which slot, which headroom), and that dropped content is exactly what `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` restore. The gap R_T is recorded qualitatively there, not set to zero.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives; no universal number does. A target must be a falsifiable convention anchored by outcomes, read per slot, comparison-shaped, fitted to a named residual, headroom-preserving where the residual is debt deflation or a liquidity trap, habitat-coupled, and revisable. Strains after re-census: **one named open item**, the operand type of the comparison when the "quantity" compared is a whole price path rather than a rate (Operators :48 defines comparison over two quantities); this needs an instrument, not an argument, and is recorded in `06_isomers_and_order.md` rather than smoothed over.
