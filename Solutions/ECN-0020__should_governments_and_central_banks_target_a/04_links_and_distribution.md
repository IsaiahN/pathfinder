## Two findings from the wider literature that bear directly on the answer

1. **The zero bound is a structural boundary, not a taste.** The ECB's own strategy page says that close to the lower bound on nominal rates, *"especially forceful or persistent monetary policy action"* is needed to stop negative deviations becoming entrenched. The liquidity-trap record adds that a zero lower bound is a necessary condition of a trap and that the authority can lose effective control of the rate. This is the world-side corroboration for F6: a zero gradient leaves no room for the ordinary stabilizing move.
2. **The bracket is not the ground.** Fisher's debt-deflation chain shows a fall in the price level raising the real debt burden, and money illusion shows that agents answer to nominal frames. So the measured index (an instrument) cannot be treated as the thing settled. This corroborates F3 and F2: read the residual per slot and keep the anchor in outcomes, not in the announced number.
