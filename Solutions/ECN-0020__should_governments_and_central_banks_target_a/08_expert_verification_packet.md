# Expert verification packet: the Tether on monetary policy targets

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary economist or central-bank researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditional branches. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a policy recommendation.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A numerical inflation target is a convention, not an anchor; the anchor for a normative target question is realized outcomes. A target is a threshold comparison over a measured subtraction, so which quantity is compared (the slot) precedes how much is targeted. Valence is relative to the live residual, so no number is universally correct. Where the residual is debt deflation or a liquidity trap, a low positive target preserves the spendable gradient and the two-signed correction loop that a zero target runs out of. Where the residual is inflation bias and credibility, a commitment-shaped rule is the right branch, provided the habitat changes with the number. Rate targeting and price-level targeting are isomers: same atoms, different bond, with make-up as the difference. No regime is final.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading the figures

- **Statement:** a target is a comparison over a subtraction, and a declared nominal target is a convention whose anchor is outcomes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :52, *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Fig 12 :112, *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :47, *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a target that states a goal without a comparison; or exhibit a nominal target whose answer does not move when agents revise expectations, making it a genuine anchor.

### C2: economics-facing, checkable against the policy record

- **Statement:** zero inflation is the boundary term where the spendable gradient and the two-signed correction loop run out; a low positive target preserves headroom. This is `specified`, not a figure prohibition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :46, *"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."* Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **What to check:** is there any realized zero-inflation episode in which the correction loop was two-signed throughout and no downward nominal move was owed? The ECB strategy page states that near the lower bound, *"especially forceful or persistent monetary policy action"* is required to stop negative deviations becoming entrenched; the liquidity-trap record states that a zero lower bound is a necessary condition of a trap. Those are corroboration, not proof.
- **Refuter:** a stable zero-inflation regime with a two-signed stabilizer on every realized path. If it exists, this branch is withdrawn.

### C3: structural, checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** a rate rule and a level (or path) rule are isomers over the shared atoms, and the bond is make-up.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :111, *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- **What to check:** after a miss, does a level rule change any action relative to a rate rule? If make-up changes nothing observable, the two are one term and the isomer claim fails.
- **Refuter:** observational equivalence of the two rules after a miss.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the framework yields 2 percent, 0 percent, or any third number. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the near-universal 2 percent target is evidence for 2 percent. Agreement about a shared meaning is a convention; agreement about the world with a shared evidence pool is disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40-41).
- Not that the CPI or HICP is the ground. It is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :36-38).
- Not that changing a target statement alone changes outcomes. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that distributional residuals average out (Fig 1 :22).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation.

## 4. One live strain, disclosed

The Operators table :48 defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At the second order, the operand can be a settled comparison now serving as an atom (an expectation object), not an obvious quantity. Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom, but whether that atom counts as a quantity at the new level needs an instrument. This is reported as a strain, not a correction. A reader who resolves it changes the second-order part of the claim, not C1 or C2.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :47, and Operators table :52 (three short passages). Does the convention/anchor distinction hold for a nominal target?
2. Check C2 against the zero-bound record you know. Name one zero-inflation episode with a two-signed stabilizer, or say why none exists.
3. Check C3: after a missed target, does a level rule change any action that a rate rule does not?
4. Name the instrument that would discriminate the live residual per slot (debt deflation versus inflation bias) rather than in a headline average.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
