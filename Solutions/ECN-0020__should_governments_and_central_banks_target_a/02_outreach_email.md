# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the target

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the strain in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 is either resolved or stated in the message. A claim that smuggles a number in as a figure result collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what an inflation target has to be

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about monetary policy. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` branches; not `grounded`):** a numerical target is not an anchor. An anchor, in the framework's terms, is *"whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"*; a 2 percent or 0 percent number changes when expectations change, so it is a convention. A target is better described as a threshold comparison over a subtraction, that is, a goal stated as a quantity to be compared and a direction of travel. From that, three consequences follow that I want checked:
>
> 1. Valence is relative to the residual. Positive inflation helps a debt-deflation residual and hurts an inflation-bias residual; no number is good in the abstract. So the honest answer to "2, zero, or different" is conditional on the live residual.
> 2. Zero inflation spends the gradient down at the boundary. The stabilizer's sign is then one-sided and it has no room for the downward nominal move. A low positive target preserves headroom. This is a structural prediction, not a claim that zero is impossible.
> 3. A rate rule and a level rule are isomers: same measured atoms, different bond, and the bond is whether bygones are made up. A level rule can name a route back after a miss; a rate rule names only the current state.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework yields 2 percent, or 0 percent, or any third number. It returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that the near-universal 2 percent consensus is evidence for 2 percent: agreement about a shared meaning is a convention, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. And I am not offering a theory of everything. Any target regime is a revisable handoff.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. Derive a specific target number from mechanism checks alone, with no preference imported.
> 2. Exhibit a nominal target whose answer does not move when agents revise expectations.
> 3. Exhibit a target with positive valence across all residuals, or a stable zero-inflation regime in which the stabilizer's sign never flips.
> 4. Show that a rate rule and a level rule are observationally identical after a miss.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
