# Rate, level, and path: an isomer derivation for monetary targets

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond grammar. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question offers three options, but "low positive inflation" and "zero inflation" share a quantity (a rate) and differ only in a threshold, while "a different goal" usually changes the quantity (a level or a nominal path). Two things are owed: (1) are a rate rule and a level rule different substances under Fig 12, and (2) what is the order of the target comparison, i.e. what composes over what.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (before figure lines)

Atoms held in the policy frame: a price index at time t, a price index at time t-1, an intended value, a time ordering, and a set of past observations. Bonds available: the seven, of which `−` (subtraction), `⋛` (comparison), and `→` (sequence) are the relevant ones. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: a measured number. Anchor: realized outcomes, not the number itself. Union condition: the target question couples a measurement rule and a correction rule, which are two domains, so one lever will not clear both.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is a rate, structurally?

- Fig 13 :14: energy is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*; Fig 13 :46: *"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."*
- An inflation rate is a subtraction of two price-index readings over a time ordering: a `state → structure → state` molecule in which the structure is the difference. It is a gradient over time. The world record supports the mapping but is secondary: the measured rate is what a price basket does between two dates.
- A level is the price index itself: an ordering, spectrum-like, a *"state"* reading at a moment. Fig 13 :10: *"near, far, between, contained"*; a price level orders purchasing power across baskets and dates.
- **Result:** rate and level are built from the same atoms (index, time) joined by different bonds (subtraction versus identity/cumulation). They are candidates for isomerism. Not yet a verdict; checked next.

### Q2: Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Under a rate rule, last year's miss is a bygone: the comparison each period is measured inflation ⋛ intended inflation, and the cumulated path drifts. Under a level rule, last year's miss enters the current comparison because the intended path is indexed to the base: the cumulated error is part of the term. Same atoms, different bond, different behavior after a miss. **They are isomers.**
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** The rate rule names a state (the current rate); the level rule names a route (the path back to the base). This is exactly the difference the framework predicts a bond makes.
- Fig 12 :48: **"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."** A rate rule is the degenerate chain (repeat the same one-period comparison); a level rule branches by carrying past error forward. **Triangulated:** Fig 12 :117 plus Fig 12 :111 plus Fig 12 :48, three passages with distinct content in the same figure, plus the world record of average-inflation revisions. Still a `specified` figure claim, because no measurement is claimed here.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a rate rule and a level rule are observationally identical after a miss, i.e. if make-up never changes any action, then the difference is not in the bond and the isomer claim fails; they collapse to one term under a symmetric bond.

### Q3: Where does zero sit in this structure?

- A zero rate target is the boundary term in which the subtraction (measured minus intended) has zero intended value and therefore no directedness to spend in the downward direction. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A chain that can only close in one sign is not a closed loop; the corrective move cannot re-enter as input when it hits the boundary. Fig 13 :46 supplies the mechanism: nothing is left to spend.
- A low positive target keeps the subtraction positive and the loop two-signed. This is a **structural prediction**, not a ban on zero. It is `specified` and awaits the ground (the zero-bound episodes in the world record).
- **Falsifier:** a zero-inflation regime in which the correction loop is two-signed on every realized path and no downward nominal move is ever owed.

### Q4: What is the order of composition?

- Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."*
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Order by dependency:
  1. Read two index observations and subtract (progress). This is settled in the measurement domain.
  2. Compare the result to an intended value (the goal, `⋛`). A comparison over a subtraction.
  3. If the regime is settled (credible, observed across agents), the whole comparison becomes an atom at the next level, i.e. an expectation object. Fig 12 :104 licenses this.
  4. The expectation object then enters the habitat and changes contracts and wage setting (Fig 11). This is a cross-level composition, not a bigger version of step 2.
- Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."** The target comparison at level 1 is an atom at level 2; nothing here requires a new primitive.
- **Type strain, named not hidden:** the Operators table :48 defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At step 4 the operand is a settled comparison now serving as an atom (an expectation object), not an obvious quantity. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom, but whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument. Recorded as a strain; not smoothed.

### Q5: Analogy check (shadow before echo)

- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The residual was described in effect terms (headroom, make-up, valence relative to residual) before the control-theory and chemistry analogues were brought in.
- *Analogue A, control engineering:* a level target is integral control (cumulated error fed back); a rate target is proportional control. This corroborates Q2 from a low-overlap field, but it is a warning too: the analogy is only licensed because the bond difference was derived first. If the bond had been imported first it would be echo without shadow.
- *Analogue B, chemistry:* Fig 12 :97-98 names isomerism, silver fulminate and silver cyanate, *"same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."* Same measured atoms, different bond, different behavior. Corroborates Q2, but it shares the framework's own citation, so it is not independent evidence.

## CONCLUSION

The two rate options are one term at two thresholds; the "different goal" option is usually a different term because it changes the bond. Rate targeting and level (or path) targeting are **isomers** over the shared atoms {price index, time, intended value, past observations}: same atoms, different bond, decisive behavioral difference after a miss. Zero inflation is the boundary term where the spendable gradient and the two-signed loop run out. The target comparison is ordered as subtraction then comparison, then a settled comparison becomes an atom for the expectation habitat. The one live strain is the operand type of comparison at the second level.
