# The Tether's answer on inflation targets

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditional branches. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A monetary authority must name a quantity and a threshold. Three options (low positive inflation, zero inflation, a different goal) are not three points on one scale: they are three different couplings of an observable to the ground. The residual is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54 says *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single rate lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

## 2. The shape

1. **The target is a comparison over a subtraction, so the slot comes before the number.** Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Which quantity is compared matters more than the threshold value.
2. **The nominal target is a convention, not the anchor.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A 2 percent or 0 percent number changes when expectations change; the anchor is outcomes. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
3. **No universal number.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Positive inflation helps a debt-deflation residual and hurts an inflation-bias residual; zero helps a credibility residual and hurts a liquidity-trap residual. So the honest answer is conditional.
4. **Where the residual is debt deflation or a liquidity trap, low positive inflation with headroom is the figure-consistent branch.** Fig 13 :46: *"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A zero target spends the stabilizing gradient down at the boundary and leaves the correction loop one-signed. This is a `specified` structural prediction, corroborated (not proved) by the zero-bound record.
5. **Where the residual is inflation bias and credibility, a rule-shaped commitment is the figure-consistent branch.** The target must be *"install[ed] what can be violated"* (Fig 10 :19), and changing the number alone will not do it: Fig 11 :40 says *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* The habitat (indexation, expectations, contract length) has to change with the number.
6. **Rate and level are isomers.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* A rate rule and a level rule share the measured atoms but differ in the bond: whether past error is made up. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A level rule names a route; a rate rule names a state.
7. **No terminal target.** Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room."* Every regime is a revisable handoff (the historical sequence of target changes in the world record is the evidence for this, not the figures alone). Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 3. Direct answer to the three options

- **Low positive inflation:** the conditional winner when the live residual is debt deflation, a liquidity trap, or a union that includes them, because it preserves the directedness the stabilizer spends. Not a universal winner, and its own failure mode (inflation bias, erosion of real balances) must be read per slot.
- **Zero inflation:** not forbidden by the figures, but it is the boundary term where the spendable gradient and the two-signed loop run out. It is the figure-consistent choice only for a credibility residual in a habitat that supplies other stabilizers.
- **A different goal:** legitimate as a re-census, not as a fashion. If the problem is a union, a single lever leaves intersections standing; a level or nominal-path target changes the slot and the bond (make-up), which is a genuine structural difference. It still has to satisfy the same seven constraints above.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the framework yields 2 percent, 0 percent, or any third number. The framework returns a shape, not a name (canonical README). A number is a binding recorded as our own contribution.
- Not that the 2 percent consensus validates 2 percent. Fig 2 :40 says *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :41 calls the same agreement *"efficient for a convention"*.
- Not that a target is an anchor or that announcing one grounds it (Fig 10 :18, :49).
- Not that distributional residuals disappear in an average. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Not `grounded`. No experiment, dataset, or natural experiment was run by this derivation. The world sources were opened as corroboration only.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A real anchor, that is, a measurement or outcome that does not move because a frame thinks about it (Fig 2 :29). For this question the ground is constitutive of human outcomes, so the right anchor for "is this a good target" is the outcome itself (Fig 2 :36-38), never the price index, which is an instrument and *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

The discriminating instruments owed are:

1. **A slot-by-slot residual**, not a headline average: where is positive mass after the target is hit (debt service, food and energy, fixed-income households)?
2. **A make-up test:** does a level rule change any action after a miss relative to a rate rule, or are they observationally identical? (This is the falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q2.)
3. **A two-signed-loop test at the boundary:** is there any realized zero-inflation path on which the correction needs a downward nominal move, or is it always one-signed? (Falsifier for F6.)
4. **An anchor test for the convention:** does the target's answer change when expectations are revised? If it does, do not call it an anchor (Fig 2 :30).

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure census yields a shape with a conditional branch structure, not a settled number. The stop is at the limit of the available instruments: no source-discriminating instrument for the live residual was run in this derivation, and the world sources are secondary. The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical README): two target regimes that produce the same headline are not the same regime.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose 2 percent, zero, or a third number, and it does not claim to. It says that a target is a threshold comparison over a measured subtraction, so the choice of what to measure comes before the choice of how much; that a nominal target is a convention, not an anchor, so outcomes must do the settling; that no number is good in the abstract because valence is relative to the live residual; that where the residual is debt deflation or a liquidity trap, a low positive target preserves the headroom a zero target spends away; that a rate rule and a level rule are isomers whose difference is whether bygones are made up; and that every regime is a revisable handoff, never a last room. What it offers is that shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close each branch. The number stays open until the ground settles it.

## 8. Pre-registered refuter (restated)

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 10's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if: (1) a number is derived from mechanism checks alone with no preference imported; (2) a nominal target is exhibited that is a genuine anchor; (3) a target has positive valence across all residuals; (4) a stable two-signed zero-inflation regime exists; or (5) rate and level rules are observationally identical after a miss. Any one fires, the corresponding branch is withdrawn.
