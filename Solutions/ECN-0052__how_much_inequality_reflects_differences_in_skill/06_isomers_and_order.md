# Union, bond, order and two currencies in the inequality question

**Mode: `specified`. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question lists six sources as if they were six ingredients in one recipe. Two questions are owed:

1. Are the "skill-led" and "structure-led" readings **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, so that the difference is in the bond and not in the ingredient list?
2. What **order** do the bonds have, and what does "how much" map to when the bond is identified?

A third quantity rides with them: the two currencies of Fig 12 :125, the cost of stating a share and the cost of settling it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available in the economics frame: a method (acquired skill), an action (effort), a head start (inheritance), a coercive relation (bargaining power), a reception relation (discrimination), a condition (market structure), and an outcome at a slot.

Bonds held: the seven, including `+` conjunction, `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison, and the unary `¬` refusal. No new primitive is available without an import, and none is claimed.

Order by dependency: a head start precedes the agent's action at that slot; the action meets a reception relation; the outcome is a comparison at a threshold; a belief about the source can re-enter as a condition for the next slot (a cycle).

## Q1 - Are the six sources atoms of one kind? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST PASS

- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- First pass treated the six named sources as one list of comparable atoms and tried to price them. **Retracted.** They are of different kinds: a method (Fig 4 :22), an action, a head start (Fig 4 :11-12), two relations (Fig 11 :47), and a condition (Fig 11 :46).
- Fig 11 :45-47 enumerates exactly this distinction: *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*, *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*, *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*.
- ⇒ The fixed-atom condition for an isomer claim is **not met by the question as written**. This is refuter 6 in `01`. Kept visible. Triangulated (Fig 4, Fig 11, Fig 12).

## Q2 - Union versus partition is a difference of bond, not of ingredients

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- A partition reading joins the six with `+` (conjunction, symmetric): swap any two shares and nothing changes. Fig 12 :59: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*
- A union reading joins them with a mix of ordered bonds: inheritance `→` action `⇒` outcome, discrimination as `−` or `⋛` against the same action, market structure as a condition on what the action affords. Swap the operands and the term changes.
- ⇒ The question's symmetric phrasing silently picks one bond from seven and charges nothing for it. Fig 12 :101: *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."*
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a reading in which swapping any two sources leaves the account of a slot unchanged. Then the symmetric bond is correct and this finding fails.

## Q3 - Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- **Weak reading, safe as written.** "Skill-led" and "structure-led" accounts both contain a `state → structure → state` core, so they share a functional group: the group says what kind of explanation, the qualifier says which member. They are not isomers, because they do not share the full atom set (Q1).
- **Strong reading, checkable.** If both accounts are rewritten over the same fixed set `{method, action, head start, coercion relation, reception relation, market condition}` and differ only in which bond connects which pair, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it converts a rhetorical opposition into a structural difference with a falsifier.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either account requires an atom the other does not, or if both produce the same outcome distribution at every slot when the bonds are exchanged, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.
- Status: **triangulated for the definition** (Fig 12 :117/:123 and the chemistry instance at :97-98), **single-source and unmeasured** for its application to these two readings.

## Q4 - Order: a plan, a goal and progress

Fig 12 :111-113:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

- **Sequence (`→`).** A head start arrives before the agent's search; an action follows the head start; an outcome follows both. Fig 12 :70: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Inheritance is not contemporaneous with the effort it makes cheaper. This is the structural reason the two cannot be pooled into one additive share.
- **Production (`⇒`).** An action can produce an outcome that did not exist before the action. Fig 12 :76: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Skill as method is the re-applicable part; effort is the firing.
- **Comparison (`⋛`).** "How much" is a threshold relation between two quantities, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87). It is the goal, not the recipe. Operators :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. A "how much" question can state a goal (reduce this gap) and cannot by itself state the route to it.
- **Subtraction (`−`).** Progress is the difference between the outcome and the counterfactual outcome without the lever. That difference is the quantity an instrument would have to isolate, which is exactly what is missing in D4.

The concatenation error is treating `⋛` as if it were `+`: asking for six shares is asking a comparison operator to do a conjunction's job.

## Q5 - A settled term becomes an atom, which is why the debate recurs

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**.

A settled finding about one source (a well-identified effect of a schooling reform at one slot and one time) becomes an atom for the next debate. It is then composable at the next level. This is why the inequality literature can accumulate settled local results without accumulating a universal share: the reach grows at the local level, and the question at the distribution level asks for a composition the atoms do not license.

Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** More local decompositions re-arrange the same kinds; they do not add a new kind that would settle the universal share.

## Q6 - Two analogues the question needs: catalyst and cycle

- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* A rule or an institution that survives its own use (a bargaining institution, a credential) behaves differently from a one-off transfer. A decomposition that lists both as "inheritance" or both as "structure" has lost the distinction the bond was carrying.
- **Cycle.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Beliefs about what drives income feed back into effort and investment, as the Great Gatsby Curve's "American Dream" versus "Euro-pessimism" equilibria record. That is a cycle, not a chain. A linear decomposition of a cyclic system is a notation choice, and the sign of the loop is the thing that decides whether a lever helps. Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

## Q7 - The two currencies do not add

Fig 12 :125-127, quoted in `01` F9: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

- A six-share table is cheap in bits: six numbers fit in a paragraph.
- It is expensive in actions: each share needs a discriminator that holds the other five fixed at a slot.
- A per-slot union verdict is more expensive in bits and may be cheap in actions if the local instrument already exists.
- Neither cost predicts the other. Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

This is the structural reason a R-squared and a policy verdict are not the same object even when they use the same number.

## FALSIFIER FOR THE WHOLE DERIVATION

Written before the conclusion, repeated from `01` §3:

1. Either comparison account can be written without an atom the other requires, or both give identical outcomes when their bonds are exchanged (kills the isomer claim, Q3).
2. Swapping two named sources leaves an account unchanged (kills the bond finding, Q2).
3. A share is cheap to state and cheap to settle at the slot with an anchor that does not update (kills the two-currency claim, Q7).
4. A scheme is shown to be spent after one use and still recorded as a catalyst, with no way to tell (kills Q6).

If any fires, the structural reading is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## CONCLUSION

The six sources are not six comparable ingredients. They are a mixed set of methods, actions, head starts, relations and conditions, joined by typed and ordered bonds. The partition reading is the flat formula that records ingredients and loses arrangement. The union reading carries the arrangement. Over a fixed atom set the two comparison accounts are a real isomer claim; as written they are only a functional group. "How much" maps to `⋛`, a goal comparison, and the route to it requires `→`, `⇒` and `−`, each with its own instrument. A share is cheap to describe and expensive to settle, and the two prices cannot be added. The live strain is the fixed-atom condition (Q1), named rather than hidden.
