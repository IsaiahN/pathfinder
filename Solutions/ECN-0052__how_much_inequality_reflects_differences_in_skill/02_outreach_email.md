**Subject:** Would you count this as a failure? A note on inequality shares and overlapping causes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the separator, `gap` on the number, not `grounded`):** the question "how much inequality is skill and effort, how much is inheritance, bargaining power, discrimination or market structure" assumes the named sources form a partition of one outcome with an assignable shared fraction. The figures say otherwise. Two rules decide it:
>
> 1. The residual of an outcome is indexed by person or household, never aggregated into one national number first. "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" (Fig 1 :22).
> 2. When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before searching. A person can be low-skilled and inherit little and face a discrimination barrier and live in a thin labour market at the same time. "A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57). The intersection is a specific compound with its own loop, and the shared fraction cannot be read off the outcome.
>
> So the answer I am offering is a separator, not a percentage. It says: treat these as overlapping conditions with different anchors, keep the verdicts per slot and per domain separate, and never average them into a universal split. A variance share or a Shapley allocation is a filter for deciding what to investigate next, not a witness for which cause produced the outcome. "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict" (Fig 9 :60).
>
> **The `gap`, stated plainly:** no figure line returns a universal allocation ratio. Reporting that absence is a verdict, not a silence. I am not asking you to fill it, and I have not filled it myself.
>
> **What is still open:** whether the "skill-led" and "structure-led" readings can be written over the same fixed set of conditions with only a difference of arrangement. If they can, they are isomers in the strict sense; if they cannot, the stronger claim fails and only the weaker functional-group reading stands. I have written the test down rather than assumed the result.
>
> **What I am not claiming:**
> - Not that skill and effort are unimportant. A generator that cannot change contact changes nothing, and that is a testable prediction, not a dismissal.
> - Not that discrimination or market structure are unimportant. They are habitat relations whose valence depends on which residual you are tracking.
> - Not that an existing decomposition is wrong. A decomposition can be a fair and useful convention. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
> - Not that public agreement among economists would settle any of this. Agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. A figure line that returns a numerical share for one named source at one slot, with the shared fraction uniquely assigned.
> 2. An instrument that maps an outcome difference to one source while holding the other five fixed at that slot, with an anchor that does not update on the analyst's preference.
> 3. Evidence that the six sources are a partition with intersection terms of zero mass in the population of interest.
> 4. Evidence that the per-slot residual can be collapsed into one global number without losing the signal that says where to look.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the separator. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
