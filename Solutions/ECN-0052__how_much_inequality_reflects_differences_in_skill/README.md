# Solution ECN-0052

**Question:** How much inequality reflects differences in skill and effort, and how much reflects inheritance, bargaining power, discrimination, or market structure?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 71

**Section:** 6. Inequality, poverty, and distribution

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal percentage. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive file is labelled a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question restated as an effect

The question asks for a partition of one observed distribution among six named sources. Option A: return a set of shares, for example "X percent skill and effort, Y percent inheritance, Z percent bargaining power, W percent discrimination, V percent market structure", and let a reader act on those numbers. Option B: refuse the partition, keep the residual indexed per person or household, treat the sources as a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, and return lever-specific tests plus a declared `gap` where no instrument discriminates sources. A leads to a decision rule that silently assigns an unmeasurable shared fraction to one name. B leads to a checkable shape whose verdicts stay separate and are never averaged. This solution derives B.

**What an answer would look like (Formula step Direction, bound as an ordinary term):** a shape with (1) per-slot indexing of the residual, (2) a union rather than a partition, (3) a separate verdict and a separate anchor for each condition, (4) an instrument condition for source discrimination, (5) an honest `gap` where no figure returns a numeric share. That description is written down here before the run, so it can accrue a residual and be refused like any other term.

## What is deliberately not here

- No universal percentage. No figure line returns an allocation ratio, and assigning one would be a naming where the framework returns a shape.
- No personal emails of named individuals, no ranking of people by how likely they are to agree, and no bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this simulated derivation has measured anything. Fig 2 :29 defines an anchor as *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Tax records and markets can be anchors for the outcome. Nothing in this folder anchors the attribution.

Three figure-grounded reasons for the outreach constraints:

1. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A list selected for people likely to agree with a preferred split manufactures the very correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
2. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* More readers of the same literature raise the vocabulary, not the evidence.
3. **Do not spend the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. The public submission and opt-in channels in `03` avoid that failure as well as the structural one.

Take `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `02_outreach_email.md` to the public venues in `03`. Ask what would refute the shape. Record the verdict on stage 1 before offering any of the framework. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
