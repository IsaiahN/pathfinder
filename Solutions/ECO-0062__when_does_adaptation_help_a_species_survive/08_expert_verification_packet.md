# Expert verification packet: the Tether on adaptation and harm to other species

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or evolutionary biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Adaptation helps a species survive" and "adaptation harms other species or ecosystem functions" are not two sides of one balance sheet. The first is a drop in the focal species' residual against its own limiting condition; the second is a rise in a different slot's residual against a different ground. The harm appears when four conditions co-occur: the changed trait alters contact so its valence flips against a second residual; the effect reaches two or more steps out; the resulting actor is an addition whose contact cannot be subtracted, only substituted; and the focal gain and the other-slot loss are read on separate non-updating grounds. The question is a union compound, so verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. No universal species list, trait list or threshold is asserted; that is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Ecological, checkable by an ecologist against any focal system

- **Statement:** the sign of an adaptation is relative to the residual it is ranged against. The same trait can lower the focal species' residual and raise another species' residual, and this is a relation rather than a property of the trait.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Mechanism clause: Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** take a focal adaptation you know well. Does it change contact with another actor? If not, the claim predicts no other-slot effect. If yes, can the same trait be described as beneficial to the focal residual and harmful to a second residual without contradiction?
- **Refuter:** a focal adaptation that lowers the focal residual and leaves every enumerated other residual unchanged across an enumeration reaching at least two steps out; or an adaptation with no contact change that nevertheless changes another slot. Either breaks C1.

### C2 - Ecological, checkable against cascade measurements

- **Statement:** the harm to a function is commonly second or third order, so a first-order habitat scope misses the residual that matters. The cane toad case is the figure's own example, and the measured cascade is the one in the literature.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"*; Fig 11 :54 *"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."* Enumeration rule: Fig 11 :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
- **World witness (secondary, not decisive):** Jolly, Shine and Greenlees 2015, *Ecology and Evolution*, measured a 52% decrease in carrion removal and a 61% increase in brush turkeys in toad-invaded sites, through the decline of lace monitors. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4588653/
- **What to check:** is the indirect effect real and measured, or inferred from the same model that predicted the focal benefit? Is the ground reading (the function rate) independent of the researchers' expectation of harm? Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **Refuter:** a case where all harm is fully predicted by first-order direct contact with a measured zero second-order term across repeated systems; or a case where the function reading demonstrably moves with the researchers' expectation, so the reported decline is an artefact. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - Structural, checkable by reading Figures 9, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** the question is a union of conditions, the changed contact is an addition that cannot be subtracted, and the two effects are in separate currencies, so one lever and one net score are both invalid. The refusal of a standing harmful-species list is a negation, not a subtraction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 11 :61 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :62 *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** can the changed contact actually be subtracted from the habitat without substituting another one? Can focal fitness and a function rate legitimately be converted into one additive quantity with a shared unit? If either answer is yes, the corresponding clause is wrong.
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated subtraction with the harm ending and the system solved; or a legitimate shared-unit conversion of the two currencies; or a single intervention that clears all conditions on two independent grounds. Any of these breaks C3 (the last weakens the union clause only).

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that adaptation is generally harmful, or that it has a fixed sign. Fig 11 :49.
- Not that any named species is a standing "bad actor." Fig 11 :51.
- Not that a meta-analytic effect size decides a local case. Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not that a managed ecosystem is a solved state. Fig 11 :63 *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
- Not that a mesocosm proves field capability. Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- Not that EICA's specific defense-reallocation prediction is established. Colautti et al. 2014 found genetically based trait change after introduction but *"little general support for the specific predictions of the EICA hypothesis"*; this is a mechanism-level strain, recorded, not hidden.
- Not `grounded`. The field instrument for function-level effects is largely missing (Pelletier et al. 2009). "Not measured" is not "absent" (Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
- Not a universal trait list, species list or threshold. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

## 6. What would settle it

A source-discriminating field instrument plus a habitat enumeration, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): a color instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here:

1. Does a source-discriminating measurement of the function rate return a nonzero effect of the focal trait shift across repeated independent systems?
2. Is the effect second or third order, and which intermediate species carries it?
3. Can the changed contact be subtracted without substituting a habitat, verified in the field?
4. Standing falsifiers in `01` section 4: no valence flip; no cascade; symmetry of removal; clean substitution; additive currencies; one lever; instrumented zero.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51, :52, :61-63 and Fig 12 :125 (six short passages).
2. Take one focal adaptation you know. Name the second residual against which its valence flips, or say that none exists.
3. Name the intermediate species that would carry a second-order function effect, or say the effect is demonstrably first order.
4. Name one field instrument that could measure the function rate independently of the model that predicted the focal benefit, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
