# The Tether's answer: when does adaptation help one species and harm others or ecosystem functions?

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A focal species' heritable traits shift so that its own residual drops against a limiting condition: it survives and reproduces better. In the same move, another species' residual rises or an ecosystem function degrades. The two effects are in different slots and different currencies, so they cannot be averaged. The question asks for the **separator**, not for a verdict on adaptation.

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** The residual is a union compound of four overlapping conditions, not one dial.

## 2. The shape: four conditions, kept separate

### C1 - Valence flip against a second residual

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

The adaptation helps the focal species and harms another when the changed trait alters contact and is ranged against a second residual whose sign is negative. A trait that stays entirely within the focal organism changes nothing outside it: Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** This is the primary condition and it is **triangulated** (Fig 11 :49-51 plus Fig 11 :40, low internal overlap).

### C2 - Cascade beyond first order

Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"**
Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

The harm to a function is commonly two or three steps out. A first-order habitat scope misses it. The cane toad case has the same structure: a dispersal-enhancing trait delivers a toxic prey to naive predators, the predators decline, and scavenging falls. Jolly, Shine and Greenlees (2015) measured a 52% decrease in carrion removal and a 61% increase in brush turkeys in toad-invaded sites. World source corroborates; the figure decides. **Triangulated** for the structure (Fig 11 :52-54 plus Fig 11 :43-48 enumeration rule).

### C3 - Addition that cannot be subtracted

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :62: **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

A successful adaptation is an addition to the habitat. Once present, its contact cannot be subtracted by decree. Containment and relocation substitute a habitat rather than remove one (Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**), with two silent failures: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* (Fig 11 :68) and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"* (Fig 11 :69). **Single-source** for this figure's own asymmetry rule; it is a rule the framework imposes on itself, not a theorem corroborated by a second figure.

### C4 - Separate grounds, separate currencies

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Focal survival and the function rate are read against two different anchors, and neither reading may move when the researchers agree it is harmed. Under a fixed atom set, the two readings are isomers (Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**), not different amounts of one thing. **Triangulated** (Fig 1, Fig 12, Fig 2, distinct quantities).

### The union, not the sum

Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Where only C1 holds, there is a valence flip without a detectable function loss. Where only C2 holds, there is a cascade from some other cause. The full case, where a focal survival gain and a function loss co-occur, is a compound with its own loop and its own two grounds. One lever clears only the single-condition region.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that adaptation is generally harmful. Fig 11 :49 gives valence no standing sign. Aubree et al. (2020) found community adaptation can invert a biodiversity-productivity slope in either direction; that is the expected behaviour of a relative valence, not a conflict.
- Not that any named species is a standing "bad actor." Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
- Not that a meta-analytic effect size decides a local case. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
- Not that a managed ecosystem can be solved once and left. Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
- Not that a mesocosm or test harness proves field capability. Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement settles `left(R, φ)` for any particular pair of slots.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A per-slot enumeration plus a source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): a color instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*; it was the spectrometer that resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. Does the focal adaptation change contact with a specific other actor, and which residual of that actor moves? (Fig 11 :45-48, enumerate the habitat.)
2. Is the function effect second or third order, and which intermediate species carries it? (Fig 11 :52-54.)
3. **Function-instrument test:** does a source-discriminating field measurement of the function rate return a nonzero effect of the trait shift across repeated independent systems? Pelletier, Garant and Hendry (2009) reported *"we have no studies quantifying how ongoing evolution influences community or ecosystem variables in nature"*; that is a missing instrument, not a zero.
4. **Addition test:** can the changed contact be subtracted without substituting a habitat, verified in the field rather than in a harness? (Fig 11 :61-62, :66-70.)
5. **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: no valence flip; no cascade; symmetry of removal; clean substitution; additive currencies; one lever; instrumented zero.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The specific species, trait and threshold are owed by a ground reading. The framework returns a shape and stops at the limit of the available instruments, recording the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The live strains are the two-currency type question and the EICA binding-level conflict; both are carried visibly into `11_audit.md`, neither used to flip the verdict. The second seat is simulated and shares this evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that adaptation is good or bad, and it does not supply a list of harmful species. It says that a trait's sign is relative to the residual it is ranged against: the same change that lowers a focal species' residual can raise another's, and that is a relation, not a property of the trait. It becomes harmful to a function when the changed contact cascades two or three steps out, which a first-order habitat scope misses, and when the added actor's contact cannot be subtracted, so the ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state. Focal survival and the function rate are different currencies on different grounds, so they cannot be averaged into a net impact. The answer is a per-slot separator of four conditions with falsifiers written in advance, not a universal species list or threshold. The particular trait, species and instrument are left open until a ground reading supplies them; unreached is a verdict here, not silence.
