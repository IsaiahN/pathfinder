# Isomers and order: are "helps the focal species" and "harms the other species" the same term?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6). No measurement was taken.

## RESIDUAL

The question's two effects look like one change with two descriptions. The supporting derivation asks: over a fixed set of actors and traits, are the beneficial reading and the harmful reading the **same molecule under a different bond** (an isomer), a different member of a **functional group**, or a genuine import of a new atom? And what is the order in which the effects must be assessed?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (short form; full census in `01` section 2)

- Atoms held: the actors and traits read off a particular habitat (focal species, affected species, function carriers), the contact relation, the valence relation, the cascade relation, the ground. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :23).
- Bonds held: the seven, including `−` subtraction (the focal residual drops), `⋛` comparison (a threshold on "when"), `→` sequence (adaptation then contact then cascade), `+` conjunction (conditions co-occur). Negation `¬` is available and is **not** a bond (Operators :70-74).
- Symmetry: the two effects are not interchangeable. This is an ordered and comparative structure, not a symmetric one.
- Order by dependency (Fig 3): trait shift, then contact, then valence flip, then cascade, then the ground reading.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Same atoms, different bond: the isomer reading

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Take a fixed habitat: one focal species with a dispersal-enhancing trait, one predator with no toxin resistance, one scavenger that removes carrion, one function (carrion removal). The atoms are the same whether we are describing "the toad spreads successfully" or "the predator declines and carrion accumulates." What changes is the bond: in the first reading the trait is ranged against the focal species' residual; in the second it is ranged against the predator's residual and then, through the scavenger, against the function.

- As a flat ingredient list, *"cane toad, longer legs, toxin, predator, carrion"* the two outcomes are indistinguishable. That is the collapse Fig 12 :5 names: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- With the bond restored, they are different substances. The benefit is a subtraction on the focal slot; the harm is a comparison against a different ground plus a cascade. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the focal gain and the other-slot loss can be written over different atom sets with no shared actors (for example, the harm arises from an entirely novel actor imported from outside the enumerated habitat), the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism.

This is `triangulated` for the figure definition (Fig 12 :117 plus Fig 12 :5, distinct claims about arrangement and about the flat-formula loss) and `single-source` for the specific toad mapping, which is a world binding.

### Q2 - Functional group: the weaker but safer claim

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

If the fix is made at a different level, the shared structure is `actor → changed contact → residual flip against a ground`. Any focal species whose adaptation changes its contact with another actor belongs to this group. Which species, which trait and which function are qualifiers.

- The exemplar used the same move for GR and QM: over the full shared atom set they are isomers; as actually written they share only one atom and are a functional group. Here the parallel holds. Over a fully enumerated habitat the beneficial and harmful readings are isomers; where the habitat is only partly enumerated, the safer claim is that they are members of a functional group (a trait-driven contact change) with different qualifiers.
- **Falsifier:** a documented case where a focal adaptation changes its own residual with no contact change at all. By Fig 11 :40 (*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves"*) such a case would fall outside the functional group; it would be a pure within-slot improvement with no other-slot consequence. That is a legitimate outcome and would narrow the group, not break it.

### Q3 - Order: the sequence is part of the specification

Fig 12, sequence bond (`→`): *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. The order is not a matter of presentation.

1. **Trait shift first.** Without a heritable change in the focal trait distribution there is no adaptation to discuss. The question's own phrase "adaptation help a species survive" fixes this upstream.
2. **Contact second.** Fig 11 :40 makes contact the necessary condition for any effect outside the focal slot.
3. **Valence flip third.** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** The same trait is read against a second residual.
4. **Cascade fourth.** Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"** (the second-order effect is the one that mattered).
5. **Ground reading last.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Reversing 3 and 5 is the error the figures name most sharply. If a researcher first declares the trait harmful (a verdict) and then reads the function, the reading is a frame checking its own prediction. Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** If the broken link is the function instrument (Q4), adding more evolutionary theory upstream does not repair it.

**Falsifier on order:** a documented case where the function-level effect is measurable and reliable while the focal trait shift and contact remain uncharacterised, and the intervention still works. That would show the sequence is not a strict dependency for this case.

### Q4 - The two currencies and the settlement bond

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**

Transposed: focal survival (a demographic rate) and a function rate (carrion removal, productivity) are in different units. A single "net ecological impact" score is not a higher measurement; it is the error the figure names. This is a **live type question** and is recorded as a strain in `11_audit.md`, not hidden and not resolved by argument. What could resolve it is an instrument or a declared convention with no truth value (Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*).

The "when" itself is a `⋛` comparison: a threshold relation between two quantities (Operators :49: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). The pre-registered refuter in `01` section 4 is the test that the threshold is real: if a source-discriminating instrument returns zero function effect across repeated systems, no threshold exists to be located and the comparison is empty.

### Q5 - The standing-list refusal is a negation, not a subtraction

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

The question asks "when does adaptation become harmful," which invites a list of harmful adaptations. The figures refuse that list structurally. The refusal is a negation (`¬`): it removes the standing of a list to be composed over, without deleting anything from the record. It is **not** subtraction (`−`), which would merely remove one actor from a list. A vocabulary with subtraction but no negation could keep a list of "invasive species" and mark some as removed; it could not say the list itself has no standing. This distinction is carried into `08` C3 and into the outreach material: the answer is a separator, not a roster.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fully enumerated habitat, the beneficial and harmful readings of one adaptation are **isomers** (same actors and traits, different bond and different residual). Where the habitat is only partly enumerated, the safer claim is that they share a **functional group**, a trait-driven contact change, with different qualifiers. The order is part of the specification: trait shift, contact, valence flip, cascade, ground reading. The two effects are in different currencies and cannot be added. The refusal of a standing harmful-species list is a negation, not a subtraction. All four claims are `specified` and ungrounded; each has a falsifier above.
