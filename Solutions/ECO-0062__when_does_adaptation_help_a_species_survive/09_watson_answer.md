## QUESTION

When does adaptation help a species survive but make it more harmful to other species or ecosystem functions?

Restated as an effect: when does a drop in the focal species' R against its own limiting condition coincide with a rise in another slot's R or a degradation of a function rate? R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Option A treats adaptation as a property with a fixed sign and asks for a list of harmful cases. Option B derives a per-slot separator of conditions, keeps the two effects in separate currencies, and reports the trait and species as a gap. A sends a standing list that the figures structurally refuse; B sends a checkable shape with a pre-registered refuter.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity at least three: focal species and its adapted trait, one or more other species or a function, and the condition each is ranged against. Symmetry: not interchangeable, the two effects are ordered and in different currencies, so the structure is a comparison plus a subtraction, not a symmetric join. Scale: population over generations, community interaction web, ecosystem function rate, and lineage persistence. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Domain 1, focal adaptive loop.** Actors: the focal organism, its heritable trait distribution, its limiting condition. Currency: survival and reproduction. Anchor: a measurement of focal survival in that environment. Admitted.
- **Domain 2, habitat and interaction web.** Actors: predators, prey, competitors, mutualists, conditions and relations. Currency: contact and valence. Anchor: independent measurement of the other species' survival or abundance. Admitted.
- **Domain 3, ecosystem functions and cascade.** Actors: scavenging, nutrient cycling, decomposition, primary productivity and their carriers. Currency: function rate and second or third-order effects. Anchor: an instrument reading the function rate. Admitted.
- **Domain 4, time, maintenance and the seat.** Actors: the ground, the maintainer seat, the room chain. Currency: introduction versus exclusion. Anchor: a ground that does not update plus the recorded lineage. Admitted.
- **X1, adaptation as a standing beneficial property.** No nameable anchor, because valence flips with the residual. Not a domain, dissolved by Fig 11 :49-51.
- **X2, ecosystem health as one scalar.** No nameable anchor without choosing a slot and a function. Not a domain, dissolved by Fig 1 :19 and Fig 12 :125.

Union, not partition. D1 and D2 form the valence-flip compound; D2 and D3 form the cascade compound; D4 overlaps all three as the question of which reading may count as a verdict. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Dependency order: trait shift, contact, valence flip, cascade, ground reading (Fig 3 :20). A function reading taken before the trait and contact are characterised is a reading below the break.

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :40 (*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing"*) governs capability, which is agent-and-habitat; the focal organism's own survival against its limiting condition is its residual, a different object. Re-census dissolves the apparent contradiction. Two live strains remain and are named in the verdict fields below: the two-currency type question and the EICA binding-level conflict.

## TETHER (primary, verbatim)

Cited against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Quotes are verbatim from the SVGs.

**D1 Focal adaptive loop, status triangulated (Fig 1 per-slot residual and Fig 11 capability rule).**

- Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The adaptation cannot be assigned a sign from the focal organism alone.
- Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* An effect outside the focal slot requires a contact change.
- Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The focal gain and the other loss must not be merged.

Reading: adaptation genuinely lowers the focal residual, but that fact alone predicts nothing about other slots. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a documented focal adaptation with no contact change that nevertheless changes another slot.

**D2 Habitat and interaction web, status triangulated (Fig 11 valence and Fig 2 anchor, low overlap).**

- Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- Fig 11 :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

Reading: the harm appears when the changed trait is ranged against a second residual with the opposite sign, and which second residual exists is read off the world, not composed. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a valence-invariant actor across a documented change of residual.

- Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"*

Reading: function harm is commonly second or third order, and the function-level link is often uninstrumented. Mode `specified` for the structure, `general` for the missing-instrument rule. Refuter: a measured zero second-order term across repeated systems with a source-discriminating instrument.

**D4 Time, maintenance and seat, status single-source (Fig 11 asymmetry and Fig 10 seat rules).**

Reading: the changed contact persists as an addition; containment substitutes a habitat with two silent failures; only a non-updating ground settles a local case. Mode `general` structural, `specified` per case. Refuter: a demonstrated subtraction with the harm ending and the system solved.

**Union and method, status triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 12).**

**Which trait, species, function or threshold, status gap.** No figure line governs this after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. ## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

World sources corroborate only. Fields are chosen for low overlap with each other and with the figures. Overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Invasion ecology, case study.** Jolly, Shine and Greenlees, *Ecology and Evolution*, 2015, opened in full via PMC. Quote: *"The scarcity of scavenging lace monitors in toad-invaded areas translated into a 52% decrease in rates of carrion removal ... and an increase (by 61%) in numbers of brush turkeys."* Prediction: a focal species' spread harms a function two steps through a predator decline. **Corroborates** D3 and the C2 cascade clause. Overlap: one well-studied system; the related Phillips 2006 and Hudson 2016 papers are the same evidence pool.
- **Evolutionary ecology, mechanism.** Pelletier, Garant and Hendry, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 2009, opened in full via PMC. Quote: *"the evolution of one species often improves its ability to compete with, or to exploit, another species. This adaptation of one species then selects for countermeasures in the other species, initiating a coevolutionary arms race with a variety of possible outcomes."* Also: *"we have no studies quantifying how ongoing evolution influences community or ecosystem variables in nature."* Prediction: one species' adaptation changes another's selection regime, and the function-level instrument is missing. **Corroborates** D2 and D3. Overlap: distinct from the toad case at the mechanism level.
- **Community ecology and biodiversity-functioning.** Aubree et al., *Ecology Letters*, 2020, opened in full via PMC. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the list does not. Adaptation becomes harmful to other species or ecosystem functions when a focal trait shift changes contact so that its valence flips against a second residual (Fig 11 :49-51), the effect cascades two or three steps out so a first-order habitat scope misses it (Fig 11 :52-54), the changed actor is an addition whose contact cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :61-63), and the focal gain and the function loss are read on separate non-updating grounds in separate currencies (Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :125, Fig 2 :29). These are a union of conditions with per-domain verdicts never averaged. Over a fixed habitat the two readings are isomers; the refusal of a standing harmful-species list is a negation, not a subtraction. Which trait, species, function or threshold occupies a slot is a declared `gap` of the unreached kind. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Two live strains are named: the two-currency type question, and the EICA binding-level conflict; neither flips the verdict.

- **cost if wrong:** a wrong separator sends a manager after the wrong lever. Treating the question as one balance lets a single intervention clear the focal-survival condition while leaving the cascade and addition conditions standing, which is exactly the union failure Fig 9 :55 predicts. The deeper cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a standing species list instead of a refutable shape; Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible because the packet carries its refuters in front of its links.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a clause fails; a refusal is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than struck out. The shape, its four conditions and its seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."*). A superseding packet can replace a sent one without withdrawing any primitive.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating field instrument returning zero function effect across repeated independent systems would break the cascade and function-harm clauses; a focal adaptation with no contact change that still shifts another slot would break D1 and D2; a demonstrated subtraction of an introduced actor's contact with the harm ending would break D4; a legitimate shared-unit conversion of focal fitness and a function rate would break the two-currency refusal; and a figure re-census that supplies a universal trait list would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige, consensus and meta-analytic agreement are not such instruments.
