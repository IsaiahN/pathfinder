## 0. The question in effect terms

> When does adaptation help a species survive but make it more harmful to other species or ecosystem functions?

State it as an effect, not a cause. A focal species' heritable trait distribution shifts so that its own residual R falls against a limiting condition: it survives and reproduces better where it was previously stressed. In the same move, one or more of the following rises: the residual of a predator that now eats a toxic prey, the residual of a competitor displaced by a faster grower, or the error in an ecosystem function such as carrion removal, nutrient cycling or primary productivity. The question asks for the **conditions under which the two effects co-occur**, indexed by which species and which function.

Two things are refused at the outset.

1. **Adaptation is given no standing valence.** The ordinary term is re-grounded as a contact-changing trait shift whose sign is relative to a residual (Fig 11 :49-51). Asking whether adaptation is good or bad asks for a property of an actor. The figures refuse that.
2. **The two effects are not averaged.** The focal species' survival and the ecosystem function are different currencies in different slots (Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :125-126). A net score claiming adaptation helps overall is the aggregation failure Fig 1 :22 names.

The sought-for answer is bound as an ordinary term before the run: **a separator is a per-slot set of conditions each of which can be checked at a ground, such that when they co-occur a focal survival gain and an other-slot or function loss both register.** That is a shape, checkable and refutable. It is not a list of named species and not a universal threshold.

## 2. Domain census (written before any conclusion)

### 2a. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least three-place: (focal species and its adapted trait) / (one or more other species or a function) / (the condition each is ranged against). It is indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** A pooled "biodiversity impact" number is a different quantity and is not admitted.
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. The focal survival gain and the other-slot loss are ordered and incommensurable; this is a comparison (Operators :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*) and a subtraction on one side, not a symmetric join. Treating the two as interchangeable is the flat-formula error of Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- **Scale.** Four scales are in play: the population over generations (trait shift), the community (interaction web), the ecosystem (function rate) and the lineage (the trait is inherited and the changed contact persists). The membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30), so a claim at one scale cannot be replayed at another without a measured gap.

### 2b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain requires a nameable anchor. Where none is nameable, the item is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (ground) | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Focal adaptive loop** | the focal organism, its heritable trait distribution, its limiting condition | survival and reproduction; reduction of its own R | a measurement of focal survival or fitness in the limiting environment | admitted |
| **D2 Habitat and interaction web** | predators, prey, competitors, mutualists, light, water, temperature, season, terrain; the relations among them | contact and valence | the survival or abundance of the other species, measured independently | admitted |
| **D3 Ecosystem functions and cascade** | scavenging, nutrient cycling, decomposition, primary productivity, and the species that carry them | rate of the function; second and third-order effects | an instrument reading the function rate (for example carrion removal at bait stations) | admitted |
| **D4 Time, maintenance and the seat** | the ground, the maintainer seat, the available room chain | introduction versus exclusion; which reading may count as a verdict | the ground that does not update and the recorded lineage of the ruling | admitted |
| **X1 "Adaptation is beneficial" as a standing property** | the trait | none | none nameable: valence flips with the residual | **not a domain**, dissolved by Fig 11 :49-51 |
| **X2 "Ecosystem health" as a single scalar** | the ecosystem | one aggregated score | none nameable without choosing which function and which slot | **not a domain**, dissolved by Fig 1 :19/:22 and Fig 12 :125-126 |

### 2c. Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

- D1 ∩ D2 = the **valence-flip compound**: the adapted trait that lowers the focal residual and raises another's. It is not "a harder case of adaptation" (Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**). It owns its own loop.
- D2 ∩ D3 = the **cascade compound**: a species decline or increase that changes a function two or three steps out.
- D4 overlaps all three as the question of which reading is allowed to be a verdict.

Because it is a union, one lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56). This is why managing the focal species better and restoring the function are not the same intervention.

### 2d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces..."**

1. D1: the focal trait shift must occur and be heritable (perception and vocabulary, links 1 and 2).
2. D2: the changed trait must contact other actors; their residual shift is the objective link (link 3).
3. D3: the species shift must propagate to a function (planning and carry, links 4 and 5).
4. D4: the seat must keep a non-updating ground reachable so that 1 to 3 can be read at all.

A function-level reading taken before the focal trait and the contact are characterised is a reading below the break: Fig 3 :22-23: **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

Quotes are verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Each finding carries its own refuter before the conclusion in section 4 is reached.

### F1 - The governing line: valence is relative to the residual (decisive for "when")

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A focal species' adaptation is not good or bad. It is good *against the focal species' residual* and can be bad *against another species' residual*. The cane toad's longer legs are the same trait in both readings: they increase dispersal and survival in the new range (structural advantage to the toad) and deliver a toxic, fast-spreading prey to predators with no coevolutionary history (harm to the predator's residual). Fig 11 :50 makes this structural, not anecdotal: mutualism and parasitism are relations.

A different figure gives the second half of the mechanism. Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** An adaptation that stays entirely inside the focal organism's own physiology and changes no contact with another actor changes nothing for other species. The harm requires a contact change. This is **triangulated** (Fig 11 :49-51 from the habitat, Fig 11 :40 from the capability rule, low internal overlap).

- **Refuter:** a documented focal adaptation that lowers the focal residual and leaves every enumerated other residual and function measurement unchanged, sustained across a habitat enumeration that reaches at least two steps out. If it holds, the valence-relative claim fails for that case and the separator narrows.

### F2 - The habitat must be enumerated, not composed

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**
Fig 11 :45-47: **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"**, **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**, **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**.

The "when" cannot be derived from a general rule about adaptation. It is read off which actors, conditions and relations are actually in contact with the changed trait. Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** The list of affected species is an import from the world, with a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (does it explain a gap), not a composition inside an ecological theory. Fig 9 :27: **"describe it before you go looking, or"**; Fig 9 :28: **"any frame you pick will seem to fit"**.

- **Refuter:** a habitat enumeration generated purely by composing a fixed trait taxonomy that correctly predicts which other species and functions change, with no reading off the world. If it holds, the enumeration rule is unnecessary for this residual.

### F3 - Contact cascades: the harm is usually not first order

Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"**
Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

The figure's own example is exactly the question's second half: an intervention that helps one target propagates three steps out to a different species and a different function. Scoping to direct contact is the failure. The cane toad case is the same structure: the adapted dispersal trait kills large predators directly, and the loss of those predators changes scavenging two steps out. Jolly, Shine and Greenlees (2015, *Ecology and Evolution*, read this session) measured a **52% decrease in carrion removal** and a **61% increase in brush turkeys** in toad-invaded sites, with the mechanism running through the decline of lace monitors. The world source corroborates; the figure decides.

- **Refuter:** a case where all harm to other species and functions is fully predicted by first-order direct contact, with no measurable second or third-order term, across an enumeration that reaches three steps out. If it holds, the cascade condition is not general.

### F4 - Introduction is available, exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :62: **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

A successful adaptation is an addition to the habitat. Once the trait or the species is there, the contact cannot be subtracted by decree. This is why the answer to "when does the harm occur" includes a maintenance condition: where an actor has been introduced for its cascade, the work of managing its effects is permanent. Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."** The figure names the question's own example and names the failure mode: the cascade was not enumerated before the introduction.

- **Refuter:** a demonstrated case where a maintainer can subtract an introduced actor's contact from a habitat without substituting another habitat, so that the harm ends and the system is a solved state. If it holds, the asymmetry condition fails.

### F5 - Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

The obvious response to a harmful adaptation, moving the species or containing the interaction, is a habitat substitution with two silent failures. This matters for the "when": a proposed fix that changes the habitat can create a new harm that is invisible until it acts. Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** A mesocosm result that shows no harm proves the mesocosm's wiring, not the field's capability.

- **Refuter:** a relocation or containment that removes the harm while reproducing every goal-required contact and introducing no unchosen actor, verified in the field rather than in a harness. If it holds, the substitution condition fails for that case.

### F6 - The two effects are different currencies in different slots

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**

Focal fitness and the rate of carrion removal are in different units. Adding them into a net ecological value is the error the figure names. The two currencies are held apart, and verdicts are recorded per slot and per domain, never averaged. Over a fixed set of actors and traits, "beneficial to the focal species" and "harmful to the predator" are isomers (Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**): same actors, arranged against different residuals.

- **Refuter:** a single measurement that legitimately converts focal fitness and a function rate into one additive quantity with a shared unit and a non-updating ground. If it holds, the two-currency refusal is too strong; until then it is a live type question, named not hidden.

### F7 - Union compounds need two grounds, so one lever is not enough

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

The focal-survival condition and the other-slot-harm condition have different anchors: the focal species' measured survival and the other species' or function's measured state. An intervention that improves only one leaves the intersection standing. Effect sizes from meta-analyses are filters for where to look (Fig 9 :60), not witnesses for whether this particular slot is harmed.

- **Refuter:** a single intervention that clears both conditions in a documented case, restoring the other species or function while leaving the focal gain intact, measured on two independent grounds. If it holds, the union reading is unnecessary for that case, though not refuted generally.

### F8 - The ground must not update on the study's own report

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

A claim that an adaptation harms a function must be ranged against a reading of the function that does not move when the ecologists agree it is harmed. The habitat (the list of actors) updates; the ground (the measured carrion removal rate, the measured predator abundance) does not. If the "harm" is inferred from the same model that predicted the focal benefit, it is one frame reading itself, and it fails Fig 2 :35.

- **Refuter:** a case where the function reading is demonstrably changed by the researchers' expectation of harm (a moving anchor), so that the reported decline is an artefact of the frame rather than a ground reading. If it holds, that study is excluded; the condition itself stands.

### F9 - The broken link is often an instrument, not an argument

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 3 :29: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

The literature's own honest reports show the function-level link is the weakest. Pelletier, Garant and Hendry (2009, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, read this session): *"we have no studies quantifying how ongoing evolution influences community or ecosystem variables in nature."* Longman et al. (2025, *Ecology*, read via search index) found that selection on consumer traits produced divergent traits in the lab but *"these feedbacks were not strong enough to result in clear community effects in the field."* That is a missing or weak instrument, not evidence that the harm never occurs. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**; Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating field instrument that measures the function-level effect of a known focal trait shift directly, and returns zero across repeated independent systems. If it holds, the function-harm condition is refuted at the ground, not merely unmeasured.

### F10 - No figure line names the trait, the species or the threshold

After the re-census above, no figure line selects which concrete trait, which species or which numerical threshold occupies a slot. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The missing thing is a per-system habitat enumeration plus a function instrument, characterised in effect terms but not held by the figures.

This is a declared **gap**, not an invitation to invent a list. The world sources supply concrete instances (cane toad dispersal, EICA reallocation, parasite-mutualist shifts, Braess-style network additions), each with provenance, but they are bindings onto figure atoms, not new figure primitives. Fig 6 :23 applies: no frame escapes its own closure; the list is an import with a source gate.

- **Refuter:** a figure line that does return a universal trait list, species list or threshold for this residual. If found, the gap is withdrawn. Until then, "unreached is a verdict, not a silence."

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any conclusion or outreach: Fig 10, seat-reasoning rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The separator claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **No valence flip.** A focal adaptation lowers the focal residual and leaves every enumerated other residual and function measurement unchanged, with an enumeration reaching at least two steps out (breaks F1).
2. **No cascade.** All harm is fully predicted by first-order direct contact, with a measured zero second and third-order term across repeated systems (breaks F3).
3. **Symmetry of removal.** A maintainer can subtract an introduced actor's contact from the habitat without substituting another habitat and the harm ends, making the system a solved state (breaks F4).
4. **Clean substitution.** A relocation or containment removes the harm while reproducing every goal-required contact and introducing no unchosen actor, verified in the field (breaks F5).
5. **Additive currencies.** One measurement legitimately converts focal fitness and a function rate into one additive quantity with a shared unit and a non-updating ground (breaks F6).
6. **One lever.** A single intervention restores all conditions on two independent grounds in a documented case (weakens F7).
7. **Instrumented zero.** A source-discriminating field instrument returns zero function effect across repeated independent systems (breaks F9 at the ground).

If any fires, the relevant finding is narrowed or withdrawn and the separator is redrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. The most likely firing is number 7, because the literature itself says the function-level instrument is largely missing; that would be an honest narrowing, not a defeat of F1.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F3. The answer to "when" is a valence flip plus a contact cascade, not a general property of adaptation.
2. State the union (F7) before proposing any intervention. One lever leaves the intersection standing.
3. Bind the separated currencies (F6) as an ordinary term so a reader cannot quietly average them.
4. Enumerate the habitat from the world with provenance (F2); do not generate the affected-species list from a taxonomy.
5. Keep D4's seat conditions visible: a non-updating ground and a room that changes when the question changes (Fig 10 :71: **"The room composition is a function of the question."**, Fig 10 :72: **"When the question changes, the room should change."**).
6. Record the gap (F10) as unreached, not silence. Characterise the missing instrument in effect terms.
7. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

### Five-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is adaptation a property of the species?** No. Fig 11 :39-40, capability is agent-and-habitat; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
2. **What are the two effects?** A focal residual drop (D1) and an other-slot or function residual rise (D2/D3), in different currencies (Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :125).
3. **What connects them?** Contact, then cascade, then an addition that cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :40, :52, :61-62).
4. **How is the "when" obtained?** By enumerating the habitat from the world (Fig 11 :43, :48), not by composing a general rule; and by keeping the union unbundled (Fig 9 :54-57).
5. **What settles a particular case?** A ground reading with an anchor that does not update (Fig 2 :29, :35) and a room that changes when the question changes (Fig 10 :71-72). The universal list is a gap (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

### Updated verdict

The separator survives as a shape: **a focal adaptation becomes harmful when its changed contact carries a valence that is positive against the focal residual and negative against another slot, the contact cascades beyond first order, the resulting actor cannot be subtracted from the habitat, and the two effects are read on separate non-updating grounds.** The universal trait list, species list or threshold does not survive; it is a declared gap of the unreached kind. Strains after re-census: one live type question (two currencies), one live mechanism-level strain (EICA binding), none hidden, none forcing a change to the figure verdict.

## 7. Methods carried forward (not recordings)

- **Per-slot residual before impact:** whenever a question asks whether a change "helps" or "harms," index R per slot first and refuse aggregation (Fig 1 :19/:22).
- **Valence re-grounding:** replace "beneficial/harmful" with "which residual, ranged against which non-updating ground" (Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 2 :29).
- **Habitat enumeration:** read the actor, condition and relation lists off the world with provenance; do not compose them (Fig 11 :43-48).
- **Cascade check:** always enumerate two steps out before declaring an impact first order (Fig 11 :52-54).
- **Asymmetry check:** ask whether the changed contact can be subtracted or only substituted (Fig 11 :61-69).
- **Union check:** ask whether one lever clears all conditions or only the single-condition region (Fig 9 :54-57).
- **Refuter first:** write the failure conditions before the conclusion (seat-reasoning rule; Fig 2 :42).
