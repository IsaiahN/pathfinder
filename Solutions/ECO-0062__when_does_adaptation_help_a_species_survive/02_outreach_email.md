# Outreach draft: check one separator before deciding whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01` section 4 and the three sub-claims in `08` survive your own check. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about adaptation and harm to other species

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** "Adaptation helps a species survive" and "adaptation harms other species or ecosystem functions" are not two sides of one balance sheet. The first is a drop in the focal species' residual against its own limiting condition. The second is a rise in a different slot's residual against a different ground. The claim is that the second effect appears when four conditions co-occur:
>
> 1. the changed trait alters contact with another actor, so its valence flips from beneficial to the focal residual to harmful to another's;
> 2. the effect reaches two or more steps out, so a first-order habitat scope misses the function that matters;
> 3. the changed actor is an addition to the habitat, and the contact cannot be subtracted, only substituted;
> 4. the focal survival gain and the other-slot loss are measured on separate non-updating grounds, so neither is inferred from the same model that predicted the other.
>
> This is a shape, not a list of species and not a number. The framework returns *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). Which trait or species occupies a slot is left open and reported as a gap, not filled in.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that adaptation is generally harmful, that any named invasive species is always bad, that a meta-analytic effect size settles a local case, or that a managed ecosystem can be solved once and left. Those are bindings from a particular evidence pool onto the shape; they need provenance and a ground reading in each case. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
>
> **What would refute the output (written before this message):**
> 1. a focal adaptation that lowers the focal residual and leaves every enumerated other residual and function measurement unchanged, with enumeration two steps out;
> 2. all harm fully predicted by first-order direct contact, with a measured zero second-order term;
> 3. an introduced actor whose contact can be subtracted from the habitat so the harm ends and the system is solved;
> 4. one measurement that legitimately converts focal fitness and a function rate into one additive quantity with a shared unit and a non-updating ground;
> 5. a source-discriminating field instrument returning zero function effect across repeated independent systems.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and verbatim text (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Governing line: Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
> - Cane toad cascade, measured: Jolly, Shine and Greenlees 2015, *Ecology and Evolution*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4588653/
> - Eco-evolutionary dynamics and the missing function instrument: Pelletier, Garant and Hendry 2009, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2690510/
> - Network analogue (an addition that worsens the system): Nagurney and Nagurney 2020, *International Encyclopedia of Transportation*, https://supernet.isenberg.umass.edu/articles/braess-encyc.pdf
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the figures and formula are in the extract linked above. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
