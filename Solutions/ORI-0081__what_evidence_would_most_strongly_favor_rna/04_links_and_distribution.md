## Three things in the literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The field has an anchor problem, not a shortage of reviews.** The abiogenesis review says the transition has not been observed, and the RNA-world review says no paradigm can yet be conclusively falsified. That is the Fig 2 anchor test failing honestly, not a reason to pick a favorite.
2. **The hybrid is live, not a courtesy compromise.** Hordijk and Steel explicitly ask whether autocatalytic sets can be built with RNA and peptides together, and the proto-metabolism review says the hypotheses are *"not mutually exclusive."* That supports treating hybrid as a union compound to be tested at the intersection (F6).
3. **Modern features are lossy readings.** Nogal et al. warn that extant-life signatures can mislead, and the RNA-world review records genuine disagreement about whether RNA was first or a derivative. That is why the strongest evidence must discriminate historical sources, not retrodict a modern one (F4).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual delivers a recording, not a generator. Lead with the split and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before sending the reviews.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A mass send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the split and the shape claim; (2) the pre-registered refuter and the question "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record their verdict on stage 1; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not our summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim, refuter. No theory, no links. Record the verdict before any framework is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** offer the figure extract and the three world reviews. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. It is a `specified` structural claim with a `gap` for the historical verdict, not a winner. Nothing is `grounded` here.
