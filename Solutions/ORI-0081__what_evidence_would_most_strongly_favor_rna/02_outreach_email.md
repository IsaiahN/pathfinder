# Outreach draft: check the shape of the discriminating evidence before the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the split and the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 to F4 are stated cleanly. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What evidence would actually discriminate RNA-first from metabolism-first?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in origins of life and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with a model. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the three-way comparison (RNA-first, metabolism-first, or a hybrid) is not one hard question. Each answer keeps working well on a different subset of the evidence, so more of the same evidence does not shrink the disagreement. The "hybrid" option is not a third peer alternative; it is a union of the other two conditions, so it has to be unbundled and tested at the intersection before it is searched for. This is a structural claim about the shape of the question, not a verdict for any closure.
>
> What the figures say the discriminating evidence must look like:
> 1. It must be an anchor, a measurement or experiment that does not change its answer because of what any camp thinks. Agreement among researchers, publication counts, and "most favored paradigm" are not such an anchor.
> 2. It must register a crossing from chemistry to a system: a closed loop, a part that survives its own use, and heritable variation that selection can act on. That is the shape; the framework does not name the particular molecule.
> 3. It must discriminate historical sources. The ribosome's RNA core and the nucleotide cofactors are readings that all three models can retrodict, so another retrodiction favors nobody.
>
> **What would favor each closure, if the ground were speaking:**
> - RNA-first: an RNA-only system that self-replicates with heritable variation and Darwinian evolution, with no metabolism-like network required.
> - Metabolism-first: a self-sustaining autocatalytic network that shows heritable, selectable variation without any genetic polymer.
> - Hybrid: an evolvable system that requires both a genetic polymer and a network in mutual dependence, and loses evolvability if either is removed.
>
> I am not claiming any of these has been demonstrated. The transition from non-life to life has not been observed, so the historical verdict is currently a `gap`. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before I sent this):**
> 1. A single body of evidence that does not discriminate historical sources is shown to conclusively settle priority.
> 2. An anchor that moves with frame preference (consensus, prestige, number of adherents) is accepted as decisive.
> 3. A system is demonstrated where all three closures are simultaneously true in a way that is not a union compound with a surplus.
> 4. Sustained work on one subset makes the three-way disagreement shrink, showing it was one hard question after all.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, verbatim with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
> - RNA world, review and stated evidential limits: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_world
> - Proto-metabolism and the gene-first / metabolism-first comparison: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-metabolism
> - Abiogenesis overview, including the NASA definition of life: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiogenesis
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode (general, specified, grounded) of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the figure extract and the three world links above. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode (Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).
