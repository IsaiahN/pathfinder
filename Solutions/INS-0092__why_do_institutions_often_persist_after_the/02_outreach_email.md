**Subject:** One checkable claim about why institutions outlive their conditions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** An institution is a frame with a library Γ: rules, routines, categories and an anchor. It was settled against conditions C0, so its residual R was small. The conditions move to C1, R grows, and the institution keeps its form. What keeps Γ fixed and the channel to the moved ground closed while the residual persists and does not trend down?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Persistence is a union of two conditions, not one hard problem and not a single lever:
>
> 1. No new atom enters the institution's closure. Internal reform (more procedure, more compliance) cannot add what the changed ground requires, because *closure is idempotent* and only contact with a differing closure moves the wall.
> 2. The channel to the moved ground is decayed. The ground does not decay, the channel does: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings. The output should say "you have lost touch," but from inside it reads as "business as usual."
>
> The framework names the first failure directly: *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure."* It also notes that standing earned on the old conditions survives those conditions, which makes the occupants of a persisting institution structurally the least able to re-fit it.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that institutions are irrational, not that culture alone explains persistence, and not that a theory of everything is being offered. The framework returns a shape, not a name: it can say a room must differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced, but it cannot name the specific successor institution. No measurement here settles anything; this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.
>
> **What would refute it (written before I sent this):**
> 1. A documented ground change where the institution's Γ is re-fitted at each handoff, residual per slot trends down, and no new atom is imported.
> 2. An institution with fresh, non-saturated, per-slot instruments and an unbroken channel that still fails to register a documented change.
> 3. Two institutions with identical Γ and identical habitat that diverge in persistence with no difference in standing or seat.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
