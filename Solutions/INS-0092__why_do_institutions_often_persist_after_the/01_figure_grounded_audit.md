## 0. The residual, in effect terms

An institution is a frame with a library Γ: rules, routines, categories, standard operating procedures, a seat arrangement, and an anchor it was settled against. At t0 the conditions C0 are the ground; Γ was minted and settled against C0, so R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| was small. At t1 conditions move to C1 (new actors, a new currency, a new anchor). R now grows because Γ still predicts C0. Yet the institution's form, its Γ, its rules and its seat occupants, does not change, or changes only at the margin.

So the question is not "why is there resistance to change" (a theory word). It is: what keeps Γ fixed and the channel to the moved ground closed while R persists and does not trend down? The formula's own selector would point a refusal at exactly such a term:

> Fig 5 :59: *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at, which is the same shrinking test the selector already runs, read with the opposite sign."*

An institution that persists after its ground moved is a frame that has not fired the refusal its own shrinking test owes. The derivation asks which guard or which channel is at zero.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** a four-place relation among an institution (frame with Γ, seat, anchor), a ground (conditions C), a time ordering, and a maintainer seat that could act. It carries an ordered bond (→ sequence: conditions change, then a response is owing) and a comparison bond (⋛ threshold: R crosses what the frame will tolerate). Neither is symmetric.
- **Symmetry:** "persists" is a threshold relative to changed conditions, not a symmetric join of two equals (Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). The bond from C0 to C1 is sequence, not identity: the new conditions are not the old ones under a different name.
- **Scale:** three scales. The single institution loop (one frame settling against a ground). The population or habitat of institutions (Fig 4). The temporal chain across handoffs and generations (Fig 7). The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The institution as a frame with Γ | decision makers, rules, routines, categories, a library Γ | residual R per slot, |φ|, reach = closure(Γ) | the conditions C0 the rules were fitted to (a market, a patient outcome) (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| D2 The changed conditions / habitat | new conditions, actors, relations, customers, regulators, entrants, technologies | survival, payment, what the ground keeps (Fig 11 :14) | the ground itself, which does not update (Fig 2 :33) | admitted |
| D3 The maintainer seat / governance | boards, regulators, seat occupants, conventions | conventions, standing, what the seat may author (Fig 10 :46) | the ground, kept reachable (Fig 10 :29) | admitted |
| D4 The temporal chain / institutional memory | rooms (institutional forms at successive times), handoffs, records | agreement versus evidence (Fig 7 :13); bracket fidelity and ground pressure (Fig 7 :37-39) | the ground's pressure on the room | admitted |
| X1 "Institutional will" as a free-floating cause | a will that persists independent of contact | none nameable | none nameable | not a domain yet |
| X2 "Culture" or "tradition" as sole cause | inherited prior w, the library's own history | belief weight | already inside D1 | absorbed into D1 |
| X3 "Path dependence" as a name | a label for the mechanism | none of its own | already inside D1, D3, D4 | absorbed |

X1 is excluded by Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* A will with no nameable anchor is a label applied afterwards, not an actor.

**Partition or union: union.** Persistence requires the conjunction of two conditions:

- C1: no new atom enters the institution's closure (D1/D2). The institution searches only inside its own Γ.
- C2: the channel to the moved ground is decayed (D3/D4). The change never registers, or is pooled away.

A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*). Persistence is a specific compound with its own loop, not one hard problem.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception (does the change register?) → vocabulary (are there terms for the new conditions?) → objective → planning → learn and carry. A typical persistence break sits at perception or vocabulary. Effort at planning (restructuring, a new strategy document) lands above the break and cannot arrive: Fig 3 :19 says *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

## 3. Findings (each with verbatim lines, mode and refuter)

### F1: The room stays fixed while the question moves (decisive, triangulated)

> Fig 10 :72: **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**
>
> Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Reading (`specified`): an institution is a room; changed conditions are a moved question; persistence after the change is the named failure, not a puzzle. The "correlation" in the line is the observed stability of the institution, which is a symptom of the failure to re-fit, not evidence that the institution still fits.

Refuter: show an institution whose form stayed fixed while its question moved, where the form was re-selected against the new ground at every handoff and the residual did not rise. Triangulated across Fig 10 (seat level) and Fig 7 (chain level), two figures with different objects.

### F2: Standing is a stake that survives the outcome (triangulated)

> Fig 10 :61: **"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."**
>
> Fig 10 :80-81: **"frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."**

Reading (`specified`): the institution's authority, endowment and legitimacy were earned on C0 and survive C0. That standing is precisely a stake in the reading, which the seat rules disqualify: Fig 10 :59 says *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* So the occupants of a persisting institution are structurally the least able to re-fit it, because the thing they would have to give up is the thing that gives them their position.

Refuter: an institution whose standing is fully re-conferred by the new conditions each period (no surviving stake) and which still persists with a rising residual. Triangulated across the standing rule and the correlation-fixed-at-origin rule.

### F3: The ground does not decay, the channel does (triangulated)

> Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
>
> Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
>
> Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is"** **"you have lost touch"**

Reading (`specified`): persistence is often a channel failure, not a property of the institution's people. The instruments that read C0 go stale; the signal of C1 is dropped, saturated or pooled into an average; and the output that should read "you have lost touch" is read as "business as usual." Fig 1 :22 names the averaging: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Refuter: an institution with fresh, non-saturated, per-slot instruments and an unbroken channel that still fails to register a documented change in its ground.

### F4: Closure is idempotent; internal reform adds no atom (triangulated)

> Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**
>
> Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Reading (`specified`): more procedure, more compliance, more of the same routines cannot supply what a changed ground requires. Only contact with a differing closure moves the wall. So reform from inside Γ reproduces the very closure that is failing.

Refuter: an institution that renews its repertoire purely by internal composition, with no atom from a frame whose closure differs, and whose residual then trends down against the new ground.

### F5: Replaying the founding success (triangulated)

> Fig 4 :25-26: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**
>
> Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Reading (`specified`): the institution carries the success that founded it as a recording and replays it after the conditions that made it successful have gone. It looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. The agreement that accumulates around the replay is variation, not a verdict (Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*).

Refuter: an institution that treats its founding method as a generator (re-derived and re-tested each cycle) and still persists unchanged after a documented ground change with a rising residual.

### F6: Capability is agent-and-habitat, not agent alone (triangulated)

> Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
>
> Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Reading (`general` for the structural claim, `specified` for the institution application): persistence cannot be located in the institution's people or culture alone. It is a property of institution-and-habitat. Replacing the leadership without changing contact changes nothing, however much it improves.

Refuter: identical habitat and identical contact, institution changes form anyway, with the residual trending down. That would move the cause to the agent alone and refute the claim.

### F7: Habitat substitution and contact cascades (triangulated)

> Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
>
> Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
>
> Fig 11 :52-54: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Reading (`specified`): when conditions change, the institution has been moved into a substituted habitat. It silently fails to reproduce what its goal required, inherits actors it did not choose, and scopes its enumeration to direct contact, so the residual that matters is several steps out. This is why "we still do our core work well" coexists with a rising residual at the ground.

Refuter: an institution whose original goal is still met after a documented habitat substitution, with a full second-order enumeration showing no dropped condition and no inherited actor with adverse valence.

### F8: The anchor became a frame (triangulated)

> Fig 2 :33-34: **"The anchor must not update. If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**
>
> Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Reading (`specified`): the institution once occupied an anchor-like role: its measures settled questions. When conditions move, if its own measures become the reference (it updates on itself), the anchor becomes another frame and everything drifts together. The drift is coherent, which is exactly why the persistence looks normal from inside. Fig 10 :49 adds *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* A charter that still declares the old meaning does not re-ground the institution.

Refuter: an institution whose anchor is demonstrably externally pinned and did not move, and which still persists with a rising residual against the changed conditions.

### F9: Persistence of form can hide a change of kind (single-source)

> Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
>
> Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Reading (`specified`): an institution is a part that survives its own use. The rule "nothing records which a term is" means a part that was catalytic (regenerating) can become spent while remaining present. Observing the same form at t0 and t1 does not establish the same substance; the difference is carried by the bond to the changed ground and is invisible in the org chart.

Refuter: a record that marks, for each institutional part, whether it is spent or regenerating across uses, and shows no hidden conversion after the ground moved. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the institution-level application unmeasured.

### F10: Naming the successor institution is a `gap` (after genuine re-census)

> Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
>
> Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
>
> Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No figure line names the specific institution that should replace a persisting one. The figures give a shape: a room that differs from the last and matches the residual it surfaced (Fig 7 :36), reached by a source-gated import from a closure that differs (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). They do not give a name. This is declared `gap`, not filled by analogy.

## 4. Apparent conflicts returned to the census (never used to flip a verdict)

1. **Pierson (path dependence) looks internalist.** Increasing returns describe dynamics inside a closure. This seems to conflict with Fig 11 :40 (capability is agent-and-habitat). Re-census: increasing returns is a description of the closure's internal dynamics; the ground still settles whether the path pays. Different objects, no strain. It corroborates C1 only.
2. **Staw (self-justification) looks agent-only.** Responsibility at the individual stratum seems to conflict with Fig 11 :40. Re-census: the figure line governs capability; the seat rule Fig 10 :61 governs standing that survives an outcome. Staw is re-mapped to a standing term at the individual stratum, where it corroborates F2. It is not a refutation of the agent-and-habitat claim.
3. **DiMaggio and Powell (isomorphism) looks like a theory of persistence.** Homogenization through imitation is agreement, and Fig 2 :40 says *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Re-census: isomorphism describes why rooms resemble one another (Fig 7 :12), not why the ground pays. It corroborates D4 as a description of sameness, with zero verdict weight.

A conflict sent us back to the census. It did not change a verdict. Agreement from a shared institutional-theory evidence pool is one witness, not two.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument"* (Seat Map, failure genera).

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A documented ground change occurs (C1 moves to C1) and the institution's Γ is re-fitted against the new ground at each handoff, with R per slot trending down and no new atom imported. This kills C1.
2. An institution with fresh, non-saturated, per-slot instruments and an unbroken channel still fails to register a documented change. This kills C2 (channel decay).
3. Two institutions with identical Γ and identical habitat diverge in persistence with no difference in standing or seat. This kills the agent-and-habitat reading at F6.
4. A source-discriminating instrument shows that the institution's routines still pay against C1 while the recorded form is unchanged. This kills the "spent part still present" reading at F9.
5. A genuine re-census admits a figure line that names the specific successor form rather than a shape. This closes F10.

Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union, not a single lever. F1 to F4 and F7 give two conditions; a restructuring that clears only C1 leaves C2 standing (Fig 9 :55-57).
2. Ask which guard is at zero before proposing a reform: support, reach, novelty, settlement (Formula step 3). A reform that does not pass a guard is inert.
3. Treat stability as a symptom to be checked, not a virtue to be defended (F1). The correlation between an institution and its old ground is exactly what the moved question makes visible.
4. Build a channel that can say "you have lost touch" (F3) and give it a seat whose standing does not survive the outcome (F2, Fig 10 :59).
5. Send the generator, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :22 says *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
