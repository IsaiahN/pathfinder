# Expert verification packet: why institutions persist after conditions change

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of institutional persistence can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of any real institution is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An institution is a frame with a library Γ (rules, routines, categories, a seat and an anchor). It was settled against conditions C0. When conditions move to C1, the residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| grows per slot while the form stays fixed. Persistence is a union of two conditions, not one problem and not one lever: (1) no new atom enters the institution's closure, so internal reform cannot supply what the new ground requires; (2) the channel to the ground decays, so the signal that should say "you have lost touch" is read as business as usual. Standing earned on C0 survives C0, making the occupants structurally the least able to re-fit the room. The successor, when one is owed, is a room that differs from the last and matches the residual it surfaced. The framework names that shape, not the successor, and states three refuters in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal plus institution-facing; checkable by reading Figure 10

- **Statement:** a room that stays fixed while the question moves is the named failure; the observed stability is a symptom, not evidence of fit.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :72: **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."** Triangulated at a different scale by Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
- **What to check:** is the mapping of "institution" to "room" faithful, and is the standing rule Fig 10 :61 (**"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."**) a real obstacle to re-fitting in the case you know?
- **Refuter:** a documented ground change where the institution's Γ is re-fitted at each handoff, residual per slot trends down and no new atom is imported.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8 and 6

- **Statement:** internal reform cannot add what a changed ground requires. Closure is idempotent and only contact with a differing closure moves the wall.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
- **What to check:** does the institution's reform repertoire actually stay inside its own closure, or does it import routines from a frame with a different ground? If it imports, C1 is not the binding condition in that case.
- **Refuter:** an institution that renews its repertoire purely by internal composition, with no atom from a differing closure, and whose residual then trends down against the new ground.

### C3: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 1

- **Statement:** a large part of persistence is channel failure. The ground does not decay; the instruments do. The output should be "you have lost touch," but pooled readings make it look like normal operation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** and Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
- **What to check:** are there instruments in the case you know that still return per-slot readings against the new conditions, or are the metrics saturated and pooled? Note Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
- **Refuter:** an institution with fresh, non-saturated, per-slot instruments and an unbroken channel that still fails to register a documented change in its ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that institutions are irrational, or that a collective will sustains them (Fig 11 :36: *"Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*).
- Not that culture, tradition or path dependence is a separate cause. They are absorbed into Γ, the seat and the chain.
- Not that one lever is sufficient. The problem is a union; a single lever leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :57).
- Not that the specific successor institution can be named. The framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement.
- Not an authored empirical submission. This is a simulated derivation; the AOM AI policy requires authored work for its journals.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot residual series showing whether R trends down after the conditions moved.
2. A fresh instrument that can report contact loss, or proof that all instruments are saturated and pooled.
3. A provenance record for any atom imported from a differing closure.
4. A record marking each institutional part as spent or regenerating across uses (Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :72, Fig 8 :32 and Fig 10 :24, three short passages.
2. Check C1 against one institution you know: did the room stay fixed while the question moved?
3. Check C3: is there an instrument that would report "you have lost touch," or is every reading pooled?
4. Name one observation that would refute the union. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
