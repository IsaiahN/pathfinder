## Wider literature actually read (secondary, corroboration only)

These are the low-overlap witnesses. Full bibliographic detail and quotes are in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

1. **Organizational sociology (one evidence pool).** Hannan and Freeman, *Structural Inertia and Organizational Change*, American Sociological Review 49(2), 1984. *"dampened response to environmental threats and opportunities is the price paid for reliable and accountable collective action."* Predicts that the reliability that earned the institution its standing is the same property that dampens response. Corroborates F2 and F3. DiMaggio and Powell, *The Iron Cage Revisited*, American Sociological Review 48(2), 1983, is the same pool: *"rational actors make their organizations increasingly similar as they try to change them."* It corroborates the sameness described in Fig 7 :12, not the ground verdict.
2. **Political science (moderate overlap through North).** Pierson, *Increasing Returns, Path Dependence, and the Study of Politics*, American Political Science Review 94(2), 2000. *"the factors that gave it an original advantage may have long since passed away."* Predicts that lock-in survives the disappearance of the original advantage, which is the question's own premise. Corroborates F2 and C1.
3. **Cognitive and decision science (low overlap).** Staw, *Knee-Deep in the Big Muddy*, Organizational Behavior and Human Performance 16, 1976. *"individuals actively seek to maintain or restore the appearance of rationality to a previously chosen course of action."* Predicts persistence after negative feedback when the decision maker is responsible. Corroborates F2 at the individual stratum.
4. **Ecology and resilience (low overlap).** Carpenter and Brock, *Adaptive Capacity and Traps*, Ecology and Society 13(2):40, 2008. *"In a social–ecological rigidity trap, strong self-reinforcing controls prevent the flexibility needed for adaptation."* Predicts that highly connected, inflexible systems lose adaptive capacity. Corroborates the two-condition shape at the habitat scale.
5. **Animal behaviour (low overlap).** Bolcato and Aplin, *The effect of habitat health and environmental change on cultural diversity and richness in animals*, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 380(1925), 2025. Reports the *"fragility of traditions"* and a *"time-lag effect"* called *"cultural inertia"*. Predicts that socially learned traditions persist beyond the conditions that made them adaptive. Corroborates D4 at a non-institutional stratum.

No world source was allowed to overturn a figure verdict. Where a source appeared to conflict, it was returned to the domain census and re-mapped (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4). Because most sources share an institutional-theory evidence pool, their agreement is one witness, not several.
