# Union, order and the catalyst analogue

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Two structures need a supporting derivation: (1) is persistence one problem or a union of conditions, and what follows for the order of intervention; (2) can an institution keep its form while changing kind, and what figure structure names that?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a frame with Γ, a ground, a channel, a seat, a room-chain, a library of terms. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The two candidate structures are C1 (no atom enters the closure) and C2 (the channel decays).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Fig 9 :56: a single lever *"clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

⇒ Persistence is a union of C1 and C2. A leadership change (clears part of C2) leaves C1 standing; an import of new routines (clears part of C1) leaves the decayed channel standing. The compound is categorically different from either condition alone. Triangulated (Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 3 dependency order).

### Q2: What order do the interventions have?

- Fig 3 :4-17 names the chain: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

⇒ The break in a persisting institution is normally at perception or vocabulary: the changed conditions have no per-slot instrument and no term. Effort at planning (restructuring) lands above the break. The order of work is: first an instrument that can say "you have lost touch," then a term for the new conditions, then an objective, then a plan. Reversing this is the dead-reckoning error.

### Q3: Is the persisting institution an isomer or a catalyst?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

⇒ Persistence of form is not evidence of persistence of substance. Under the catalyst reading, a part that was regenerating can become spent while remaining present, and the record does not encode which. Under the isomer reading, two institutions with the same atoms under different bonds to the changed ground are different substances, invisible in the org chart. The catalyst reading is the one that fits "persists after the conditions changed"; the isomer reading explains why two persisting institutions can behave oppositely.

### Q4: Is a refusal owed before a mint?

- Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
- Fig 5 :59: **"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"**
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: REFUTED → *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*

⇒ The first move is to refuse the term "still works" that the institution holds about its old routines. Minting a successor before that refusal is out of order (Fig 6 :42-43: the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"*). The refusal adds a term and removes the standing of the old one to be composed over.

### Q5: What is the round trip gap R_T here?

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

- **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 10 :72 (room must change), Fig 8 :32 (closure idempotent), Fig 10 :24 (channel decay), Fig 7 :36 (room must differ).
- **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "persistence is a union of closure and channel conditions."
- **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete institutional diagnosis naming which guard is at zero and which seat holds the standing.

**R_T is nonzero and qualitative, not measured.** What the shorthand drops: the per-slot indexing of R (Fig 1 :19), the standing that survives the outcome (Fig 10 :61), the catalyst-versus-spent distinction (Fig 12 :119), and the requirement that the successor be matched to the residual rather than named. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error. No ground reading exists to give R_T a number.

## FALSIFIER (written before the run)

If an institution is shown whose Γ is re-fitted at each handoff with residual per slot trending down and no import, while its channel instruments are fresh and per-slot, then the union of C1 and C2 is not necessary and the shape is refuted. A secondary falsifier: if a record can mark each institutional part as spent or regenerating across uses and shows no conversion after a ground change, the catalyst reading is refuted.
