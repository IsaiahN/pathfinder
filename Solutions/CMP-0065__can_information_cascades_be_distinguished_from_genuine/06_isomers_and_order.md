## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two hypotheses isomers of the act?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

Hold the act A fixed.

- H_c: A is joined to the world by **imitation**, with the private signal ignored. BHW 1992: *"An informational cascade occurs if an individual's action does not depend on his private information signal."*
- H_g: A is joined to the world by a **private signal or changed belief**, with imitation not decisive.

Same atoms (person, act, time, signal source available in principle), different bond, and the behavior is decisive in a specific sense: a cascade once started lasts even if it is wrong, and mass behavior is fragile to a small shock (BHW 1992). A genuine change of belief is not fragile in that way, because the new belief is carried by the person rather than by the sequence. So the two are isomers under the figure definition, and the difference is invisible in the observation of the act alone.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if downstream behavior is provably identical under the two bonds for all states of the world, for example if the act is irreversible and no later information is ever observed, then the bond carries nothing observable and the isomer claim is idle. The claim also fails if the two hypotheses cannot be written over a shared atom set at all; then they are not isomers but unrelated molecules, and the weaker reading is that they share an ingredient (the act) only.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the behavioral difference corroborated by BHW 1992 and by Celen and Kariv (2004), which are low-overlap with the figure text.

### Q2 - Is the question a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The question bundles three conditions: identification in principle, field measurement, and the definition of "genuine." A single lever, for example adding more observational data, clears the first condition's illusion of a problem while leaving the second and third standing. The residue after such a lever is categorically different: it is a measurement-and-definition compound, not a harder data problem.

**Falsifier:** a re-census showing the three conditions share one anchor and one currency, so one loop closes them. The Celen and Kariv result is evidence against this: their apparatus is needed precisely because the identification condition and the field-measurement condition have different anchors.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :19-21: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Order: perception of the act and the observation order (works in digital traces) -> vocabulary for source and belief (works in the field) -> objective, a discriminator that fires differently (works as a specification) -> planning, an instrument that reads the source (works in the lab, usually not in the field) -> learn and carry (blocked downstream of the break).

Effort spent improving the perception link, for example collecting larger adoption datasets, cannot rescue the missing source instrument. That is the figure's dependency law, not a rhetorical preference. Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** The lab apparatus is that worse instrument already returning something.

**Falsifier:** a field dataset in which private signals were recorded at the time of each decision. That would move the instrument to link 4 and dissolve this particular break.

### Q4 - What is lost in the round trip from lab to field?

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 8 :51 (contents do not separate source), Fig 2 :36-38 (person is the anchor for a question about people), Fig 9 :54 (unbundle).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01`, "you cannot tell source from act; use private belief; split the question."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim that the laboratory belief-elicitation design resolves the field question.

**R_T is nonzero.** What the shorthand holds does not survive the trip back down: the stake condition on private elicitation (Fig 10 :59), the substituted-habitat limit (Fig 11 :70), and the requirement that the two frames stay at low overlap (Fig 2 :31-32). What survives cleanly is the shape of the discriminator and the union reading. R_T is not measured numerically because no field ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the lab result settles the field question" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

### Q5 - Three analogues, and what each does and does not license

- **Color metamerism (engineering / measurement).** Kerr (Stanford): *"We can have several instances of light with different spectrums which nevertheless have the same color. In fact there is an infinity of spectrums that will have any given color."* This licenses the many-to-one claim and the remedy: a better instrument (a spectrometer) discriminates sources the eye cannot. It does **not** license naming a particular spectrometer for beliefs.
- **Fundamental Problem of Causal Inference (mathematics / statistics).** Holland 1986: *"It is impossible to observe the value of Y_t(u) and Y_c(u) on the same unit and, therefore, it is impossible to observe the effect of t on u."* This licenses the claim that one observed act cannot show both the cascade and the non-cascade counterpart for the same person. It also carries the honest correction: *"What is impossible is causal inference without making untested assumptions."* So "cannot observe both" is not "cannot infer."
- **Public compliance versus private acceptance (human cognition).** The social psychology source: *"Public compliance is a superficial change in behavior (including the public expression of opinions) that is not accompanied by an actual change in one's private opinion."* This licenses the two-slot census. Its caveat, *"it may not be possible to fully differentiate them,"* is a real conflict and is sent back to the census: the slots are kept distinct because the source at the moment of the act is still what an instrument must read, even when a person later moves from compliance to acceptance.

Fig 8 :50-52 separates the cases: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* The analogues agree at maximum independence because colorimetry, causal inference and social psychology draw on different evidence pools. The framework essay on source is excluded from this count because it shares the figures' pool.

## CONCLUSION

The two hypotheses are **isomers of the act**: same ingredient set, different bond, decisive downstream difference, invisible in the observation of the act. The question is a **union** that must be unbundled; the field chain **breaks at the source-instrument link**; and the laboratory-to-field trip carries a **nonzero R_T** that must be reported rather than assumed away. The isomer reading rescues what is real in the distinction without requiring a new primitive or a claim that the lab settles the field.
