# Solution CMP-0065 - figure-grounded package

**Question:** Can information cascades be distinguished from genuine changes in people's beliefs or preferences?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 80 (question number 65 in that file).

**Section:** 7. Epidemics, information, and social dynamics.

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive verdict and on the shape of the required instrument. `general` on the structural claim that a source cannot be read off an artifact. Nothing here is `grounded`: no experiment was run and no measurement was taken. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6). It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals and no list of people ranked by likely agreement.
- No bulk-send plan and no request for endorsement.
- No claim that the figures settle an empirical identification question by themselves. The figures decide the shape of the discriminator and the status of the evidence; the ground decides the fact.

Three figure-grounded reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A crowd's agreement is not a verdict. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A list assembled from people likely to agree manufactures a convention, not corroboration.
2. Shared evidence disqualifies agreement as a verdict. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Followers in a cascade draw on the same pool by definition.
3. Sending the full frame before asking for a check spends the reader's independence. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
