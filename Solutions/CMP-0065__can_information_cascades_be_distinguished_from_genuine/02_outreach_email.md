**Subject:** One checkable claim about telling a cascade from a real belief change

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on information cascades and social diffusion. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can an information cascade be distinguished from a genuine change in belief or preference?" is a union of three questions that are usually bundled: identification in principle, measurement from field data, and what is to count as "genuine." The figure-grounded derivation says the following.
>
> 1. From the observed act and the aggregate adoption curve alone, the two cannot be distinguished. A cascade is defined by an act that does not depend on the person's private signal; a genuine change is defined by the opposite dependence. Both produce the same act. In the framework's words, convergent derivation and adopted import *"look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*
> 2. The legitimate anchor for "genuine belief" is the person's own private judgment, because the question is constitutively about people, not about a count or a market. A crowd's agreement is not a verdict: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* It is evidence only if the agreeing people reached the act from independent private signals.
> 3. The two can be distinguished where there is a source-discriminating instrument or a provenance record. The laboratory has one: it elicits beliefs and controls private signals, which is exactly what field data lacks. That is a proof in a substituted habitat, not a field result.
>
> So the answer is **no** from behavior alone and **yes** where a source record or instrument exists. What the derivation offers is the shape such an instrument must have, not a claim that one is already deployed in the field.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular signature (fragility, excess unanimity, serial correlation) is by itself a discriminator; that the laboratory result settles the field question; that self-report is a clean anchor when it is public and stake-laden; or that a failure to discriminate means the two states are identical. Indiscernibility is not identity.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an act-level statistic whose distributions under cascade and genuine change are provably disjoint at fixed observed act;
> 2. a field instrument that reads the private source without changing the act and validates against randomised information;
> 3. private, stake-reduced elicitation that fails to track the latent belief under experimental control;
> 4. a re-census showing the three bundled questions are one question after all.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, where cited, are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Full verbatim figure text and line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The experimental discrimination result: Celen and Kariv, "Distinguishing Informational Cascades from Herd Behavior in the Laboratory," American Economic Review 94(3), 2004: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/0002828041464461
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
