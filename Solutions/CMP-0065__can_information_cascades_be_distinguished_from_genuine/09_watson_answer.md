## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether an information cascade can be distinguished from a genuine change in people's beliefs or preferences. Option A: yes, from the observed acts and aggregate patterns. Option B: no, not from the act alone; the discrimination requires a source record or a source-discriminating instrument, and where neither exists the honest report is unreached. What each leads to: A treats the artifact as if it carried its own source and will certify a cascade as a belief change (or the reverse); B keeps the two hypotheses separate, specifies what instrument would discriminate them, and stops honestly where none is available.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two hypotheses over one observed act, so a discrimination problem rather than a single quantity. Symmetry: the map from latent source to act is many-to-one, so from the act the two hypotheses are interchangeable. Scale: individual (private signal, belief, preference) -> network (who observes whom, in what order) -> aggregate (market, crowd, vote). Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The live residual sits in the unread source of a slot, not in the crowd average.
- **Domain 1, the act and its record.** Actors: individuals as slots, acts, timestamps, public signals. Currency: observed choice. Anchor: the record of the act, legitimate for *what happened* and only an instrument for *why it happened*. Fig 2 :36-38: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it. Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
- **Domain 2, the private source.** Actors: the person's private signal, belief, preference. Currency: conviction. Anchor: private, stake-reduced self-report, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people. Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
- **Domain 3, the diffusion network.** Actors: early movers, followers, observation ties, timing. Currency: social information and imitation. Anchor: an intervention that severs the tie, such as randomised private information. Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **Domain 4, the checking arrangement.** Actors: researcher, experiment, outside reader. Currency: independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Union, not partition.** Three bundled conditions: identification in principle, field measurement, definition of genuine. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception of act and order (works) -> vocabulary for source (works) -> objective, a discriminator (works as specification) -> planning, a field instrument (works in lab, usually not in field) -> learn and carry (blocked downstream). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. Overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Economics, canonical definition.** Sushil Bikhchandani, David Hirshleifer and Ivo Welch, "A Theory of Fads, Fashion, Custom, and Cultural Change as Informational Cascades," Journal of Political Economy 100(5), 1992: *"An informational cascade occurs if an individual's action does not depend on his private information signal."* Predicts that the distinguishing feature is unobservable from the act; corroborates Fig 8 :51.
- **Economics, survey.** Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer, Omer Tamuz and Welch, "Information Cascades and Social Learning," 2022: *"In an information cascade, an agent who observes others chooses the same action regardless of her own private information signal."* Predicts the same equivalence and declares open empirical questions; corroborates.
- **Experimental economics, the closest prior art.** Bogachan Celen and Shachar Kariv, "Distinguishing Informational Cascades from Herd Behavior in the Laboratory," American Economic Review 94(3), 2004: *"informational cascades, which are defined in terms of (unobservable) beliefs, are much harder to identify than herds, which are defined in terms of (observable) actions. In market settings, we observe behavior but not beliefs or private information."* Their abstract: *"By using techniques only available in the laboratory, we elicit subjects' beliefs. This allows us to distinguish informational cascades from herd behavior."* Predicts that the distinction is achievable with an instrument in a lab and absent from market data; corroborates Fig 6 :32 and Fig 11 :70.
- **Mathematics and statistics, identification limit.** Paul W. Holland, "Statistics and Causal Inference," Journal of the American Statistical Association 81(396), 1986: *"It is impossible to observe the value of Y_t(u) and Y_c(u) on the same unit and, therefore, it is impossible to observe the effect of t on u."* Also: *"What is impossible is causal inference without making untested assumptions."* Predicts that one act cannot show both counterfactual sources for one person, while leaving inference possible under assumptions; corroborates the reach framing.
- **Engineering and measurement, metamerism.** Michael Kerr, "The CIE XYZ and xyY Color Spaces," Stanford CS148, 2010: *"We can have several instances of light with different spectrums which nevertheless have the same color. In fact there is an infinity of spectrums that will have any given color."* Predicts that a many-to-one reading requires a better instrument; independent witness for the source argument.
- **Human cognition, public compliance versus private acceptance.** Principles of Social Psychology, University of Oregon open text, 2022: *"Public compliance is a superficial change in behavior (including the public expression of opinions) that is not accompanied by an actual change in one's private opinion."* Predicts the two-slot census; also supplies the caveat that the distinction may not be fully differentiable, which is recorded as a conflict and sent back to the census.
- **Animal behaviour.** Joseph S. McCormick, Thomas E. White, Eliza J. T. Middleton and Tanya Latty, "Information cascades spread adaptive and maladaptive behaviours in group-living animals," Animal Behaviour 209, 2024: *"Information cascades have been used to explain a variety of collective behaviours in nonhuman animals, including mate selection, antipredator responses and social foraging."* Predicts that transmission mechanism matters and that terminology across fields is inconsistent; corroborates the habitat-enumeration rule.
- **Framework essay, source discrimination.** Isaiah N, "The Hard Problem is About Source," adventuresinml, 2026: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* Predicts the need for a source-discriminating instrument; corroborates, but shares the figures' author and evidence pool, so it is weak corroboration (high overlap).

The one conflict (compliance versus acceptance differentiability) was not used to flip the verdict. It was re-censused: the units are the source at the moment of the act, not a permanent trait of the person. Agreement from a shared pool, such as the four framework essays and the figures, is counted as one witness.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The answer is two-part and `specified`, not `grounded`. From the act and the aggregate alone the two cannot be distinguished: they are identical in content, crowd agreement shares an evidence pool, and aggregate series hide the per-person residual. With a provenanced source or a source-discriminating instrument they can be distinguished, and the laboratory has already done so in a substituted habitat. The question is a union that must be unbundled before further search. The two are isomers of the act, so the distinction is real rather than idle. No field discriminator was run in this derivation; the remaining state is unreached, not identity and not silence.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is treating a cascade as a genuine belief change, or the reverse, on the basis of a crowd pattern. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the source question and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The two-part verdict can be revised domain by domain without discarding the derivation. A working field instrument would move D2 and D3 from unreached to settled while leaving D1 intact. A lab-to-field R_T measurement would shrink the one named strain. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A validated field instrument that reads private source at fixed act would establish the positive part; a proof of observational equivalence strong enough to move the verdict from unreached to underivable in principle would establish the negative part; a re-census showing the three bundled conditions are one would reverse the union reading; private elicitation that fails to track the manipulated private signal would reverse the anchor verdict. Prestige or a large list of agreeing researchers is not such a ground.
