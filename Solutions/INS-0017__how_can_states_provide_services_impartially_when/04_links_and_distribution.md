## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The selection condition has a known habitat fix, and it is a habitat fix.** The merit-system and spoils-system sources independently describe appointment by competitive, anonymous examination and an independent commission. Wikipedia's merit-system page: "The answers submitted by candidates must be unsigned, so as to obviate the possibility of favoritism on the part of the examiners." That is a change to the structure of contact, not an appeal to better character. It corroborates Fig 11 :39-40.
2. **The adjudication condition has a known outside frame.** The ombudsman source states the structural advantage of a complainant-facing office outside the institution, and immediately states the seat problem: success depends on the occupant and on cooperation from within. That is Fig 10 :58 and Fig 2 :31-32 in a different vocabulary. Agreement here is weak corroboration because both the framework and these sources describe the same institutional pattern; it is not two independent derivations.
3. **The measurement is the missing thing.** The WGI provides system-level perception indicators and explicitly refuses to be a definitive rating. The source essay on measurement says "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists" and that the resolution came "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." What this question needs is the case-level analogue: an instrument that flags two materially identical cases with different outcomes correlated to political connection. No such instrument was built or used in this folder. That is an honest gap, not a refusal to answer.
4. **Discretion is not removable.** Lipsky's street-level bureaucracy literature holds that human judgment is in the nature of service work. So the goal cannot be "remove discretion"; it must be "make the comparison visible and the grading external". This sharpens F9 rather than contradicting it.

None of these sources conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Where they agree, they often share an evidence pool with the question's own literature, so their agreement carries limited independent weight and is recorded as such in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the whole framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source links.
- **Fig 7 :35** "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A mass send selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is a state allocating services on connection rather than merit, which is high, so seek genuinely independent readers even though they are harder to reach.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuters. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement. Record the verdict before showing any theory (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
2. **Stage 2, opt in only.** If the reader is curious, offer the three source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they hold the framework they share its evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator. Each reader must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.
