## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the virtue of individual officials but whether the allocation of public services stays independent of political connection. In effect terms: an administration maps each citizen case (slot s) to a service decision. The residual is R_s = |predicted allocation on the merits - observed allocation|, measured per case slot, not averaged across citizens (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."). The question says the official's career is coupled to a political network, so the currency that drives the decision is advancement, not the merits of the case.

- Option A, "impartiality is a property of the official's character": leads to the prediction that hiring virtuous people and training them is sufficient, and that failure is a personnel problem.
- Option B, "impartiality is a property of agent-and-habitat": leads to the prediction that the reward and oversight structure must change, and that agent-only change leaves the residual where it is (Fig 11 :39-40).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming (i) the condition under which the grading standard does not move when the political network pushes on it, and (ii) an instrument that discriminates a failure of the selection condition (who gets into office) from a failure of the adjudication condition (how cases are decided). Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of public administration.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." and Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
