## 0. The question under audit, stated in effect terms

> How can states provide services impartially when public officials depend on political networks for employment or promotion?
>
> Source: institutional questions.txt line 22. Section 2, State capacity and public administration.

An administration is a loop: perceive a citizen case, predict a decision on the merits, act, observe the outcome, compute R_s per case slot. The question names a specific coupling: the official's employment and promotion depend on a political network. In the figures' vocabulary that makes the official an agent whose currency (career) is paid by a frame whose closure differs from the frame that should grade the decision (the merits of the case).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"):

> A **shape** with two parts: (i) a condition under which the grading anchor does not move when the political network pushes on it, and (ii) an instrument that separates a failure of selection (the wrong people occupy office) from a failure of adjudication (the right people decide under a captured reward structure). The particular institutional design that realises this shape is not named by the figures.

Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, character first.** Impartiality is a property of the official. Hire good people, train them, publish a code. Leads to the prediction that failure is a personnel defect and that fixing the person fixes the allocation.
- **Option B, habitat first.** Impartiality is a property of agent-and-habitat. Separate the actor whose career is at stake from the ground that grades their decisions, and change the habitat. Leads to the prediction that agent-only change leaves R_s where it is.

The finding of this audit is Option B in `specified` mode.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain; it is a cavity in the system.

- **R, the residual.** Arity: at least two conditions, selection and adjudication, so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). Symmetry: impartiality is a *comparison* between like cases, not a quantity held by one case (Operators table :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities"). Scale: case-level at the street, system-level at the career and reward structure. The loop closes within one scale and crosses the membrane as a method, never as a recording (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, street-level adjudication.** Actors: officials and citizens. Currency: service decisions and discretion. Anchor: the merits of the case, which must be a reading that does not move when a frame pushes on it. Legitimate because the question is constitutively about the treatment of cases (Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it.").
- **Domain 2, career and appointment.** Actors: officials, political networks, appointing authorities. Currency: employment and promotion. Anchor: **none as described**. When promotion is decided by network loyalty, the "standard" moves when pushed, so by Fig 2 :29-30 this is not a valid anchor and Domain 2 is a cavity. It becomes a checkable domain only when an imported atom supplies a non-updating standard (an independent merit authority with a published, violable rule). This is the most important structural result of the census: the question itself identifies the missing anchor.
- **Domain 3, verification and oversight.** Actors: ombudsman, courts, audit office, legislature, press, affected citizens. Currency: complaints, findings, corrections. Anchor: case-level outcome data, never agreement among the actors. Independence between these frames is a quantity to be bought, not assumed (Fig 2 :31-32).

**Partition or union:** union. Selection and adjudication overlap; a case decided by a well-selected official under a captured reward structure sits in the intersection. Merit reform clears the selection-only region and oversight clears the adjudication-only region, and neither clears the intersection by itself (Fig 9 :54-58).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** 1 perception: can the agency see cases and outcomes at all? 2 vocabulary: does it have terms for "like case", "irrelevant feature", "merit"? 3 objective: can it say what progress is, namely R_s shrinking per slot? 4 planning: a route that separates selection from adjudication and buys independent frames. 5 learn and carry: name what worked, settle it against the ground, promote the method. The chain breaks most often at 2 to 3, which has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious instrument (Fig 3 :24-27).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The stake in the reading disqualifies the official as maintainer (decisive)

Fig 10 :86 "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."
Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
Fig 10 :58 "A seat with no stake at all does not exist."
Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."
Fig 10 :61 "standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."
Fig 10 :93 "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

An official whose employment and promotion depend on a political network is measured by a reading in which the network has an interest. That official cannot own the standard of impartial service. This is not a claim that officials are corrupt; it is a structural claim about the seat. Fig 10 :58 removes the idealisation: a seat with no stake at all does not exist, so the remedy is not to find a saint but to declare the stake and move the grading outside the slice.

**Triangulated** across Fig 10 :86 and :59 (low overlap: the seat rule and the interest rule are separate panels). World corroboration: the UK Civil Service Code requires officials to serve "governments of different political persuasions" and routes unresolved concerns to the Civil Service Commission, which is outside the employing department.

**Refuter:** show an official who owns the grading standard, is themselves graded by that standard, and does not degrade it over repeated cases. Under Fig 10 :86 that is a contradiction in terms; a demonstration would refute the seat rule.

### F2 - If the standard moves when the network pushes, it is not an anchor

Fig 2 :25 "pinned: it does not update on A or B"
Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :29-30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

The failure the question describes is Fig 2 collapse 1: "no anchor; each updates on the others", and "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". It is also collapse 2 when every official draws on one network's evidence: "one evidence pool", "they never talk, and still agree", so "the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other".

The anchor for impartial service cannot be a statement of values that the network may reinterpret. It must be a case-level reading that does not move when the network pushes: comparable cases receiving comparable treatment, recorded and auditable. The WGI dataset is a *candidate* system-level proxy (Government Effectiveness, Rule of Law), and its own usage advisory says scores "should be treated as estimates rather than definitive ratings of government performance", so it is an instrument to be improved, not a final ground.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 :29-35 and :13-21. **Refuter:** show a standard that the network can rewrite and that still settles like cases alike across repeated trials. If it can be rewritten, it fails Fig 2 :33; if it still settles correctly, the metalaw is wrong.

### F3 - Independence must be bought from low-overlap frames

Fig 2 :31-32 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 2 :31-32 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

A bureaucracy staffed by people who all depend on one network is one frame wearing many names. Adding more such officials raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor. The ombudsman, courts, audit office, legislature, press and affected citizens are separate frames only if they do not draw on the network's evidence pool. The world source on ombudsmen states the advantage precisely: they "examine complaints from outside the offending state institution, thus avoiding the conflicts of interest inherent in self-policing." The same source records the limit: the institution "relies heavily on the selection of an appropriate individual for the office", which is exactly Fig 10's seat problem one level up.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 7. **Refuter:** show two oversight bodies whose agreement is worth as much after they have been given the same dossier as before, or show that a chain of like-minded reviewers adds evidence rather than spelling.

### F4 - Change contact, not the agent in isolation

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"
Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :72 "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim"
Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

This is the load-bearing derive-first move. The question is phrased as a personnel problem ("officials depend on political networks"), so the tempting answer is to improve the person. Fig 11 forbids stopping there: impartiality is a property of the agent-and-habitat. The habitat is the appointment rule, the promotion rule, the case-assignment rule, the publication of decisions, and the oversight channel. Ethics training that leaves all of these unchanged improves the agent in isolation and changes no contact.

Fig 11 :72 is the standing refuter, written into the figure itself. It is adopted here as the pre-registered refuter R1 in section 5.

**Triangulated** across Fig 11 :39-40 and Fig 10 :19. **Refuter:** any agent-only improvement that moves the case-level ground. If one is demonstrated, the capability claim falls and with it the habitat-first verdict.

### F5 - A declared meaning is not a grounded one

Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"
Fig 10 :19 "install what can be violated"
Fig 10 :16-17 "the convention does not fix the bug it makes the bug statable"
Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :48 "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."

This finding is a correction to a first reading, recorded here rather than erased. My initial draft treated the Civil Service Code as evidence that impartiality can be legislated by declaring values. The figures separate declaration from grounding. The UK code is a convention: it defines "impartiality is acting solely according to the merits of the case and serving equally well governments of different political persuasions" and prohibits acting "in a way that is determined by party political considerations". Valuable as it is, that is a declared meaning. What makes it more than a slogan is the violable part: the code's own escalation route to the Civil Service Commission and its whistleblowing provisions. That is the installed, checkable mechanism. Fig 10 :16-17 is careful: the convention does not fix the bug, it makes the bug statable. A code is a precondition for correction, not the correction.

**Triangulated** across Fig 10 :16-19 and the world source (gov.uk, 2015). **Refuter:** show a code of conduct that, with no change to incentives, oversight, or case assignment, moves the case-level impartiality ground. If it does, declared meanings can ground outcomes and this finding is withdrawn.

### F6 - The chain breaks at vocabulary and objective, where there is no instrument

Fig 3 :24 "Why the middle link is where chains usually break."
Fig 3 :26-27 "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Agencies can perceive applications (link 1) and can plan sanctions (link 4). What they often lack is a term and an instrument for "two materially identical cases received different outcomes, and the difference tracks political connection." Without that term, R is not seen per slot. Aggregate satisfaction scores make it disappear: a live signal averaged into a mean. This is a diagnosis of why the question stays open, not a claim that every agency lacks the instrument.

**Triangulated** across Fig 3 and Fig 1. **Refuter:** show an agency whose aggregate metrics reliably locate the discriminated slot without a per-slot instrument, or show that the middle link has an obvious instrument and obvious test.

### F7 - Unbundle before searching: this is a union compound

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

The question bundles at least two conditions: (S) who is selected into and retained in office, and (A) how cases are decided under the live reward structure. A merit-exam reform acts on S and leaves A standing; an ombudsman acts on A and leaves S standing. Their intersection, a connected appointee deciding a case under a network-coupled career incentive, is a compound with its own loop. The Library of Babel essay names the same move: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

**Single-source for the figure rule** (Fig 9 only, framework-internal), with world corroboration from two independent literatures that arrived at the same split: the spoils/merit history (selection) and the ombudsman/oversight literature (adjudication). These two literatures have low overlap with each other, so their agreement is weak corroboration rather than an evidence pool. **Refuter:** show a single lever that clears both S and A including their intersection, with measured per-slot R falling in all three regions.

### F8 - Import from a frame whose closure differs, debited against independence

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."
Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 8 :46 "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."
Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."
Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

A captured administration cannot derive its own correction from inside its closure; composition inside a closure never adds an atom. The remedy is an import from a frame whose closure differs, and Fig 6 :24 prices it: the import is debited against the importing frame's independence, and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives. This is why the order matters. Describe R in effect terms first (which slots deviate, which condition binds), then find the frame. Picking a fashionable reform first guarantees that some feature of the question will seem to fit it.

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A reform copied as a recording, for example transcribing another country's civil service statute without the method that produced it, will not transfer; the transform loses information and the round trip must be measured.

**Triangulated** across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 9. **Refuter:** show a system that escaped its own closure by internal composition alone, or show that an import carries no cost to independence.

### F9 - Impartiality is a comparison, and the operand type is strained

Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
Operators table :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"
Operators table :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities"

Impartiality is "treat like cases alike": a comparison (⋛) between two cases on the relevant dimensions while ignoring irrelevant ones. A vocabulary holding only headcount, budget and throughput can name a state of the agency and cannot state the goal. Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two agencies with the same staff and budget are different substances if the arrangement of appointment, assignment and oversight differs.

**Strain, named not hidden.** The Operators table restricts ⋛ to *two quantities*, whereas comparing two cases compares two structured objects. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." permits a settled case to serve as an atom at the next level, and level-relativity may then make the operand a quantity at that level. Whether it does needs an instrument, not an argument. This strain is carried into `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

**Single-source** (Operators and Fig 12 are one framework). **Refuter:** show that the goal of impartial treatment can be stated in an ingredient-only vocabulary, or show that ⋛'s two-quantity restriction cannot be relaxed at any level.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written here before the conclusion, per Fig 10's discipline that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1, the capability refuter.** An agent-only improvement (selecting more virtuous officials, ethics training, or a values code) with no change to appointment, promotion, case assignment, publication, or oversight contact moves the case-level impartiality ground. This is Fig 11 :72's own standing refuter.
2. **R2, the anchor refuter.** A grading standard that the political network can rewrite still settles like cases alike across repeated trials. This breaks Fig 2 :29-33.
3. **R3, the union refuter.** A single lever clears both the selection and adjudication conditions, including their intersection, with measured per-slot R falling in all three regions. This breaks Fig 9 :54-58.
4. **R4, the seat refuter.** An official who is graded by a reading can also own that reading, over repeated cases, without degrading it. This breaks Fig 10 :86.
5. **R5, the operand refuter.** The comparison goal of impartial treatment is fully statable in an ingredient-only vocabulary, or ⋛'s two-quantity restriction admits structured cases at every level with no demotion. This breaks F9.

If any fires, the affected claim is withdrawn and the census is rerun. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape, not with a reform list. State R1 to R5 before any theory.
2. Unbundle selection from adjudication; run two loops and keep their verdicts separate (F7).
3. Treat the impartiality standard as an anchor that must not update; use case-level outcome data, not aggregate satisfaction, as the reading (F2, F6).
4. Move the grading outside the slice being graded and declare the remaining stake (F1).
5. Buy independence from low-overlap frames and price the import (F3, F8).
6. Do not present a code of ethics as the fix. It makes the failure statable; it does not ground the correction (F5).
7. Keep the ⋛ operand-type strain visible (F9).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is this one hard question?** No. Union compound, selection plus adjudication (Fig 9 :55).
2. **What is the residual?** Per-case deviation from treatment on the merits, hidden by averaging (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. **Who may grade it?** Not the official whose career is in the slice; an outside seat, stake declared (Fig 10 :86, :60).
4. **What must the standard be?** An anchor that does not update when pushed (Fig 2 :29-33).
5. **Where does the correction come from?** An import from a low-overlap frame, debited against independence (Fig 6 :23-24, Fig 8 :45-46).
6. **Agent or habitat?** Habitat. Agent-only change cannot move the ground (Fig 11 :39-40).
7. **What stops the loop?** An honest stop at the instrument limit: a per-case instrument that discriminates connected from unconnected treatment is still owed. No such measurement was taken here, so status is `candidate`, not `grounded`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a non-updating anchor, a grading seat outside the slice, independence bought from low-overlap frames, habitat change rather than agent-only change, and selection unbundled from adjudication. Specific institutional designs do not follow from the figures and are imports to be provenanced. Strains after re-census: one, the ⋛ operand type, named and carried forward. No figure conflict was found; the earlier apparent strains were paraphrase errors and were retracted above.
