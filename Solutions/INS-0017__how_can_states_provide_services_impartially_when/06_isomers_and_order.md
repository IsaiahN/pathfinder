# Union, comparison and order: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question names one failure, "services are not impartial when officials depend on political networks". The residual may be a union of conditions, and the remedy may be sensitive to order. Three questions are owed:

1. Is the failure a partition (one condition) or a union (overlapping conditions)?
2. What kind of relation is impartiality: a quantity, a bond, or a comparison?
3. What is the dependency order of a repair, and what are the competing explanations at each link?

Atoms held for this derivation: the two conditions S (selection into and retention in office) and A (adjudication under the live reward structure); the anchor (case-level comparable treatment); the outside grading seat; independent frames; the habitat (appointment, promotion, assignment, publication, oversight). Bonds held: sequence →, comparison ⋛, negation ¬ (refusal), subtraction −. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- Conditions: S selects who serves; A governs how a case is decided given the reward structure. A well-selected official under a network-coupled career incentive is in the intersection.
- If it were a partition, one lever would clear everything and the regions would be independent. They are not: merit reform leaves A, oversight leaves S.
- ⇒ **Union**. Two loops, kept separate, plus an explicit loop for the intersection. Triangulated for the shape by *The Library of Babel*: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" Single-source for the figure rule itself (Fig 9).
- **Falsifier F-Q1:** a single intervention that lowers per-slot R in S-only, A-only and intersection cases, with each region measured separately, falsifies the union reading.

### Q2 - What kind of relation is impartiality?

- Operators table :48: comparison is "a threshold relation between two quantities"; test: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- Impartiality is not a stock an agency holds. It is a relation between like cases: treat equivalent cases equivalently, ignore the irrelevant feature (political connection). An ingredient-only vocabulary (headcount, budget, throughput) can state a state of the agency and cannot state this goal.
- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two agencies with identical staff and budget are different substances when the arrangement of appointment, assignment and oversight differs. In that precise sense the merit-system design and the ombudsman design are **not** isomers of one another: they share almost no atoms and act on different conditions. They are complementary imports, which is why their agreement is not corroboration.
- **Strain, held open.** ⋛ is defined for two quantities; comparing two cases compares structured objects. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." permits demotion to atom at a level and may make the operands quantities there. Whether it does needs an instrument. This is not smoothed over.
- **Falsifier F-Q2:** state the goal of impartial treatment completely with headcount, budget and throughput alone, or show that ⋛ accepts structured cases at every level without demotion. Either falsifies this finding.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order, and where does it break?

Fig 3 is ordered by dependency, not importance. The repair chain:

| link | what it requires | failure if absent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | case-level records of decisions and outcomes exist | R cannot be seen at all |
| 2 vocabulary | terms for "like case", "irrelevant feature", "merit" exist | the middle link, no obvious instrument |
| 3 objective | progress is defined as per-slot R shrinking, not average satisfaction rising | effort lands on the wrong quantity |
| 4 planning | a route that separates S from A and buys independent frames | levers applied to the wrong condition |
| 5 learn and carry | name what worked, settle against the ground, promote the method | no method crosses the membrane (Fig 4 :22) |

- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- The likely break is 2 to 3. Most reform effort lands above the break, at perception (publish data) or planning (create a body), because those have visible tests (Fig 3 :29-30). The middle link, defining what counts as a like case and an irrelevant feature, attracts the fewest instruments.
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." More oversight cannot rescue an agency with no term for the deviation it is meant to detect.
- **Falsifier F-Q3:** show an agency whose repair succeeded while link 2 remained uninstrumented, with the residual measured per case before and after.

### Q4 - Competing explanations, kept separate

Four explanations are on the table. They are not averaged.

| explanation | what it predicts | figure bearing | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 character: hire and train better people | agent-only change moves per-slot R | refuted by Fig 11 :40 unless R1 fires | candidate, standing refuter live |
| E2 selection: change who enters and stays | S-only regions improve; intersection stands | Fig 9 :55-58, merit-system import | partial |
| E3 oversight: change who grades | A-only regions improve; intersection stands | Fig 10 :86, ombudsman import | partial |
| E4 habitat: change appointment, promotion, assignment, publication and oversight together | all three regions can improve, if the anchor does not update | Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 2 :29-33 | the shape, `specified` |

- E1 is not dismissed as morally false; it is structurally insufficient unless Fig 11 :72's refuter fires. That is why R1 is written before the conclusion.
- E2 and E3 are real but partial. Their imports carry a cost to independence (Fig 6 :24) and must be provenanced (Fig 8 :50-52).
- E4 is the synthesized shape. It is a shape, not a design: the figures do not say which appointment rule, which oversight body, or which data instrument.

### Q5 - Order of refusal before mint

Formula step 2 sorts a gap before minting a term. The "impartiality = virtue" term should be routed to REFUTED rather than merely amended: the claim that agent-only change is sufficient is false under Fig 11 unless R1 fires. A refusal adds a term (¬) and removes nothing; the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :41-43). The correction is therefore not "add ethics training to the habitat list" but "refuse the sufficiency of agent-only change, then mint the habitat route".

## CONCLUSION

The failure is a union of selection and adjudication, so the repair must be two loops plus one for the intersection. Impartiality is a comparison goal, not a quantity, and the ⋛ operand type is a named strain. The repair chain breaks most plausibly at the vocabulary/objective link, where no instrument exists. Of four competing explanations, agent-only change is structurally insufficient, selection-only and oversight-only are partial, and the habitat reading is the figure-grounded shape. All of this is `specified` and unmeasured.
