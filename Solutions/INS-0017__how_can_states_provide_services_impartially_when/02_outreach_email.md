**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0 and the refuters R1 to R5 are stated cleanly. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output cannot survive checking.

**Subject:** Check one claim about impartial public service before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about public administration. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** if a public service is to be impartial, the standard that grades a case cannot be a statement the political network is free to reinterpret. It has to be a reading that does not move when someone pushes on it, in practice a case-level record of comparable cases receiving comparable treatment. The official whose employment and promotion depend on that network is measured by the reading, so that official cannot also own it. From this it follows that changing the person alone, by hiring well or by publishing a code of values, does not change the allocation unless the appointment, promotion, case-assignment, publication or oversight contact changes as well.
>
> This is a prediction about **shape**, not a measurement. The figures return the conditions any candidate answer must satisfy, not the name of a particular commission, exam or ombudsman model.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any existing agency is corrupt, that a particular country's civil service is impartial or not, or that one institutional design settles the question. Those are bindings to be provenanced and checked against case-level data. I am also not claiming the framework is the only true account of public administration.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before writing the rest:
>
> 1. An agent-only change (better people, training, a values code) with no change to appointment, promotion, assignment, publication or oversight moves the case-level impartiality ground.
> 2. A standard the political network can rewrite still settles like cases alike over repeated trials.
> 3. One lever clears both the selection problem and the adjudication problem, including their intersection, with per-case deviation falling in all three regions.
> 4. An official who is graded by a reading can also own that reading over repeated cases without degrading it.
> 5. The goal of impartial treatment is fully statable with only headcount, budget and throughput, with no comparison between cases.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, public branch (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The full architecture essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a public-service problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
