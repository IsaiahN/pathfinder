# Expert verification packet - the Tether on impartial public service

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public administration researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No case-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

If public services are to be impartial, the standard that grades a case must be an anchor that does not move when a political network pushes on it, and the official whose employment and promotion depend on that network cannot own the reading that grades their decisions. Independence must be bought from frames that do not share the network's evidence, and the reward and oversight habitat must change, because changing the person alone changes no contact and no allocation. Selection into office and adjudication of cases are two conditions, so they must be unbundled before repair. The framework returns this as a shape, not as a named commission, exam or ombudsman model. What would close it is a case-level comparator and the five refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the grading standard must not update on what a frame thinks, and the person graded by a reading cannot own it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :29-30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 10 :86 "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it." Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
- **Refuter:** R2, show a standard the network can rewrite that still settles like cases alike over repeated trials; R4, show an official who owns their grading reading over repeated cases without degrading it.

### C2 - framework-internal derivation with a habitat-facing check

- **Statement:** agent-only change cannot move the ground; the habitat (appointment, promotion, case assignment, publication, oversight) is the lever, and a declared code makes the failure statable but does not ground the correction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" and Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"; Fig 10 :16-17 "the convention does not fix the bug it makes the bug statable".
- **What to check against the field:** the UK Civil Service Code (gov.uk, 2015) defines impartiality and forbids party-political determination, yet routes unresolved concerns to a separate Civil Service Commission. Does the code work because of the declaration, or because of the violable escalation route? The figure predicts the latter.
- **Refuter:** R1, an agent-only improvement with no change to appointment, promotion, assignment, publication or oversight that moves the case-level ground. This is the figure's own standing refuter, Fig 11 :72 "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim".

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, union and comparison

- **Statement:** the failure is a union of selection and adjudication, and impartiality is a comparison goal, so one lever and an ingredient-only vocabulary are insufficient.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Operators table :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one".
- **What to check:** can the goal of impartial treatment be stated completely with headcount, budget and throughput alone? Is there an intervention that lowers per-case deviation in selection-only, adjudication-only and intersection cases simultaneously?
- **Refuter:** R3, a single lever that clears both conditions including the intersection; R5, a complete statement of the goal in an ingredient-only vocabulary.
- **Open strain:** the Operators table restricts ⋛ to "a threshold relation between two quantities", while comparing two cases compares structured objects. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." may permit demotion at a level. Whether it does needs an instrument, not an argument. This is named, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a finding that any country, agency or individual is corrupt or impartial. No ground reading was taken.
- Not that virtue is irrelevant, only that agent-only change is insufficient unless R1 fires.
- Not that a code of ethics is useless. It makes the failure statable; it is not the correction.
- Not that one lever fixes selection and adjudication together.
- Not a specific institutional design. A commission, exam, ombudsman or indicator is an import requiring provenance and a shadow test.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by case-level data.

## 5. What would settle it

A case-level comparator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; it was "the arrival of a better instrument" that resolved the case.

1. For materially identical case pairs, does outcome differ with political connection after relevant features are held constant?
2. Can the published standard be rewritten by the network? If yes, it is not an anchor.
3. Is the grader outside the slice being graded, with the stake declared?
4. Does an agent-only change move the comparator? That is R1.
5. Does one lever clear S, A and the intersection? That is R3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-35 and Fig 10 :16-19, :58-61, :86, :93. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the Civil Service Code: is the declaration doing the work, or the escalation route? Name one case where a code alone changed an allocation.
3. Name one instrument that would flag two like cases with different outcomes correlated to political connection, or say why none is possible at current resolution.
4. Pick one of S or A that your own work bears on, and say whether a single lever could clear both.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
