# Isomers, order and the commitment-device analogue

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the union finding.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether one institution produces one result (improve outcomes) or the opposite result (weaken accountability). The derivation must decide whether these are two spellings of one term, two different terms, or a compound with its own loop, and in what order they must be checked.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (restated from `01`)

- Atoms in the field's vocabulary: independence, mandate, inflation, output, electorate, legitimacy, transparency, oversight, appointment/removal rules. No new primitive is introduced.
- Bonds available: the framework's seven plus negation. The relevant ones here are `+` (both hold), `⋛` (comparison, a threshold relation between two quantities), `→` (sequence) and `¬` (refusal of a claim).
- Anchors: economic data (D1), human judgment (D2), the ground never agreement (D3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :4: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- A **partition** would mean the two effects never overlap: some countries have the outcome effect, others the accountability effect, and one lever clears each region. A **union** means the same institution sits in both conditions, so a lever that clears the outcome condition leaves the accountability condition standing.
- Central bank independence is in both conditions at once. Therefore **union**, and the compound has its own loop and its own ground.
- **Falsifier (written before the run):** exhibit a country or design in which the outcome effect is present and the accountability condition is absent, with a measured anchor for each, so the two regions are disjoint. Then it is a partition and the union finding is withdrawn.

### Q2 - Are the two effects isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Written narrowly, the two effects do not share the same atoms: D1 uses inflation/output data and a monetary authority; D2 uses an electorate, elected officials and legitimacy. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are a functional group (Fig 12 :124: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*) of "institutional arrangement with an anchor."
- Written over the full shared institutional set (mandate, appointment rules, reporting, independence statute), differing only in which bond connects the institution to which anchor, they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the stronger, checkable claim.
- **Falsifier:** if either effect can be written using atoms the other does not require at all, and no shared arrangement can be shown, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Ordered chain for this question:
  1. **Which anchor is legitimate for which question?** (objective link). This is where a reading below the break would be a reading of nothing. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
  2. **D1 outcome claim**, ranged against economic data.
  3. **D2 accountability claim**, ranged against human judgment.
  4. **D3 design seam**, which consumes both: Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* and Fig 10 :29 *"keep the anchor reachable."*
- Effort spent on step 2 cannot rescue a broken step 1. A better inflation study does not tell you which anchor is legitimate for accountability.

### Q4 - The commitment-device analogue (low-overlap field: behavioural economics / cognition)

- World source opened 2026-11-23: *"A commitment device is a mechanism or arrangement that implements precommitment by deliberately restricting one's own future options in order to make a commitment credible."*
- The same source records Schelling's strategic commitment, Strotz-style time inconsistency, and the split between *"immutable"* and *"mutable"* devices, with field evidence that voluntary take-up is low and defaults occur.
- Figure match: Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* A commitment device is exactly an arrangement that makes a future self unable to update on a reversed preference. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* matches the device literature's point that a costly, credible constraint is bought when the cost of the alternative is high.
- **What the analogue corroborates:** the *shape* of D1 (insulation as a deliberately non-updating arrangement).
- **What it also warns:** a mutable device (one that can be quietly reversed) is close in vocabulary but different in substance. This is Fig 12 :117 again, and it is why a formal statute and actual independence are not the same term.
- **Falsifier:** if commitment devices in the field evidence never restrict future options in practice (all are mutable in effect), the analogue loses its force. The source reports the opposite, so it stands as corroboration, not as a witness for the verdict.

### Q5 - The independent-director analogue (low-overlap field: business governance)

- World source opened 2026-11-23: *"Some researchers have complained that firms have appointed 'independent directors who are overly sympathetic to management, while still technically independent according to regulatory definitions.'"*
- The same source records the legal definition of independence as the absence of a material relationship, not the absence of influence.
- Figure match: Fig 8 :50-51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*; Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* A board that is independent on paper but shares the cognitive pool of management is the common-cause collapse of Fig 2, arrived at in a different field.
- **Falsifier:** if formal independence criteria perfectly predicted actual independence of judgment in every case, the warning would be void. The source says the criteria can be met while sympathy to management remains, so it stands.

### Q6 - What order does the union imply for the answer?

- Fig 8 :53 *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- So: describe both effects in effect terms before choosing which literature to trust. This solution did that in `01` §0-1 before opening the world sources in `04` and `09`, so the economics frame did not pick the residual; the residual picked the frame.

## CONCLUSION

The two effects are a **union compound**, not one question and not a partition. Over the narrow atom sets they are a functional group; over the full shared institutional set they are a genuine Fig 12 isomer claim. The dependency order is anchor legitimacy first, D1 second, D2 third, D3 last. Two low-overlap analogues (commitment devices, independent directors) corroborate the *shape* of the non-updating anchor and the formal-versus-actual distinction, and neither is allowed to decide the verdict. Both have stated falsifiers.
