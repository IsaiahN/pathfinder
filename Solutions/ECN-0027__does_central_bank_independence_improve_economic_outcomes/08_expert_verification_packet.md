# Expert verification packet - the Tether on central-bank independence

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist or political scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the union-compound finding, `specified` on the per-domain claims. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the level of the whole question is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does central-bank independence improve economic outcomes, or weaken democratic accountability?" is a union compound, not one question with one answer. The two effects have different anchors: economic data for outcomes, human judgment for legitimacy. So there are two verdicts, never averaged. In the outcomes domain, independence is an attempt to install an anchor that does not update on short-term political pressure; that is a shape, and whether outcomes actually improve is a ground question. In the accountability domain, the anchor is constitutively human, so an institution pinned against the political frame cannot be corrected by it; the structural cost is real, its weight is a judgment. The reconciliation is a design seam that keeps the channel to the ground reachable while the anchor does not update. Formal independence is not actual independence.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1
- **Statement:** either-or is the wrong form; the compound must be split and the verdicts kept separate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*; Fig 9 :54 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*; Fig 9 :57 *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*; Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit one anchor that settles both effects on one metric, or a clean partition in which the outcome effect and the accountability effect never co-occur, with a measured anchor for each. Either breaks the union finding.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against the inflation and output literature
- **Statement:** independence is a non-updating anchor shape, and the outcome claim is narrower than "improves economic outcomes."
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."*; Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*; Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*; Formula step 5 *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
- **What to check:** does Alesina and Summers's reported result support an inflation association and *"little evidence"* for growth, unemployment and real rates? If so, "improves outcomes" is not one effect and cannot be treated as one verdict.
- **Refuter:** data showing no systematic outcome difference between independent and dependent banks under comparable mandates; or evidence that the institution updates on political pressure, in which case it is not an anchor in this sense.

### C3 - politics-facing; checkable against the legitimacy literature
- **Statement:** the legitimacy anchor is human, so insulation has a structural correction cost, and the design seam must keep the channel reachable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*; Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*; Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :19 *"install what can be violated."*
- **What to check:** Fernández-Albertos (2015) argues central banks *"are unavoidably political institutions"*; the IMF says independence and accountability are two sides of one coin bridged by transparency; Dincer et al. find transparency helps and regulatory complexity hurts. Do these three instruments support the seam reading rather than a single verdict?
- **Refuter:** evidence that transparency and oversight fully restore democratic correction without making the anchor update on transient politics; or a demonstration that the accountability domain has no anchor constitutively about people.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that independence always lowers inflation, or improves all outcomes.
- Not that democratic accountability necessarily worsens on balance.
- Not that there is a single optimal degree of independence.
- Not that economists' consensus is corroboration (Fig 2 :39-40, Fig 7 :13).
- Not that a formal statute is the same as actual independence (Fig 12 :117, and the independent-director analogue).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A matched-design ground reading of inflation and output under comparable mandates, with source discrimination so the cause is not read off a single index.
2. A measure of actual democratic correction (oversight, removal, mandate change, reporting quality) ranged against the electorate's judgment.
3. A demonstration that a single anchor settles both effects; or that the two effects partition cleanly.
4. A demonstration that the institution updates on political pressure, which would retire the anchor framing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4, :54, :57 and Fig 2 :29, :33, :37 (seven short passages).
2. Check C2 against the Alesina-Summers abstract: is the result inflation-specific?
