**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union-compound finding (F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`) and the two separate domain verdicts are intact in the text. The offer collapses on contact if the two effects are averaged back into one score.

**Subject:** A one-question check on the central-bank-independence trade-off

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the domain claims, not `grounded`):** the question "does central-bank independence improve economic outcomes, or weaken democratic accountability?" bundles two effects with different anchors. "Improves outcomes" is a measurement against economic data. "Weakens accountability" is a judgment about a legitimacy relation between citizens and an institution. A single verdict would require one anchor that settles both. The derivation finds two anchors, so this is a union compound, not one hard question. The two verdicts have to be kept separate and never averaged.
>
> In the outcomes domain, independence is an attempt to install an anchor that does not update on short-term political pressure. That is a structural shape, not a causal result. Whether inflation or output actually improve is a ground question, and the derivation stops there.
>
> In the accountability domain, the legitimate anchor is constitutively human. An institution pinned so that it does not update on the political frame cannot, by construction, be corrected by that frame. That is a real structural cost. Whether it is a net loss depends on compensating mechanisms such as transparency, reporting and review, and on what the community counts as legitimate.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that independence always lowers inflation; that all economic outcomes improve; that democratic accountability necessarily worsens on balance; or that there is a single optimum degree of independence. The framework returns a shape, not a verdict, and these empirical balances need a ground, not an argument.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. A single anchor that settles both effects on one metric, so the compound finding is false.
> 2. Data showing no systematic outcome difference between independent and dependent banks under comparable mandates.
> 3. Evidence that transparency and oversight fully restore democratic correction without making the monetary anchor update on transient politics.
> 4. Evidence that the institution in question does update on political pressure, in which case it is not an anchor in this sense.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the relevant claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The economics literature this was checked against: Alesina and Summers (1993), Dincer, Eichengreen and Martinez (2024), and Fernández-Albertos (2015); links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
