## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question is a single either-or sentence, but it names two different effects and two different anchors:

- **Effect A:** an institutional arrangement (insulating monetary policy from elected government) is claimed to change measurable economic outcomes.
- **Effect B:** the same arrangement is claimed to change a legitimacy relation (who may correct whom, and on whose authority, in a democracy).

**R (the residual)** is the gap between the question's either-or framing and the two loops it actually describes. Arity: two conditions. Symmetry: not symmetric, one is a measurement against economic data, the other is a judgment about democratic legitimacy. Scale: institutional and policy level, spanning a technocratic frame and a democratic frame.

Before choosing any figure line, the domain census. No verdict yet.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, so "A or B" is malformed (decisive, structural)

Fig 9 :4: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :57: *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
The seat map (:211) states the same law: *"And when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before searching again."*

The two effects share an institution but not an anchor. A lever that clears the outcome condition (insulation) leaves the accountability condition standing, because the second condition still binds. So the verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Status:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and the seat map (two instruments in the same framework, low overlap in phrasing, high overlap in evidence pool) and, in the world, by *The Library of Babel* essay: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is a structural finding about the *shape* of the question, not an empirical result.

**Refuter:** if a single anchor can settle both improvement and accountability on one metric, so that the two effects share a ground, this finding fails. That would require demonstrating that democratic legitimacy is a subset of economic outcomes, which no source opened here does.

### F2 - In D1, independence is an attempt to install a non-updating anchor (shape only)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Central bank independence, in effect terms, is an attempt to pin the monetary authority so that it does not update on the government's short-term political frame. That is exactly the figure condition for a usable anchor, and Fig 2 :42 prices the amount of independence by the cost of being wrong. The **shape** is figure-licensed: an anchor that does not update.

What is **not** figure-licensed is the verdict that outcomes improve. Fig 2 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Whether inflation actually falls is a measurement question, not a derivation. This package reports a shape and a route, not a causal result.

World corroboration (secondary, not decisive): Alesina and Summers (1993) find that politically controlled central banks are more likely to produce high and variable inflation, *"little evidence"* of an effect on growth or unemployment. So the outcome claim is narrower than the question implies: an inflation association, not a general improvement in economic outcomes.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source for the figure shape (Fig 2), corroborated in the world. The ground verdict is `gap`, unreached.

**Refuter:** if measured outcomes show no systematic difference between independent and dependent banks under comparable mandates, the D1 shape loses its ground support. Also, if the institution demonstrably updates on political pressure, then it is not an anchor in the figure sense and the framing itself fails.

### F3 - In D2, the anchor is constitutively human, so insulation has a structural cost

Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

The accountability question is constitutively about people, so its legitimate anchor is human or democratic judgment, not inflation data. An anchor that is pinned *"does not update on A or B"* (Fig 2 :25) by construction cannot be corrected by the democratic frame. That is the structural weakening named in the question. Fig 10 :93 names the failure mode when the protected institution acquires a reputation to protect: its keeper is disqualified as a neutral ground. Fig 10 :47 names the other failure: a rule (convention) that actually decides outcomes has stopped being a neutral convention.

But the figures do **not** settle the normative verdict that this weakening is bad on balance. They settle the *effect* (a pinned anchor cannot update); the *valuation* is a human judgment, which is the domain's own anchor. Mode stays `specified`.

World corroboration: Fernández-Albertos (2015) argues that central banks *"are unavoidably political institutions"* making distributional choices. The IMF (Adrian and Khan, 2019) states that *"Independence and accountability are two sides of the same coin; transparency bridges them."* Dincer, Eichengreen and Martinez (2024) find that disclosure and reporting obligations contribute most positively to independence, while regulatory powers *"make accountability more complex, and render independence problematic."*

**Status:** `specified`, single-source for the figure effect (Fig 2 + Fig 10, two separate figure instruments, so low overlap within the framework), corroborated by three independent world fields. The normative weighting is a gap, not a silence.

**Refuter:** if the accountability domain has no legitimate anchor that is constitutively about people, or if transparency and oversight fully restore the channel without making the anchor update on transient politics, then the claimed structural weakening is not a loss and this finding must be narrowed.

### F4 - Reconciliation is a design seam, not a compromise verdict (general)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 10 :29: *"keep the anchor reachable"*
Fig 10 :19: *"install what can be violated"*
Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*

D3 is the seam between a non-updating anchor (D1) and a reachable democratic ground (D2). The figures do not say "split the difference." They say to keep the channel to the ground reachable while the ground itself does not update. Concretely, in this domain that points to clear mandates, transparent reporting, reviewable appointments and removal for cause: conventions that can be violated and therefore checked, rather than declarations of independence that merely assert a meaning.

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A formal independence statute that changes no actual contact is a recording, not a generator.

**Status:** `general` design shape, triangulated (Fig 10 + Fig 11). Not a policy recommendation with a `grounded` verdict; it is the shape any answer here must have.

**Refuter:** if oversight restores democratic contact only by making the monetary anchor update on the political frame, the design shape fails and the two conditions are genuinely irreconcilable at this level. That would be a real compound requiring a new instrument, not a compromise.

### F6 - Formal and actual independence are not the same term (isomer warning)

**Refuter:** if a declared statute and actual operational insulation are the same term in every case, the isomer distinction collapses and the warning is void. The governance and commitment-device evidence says otherwise.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The findings are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single anchor settles both improvement and accountability on one metric, so the union-compound finding (F1) is false.
2. Inflation and output data show no systematic difference between independent and dependent banks under comparable mandates, so D1 loses its ground support.
3. The accountability domain has no anchor constitutively about people, or transparency fully restores democratic correction without making the anchor update on transient politics, so D2's structural cost is not a loss.
4. The institution demonstrably updates on political pressure, in which case it is not an anchor in the figure sense and the whole framing fails.
5. Oversight can restore democratic contact only by making the anchor update on the political frame, so D3's design shape is impossible.

If any fires, the relevant domain's verdict is withdrawn, not smoothed over.

## 8. Bottom line

The question is a union compound. The two halves have different anchors, so there is no single A-or-B answer and no average. The figures license a shape: independence is an attempt at a non-updating anchor (D1), the legitimacy anchor is constitutively human so insulation has a structural correction cost (D2), and the reconciliation is a design seam that keeps the channel to the ground reachable (D3). The empirical balance is a ground question the figures cannot settle. Everything here is `specified` or `general`, and this simulated derivation cannot settle the question it reports on.
