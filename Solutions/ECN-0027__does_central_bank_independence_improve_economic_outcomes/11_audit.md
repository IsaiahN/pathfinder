## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :4/:54/:57 (union split), Fig 2 :29/:33/:37 (anchor, non-updating, human anchor), Fig 10 :19/:24/:29 (channel), Fig 11 :40 (contact).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: the question is a union compound; independence is a non-updating anchor; the legitimacy anchor is human; the reconciliation is a design seam; keep two verdicts and never average.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a reader's likely concrete claim: "independence lowers inflation and weakens democracy, so pick a compromise," or "the Tether proves independence is good/bad."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **ground gap**, which a reader tends to convert into a causal verdict ("independence improves outcomes"), contrary to Formula step 5;
- the **two-anchor separation**, which a reader tends to collapse into a single weighted score, contrary to Fig 1 :19;
- the **design-seam shape**, which a reader tends to turn into a policy prescription, contrary to Fig 10 :19 and the `general` mode label.

One thing survives cleanly: a union compound with two different anchors must be split before it is searched, and the verdicts cannot be averaged. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the condensation as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. `09_watson_answer.md` states this honestly.
