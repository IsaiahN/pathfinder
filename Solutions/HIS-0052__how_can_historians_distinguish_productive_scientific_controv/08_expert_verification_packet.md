# Expert verification packet: the Tether on productive controversy vs status rivalry

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or historian of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the discriminators, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No archival case was checked. This is a shape and a route, not a historiography.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A controversy is productive to the extent that three structural conditions are visible in the record: an anchor that does not move when a party pushes on it exists and someone ranges against it; the trajectory pays refusals, replications, novel predictions or a portable method rather than credit and priority; and a reusable method or named failure crosses out of the episode. A dispute is status-driven to the extent that the invoked ground is itself a party with a reputation, positions converge on whoever had the most influence, or the camps draw on one evidence pool. The claim-level and standing-level verdicts form a union compound. They are reported separately and never averaged. The framework does not name a winner in any specific case and does not read motives.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** the discriminator is the anchor, not the number or eminence of the parties. A ground that moves when pushed is not a ground, and an arbiter with a reputation to protect has become a party.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :14 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence." Fig 2 :20 "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Fig 10 :93 "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
- **Refuter:** a documented episode in which the arbiter demonstrably moved when standing changed and the dispute nevertheless yielded novel predictions and a portable method; or an episode with a fully unmoving anchor that produced only position-taking.

### C2: history-facing; checkable against one episode the reader knows well

- **Statement:** the currency of a productive trajectory includes refusals and portable methods; a status-driven trajectory pays credibility and priority. Fig 1 :26 "predicates minted, and credibility accrued"; Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 10 :61 "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."
- **What to check:** in one controversy you know from the documents, list what left the episode and was reused by later workers. Then list what was refused rather than protected. If the second list is empty while the first is full of names and credit, does the classification still come out as productive?
- **Refuter:** a controversy judged productive by later practitioners whose only residue is one camp's victory, with no transportable practice, instrument, standard, refusal or novel prediction traceable to the dispute.
- **Outside check, corroboration only:** the SEP entry on Lakatos defines a progressive programme by "novel and hitherto unexpected facts"; the Collins passage on the experimenter's regress says "since experimentation is a matter of skilful practice, it can never be clear whether a second experiment has been done sufficiently well to count as a check on the results of a first." Neither overrules the figure lines.

### C3: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the two drivers are a union compound, not a spectrum, and the historian occupies a seat that must not read motives. Two verdicts are owed, claim-level and standing-level, and they are never averaged. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :51 "what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
- **Refuter:** a class of episodes in which fact and standing are strictly separable and removing standing from the record changes no classification; or a refutation produced from the public record and residual alone, with no access to private motives, that overturns the two-verdict reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict on any named controversy, school, institution or person.
- Not a claim that motives can be read. The seat is denied the interior.
- Not a claim that eminence, office, majority or editorial authority settles a claim.
- Not a claim that all agreement is corrupt. Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it".
- Not a claim that a missing method proves nobody was right. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not grounded. No archival test was run in this session.
- Not a theory of everything about historiography. It is a generator of a discriminator shape with stated losing conditions.

## 5. What would settle it

Documents that act as instruments already returning something (Fig 6 :32): a dated ruling showing that the arbiter moved under standing pressure; a citation or replication trail discriminating independent derivation from one shared pool; an inventory of methods, refusals and predictions that crossed out of the episode and were reused. No further argument about "what really drives controversies" substitutes for these.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29 to :30, :14, :20, Fig 4 :22, Fig 9 :54 and :57. Six short passages.
2. Pick one controversy you know from the documents. Name the thing in it that could not be moved by the parties, or say that there was none.
3. List what crossed out of that episode and was reused later. Separate a method from a winner's name.
4. Try to produce the converse of C2: a productive episode with nothing but victories as residue.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
