## 0. The question in effect terms (the residual, bound as an ordinary term)

A historian faces a documented controversy and must choose between two accounts of what is driving it:

- **A, ground-tracking:** the disagreement ranges against something that does not move when a participant pushes on it, and the trajectory yields novel predictions, replication attempts, refusals and a portable method.
- **B, status-driven:** the disagreement ranges against the participants themselves. Positions converge on whoever had the most influence, camps share one evidence pool, the "ground" invoked by a party is itself a party with a reputation, and the only residue is a winner.

The residual R is the gap between what the controversy says it is about (a disputed claim about the world) and what its trajectory actually pays (standing, priority, institutional position). Stated in the framework's notation, R is per slot, never an average: the historian should ask, for each disputed claim and each episode, whether the observed move was a response to the ground or to the frame. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

**The shape I commit to before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): *a controversy is productive to the extent that an anchor that does not update on anyone's opinion is reachable, frames range against it separately, a term that fails is refused rather than protected, and something portable crosses out of the episode; it is status-driven to the extent that those conditions fail and the currency is the participants' standing.* This is an ordinary term here. It can accrue a residual and be refused (F9 and the pre-registered refuter).

**What an answer would look like:** a short list of discriminators, each with a named ground, a named currency and a stated refuter, plus the honest admission that the figure set supplies the shape of the discriminator and not the verdict on any named episode. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two. There is a claim-level question (what would settle this disputed point?) and a trajectory-level question (what actually moved the participants?). Treating them as one question is the bundling error in Fig 9 :54 to :58.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The relation between a frame and its ground is not a negotiation. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Averaging the two sides' standing is not a symmetric solution; it is the anchor test failing.
- **Scale:** two. Within one episode (participants, instruments, claims) and across episodes (successive rooms in a tradition). Fig 7 :35 distinguishes the two: a room's internal agreement is not the same object as selection between rooms.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, epistemic content of the controversy | competing researchers, instruments, claims, methods, replications | residuals, predictions, replications, portable methods | yes: whatever would settle the disputed claim, an experiment, dataset, observation or calculation that does not move with opinion | legitimate domain |
| D2, social and institutional field | departments, journals, funders, priority claimants, reputations | standing, credit, priority, funding, reputation | **no**: its reference is the agreement of the parties, which is Fig 2's mutual update or common cause | NOT a domain with its own ground; a collapse pattern inside D1 |
| D3, diachronic room chain | successive cohorts, schools, generations of a field | methods carried up, residuals handed on, head starts sent down | yes: the ground's pressure on each room, Fig 7 :39 | legitimate domain, dependent on D1 |
| D4, the historian's verification seat | historian, documentary record, outside readers | provenance, residuals, declared stakes | yes: the original ground, reachable through records the historian did not author | legitimate seat, dependent on D1 and D3 |

**The decisive census move.** D2 fails the anchor test. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Institutional standing moves when the institution is pushed; it is the agreement of the parties in another form. So status rivalry is not a rival frame with its own valid ground. It is the name we give to a failure of anchoring in D1. This is why it gets zero verdict weight below, and why the historian must not treat "the institution won" as an alternative explanation of equal rank to "the ground settled it."

I initially tried to give D2 its own anchor, namely institutional survival. **Retracted at census** (see §6): survival is the social analogue of fitness without a maintained ground, and the framework's own rule gives it no verdict standing.

### 1.3 Partition or union

This is a **union compound**, not a partition. A controversy can be ground-tracking in one slot and status-driven in another; the standing question changes which evidence is admitted, so the two conditions overlap. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged into a score such as "60 percent productive."

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Is there a fact at stake and an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve? Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." The other two instruments assume this answer.
2. Which link does the chain currently stop at? Fig 3 :29: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed." The middle link (what would count as progress here) is the one with no instrument, Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
3. Only then judge the social behaviour. Effort spent on step 3 cannot rescue a break at step 1 or 2: Fig 3, "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

### 1.5 Strain test applied during the census

I first tried to force Fig 13's six-term substrate (states and structures) onto historical controversies, reading "productive controversy" as a cross-kind composition. That is a strain: historical disputes have actors and currencies, not physical states and structures. Re-census: Fig 13 governs composition of the substrate, not the classification of disputes. The governing lines for this question are Fig 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 10. No strained line is retained.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1: The discriminator is the anchor, not the number of participants or the eminence of the parties

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :13 to :15 names the status-driven trajectory directly: "no anchor; each updates on the others", then "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

So the first question for a historian is not "who won?" nor "how many supported each side?" but "what, in this episode, could not be moved by the parties, and did anyone actually range against it?" Where the invoked ground is a journal, a society or a committee with a reputation to protect, Fig 10 :93 applies: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

**Refuter:** a documented case in which an anchor demonstrably moved when pushed (the arbiter changed its ruling when the parties' standing changed) and the controversy nevertheless delivered novel predictions and a portable method; or a case with a fully unmoving anchor that produced only position-taking and no method. Either breaks the discriminator.

### F2: Credibility and standing are frame-internal and cannot be verdict weights

Fig 1 :26: "predicates minted, and credibility accrued". Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on." Institutional rivalry is exactly standing functioning as a stake after the episode that earned it.

Fig 2 gives the two-sided nuance: :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool", but :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". So agreement inside a school is not automatically corrupt when the product is a shared convention. It becomes disqualifying when it is used as a verdict about the world. This is the trap for the historian: a dispute over standards may be partly a convention negotiation and partly a claim about nature; the two must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54).

**Refuter:** a case where the historian's classification changes when the participants' eminence or institutional office is removed from the documentary record while all evidence-bearing facts are held fixed. If the classification changes, eminence was doing verdict work and this finding stands confirmed as a warning; if a classification can be produced that depends only on eminence and still tracks the ground, F2 is broken.

### F3: A productive controversy leaves a method, not just a winner

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Applied literally: if the only thing that survives a controversy is a name in a textbook and a list of victors, that is a recording. If what survives is a reusable way of testing, an instrument protocol, a standard of evidence or a refined research programme, a generator crossed the membrane.

This triangulates with the world sources read this session. Shapin and Schaffer's account of the Boyle-Linus exchange, as summarised in the fetched source, says Boyle "would therefore demonstrate not merely that Linus was wrong, but also how experimental controversies ought to be conducted." That is a method crossing outward after a dispute, arrived at in a different field from the figures. Lakatos's SEP entry gives the parallel criterion for a research programme: a "progressive" programme is theoretically and empirically progressive, predicting "novel and hitherto unexpected facts"; a degenerating one only accommodates known facts. Collins's experimenter's regress gives the negative case: "The problem is that, since experimentation is a matter of skilful practice, it can never be clear whether a second experiment has been done sufficiently well to count as a check on the results of a first."

**Refuter:** a controversy universally judged productive by later practitioners whose only residue is the victory of one camp, with no transportable practice, instrument, standard or novel prediction traceable to the dispute. I also state the converse: if such a case is produced, "leaves a method" is too strong and must be weakened to "often leaves a method."

### F4: Agreement is not evidence; independence decides what agreement means

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

Two camps that cite the same review, the same mentor and the same journal are one evidence pool. Fig 2 :19 to :20: "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." A historian therefore asks about the *source* of each position, not its popularity. Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

**Refuter:** a case in which two camps demonstrably drew on one evidence pool and their agreement nevertheless predicted a novel result that neither had previously held, with the provenance showing no import from outside that pool. That would falsify the independence requirement here.

### F5: Productive controversy often ends in a refusal, not a synthesis

Fig 5 :50: "the gap has a fifth kind, and the pipeline has no branch for it". Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". The Operators table: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". A historian who looks only for synthesis, reconciliation or a compromise will misread a dispute that productively killed a held claim. The right residue may be a named failure class that future workers can avoid.

Formula step 2 orders this: REFUTED is sorted by what is WRONG, and "no arrangement of the other four reaches it." So a dispute that merely adds new terms while protecting the one that keeps failing is status-preserving, not productive, even if it is busy.

**Refuter:** a documented productive controversy in which no held term was refused, demoted or marked as failed, only added to. If common, the negation requirement is a figure-level idealisation and must be recorded as such (Seat Map :53: "A prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong").

### F6: Union, not spectrum: a controversy can be both, and the two verdicts must stay separate

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :55 to :58 explains why a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing as a specific compound.

So "productive or status-driven?" is a false either/or for many episodes. The historian owes two verdicts: what the claim-level trajectory did (ground-tracking or not) and what the standing-level trajectory did (frame-internal or not). They are never averaged. Fig 1 :19 forbids the aggregate: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

**Refuter:** a controversy in which the fact question and the standing question are strictly separable, the standing question never affects which evidence is admitted, and the same historian reaches the same classification with the standing facts entirely removed. That would refute the union reading for that class of case.

### F7: The instrument test: no nameable anchor, no domain

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :34 to :35: "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

For the historian, this is a check on the claim that a dispute was "really about" something unobservable, such as the true motive of a participant. motives are inland of the frame. The seat is denied the interior. Fig 9 :51 says what the verifier may see: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it." Fig 10 :50: "Looking inside the frame it grades is how the seat stops being outside." So the discriminator must be structural and documentary (what was claimed, what was tested, what moved when pushed, what crossed out), not psychological.

**Refuter:** a case in which no document, instrument or outcome at any resolution registers a difference between the two accounts, yet a later generation settles the classification by a new instrument that was already returning something. That is the diamond case of *How Intelligence Actually Works*; it would show the instrument test was too conservative here, not that it was wrong.

### F8: The historian occupies a seat, not a throne

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist." Fig 10 :59 to :60 require the undeclared interest to be declared. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

So a historian who announces in advance that a particular institution is always the corrupt party has acquired a fixed valence and left the seat. The classification must be recomputed per residual. Fig 10 :65: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record." A well-sourced narrative of a dispute can be perfectly provenanced and still wrong.

**Refuter:** a refutation produced by a reader who saw only the residual description and the public record, with no access to the participants' private motives, that overturns the classification. That is the intended hit, not a failure.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the verdict above was assembled, per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is documented:

1. A controversy whose arbiter demonstrably moved when the parties' standing changed, yet which produced novel predictions and a portable method (breaks F1).
2. A classification of the same episode that flips when eminence and office are removed while all evidence-bearing facts are held fixed (breaks F2).
3. A controversy judged productive by later practitioners with no residue beyond the victory of one camp, no method, no refusal and no novel prediction (breaks F3).
4. Two camps sharing one evidence pool whose agreement nevertheless yielded a novel held result with no outside import (breaks F4).
5. A productive controversy in which no held term was ever refused (breaks F5, or demotes it to an idealisation).
6. A class of episodes where fact and standing are strictly separable and removing standing changes nothing (breaks F6 for that class).

Until one of these is run against an archival case, no verdict from this folder is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the anchor test and the refuter, not with a theory of scientific change (F1, F4).
2. Never use the number of supporters, their eminence or their offices as evidence about the claim. Use them only as contact cost when choosing whom to ask (F2; Fig 9 :51).
3. Ask what crossed out of the episode: method, instrument, standard, refusal. That is the visible residue of a generator (F3).
4. Unbundle before judging. Give two separate verdicts, claim-level and standing-level, and never average them (F6).
5. Declare the historian's own stake. Valence is relative to the residual (F8).
6. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Any condensation of these figures into a checklist must carry its round-trip gap, which is recorded in `11_audit.md`.

### Method carried forward

When a question asks "how can X distinguish A from B?", do not average A and B. Census the actor, currency and anchor of each; if one candidate has no nameable anchor, classify it as a failure of anchoring rather than as a rival domain. Then look for the portable residue. This method, not its current verdict, is what should be reused.
