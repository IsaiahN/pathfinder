# Solution HIS-0052: figure-grounded package

**Question:** How can historians distinguish productive scientific controversy from disputes driven mainly by status or institutional rivalry?

**Source:** `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 65.

**Section:** 6. Disagreement, controversy, and consensus.

**Mode:** `specified` on the discriminators, `general` on the structural shape. Nothing here is `grounded`: no archival case was checked against the ground in this run.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

## What an answer looks like (bound as an ordinary term)

A successful answer is a set of discriminators that a historian can apply to a documented controversy and that can come back wrong. It is not a verdict on any named episode and it is not a ranking of the participants. The sought-for shape is bound in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §0 and carried through every file, so it can accrue a residual and be refused rather than remaining an invisible preference (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :62 to :66).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named historians.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation settles any actual historical controversy. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
