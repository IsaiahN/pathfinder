## What two of the external sources change about the pitch

1. **Collins's experimenter's regress** is the cleanest outside statement of the anchor problem: without an instrument that can be checked separately, a replication dispute has no stopping rule. This corroborates Fig 2's anchor test and Fig 6's instrument rule. It also warns against overclaiming: the historian cannot assume an anchor exists merely because both sides cite one.
2. **Shapin and Schaffer's Boyle/Linus episode** is a precedent for the method-residue test. The fetched account says Boyle used the exchange to show "how experimental controversies ought to be conducted", while the book's concluding claim that "He who has the most, and the most powerful, allies wins" warns that assent has political conditions. Both halves belong in the pitch; using only the second would turn a source about assent into a blanket denial of ground.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the discriminator and its refuter first; the architecture is a recording until someone has stated a residual.
- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A reader who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame.
- **Fig 9 :27:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see in a productive controversy before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not select recipients for likely agreement.
- **Fig 9 :60:** "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Contact cost decides whom to ask first, never whether an answer counts.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the discriminator and its three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method each reader can reuse with their own refuter.
