## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the historian's task: distinguishing a controversy that tracks a ground from one driven mainly by status or institutional rivalry. Option A: treat the two drivers as ends of one scale and score each episode on a single spectrum. Option B: census actors, currency and anchor, refuse the second driver a ground of its own, and give two separate verdicts. What each leads to: A produces an average that hides the intersection and lets eminence enter through the score; B produces a checkable anchor test, a currency test, a residue test, written refuters and an honest stop.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a controversy says it is about and what its trajectory actually pays. Arity: two, claim-level and trajectory-level. Symmetry: asymmetric, since frames range against a ground and do not negotiate with it (Fig 2 :45). Scale: within one episode and across successive rooms in a tradition.
- **Domain 1, epistemic content.** Actors: researchers, instruments, claims, methods, replications. Currency: residuals, predictions, refusals, methods. Anchor: whatever would settle the disputed claim and does not move with opinion. Legitimate domain.
- **Domain 2, social and institutional field.** Actors: departments, journals, funders, priority claimants. Currency: standing, credit, reputation. Anchor: none nameable. Its reference is the parties' agreement, which is Fig 2's mutual-update or one-pool collapse. Recorded as a failure of anchoring inside D1, not as a rival ground. Zero verdict weight.
- **Domain 3, the diachronic room chain.** Actors: successive cohorts of a field. Currency: methods carried up, head starts down, residuals handed on. Anchor: the ground's pressure on each room. Legitimate but dependent on D1.
- **Domain 4, the historian's verification seat.** Actors: historian, documentary record, outside readers. Currency: provenance and residuals. Anchor: the original ground, reachable through records the historian did not author. Legitimate, dependent on D1 and D3.
- **Partition or union:** union. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** establish an instrument already returning something, then ask what would count as progress in the participants' own terms, then judge what was carried. Formula step 7 asks SEARCHED before INWARD or OUTWARD.

Strain test: an initial attempt to apply Fig 13's six-term substrate to disputes was strained and returned to census. No strained line is retained.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the case-level verdict does not exist in this run. A controversy is productive to the extent that an anchor that does not move with opinion is reachable and ranged against, the trajectory pays refusals, replications and portable methods rather than credit and priority, and a method or named failure crosses out. A dispute is status-driven to the extent that the invoked ground is a party, positions converge on influence, or the camps share one pool. The two verdicts form a union compound and are never averaged. The framework does not name a winner, read motives, or treat eminence as evidence. Everything here is `specified` and ungrounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is using eminence, headcount or a room's agreement as a verdict channel, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the anchor test and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The three discriminators and the two-verdict rule can be revised or withdrawn without touching a case verdict, because no case verdict was issued. A superseding packet carries its own refuter and provenance. Nothing has to be deleted from the record; a refusal removes standing, not history.
- **reversed by:** a documentary or instrumental reading, not agreement. A dated ruling showing the arbiter moved under standing pressure, a citation trail discriminating convergent derivation from one pool, an inventory showing no method or refusal left the episode, or a class of cases where removing standing changes no classification. Any of these fires the pre-registered refuter. Prestige or majority assent is not such an instrument.
