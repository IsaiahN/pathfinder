# Outreach draft: check the discriminator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a public submission through the venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two-stage order below is respected. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.

---

**Subject:** Would this discriminator change how you read a controversy?

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm working on a small checkable claim about how historians tell a productive scientific controversy from a dispute driven mainly by standing. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the discriminator fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a controversy is productive to the extent that three things are visible in the record. (1) Something in the episode could not be moved by the parties, and at least one participant ranged against it. (2) The currency includes refusals, replication attempts, novel predictions or a reusable method, not just victories and credit. (3) The claim-level question and the standing-level question can be unbundled and given separate verdicts. A dispute is status-driven to the extent that the "ground" invoked by a party is itself a party with a reputation, positions converge on whoever had the most influence, and the camps share one evidence pool.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named episode is productive or corrupt. That a historian can read motives. That a majority, a prestigious journal or a well-credentialled editor settles anything. Credit and office are frame-internal quantities here; they give a claim a budget to be tried, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a controversy whose arbiter demonstrably moved when the parties' standing changed, yet which delivered novel predictions and a portable method;
> 2. a classification that flips when eminence and office are removed from the record while all evidence-bearing facts are held fixed;
> 3. a controversy judged productive by later practitioners whose only residue is the victory of one camp.
>
> I wrote these before writing the conclusion. If any one holds, I withdraw the discriminator. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down your own refuter before you run it. A loop cannot check the direction of its own question.

## Three-stage sequence

1. **Stage 1:** send the discriminator and its refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2 (opt-in):** only after stage 1 is recorded, offer the links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person write their own refuter and state their own mode.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
