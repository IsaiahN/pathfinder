# The Tether's answer: distinguishing productive controversy from status rivalry

**Mode: `specified` on the discriminators, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading of any historical episode.

## 1. The residual it answers

Historians must classify a controversy from a documentary record. The residual is the gap between what the dispute says it is about (a disputed claim) and what its trajectory actually pays (a result, or standing). The classification is per claim and per episode, not an average over a career or a field. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## 2. The shape: three discriminators, two separate verdicts

1. **The anchor test.** Productive controversy has something in the episode that does not move when a party pushes on it, and at least one participant ranges against it. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Status-driven controversy shows the mutual-update or one-pool collapse: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence" (Fig 2 :14), or "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other" (Fig 2 :20). If the invoked ground is an interested party, Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
2. **The currency test.** Look at what the dispute pays. Productive trajectories mint refusals, replication attempts, novel predictions and methods. Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Status-driven trajectories pay credibility and priority. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."
3. **The residue test.** Ask what crossed out of the episode. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A winner's name is a recording; a reusable standard, instrument, research programme or named failure is a generator. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

**Keep two verdicts, never one score.** The claim-level trajectory and the standing-level trajectory are a union compound, not ends of a spectrum (Fig 9 :54 and :57). A dispute can be ground-tracking on one claim and influence-driven on another. Report both and do not average them. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

**Provenance, not content, separates convergence from adoption.** Two sides reaching the same position independently and two sides copying one source look identical in the documents. Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named controversy is productive or corrupt. No case was checked here.
- Not that motives can be read. Fig 9 :51 denies the verifier evidence bearing only on the maker; Fig 10 :50 says looking inside the frame grades is how the seat stops being outside.
- Not that a majority, an eminent reviewer, a prestigious journal or an editor settles anything. Those are frame-internal quantities or interested arbiters.
- Not that agreement is always corrupt. Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". It is disqualifying for a verdict about the world, not for a convention about meaning.
- Not that "no method survived" proves nobody was right. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not grounded. Nothing here is settled by an archive or an instrument in this run.

## 4. What would settle a case, and what would settle the shape

For a **case**, the ground is documentary and instrumental: a dated record showing that the arbiter changed its ruling when standing changed; a citation and replication trail showing whether the camps were independent pools; a list of methods, refusals and predictions that left the episode and were reused elsewhere. These are instruments already returning something, which is where instrument work must start (Fig 6 :32).

For the **shape itself**, the pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are the losing conditions. Three suffice: (1) an arbiter that moved under standing pressure yet the dispute still produced novel predictions and a portable method; (2) a classification that flips when eminence is removed with all evidence held fixed, showing eminence was doing verdict work in the opposite direction; (3) a controversy judged productive with no residue beyond a victory. If any fires, the discriminator is revised or withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

The figures give the shape of a discriminator, not a name for a historical verdict. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and I record explicitly that this run did not open an archive. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured, and `11_audit.md` enumerates the gap `R_T` rather than assuming it is zero. The work stops at the limit of what these instruments distinguish, with the lineage recorded so a reader can locate the error.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not declare which past controversy was productive. It gives the shape a discriminator must have. Ask three things: was there something in the episode that did not move when the parties pushed on it, and did anyone range against it; did the trajectory pay refusals, replications and portable methods rather than credit and priority; and what crossed out of the episode that a later worker could reuse. Then give two separate verdicts, one about the disputed claim and one about standing, because a dispute can be both, and never average them into a score. Agreement among peers is not evidence about the world when they share one pool, though it is the right product when the thing being made is a convention. The figure set refuses to name a winner and refuses to read motives; it offers a checkable shape, written refuters, and the documentary instruments that would close it. The case stays open until the record settles it.
