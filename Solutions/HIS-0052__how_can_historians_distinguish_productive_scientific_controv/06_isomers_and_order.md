# Structure, order and analogues for the two-driver question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a distinction between two drivers of controversy. Before using the word "distinction", I have to decide what kind of object the two drivers are: ends of one scale, two separate kinds, or overlapping conditions in a compound. The answer changes what a historian is allowed to conclude.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried from `01`, not re-derived here)

D1 epistemic content (currency: residuals, methods; anchor: the disputed fact). D2 social field (currency: standing; no anchor, so a collapse pattern inside D1). D3 room chain (currency: methods and residuals; anchor: ground pressure). D4 historian's seat (currency: provenance; anchor: the original ground through records). D1 and D3 are legitimate domains; D2 is a failure of anchoring; D4 depends on both.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are "productive" and "status-driven" ends of a spectrum?

No. A spectrum would make them readings of one quantity, so that more evidence is less status. Fig 13 :35 to :37: "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot." Applied by analogy at the classification level: the claim-level question (what would settle this?) and the standing-level question (who gains by this?) answer different questions and cannot be traded against one another.

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." So the two drivers form a **union compound**. A controversy can be high on ground-tracking and high on standing simultaneously, because the standing question often decides which evidence is admitted.

**Falsifier (Q1):** a class of episodes in which the two verdicts are perfectly anti-correlated across every case, with the standing verdict always falling when the ground verdict rises and no case in the intersection. That would support a spectrum reading and refute the compound reading for that class.

### Q2: Are they isomers under Fig 12?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

Take a dispute as the molecule. The "atoms" are participants, claims, instruments, documents and a forum. The bond differs:

- ground-directed bond: the parties range separately against something that does not move (Fig 2 :45).
- influence-directed bond: the parties update on one another or draw on one pool (Fig 2 :14, :19 to :20).

Same ingredients, different bond, different substance. This is a genuine structural claim under the figure's own definition, not a chemical analogy smuggled in as a fact about historians. It is also limited: disputes have no typed bond set equivalent to the seven operators, so the molecule here is an analogue at one remove. Mark it `specified`, not `grounded`.

**Falsifier (Q2):** two documented disputes with identical participants, claims, instruments and documents, where switching only the bond description produces no difference in the surviving record (same methods, same refusals, same citations). If the substance does not differ, the isomer reading fails and only the weaker "two labels for one process" reading stands.

### Q3: What is the order of checks?

Dependency order from Fig 3:

1. **Perception link:** is there anything in the record that returns a difference? Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
2. **Vocabulary and objective link:** can we say what would count as progress here, in the participants' own terms? This is the middle link with no instrument, where chains usually break (Fig 3 :24 to :27).
3. **Planning and carrying link:** what was actually tried, refused and carried to the next dispute? Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

Formula step 7 adds the search order: ask SEARCHED first (is it in the record and unfound?), then INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something), then OUTWARD (find a frame whose closure predicts the shape). Never read INWARD against OUTWARD.

### Q4: Which analogues are admissible, and which are not?

- **Lakatos, from the SEP entry (admissible, low overlap):** a programme is progressive when it predicts "novel and hitherto unexpected facts" and degenerate when it only protects its core. This corroborates the method-residue discriminator, but it is a philosophy-of-science frame, not the historian's ground.
- **Collins's experimenters' regress (admissible, low overlap):** "since experimentation is a matter of skilful practice, it can never be clear whether a second experiment has been done sufficiently well to count as a check on the results of a first." This corroborates the anchor test and warns that a claimed replication may itself be a status move.
- **Matthew effect (admissible with a narrow use):** credit accumulates to the already eminent. Fig 1 :26 disqualifies "credibility accrued" as a verdict metric, so this is a description of the status currency, never a measure of correctness.
- **Shapin and Schaffer (admissible with both halves):** "He who has the most, and the most powerful, allies wins" describes the conditions of assent; the same fetched account records Boyle defending "how experimental controversies ought to be conducted". Using only the first half would import a conclusion the source does not carry.
- **Popular framing of "scientific revolutions" as replacements (not admissible as a verdict):** the SEP entry says such attributions are often "a retrospective judgment, a judgment of eventual consequences". That is a warning about the historian's own frame, not a licence to decide by hindsight.

All world analogues are secondary. Where one conflicts with a figure line, the conflict goes back to the census; it never flips the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The two drivers are a union compound, not a spectrum. At the level of structure they are Fig 12 isomers: same dispute ingredients, different bond, different surviving substance. The order of inquiry is perception, objective, then carrying, with SEARCHED before INWARD or OUTWARD. Four low-overlap analogues corroborate different parts of the shape and none overrules it. Two falsifiers are written above, before any case was run.
