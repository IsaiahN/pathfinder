## Independent domain census (written before reading the primary census)

I redid the census from the question as an effect, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R.** The historian must decide what drove a controversy, given only a record. Arity: two, because the claim-level question and the trajectory-level question can come apart. Symmetry: asymmetric, since a ground is not a negotiateable third party. Scale: one episode and a chain of episodes.
- **Domain A, the disputed claim.** Actors: claimants, instruments, documents. Currency: residuals, tests, methods. Anchor: whatever settles the claim and does not move on opinion. Legitimate domain.
- **Domain B, the standing field.** Actors: offices, journals, funders, priority claimants. Currency: credit and standing. Anchor: none. Its only reference is the parties' agreement. Under the anchor test this is not a domain with its own ground. It is the mutual-update or common-cause shape.
- **Domain C, the tradition over time.** Actors: successive cohorts. Currency: methods and residuals handed on. Anchor: the ground's pressure between rooms. Legitimate, depends on A.
- **Domain D, the checking position.** Actors: historian and public readers. Currency: provenance. Anchor: the original ground reached through records. Legitimate, depends on A and C.
- **Union, not partition.** The two drivers can bind at once, so two verdicts are owed and no average is admissible.
- **Order:** instrument first, objective second, carrying third. SEARCHED before INWARD or OUTWARD.
