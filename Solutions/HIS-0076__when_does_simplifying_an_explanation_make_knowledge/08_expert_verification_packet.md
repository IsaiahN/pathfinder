# Expert verification packet - the Tether on simplification and uncertainty

**What this is:** a claim in a form a science communication researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No receiver measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A simplification is a lossy transform across a membrane. Loss is not the defect; an unreported loss is. A simplification makes knowledge accessible when it hands over a method the receiver can re-derive and check, keeps the relations that decide behaviour, reports the round-trip gap, and lowers the receiver's residual per claim and per decision against a ground they can reach. It distorts the underlying uncertainty when any of those is missing while a frame-internal metric such as clarity, coverage or clicks reports success. The two conditions overlap and must be kept separate, never averaged.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 4

- **Statement:** the relevant quantity is the round trip and its gap. A short account that does not report what it dropped is in the distortion case even when it reads well.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27 - *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 4 :31 - *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*
- **Method test:** can the receiver reconstruct the claim from the short version and carry it back to a specific decision, with the gap measured rather than assumed?
- **Refuter:** a case where the round-trip gap is unmeasured, the receiver predicts correctly on new cases, and nothing is left unexplained. If that holds, unreported loss is not the defect.

### C2 - Framework-internal with a world-facing test; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the unit a simplification loses is the relation, not the fact. Two short accounts with the same facts and different relations are isomers, and the difference is decisive in behaviour and invisible in the flat text.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :16 - *"and nothing signals that anything was lost"*
- **What to check:** give two groups the same fact set with different relations. Do their predictions differ? Can either group tell, from the text alone, which relation was used?
- **Refuter:** identical per-slot receiver residuals across the two arrangements, with the relation difference shown to have no behavioural consequence. That kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim (Fig 12 :124) standing instead.

### C3 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** accessibility is a per-slot, ground-referenced quantity. Clarity, coverage and credibility are frame-internal and cannot decide it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What to check:** is there a mean comprehension or clarity score hiding a live residual on one claim or one decision? Is the ground one the receiver can reach, or is it the explainer's own rating?
- **Refuter:** an independently ground-referenced measurement showing that a frame-internal metric tracks per-slot receiver error with no unexplained residual. Then the metric is a witness after all.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that shorter is always worse, or longer always better. Length is not the variable.
- Not a best reading level, template, or optimum for a named audience. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that audience agreement proves accuracy. Agreement on a shared meaning is a convention (Fig 2 :41); agreement about the world after a shared evidence pool is disqualified (Fig 2 :40).
- Not a replacement for science communication research. World sources are secondary and corroborate only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a receiver measurement.
- Not a claim that this packet was written outside the framework. It was not, and the second seat in `11_audit.md` is simulated. Its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 5. What would settle it

A receiver-side instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it by measurement. Here:

1. **Round-trip test:** reconstruct and carry back; measure the gap.
2. **Bond test:** same facts, different relations, measure receiver predictions.
3. **Method test:** use the account on a new case without re-deriving.
4. **Per-slot calibration:** receiver prediction against a reachable ground, after the explainer's framing is removed.
5. **Receiver dependence:** repeat across receivers with measured differences in prior knowledge.

The five pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are written before this run. Any one that fires withdraws the shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :27 and :30-31, Fig 12 :5 and :117, Fig 1 :19, :22 and :27. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: can you name a case where two accounts with the same facts in a different arrangement produced different public understanding? If yes, did the simplified text show the arrangement?
3. Name one instrument that would measure the round-trip gap at the receiver, or say why none is possible.
4. Say which of the five refuters you would try first.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
