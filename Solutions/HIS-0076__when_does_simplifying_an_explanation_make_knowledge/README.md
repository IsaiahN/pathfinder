## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether a simplification is short or long, clear or dense. It is what happens to the **per-slot residual** at the receiver. Let a source frame hold an explanation Γ_S with its atoms, its bonds, and the structure of its uncertainty. Let a simplification be a lossy transform T across a membrane. Let a receiver frame hold Γ_R and act on a decision. R_s = |Γ_R(b,a) - o'| is measured per slot s, never as one global score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The anchor is the ground that settles the receiver's prediction, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29.

- Option A, "simplification is the removal of difficulty": predicts accessibility rises monotonically as content is removed.
- Option B, "simplification is a lossy transform whose round-trip gap must be reported": predicts accessibility rises only when the receiver's per-slot residual falls against the ground AND the gap the coarser description cannot hold is reported; distortion occurs when the gap is hidden or the bonds that carry decisive difference are dropped (Fig 4 :30, Fig 4 :31, Fig 12 :5).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* stating the two conditions that separate accessible transmission from distorting transmission, plus an instrument that measures the round-trip gap per slot. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The specific best simplification for a given audience is declared a `gap`, not an answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the science communication question, and no claim that the framework is the only account of explanation.

Four reasons, three figure-grounded and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
