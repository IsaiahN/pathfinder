# The Tether's answer on simplification, accessibility, and uncertainty

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

When an explanation is compressed, what happens to the gap between what a receiver predicts and what the ground returns, per claim and per decision? R_s = |Γ_R(b,a) - o'| is indexed per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). A second quantity, R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)), measures what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :30). The answer has to be about both, because either one alone can look good while the other fails.

## 2. The shape

1. **Simplification is a lossy transform across a membrane, and loss is not the defect.** Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* The defect is an unreported loss. Fig 4 :31: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold"*

2. **It is accessible when a method crosses, not a recording.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* A receiver who can re-derive and check makes new predictions; a receiver who only has the concluded sentence repeats one occasion.

3. **It distorts when the bond is dropped and no record marks the drop.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Two short accounts with the same facts and different relations are isomers. The simplified text cannot show the difference that decides the outcome.

4. **The verdict is per slot, against a reachable ground.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. A mean comprehension score cannot witness accessibility.

5. **No self-produced metric may decide it.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Clarity, coverage and clicks are budget filters. They are not witnesses.

6. **Accessibility is relative to the receiver.** Fig 4 :20: *"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The same text can lower R for a novice and raise it for an expert. The receiver's Γ_R, their prior terms and their decision must be named before the question is well posed.

7. **The two conditions form a union, not a scale.** Fig 9 :57: *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Reporting the gap and keeping the bonds are separate conditions; clearing one does not clear their overlap.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that shorter is always worse, or that longer is always better. Length is not the variable.
- Not that any simplification is the best one for a named audience. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that an audience's agreement proves accuracy. It settles a convention, which is a different and legitimate product (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a compression or clarity metric is a verdict. These are frame-internal.
- Not `grounded`. No receiver measurement is claimed here.
- Not a substitution for the science communication literature. The world sources in `09` are secondary corroboration, not the authority.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating and receiver-referenced instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* says an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; what is needed is a spectrometer. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Round-trip test:** ask the receiver to reconstruct the claim from the simplified version, carry it back to a specific decision, and measure the gap. If the gap is unreported, the account is in the distortion case even if it feels clear.
- **Bond test:** give two groups the same facts with different relations. If their predictions differ and the text does not show why, the isomer failure is confirmed.
- **Method test:** ask the receiver to use the account on a genuinely new case without re-deriving it. Success supports the catalyst reading; failure means a recording crossed.
- **Per-slot calibration:** measure receiver predictions against a ground they can reach, per claim and per decision, after the explainer's framing is removed.
- **Receiver dependence:** repeat with receivers whose prior knowledge measurably differs. Identical results would refute F7.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** the five pre-registered conditions in section 4. Any one fires against the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The best simplification for a particular audience is a declared `gap`. *They're Scanning Books* warns that *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The missing instrument is characterised in effect terms: it discriminates a dropped bond from a legitimately absent one, at the receiver, against the ground. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A simplification is a lossy transform, and loss itself is not the problem. The problem is an unreported loss. The Tether's shape has two conditions that must both hold. First, the short version must carry a method a receiver can re-derive and check, not just the conclusion of one occasion. Second, it must keep the relations between the facts, because two accounts with the same facts in different arrangements behave differently and the flat text cannot show the difference. The gap the short version cannot hold has to be reported, and the receiver's error has to be measured per claim and per decision against a ground they can reach. Clarity ratings, coverage and clicks are budget filters, not verdicts, because a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces. What the framework does not give is the best wording for your audience. It gives the shape any checkable answer must have, the measuring instrument that would close it, and five falsifiers written in advance. The audience agreement is a convention; the ground is the verdict.
