# Isomers, arrangement, and order in a simplification

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks where the boundary falls between accessible and distorting simplification. Two supporting derivations are owed: (1) what is the unit that a simplification loses, atoms or bonds, and (2) in what order do the links of the transmission chain fail, so that effort is spent at the right link. A third question follows: are two simplified accounts with the same content isomers or members of one functional group?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the facts a simplified explanation keeps. Bonds held: the seven typed relations, of which `→` sequence and `⋛` comparison are the ones most often dropped in a summary. No new primitive is available without import. The transmission chain has four admitted domains, from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 1. Anchor: the receiver's ground, Fig 2 :29.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the unit of loss?

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

A simplification can preserve every fact and still change the account, because the relation between the facts is the part that carries the prediction. So the unit of loss is the **bond**, not the atom. This resolves an apparent puzzle in the question: two explanations can be shorter and still distort more, if the shortening removed a relation rather than a redundancy. Conversely a shorter account can be more accessible if it removed a redundant spelling while keeping the bond.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a documented set of cases shows that removing a typed relation never changes a receiver's prediction or uncertainty judgment against the ground, the bond is not the unit of loss here and F3 of `01` is refuted.

### Q2 - Are two simplified accounts isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Take two short accounts of the same topic, A and B.

- If A and B keep the same set of facts but join them with different relations, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. A receiver who acts on A and a receiver who acts on B will make different predictions, and the simplified text will not show why.
- If A and B share a common structure, for example "a cause, a mechanism, an effect", but use different facts in the slots, they are members of one **functional group** under Fig 12 :124. The group says what kind of account it is; the qualifier says which one.
- If A and B share no structure at all, they are not comparable by this test. Calling one a simplification of the other would be an adopted import, not a derivation (Fig 8 :50).

This is the analogue the question needs. "Simplified" does not name one kind of object. Two simplifications with the same words can be isomers, and the difference that matters is precisely the one the flat notation cannot show.

**Falsifier:** if two accounts with identical atom sets and different bonds are shown to produce identical receiver residuals on every slot under a ground test, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3 - Which structure is the simplified account carrying?

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A reusable method is a catalyst in Fig 12's sense: it survives its own use. A recording of one occasion is a spent part; it can be replayed but does not regenerate. This is the structural reason F2 of `01` separates methods from recordings. It also gives a test: ask whether the simplified account can be used twice on different cases without being re-derived. If not, it is a recording.

**Falsifier:** a recorded conclusion that survives repeated use on genuinely new cases with no new derivation would refute the catalyst distinction.

### Q4 - What order do the links fail in?

Fig 3 :19:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The dependency order is perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to learn and carry (Fig 3). Applied to transmission:

1. **Perception.** Can the receiver tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before? Fails when the simplified account erases the distinction the decision depends on.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the receiver have terms for what the situation affords? Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* This is the link the question is really about.
3. **Objective.** Can the receiver say what would count as progress here, in its own terms? This is where a clarity metric wrongly installed as a verdict does its damage.
4. **Planning.** Can the receiver form a route under incomplete information? Fails when the uncertainty structure was dropped.
5. **Learn and carry.** Can the receiver name what worked, verify it, and carry it? Fails when no ground reading is available.

A break between links 3 and 4 is the common case: the receiver can say the account is clear and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it. Effort spent improving link 1 (more vivid graphics) cannot rescue a break at link 3. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

**Falsifier:** if improving an upstream link measurably lowers per-slot residual when the downstream link is demonstrably broken, the dependency order does not govern this chain and Fig 3 has been misapplied.

### Q5 - Union or partition of the two conditions?

Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Accessibility and distortion overlap in the transmission channel D2. A single lever that fixes one can leave the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds. Fig 9 :57: *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* So the answer must be two conditions kept separate, never one score with a threshold. What remains after one lever is applied is *"categorically different"* (Fig 9 :58), and that is the part worth describing.

**Falsifier:** one intervention that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue would refute the union reading.

### Q6 - The comparison is a convention here, not a verdict

Fig 2 :41:

> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Two receivers who read the same simplified account and agree on what it means have succeeded at a convention. That is a real product and it is not corroboration of the account's truth. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies agreement about the world when the frames share an evidence pool. The order matters: settle the convention first, then range each frame separately against the ground.

**Falsifier:** a case where shared-meaning agreement also settles the ground question without any independent anchor would refute this separation.

## CONCLUSION

The unit a simplification loses is the bond, not the atom. Two simplifications over the same atoms with different bonds are isomers, and the difference is decisive in behaviour and invisible in the flat text. A reusable method is a catalyst and survives its use; a recording does not. The chain fails in dependency order, most often between vocabulary and objective, so better wording cannot rescue a missing ground link. The two conditions in the question form a union, not a partition, and audience agreement settles a convention rather than a verdict.

This framing rescues what is defensible in ordinary science communication advice without naming an optimum: the isomer and catalyst tests need no new primitive and no claim about the best reading level.
