## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The field has moved past one-way transfer.** The information deficit model assumes a knowledge deficit can be fixed by giving more information. The Wikipedia summary states that many studies challenge it because it ignores cognitive, social and affective factors. Prediction here: adding information does not by itself lower receiver R; the receiver's Γ_R must be in the description. **Corroborates** F7 and the D3 census line. Orientation only because the entry carries a style warning.
2. **Measures of understanding are not interchangeable.** The public awareness of science article separates factual knowledge, self-reported familiarity, structural knowledge and trust, and cites Ladwig 2012 for the claim that factual and perceived knowledge do not reflect the same underlying structures. Prediction: a clarity or familiarity rating cannot witness accessibility. **Corroborates** F4 and F5. This is the closest world source to the question's own field.
3. **Smooth explanation can inflate felt understanding.** Rozenblit and Keil's 12 studies find that people rate their own understanding lower after trying to explain a mechanism. Prediction: a compact explanation can leave per-slot R high while a comprehension rating looks good. **Corroborates** F1, F4 and F5. Low overlap with the figures, independent witness.
4. **The same simplification has opposite effects on different receivers.** The expertise reversal effect, quoted from Kalyuga et al. 2003, says guidance essential for novices can harm more experienced learners. Prediction: accessibility is a property of receiver and habitat, not of the text. **Corroborates** F7. Low overlap.
5. **The representation can be the obstacle, not the mind.** Gigerenzer's natural frequencies let people make correct Bayesian inferences without outside help. Prediction: a representation matched to the receiver's inference lowers R without adding content. **Corroborates** F2 and F7. Low overlap.
6. **The expert misreads the receiver.** The curse of knowledge says a person with specialised knowledge assumes others share it. Prediction: the simplifier aims at the wrong slots. **Corroborates** F4 and F7. Low overlap.
7. **A shared simplified object can coordinate without consensus.** Star and Griesemer's boundary object is plastic locally and robust enough to keep a common identity across sites. Galison's trading zone has groups agreeing on rules of exchange while ascribing different significance. Prediction: a simplified common object enables coordination (accessibility) while carrying different local meanings (distortion if treated as identical). **Corroborates** F3 and the Fig 2 :41 convention rule. Low overlap, two distinct sources from sociology of science and history of science.
8. **All simplifications are wrong; some are useful.** Box and Draper's aphorism makes usefulness a question about the ground, not about elegance. Prediction: the "wrongness" must be reported as R_T. **Corroborates** F1. Low overlap, statistics.
9. **An honest simplification marks itself as incomplete.** Lie-to-children is deliberately a simplification used in teaching, with an explicit warning that the model is not the truth and a progression toward a better model. Kastens and Chayes add the criteria that the teacher must understand the truth beyond the simplification and have a learning progression. Prediction: the mark and the progression are the world analogue of reporting R_T. **Corroborates** F2 and F6. Low overlap.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :25** - *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full derivation before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28** - *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the verdict; (3) then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula only if they ask for a method to reuse.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` Direction: a frame cannot check what it is looking for, because the sought shape is not a term it holds. Each user writes down what would refute their result before running it, and labels the mode of what they get.
