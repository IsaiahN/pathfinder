# Outreach draft - check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive a read by someone outside this derivation. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about simplification before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** when an explanation is simplified, what happens to the residual at the receiver, measured per claim and per decision against the ground, rather than as one comprehension average?
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a simplification makes knowledge accessible when it does four things at once. (1) It hands over a reusable method, not just the conclusion of one occasion. (2) It keeps the relations between the facts, because two accounts with the same facts in a different arrangement behave differently and the simplified notation cannot show the difference. (3) It reports what the coarser description cannot hold, the round-trip gap, instead of presenting the short version as lossless. (4) It lowers the receiver's error per claim against a ground the receiver can still reach. Distortion is what you get when one of those is missing and a frame-internal metric such as clarity, coverage or clicks reports success anyway.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a short explanation is always worse, that any particular simplification is the best one for your audience, or that this derivation settles the science communication literature. It does not name an optimum. The framework returns a shape and a check, not the answer for your case.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. One scalar measure of accessibility accounts for both the benefit and the distortion, with no per-claim residual and no round-trip gap left over.
> 2. A simplified conclusion reliably produces correct new predictions for a receiver who holds no method and whose unexplained remainder measures zero.
> 3. Removing the relations between facts leaves predictions and uncertainty judgments unchanged across a documented set of cases.
> 4. A frame-internal clarity or credibility metric is shown to be the anchor that settles receiver error at the ground.
> 5. The same simplification produces the same per-claim result for receivers whose prior knowledge measurably differs.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Journal of Science Communication, public author guidelines and submission route: https://jcom.sissa.it/site/authors
> - PCST Network, the global network for science communication: https://www.pcst.network/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device (seat map rule, carried in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4). |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` - *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Per-slot residual, not an average | Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* |
| Round trip reported, not hidden | Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* |
| Relations kept, not just facts | Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* |
| No clarity or click metric as a verdict | Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* |
| No optimum named | Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* |
| No request for trust or prestige | *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (essay source, not a figure quote). |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` Direction: a frame cannot check what it is looking for, so the sought shape must be written down as an ordinary term. |

**Stage order.** (1) The claim and its five refuters. (2) Ask what they would count as a failure and record their verdict before any theory is shown. (3) Only then send the links. (4) If they want the method, send the formula with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
