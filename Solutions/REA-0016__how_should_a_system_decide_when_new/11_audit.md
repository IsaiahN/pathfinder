## Independent domain census (written from the residual line alone)

I redid the census before relying on the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the decision of what to do with a mismatch between a learned belief and new evidence. Arity: unary per object slot, so a set of per-slot verdicts rather than one scalar. Symmetry: asymmetric, because the frame ranges against the anchor and not the reverse. Scale: closes within one scale; promotion across scales is a separate step. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
- **Domain 1, the controller.** Actors: b, w, a, o-prime, R per slot, candidate terms. Currency: residual and cost. Anchor: the ground via the observation, the only metric (Fig 1 :23).
- **Domain 2, the library and reach.** Actors: gamma, atoms(gamma), closure(gamma), a refusal term, dependent derivations. Currency: reachability and settlement. Anchor: derivation itself (Fig 6 :44).
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: ground maintainer, outside frames, records. Currency: settlement and independence. Anchor: the immovable ground (Fig 2 :29). In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Domain 4, direction.** Actors: the sought-for shape as an unbound preference; the pre-registered prediction. Currency: a residual the preference does not accrue. Anchor: the prediction, only if written before the run. Unbound, it is not a domain, which is the Fig 5 :61-68 limit.

Question type: a union compound with a dependency order, not a partition. Order by dependency: perceive, route, rank, mint or refuse, settle, promote (Fig 3 :19-20; Formula steps 1 to 6).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. I derived from Fig 1 per-slot indexing and Fig 3 dependency order; the primary derived from Fig 5's fifth bin and Fig 3. Different starting figures, same boundaries. That is weak agreement within one framework, not independent corroboration.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things in the shorthand do not survive the trip back down without extra instruments:

- The meaning of "persists and does not trend down" needs a per-term residual trace and a estimator; the figures supply the quantity, not its estimator.
- The difference between a mint and a refusal must be written into the record, or the isomer distinction of `06` Q3 collapses to "term added".
- The both-settled case (Fig 6 :47) has no figure decision, so an implementation must either leave it open or author a convention, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a bound term whose residual persists flat should be refused by addition, with the anchor fixed, and the direction written down first. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
