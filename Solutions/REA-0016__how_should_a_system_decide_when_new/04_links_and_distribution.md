## 2. Wider literature (secondary corroboration only)

These were read in low-overlap fields: formal philosophy, cognitive/social psychology, neuroscience, machine learning. None overrules a figure. Agreement is retrodiction after the derivation, not a forward test.

- **Formal epistemology, belief revision.** Sven Ove Hansson, "Logic of Belief Revision", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 2006, substantive revision 2026, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/. Quote: "The epistemic agent should give up beliefs only when forced to do so, and should then give up as few of them as possible." Also "In belief contraction, the beliefs with the lowest entrenchment should be the ones that are most readily given up." Predicts minimal, ranked change; corroborates F2 and F4. Note the Levi identity, "K* p = (K÷ ¬p)+p": revision is contraction by the negation followed by addition, structurally close to the figure's add-a-refusal mechanism (F3). High independence from the framework, so useful; but it addresses rational postulates, not the per-slot trigger.
- **Formal epistemology, Bayesianism.** "Bayesian Epistemology", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 2022, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-bayesian/. Quote: "no credence change is permitted without new evidence" (the old-evidence objection to strict conditionalization). Predicts that a bare "change on surprise" rule is incomplete without a trigger; corroborates F2's support gate and F8's open case. Corroborates, does not decide.
- **Cognitive and social psychology of belief.** Matthew Tyler Boden, Howard Berenbaum and James J Gross, "Why Do People Believe What They Do? A Functionalist Perspective", *Review of General Psychology* 20(4):399-411, 2016, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824436/. Quote: "Inaccurate and falsified beliefs persist even when people have the resources required for accuracy." Predicts that flat, persisting residuals are the diagnostic signal, and that a belief serving a hedonic function will not update on accuracy grounds alone (a structural "interest in the reading", comparable to Seat Map :171). Corroborates F2 and the stake rule.
- **Confirmation bias.** Raymond S. Nickerson, "Confirmation Bias: A Ubiquitous Phenomenon in Many Guises", *Review of General Psychology* 2(2):175-220, 1998, https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175. Quote (abstract): "Confirmation bias, as the term is typically used in the psychological literature, connotes the seeking or interpreting of evidence in ways that are partial to existing beliefs, expectations, or a hypothesis in hand." Predicts that the target of a refusal may be the frame's preferred direction rather than the wrong belief; corroborates F6.
- **Neuroscience, prediction error.** Wolfram Schultz, "Dopamine reward prediction error coding", *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience* 18(1):23-32, 2016, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4826767/. Quote: "if the reward is exactly as predicted (blue), there is no prediction error, and we keep our prediction and behavior unchanged; we learn nothing." Also "If the reward is different from its prediction, a prediction error exists, and we should update the prediction and change our behavior." Predicts an error-driven update with a zero-error no-change condition; corroborates the per-slot residual and the support gate (F2, Fig 1 :23). Independent field, strong corroboration of the shape, not of the framework.
- **Machine learning, catastrophic forgetting.** German I. Parisi, Ronald Kemker, Jose L. Part, Christopher Kanan and Stefan Wermter, "Continual Lifelong Learning with Neural Networks: A Review", *Neural Networks*, 2019 (arXiv:1802.07569), https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07569. Quote: "the continual acquisition of incrementally available information from non-stationary data distributions generally leads to catastrophic forgetting or interference." Predicts that naive updating overwrites older knowledge; corroborates F3 (add, do not delete) and F4 (price the change). Independent field.
- **Machine learning, protecting important weights.** James Kirkpatrick et al., "Overcoming catastrophic forgetting in neural networks", *PNAS*, 2017, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1611835114 (page returned 403 on direct fetch; content was read via the indexed full-text result and the abstract is also at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28292907). Quote: "Our approach remembers old tasks by selectively slowing down learning on the weights important for those tasks." Predicts per-slot, importance-weighted protection of prior beliefs; corroborates Fig 1 :19 and F2. Marked as partially verified because of the 403.

No world source conflicts with a figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. If a conflict appeared, it would send us back to the domain census, never flip a verdict.

## 3. Distribution rules: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Do not send the architecture before the recipient has given a verdict on the decision rule.
- **Fig 10 :52:** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the mismatch, the five bins and the refuter.
- **Fig 2 :45:** "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Ask what would count as a failure, not what they think of the theory.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Describe the residual before choosing a venue or a reader.
- **Fig 2 :40:** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Seat Map :163:** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." No receptiveness ranking, no bulk send.
- **Fig 4 :22:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Stage 3 sends a method (the formula), not an answer.

## 4. The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the decision rule, its mode (`specified`), the per-slot trigger, the persistent-and-flat ranking, the add-a-refusal mechanism, the fixed-anchor condition and the four pre-registered refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the four framework links in section 1. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction to write a refuter first and state a mode. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question (Fig 5 :61-66), so each user must bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 rule survives. The honest open case (Fig 6 :47, a term and its refusal both settled) must be included, not hidden.
