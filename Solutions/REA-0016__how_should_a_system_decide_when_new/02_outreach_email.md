# Outreach draft: validate the decision rule first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter to a public submission through the venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output below is a `specified` decision rule with a pre-registered refuter. If a recipient shows that the rule fires on a residual that is trending down while another bound term persists flat, there is nothing else to look at. Send the rule, not the framework.

---

**Subject:** one checkable claim about when evidence should change a belief

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about belief updating. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A system holds beliefs. New evidence arrives. The claim is that a belief should change only on a particular route, and that the route is already named in a figure set I am testing:
>
> 1. Measure the mismatch per object slot, not as a global average. A global error near zero with one live slot is a legal state. (Fig 1 :19, :22)
> 2. Sort the mismatch into five bins. Four sort it by what is MISSING. The fifth sorts it by what is WRONG: "a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so" (Fig 5 :53). Only the fifth licenses a belief change.
> 3. Choose the target by one recorded quantity: among terms currently bound and predicting, the one whose residual "PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN" (Fig 5 :58-60), read with the opposite sign from the shrinking test that asks which residual is improving.
> 4. Change it by adding a refusal, not by deleting the belief. "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." (Fig 6 :43)
> 5. Hold the anchor fixed. "The anchor must not update." (Fig 2 :33) If the measurement moves with the frames, that is coherent drift, not correction.
>
> The rule is a shape and a decision procedure, not a measurement. I am claiming it is `specified`, not that it is settled by experiment.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that this is a new Bayesian or AGM result. Belief revision and continual learning already have strong accounts of minimal change and of protecting important prior knowledge. The claim is narrower and structural: the fifth bin, the persistent-and-flat ranking, the add-a-refusal mechanism, and the fixed anchor together decide *when* and *which* belief, and the framework leaves open what happens when a term and its refusal are both settled (Fig 6 :47). I do not close that case.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. a belief change correctly described as deletion from the record, where the system can still tell never-held from held-and-withdrawn;
> 2. a system that changes a belief whose residual is trending down while another bound term's residual persists flat under the same ground;
> 3. a correction in which the measurement used to settle the belief was itself adjusted in response to the belief;
> 4. a frame that reliably refuses its own unstated search preference using only mechanism checks, with no prediction written down before the run.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a belief-update problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape it seeks is a preference, not a term it holds (Fig 5 :61-66). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]
