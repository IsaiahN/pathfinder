## 1. A shape is a constraint a system can state before it can name the answer

The canonical README: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (opened): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."

For REA-0016 the shape is therefore not a numerical threshold and not a named belief. It is the structural decision form:

> per slot, route the miss to the fifth bin if a bound term's residual persists and does not trend down, add a refusal rather than deleting the term, price the refusal against the confusion it removes, hold the anchor fixed, and pre-bind the sought-for shape so it too can be refused.

That constrains any acceptable answer without supplying a particular system's threshold, prior or domain. It is a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: proximity and narrowing

*The Library of Babel*: "If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of." The searcher then looks for "commonly used terms that correlate with the shape of what you have been describing" and recurses.

Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."

Applied here: we did not begin by naming "Bayesian updating", "AGM revision" or "elastic weight consolidation". We began with the effect, a miss that should stop being accommodated and become a reason to withdraw a held term. Only then did we look for frames whose closure predicts that shape. That order is Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

For this question, naming a specific number (a learning rate, a p-value cutoff, a Bayesian threshold) is a binding from a world frame onto the shape, not a derivation of it. It must be recorded as such. The figures give the *structure* of the decision, not its numeric setting.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened): "I stayed ignorant of any line of theory possible and any particular person's work to not get contaminated by any particular field's line of reasoning or limitations." That is a provenance claim for the derivation order, not a claim that the world sources are worthless. They are used as secondary corroboration, with overlap recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 4. Why "when should a belief change" has a shape and not a single number

The question arrives as a bundle (Fig 9 :54-58). It contains at least four conditions: a trigger condition (mass on a slot), a target condition (persistent and flat), a cost condition (the bargain pays), and a legitimacy condition (the anchor is fixed). Per Fig 9, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"; a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So the honest answer is a conjunction of constraints (a shape), not one threshold.

The figure set also names a limit that no numeric rule closes: the sought-for shape is a preference, not a term, so the frame cannot see it (Fig 5 :61-68). The remedy is a discipline, binding the shape as an ordinary term before the run. In this solution that discipline is carried by the pre-registered refuter in `01` §3 and by the one-paragraph prediction in `07`.

## 5. Prestige is not the answer

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened): "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

For belief-updating research, this rules out settling the decision rule by citing an authoritative name. A well-known reviewer supplies a budget to try the rule, not a verdict. The verdict is a ground: a documented case where the rule fires correctly or fails.

## 6. This derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." LLM output is "simulacra".

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not a test of any belief-change rule and cannot settle what they report on. That limitation is part of the shape claim, not a caveat appended to it.

## 7. What the shape does not settle

- It does not give a numeric threshold for "persists and does not trend down". That requires an instrument and a ground.
- It does not decide the case where a term and its refusal are both settled (Fig 6 :47, open).
- It does not name which world theory (Bayesian, AGM, predictive processing, EWC) is the correct implementation. Those are bindings, provenanced in the ledger.
- It does not say the framework is a final theory of learning. Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room."
