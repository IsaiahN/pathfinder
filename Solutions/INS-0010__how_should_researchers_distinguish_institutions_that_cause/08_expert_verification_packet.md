# Expert verification packet - the Tether on institutions and prosperity

**What this is:** a claim in a form an institutional economist, development economist or causal-inference researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the identity of the cause. Not `grounded`.** No country-level direction is asserted.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The observed association between an institution and a prosperity measure is symmetric, while "causes" and "arises as a consequence" are ordered, so no coefficient, significance level or ranking can assign direction alone. A valid direction check must import an asymmetry from a frame whose closure differs, range against an anchor whose value does not move with the prosperity outcome or with the preferred story, keep verdicts per institution-slot and per prosperity-slot, and carry a failure condition written in advance. The question is a union of direction, institution, prosperity measure, time scale and level, not one question with two opposite answers. The two causal stories are isomers over a fixed atom set, the feedback case is a cycle, and no figure line names a specific institution as the cause. What is offered is that shape and its refuters, not a winner in the institutions-versus-growth debate.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading the Operators table and Figure 12

- **Statement:** the association is a symmetric bond; the causal claim is an ordered bond; a symmetric statistic cannot choose the order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :15, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Operators table :33, "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Fig 12 :92, "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." Fig 12 :5, "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **What to check:** are the two bond entries correctly transcribed, and does the symmetry test actually separate a pooled coefficient from a directed claim?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a statistic computed solely from the joint distribution of I and P that entails a directive verdict with no added asymmetry, no import and a non-updating anchor. If it exists, C1 fails.

### C2 - identification-facing; checkable by an econometrician against the instruments literature

- **Statement:** the arrow cannot be minted from the association; it must be imported from a differing closure with provenance, a shadow test and an anchor that does not update.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23, "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32, "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 8 :51, "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 2 :33, "The anchor must not update."
- **What to check:** does the exclusion assumption of a candidate instrument (settler mortality, a language-share instrument, a randomised evaluation) do the work the figure calls an import, and is its independence from current prosperity argued rather than assumed? Pearl 2009 states the statistical version: a causal assumption "is not testable in observational studies."
- **Refuter:** derive a direction discriminator purely from the joint distribution of I and P with no added assumption and no import; or exhibit an institutional measure invariant to current prosperity and to the preferred theory that still predicts later prosperity out of sample. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - habitat and method; checkable by an institutional economist against cases

- **Statement:** verdicts must be per slot, no standing list of good institutions survives a change of residual, and the feedback case is a cycle that a chain cannot represent.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :49, "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 12 :121, "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What to check:** can a single pooled verdict survive the slot reversal the literature itself reports (property rights raising investment while entrenching exclusion), and can a linear estimate represent a signed feedback loop?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate one aggregate direction verdict invariant across every institution and prosperity slot, including sign reversals, or show that a chain estimate does represent the loop sign. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that institutions do not matter. The claim is about assigning direction, not about effect existence.
- Not that any named institution is the cause. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The slot is a `gap`, not silence and not an invitation to name a favourite.
- Not that AJR 2001 refutes Glaeser et al. 2004, or the reverse. Different anchors, different measures; the disagreement supports the union reading.
- Not that instruments are invalid. They are imports that need provenance and a declared exclusion assumption. Fig 6 :24: "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."
- Not that a significance threshold or a coefficient size is a verdict. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour-meter "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Anchor test:** does the candidate instrument's value stay invariant to current prosperity and to the preferred theory, while predicting later prosperity out of sample?
2. **Production test:** was the prosperity outcome absent in the same form before the institution fired, and present after, under a counterfactual contrast? Fig 12 :74 and Operators :33.
3. **Shadow test:** does the imported frame explain the gap the association left, or only rename it? Fig 8 :51.
4. **Loop-sign test:** if both directions hold, does the cycle settle or run away? Fig 12 :121.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Operators table :15 and :33, Fig 12 :92 and :117, Fig 6 :23, Fig 2 :33, and Fig 11 :49-51. Seven short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own knowledge of the institutions-and-growth regression literature: does a pooled coefficient carry any direction information on its own?
3. Name one instrument you know whose exclusion assumption you would be willing to defend in public, or say why none is defensible. That is the import with its source gate.
4. Give a case where the same institution changed sign when the prosperity measure changed. That is the valence test.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
