# What "shape" means - canonical reading from the eight essays

**Sources.** Eight essays by Isaiah N. Three were opened directly this session and are quoted from the fetched text: (1) https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source , (2) https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-recursive-self-improvement-trap , (3) https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-library-of-babel-locating-the . Five are carried from the completed exemplar's canonical mapping and marked [carried]: *How Intelligence Actually Works*, *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, *How a Society of Agents Would Work*, *They're Scanning Books When They Should*, *Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether*. The governing essay for this question is (1), the source-discrimination essay.

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 6 :26: "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, a shape is: an asymmetry imported from a differing closure, an anchor that does not update, per-slot direction verdicts, a provenance record and a refuter written in advance. "Institutions cause prosperity" is a name. "Here is what any valid direction check must look like" is a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." [fetched directly]
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* [carried]: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *The Library of Babel* on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." The paradox is that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it." [fetched directly]

For this question the effect description is not "institution" and not "prosperity", both are borrowed words from a literature whose measures the framework did not build. It is: "an unassigned direction between two jointly observed quantities, where the observation is symmetric and the claim is ordered." That description travels to a statistics frame (Pearl), to an experimental frame (J-PAL), and to a natural-experiment frame (AJR), each of which already holds a term for the missing asymmetry.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test. Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* [carried]: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

So an institutional index is a visible guess, not a discovery of nature's own list of causes. Naming one index as the source of prosperity turns a visible choice into an invisible one. The same applies to a candidate instrument: its exclusion assumption is the searcher's contribution and must be recorded as such (Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged.").

## 4. The governing essay: source discrimination

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism that decides this question. [fetched directly]

> "Here's the real problem in grounded terms: What if you have metrics that do not discriminate about its inputs? The Problem: You can have several underlying causes that create identical readings but the definition of the output would be read as the same. If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."

The colour-meter example: the instrument "is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered, all give the same reading. And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." The resolution came "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument": the spectrometer.

Mapped here: a cross-country correlation between institutional quality and income is the reading. Its possible sources include institutions producing income, income producing institutional scores, human capital producing both, geography operating through institutions, and colonial history operating through settlement. The reading does not discriminate among them. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument about the coefficient. Pearl's formal result is the statistical version of the same claim.

This is also why F1 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is structural rather than a complaint about data volume: a green reading stays green however many times you read it.

## 5. This question is a bundle, and the bundle is visible in the essay

*The Library of Babel* [fetched directly]:

> "If the final book in your exhaustive search doesn't answer the question, then there are only two possibilities: the nature of your question was unanswerable, or malformed. Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

Fig 9 :55 says the same in the figure language: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The question "do institutions cause prosperity or the reverse" bundles direction, institution-slot, prosperity-slot, time scale and level. The five are not variants of one question. They are overlapping conditions, so one lever clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57). The honest response is to unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).

## 6. Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any institution in this answer. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the frame's library. "Property rights", "courts" and "schooling" are already in the institutional-economics library, so naming one as a candidate cause is a **binding** from that frame onto an atom the frame already holds, not the invention of a seventh term. Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." The binding adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :26).

What remains wrong is treating the binding as though the framework had selected it. No figure line selects which institution occupies the cause slot. The `gap` in `01` F10 survives for that reason, not because naming is forbidden. Both statements are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* [fetched directly]: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof. Someone let reputation substitute for result. Someone let the trusted name validate the untested claim." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A list of famous economists selected for their agreement about institutions is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give the discriminator a budget to be tried and let the ground - an instrument, a dataset, a natural experiment - settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So every substantive file in this folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
