**Question:** How should researchers distinguish institutions that cause prosperity from those that arise as a consequence of it?

**Source:** `institutional questions.txt` line 13. **Section:** 1. Institutional origins and development.

**Mode:** `specified` on the separator and on each per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the identity of any named institution as the cause, not `grounded`. **Status:** done. **Registry status:** pending.

**Boundary.** Nothing in this folder is in the Tether repository. No repo file was read for the purpose of editing and no file outside this solution folder was changed. ## Restating the question as an effect

We observe an institution I and a prosperity measure P together. We want a procedure that, applied to a case, tells us which of four things is true: I produces P; P produces I; both form a loop; or a third thing produces both. Each answer leads somewhere different. If I produces P, reform the institution. If P produces I, wait for growth and education. If both, expect a feedback loop with a sign that can close or run away. If a common cause produces both, neither lever moves the other. What an answer would look like is a **direction discriminator**: a check that supplies an asymmetry not contained in the observed association, ranges against an anchor that does not move with the researcher's preferred story, returns a verdict per institution-slot and per prosperity-slot, and carries a refuter written before the run. Binding that ordinary term is the framework's "Direction" discipline, so the thing being searched for can accrue a residual and be refused like any other term.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that institutions cause prosperity.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list of readers chosen for likely agreement is a mirror.
2. **Correlation has valence.** Fig 2 :39-41: it is "efficient for a convention" and "disqualifying for a verdict." Selecting recipients by receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The two-stage offer exists so a reader's verdict on the discriminator is recorded before any theory is shown.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
