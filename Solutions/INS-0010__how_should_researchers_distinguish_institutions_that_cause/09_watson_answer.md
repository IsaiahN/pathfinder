## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what follows from observing institutions I and prosperity P together. Option A: treat a positive, significant association as evidence that institutions cause prosperity. Option B: treat the same association as evidence that prosperity produces better institutions. Option C: return a separator that says what any valid direction check must contain, and refuses to choose between A and B until a ground settles it. What each leads to: A reforms an institution that may be an effect; B waits for growth that may not produce the institution; C returns a per-slot shape, a provenance requirement and a refuter, and leaves the cause slot open.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The unassigned direction between I and P. Arity: two variables plus at least one candidate common cause (geography, disease environment, human capital, colonial history), so three or more. Symmetry: the observation is symmetric, the claim is ordered. Scale: cross-country panels, within-country time series, sub-national regions and experimental margins, so scale is a slot index. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **Domain 1, the observational / statistical frame.** Actors: variables I and P, covariates, coefficients, standard errors. Currency: association, variance explained, prediction. Anchor: none inside the frame, because Fig 1 :27 says such quantities "are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Not yet a domain until an anchor is imported. Question type: a convention, not a verdict.
- **Domain 2, the identification frame.** Actors: instruments, natural experiments, discontinuities, random assignment, temporal order, process traces. Currency: exogenous variation and a counterfactual contrast. Anchor: a source of variation that does not move with P or with the preferred theory. Question type: import; the arrow is not in the association's closure.
- **Domain 3, the institutional habitat.** Actors: rules, rulers, citizens, policies, and conditions in contact with them. Currency: persistence of rules and contact with material outcomes. Anchor: outcomes that do not move because a researcher prefers a story. Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- **Domain 4, the verification / review frame.** Actors: reviewers, seminar audiences, replicators. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Five overlapping conditions: direction, institution-slot, prosperity-slot, time scale, level. One lever clears the region where only direction binds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive each slot, build vocabulary for what each institution affords, bind the objective (the direction discriminator), choose an identification frame, carry the method (Fig 3 :20; Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: the AJR versus Glaeser et al. disagreement was first read as a world/figure conflict. Re-census assigns the two papers to different anchors and different institutional measures, so they are two frames, not one contradiction. The conflict rule returned to the census; the verdict was not flipped. One live strain remains (S1, an instrument's exclusion assumption is not testable from the distribution alone) and is carried as the price of the import.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The separator survives; no named cause does. A symmetric association cannot assign an ordered direction, so researchers must import an asymmetry from a differing closure with provenance and a shadow test, range it against an anchor that does not update with prosperity or with the preferred story, keep verdicts per institution and prosperity slot, and write a refuter before the run. The question is a union of five overlapping conditions, not a two-option partition. The two causal stories are isomers over a fixed atom set, the feedback case is a cycle a chain cannot close, and no standing list of good institutions survives a change of residual. Which institution occupies the cause slot is a `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind. This is `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, ungrounded candidate throughout.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the two-stage order is kept. The packet states its mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording (a fitted estimate and a list of agreeable economists) instead of a generator (the separator and its refuter). Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is avoidable by leading with the separator.
- **undoable:** yes. The named-cause temptation is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A published separator can be superseded by a corrected one, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A refutation is added as a negation, not silently erased (Operators table :58), so the reach falls safely rather than the record being rewritten.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument showing that a candidate measure is invariant to current prosperity and still predicts later prosperity out of sample would satisfy refuter 2; a statistic that assigns direction from the joint distribution alone would satisfy refuter 1; a harness that reproduces direction without an import would satisfy refuter 3; a verbatim figure line naming a cause would close the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
