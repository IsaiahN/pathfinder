# Outreach draft - check the discriminator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the two-stage order only works if the stage-1 object is checkable on its own. The discriminator below is a shape with a written refuter, not an estimate. If you add a result for a particular country before the verdict is recorded, the offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One shape claim to check: assigning the direction between institutions and prosperity

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the observed association between an institution and a prosperity measure is symmetric, while "causes" and "arises as a consequence of" are ordered relations. A symmetric reading cannot choose an ordered bond. So no coefficient, significance level or cross-country ranking, taken alone, assigns a direction. What a direction check needs is four things: an asymmetry imported from a frame whose evidence differs from the association's own frame, an anchor whose value does not move with the prosperity outcome or with the researcher's preferred story, a verdict stated per institution and per prosperity measure rather than pooled, and a failure condition written in advance. The framework's own terms for the first and second are "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." (Fig 6 :23) and "The anchor must not update." (Fig 2 :33).
>
> The question as posed is also a bundle, not one question: which direction, which institution, which prosperity measure, which time scale, which level. Fig 9 says "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." One lever will not clear the intersections, so the output is a separator with per-slot verdicts, not a global answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named institution (property rights, education, electoral rules, courts) is *the* cause. The framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). Which institution occupies the cause slot in a given place is for the ground to settle, not for the figures. I am also not claiming that a published instrument settles the matter: an imported asymmetry needs provenance and a shadow test, because "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" (Fig 8 :51).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
>
> 1. A statistic computed only from the joint distribution of the institution and prosperity variables that assigns a direction with no added asymmetry, no import and a non-updating anchor.
> 2. An institutional measure whose value is invariant both to current prosperity and to the researcher's preferred theory, while still predicting later prosperity out of sample.
> 3. A purely observational or synthetic harness that reproduces the direction verdict without importing an asymmetry from a differing frame. Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
> 4. A verbatim figure line that names a specific institution as the cause of prosperity.
> 5. A single aggregate direction verdict that stays the same across every institution and prosperity measure, including the ones whose sign reverses in the literature.
> 6. A fixed significance threshold or coding rule that decides a causal outcome while still counting as a neutral convention. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong in that respect and I will record the correction in the public retraction log. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Check Fig 2 :29/:33/:39-43, Fig 6 :21/:23, Fig 8 :32/:45/:51, Fig 9 :27-28/:32/:55-57, Fig 11 :43/:49/:51/:70, Fig 12 :5/:92/:94, Operators table :15/:33/:58.
> - Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson 2001, "The Colonial Origins of Comparative Development," *American Economic Review*: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.91.5.1369
> - Glaeser, La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes and Shleifer 2004, "Do Institutions Cause Growth?": https://www.nber.org/papers/w10568
> - Pearl 2009, "Causal inference in statistics: An overview": https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistics-surveys/volume-3/issue-none/Causal-inference-in-statistics-An-overview/10.1214/09-SS057.short
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local framework file `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a question you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape being sought is not a term it holds. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for reading this far.
>

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | The author's seat-reasoning rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. Also *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| No named institution asserted as the cause | Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The cause slot is a `gap`, not silence, and not an invitation to invent a primitive. |
| Union, not one question | Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." |
| Instrument provenance before verdict weight | Fig 8 :51: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. Fig 6 :24 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives. |
| Anchor test applied to the instrument | Fig 2 :29/:33 and Fig 2 :34: if the anchor updates, the panel becomes mutual update and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices. |
| Figure glyphs and lines only, checked verbatim | Every quoted string above was checked against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. |
| No request for prestige or trust | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not ask for endorsement. |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." |
| Links come after the verdict | Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). |
