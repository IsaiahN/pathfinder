# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four bindings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7 are written down. The output must survive a check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on stable surface water, and the failure condition I wrote first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **Question in effect terms:** how often do planets keep liquid surface water at the surface over a geological interval? The framework cannot produce that number. It can say what any answer must look like, and it can say which part is owed by a measurement rather than by a theory. That is the output I am offering.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the structure, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The question is a union of at least four conditions, not one frequency: reference class, a test for surface liquid water, a duration, and a maintenance condition. A single lever such as orbital position clears only the cases where one condition binds. The intersections survive.
> 2. Persistence over a gigayear is a cycle, not a chain. A chain cannot close by construction, so an orbital address alone runs down. What closes the loop is a feedback with resupply, for example carbonate-silicate weathering supplied by tectonics.
> 3. Inventory is not enough. Two planets with the same water, rock and carbon can differ in phase and arrangement, and the difference is decisive for stability but invisible in a bulk composition list.
> 4. The numerical frequency is therefore unreachable from the framework as it stands. That is a verdict, not a silence. It is owed by a survey plus an instrument that can see the reservoir, under a definition fixed in advance.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework gives a percentage, that being in a habitable zone is the same as having stable surface water, or that any published occurrence rate is an answer to this question. The published numbers I have read use different radius bands, instellation ranges and completeness assumptions, so they are different objectives, not competing estimates of one quantity.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. derive a numerical frequency from the six-term substrate and bonds alone, with no survey, instrument or external catalog;
> 2. show one lever that clears all four bundled conditions at once, so the union reading is wrong;
> 3. show a planet that holds liquid surface water for a gigayear with no closing feedback loop and no resupply;
> 4. show that bulk water fraction alone predicts persistence without dependence on phase or arrangement.
>
> If any holds, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** what would you count as a failure of this shape claim? I am recording your verdict on this output before showing you any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The architecture and formula: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The architecture essay warns that LLM outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula," so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
