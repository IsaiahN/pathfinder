## 0. The residual in effect terms

R = the gap between what a population-level frequency claim predicts and what a survey plus a maintenance analysis actually returns. The question asks for f = N(planets with stable surface water over interval T) / N(planets in reference class C). Four quantities are unresolved by the question as written: C, the test for surface liquid water, the length of T, and whatever keeps the reservoir from being consumed, boiled off, or frozen before T elapses.

**Direction, written as an ordinary term before the run:** an answer is answerable only when C, the water test, T, and a maintenance condition are bound; the figures constrain the maintenance condition structurally and cannot supply f. This term is allowed to accrue a residual like anything else (section 5 refutes it).

---

## 1. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** f is a ratio of two counts, so at least two quantities. The numerator is itself a conjunction of four conditions: water present, at the surface, liquid and stable, persistent over T. The denominator requires a defined reference class. Arity is therefore compound, not a single count.
- **Symmetry.** Counting planets is symmetric under permutation of the planets, which is the `+` bond: *"both hold at once"* and *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No"* (Operators table :11-15). But persistence over T introduces order and repetition: a reservoir that survives is a loop, not a list. Under a symmetric bond k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92), so listing the four conditions in different orders does not change what is being counted. Order enters only with the cyclic maintenance condition (Fig 12 :121). Confusing these two is the flat-formula error.
- **Scale.** One planet over gigayears (the climate scale) and an ensemble of planets (the survey scale). Two scales mean a membrane and a round-trip gap (Fig 4 :27-32). A one-planet climate result cannot be carried up to a frequency without losing information, and the gap must be measured, not assumed.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one frequency (decisive for the method)

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The numerator of f bundles water presence, surface location, liquid phase, persistence and the reference class. The Library of Babel essay (opened this session) names the same failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* Unbundle into D1 through D4, answer each, then recombine. Mode `general`. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 9 plus essay, low overlap between a composition rule and a search-narrative rule).

**Refuter:** exhibit a single lever that raises the count to the same degree in every condition, including the maintenance condition. Then the union reading is wrong and a single frequency is licit.

### F2 - No figure line yields the number: this is a gap for f, not a silence about it

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* Fig 8 :40-41: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."*

Six terms and seven bonds compose structures; they do not enumerate a planet population. A frequency is an empirical count, so its φ* is not in closure(Γ). This is **underivable in principle from the figures**, not unreachable at this budget, so the instruction is OUTWARD: characterise R in effect terms and go to a frame whose closure predicts its shape (the astronomical and Earth-system frames in section 7). Importing an occurrence-rate number into the figure set would add no atom to the world and would mislabel a measurement as a derivation. Mode `general`. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 13).

**Refuter:** derive a numerical frequency from the six terms and bonds alone, with no survey, instrument, or external catalog. Until that is done, "gap for the number" stands.

### F3 - The anchor for a frequency is a counter and a measurement, not agreement

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

The ground here is an observation that does not move when a climate model or an occurrence-rate prior pushes on it. Agreement among models is a shared evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* So the witness set must include frames that do not draw on the same catalog or the same model family. Mode `general`. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 2 anchors plus the effective-sample-size rule carried in the Society of Agents essay).

**Refuter:** show a count that changes when a frame revises its prior yet still settles the question. That would make the count a frame and collapse Fig 2 panel 3 into panel 1.

### F4 - The habitat must be enumerated; the figure already lists the relevant conditions

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

The relevant list for stable surface water is not generated from the six terms. It is read off published planetary physics: stellar spectrum and luminosity, orbital distance and eccentricity, atmospheric CO2 and H2O, surface mineralogy, volcanic resupply, plate tectonic regime, ocean and mantle partitioning, and impact history. The figure's own condition list includes water and temperature; the cascade reaches plate tectonics and mineral resupply at second and third order, which is exactly where the world literature locates the problem (Coy, Kite and Graham 2025; Cowan and Abbot 2014, section 7). Mode `specified`. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 11 plus two Earth-system papers with different methods).

**Refuter:** derive the complete condition list a priori from the six terms and show that no condition outside them is load-bearing for surface water persistence. The world literature says otherwise, so this refuter is live and checkable.

### F5 - Persistence is a cycle, not a chain

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

A planet placed in a habitable zone is a chain: star, orbit, temperature, water. Chains cannot close by construction, so they run down. The carbonate-silicate weathering thermostat is Fig 12's cycle: warming increases weathering, which draws down CO2, which opposes the warming. The world source states it directly: *"Carbonate-silicate weathering feedback is thought to stabilize Earth's climate on geologic timescales"* (Coy, Kite and Graham 2025, arXiv:2507.23124). Without mineral resupply, *"the feedback's strength wanes, making a planet susceptible to catastrophic warming events or hard snowballs where the surface becomes frozen over"* (same source). So the property asked about is not an orbital address; it is a closed loop with a resupply term. Mode `specified`. Verdict **single-source for the figure definition**, corroborated by an Earth-system source with low overlap.

**Refuter:** exhibit a planet that keeps liquid surface water for a gigayear with a purely one-way reservoir (no recycling, no resupply, no feedback) and with no external replenishment. Under Fig 12 :121 this cannot happen by construction; if it is observed, the cycle claim is wrong.

### F6 - Arrangement is decisive and invisible in the inventory

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Two planets with the same bulk inventory, water, rock, carbon and atmosphere, can be a stable ocean world or a runaway greenhouse, depending on arrangement and bond. A survey that counts water-bearing planets by bulk composition commits the flat-formula error the figure names: the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not. Cowan and Abbot (2014, arXiv:1401.0720) report the physical version: *"waterworlds lack a silicate weathering thermostat so their climate is predicted to be less stable than that of planets with exposed continents."* Mode `specified`. Verdict **single-source for the figure definition**, with a physics-facing check still unmeasured for any specific exoplanet.

**Refuter:** show that bulk water fraction alone predicts gigayear stability with no dependence on phase, exposed land, or tectonic arrangement. The Cowan and Abbot result predicts this refuter will fire against the inventory-only claim.

### F7 - Definition breaks the dependency chain before instruments can help

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The operational definition of the numerator is the middle link (objective), and it is not fixed. Published estimates for the occurrence of rocky planets in habitable zones differ by construction: 11 +/- 4 percent for Earth-size planets receiving one to four times Earth's intensity (Petigura, Howard and Marcy 2013), 36 +/- 14 percent for habitable-zone occurrence from EPOS (Mulders, Pascucci, Apai and Ciesla 2018), and 0.37 to 0.60 planets per star for a conservative habitable zone with large uncertainties driven by the small number of detected small habitable-zone planets (Bryson et al 2021). These are not three answers to one question; they are three objectives. Better telescopes cannot rescue a broken objective link. Mode `specified`. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 3 plus three occurrence studies with explicitly different definitions).

**Refuter:** show one operational definition of "stable surface water over long periods" on which all three studies agree, and that their numbers then converge. They do not: the radius bands, instellation ranges and completeness assumptions differ.

### F8 - The cross-scale round trip has a nonzero gap R_T

Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

Carrying a one-planet climate result up to an ensemble rate discards the maintenance loop (duration, resupply, feedback) and keeps the orbital address. Bringing that rate back down to a claim about a particular planet loses the history that the planet actually retained water. R_T is nonzero and is reported qualitatively in `11_audit.md`. Treating an occurrence rate as if it were a direct count of stable-water planets sets R_T to zero and is the dead-reckoning error. Mode `general`. Verdict **single-source**.

**Refuter:** provide a sufficient statistic for a planet ensemble from which both the one-planet maintenance history and the ensemble frequency are recoverable. No such statistic is held here.

### F9 - Selection effects are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Kepler completeness and reliability, Gaia stellar properties and detection thresholds decide how much of the space is searched. They do not decide whether a planet has stable surface water. Bryson et al (2021) state that their occurrence bounds *"reflect two extreme assumptions about the extrapolation of completeness"* and that *"the large uncertainties are due to the small number of detected small HZ planets."* That is an honest report of a budget filter, not a verdict about frequency. Mode `general`. Verdict **triangulated** (Fig 9 plus the occurrence paper's own uncertainty statement).

**Refuter:** show a selection correction that changes the physical state of a planet rather than the estimated count. Until then the correction belongs in the budget column.

### F10 - A channel can go stale while the ground does not

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

Catalog counts change as surveys are processed and validated; the planets do not change with them. The NASA Exoplanet Archive showed 6,473 confirmed planets on 2026-11-19, a number that is a channel reading, not the ground. Any frequency derived from a catalog must carry its access date and completeness assumptions so the channel can be refreshed. Mode `general`. Verdict **single-source**.

**Refuter:** exhibit a planet population that changes size because a frame revised its catalog, with no corresponding physical change. That would make the catalog an anchor instead of a channel, contradicting Fig 10 :24.
