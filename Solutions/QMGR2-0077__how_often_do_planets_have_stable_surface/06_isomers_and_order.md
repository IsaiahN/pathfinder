## Q1 - Why persistence is a cycle and not a chain

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A planet with water, rock and sunlight is a chain if the reservoir is consumed without return. A chain cannot close by construction, so it terminates in freezing, evaporation, photodissociation or sequestration, whatever the duration T.
- The carbonate-silicate thermostat is a cycle: warming increases weathering, weathering draws down CO2, cooling reduces warming. Coy, Kite and Graham (2025, arXiv:2507.23124) report that without mineral resupply *"the feedback's strength wanes, making a planet susceptible to catastrophic warming events or hard snowballs where the surface becomes frozen over."*
- Cowan and Abbot (2014, arXiv:1401.0720) add the water-cycling loop: *"Plate tectonics transports water between these reservoirs on geological timescales."* Gilbert-Janizek et al (2026, PSJ) couple interior and atmosphere over 5 Gyr and report *"life may not be required to maintain long-term habitable surface conditions."*
- ⇒ The maintenance condition in the numerator is a **cycle**, and the world literature supplies its physical name and its load-bearing parts. Triangulated for the structure (Fig 12 plus three papers with different models); unmeasured for any specific exoplanet.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a planet observed with liquid surface water over a gigayear with no closing feedback, no recycling and no external resupply. If observed, the cycle claim fails.

## Q2 - Is a stable-water planet an isomer of an unstable one?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Two planets with the same bulk inventory (water, rock, carbon, nitrogen) but different phase and tectonic arrangement are **isomers** under the figure's definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Bulk composition is the flat formula that recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement.
- "Habitable zone" is not a member; it is a **functional group** in the figure's sense. It says what kind of orbital setup is relevant, not which member persists. The qualifier, resupply plus water cycling, says which member.
- Cowan and Abbot (2014) give the physical instance: waterworlds *"lack a silicate weathering thermostat so their climate is predicted to be less stable than that of planets with exposed continents."* Same ingredients, different arrangement, different outcome.
- **Falsifier:** bulk water fraction alone predicts gigayear persistence with no dependence on exposed land, phase or tectonic arrangement. If that were shown, the isomer reading would fail and the inventory-only reading would stand.

## Q3 - What order does "over long periods" introduce?

- The four conjunctive conditions (reference class, water test, duration, maintenance) join under the symmetric bond `+`: *"both hold at once"* (Operators table :12). Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92). So listing them in any order does not change what is counted.
- Persistence itself is not symmetric. It is a repeated return, which is the cycle of Q1 and the sequence bond `→` at the level of individual states. Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** The architecture essay's own caution applies: *"we cannot say which state composed first, and we don't know if the order mattered"* (How Intelligence Actually Works, 2026-09-16).
- ⇒ The question smuggles an ordering in through "over long periods." The answer must state the ordering explicitly: maintenance loop first, then the count over the interval, never the reverse. A survey that counts first and asks about persistence later has read the route backwards.
- **Falsifier:** show a frequency that is invariant to whether persistence is tested before or after counting. If it is invariant, the ordering is cosmetic and this finding is withdrawn.

## Q4 - Valence is relative to the residual

- Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
- Stellar luminosity is not "good" or "bad" for surface water in itself. For a slowly rotating M dwarf it can strip an atmosphere and freeze a reservoir; for a planet with a strong weathering loop it can be the energy that drives the cycle. Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another."*
- ⇒ A ranked list of "habitable star properties" is a standing list of good actors, which the figure forbids: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual"* (Fig 11 :51). The residual here is specifically persistence of liquid surface water over T.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a property that is beneficial for surface-water persistence in every habitat without exception. The world literature predicts this refuter will fire against any such universal property.

## Q5 - The round trip between one planet and an ensemble

- Fig 4 :19-20: **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**
- Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
- Sent up: the verbatim figure set (cross-kind composition, cycle, isomer, functional group). Abstracted: a one-planet maintenance loop plus a definition. Brought back down: an occurrence rate from Kepler or TESS. Three things do not survive: the duration T, the resupply term, and the phase distinction between inventory and liquid surface state. That set is the round-trip gap R_T, reported qualitatively in `11_audit.md`.
- **Falsifier:** provide a sufficient statistic for an ensemble from which both the one-planet maintenance history and the ensemble frequency are recoverable. None is held here.

## CONCLUSION

Long-term stable surface water is a **cycle whose arrangement is decisive and whose ordering must be stated**, not an orbital address and not a bulk inventory. "Habitable zone" is a functional group; two planets with the same inventory but different phase and tectonic arrangement are isomers. The frequency over such planets is a world import, not a composition of the six terms, and must be carried across scales with a measured R_T.
