# Expert verification packet: stable surface water on planets

**What this is:** a claim in a form a planetary scientist or exoplanet astronomer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the structural findings, `gap` on the frequency. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of gigayear persistence is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how often do planets have stable surface water over long periods?" is a union compound, not one frequency. Its persistence condition is a closing feedback cycle with resupply; a chain cannot close by construction, so a habitable-zone address alone is insufficient. Arrangement is decisive: two planets with the same bulk inventory can be isomers in the figure's sense, with different surface states and different stability. The numerical frequency is unreachable from the six-term substrate and is owed by a survey plus an instrument under a definition fixed in advance. The framework returns that shape and the falsifiers, not the number.

- **Statement:** the question is a union of overlapping conditions, and persistence requires a closing cycle rather than a chain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. - **Refuter:** exhibit one lever that clears all four conditions at once, or a planet that keeps liquid surface water for a gigayear with no closing loop and no resupply.

### C2 - physics-facing, checkable against published planetary physics

- **Statement:** inventory and orbital address do not decide persistence; arrangement and a resupply feedback do. "Habitable zone" is a functional group, not a member.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** is the Earth-system literature consistent with this? Coy, Kite and Graham (2025, arXiv:2507.23124) report that carbonate-silicate weathering *"is thought to stabilize Earth's climate on geologic timescales"* and that without resupply *"the feedback's strength wanes."* Cowan and Abbot (2014, arXiv:1401.0720) report that *"waterworlds lack a silicate weathering thermostat so their climate is predicted to be less stable than that of planets with exposed continents."*
- **Refuter:** show bulk water fraction alone predicts gigayear persistence with no dependence on exposed land, phase or tectonic arrangement.

## 5. What would settle it

A fixed definition and a source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. Which instrument distinguishes a persistent surface reservoir from a transient one on a rocky exoplanet? No current survey measures the gigayear history.
2. Resupply test: can the weathering feedback be shown to stay closed on a specific planet over the stated interval? If not, the numerator is zero for that planet.
3. Definition test: fix radius band, instellation range, completeness assumption and duration, then re-estimate. If the three studies converge, the union reading weakens.

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 12 :117, :121, :123, four short passages.

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
