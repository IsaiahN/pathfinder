## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical limit is that the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32 says "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 says it can characterise what is missing in effect terms, then stops at the limit of the instruments.

*The Library of Babel* gives the operational form: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can settle it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- A name would be "information is discrete" or "information is continuous".
- The shape is: both are level-relative leftovers of composition over the six-term set, and a ground decides which one appears at a given level.

The shape is not a compromise between the two names. It is a different kind of answer: it says what any candidate answer must look like, and what would have to be true for either name to be licensed.

## 2. How the shape is obtained here

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrapping by proximity. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Here the known terms were "bit", "entropy", "continuum", "spectrum", "finiteness"; the recurring new terms from the figure census were "leftover", "seventh member", "level-relative irreducible".
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." The effect is: a count terminates, or an ordering has no gaps. The cause ("information is discrete") is not what the figure returns.
- *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." A discrete reading and a smooth reading can be projections of the same underlying composition. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." Here the stop is the shape plus its refuter, not a verdict about the Planck scale.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." For this question that means an instrument that discriminates discrete from continuous at a named level. *Hard Problem*: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).

Neither is in hand. So neither name is licensed. The shape stands as `specified` / `general`, `candidate`.

## 4. The shape for this question, stated in essay terms

From *The Library of Babel*, the question is "malformed" only in the sense that it bundles several problems: primitive status, composition, level and instrument. The figure text's own word is a union compound (Fig 9 :54). The shape unbundles it:

> Neither "discrete" nor "continuous" is a seventh member. Both are what a particular composition leaves behind. Irreducibility is a fact about a level, not about the world. Which leftover a physical carrier returns is owed to an instrument, and the instrument is not yet available.

This is the answer the framework can give. It is not "both are true", which would be a verdict stated in `general` mode with no ground. It is a claim about the standing of the two terms.

## 5. Correction to my own earlier reading

I first wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming either discreteness or continuity, because the framework returns a kind of thing and not an answer. That was too broad. The README's phrase governs *"a **missing** thing"*, a primitive not held in the library. Here nothing is missing in that sense: the six terms are held, and the two leftovers are already derivable from them.

The correct statement is narrower:

- Naming a leftover as *the primitive* is forbidden (F1, F2 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- Naming a leftover as *the leftover at a level* is a binding, licensed only by a ground reading or a provenanced import.
- The shape claim is about which compositions produce which leftovers, not about banning the words "discrete" and "continuous".

Both readings are recorded so the correction is visible rather than silently erased.

## 6. Two consequences

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous names who agree that information is discrete (or continuous) is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the refuter and let an instrument settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post." and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim, and it cannot settle anything it reports on.
