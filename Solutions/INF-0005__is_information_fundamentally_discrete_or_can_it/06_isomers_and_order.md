# Structure, order and isomers of the two leftovers

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

F1 and F2 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` establish that neither discreteness nor the continuum is a primitive. Two questions remain: (1) are the two leftovers **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or merely members of one functional group, and (2) in what order must the pieces be settled so the derivation does not run above a broken link (Fig 3)?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the structure of each leftover?

- **Discrete leftover.** Fig 13 :68: "finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate". Counting needs a bounded region and a terminating operation. Fig 12 :43: "one bond type costs nothing; seven cost 2.8 bits each" prices the arrangement over integer counts of atoms and bond types. So the discrete leftover is what a bounded, counted composition leaves behind at the description level. It is not a primitive bit.
- **Smooth leftover.** Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said". Fig 13 :43-44: "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time." So the smooth leftover is what an unbounded ordering bonded to space and time leaves behind. It is also not a primitive.
- **Level rule.** Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom". A bit can be an atom for coding theory and a derivative of the substrate at the same time. These are different levels, not contradictory claims.

Both routes are legal compositions over the same six terms. **Status:** triangulated for legality (Fig 13 :20, :68, :43-44 plus Fig 12 :104), `specified` for the binding of counting to finiteness.

### Q2: Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}` the two routes share every atom and differ in which structure is bonded to which state and whether the composition terminates in a count or an unbounded ordering. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different leftover substance. The distinction is invisible if you only record the ingredient list ("information", "physical level") and decisive in what an instrument returns.

**This is a `specified` structural claim, not a measurement.** Whether a real physical carrier instantiates one route or the other is the F6 instrument gap. `single-source` for the figure definition of isomer (Fig 12), with the physics-facing check unmeasured.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one route requires an atom the other does not (a primitive bit for the discrete route, or a primitive continuum for the smooth route), the isomer claim fails. Each would instead be a seventh member, which breaks Fig 13 :41, "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it." The weaker functional-group reading would then be all that remains.

### Q3: The weaker reading, functional group

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

If the isomer test fails for lack of a shared atom set, both leftovers are still members of the functional group "what a composition over the six leaves behind". The group fixes the kind (derivative, not primitive); the qualifier fixes which member (counted or unbounded). This is the claim that survives the falsifier in Q2. It is `general` and figure-derived.

### Q4: What order does the derivation require?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception:** name what is observed. A count terminates; an ordering has no gaps. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Do not average the two into "information is mostly discrete".
2. **Vocabulary:** do we have terms for what these afford? Yes: finiteness, counting, ordering, spectrum, leftover, level.
3. **Objective:** what counts as progress here? Fig 12 :52, "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." The goal is a comparison between two leftovers at a named level, not a win for one side.
4. **Refuse before mint.** Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." The terms "information is fundamentally discrete" and "information is continuous at the physical level" are held and predicting. They are candidates for refusal (Formula step 2), because they promote a derivative to a primitive. Refusal removes their standing to be composed over; it deletes nothing from the record (Fig 6 :42-43).
5. **Mint only if the bargain pays.** Fig 5 :43-44: a term that explains everything by saying nothing fails, and so does one that explains one occasion perfectly. The shape claim explains the standing of both terms without naming a winner. That is the cheaper statement.

A measurement at the Planck scale belongs at step 1, not at step 4. The 20-minute check in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` starts at the figure text, not at the instrument.

### Q5: Analogues from the world sources (corroboration only)

- **Metamerism.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading "cannot comment on the source". A discrete-looking reading and a smooth-looking reading may be projections of the same composition. What would settle it is a discriminator, not an argument. Corroborates Q1.
- **Subitizing.** The Wikipedia entry on subitizing reports a fast, effortless regime up to about four items and a different counting regime above that. Discrete number access is a level-local cognitive achievement, not a cosmological primitive. Corroborates Q1 and F5.
- **Analog computation.** The Wikipedia entry on analog computers reports that digital machines represent quantities "by discrete values of both time and amplitude", while analog machines use continuously varying physical quantities. Both are engineered at a level. Corroborates the functional-group reading in Q3.

These analogues share no author with the figures, but they are secondary. They do not change the figure verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The two leftovers are **isomers over the full shared atom set**: same six terms, different arrangement, different leftover. If the shared-atom test fails, the weaker **functional-group** claim stands. The derivation must refuse the two primitive-promoting readings *before* minting anything, and must settle the level before asking for a physical measurement. One falsifier is written in advance (Q2) and one instrument gap is declared (F6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
