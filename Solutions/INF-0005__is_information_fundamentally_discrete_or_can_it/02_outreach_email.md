> **The output (`specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape):**
>
> The question "is information fundamentally discrete or continuous?" is a union compound, not one binary. Under a six-term generating set (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures), both "discrete" and "continuous" are composition leftovers rather than primitives. The figure text says a continuum "was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time." It also says "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." Irreducibility is level-relative: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom".
>
> So the shape claim is: neither option wins at the primitive level; discreteness and smoothness are what different compositions over the same six terms leave behind; and which leftover appears at a given level is a question for an instrument, not for an argument. This is a shape, not a measurement, and I make no claim about which one the Planck scale returns.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is no such thing as a bit, that Shannon entropy is wrong, that physical spacetime is smooth, or that physics has settled the question. The bit is a unit of description, priced in bits over counted atoms and bonds in the framework. That price is discrete, but the framework does not promote discreteness to a primitive. I am explicitly flagging that as a tension rather than hiding it.
