## 1. The question restated as an effect

Option A, "information is fundamentally discrete": there is a smallest unit of information, a bit-like atom that cannot be composed further, and any continuous appearance is a coarse description over counts.

Option B, "information can be continuous at the physical level": the physical carrier admits arbitrary fine-grained values, and any discreteness is a coarse description over a smooth quantity.

What each leads to: A licenses a search for the atom and treats the continuum as derivative. B licenses a search for the smooth carrier and treats the bit as derivative. The residual is that both options treat "discrete" and "continuous" as candidates for a primitive, and both treat "fundamental" as naming a privileged stratum. The figures bear on both moves directly.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a statement of which compositions over the six-term set leave discreteness behind, which leave smoothness behind, and what ground would decide which leftover appears at a given level. That bound shape is written down in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0, so it can accrue a residual like any other term (Formula, "Direction").

## 3. What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that discreteness or continuity wins.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not upgrade the label. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35). Selecting for likely agreement manufactures exactly the correlation that makes agreement worthless: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* (Seat Map :163, local file).
2. Asking people to trust the framework before they check spends the only thing they contribute. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42).
3. The ground is the anchor, not the crowd. *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29). For this question that means an instrument or a coding theorem, not a vote.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
