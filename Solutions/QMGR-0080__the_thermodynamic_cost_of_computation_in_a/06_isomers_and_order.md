## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the three conditions isomers, or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Written as they appear in the literature:

- Logical erasure uses {bit states, reservoir, erased alternative}, with energy and temperature as the physical operands.
- Gravitational referencing uses {metric, lapse, location}, with energy referred to infinity.
- Horizon bound uses {horizon area, surface gravity, entropy}.

As written, the three share no single common atom set. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are three members of a **functional group**: each is a per-slot price composed across kinds, and the group fixes the kind of answer while the qualifier fixes which condition is being priced.

The stronger form: if all three are rewritten over the full shared substrate {Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair and which bond carries the comparison, then they **are** isomers. This is the checkable structural claim, and it needs an instrument, not an argument, to confirm the bindings.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one of the three conditions can be written without an atom the others require (for example, gravitational referencing with no lapse, or erasure with no erased alternative), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q2 — What order do the conditions compose in?

- Fig 3 :20/21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :22/23: *"A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

Dependency order:

1. **Local erasure price first.** Without a settled local bound, the gravitational rebinding has nothing to act on. The experiments (Bérut et al.) already return a reading here.
2. **Gravitational referencing second.** It consumes the local price and changes the slicing to which energy is referred. This is Fig 4's transform between scales, and it carries a round-trip gap `R_T`.
3. **Horizon bound third, where a horizon exists.** It consumes the referred energy and the area law. Where there is no horizon, this link is simply absent, not failed.

Optimizing step 3 before step 1 is the named failure: effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

### Q3 — What do the bonds contribute that the operands do not?

- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*
- Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*

The cost is not a list of ingredients. The same energy and temperature under `−` (what is removed from the memory) and under `⋛` (which temperature is larger, local or horizon) are different substances. A flat formula that writes only the ingredients records half the answer. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

This is why adding the two currencies is not just numerically wrong but a notation error: it treats a subtraction and a comparison as one conjunction.

### Q4 — Is the union second-order?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*

Each condition is settled against a ground of its own kind (erasure experiment, horizon thermodynamics, first law). Each then becomes an atom for the next level, and the union is a second-order composition of settled molecules. This needs no seventh primitive. It is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import that moves the wall.

**Strain, named.** The Operators table defines `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level is a type question that needs an instrument. I record this as a strain, not a correction. The parallel strain in the exemplar is the same operator's operand type at second order; here it is not smoothed either.

### Q5 — What is the round-trip gap `R_T` on the local-to-infinity transform?

- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- Symbols table :77: `R_T` is *"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."*

**Sent up (x):** the local erasure reading `kT_loc ln 2` on a specified device.
**Abstraction (T_A):** "the minimum cost of erasing a bit."
**Concretisation (T_E):** the cost referred to infinity, `kT_loc N(r) ln 2` (Herrera relation as reported in the Bardeen paper).

`R_T` is **nonzero and structural** here: the coarser description drops the slicing. Crucially, the world source says the lapse is *"the gravitational redshift, rather than a modification of the local thermodynamic statement itself."* So the gap is a change of reference, not a new local law. It has not been measured numerically for the same device at two radii, and no such measurement is claimed here. Treating the local and referred costs as identical would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are a **functional group**, not isomers as written; they become a real isomer claim only when each is rewritten over the full shared six-term substrate, with a stated falsifier. Their dependency order is local price, then gravitational referencing, then horizon bound. The bonds carry the meaning: subtraction and comparison are not interchangeable, so adding the currencies is a notation error before it is a numerical one. The union is second-order by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule, with one live type strain on `⋛` operands. The local-to-infinity transform has a nonzero round-trip gap that is structural, not measured.
