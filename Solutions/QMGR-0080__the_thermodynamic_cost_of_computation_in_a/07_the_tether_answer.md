# The Tether's answer: the thermodynamic cost of computation in a gravitational setting

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain findings, `gap` on the numerical gravitational correction. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A device changes a memory. Heat appears. The question asks what that costs when a gravitational potential, a lapse factor or a horizon is part of the setting. The common form of the question asks for one number, a gravitational correction to `kT ln 2`. The residual is that this bundles at least three conditions that have different anchors and different currencies. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

## 2. The shape

1. **One price is not the right object.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* A description is priced in bits; the physical settling is priced in actions. Adding them into a single joules-per-operation number is the notation error Fig 12 :130 names. The gravitational rebinding is a third term, referred to a different slicing.
2. **The local price is a bond price, not a constant of nature.** Erasure collapses alternatives into one state. Fig 12 :99: *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."* The price is logarithmic in the alternatives. `kT ln 2` is the physical instance, measured by Bérut et al., not a figure line.
3. **Whether the bill recurs depends on recording.** Fig 12 :108/109: *"where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."* Retaining the history is the mechanism by which the recurring price is avoided (Bennett 1973).
4. **Gravity contributes a spendable gradient and a rebinding, not a new primitive.** Fig 13 :14 describes energy as *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*; Fig 13 :22 describes the gradient as *"a difference that can be spent"*. The lapse changes which energy is referred to, not the local thermodynamic statement. The world literature reports that gravitational hair and cosmic expansion shift the minimum heat, while vanishing Hawking temperature does not force the heat to vanish.
5. **The price is per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A global average hides the very thing that carries the signal: which memory, which reservoir level, which radius, which horizon level.
6. **The number is open.** Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* The figures constrain the form of any answer and stop before its value. The disagreement among the gravitational papers is recorded rather than averaged.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal gravitational correction to Landauer's bound. No value is asserted.
- Not that the two currencies can be converted into one another by a fixed exchange rate. Fig 12 :130 forbids the sum; the exchange, if any, needs a ground.
- Not that gravity changes the local erasure law. The lapse is a redshift, not a modification of the local statement.
- Not that the six-term substrate derives a gravitational temperature. That would be an import with source gate and shadow test, not a derivation.
- Not that the question has one answer. It is a union compound with three loops and a second-order composition.
- Not grounded. No measurement with the gravitational variables controlled was used here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and an instrument that cannot discriminate cases *"demonstrates its own inadequacy."* Here:

- **Currency separation:** an experiment that prices a logical operation in bits and its physical settling in actions separately, showing whether a fixed exchange exists.
- **Recording test:** a controlled comparison of erasure with retained versus discarded history, measuring heat per logical operation (refuter 2).
- **Rebinding test:** the same device at two gravitational potentials with the local and referred-to-infinity energies both resolved, measuring the round-trip gap `R_T`.
- **Per-slot test:** resolution by reservoir level and horizon area level, not a single global average.
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** a context-free number for all backgrounds; reduction of the three conditions to one predicate; derivation of a gravitational temperature from the six alone; a global average that settles the question.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The instruments already exist in the opened literature: erasure experiments, cavity gaps, horizon thermodynamics. They have not been aimed at a common bracket. The stop is at the limit of what those instruments can currently distinguish. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

It says that the request bundles several prices: a logical description price in bits, a physical settling price in actions, and a gravitational rebinding between energy measured locally and energy referred to infinity, and that these do not add. The local erasure price is the choice erased, logarithmic in the alternatives; whether it recurs depends on whether the computation history is retained, a point independently established in reversible computing. Gravity supplies a spendable gradient and changes the slicing of the energy, not the local thermodynamic law. The cost is per memory, per reservoir and per horizon level, never a global average. What the framework returns is the shape any answer must have and the refuters written in advance, not a value. The number stays open until an instrument resolves the two currencies, the recording condition and the local-to-infinity gap at once.
