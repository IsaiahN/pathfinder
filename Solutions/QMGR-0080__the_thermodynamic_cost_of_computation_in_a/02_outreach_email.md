**Subject:** One checkable output on the thermodynamic cost of computation with gravity present

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as an effect.** What does a device have to pay, physically, to change a memory when a gravitational potential, a lapse factor or a horizon is part of the setting? The usual request is for one number, a gravitational correction to `kT ln 2`. The output below says that request bundles at least three different things.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the parts, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The price is not one currency. A description price in bits and an action price in the physical settling do not add. Fig 12 :125, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130, *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
> 2. The local erasure price is the choice erased, logarithmic in the alternatives. `kT ln 2` is a physical instance of that shape, not something the figures name.
> 3. Whether the price recurs depends on whether the computation history is retained. Fig 12 :108/109, *"where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."*
> 4. Gravity contributes a spendable gradient and a rebinding of energy between the local device and infinity, not a new primitive. The lapse changes where the energy is referred, not the local thermodynamic statement.
> 5. The price is per memory, per reservoir, per radius, per horizon level. A global joules-per-operation average hides the live signal (Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any of this yields a new numerical gravitational correction. The slot is open. The figures return a shape, not a name, and the world literature disagrees about the sign of the correction: gravitational hair reduces it, cosmic expansion reduces it, and vanishing Hawking temperature does not force the transferred heat to vanish. I record that disagreement rather than averaging it. No amount is asserted as settled by measurement here.
>
> **What would refute the output (written before this message):**
> 1. One context-free number settles the cost for all backgrounds and all reservoirs without separating the two currencies.
> 2. Retaining versus discarding the computation history makes no difference to the dissipated heat per logical operation.
> 3. The erasure cost, the gravitational referencing and the horizon bound reduce to one predicate with one instrument.
> 4. A gravitational temperature is derived from the six-term set alone, with no import and no ground reading.
> 5. A global average, with no per-slot resolution, settles the question.
>
> I wrote these down before sending. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
