# Outreach draft: check the residual first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No request for trust, no request for agreement.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims and their refuters in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. If a sub-claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** Three failure conditions for reliable cell signalling, and what would refute each

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow question and three pre-written refuters. I am not asking you to read a framework
> first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the refuters do not survive your
> knowledge of the field, the rest is irrelevant.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the mapping).** A cell
> maps an external signal to a correct response. That mapping stays stable even though the molecules
> carrying it fluctuate at low copy number and are themselves degraded and replaced. I am treating
> "noise" and "turnover" as two different conditions of one union, not as two words for one problem.
> A mechanism that fixes stronger binding can still fail when the receptor is replaced; a mechanism
> that maintains the channel can still respond to the wrong molecule.
>
> **The three shapes I think a mechanism must have, and what would kill each:**
>
> 1. **Refusal, not just stronger binding.** Discrimination against a near-match needs an operator
>    that says "this one is wrong." In the framework's vocabulary a vocabulary with only bonds can
>    accumulate and cannot refuse. Refuter: an observed error rate reached at equilibrium, with no
>    energy-dissipating delay or rejection step.
> 2. **Maintenance of a decaying channel.** The ground does not decay, the channel does. Refuter:
>    exact signal-to-response contact maintained across full component replacement with no
>    replacement process, feedback, or chaperone-like maintenance.
> 3. **Independent paths rather than averaged copies.** Two identical noisy copies share the same
>    error; averaging them is one frame wearing two names. Refuter: reliability shown to come from
>    averaging identical correlated copies, with structurally different paths contributing nothing.
>
> I wrote these down before looking up the biology literature, so they are falsifiable rather than
> fitted. The figures say the framework returns a shape, not a name, so I am deliberately **not**
> claiming it derives kinetic proofreading, chaperones, or integral feedback. Those are molecular
> bindings from outside, and I am asking you whether the shapes are right, not whether the bindings
> are clever.
>
> **One thing I am explicitly not asking:** I am not asking whether the framework is impressive or
> whether it is the only one. A failure is more useful to me than an endorsement. If you would
> rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources and the full check are in the attached packet. The
> figures are the authority, not my summary of them. If you want to use the method, the formula is in
> the figures link; write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state the
> mode of what you get.
>
> [Your name]
