## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union, not partition

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The three conditions (private cost C1, channel contact C2, frame correlation C3) form overlapping sets. The region C1 only is reachable by selective incentives (Olson's world claim, secondary). The region C2 only is reachable by a safe, confidential, acted-on channel (TI's ALAC description, secondary). The region C3 only is reachable by independent oversight (Fig 2 :31). The intersections C1&C2, C2&C3, and C1&C3 are not harder versions of these; they are compounds that require a different lever each. So an anti-corruption programme is not a list of three reforms to be applied in any order. It is a union to be unbundled first.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a single intervention that clears all three regions and leaves no categorically different residue.

### Q2 - Dependency order, and why the middle link is where this breaks

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :24-26: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break. Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

Mapped to this question, the chain is:

1. **Perception:** notice corruption (obvious failures, obvious tests).
2. **Vocabulary:** have terms for what reporting affords, safe channels, protections.
3. **Objective:** state what would count as progress, in the institution's own terms. **This is the missing middle link.**
4. **Planning:** a route from a citizen to a channel to an outcome.
5. **Learn and carry:** a protected precedent that makes the next report cheaper.

Most anti-corruption effort lands at steps 1, 2, and 4, where measurements already exist: awareness campaigns, legal definitions of corruption, hotlines. Step 3 is usually filled by a count of reports or a perception index, both frame-internal (Fig 1 :24-27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*). Optimising the hotline cannot rescue a step-3 objective that cannot say what would count as success.

**Falsifier:** an institution that states progress as a ground-valid subtraction, independently verified, and shows that measure driving the correction rather than merely counting activity.

### Q3 - Are a captured and a clean regime isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Take two regimes over the same roster of atoms `{citizen, report, channel, oversight, sanction, norm}`:

- **Regime A (ground-reaching):** `citizen → protected report → independent oversight ⋛ verified outcome → sanction`. Verification ranges against an outcome the oversight body cannot move (Fig 2 :29, :33). Reporter and verifier are different seats (Fig 10 :86).
- **Regime B (captured/mirror):** `citizen → report → oversight ⋛ oversight's own clearance metric → sanction`. The "ground" is the oversight body's own statistic, so it moves when the body pushes on it (Fig 2 :34: *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*).

Both contain the same atoms. They share the functional group `report → verification → outcome` (a plan as sequence, Fig 12 :112) and the group's presence fixes what kind of thing they are: an accountability regime. The qualifier, which bond connects verification to outcome, fixes which substance they are. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, difference invisible in the roster of institutions and decisive in behaviour. A reform that copies Regime A's atom list into a Regime B setting and leaves the bond alone has copied the formula and not the substance.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either regime can be written without an atom the other requires (for example a ground-reaching regime with no independent oversight, or a captured regime that needs no verification at all), the isomer claim fails and only the weaker functional-group claim stands. This is checkable from an institutional description, so an outside frame can test it in minutes.

### Q4 - Analogues: describe the residual, then pick the frame

- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*; Fig 9 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*; Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

The analogues in `04` and `09` were selected *after* the residual was written in effect terms, not before. Their role is to corroborate the mechanism shape, not to supply a name:

| analogue | field (low overlap where possible) | what it predicts here | figure it corroborates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Olson, selective incentives | economics / collective action | private cost blocks contribution to a public good | F1, F2 |
| Fehr and Gächter, altruistic punishment | human behaviour / evolution | costly enforcement sustains cooperation when the habitat allows it | F2, F5 |
| Ostrom, commons governance | institutional economics / ecology | affected users can build sustainable rules | F2, F7 |
| Bystander effect / diffusion of responsibility | social psychology | others' presence and fear of consequences inhibit reporting; correlated crowds inhibit more than they help | F4, F5 |

Olson and the bystander literature are both about the failure to act, but they draw on different evidence pools (formal group theory versus laboratory and field social psychology), so they are not automatically one witness. Fehr and Gächter and Ostrom arrive at "habitat matters" from different methods again. Agreement across them corroborates the shape; it does not license a policy name.

**Falsifier for the analogue set:** a world source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that private cost is not the operative variable in citizen reporting once the channel is safe.

## CONCLUSION

The problem is a union to be unbundled (Q1), the chain breaks at the unmeasured objective link (Q2), and a captured and a ground-reaching regime are isomers over the same institutional atom set (Q3), which is why copying an institution list without the bond fails. The analogue set corroborates the mechanism shape from low-overlap fields but names no programme. Mode `specified` throughout; the isomer claim is checkable from public institutional descriptions and remains ungrounded here.
