# Expert verification packet: INS-0040

**What this is:** a claim in a form an anti-corruption researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural verdict, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the specific mechanism. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of this derivation is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Coordination against corruption under high personal reporting cost fails when three conditions bind as a union: a negative private payoff to the individual reporter, a channel that can lose contact with the ground, and frames that are correlated rather than independent. A single lever clears the easy region and leaves the intersections standing. The figure-derived shape is: change the habitat rather than exhort the individual; keep the anchor reachable through a channel whose silence means lost contact, not no corruption; buy independence in proportion to the high cost of being wrong; install the missing middle link, a ground-valid subtraction for progress rather than a count of reports; and put the verifier outside the slice being graded, selected by an unauthored mechanism. Which specific mechanism wins is a declared `gap`. The framework returns a shape, not a programme name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus practitioner-facing; checkable against public anti-corruption practice

- **Statement:** the problem is a union of three conditions, and the reporting channel's output is not a verdict about corruption; if the channel loses contact, the honest report is "lost touch".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :28 - *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*; Fig 10 :29 - *"keep the anchor reachable"*.
- **What to check:** is the anchor in a real setting an outcome the institution cannot move (actual reduction, independently verified), or the institution's own clearance rate? If the latter, Fig 2 :33 (*"The anchor must not update."*) says it is not an anchor.
- **Refuter:** a channel whose own statistic is treated as ground but moves when the institution pushes on it, with no ground-valid outcome behind it; or one lever clearing all three conditions with no categorically different residue.

### C2 - cross-domain; checkable against the researcher's own field

- **Statement:** independent frames, not a larger agreeing crowd, are what move the ground; and capability lives in agent-and-habitat, not in the individual citizen.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :42 - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 8 :49 - *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** in a case the reader knows, did ground-valid corrections come from frames with separate evidence (different institutions, different data), or from a chain of mutually agreeing reporters reading one source? Did a citizen-only intervention change actual corruption, or only reported willingness?
- **Refuter:** a single-source agreement chain that moves the ground; or a controlled citizen-only intervention that lowers corruption with no channel or habitat change.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 12, 3, and 10

- **Statement:** the chain breaks at the unmeasured objective link; a captured and a ground-reaching regime are isomers over the same institutional atom set; and the verifier must sit outside the graded slice, selected by an unauthored mechanism.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 - *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 10 :86 - *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."*
- **What to check:** is a typical programme's progress measure a count it also produces (frame-internal), or a subtraction against an external ground? Do two regimes with the same institution roster differ in the verification bond, and does that difference, not the roster, track outcomes? Is the checker selected by a mechanism (lottery, all who qualify, a pre-existing set) rather than hand-picked for agreement?
- **Refuter:** an institution whose progress subtraction is ground-valid and demonstrably drives correction; or a regime pair where one can be written without an atom the other requires (which collapses the isomer claim to the weaker functional-group claim); or hand-picked correlated checkers producing ground-valid corrections with no unauthored selection.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a policy recommendation. No agency, law, sanction, or national reform is named.
- Not that reporting more, or a higher perception score, is the goal; those are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not that agreement is useless in general; it is efficient for a convention (Fig 2 :41), only disqualifying for a verdict about the world.
- Not that citizens are apathetic; the private-cost structure is the object.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground-valid, source-discriminating instrument, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Concretely:

1. A channel test separating actual reduction from the agency's own metric.
2. An independence test: do corrections survive when reporting frames are made genuinely separate?
3. A middle-link test: a progress subtraction against an external ground that is not activity the institution also produces.
4. A checker test: do results hold with an unauthored, outside-the-slice verifier?
5. The five standing falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

1. Read Fig 9 :55, :57; Fig 10 :24, :28-29; Fig 2 :42; Fig 8 :49; Fig 11 :40; Fig 12 :117; Fig 3 :27; Fig 10 :86. Short passages only.
