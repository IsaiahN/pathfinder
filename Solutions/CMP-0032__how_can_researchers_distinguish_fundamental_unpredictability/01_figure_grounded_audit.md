## 0. The residual under audit

State the question in effect terms before choosing any figure line.

> A system acts: belief `b`, action `a`, prediction `Γ(b,a)`, observation `o′`. On object slot `s` the residual `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` is live, that is `R⁺_s > 0` (Fig 1 :11, :19; Symbols table :36-48).
>
> The forecast fails. Two explanations compete:
>
> - **A, fundamental unpredictability:** there is no composition in `closure(Γ)` that reaches the target. The atom is absent from the library. More data, longer records and better models inside this frame will not close `R_s`.
> - **B, incomplete data or imperfect models:** the term is already in the closure but was not found, the instrument is too coarse, the channel is lossy, or the record is too short. More search or a better instrument would close `R_s`.
>
> **The residual is the discriminator itself:** what observation would license A rather than B, and what would reverse a claim of A?

This is the question the figures govern directly. It is not a request for a universal number.

### 0.1 What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction")

`THE_FORMULA.md` warns: *"the shape a frame is searching for is a PREFERENCE, not a term. Unbound, predicting nothing, no residual - so nothing above can see it."* So bind the sought-for shape before the run:

> **SHAPE CLAIM (written before the derivation):** A failure to predict licenses "fundamental" only when (i) the two candidate causes are shown to be different facts with different remedies, (ii) the failure cannot be made to trend down by more search or a sharper instrument *in the inward track*, and (iii) an outside frame with a different closure corroborates the absence. A global error rate never decides it. "Fundamental" is level-relative irreducibility, not an absolute bottom.

This claim is now refusable by the same test as everything else (Fig 5 :65-66).

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

Describe `R` by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound**, not a two-choice partition (Fig 9 :54-55). Both can hold at once: a system can have an absent atom *and* a lossy channel. Symmetry: the failed reading is symmetric under cause (the same `R_s` whichever cause produced it), but the discriminator is an ordered threshold comparison on where the wall sits, not a symmetric join. Scale: first order inside one model, second order across frames.
- **Domain 1, the model frame.** Actors: `Γ`, `atoms(Γ)`, the seven bonds, negation, the bound terms, the slots `s`. Currency: description cost in bits, actions spent, and `R_s` per slot. Anchor: **none inside it**. A frame cannot certify its own limit (Fig 8 :37). This is a partition question about composition legality. Not a verdict domain on its own.
- **Domain 2, the instrument and data channel.** Actors: sensor, resolution, sampling interval, record length, noise, surrogate data. Currency: `o′`, action cost, descriptive cost. Anchor: **the ground**, which does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Question type: *inward* (Fig 6 :29). Dependency: before Domain 3, because a reading must register before the ground can settle anything.
- **Domain 3, the ground / experiment.** Actors: measurement, experiment, counter, market, patient outcome. Currency: payment of terms, verdicts. Anchor: **itself, pinned**; it does not update on what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29-30, :33). Question type: verdict. Dependency: after Domain 2, before Domain 4.
- **Domain 4, independent peer frames and the public record.** Actors: other disciplines, other instruments, other closures, provenance records. Currency: imports with source gate and shadow test, triangulation. Anchor: **the ground, never agreement** (Fig 2 :35, :45). Question type: *outward* and union (Fig 6 :30; Fig 8 :45). Dependency: last, after the residual is described in effect terms (Fig 8 :53).
- **Partition or union:** union. A single lever (more data) clears the contingent region and leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :56). One intervention cannot decide the whole question.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception (is anything registering at all? Fig 6 :34-35) → vocabulary (is there a term for the shape of the missing thing? Fig 9 :32) → objective (what counts as closing `R_s`?) → planning → learn and carry. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

**Strain test applied during the census:** the phrase "fundamental unpredictability" strains against Fig 12 :104, which makes irreducibility a fact about a level. That is a signal to re-census the word, not to soften the figure. Re-census result recorded as S2 in section 7: the ordinary term "fundamental" is re-grounded as level-relative irreducibility.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The two causes are categorically different facts with different instructions (decisive)

Fig 8 :40-43, read verbatim:

> **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."**

This is the question's own distinction, stated in the figures. The ordinary phrase "uncertainty caused by incomplete data or imperfect models" maps to *unreachable at this budget*: the term is in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom. The ordinary phrase "fundamental unpredictability" maps to *underivable in principle*: no composition in the present closure reaches it, so more search inside that closure is the wrong instrument.

The figure adds a price: *"Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed, and Figure 6 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives"* (Fig 8 :42-43). So declaring a case fundamental when it is merely under-sampled is not a neutral error. It mints an atom that was not owed and debits independence.

**Refuter:** a single-frame computation that certifies `φ*` is absent from `closure(Γ)` with no import from a differing closure breaks this finding. That is the Fig 9 :20 case ("it is not in here" cannot be proved from inside).

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated with Fig 9 :6 (:6 "the verdict: is φ* in here at all?") and Fig 6 :21 (*"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*). Low overlap: Fig 8 governs the *kinds of failure*, Fig 6 governs what the *mint operator* can do.

### F2 - Failure to predict is not a proof of absence

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

Fig 9 :18: **"φ* out of reach: nothing inside can prove that"**

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

A research programme that declares "fundamental" because a model keeps failing has made the exact inference the figures forbid. The failure is real (`R_s` persists), but it is a slice: *"what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read"* (Symbols table :38-40). The verdict "fundamental" is owed by a barrier proof or an outside witness, not by accumulated failure.

**Refuter:** exhibit a sealed frame, that is a frame whose closure is demonstrably complete and fully searched. Fig 9 :44 says no frame is a sealed room, so this refuter is a design instrument: if someone produces one, F2 fails.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 9 (separate figures, low overlap).

### F3 - From inside the frame the two cases look the same

Fig 6 :27: **"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."**

This is the central strain of the question. The researcher asking "which is it?" is inside the frame that produced the failed forecast. The figures do not give an internal diagnostic. They give a *protocol*:

- **Inward track**, Fig 6 :29: **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**
- **Outward track**, Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
- Fig 6 :31: **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**

So the honest answer to "how can researchers distinguish" is: run both tracks separately, never treat the inward success as evidence against the outward verdict or vice versa. A case can be deep (more data helps) on one axis and genuinely absent (an import is owed) on another. Averaging the two destroys the signal, exactly as Fig 1 :22 warns: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Refuter:** an internal test that reliably separates depth from absence without any instrument change and without a second frame would falsify the "look the same" claim.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated with Fig 8 :53-54.

### F4 - The instrument test: is anything already registering?

Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 6 :34: **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**

Fig 6 :35: **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

This is the operational discriminator for "incomplete data or imperfect models." If something is already returning a weak, noisy or low-resolution reading that fails to resolve, the inward track is live: sharpen it, lengthen the record, add sensors. If nothing registers at any resolution, there is no edge to extend from, and claiming "more data would fix it" is not a plan (it is a preference). Note the limit, stated plainly in Fig 6 :36-40: the figure does not forbid theory preceding its instrument; it forbids a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere.

**Refuter:** produce a case where nothing registered at any resolution and yet a better instrument was specified from a description alone and worked. That would break Fig 6 :32-35. (The figure itself notes Neptune was calculated before it was seen, Fig 6 :38: this is a prediction about where to point an existing instrument, not a sensor for a never-registered quantity.)

**Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 6 only), corroborated by world sources that measure Lyapunov exponents from sparse sensors (arXiv:2611.23434) and improve parameter identifiability by experimental design (arXiv:2611.23526). Low overlap: those are working instruments, not the figure text.

### F5 - A lossy channel is not a fundamental limit

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :25: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

A saturated metric, a pooled reading, a dropped transient: these are exactly the conditions researchers call "imperfect models" or "incomplete data." The figures put them in the channel, not in the ground and not in the closure. So a residual that trends down when the channel is repaired was never fundamental. The ground did not change; contact was restored.

**Refuter:** show a case where repairing a saturated metric, a pooled reading or a dropped transient leaves a residual that nevertheless proves underivable in principle. That would collapse the channel/ground distinction.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated with Fig 2 :29-30 (anchor must not move) and the world source on identifiability (arXiv:2611.23526: "Fast-process parameters remain weakly constrained by slow-variable records across the tested 2 to 100 MTU windows, while adding fast-field averages improves their recovery"). Low overlap: observation design versus channel theory.

### F6 - "Fundamental" is level-relative; there is no bottom

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

Fig 13 :124: **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."**

The ordinary word "fundamental" asks for an absolute bottom. The figures forbid one. What can honestly be claimed is: (a) underivable in principle relative to a *declared* `closure(Γ)`, established by the metaframe/limit arguments, or (b) irreducible *at a level*, where a settled molecule becomes an atom for what composes over it. Chaos is a derivative of a composition, not a primitive. So "fundamental unpredictability" must be re-grounded before it can be used. An unreachable target at one level can be an atom at the next.

**Refuter:** derive a seventh member from the six, or exhibit a level with no possible higher composition. Both break Fig 13 and Fig 12 respectively.

**Status:** `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the re-grounding, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 13 (different objects: composition recursion vs the minimal set).

### F7 - Proved limits versus stipulated limits

Fig 8 :34: **"A frame cannot reach its own metaframe. Godel's second theorem: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency."**

Fig 8 :37: **"A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity."**

Fig 9 :18: **"φ* out of reach: nothing inside can prove that"**

These are the only figure-licensed routes to a *positive* claim of in-principle unpredictability: a barrier theorem proved from outside the system, not a stipulation. The distinction matters because a research programme can simply *define* its target as unpredictable (the move the author criticises in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"Gödel's impossibility theorem demonstrates a true limit, while Chalmers defines one and asks us to accept it as truth"*). A proved limit is a verdict; a stipulated limit is a convention dressed as one. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

World corroboration, low overlap: Chaitin's constant is *"not a computable number"* and *"no bit of Ω after the Nth can be proven to be 1 or 0 within that system"* (Wikipedia, Chaitin's constant, 2026). This is a mathematical instance of the figure's Chaitin line, not the source of it.

**Refuter:** a proof that a given system certifies its own limit from inside. That would break Fig 8 :37 and the three theorems cited there.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 8 theorems + Fig 9 :18), with one genuinely low-overlap world witness (mathematics).

### F8 - The residual must stay indexed per slot; a global error rate cannot decide

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**

A model can have excellent aggregate skill and one slot that is fundamentally unpredictable; it can also have poor aggregate skill because of a single fixable sensor. A single accuracy number cannot tell them apart. The world source on weather makes the same structural point: *"the entirety of weather possesses a dual nature of chaos and order with distinct predictability"* (Wikipedia, Predictability, quoting Shen et al. 2021). Verdicts are per slot, per domain, never averaged.

**Refuter:** a case where the global error rate picks out the slot whose residual persists. That would break the per-slot law.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 + world source on dual predictability).

### F9 - Chaos and noise are different compositions, with different remedies

Fig 13 :32: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."**

Fig 13 :124: **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."**

This gives a positive *shape* for the two ordinary causes: chaos is a residual that re-enters and compounds through an amplifying chain; noise is detail discarded at a crossing and reappearing above it. They are not the same object, and they do not take the same remedy. The inward track applies to discarded detail (recover it, measure it); the compounding remainder is structural and needs the level-relative reading of F6. Importantly, chaos is *not* in-principle unpredictable according to the world sources: *"In principle, the deterministic systems that chaos theory attempts to analyze can be predicted, but uncertainty in a forecast increases exponentially with elapsed time"* (Wikipedia, Predictability). So chaos belongs mostly on the "practically limited, not fundamentally absent" side, with a finite predictability horizon set by the Lyapunov time.

This is a world/figure tension worth naming, not hiding. Re-census: Fig 13 :32 governs the *source of the residual within a chain*, not the deterministic/stochastic ontology of the system. Different objects, so no contradiction. Recorded as S3.

**Refuter:** a case where a discarded detail and a compounding remainder are indistinguishable even with an instrument that discriminates sources. That would dissolve the shape distinction.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 13), corroborated by two genuinely low-overlap world sources (chaos theory, Lyapunov exponent).

### F10 - The decision threshold is a gap, not a silence

No figure line returns a universal number (a Lyapunov exponent cutoff, an entropy rate, a p-value, a record length) that decides fundamental versus contingent for an arbitrary dataset. The world literature offers operational discriminators: a positive maximal Lyapunov exponent *"is usually taken as an indication that the system is chaotic"* (Wikipedia, Chaos theory), surrogate data testing *"for testing for non-linear structure in the empirical data"* (Wikipedia, Surrogate data), and identifiability diagnostics (arXiv:2611.23526). Those are instruments and filters, not figure-derived verdicts. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**; Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**; Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

So the numeric threshold is a declared `gap` of the *shape-not-a-name* kind, not a failure to search. An instrument is owed. A reported number would be a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict.

**Refuter (this one would overturn the gap):** exhibit a figure line that returns such a threshold. Until then the gap stands.

**Status:** `gap` (verdict, not silence), triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 13.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

1. A single-frame computation certifies that `φ*` is absent from `closure(Γ)` with no import from a differing closure (breaks Fig 9 :18, :44 and Fig 8 :39).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or revised in a new, dated section. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 8. Updated verdict
