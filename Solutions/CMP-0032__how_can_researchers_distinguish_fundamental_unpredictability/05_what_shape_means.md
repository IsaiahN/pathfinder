## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :138: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."* and Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (opened this session): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is not "the answer is X is fundamental." It is:

> The failure separates into two categorically different facts with different remedies; the separation is invisible from inside one frame; the inward and outward tracks must be run apart; "fundamental" is level-relative irreducibility; chaos and discarded noise are different compositions; the numeric cutoff is not supplied.

That is a shape. A verdict on a particular time series would be a name, and this folder does not produce one.

## 2. How the shape was obtained

- *Library of Babel* (opened this session): use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened this session): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel*: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*; the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the residual was described in effect terms first (a missed forecast on a slot), then mapped to the figure vocabulary (closure, inward, outward, channel, level), then narrowed to a five-part shape. The ordinary words "fundamental" and "uncertainty" were kept as hints, never as terms that decide.

## 3. Bundles are not one question

*The Library of Babel* (opened this session) asks: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* Fig 9 :55 says: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

This is decisive for CMP-0032. "Fundamental versus incomplete" is not a two-choice partition. A system can have an absent atom **and** a saturated channel. More data clears the second condition and leaves the first standing (Fig 9 :56). The researcher who frames the question as a binary has already bundled it, and one lever will appear to "solve" it while the intersection remains.

The same essay adds the instrument point in ordinary language: *"If you don't have the answer to a question, it's because you are lacking certain things in the library (instruments, information) that would help, or perhaps you are asking the wrong question altogether."* That is F4 and F10 in non-figure words.

## 4. Proved limits versus stipulated limits

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session) draws exactly the line this question needs:

> *"Gödel's impossibility theorem demonstrates a true limit, while Chalmers defines one and asks us to accept it as truth."*

And on indistinguishability:

> *"The Problem: You can have several underlying causes that create identical readings but the definition of the output would be read as the same."*
> *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*

Applied here: a research programme that *defines* its target as unpredictable has not established Fig 8 :34-37's proved limit; it has authored a convention (Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*). And a single time series that could arise from chaos or from noise is a projection with lossy information, which is why Fig 13 :32 gives a *shape* (compounding remainder versus discarded detail) rather than a reading.

The same essay: *"What the Spectrometer Example Reveals: The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* That is the inward track of Fig 6 :29 in a different field.

## 5. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The figures README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

For CMP-0032 neither has occurred. No measurement settles a particular series here, and no outside frame has been imported with provenance. So the folder returns the shape and the protocol, not a name.

### 5b. Correction to my own §1 (kept visible)

I initially wrote that naming the two causes is forbidden by "shape, not a name." That overstates the rule. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* It governs a primitive not held in `Γ`. "Fundamental unpredictability", "chaos" and "noise" are not candidates for a seventh primitive (Fig 13 :124: *"none is a seventh member"*). Naming them is a **binding from the research frame** onto figure-held notions, not the invention of a new member. What remains forbidden is claiming that the binding itself is a derivation, or that a figure line returns the numeric cutoff. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened this session): *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous people who pronounce a system fundamental is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the separator a budget to be tried against a ground.

**The two tracks are opposed, and agreement inside a pool is worthless.** *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened this session): *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."* And: *"High contact builds exactly the shared understanding that makes communication work, but that same factor makes the learner's agreement with the teacher worthless as a check."* This is Fig 2 :40 and Fig 6 :31 in cognitive terms: two researchers reading the same model output are one frame wearing two names, and sharpening the inward track cannot certify the outward verdict.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened this session): *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And: *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of any forecasting system. They cannot settle the question they report on, and no claim in them is labelled `grounded`.
