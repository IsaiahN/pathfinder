# The Tether's answer: fundamental unpredictability versus incomplete data or imperfect models

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain distinction, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal numeric threshold. Not `grounded`.**

This is a shape and a protocol, not a result on any particular system. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A forecast misses an observation on a tracked slot. Research needs to know whether the miss is an absent atom (fundamental) or a term in the closure that was not reached (incomplete data, imperfect model). These look identical from inside the frame that made the forecast. The answer cannot be a single number; the question is a union compound with per-slot verdicts (Fig 9 :54-55; Fig 1 :19).

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **The two causes are different facts with different instructions.** Fig 8 :40-43: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."* The ordinary "incomplete data or imperfect models" is the second kind. Declaring it fundamental mints an atom that was not owed.
2. **Failure cannot prove absence.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* A long record of failure is a residual that persists, not a barrier theorem.
3. **From inside, the two look the same, so run two tracks.** Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."* Inward (sharpen the instrument); outward (find a frame with a differing closure). Keep the verdicts separate.
4. **Diagnose the channel before the closure.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; and :25 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. A repaired channel that removes the residual shows that the case was never fundamental. Fig 6 :34: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
5. **"Fundamental" is relative, not absolute.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*. A positive claim of in-principle unpredictability needs a proved barrier (Fig 8 :34, :37) or an outside witness (Fig 9 :19), never a stipulation.
6. **Chaos and noise are different compositions.** Fig 13 :32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Neither is a seventh member (Fig 13 :124). Chaos is a finite horizon, not an absent atom, according to the world sources.
7. **Per slot, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A global accuracy score cannot decide a per-slot question.
8. **The numeric cutoff is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. An instrument is owed; no figure line supplies a universal threshold.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular model, time series or system is fundamental or contingent. That needs a ground reading.
- Not that failure to predict proves fundamentality. Fig 8 :39 forbids it.
- Not that more data always helps. It helps only on the inward track, where something already registers.
- Not that chaos is an absence. Fig 13 :124 makes it a derivative; world sources make it in-principle predictable with a finite horizon.
- Not that Lyapunov exponents, surrogate tests, entropy rates or p-values decide the question. They are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that "fundamental" is absolute. Fig 12 :104 forbids a bottom.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument and an independent frame, in that dependency order:

1. Does anything register at any resolution (Fig 6 :34-35)? If yes, sharpen it inward.
2. Does the residual trend down under longer records, added observed variables, or a repaired channel? World sources give concrete forms: adding fast-field averages recovers parameters that slow-variable records leave weakly constrained (arXiv:2611.23526); sparse sensors can still recover leading Lyapunov exponents (arXiv:2611.23434).
3. Is there a proved barrier relative to a declared closure: a metaframe or limit argument of the kind in Fig 8 :34, :37? A stipulated limit does not count.
4. Does a frame with measurably different evidence corroborate the absence, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :51; Fig 2 :35)?
5. Is the verdict indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19)? If it is presented as one global number, the reading is unsafe.

## 6. Standing falsifiers

The five pre-registered conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, plus the structural ones in `06_isomers_and_order.md`: a single-frame proof of absence; a residual that trends down after inward repair; an atom found in the same closure after nothing registered; two differing frames agreeing and the ground settling the other way; a figure line supplying the numeric threshold; a seventh primitive or a derivation of one of the six from the other five; a global metric that correctly picks the live slot. Any one firing revises or withdraws the shape.

## 7. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set gives the separator, the prohibition, the protocol, the channel diagnosis and the shape. It does not give a decision number for a named system, and no instrument was run here. The world literature gives working discriminators, but a discriminator is a filter for the budget; the verdict still belongs to the ground. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* (figures README). We stop at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and record the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures already draw the distinction researchers need. Something that is underivable in principle from a model's closure and something merely unreachable at the current budget are different facts with different remedies: the first needs a proved barrier or a witness from a different frame, the second needs more search or a better instrument. Declaring the second kind fundamental adds an atom that was not owed. Failure to predict never proves absence, because no frame is a sealed room and "it is not in here" cannot be proved from inside. From inside the two cases look the same, so researchers must run the inward track (sharpen the instrument, repair the channel) and the outward track (find a frame with different evidence) separately and never read one against the other. "Fundamental" is irreducibility at a level, not an absolute bottom; chaos is a compounding remainder, noise is discarded detail reappearing, and neither is a new primitive. Verdicts are per slot, never a global average. What the framework does not supply is a universal numeric cutoff, and that is recorded as a gap with an instrument owed, not as silence.
