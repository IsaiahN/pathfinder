**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0.1 and the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The stage-1 output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable distinction between fundamental unpredictability and bad data

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A forecast misses an observation on a tracked slot. Two explanations compete. One says the target is underivable in principle from the model's current closure, so no amount of data or model refinement inside that frame will reach it. The other says the target is already reachable and the failure comes from a short record, a coarse sensor, a saturated metric, or a wrong model. These look the same from inside the model.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figure set I work from separates them as two different facts with different instructions. The underivable case requires a proved barrier or a witness from a frame whose closure differs. The unreachable-at-budget case requires more search or a better instrument, and declaring it fundamental adds an atom that was not owed. A failure to predict is never, by itself, a proof of absence, and "it is not in here" cannot be proved from inside. From inside, the two cases look the same, so the inward track (sharpen the instrument) and the outward track (find the other frame) have to be run separately and never read against each other. "Fundamental" is level-relative irreducibility, not an absolute bottom. Chaotic amplification and discarded-detail noise are different compositions with different remedies. I do not have a universal numeric cutoff; that part is a declared gap with an instrument owed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can decide your specific system from this email; that a Lyapunov number, an entropy estimate or a p-value settles the question; that long failure establishes fundamentality; or that a room of people agreeing would corroborate it. Verdicts are per slot and per domain, never averaged into one accuracy score.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this message:**
> 1. a single-frame computation that certifies a target is absent from the closure with no outside witness;
> 2. a case called underivable whose residual trends down when the record is lengthened or the instrument sharpened;
> 3. an instrument that returns nothing at any resolution and yet the atom later turns out to be in the same closure;
> 4. two frames with genuinely different evidence both calling a case fundamental and the ground later settling it as contingent;
> 5. a figure line that returns a universal numeric decision threshold (this would overturn my declared gap, which I would welcome).
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** not whether you like the framework, but what you would count as a failure of the separator, and which instrument in your field actually discriminates these two causes. Please record that answer before looking at any source I sent.
>
> **Only if you are still curious:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the method:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, state the mode of what you get, and keep inward and outward as separate tracks. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name / role]
