## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The figure set separates "underivable in principle from this closure" from "unreachable at this budget" as two different facts with different instructions (Fig 8 :40-43). The first requires a proved barrier or a witness from a frame whose closure differs; the second is already in the closure and calls for more search or a better instrument, not a new atom. A failure to predict is never a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39), "it is not in here" cannot be proved from inside (Fig 9 :18), and from inside the frame the two cases look the same (Fig 6 :27), so the inward and outward tracks must be run separately (Fig 6 :31). A saturated metric or pooled reading is a channel failure, not a fundamental limit (Fig 10 :24-28). "Fundamental" is irreducibility at a level, with no absolute bottom (Fig 12 :104). Chaos is a compounding remainder and noise is discarded detail reappearing above a crossing (Fig 13 :32); neither is a seventh member. Verdicts are per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). No universal numeric cutoff is supplied; that is a declared gap with an instrument owed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 8 and Figure 9
- **Statement:** the two candidate causes are different facts with different remedies, and a failed search cannot certify absence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :40-43 - *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."* Also Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* And Fig 9 :18 - *"φ* out of reach: nothing inside can prove that"*
- **Refuter:** a single-frame computation that certifies a target is absent from the closure with no import from a differing closure; or a sealed frame whose closure is demonstrably complete and fully searched. If either exists, C1 fails.

### C2 - operational; checkable against the researcher's own instrument and data
- **Statement:** the inward and outward tracks are opposed and must not be read against each other; the inward track is live only when something already registers, and a repaired channel removes a merely contingent residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :27 - *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 6 :31 - *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."* Fig 6 :34 - *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, does anything register at any resolution? Does lengthening the record, adding an observed variable or repairing the metric drive the residual down? If so, the case was unreachable at budget, not underivable.
- **Refuter:** a residual called underivable that trends down when the inward track is applied; or a case where nothing registered at any resolution and the atom is later found in the same closure without new growth. If either, C2 fails.

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading Figure 12 and Figure 13
- **Statement:** "fundamental" is level-relative, and chaos and discarded-detail noise are different compositions rather than new primitives.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 - *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"* Fig 13 :32 - *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Fig 13 :124 - *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*
- **What to check:** can you state the closure and the level relative to which "fundamental" is claimed? Can an instrument in your field distinguish a compounding remainder from discarded detail? World sources offer surrogate data testing and maximal Lyapunov exponents but also record their limits (the Perron effect, and sensitivity to noise).
- **Refuter:** derive one of the six terms from the other five, admit a seventh member, or show that a compounding remainder and discarded detail are indistinguishable at the same level by an instrument that normally discriminates sources. If any, C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict on any named system, dataset or model.
- Not that a long failed search proves fundamentality.
- Not that more data always helps; it helps only where something already registers.
- Not that chaos is an absence, or a seventh primitive.
- Not that a Lyapunov exponent, entropy rate, surrogate test or p-value decides the question. These are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not that a room of agreeing experts corroborates anything (Fig 2 :35, :40).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument and an outside frame, not more agreement:

1. Does anything register at any resolution (Fig 6 :34-35)?
2. Does the residual trend down under a longer record, an added observed variable, or a repaired channel? Concrete forms exist in the opened literature: adding fast-field averages or using sparse sensors can recover quantities that looked irreducible.
3. Is there a proved barrier relative to a declared closure (Fig 8 :34, :37), rather than a stipulated one?
4. Does a frame with measurably different evidence corroborate the absence, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :51; Fig 2 :35)?
5. Is the verdict indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19)? A single global number is unsafe.
