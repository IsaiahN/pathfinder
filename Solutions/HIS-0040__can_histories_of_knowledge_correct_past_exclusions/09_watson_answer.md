## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about correcting past exclusions in histories of knowledge. Option A: accept that a correction can simply add the missing voices, which treats a community as a single recoverable block. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, namely that the correction is possible only under six disciplines, and record the open slot where a ground is required. What each leads to: A produces a canonical list of recovered ingredients with the bonds omitted, replaces one monolith with another, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a history of knowledge predicts about who may count as a knower and what the ground returns in a specific slot. Arity: many, indexed per contribution, practitioner, place, institution and moment; a union of overlapping conditions, not a binary centre and periphery. Symmetry: asymmetric, because the original exclusion is a subtraction whose second operand's absence is the point, so the repair owes a negation rather than a symmetric join. Scale: two, first order the individual slot and second order the community aggregate, with a lossy membrane between. Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- **Domain 1, the record frame.** Actors: archives, canons, citation indexes, periodisations, editors, peer reviewers. Currency: credibility and credit. Anchor: the artefact and surviving practice, which do not update when a historian changes their mind. Question type: a union of overlapping conditions.
- **Domain 2, the community or tradition frame.** Actors: practitioners, dissenters, hybrid careers, rival schools, local lineages. Currency: practice, tool use, teaching, transmission, repair. Anchor: the ground as contacted by that practice. Question type: habitat to be enumerated, not composed; valence relative to the residual.
- **Domain 3, the correction and verification frame.** Actors: historians, archivists, instrument builders, community readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run the seats draw on overlapping evidence, so their independence is low and must be audited, not assumed.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); unbundle the union (Fig 9 :54); refuse the exclusion before minting a replacement term (Fig 12 :140); import with provenance (Fig 6 :23, :25-26); settle against the ground (Fig 2 :45).

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :51 (no standing list of good actors) versus citing a specific excluded contributor. Re-census resolves it: Fig 11 :51 governs a list whose valence is fixed ahead of the residual, while a citation driven by positive residual mass is valence relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). Different objects, no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The conditional yes survives; the unconditional no and the unconditional yes both fail. An exclusion can be corrected without making a community uniform only if the residual stays per slot, the community is unbundled as a union, the habitat is enumerated rather than composed, the exclusion is refused with a negation, the import carries provenance and adds access rather than a primitive, and the check is against a ground rather than shared agreement. Drop any condition and the correction produces a flat ingredient list with the bonds omitted, which is a second exclusion in the vocabulary of the first. The result is `specified`, ungrounded and open at the naming slot. The strongest single figure point is Fig 11 :51: a standing list of good actors does not survive a change of residual.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, but not trivial. If the six conditions are load-bearing and a historian ignores them, the correction installs a new canonical list whose representatives were appointed before the residual was described. That error persists in curricula and attribution. The packet makes it findable by stating the refuter and the provenance requirement up front, and by refusing to let a convention decide the outcome (Fig 10 :47). The avoidable part of the cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a characterised residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices directly.
- **undoable:** yes. A representative-voice binding is a term, not a primitive. Refusing it lowers the reach without deleting anything from the record, and the correction can be superseded by a better-characterised one (Fig 6 amendment: what is recorded only grows; what is reachable is derived and can fall). No tradition has to be withdrawn to repair a bad correction.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot audit showing that slot residuals collapse into one group-level variable without loss would break condition 1; a standing list that survives a change of residual and still matches the ground would break condition 3; a demonstration that additive insertion alone removes the excluded claim's standing would break condition 4; a partition proof on the exclusion conditions would break condition 2; a figure re-census admitting that a symmetric bond can carry an exclusion would break the asymmetry premise. Prestige, consensus and a shared evidence pool are not such instruments (Fig 2 :42).
