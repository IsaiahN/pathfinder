## 0. The question restated in effect terms

The question asks whether a repair is possible. A history of knowledge returns a record in which a contribution, a testimony, a technique, or a practitioner is missing or miscredited. The residual R is the gap between what that record predicts, namely who counts as a knower and what counts as knowledge, and what the ground returns, namely the artefact, the instrument, the surviving practice, the citation, the outcome. The repair is not simply to add the missing name. The question asks whether the repair can be made without introducing a second defect: treating the community or tradition as a single, internally uniform, static thing with one authentic voice.

Two failure modes are already visible in the effect statement:

1. **Aggregation.** If R is averaged across persons, places, moments and practices, the specific slot that failed to resolve disappears into the mean. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
2. **Composition of a representative.** If the correcting historian composes a list of good representatives for the community, that list is authored by the historian, not enumerated from the habitat. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The audit below derives what remains of the repair once both failure modes are refused.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- Arity: many. The residual is indexed per contribution, practitioner, place, institution and moment. It is a union of overlapping conditions (gender, race, class, language, archive survival, institutional position), not a binary opposition between a centre and a periphery. Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Symmetry: asymmetric. The original exclusion is a subtraction from the record, a `-` bond in which the second operand's absence is the point. The correction therefore owes a negation, not a symmetric join. Fig 12 :140: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."
- Scale: two. First order is the individual contribution and its slot in the record. Second order is the community or tradition as an aggregate. The membrane between them carries methods upward and head starts downward, never recordings. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

**One line per domain. Actors / currency / anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the record frame (historiography, archives, canons, citation, periodisation) | editors, archivists, citation indexes, peer reviewers, curricula | credibility and credit: who is cited, who is believed, whose dates are taught | the artefact and the surviving practice; it does not update when a historian changes their mind | a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition |
| D2, the community or tradition frame | practitioners, dissenters, hybrid careers, rival schools, local lineages | practice, tool use, teaching, repair, transmission | the ground as contacted by that practice, independent of D1's record | habitat to be enumerated, never composed |
| D3, the correction and verification frame | historians, archivists, instrument builders, community readers, editors | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | triangulation between low-overlap frames |

**Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A community is not a partition of separate kinds of person. It is a set of overlapping conditions, and a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds while leaving the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57).

**Dependency order.** (i) Describe the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28, :30-31). (ii) Unbundle the union (Fig 9 :54). (iii) Refuse the false exclusion with a negation before minting a replacement term (Formula step 2; Fig 12 :140). (iv) Import a provenanced atom from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23, :25-26). (v) Settle against the ground (Fig 2 :45).

**Strain test applied.** Fig 11 :51 (no standing list of good actors) could appear to conflict with the legitimate practice of citing a specific previously excluded contributor. Re-census resolves it: Fig 11 :51 governs a standing list whose valence is fixed ahead of the residual; citing a contributor because their slot carries positive residual mass is valence relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). Different objects, no strain after re-census. The reading that would make them conflict has misidentified the domain.

## 3. Findings

### F1. Aggregating the residual manufactures the very uniformity the question forbids (decisive)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"

An exclusion is not a property of a tradition as a whole. It is a per-slot failure: this observation was not recorded, this technique was attributed to the wrong person, this testimony was discounted, this archive was not preserved. A corrective history that reports, for example, that a tradition was suppressed has collapsed many live slots into one sentence. By Fig 1 :22 the signal that says where to look is gone. The correct form keeps the slots: for each contribution, what the record predicted and what the ground returns.

This is the figure-grounded reason the question's caution is well placed. The mistake is not moral only; it is an information-loss result.

**Refuter:** show that the per-slot residuals collapse into one group-level variable with no loss, that is, that a single tradition-level parameter predicts every slot's residual as well as the slot-level description. If that holds, aggregation loses nothing and F1 falls.

### F2. The uniform community is a composed habitat, not an enumerated one (decisive)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

To treat a community as internally uniform is to compose a habitat: to appoint a fixed cast of representatives whose valence is assumed in advance of the residual. Fig 11 :51 forbids the standing list. Fig 11 :49 gives the alternate rule: an actor is a benefactor or malfactor relative to a specific residual, so the same person may be a restorer for one slot and an obstruction for another. The enumeration rule in Fig 11 :43 says to list everything in contact with the residual and follow the cascade outward, not to assemble a representative sample chosen by the correcting historian.

Fig 11 :39 completes the point. Capability lives in agent-and-habitat. A correction that improves the historian's vocabulary without changing contact with the practice changes nothing (Fig 11: an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing).

**Refuter:** produce a correction that fixes a standing representative list in advance, keeps that list fixed as the residual changes from slot to slot, and still matches the ground per slot. If it does, the standing list is not disqualifying and F2 falls.

### F3. A union must be unbundled before a frame is chosen

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The exclusion of a community is a compound of conditions: gender, race, class, language, institutional affiliation, archive survival. It is not a list of separate exclusions that can be repaired one at a time. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The residual that remains after one lever is not a harder case of the same kind; it is a specific compound (Fig 9 :57-58).

This is the figure that governs the phrase "internally uniform". A uniform community is a bundle treated as if it were a single condition. The repair is to unbundle, not to speak on behalf of the bundle.

**Refuter:** show that the exclusion is a partition, that each condition applies to a disjoint set of slots, and that a single lever per condition clears the whole record without residue. Then the union reading is wrong.

### F4. Describe the residual before naming the frame

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

A correction that begins by choosing a community and then finding its contributions has inverted the order. Any frame will seem to fit once chosen. The figure requires the residual to be described in effect terms first: what did the record predict, what did the ground return, in which slot, at what scale. Only then is a frame selected, and the selection is a one-pass check against the described shape rather than a search (Fig 9 :29).

Fig 9 :32 limits what the description buys: a kind of thing, not the answer. The corrected history gets the shape of the missing contribution, not a name that can be asserted without a ground.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a frame selection that was made first and still matched every slot's residual, with the description written afterwards and shown to be redundant. Then the order claim is not load-bearing.

### F5. Refusing the exclusion is a negation, not an accumulation of names

Fig 12 :140: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."
Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

An additive correction, one that inserts recovered names into the existing canon without changing the claim that excluded them, leaves the exclusion standing as a term in the library. The vocabulary can only accumulate. The framework's refuted gap (Formula step 2; Fig 5 amendment) requires a term that refuses the earlier claim: not merely "X was also present" but "the claim that X could not be a knower is false". This is negation, a unary operator, not subtraction. Subtraction would remove a name; negation refuses a claim while leaving the record intact.

Fig 12 :5 explains why additive recovery fails structurally. A list of recovered names is an ingredient list. The bond, that is, the relation that made the contribution knowledge in its own habitat, is the half nothing was recording. Two histories can carry the same set of names and be different substances because the arrangement differs.

**Refuter:** show that additive insertion alone removes the excluded claim's standing to be composed over, not merely its frequency in the record. If insertion does that, the negation requirement is unnecessary and F5 falls.

### F6. The correction adds access, not a new primitive

Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."
Fig 6 :26: "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

A past exclusion is a wall in the record's closure. The repair imports a term from a frame whose closure differs, the practice frame, the instrument frame, the community's own transmission. Fig 6 :25-26 is explicit about what this is: the world does not gain a primitive; the record gains access to something already there. This is the precise sense in which a correction can be genuine without inventing a tradition or declaring a new kind of knowledge.

The source gate and shadow test from Fig 8 still apply. Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". A claim that a historian derived a contribution from the archive and a claim that it was imported from a practitioner look the same in the finished text. Provenance is the only discriminator.

**Refuter:** show that the corrected history genuinely added a primitive to the world, that is, that the contribution did not exist in any practice before the correction imported it. Then Fig 6 :25 is inapplicable to this domain.

### F7. Agreement is not corroboration when the frames share a literature

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

A committee of historians who all read the same secondary literature is one frame wearing many names. Their agreement on a corrected narrative is the pool talking, not a confirmation. Triangulation requires a second frame with a different closure and low overlap: the practitioner's instrument, the surviving artefact, the citation record, the oral transmission. Fig 2 :42 sets the budget rule: buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, which for a historical exclusion is high because the error persists in curricula and attribution for generations.

Fig 8 :45 and :47 give the positive form: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." and "Independence is one quantity read twice." The same low overlap that makes agreement meaningful is what gives the union a surplus.

**Refuter:** exhibit two correcting frames whose agreement survives a demonstrated audit of their evidence sources, showing low overlap. That would be a real second witness rather than a pool effect, and would strengthen rather than weaken the verdict.

### F8. The verifying seat must declare its stake and must not author the outcome

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"
Fig 10 :62-63: "A ruling carries provenance like an atom. The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."

A correction can become a new orthodoxy by convention: a committee declares a community's knowledge valid, and the declaration decides the outcome. Fig 10 :47 says a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. The legitimate form is to install what can be violated: a claim about which slots were excluded, with a refuter, checked against a ground. Fig 10 :59 forbids the fantasy of a disinterested historian but permits an interested one who declares the interest. The interest in the reading, meaning a stake in what value the ground returns today, is disqualifying.

Fig 10 :52 governs the outreach sequence: bring the characterised residual, not the whole theory, so the checker can refuse it without having joined the frame.

**Refuter:** show a correction whose authority comes entirely from a declared convention and which nevertheless settles the per-slot residual against the ground. If a convention can settle, the seat rules do not apply to this domain.

### F9. Different routes can arrive at the same correction

Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :96: "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."

Two corrected histories may cite exactly the same set of recovered contributors and differ completely, because one preserves the bond to the practice and the other flattens the contribution into an ingredient. Fig 12 :117 makes this a structural fact, not a rhetorical preference. Fig 13 :121 adds that different routes can converge on the same substance, which is why independent derivation from a different closure is corroboration when it genuinely is independent.

This finding is where the question's second half is answered positively. The tradition is not a fixed substance with one correct telling. It is the set of arrangements over the contributions, and the arrangements are not exhausted by any one history.

**Refuter:** exhibit two corrected histories with identical ingredient sets and identical bonds that still behave differently at the ground. Then the bond is not carrying the difference and F9 falls.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the framework's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The verdict, namely that the correction is possible only under the six disciplines above, is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A corrected history that treats a community as a single frame and fixes a standing representative list still matches the ground per slot across a change of residual (breaks F2).
2. The per-slot residuals are shown to be a sufficient statistic of one group-level variable, so aggregation loses no signal (breaks F1).
3. Additive insertion of names alone is shown to remove the excluded claim's standing to be composed over, without a refusal (breaks F5).
4. The exclusion is shown to be a partition into disjoint conditions, so a single lever per condition clears every intersection (breaks F3).
5. A correction's authority is shown to come from a declared convention that nevertheless settles the residual against the ground (breaks F8).

If any fires, the conditional yes becomes an unconditional yes for that domain and the relevant discipline is withdrawn. Until one is run, the verdict stays `candidate`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Keep the residual per slot. Report which contribution, which attribution, which archive, which moment. Never report a tradition-level average as the finding.
2. Enumerate the habitat outward from the residual. Who is in contact with this slot, and who is in contact with them? Do not appoint representatives.
3. Write the effect description before choosing the frame. Cast it in effect terms so it can travel to a frame with a different closure.
4. Refuse the exclusion explicitly. Add the provenanced contribution and refuse the claim that excluded it. Record provenance and date.
5. Buy independence where the cost of being wrong is high. Pair an archive-based reader with a frame that has not read the same literature, and record the evidence overlap before pooling anything.
6. Declare the correcting historian's stake. Do not let a convention decide the outcome.
7. Send the residual and its refuter before the theory (Fig 10 :52). Ask what would refute the correction, not whether the reader agrees.
