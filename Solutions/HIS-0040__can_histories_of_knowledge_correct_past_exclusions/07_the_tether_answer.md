# The Tether's answer: correcting exclusions without flattening communities

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A history of knowledge predicts who counts as a knower and what counts as knowledge. The ground is the artefact, the instrument, the testimony, the surviving practice. The residual is the gap between the two, indexed per contribution, practitioner, place and moment. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The past exclusion is a subtraction from the record; the correction owes a refusal of the claim that excluded it (Fig 12 :140: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate.").

The question is not whether to correct. It is whether the correction can avoid a second error: replacing a canonical monolith with a canonical list of recovered voices.

## 2. The answer

**Yes, conditionally.** The correction is possible without treating a community or tradition as internally uniform if and only if all six conditions hold:

1. **Keep the residual per slot.** Aggregation is how the live signal disappears (Fig 1 :22). A tradition-level average is not a finding.
2. **Treat the community as a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).
3. **Enumerate the habitat, do not compose a representative list.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Valence is relative to the residual, not a property carried by a person (Fig 11 :49).
4. **Refuse the exclusion explicitly.** Add the provenanced contribution and refuse the claim that excluded it. This is a negation, not an accumulation of names, and not a subtraction of a name.
5. **Import access with provenance.** Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :26: "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access." The record gains access to knowledge a practice already held. Source gate and shadow test are required (Fig 8 :50-52).
6. **Triangulate against a ground that does not update.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A committee that read the same literature is one frame with many names (Fig 2 :40). Declare the correcting historian's stake (Fig 10 :59).

Drop any one and the correction manufactures the uniformity it was supposed to avoid. A correction with a fixed representative list, an additive name list, and no provenance is a flat formula (Fig 12 :5): it records ingredients and omits the bonds that made them knowledge.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any community has a single authentic voice.
- Not that corrections are neutral. The primitives and inclusion rules are a visible choice, and visible is not neutral.
- Not that adding names to a canon is by itself a repair.
- Not that consensus among historians settles anything. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the framework returns a name for the excluded contribution. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. No citation index, archive or curriculum was measured.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A measurement, not a further argument. The relevant instruments would be:

1. A per-slot audit showing whether slot residuals collapse into one group-level variable without loss. If they do, condition 1 falls.
2. A change-of-residual test on any proposed standing representative list. If the list survives and still matches the ground per slot, condition 3 falls.
3. A test of whether additive insertion alone removes the excluded claim's standing to be composed over. If it does, condition 4 falls.
4. A partition test on the conditions of exclusion. If each condition applies to a disjoint set of slots and one lever per condition clears the intersections, condition 2 falls.
5. A provenance audit pairing an archive-based reader with a practitioner who has not read the same literature. If their low-overlap agreement survives, the correction gains a real second witness (Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone.").

The pre-registered refuter is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 and in the outreach packet. No measurement has been run.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is available; the names are not. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The corrected history stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Different routes can arrive at the same substance (Fig 13 :121), so a single history never exhausts a tradition; but agreement among routes that share an evidence pool is not corroboration.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether's answer is yes, but conditionally. A history of knowledge can correct a past exclusion without treating a community as internally uniform if it keeps the residual indexed per contribution and practitioner, treats the community as a union of overlapping conditions rather than a single frame, enumerates the habitat instead of appointing representatives, refuses the claim that excluded the contribution rather than merely adding names, imports access from a frame whose closure differs with provenance recorded, and checks the result against a ground rather than against agreement among readers of the same literature. Drop any one of those and the repair replaces one monolith with another, a flat list of recovered ingredients with the bonds left out. The framework returns that shape, not a name for the tradition or its authentic voice. What would settle it is a measurement, not another argument: a per-slot audit, a change-of-residual test on any representative list, and a low-overlap pair of independent frames. Until those run, the answer stays a specified, ungrounded candidate.
