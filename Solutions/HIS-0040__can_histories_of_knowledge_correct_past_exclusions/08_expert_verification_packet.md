# Expert verification packet: the Tether on correcting past exclusions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of knowledge, archivist or social epistemologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No historical measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A history of knowledge can correct a past exclusion without treating a community or tradition as internally uniform, but only under six conditions. The residual, the gap between what the record predicts about who may count as a knower and what the ground returns, must be kept per contribution, practitioner, place and moment. The community must be treated as a union of overlapping conditions and unbundled before a frame is chosen. The habitat must be enumerated from the residual outward, with no standing list of representatives fixed in advance. The exclusion claim must be refused with a negation, not merely outnumbered by added names. The repair imports access to knowledge a practice already held, with provenance and a shadow test. The result must be triangulated against a ground, not against agreement among readers of the same literature. Drop any one and the correction produces a second exclusion, a canonical list of recovered ingredients with the bonds left out.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** aggregation and a standing representative list are disqualifying, and the community is a union to be unbundled.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **Refuter:** show that per-slot residuals are a sufficient statistic of one group-level variable with no loss, OR that a standing representative list survives a change of residual and still matches the ground per slot, OR that the conditions of exclusion form a partition with disjoint slots. Any one of these breaks C1.

### C2. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 12 and the formula's route step

- **Statement:** the repair owes a refusal before a mint, and imports access rather than adding a primitive.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :26 "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access." Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
- **What to check:** is a refusal actually required, or does insertion of a name remove the excluded claim's standing by itself? Does the imported term add access to existing practice, or is a genuinely new primitive being smuggled in without a source gate? Is provenance recorded?
- **Refuter:** show that additive insertion alone removes the excluded claim's standing to be composed over, OR that the correction genuinely added a primitive to the world rather than access to one already held. Either breaks C2.

### C3. Cross-frame; checkable against the actual historiography

- **Statement:** agreement among readers of the same literature is not corroboration, and a low-overlap second frame is what can move the record.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **What to check:** in a specific correction, did the agreeing historians draw on the same secondary literature? If so, their agreement is one witness. Is there a frame with a genuinely different closure, such as a practitioner community, an instrument, or an archive assembled before the question was asked?
- **Refuter:** exhibit two correcting frames whose agreement survives a documented audit of their evidence sources showing low overlap. That strengthens the verdict rather than breaking it; if no such pair can exist for this domain, C3 becomes vacuous and should be withdrawn.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any community has a single authentic voice or that a tradition is a fixed object.
- Not that corrective histories are neutral. The primitives and inclusion rules are a visible choice, and visibility is not neutrality.
- Not that adding names to a canon is by itself a repair.
- Not that consensus among historians settles anything (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the framework names the excluded contribution or its proper representative. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not `grounded`. No archive, citation index or curriculum was measured in this derivation.
- Not a claim that world sources decide the answer. They corroborate at low overlap; the figures decide.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot audit: do slot residuals collapse into one group-level variable without loss?
2. A change-of-residual test: does any proposed standing representative list survive and still match the ground per slot?
3. An insertion test: does adding names alone remove the excluded claim's standing to be composed over?
4. A partition test: are the exclusion conditions disjoint, so one lever per condition clears the intersections?
5. A provenance and overlap audit: can two genuinely low-overlap frames be found for a specific correction?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 9 :54 and :55, Fig 11 :43 and :51. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one correction you know: was the residual kept per slot, and was a representative list fixed in advance?
3. Check C3 against the same correction: did the agreeing readers share a literature? Name one frame with a different closure, or say why none exists.
4. Run one of the five tests in section 5 on a case you know well.
5. Send the failure. A refutation is more useful than an endorsement.
