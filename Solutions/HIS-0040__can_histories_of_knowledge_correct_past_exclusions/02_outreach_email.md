# Outreach draft: check the shape of a correction before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Not for bulk send. No request for agreement, no request for trust.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode label and the refuter below are intact. If either is removed, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about correcting exclusions in histories of knowledge

> Hello [name],
>
> I am working on a question in the historiography of knowledge and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a history of knowledge can correct a past exclusion without treating a community or tradition as internally uniform, but only under six conditions. The residual, meaning the gap between what the record predicts about who counts as a knower and what the ground actually returns, must be kept per contribution, practitioner, place and moment. Fig 1 :19 states the rule: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A tradition must be treated as a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate kinds of person (Fig 9 :55). The habitat must be enumerated from the residual outward, and no standing list of representatives may be fixed in advance (Fig 11 :43, :51). The exclusion claim must be refused explicitly, not merely outnumbered by added names, because a vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate (Fig 12 :140). The repair imports access to knowledge the practice already held (Fig 6 :25), with provenance recorded. Agreement among readers of the same literature is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
>
> Drop any one of those six and the correction replaces one monolith with another: a canonical list of recovered voices whose valence was fixed before the residual was described.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any community has a single authentic voice; that the corrective history is neutral; that adding names to a canon is by itself a repair; that consensus among historians settles anything; or that this output is settled by a measurement. It is a shape, not a verdict. The stake of the person writing the correction is declared, not denied, because an interest in the reading is disqualifying even though an interest in the outcome is unavoidable (Fig 10 :59).
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. per-slot residuals are a sufficient statistic of one group-level variable, so aggregation loses no signal;
> 2. a standing representative list survives a change of residual and still matches the ground per slot;
> 3. additive insertion of names alone removes the excluded claim's standing to be composed over, with no refusal;
> 4. the exclusion is a partition into disjoint conditions, so one lever per condition clears every intersection;
> 5. a declared convention settles the residual against the ground.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding condition. A one-line note beginning with this fails because is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. Figures first, summaries second.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: local copy in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, canonical figures README on the public repository at https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on epistemic injustice, https://iep.utm.edu/epistemic-injustice/
> - Wikipedia entry on epistemic injustice with its bibliography, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_injustice
>
> I am not asking you to endorse any of them. They are offered so you can locate the claims if you want to.
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** run it on a correction you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result before you start. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why the order is this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter before the theory | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A checker who has absorbed the framework has joined the frame being checked. |
| No request for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule: general, specified, grounded; state which. |
| Refuter written before the run | Pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5. |
| Links after the verdict | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the whole architecture first sends a recording, not a generator. |
| No representative voice appointed | Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." |
| Union, not partition | Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." |
| Provenance required | Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" |
| Stake declared | Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying" |
| Failure more useful than success | Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." |

## What this message does not do

It does not ask the recipient to trust the framework, to accept the author's authority, or to join a list of supporters. It does not select recipients by likely agreement. It does not send an attachment to a distribution list. It does not present agreement from a shared evidence pool as a witness (Fig 2 :40).
