# Productive and entrenched as isomers, and the order of the loop

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer claim and the order claim, `general` on the structural analogy.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit in `01` returned a union separator but left two structural questions open. First: if a productive controversy and an entrenched controversy are built from the same ingredients, what makes them different substances? Second: in what order must the checks run, and which orderings are forbidden? A third, smaller question is whether the four kinds of controversy distinguished in the science-education source are different atoms or different bonds.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried, not repeated)

Atoms held for this question: ground, independence, per-slot residual, discriminator, method, import, refusal, room. Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Actors, currency, and anchor for D1 through D4 are in `01` section 1.2.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are productive and entrenched controversies isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94-96: **"The arrangement is the substance. Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**
- Fig 12 :97-98 supplies the canonical case: *"Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation. Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; named isomerism by Berzelius, 1830."*

Take a productive controversy and an entrenched one over the **same atom set**: two frames, a ground, a residual, a discriminator, a method, a community, and a room. The ingredient list can be identical. What differs is the arrangement:

- Productive: frames **range against** the ground separately, the discriminator **crosses** the membrane, the method **is imported** from a differing closure, and the community **refuses** what the ground rejects. The bond between frames and ground is a ranging relation, not a mutual update.
- Entrenched: frames **update on each other**, the room is handed along unchanged, the recording is **replayed**, and the community **accumulates** without a refusal operator. The bond between frames and ground has been replaced by a bond among the frames themselves.

Same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The difference is invisible in an ingredient list ("both had two camps, evidence, and a journal") and decisive in the outcome. That is Fig 12's definition applied, not an analogy imported from chemistry. The chemistry case is cited by the figure itself, so it is part of the figure's own grounding rather than a separate world witness.

**Qualification.** The isomer reading holds only over a **fixed atom set**. If the productive controversy has a discriminator and a provenanced import that the entrenched one genuinely lacks, then the atom sets differ and the two are not isomers; they are two different compositions, one with an atom the other has no access to (Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*). For continental drift versus cold fusion, the atom sets do differ: seafloor magnetometry and mantle convection are present in one and absent in the other. So the isomer claim is a **structural claim about the separator**, not a historical claim about those two cases. This qualification is load-bearing and is named as a strain in section "STRAINS".

### Q2. Which bond differs, exactly?

Two candidate bonds are available in the figure text:

- Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Triangulation is the grounded arrangement.
- Fig 2 :13-15: **"no anchor; each updates on the others / they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"** (collapse 1, coherent drift). This is the entrenched arrangement.
- Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (collapse 3, the mirror chain).

The differing bond is therefore not "amount of disagreement" and not "quality of evidence". It is the **relation between the frames and the ground**: ranging against a non-updating anchor on one side, mutual update and handed-along agreement on the other. Under Fig 12 :59 the test for a symmetric bond is to swap the operands and ask whether meaning changes. Swapping "frame ranges against ground" with "ground updates on frame" plainly changes the substance, so the bond is ordered. That matches the finding in `01` that the process is ordered and "entrenched" is not a symmetric mirror of "productive".

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** A discriminator or a reapplicable method is a catalyst in this reading: it survives its use and changes behaviour across uses. Fig 12 :119 also says *"Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* A controversy that replays a one-occasion recording has a spent part where a catalyst should be, which is why it cannot produce a new result (Fig 4 :25).
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input."** A mirror chain is a positive loop with no ground pressure; that is why agreement compounds without evidence.
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."** The community-with-a-refusal-seat is a functional group in this reading: its presence fixes whether a held term can be refused, and the qualifier says which community it is.

### Q3. What is the order, and which orders are forbidden?

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 9 :43: **"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."** The three ranges are ordered and cheap only in that order (Fig 9 :45: *"Describing the residual is the expensive part. Leaving, looking up and recognising are one step each, and cheap only in that order."*).
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: refuse before mint. A term held to be false owes a refusal, and *"no arrangement of the other four reaches it"* (the fifth bin). This is why a "more evidence" response to an entrenched controversy can never settle it if the held regularity itself is false.
- Fig 12 :106: **"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."** Settlement is necessary before a term can be composed over, so a method cannot be carried up until it has paid.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Residual first, frame second (Fig 8 :53).

Forbidden orders: (a) choosing the explanatory frame before describing the residual; (b) minting a new term before refusing the false held one; (c) carrying a method up before the ground has paid for it; (d) optimising a link above the break when the objective link is missing. Each is a named failure in `01` findings F3, F7, F5, and F4 respectively.

### Q4. Are the four kinds of controversy different atoms or different bonds?

The UC Berkeley toolkit distinguishes fundamental scientific controversy, secondary scientific controversy, conflict over the ethicality of methods, conflict over applications, and conflict between a scientific idea and a non-scientific viewpoint. It says true controversy *"sparks careful examination of the data and additional research"*, while a conflict over applications is *"not a conflict over a scientific idea, but over how such ideas should be applied."*

Under Fig 12 these are **different bonds over a partly shared atom set**, not different atoms:

- Fundamental and secondary controversy both involve frames, a ground, and a residual; they differ in scale, which is a parameter of the network atom (Fig 13 :24, *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*), not a new member of the set.
- Ethical and applications conflicts bind the same idea to a different second operand (a method's appropriateness, a technology's use). Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- A conflict between a scientific idea and a non-scientific viewpoint has no shared ground that both parties range against. By the anchor test (Fig 2 :29), it is **not yet a scientific domain** in this census; it is excluded here and belongs to a different question.

This is why the audit treats the four kinds as a union requiring unbundling (Fig 9 :54-58), not as four variables to be averaged.

## CONCLUSION

Productive and entrenched controversies are **isomers over a fixed atom set**: same frames, ground, residual, method, and community, different bond between frames and ground, decisive difference in outcome, invisible in an ingredient list. The historical pairs used as world sources are **not** themselves isomers, because their atom sets differ by an imported discriminator and mechanism; that qualification is kept visible. The order of the checks is fixed by dependency: describe the residual, refuse what is false, settle before composing, then carry the method up and import from a differing closure. The four public kinds of controversy are different bonds over partly shared atoms, and one of them has no scientific anchor at all and is excluded.

## FALSIFIER, written before this run

The isomer claim fails if either outcome class can be written with an atom the other does not require, so that the shared-atom condition is not met. The bond claim fails if a case is found where frames update on each other and the reference moves when pushed, yet a new ground-settled method still crosses. The order claim fails if a controversy is settled by carrying a method up before the ground has paid for it, or by minting before refusing a held false term, with the settlement verified against ground. If any fires, `07_the_tether_answer.md` is revised and the failure is recorded in `01` section 7 rather than hidden.
