# Expert verification packet: the Tether on scientific controversy

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the missing condition of any particular controversy. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of a controversy is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The outcome split between productive and entrenched scientific disagreements is a union of overlapping conditions, not two kinds of controversy and not a single cause with a right amount. A disagreement becomes productive when six conditions hold jointly across four domains: its frames draw on different evidence and range separately against a ground that does not move when they push on it; its residual is indexed per slot and a discriminator exists; a reapplicable method crosses in and any missing atom is imported with provenance from a differing closure; and its community can refuse one of its own held terms and changes rooms as the question changes. An entrenched disagreement is missing at least one of those, and which one differs case by case. The two outcome classes are isomers over a fixed atom set: same atoms, different bond, decisive difference in behaviour. The framework returns that shape, not the name of the missing condition in any historical case.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal plus history-facing: the ground and independence conditions

- **Statement:** a non-updating anchor and evidence-level independence are jointly necessary for a disagreement to range against a ground. When both fail, agreement is coherent drift or a shared pool talking, not triangulation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **What to check:** in a controversy you know, identify the thing that did not move when a disputant pushed on it. If there was none, say so. Then ask whether the two camps drew on different evidence or read the same reports. The Wikipedia pages on cold fusion and continental drift are offered as illustrations, not as evidence for the framework.
- **Refuter:** a controversy settled by ground while both frames shared one evidence pool and the reference demonstrably moved when they pushed on it, with no new non-updating anchor introduced.

### C2. Framework-internal: union structure and per-slot indexing

- **Statement:** a disagreement that does not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, is a bundled union of well-formed questions and must be split before it is searched; residuals are per slot, never pooled.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **What to check:** take an entrenched controversy you know and try to name the subsets on which each side's rule works. If you can name two or more, the union reading applies. If you cannot, the claim may not apply to that case; that is useful information, not a failure of the case.
- **Refuter:** a bundled controversy settled by one lever across every overlapping condition, with no unbundling and no per-slot residual, verified against ground.

### C3. Framework-internal plus method-facing: methods, imports, and refusal

- **Statement:** only a reapplicable method crosses a scale boundary; a recording of one occasion produces repetition, not a new result. A missing atom must be imported from a differing closure with provenance and a shadow test, and a community must be able to refuse a held term before a new one can be minted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Operators :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
- **What to check:** in a controversy that became productive, name what actually crossed: a method, an instrument, a provenanced import, or a replay of the original result. Then ask who could say that a held term was wrong. If nobody could, the refusal condition fails.
- **Refuter:** a controversy settled by replaying one successful result with no method and no import, or settled in a community with no refusal operator and no external seat able to refuse for it, with the settlement paying against ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a two-list partition of controversies, not a personality or status explanation, and not a universal recipe.
- Not a productivity score, a ranking of the six conditions, or an average across domains.
- Not a name for the missing condition in a specific controversy. That is a declared `gap` (F10 in `01`), and *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* (Fig 13 :134).
- Not a derivation of the historical illustrations. They are corroboration with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`, and convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, so only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not an explanation of science versus non-science conflicts, which have no shared scientific anchor (UC Berkeley toolkit; Fig 2 :29).
- Not `grounded`. Every sub-claim is `specified` or `general`; nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried in `05`): a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, so the instrument, not the debate, resolves the case. Here the instrument would have to report, per controversy slot: whether the reference is non-updating, whether the frames are independent at the evidence level, whether the residual is indexed and the union unbundled, whether an instrument already returns something unresolved, whether a method rather than a recording crossed, whether an import has provenance and a shadow test, and whether refusal is possible. No such instrument is claimed to exist. The seven refuters in section 2 and in `01` section 4 are cheaper tests; any one of them firing settles the matter against the claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :31, :35 and Fig 9 :4-5 (four short passages, two minutes).
2. Pick one controversy you know well. Name the non-updating anchor, if any, and the evidence pools of the two sides (five minutes).
3. Try to name the subsets on which each side's rule works. If you find two or more, test whether anyone unbundled them (five minutes).
4. Name what crossed: a method, an imported atom with provenance, or a replay of one occasion. Name who could refuse a held term (five minutes).
5. Send the refutation, or say which condition you could not locate. A failure is more useful than an endorsement (three minutes).
