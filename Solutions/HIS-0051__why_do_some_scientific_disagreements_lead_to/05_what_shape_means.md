## 2. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (carried): *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the specific missing thing. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- **Shape:** a union separator of six jointly necessary conditions over four domains, each with a ground and a refuter, yielding per-controversy verdicts and no average.
- **Not a shape but a name:** "the reason cold fusion failed was confirmation bias", "the reason plate tectonics succeeded was better evidence", "the productivity of a controversy is proportional to its methodological diversity". Each fills the slot with a specific thing the framework does not return.

## 5. Correction to an overstatement (kept visible)

A first pass of this file read the "shape, not a name" rule as forbidding the naming of any condition at all. That was wrong, and the correction is kept here rather than silently erasing the earlier reading.

The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive **not held in Γ**. The conditions in this separator (anchor, independence, per-slot residual, discriminator, method crossing, refusal, room change) are not missing primitives; they are bindings onto figure terms that are already held: the ground (Symbols :102-106), independence (Fig 2 :31), the per-slot residual (Fig 1 :19), the instrument (Fig 6 :32), the membrane (Fig 4 :22), negation (Operators :58), and the room (Fig 7). Naming them is **provenance**, not the invention of a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"*).

What **remains** forbidden is what F10 records: naming the primitive missing from a specific controversy, or returning a productivity number. That is the genuine shape limit and it is not retracted.

The exemplar's `05` section 5b and `06` Q1 made the same correction for Gradient and Spectrum. This is the same rule applied to this question, not a new exception.

## 6. The source-discrimination point

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried) gives the mechanism that matters for history of science. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*, one green light or yellow plus blue or RGB or CMYK or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a controversy can appear entrenched for several different reasons that produce the same observable reading (two camps that will not agree). Calling all of them "bias" or "status" reads the source off a projection. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. This is why F4 and F6 demand an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve, and why the answer stops at a shape.

## 7. Two consequences for this solution

It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action.
