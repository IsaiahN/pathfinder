# The Tether's answer: why some disagreements become productive and others stay entrenched

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and on each domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the missing condition of any particular controversy. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A variance is observed across controversies: some end in new methods and stronger explanations, others persist unchanged for decades. The question asks what separates the two classes. The residual is the gap between the observed outcome class and the outcome predicted by the conditions present in that controversy, **indexed per controversy slot** (Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Pooling controversies into a field-wide or era-wide rate would throw away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :22).

The question is a **union compound**, not a two-list partition. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."* The UC Berkeley toolkit's distinction between fundamental, secondary, ethical, applications, and non-scientific conflicts is the same warning from science education. So the answer is a conjunction of necessary conditions with per-domain verdicts, never a single cause, never an average.

## 2. The shape: one loop per domain

### Loop D1, the frames in disagreement

- **Actors:** two or more research programs, each a frame with a library, atoms, bonds, and a weighting.
- **Currency:** predictions, residuals, and the evidential pool they draw on.
- **Anchor:** a ground each frame can range against separately.
- **Verdict (`specified`, triangulated):** productive when the frames draw on genuinely different evidence (Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*) and range against a non-updating ground. Entrenched when they share one evidence pool and update on each other (Fig 2 :13-15). Independence is one quantity read twice (Fig 8 :47), so it is necessary, not sufficient, and it also raises the cost of agreement (Fig 2 :42).
- **Refuter:** a controversy settled by ground while both frames shared a demonstrably identical pool throughout and no new low-overlap frame entered.

### Loop D2, the ground and instruments

- **Actors:** instruments, replications, panels, discriminators.
- **Currency:** contact with a non-updating ground; description cost in bits and action cost in settling (Fig 12 :125-127).
- **Anchor:** the ground itself.
- **Verdict (`specified`, triangulated):** productive when the residual is stated per slot and a discriminator exists, an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :32-34: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*). Entrenched when the middle link, what would count as progress, has no instrument (Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*). The two currencies do not add; a cheap description over a costly operand may take a whole budget to settle (Fig 12 :128-129).
- **Refuter:** a controversy that remained entrenched while an accepted, well-instrumented objective criterion was continuously available to both sides.

### Loop D3, the community and checking arrangement

- **Actors:** reviewers, editors, rooms, funding panels.
- **Currency:** agreement, prestige, residuals, conventions.
- **Anchor:** the ground, never agreement.
- **Verdict (`specified`, triangulated):** productive when the community can refuse one of its own held terms (Operators :58, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*) and when the room changes as the question changes (Fig 10 :72). Entrenched when the reference updates on the community's own reports, so the drift is coherent and nobody notices (Fig 2 :34), when a convention decides an outcome (Fig 10 :47), or when agreement compounds while evidence does not (Fig 7 :12-13). Prestige is a budget filter here, never a witness (Fig 9 :60; RSI essay, *"Prestige can fund an action... But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*).
- **Refuter:** a community with no refusal operator that nevertheless settles a disagreement by ground, or a chain of demonstrably identical rooms that produces ground-settled progress.

### Loop D4, the temporal and method channel

- **Actors:** methods (generators), recordings, head starts, imported atoms, the next room.
- **Currency:** reapplicable method versus one-occasion recording; bits and actions.
- **Anchor:** settlement of a term before it can be composed over.
- **Verdict (`specified`, triangulated):** productive when a generator crosses up and a head start comes down (Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*), and when a missing atom is imported from a frame whose closure differs with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23). Entrenched when a recording is replayed (Fig 4 :25), when the frame searches for what is underivable from inside (Fig 8 :32, *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*), or when the wrong atom is imported at a cost above starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24). Histories illustrate rather than decide: continental drift gained a mechanism and instruments from oceanography and geophysics; cold fusion renamed its community rather than moving a wall.
- **Refuter:** a controversy settled by replaying one successful result into a substituted habitat with no method and no import, and with the settlement paying against ground.

### The union, unbundled

The four loops do not average. A controversy can have a fine ground (D2) and a mirror community (D3); that is not "moderately productive". A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-58). The answer is therefore:

> **Productive** = all six conditions present across D1 to D4 and reachable at the budget. **Entrenched** = at least one necessary condition absent, and which one differs per case. The outcome classes are isomers over a fixed atom set (`06_isomers_and_order.md`), same atoms, different bond, different substance.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a two-list partition, not a personality account, and not a statement that one controversy is a "harder case" of another. A case in two conditions is a compound, not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57-58).
- Not a universal recipe, a productivity percentage, or a ranking of conditions. *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."* (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a name for the missing condition in a particular controversy. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32). F10 is a declared `gap`, and *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* (Fig 13 :134).
- Not a derivation of the historical cases. They were chosen after the residual was described, so they are corroboration with provenance, not products of the loop (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not an explanation of conflicts between science and non-scientific viewpoints. Those have no shared scientific anchor and are outside this census (UC Berkeley toolkit; Fig 2 :29). HIS-0052 covers the status and institutional-rivalry slice of D3.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a controversy was taken for this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A discriminator is owed, not a further argument. The relevant questions for a particular controversy are:

1. Does the reference move when a frame pushes on it? If yes, there is no anchor in that case (Fig 2 :29-30).
2. Do the two frames draw on the same evidence pool? If yes, their agreement is the pool talking and their union has no surplus (Fig 2 :40, Fig 8 :47-49).
3. Is the residual indexed per slot, and is the compound unbundled? If not, one lever will leave intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-58, Fig 1 :19).
4. Is there an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve? If nothing has ever registered at any resolution, this is not an instrument problem but an absence, and the two must not be confused (Fig 6 :32-40; Fig 8 :40-43).
5. Did a method, rather than a recording of one occasion, cross (Fig 4 :22-26)?
6. Was the atom imported with provenance and a shadow test, or adopted without a record (Fig 8 :50-52)?
7. Could the community refuse a held term, and did the room change with the question (Operators :58, Fig 10 :72)?

A source-discriminating instrument, in the sense of *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried in `05`), would be one that reports these seven per slot and returns a per-controversy verdict. Building it is a separate task; nothing here claims it has been built.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The loop stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* (figures README). There is no last room (Fig 7 :6), so a complete theory of scientific controversy is not on offer; each handoff to a different closure (philosophy of science, STS, cognition, history of science) closes some of the gap and none arrives (Fig 7 :30-32). The world sources used here agree with the shape, but most are low-overlap only relative to the figures; the framework essay shares the author's evidence pool completely and is not a second witness. If a seventh figure line were produced that named the missing primitive of a specific controversy, the gap would close. Until then the verdict stands as `candidate`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a recipe for productive controversy and does not rank causes. It says the question is a union of overlapping conditions, not two kinds of disagreement. A controversy becomes productive when its frames draw on different evidence and range separately against something that does not move when they push on it, when its residual is stated per slot and a discriminator exists, when a reapplicable method crosses in and any new atom is imported with provenance from a differing frame, and when its community can refuse its own held terms and changes rooms as the question changes. An entrenched controversy is missing at least one of those, and which one differs per case; the two outcome classes are isomers, same atoms arranged with a different bond. The framework returns that shape, not the name of the missing condition in any historical case, and it writes its failure conditions before the check. The gap stays open until an instrument settles it.
