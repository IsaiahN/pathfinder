## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, between two offered answers to Information Theory.txt line 21: Option A, the information content of a physical system is observer-dependent; Option B, it can be specified objectively. What each leads to: A makes the value depend on a frame that can change, so the thing that would settle the value moves when pushed; B requires a value invariant across frames fixed by something that is not a frame, and leaves the choice of coarse-graining unexplained. A third move is available in the figures: refuse the "or" as a union compound, split the bundle, and return a shape with separate per-domain verdicts. The pre-bound sought-for shape is written in `01` section 0 so it can be refused by the same test as everything else.

- **Domain 2, frame and pose.** Actors: observer, measurement context, assigned distribution, pose, interpreter, reference machine. Currency: mutual information, conditional entropy, description length. Anchor: not the observer's judgment, but the ground of Domain 1, which does not move when a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29-30). Nameable as a frame, not as an anchor.
- **Domain 3, semantic and interpretive.** Actors: interpreter, code, meaning, truthfulness. Currency: semantic content. Anchor: no nameable non-updating anchor was found at this budget, so this is not yet a domain. It is a bundled condition to split off (Fig 9 :54).
- **Domain 4, framework-internal.** Actors: Γ, R, slots s, F, T_A and T_E, ground. Currency: residual, cost. Anchor: the ground (Symbols :104-106), which is outside the library and does not update.
- **Partition or union:** union. The offered "or" bundles three conditions. "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57-58).
- **Order by dependency:** refuse the forced "or" first (Formula step 2), settle the physical ground second, apply the pose transform third, measure the cross-scale gap fourth, take up the semantic add-on last (Fig 3 :19-23).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The forced "or" is false as stated. Observer dependence in the coordinate sense (pose, coarse-graining, abstraction) is real and structural; objectivity in the sense of invariance across independent frames ranging against one unmoving ground is also real. They are not competitors at one level, so the question is a union compound and must be split. The figure-grounded shape is that any candidate notion of information content must state its pose, its coarse-graining and its ground, must be invariant across independent frames if it claims objectivity, must report the abstraction gap R_T as a measured cost rather than zero, must not promote the observer to an anchor, and must leave the semantic sub-question as `gap` and the structure binding as unproven. Mode `specified`, not `grounded`. Cost if wrong: bounded and locatable, because the union split, the mode and the refuters are written first; the real cost is promoting an observer to an anchor, which Fig 2 :34 says produces coherent drift that nobody notices. Undoable: yes, split the union and re-run each sub-question separately; no primitive has to be withdrawn. Reversed by: a ground reading, not agreement. A convergence failure across independent instruments, an anchor that moves when pushed, a re-census admitting a direct structure-to-structure bond, or an instrument that settles semantic content would each change the verdict. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
