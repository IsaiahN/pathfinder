# Structure, order and analogues for "information content"

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The figure set gives a shape but not a binding. Two supporting questions are owed. (1) Can "information content" be written as a molecule over the six-term substrate, and if so, in what order? (2) Are the observer-dependent and objective readings isomers, or a functional group, or something else? A third question follows from the formula: in what order must the bundle be refused and split?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven, plus the unary negation. Frame transform F is available but F is not an atom and not a bond (Symbols :79-82). No new primitive is available without import.

The question's two terms are not themselves atoms. "Observer" does not appear in the six; it enters as a frame with a pose and a library (Symbols :111-112). "Information content" is not a seventh; it is a candidate composite. So the derivation must not smuggle in an observer atom or mint a seventh term.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is information content a state, a structure, or neither alone?

- Fig 13 :29-30: **"A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what? A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."**
- Fig 13 :33: **"Neither kind generates anything alone. Neither denotes anything alone."**
- Therefore a quantity that says "how much" and "ordered how" cannot be a bare state or a bare structure. It must be a cross-kind composition (Fig 13 :74).
- Spectrum is defined as "ordering, so more and less can be said" (Fig 13 :20). Entropy is spectrum-like in form. Gradient is "a difference that can be spent" (Fig 13 :22). Erasure cost is gradient-like and couples to energy, which is "conserved, finite, directionless alone" (Fig 13 :14).
- **Result:** the natural figure reading is a *composite* of an ordering structure and a directedness structure, each bonded through a state. Neither alone is sufficient. This is a shape, not an answer: which state mediates which structure is not selected by the six.

### Q2 - What order does the composition require?

- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
- Two candidate first-order molecules are therefore legal:
  - `Energy - Gradient - Time` (directedness of erasure across an ordered succession)
  - `Energy - Spectrum - Time` (ordering of possibilities across an ordered succession)
- Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."** So the same structure bonded to different states yields different molecules. The pose and the coarse-graining select which molecule is being read. This is the figure-internal reason observer indexing is structural (F2 in `01`).
- A direct `Spectrum - Gradient` bond is forbidden. Any account that identifies "information content" with "entropy plus erasure cost" in one flat term is a flat-formula collapse (Fig 12 :6-8): it records the ingredients and not the bond.
- **Result:** first-order molecules only; a state must sit between any two structures. Writing the order is mandatory, and the order is not recoverable from the ingredients.

### Q3 - Are the two readings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As usually stated, the observer-dependent reading includes an observer and the objective reading does not. The observer is not one of the six, so the two do **not** share an atom set. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are different frames with different closures, and the difference is an import question (Fig 6 :23).
- The stronger version: force both readings onto the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}` and let them differ only in which structure mediates which state pair and in whether the reader is ranged against the ground or against its own library. Then they are an isomer claim in the figure's sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is the checkable structural claim worth sending.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either reading cannot be written over the shared six-term set (for example, the observer-dependent reading requires a primitive outside the six that is not an importable atom), the isomer claim fails and the different-closures reading stands instead.

### Q4 - What is the order of refusal and splitting?

- Formula step 2 sorts a gap by what is missing, then names the fifth kind: **REFUTED**, "a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation". The forced "or" is a held term that is false: the bundle is not one question with two answers (Fig 9 :55).
- Negation is available as a unary operator (Operators table :70-74: "Negation joins nothing, so its symmetry entry is empty rather than no"). So the refusal can be written rather than merely felt.
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Order: refuse the "or" first, split D1/D2/D3 second, search each third. Minting an answer to the bundle before the refusal is the reversed order named in Formula step 2.
- **Falsifier:** if a figure re-census shows the two readings are a genuine partition (mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive over one atom set), the refusal is wrong and the forced "or" stands. None of the sources read this session supports that; the claim is recorded as checkable, not as proven.

## CONCLUSION

"Information content" cannot be a bare state or a bare structure under Fig 13 :29-30. It is a first-order, cross-kind molecule, and any two structures in it must be separated by a state (Fig 13 :75). The observer is not an atom in the six and enters as frame F, so the two readings are not isomers as usually stated; over the shared six-term set they become a checkable isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The forced "or" must be refused before either side is searched, because the residual is a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58). One live strain remains: which structure mediates which state pair cannot be selected by the six, and is a binding, not a derivation.
