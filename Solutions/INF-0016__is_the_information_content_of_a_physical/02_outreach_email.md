# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the strain in section 4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is stated as open in the cover note. The claim must survive as a shape with an admitted binding gap, or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one claim about information and observation before you decide about the framework

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "is the information content of a physical system observer-dependent or objective?" is a forced choice over a bundle. Once you separate the physical ground from the frame's pose, two things are true at once and neither is a version of the other. First, the value a frame assigns is indexed by that frame's coarse-graining, assigned distribution, measurement context and pose. The frame transform in the framework "cannot be run without a pose", so this part is structural and harmless. Second, an "objective" value does not mean a view from nowhere. It means invariance across observers whose evidence is independent, each ranging against the same ground that does not move when a frame changes its mind. Two people using the same code and the same coarse-graining are one witness, not two.
>
> The part that genuinely depends on the observer is the step between scales: abstraction throws away detail, and the round trip does not return what was sent. The framework calls that loss R_T and says to measure it rather than call it zero. That is a coordinate cost, not a second ground.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular physical quantity *is* one of the framework's six terms; that the semantic sense of information has been settled; that all observers must agree; or that this is measured. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which structure sits in the slot stays open, and the interpretive part of the question is marked as a gap, not answered.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. For a fixed process and declared coarse-graining, two observers known to draw on different evidence return values that do not converge against the ground.
> 2. The ground for a physical system's information content turns out to include the observer's current belief state, so the anchor moves when pushed.
> 3. A re-census of the figure set permits two structures to bond directly, removing the need for a state between them.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the links are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Information*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information/
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Semantic Conceptions of Information*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information-semantic/
> - Faist et al., "The minimal work cost of information processing", Nature Communications 6:7669 (2015): https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms8669
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the local figure extract and formula in this folder are generators, so sharing them gives away a method rather than an answer. Write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1.** Send the claim, the pose/coarse-graining requirement, and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only.** If curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because agreement inside a shared evidence pool is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user states their own ground, writes their own refuter and labels their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. As written it is a shape with one declared binding strain and an open semantic gap. Keep both visible; do not tighten the claim to make it nicer.
