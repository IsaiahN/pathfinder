## What the wider literature does with this question

1. **The question does not have one literature.** SEP splits information into quantitative, semantic, agent-state and physical families and says of three influential definitions that "they have next to nothing in common". That is a domain-census warning from outside the framework: forcing a single verdict over the bundle would repeat the error Fig 9 :54 names.
2. **The frame dependence is already formalised.** Faist et al. tie the entropy function to "a macroscopic observer who is maximally ignorant of the microscopic degrees of freedom", and separately say the work bound is computed "from the viewpoint of the observer who has completed the computation". Their result that the state function is emergent rather than microscopic is a low-overlap corroboration of the figure claim that the objective quantity is convergence plus ground, not a view from nowhere.
3. **Invariance is the mathematical form of objectivity.** SEP records that for Kolmogorov complexity, "in the limit the complexity assigned to a dataset by two different universal Turing machines only differs by a constant." That is a mathematically precise version of Fig 8 :50: independent frames converge up to a declared tolerance.
4. **The semantic residue stays open.** Shannon's own boundary, "These semantic aspects of communication are irrelevant to the engineering problem", is not a denial of semantics but a scoping decision. SEP's semantic entry records a plurality of theories and no single concept. This corroborates the `gap` verdict for D3 without borrowing the framework's authority.
5. **Instrument limits decide, not argument.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the metamerism case: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". This is a high-overlap source (same author as the figures), so it can illustrate the shape but cannot independently corroborate it.
