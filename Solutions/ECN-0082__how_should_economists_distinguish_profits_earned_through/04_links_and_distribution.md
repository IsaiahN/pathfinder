## What the literature bears on the pitch

1. **The distinction is live and explicitly unresolved in the field.** Tax Justice Network states "Distinguishing these two is critical" and notes that headline tax "does not distinguish between 'productive profits' and rents from market power". Wikipedia records "several substantially different conflicting definitions" of economic rent. The separation shape is not a strawman.
2. **The categories overlap, they do not partition.** Teece and Coleman list three rents; UBS records Tullock's "good rents" versus "bad rent-seeking"; Boldrin and Levine argue competitive rents can sustain innovation without monopoly power. This supports the union finding (F5).
3. **The measurement instrument is contested.** The De Loecker et al. dispute is a real instance of F4: an instrument is sharpened from a worse one, and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives. It is not evidence that no separation is possible, only that the first instrument is not the last word.
4. **Identification supplies the source-discriminating shape.** Angrist and Krueger and the ecology commentary converge on the same requirement: not more of the same variation, but variation generated by an exogenous instrument. This is the world-side analogue of Fig 6 :32.
