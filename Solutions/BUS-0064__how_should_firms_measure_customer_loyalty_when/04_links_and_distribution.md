## What the wider literature adds, and what it does not

1. **The business pool is one witness.** Wikipedia's Brand loyalty page, the Net promoter score page and the Dick and Basu abstract all draw on the same marketing literature (Dick and Basu, Jones et al., Reichheld). Their agreement about attitudinal versus behavioral loyalty is one evidence pool, not three independent confirmations. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
2. **The low-overlap witness is attribution research.** Kelley's covariation model independently says that a disposition is attributed only when the cause covaries with the effect across consensus, distinctiveness and consistency, and that the fundamental attribution error is the tendency to read a disposition while ignoring the situation. That is the same shape as "do not read loyalty from a repeat purchase until you have varied the conditions", reached without the business literature.
3. **The NPS criticism is a practical instance of the figure point.** A single item score that "does not measure anything different from other conventional loyalty-related questions" is a pooled reading; using it to set retention targets is a convention deciding an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
4. **No world source settles the figure verdict.** The essays share the framework author, the business sources share a pool, and the attribution source corroborates a mechanism shape, not a firm-level measurement. This run is a simulated derivation throughout.
