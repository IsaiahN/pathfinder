# The Tether's answer: measuring loyalty from an ambiguous behavior

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm sees a repeat purchase but wants a commitment that would survive a rival. The same reading is produced by habit, switching cost or lock-in, and no viable alternative. The residual is the unperceived source, indexed per customer and per occasion (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). It is a union compound, not one hard question: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"* (Fig 9 :55). One lever clears single-condition regions and leaves the intersections.

## 2. The shape

1. **The reading is lossy.** A repeat purchase records the behavior, not the arrangement that produced it. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Four causal histories give one observable; they differ in what would make the customer leave. This is a `specified` structural analogy with a named strain (S1 in `01`), not a figure derivation of causal bonds.
2. **Index the residual per customer, never aggregate it.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A firm-level retention rate is a filter for the budget, not a witness for what the behavior means (Fig 9 :60).
3. **The anchor is the market, not the metric.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The question is constitutively about whether the customer stays when an alternative exists, so the market is the legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :36). A score computed from the same behavior it is meant to explain updates on the frames that use it, and the drift is coherent (Fig 2 :34).
4. **Unbundle the union by varying the conditions.** Enumerate the habitat (rivals, switching costs by type, habit cues, availability) and read it off the world (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*). Then sharpen the existing reading by intervention: rival present or absent, switching cost high or low, cue broken or intact, choice set narrow or wide. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* This is INWARD, not an import of a new frame.
5. **Triangulate low-overlap frames separately.** Behavioral logs, stated-preference surveys and a controlled rival experiment count as three frames only if they draw on different evidence. Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* A shared sample is one witness, not three.
6. **Keep verdicts per domain and per slot, never averaged.** Lock-in, habit and commitment get separate verdicts. The same behavior changes valence when the residual changes (Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*).
7. **No figure line names a scalar.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Naming NPS, retention or share of wallet as *the* recovered source is a binding from the business frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation. A number that explains all four sources by collapsing them into one reading fails the acceptance bargain (Fig 5 :43-44).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that surveys or scores are useless. They are legitimate filters for the budget and low-overlap readings when their evidence base differs.
- Not that satisfaction is irrelevant. It is one habitat condition among many, and its valence is residual-relative.
- Not that a universal loyalty percentage exists. No figure line governs one.
- Not that the four sources are chemical isomers in the strict Fig 12 sense. That is a `specified` analogy with a named strain.
- Not `grounded`. No customer, market or experiment was measured; this is a simulated derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The corresponding checks here:

- **Rival test.** Does the customer stay when a real alternative appears with comparable price and quality? Commitment predicts yes; lock-in predicts yes only while the cost binds.
- **Cost-removal test.** Does the customer leave when the switching cost is reduced? Lock-in predicts yes; commitment predicts no; habit predicts continued inertia only until the cue changes.
- **Cue-break test.** Does the behavior change when the habitual context changes (channel, time, shelf position, default)? Habit predicts yes.
- **Choice-set test.** Does the customer switch when the set widens? "No alternative" predicts yes.
- **Independence test.** Do behavioral, stated and experimental readings agree while drawing on different evidence? If they share a sample, their agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** a non-lossy reading, an aggregate that predicts individual switching as well as a per-slot measure, one lever that clears the union, or a scalar that recovers the source against the ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four-source list is a choice from the business frame, made visible by recording it (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*). The framework can characterise the missing discriminator in effect terms but cannot name it. If no instrument at any resolution registers a difference among the sources, the correct verdict changes to *unreachable in principle* and the intervention prescription is withdrawn. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer a better loyalty score. It says that a repeat purchase is a reading that cannot comment on its own source: habit, lock-in and the absence of alternatives all produce "bought again". So the residual must be kept per customer, the four conditions treated as an overlapping union rather than a list of causes, and the market used as the anchor that does not move when the metric is redefined. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, built by sharpening the existing reading, varying the rival, the switching cost, the habit cue and the choice set, and ranging behavioral, stated and experimental frames separately against the ground. Verdicts are kept per slot and never averaged into a company-wide number. The framework returns that shape, not the name of a metric. It writes its refuters before the run and stops honestly where the instruments stop.

## 7. Verdict fields

- **cost if wrong:** a firm is sold a retention program aimed at lock-in while believing it bought commitment, and an outside reader's twenty minutes are spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. Bounded and locatable because the protocol, mode and refuters are recorded.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its seven falsifiers are in a provenance trail.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the refuters in `01` section 3, or a source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-slot source discrimination adds no predictive power over a single aggregate score.
