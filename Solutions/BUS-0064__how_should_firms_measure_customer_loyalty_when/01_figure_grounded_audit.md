## 0. The claim under audit and the residual in effect terms

The question asks how to measure loyalty when the same behavior can be produced by habit, switching costs or a lack of alternatives. Stated in effect terms:

> A firm observes `o' = bought again` for customer slot `s` at time `t`. It wants a latent state `L` (commitment that would survive a rival). But `o'` is a projection: at least four sources can generate the same reading, commitment, habit, switching cost or lock-in, and no viable alternative. The residual is not measurement noise to be reduced by a larger sample. It is **source ambiguity**: the instrument reads the behavior and cannot comment on which source produced it.

Formally, with the Symbols table definitions: `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot. The Symbols table :38-40 says of R: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice"* and *"what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."* The unperceived thing here is the **source**, not the behavior.

Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale before choosing any figure line:

- **Arity.** A many-to-one projection. At least four latent sources map to one observable reading. The map is not invertible, so no reading alone returns the source. This is at least a four-way ambiguity, which is a union question, not a two-way partition.
- **Symmetry.** The collapse is symmetric in its sources: all four produce the same `bought again` reading. But the sources are not interchangeable with respect to the ground. They separate only under an ordered intervention (present a rival, lower a switching cost, break a cue, widen the set), which is a threshold/comparison operation, not a symmetric conjunction. So the shape carries an ordered and a comparison bond, not a symmetric one.
- **Scale.** Per customer slot and per purchase occasion, ordered in time. Aggregating to a firm-level retention percentage discards the very index that carries the source information (Fig 1 :19, :22). The membrane between customer scale and firm scale is lossy (Fig 4 :27-29).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a per-customer protocol that enumerates the sources, varies the conditions that would break each one, ranges the resulting readings separately against a market anchor that does not update, and refuses any single number that claims to have recovered the source. This is the shape we test, and the refuter below can kill it.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. A customer can be habitual *and* locked in *and* have no alternative. Figure 9 :55 says *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A lever that removes only one condition clears the region where only that condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, so a single loyalty lever cannot separate the sources (Fig 9 :56-57). The four sources must be unbundled before a search is run (Fig 9 :54).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** perception (the repeat purchase is observable) → vocabulary (terms for what an occasion affords: which alternatives existed, what switching would cost, what cue fired) → objective (what counts as progress: discriminating the source, not raising the score) → planning (design interventions that break each source) → learn and carry (name what worked and refute what did not). The chain in practice breaks at link 3: firms have abundant perception (transaction logs) and no instrument for the objective. Fig 3 :27 says *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Effort spent logging more purchases is effort above the break (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).

**Strain test rule applied.** Where a figure line fits only by forcing, it governs another quantity and we re-census. Two strains were found and are named in section 6 rather than smoothed over.

## 2. Findings

### F1: The repeat purchase is a recording of the behavior, not a reading of its source (decisive for the shape)

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :95:

> **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing."**

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 11 :48:

> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

The four sources are isomers of one observable: same reading, different arrangement of causes, different substance for the firm (a committed customer is an asset; a locked-in customer is a liability if the lock is removed). What is unrecorded is the bond between cause and behavior, and that is exactly the half that matters. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*), so the shape is "discriminate the arrangement", not "adopt metric X".

- **Mode:** `general` for the many-to-one projection, `specified` for the four sources in this market. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 arrangement rules and Fig 11 habitat enumeration, different figures with different roles, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** an instrument that maps a repeat-purchase reading one-to-one onto its source across customers, that is, a reading that is demonstrably not lossy. If such a reading exists, F1 falls.

### F2: Aggregate the residual and the live signal disappears

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Symbols table :38-40, on R: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice."*

A firm-level retention percentage or an averaged NPS collapses the per-customer signal. Worse, the metric is produced by the firm's own reporting process, so using it to score the firm's loyalty actions is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces. The signal that says "this slot is locked in, that slot is committed" is lost in the average.

- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 agent rules and the Symbols definition of R, two distinct documents).
- **Refuter:** a firm-level aggregate that predicts an individual customer's switching under a rival as well as or better than a per-slot measure. If it does, the indexing requirement is not load-bearing and F2 falls.

### F3: The anchor is the market, not the metric

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26 names the failure mode: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."*

The question is constitutively about whether a customer would stay when an alternative exists, so the market is the legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*). A loyalty score computed from the same behavior it is meant to explain is a mirror: it updates on the frames that use it, and the drift is coherent, so nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). Saturated metrics and pooled readings sever the channel while the ground does not decay.

- **Mode:** `specified` on the market as anchor. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 10 channel-decay rule, different figures).
- **Refuter:** a metric whose value is invariant to marketing effort expressly designed to raise it and to a change in its own definition. If a metric can be moved by redefining it, it is a frame, not an anchor, and F3 stands; if one is found that cannot, the anchor requirement is weaker than claimed.

### F4: The four sources form a union, so one lever leaves the intersections standing

Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57:

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

A customer who is both habitual and locked in is not a harder case of either; it is a compound with its own loop. The same repeat purchase is a benefit under one residual and a symptom under another, so valence is not carried by the behavior. This is why "raise retention" is not one intervention: it clears single-condition regions and leaves the intersections.

- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 union rule and Fig 11 valence rule).
- **Refuter:** a single intervention that removes commitment, habit, switching cost and absence of alternatives simultaneously and leaves one clean residue. If that is possible, the union reading is unnecessary and F4 falls.

### F5: What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, sharpened from a reading that already exists

Fig 6 :32:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 6 :34:

> **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."**

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The existing reading (repeat purchase) is already returning something, so this is an INWARD move: sharpen the instrument in our own terms by varying the conditions it is in contact with. Introduce or observe a rival (does the customer stay?), lower or remove a switching cost (does the customer leave?), break or change the habit cue (does the behavior persist?), widen the choice set (was the old behavior due to no alternative?). Each intervention is a sharper version of the same reading, not an imported new frame. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* If nothing registers even under intervention, that is unreachability, not silence, and it must be reported as such.

- **Mode:** `general` for the instrument rule, `specified` for the four interventions. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 6 reachability instrument and Fig 3 dependency link).
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that no instrument, at any resolution anywhere, registers a difference among the four sources. Then the sources are indiscernible in principle, not merely unresolved at this budget, and the prescription changes to "do not try".

### F6: Enumerate the habitat; do not compose a standing list of loyalty drivers

Fig 11 :43:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :48:

> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Fig 11 :52-54: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

List what is in contact with the purchase: rivals, their entry and exit, switching costs by type, habit cues, shelf position, availability, convenience. Then list what contacts those (a competitor's price change, a platform default, a regulatory change). First-order enumeration misses second-order effects, so the habitat must be followed outward until the cascade stops mattering. This reading is `single-source` on the figure side; the world corroboration (Kelley's covariation model) is secondary and is declared as such in `09`.

- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status: single-source** (Fig 11 only on the figure side).
- **Refuter:** a standing list of loyalty drivers whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual. If such a list exists, the enumeration requirement is vacuous and F6 falls.

### F7: Triangulate low-overlap frames; never pool a shared evidence pool

Fig 2 :31:

> **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*

A behavioral log, a stated-preference survey and a controlled rival-introduction experiment are three frames only if they draw on different evidence. If the survey and the log come from the same customers at the same moment, their agreement is one pool talking, not two witnesses (Fig 2 :18-21). Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 independence rule and Fig 8 surplus rule).
- **Refuter:** a frame whose verdict on a customer is fully determined by another frame's verdict plus a shared data source. If every candidate frame is like that, no triangulation is available and F7 becomes an unfunded recommendation, not a checkable one.

### F8: A segment score is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

"High-value segment" and "frequent buyer" are budget filters: they decide where to look. They cannot decide whether the behavior is loyal. The persistence of one rule on one subset and a different rule on another subset is the signal to split, not to average.

- **Mode:** `specified`. **Status: single-source** (Fig 9; the metalaws doc restates the same line and is not a second witness).
- **Refuter:** a segmentation filter used as evidence that a behavior is genuine loyalty, with no independent ground reading. If that move is legitimate under the figures, F8 falls.

### F9: No single number survives; that is a declared `gap`, not a silence

Fig 5 :43-44:

> **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."**

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No figure line selects NPS, retention rate, share of wallet or any other scalar as *the* loyalty measure. A single number that "explains" commitment, habit, lock-in and no-alternative by collapsing them into one reading is a term that explains everything by saying nothing, which the acceptance test fails. The legitimate output is the shape plus a falsifier plus the instruments that would close it. Naming a specific off-the-shelf metric is a binding from the business frame, provenanced as such, not a figure-derived name.

- **Mode:** `general` for the gap verdict. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 5 acceptance test, Fig 9 limit, Fig 13 verdict, Fig 8 search limit, low overlap across the four).
- **Refuter:** a figure line that governs a specific scalar loyalty metric, or a measurement that shows one scalar recovers the source with no loss. Until one is produced, the gap stands.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per `THE_FORMULA.md` and the field guide: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written 2026-11-22, before the verdict was drafted. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Non-lossy reading.** A repeat-purchase reading maps one-to-one onto its source across customers (kills F1).
2. **Aggregate suffices.** A firm-level aggregate predicts individual switching under a rival at least as well as a per-slot measure (kills F2).
3. **Self-moving anchor.** A metric's value is invariant to the effort used to raise it and to its own definition change, so it can serve as the anchor (weakens F3).
4. **Single lever clears the union.** One intervention removes all four sources at once and leaves one clean residue (kills F4).
5. **Genuine indiscernibility.** No instrument at any resolution anywhere registers a difference among the sources. Then the correct verdict is *unreachable*, and the intervention prescription is withdrawn (kills F5).
6. **Invariant valence.** A standing list of loyalty drivers holds its sign across a documented change of residual (kills F6).
7. **One number recovers the source.** A scalar metric is shown, against the ground, to recover which source produced each repeat purchase (kills F9).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed in a visible retraction. Until one is run, no part of this is labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Stop treating repeat purchase rate, or any single aggregate, as a loyalty measure. Report the residual per customer and per occasion (F1, F2).
2. Enumerate the habitat before measuring: rivals, switching costs by type, habit cues, availability, and their second-order contacts. Do not compose a standing driver list (F6).
3. Unbundle the union. Ask which of the four conditions is present for this slot, and do not average the answers (F4, F8).
4. Build source-discriminating readings by varying conditions: rival present or absent, switching cost high or low, cue broken or intact, choice set narrow or wide. Each is a sharper version of the existing reading, an INWARD move (F5).
5. Range at least three low-overlap frames separately against the market anchor: behavioral, stated-preference, experimental. Record the verdict from stage 1 before showing any theory (F3, F7).
6. State the mode on every claim, keep the refuter in front of the links, and refuse the request for one number (F9). Figure 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the protocol and its refuter, not a score.

## 6. Strain test and re-census

- **S1, live and named.** The isomer analogy in F1. The analogy is kept at `specified` strength, and the world source on source-discrimination carries the mechanism, not the figure. - **S2, dissolved by re-census.** Fig 1 :19 says index per slot, while the business question asks for a firm-level policy. Re-census: the question needs both scales, and Fig 4 :20 says *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The firm-level policy is the membrane carrying a method down, not an aggregate that replaces the per-slot reading. - **S3, dissolved by re-census.** Fig 2 :40 says correlated agreement is worthless for a verdict, while business practice treats a convergence of survey and log as confirmation. Re-census: agreement about a shared meaning is a convention and is efficient (Fig 2 :41), but agreement about the world from one pool is one witness. Both are true of different quantities.
