# Solution BUS-0064: measuring loyalty when the behavior is ambiguous

**Question:** How should firms measure customer loyalty when repeat purchases may reflect habit, switching costs, or a lack of alternatives?

**Status:** done

## The question restated as an effect

A firm wants a latent state, genuine commitment that would survive a rival. Its instrument returns one observable, a repeat purchase. The same observable is produced by at least four different sources: commitment, habit, switching cost or lock-in, and the absence of a viable alternative. The firm must decide what to count as loyalty and what each choice of metric leads to.

- Count repeat purchase as loyalty: the firm rewards lock-in and habit as if they were commitment, and its retention spend targets the wrong customers.
- Add a survey score and average it into one number: the firm pools readings, keeps the ambiguity, and gains a quantity it also produces, which cannot score itself.
- Refuse the single reading and discriminate the source by changing the conditions: the firm gets a per-customer verdict, a stated refuter, and an anchor that does not move when the metric is changed.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a **source-discriminating, per-slot measurement protocol with a non-updating market anchor and low-overlap frames**, not a single number. This is the sought-for shape made refusable, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction".

## Verdict in one line

The repeat purchase is a lossy projection, not a source reading. The four sources form a union of overlapping conditions, so one lever leaves the intersections standing. What is owed is an instrument that varies the conditions (introduce an alternative, lower the switching cost, break the habit cue, widen the choice set), kept per customer, ranged against a market anchor, with verdicts per domain never averaged. No figure line licenses a single replacement number. That is a declared `gap`, a verdict and not a silence.
