# Expert verification packet: the Tether on loyalty measurement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a loyalty researcher or marketing-metrics specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No customer data, survey result or experiment was used. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A repeat purchase is a lossy projection: commitment, habit, switching cost or lock-in, and the absence of a viable alternative all produce the same reading. The measurement residual must therefore be indexed per customer and per occasion, the four conditions treated as a union of overlapping conditions, and the market used as an anchor that does not move when the metric is redefined. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, built by sharpening the existing reading and varying the rival, the switching cost, the habit cue and the choice set, with behavioral, stated and experimental frames ranged separately against the ground. Verdicts are kept per slot and never averaged into a company-wide loyalty score. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not the name of a metric.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Reading versus source (framework-internal and measurement-facing)

- **Statement:** a single repeat-purchase reading cannot recover which of the four sources produced it; the arrangement is the information that is lost.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 11 :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
- **What to check:** is the four-source list exhaustive enough for the market in question, and is any one of the four actually recoverable from behavior alone? The business literature distinguishes attitudinal from behavioral loyalty and names "spurious loyalty" for repeat purchase driven by situational constraint, which is consistent with the shape but is one evidence pool.
- **Refuter:** a reading that maps one-to-one onto the source across customers, so the behavior is not lossy. If it exists, C1 fails.

### C2: Union and non-updating anchor (framework-internal derivation)

- **Statement:** the four conditions overlap, so one lever leaves the intersections standing; and the anchor must be the market, not the metric, because the question is constitutively about whether the customer stays when an alternative exists.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **What to check:** can the four conditions be independently manipulated in this market, and does the market choice actually settle the question? If switching costs cannot be varied, the discriminating instrument is unavailable and C2 becomes a recommendation without an implementation.
- **Refuter:** one intervention that removes all four sources at once and leaves a clean residue, or a metric whose value is invariant to the effort used to raise it and to its own redefinition. Either would break C2.

### C3: The instrument and the independence of frames (method-facing)

- **Statement:** the discriminator is built from the existing reading by varying conditions, and three frames count as three only if they draw on different evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 2 :31 *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
- **What to check:** do the behavioral log, the stated-preference survey and the controlled rival experiment use disjoint evidence? If the survey is administered to the same customers in the same period as the log, their agreement is one witness. Kelley's covariation model (attribution research, low overlap) independently requires consensus, distinctiveness and consistency information before attributing a disposition rather than a situation.
- **Refuter:** no instrument at any resolution registers a difference among the sources (then the verdict becomes unreachable in principle), or every candidate frame is fully determined by another frame plus a shared data source (then no triangulation is available).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that surveys, NPS or retention rates are useless. They are filters for the budget and low-overlap readings, not witnesses for the source.
- Not that satisfaction is the sole cause of loyalty, and not that any universal percentage of loyal customers exists.
- Not that the four sources are strict chemical isomers. That is a `specified` analogy with a recorded strain (S1 in `01`).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was made and no customer was observed.
- Not that the framework names a specific commercial metric. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* and Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. Four practical tests follow from the shape:

1. **Rival test:** does the customer stay when a real alternative appears?
2. **Cost-removal test:** does the customer leave when the switching cost is reduced?
3. **Cue-break test:** does the behavior change when the habitual context changes?
4. **Choice-set test:** does the customer switch when the set widens?

Plus the independence test: do the three frames agree while drawing on genuinely different evidence? And the standing refuters in `01` section 3, written before the run: a non-lossy reading, an aggregate that matches per-slot prediction, one lever clearing the union, or a scalar that recovers the source against the ground.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5 and :94, Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 2 :29 and :31, and Fig 6 :32. Seven short passages.
2. Check C1 against the loyalty literature you know: is repeat purchase a projection with lossy information in your field, or is one of the four sources recoverable from behavior alone?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish commitment from lock-in in your market, or say why none is possible. That is the whole of C3.
4. Check whether the four conditions can be independently varied where you work. If they cannot, say so; that is a refutation of the implementation, not of the shape.
5. Send the failure. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
