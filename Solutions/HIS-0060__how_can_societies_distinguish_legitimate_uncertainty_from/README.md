# Tether outreach: figure-grounded package

**ID:** HIS-0060
**Question:** How can societies distinguish legitimate uncertainty from strategically manufactured doubt?
**Source:** `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 73
**Section:** 6. Disagreement, controversy, and consensus
**Field for public contact points:** uncertainty and doubt research

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on each discriminator. Nothing here is
`grounded`: no case was adjudicated by a ground reading, and no single figure line closes the
question. Every substantive claim is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency
against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done
**Registry status:** pending (set by the parent session)

## What is being decided, in effect terms

A public claim of "uncertainty" is presented to a society. Treating manufactured doubt as
legitimate buys delay and puts the ground on trial as just another party. Treating legitimate
uncertainty as manufactured closes a live slot early and hides a real gap inside an average.
Both have costs, so the discriminator must be per-slot and ground-referenced, never a vote
about who seems more reasonable.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make its agreement worthless (Fig 2 :40).
2. **The ground is not a party to the disagreement.** Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* A "both sides" format is exactly this failure.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: public opt-in and institutional channels avoid GDPR and CAN-SPAM style problems that
follow from unsolicited bulk email. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
