## 0. The residual, in effect terms

A society must classify a public assertion of uncertainty.

- **Reading A (legitimate):** the assertion marks a live residual on a named slot, positive mass
  somewhere, shrinking with inquiry, ranged against a ground that does not move when argued with.
- **Reading B (manufactured):** the assertion is an engineered appearance of a residual: no named
  slot, no positive mass, no ground contact, produced regardless of what measurements return,
  and sustained by a loop whose output (doubt) re-enters as its input (funding, attention).

What each leads to: A-read-B buys delay and puts the ground on trial as one party among many.
A-read-A closes a live slot early and hides a real gap inside an average. Both are costly, so the
discriminator has to be per-slot and ground-referenced, never a headcount or a vote on tone.

**Direction bound as an ordinary term before the run.** The sought-for shape is written down and
bound so it can accrue a residual like anything else: *an operational test that, for any public
uncertainty claim, returns one of three values: (i) live residual on a named slot, (ii) preference
masquerading as a residual, or (iii) undecidable at this budget.* This is the discipline named in
`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question unless the
sought-for shape is made an ordinary term. Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM.
Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything
else."*

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity / symmetry / scale.**

- **Arity:** at least four terms. A knowledge frame K, a doubting frame D, a ground G, and a
  society S that must decide. The compound asks four sub-questions: is there a live residual in K;
  is D ranging against G independently; is D's claim a bound predicting term or an unbound
  preference; does D's position change as G is read over time.
- **Symmetry:** manufactured doubt presents as symmetric, two sides with equal weight. The figures
  deny that symmetry: the anchor is *"not a third party to the disagreement"* (Fig 2 :46). So the
  two readings are not the same kind of thing presented twice. The apparent symmetry is the
  phenomenon to be explained.
- **Scale:** slot (one claim), closure (one frame's library), room chain (a society handing the
  claim along). A verdict at one scale is not a verdict at another.

**One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor).**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | a domain? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 knowledge production | researchers, instruments, peer review, funders | residuals settled against the ground | measurement, experiment (Fig 2 :29) | yes, nameable anchor |
| D2 doubt production | funders, think tanks, contrarian experts, media intermediaries | doubt as output, attention, funding | none that settles truth; its sponsor interest moves when pushed (Fig 2 :30) | yes for the habitat census, not an anchor for truth (Fig 2 :6-7 common cause) |
| D3 public decision | public, journalists, policymakers | agreement, attention, votes | should be the ground; often becomes agreement (Fig 2 :34) | yes |
| D4 verification (this packet) | expert leads in the field | residuals and refutations | the ground, never agreement | yes; in this simulated run shares one evidence pool, so independence = 0 |

**Partition or union?** A **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split
lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :4-5: *"First:
is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working
well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it
rather than search."* Each overlap (for example a genuine slot residual that is then exploited by
a funder) is its own compound with its own loop.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** perception (is there positive mass on a named slot?) → vocabulary
(can the doubt be stated as a bound, predicting term?) → objective (can the claimant say in advance
what would settle it?) → plan and learn (does the position track the ground over effort?). Fig 3
:19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link
with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* A frame that
stops at the vocabulary/objective link cannot be diagnosed from below.

## 2. Findings, with verbatim lines quoted before concluding

### F1 - The SUPPORT guard is the first discriminator (decisive at the slot level)

**Verbatim.** Fig 5 :20: *"is there anything to explain?"* Fig 5 :21: *"the gap has real mass
somewhere"*. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 3 (not a figure line): SUPPORT `|R⁺_s| > 0 for some s` before
the search. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22:
*"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 5 :48: *"Its own
output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce
nothing."*

**Derivation.** Legitimate uncertainty is a live per-slot residual with positive mass: something
is failing to resolve, and we can say where. Manufactured doubt typically presents as a global
aggregate, "the science is unsettled", that hides a settled ground in an average (Fig 1 :22). A
doubt-producing frame can output doubt continuously while SUPPORT is zero, because its output is a
product, not a measurement (Fig 5 :48). Ask four questions: which slot, what failed to resolve
there, what is the positive mass, and what observation would shrink it. If the claim cannot name a
slot, SUPPORT = 0 and the mint is inert: Fig 5 :41 *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint
entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."*

**Mode:** `general` (structural), `specified` when applied to a named case.
**Status:** triangulated (Fig 5 support guard + Fig 1 slot indexing + formula SUPPORT; low overlap).
**Refuter:** a manufactured-doubt case with a named slot, positive residual and a ground-predicting
term; or a legitimate-uncertainty case that is a pure aggregate with no slot.

### F2 - Treat the doubt claim as a term, not a preference: demand the falsifier in advance

**Verbatim.** Fig 5 :61: *"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."*
Fig 5 :62-64: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a
PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no
residual, so nothing here can see it."* Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM.
Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything
else."* Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

**Derivation.** "There is still uncertainty" as deployed by a doubt frame is usually a preference
about the shape of the answer, not a term: unbound, predicting nothing, accruing no residual, and
therefore invisible to every check in the pipeline (Fig 5 :63). The remedy is a discipline, not a
new branch: require the claimant to write down what observation would make them concede, bind it,
and let it accrue a residual like anything else. This is the pre-registered refuter applied to the
claimant rather than only to ourselves. A refusal to state a falsifier is itself data: it marks the
claim as a preference, which cannot be composed over or checked.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 5 amendment + Fig 10 :52 + formula "Direction").
**Refuter:** a doubt claim that does state a falsifier, predicts, and is checked, yet still
functions as manufactured doubt. Then preference-versus-term is not the discriminator.

### F3 - The ground is not a party: false balance is an anchor failure

**Verbatim.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its
answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not
an anchor."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into
panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent,
which is why nobody notices."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror
cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a
frame."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is
where the ground votes."*

**Derivation.** Manufactured doubt works by promoting the settled record into one side of a
two-sided debate. The balanced format is Fig 2 panel 1 (mutual update) or panel 2 (common cause),
not panel 3 (an anchor both frames range against separately). The anchor is not a third party and
not a tie-breaker (Fig 2 :46). Legitimate uncertainty, by contrast, is resolved by ranging both
frames against a ground separately. Operationally: ask what would count as the ground here, check
whether the format lets the ground be cross-examined, and check whether a "balance" verdict would
change if a measurement changed. If the latter is yes, the balance is preference, not evidence.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 multiple low-overlap lines + Fig 7 :35).
**Refuter:** a case where symmetric presentation against a non-updating ground still manufactures
doubt. Then symmetry is not the mechanism and this finding is demoted.

### F4 - Count frames by independence, not by heads

**Verbatim.** Fig 2 :19-21: *"they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool
talking, / not two frames confirming each other"*. Fig 2 :40:
*"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 2 :44:
*"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey
statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."* Fig 7 :13:
*"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The
next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

**Derivation.** A dozen contrarian voices drawing on one funding pool are one witness with twelve
names, not twelve corroborations. The arithmetic is the standard effective-sample-size collapse
Fig 2 :44 names. Operationally: trace where each "independent" expert's evidence and funding
come from, and ask whether two of them would still agree if one pool were removed. Note the
caution from Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately,
which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Shared content alone is not evidence of a shared
pool; the record of provenance separates convergence from adoption (Fig 8 :51-52).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 7 + Fig 8).
**Refuter:** a manufactured-doubt campaign whose experts are genuinely low-overlap and convergent,
yet the doubt is manufactured. Then common cause is not necessary for this class.

### F5 - Does the asserted uncertainty trend down with effort? Split rather than search

**Verbatim.** Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with
effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but
several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 (not a figure
line): *"the residual that PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"*. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can
be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*

**Derivation.** Legitimate uncertainty is a residual that shrinks as inquiry proceeds; the
selector's shrinking test reads it directly. Manufactured doubt is a disagreement that does not
shrink with effort, because the asserted residual is not registered against a ground. The
operational test is temporal: plot the claim's residual against effort and measurement. If it does
not trend down, either the question is a union compound that must be split (Fig 9 :54), or the
"uncertainty" is a preference held for its own sake. Important honesty clause: a residual that
does not trend down can also be genuinely unresolvable at this budget or in principle. Fig 9 :40-43
distinguishes underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-budget, and Fig 8 :39 says *"So a search
that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The test flags, it does not convict.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 + formula shrinking test).
**Refuter:** a manufactured-doubt case whose asserted uncertainty does trend down with study, or a
legitimate uncertainty that is genuinely radical and non-shrinking (Knightian, unresolvable by
acquiring knowledge). The latter is a real limit, not an embarrassment; it is recorded in §6.

### F6 - Stake and provenance: declare interests, but they are not a refutation

**Verbatim.** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is
disqualifying."* Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest
is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."* Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a
reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
Fig 6 :12: *"source gate: provenance"*; Fig 6 :13: *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. Fig 10 :65-66:
*"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does
that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

**Derivation.** Follow the funding and declare the stake. A doubt frame with an undeclared sponsor
lets an unnamed model run unchallenged. But the funding trail is a stake channel, not a ground
reading: a funded contrarian can still be right, and a clean provenance cannot make a wrong claim
true. The correct use is to set the burden of independence and to require the shadow test (does
the claim explain a gap that was actually observed?), not to declare the claim refuted by its
sponsor.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 + Fig 6).
**Refuter:** a case where declaring the stake changes neither the residual nor the independence
assessment, so provenance is idle. Then F6 is a transparency rule only, not a discriminator.
**RETRACTION recorded in §6:** an earlier draft of this file said "funding proves the doubt is
manufactured." That is withdrawn; Fig 10 :65-66 forbids it.

### F7 - Valence is relative to the residual: enumerate the habitat

**Verbatim.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List
everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the
cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a
property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor
to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No
standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

**Derivation.** You cannot classify "skeptics" as manufactured-doubt agents by kind. The same
person who is a legitimate questioner on one residual can be a doubt merchant on another. You must
enumerate the habitat (funders, journals, platforms, media routines, regulatory arenas) by contact
with the residual, cascade outward, and then read the ground. This also defeats a lazy version of
the question: there is no permanent list of good and bad actors to publish, only a method for
reading relations to a specific residual.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 10 seat rules).
**Refuter:** a stable actor type that is a malfactor to every residual with no exception. That
would break the valence-relative claim.

### F8 - Manufactured doubt as a cycle, and the vocabulary needed to refuse it

**Verbatim.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides
whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that
survives its own use."* Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*
Operators table :58 (not a figure line): *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary
without it can only accumulate."*

**Derivation.** Manufactured doubt is a positive-feedback cycle: produced doubt attracts funding
and attention, which produces more doubt, and the loop never closes against a ground. Legitimate
inquiry is also a cycle, but one whose sign closes against the ground (the residual is returned to
the measurement). The catalyst is the credential or outlet that survives its own use across many
issues, spending and regenerating across uses. Recognising the cycle requires a vocabulary with
negation: without it a frame can only accumulate doubt claims and cannot carry the correction
that a claim has been refused.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source for the cycle reading (Fig 12), triangulated with
Fig 7 :13 for the compounding claim.
**Refuter:** a manufactured-doubt case that is a one-shot act with no feedback loop. Then the cycle
reading is too narrow and must be restated as a sufficient, not necessary, condition.
**Strain recorded:** "sign of the loop" is not itself an instrumented figure quantity here. Marked
as strained-for-now, not smoothed over.

### F9 - The honest stop: a conjunction and a gap, not a single-line test

**Verbatim.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 9 :60: *"Use
filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 13
:134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing
is never a proof of absence."*

**Derivation.** No single figure line separates legitimate uncertainty from manufactured doubt in
general. What the figures license is a conjunction of checks (F1 to F8), and applying that
conjunction to a specific case is a ground question requiring that case's history, funding
provenance and residual trajectory. The output of this derivation is therefore a shape: a set of
tests a society can run, not a name attached to any particular doubt. Calling a specific campaign
manufactured is a further move that needs a ground reading, and this simulated derivation does not
supply one.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** gap for a single-line verdict; the conjunction is triangulated
across Fig 1, Fig 2, Fig 5 and Fig 9.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A case can be shown that satisfies all of F1 to F7 (named live slot with positive mass, bound
   predicting term with an advance falsifier, independent frames, a residual that tracks the
   ground, a declared stake, an enumerated habitat) and is nevertheless strategically
   manufactured; or a case that fails them and is nevertheless legitimate. That breaks the shape.
2. The ground for "is this manufactured?" turns out itself to be a frame that updates on opinion,
   so no anchor exists for the question (Fig 2 :33-34, Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an
   outcome has stopped being one"*). Then the question is a convention, not a verdict.
3. Independence cannot be established for a whole class because every frame draws on one evidence
   pool (Fig 2 :40), so no triangulation is possible even in principle for that class.
4. A single figure line is found that cleanly separates the two in all cases without the
   conjunction. Then F9's gap claim is false and the checklist should be replaced by that line.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be marked
`grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Publish the discriminator as an operational checklist with its three-valued output and its
   falsifier, not as a verdict about named campaigns (F9).
2. Lead with the question "which slot, what failed to resolve, what would settle it?" (F1, F2).
   That converts a preference into a checkable term before any theory is shown.
3. Keep agreement out of the evidence column. Count frames by independence and buy it in
   proportion to the cost of being wrong (F4, Fig 2 :42).
4. Refuse the balanced format that puts the ground on trial (F3).
5. Track the residual over effort and split compounds rather than search them as one hard problem
   (F5).
6. Declare funding stakes, but do not let the funding trail do the work of a refutation (F6).
7. Enumerate the habitat; never publish a permanent list of good and bad actors (F7).
8. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, that is
   the checklist and its refuter, not a dossier of conclusions about who is lying.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary, kept distinct per formula step 7)

1. **Is there a fact here, a live slot?** Ask SUPPORT first (Fig 5 :20-21). If no slot, there is no
   uncertainty claim to adjudicate, only an aggregate.
2. **Term or preference?** Demand the advance falsifier (Fig 5 :61-65). A refused falsifier marks
   a preference.
3. **What would count as progress?** Bind the objective; without it the chain stops at the middle
   link (Fig 3 :27).
4. **Does the residual trend down under effort?** Fig 9 :4-5; split if it does not.
5. **How independent are the frames?** Fig 2 :40, :42. Convergence is not the same as adoption
   (Fig 8 :50-52).
6. **What is the stake and provenance?** Fig 10 :59-60. Declare, do not refute.
7. **What remains unreachable?** SEARCHED (is the discriminating instrument already in our closure
   and unfound?); INWARD (sharpen our own residual tracking); OUTWARD (import a frame whose closure
   predicts manufactured doubt: agnotology, false-balance research, risk analysis). These three are
   kept distinct and never read against one another (Fig 6 :28-31).
