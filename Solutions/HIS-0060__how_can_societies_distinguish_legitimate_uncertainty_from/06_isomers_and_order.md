# Isomers and order: two kinds of "uncertainty", and the order of the checks

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer claim, `general` on the order. Simulated derivation checked
against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §8).**

## RESIDUAL

The public word "uncertainty" names two things that look the same in their ingredient list: an
honest live residual and an engineered appearance of one. Two questions are owed: (1) what is the
dependency order in which a society should run the checks, and (2) are the two kinds *isomers*
under Fig 12's definition, or merely two members of one functional group?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the public vocabulary: a claim C, a set of experts E, a body of evidence B, a stake
S, and a ground G. Bonds held: sequence `→`, comparison `⋛`, cycle (named but not one of the seven
bonds), and negation `¬`. No new primitive may be assumed. Partition or union: the question is a
**union compound** (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition,
unbundle before you search again."*), because a case can be both a genuine residual and a funded
campaign. That overlap is a compound with its own loop.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What order do the checks run in?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link
that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not
a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The dependency order read off Fig 3:

1. **Perception.** Is there a live slot with positive mass? Fig 5 :21: *"the gap has real mass
   somewhere"*. If no, there is no uncertainty claim to adjudicate.
2. **Vocabulary.** Can the claim be stated as a bound, predicting term with an advance falsifier?
   Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a
   PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no
   residual, so nothing here can see it."*
3. **Objective.** Can the claimant say what would count as progress, in their own terms? Fig 3 :9-10
   names this the objective link. Without it the chain stops at the middle, the least-instrumented
   link (Fig 3 :24-27).
4. **Plan and learn.** Does the position track the ground over effort? Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is
   there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working
   well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it
   rather than search."*
5. **Independence and stake.** Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being
   wrong."* Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is
   modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which
is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."* So a
society that starts at step 5 (who funded them?) before step 1 (what failed to resolve?) has
started below the break. It has produced a reading of nothing.

### Q2 - Are the two kinds isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an
identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation
cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* Fig 12 :111: *"A
vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Write the two cores over the same ingredient list `{C, E, B, S, G}`:

- **Legitimate:** `C → G` with `E` ranging separately against `G` and `B` shrinking the residual.
  The bond from the claim to the ground is sequence/contact: the claim is corrected by the ground.
- **Manufactured:** `C → S → E → C`, a cycle whose output re-enters as input. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle.
  The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot
  close by construction."* The bond to `G` is absent, so the loop never closes against a ground.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, and
their sameness in the public notation (both are sentences saying "we are uncertain") is exactly the
thing the formula cannot record. The ingredient list is not a classifier.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either kind can be written without an atom the other
requires (for example manufactured doubt with no stake S, or legitimate uncertainty with no ground
G), the isomer claim fails and the two are not isomers but different ingredient sets. Likewise, if
a case can be written both ways over the same atoms with the same bond, the bond is not the
discriminator and the isomer reading must be replaced by the functional-group reading below.

### Q3 - Are they only a functional group?

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

As the public discourse writes them, both cores reduce to the same bare substructure: an expert
set making a claim over a body of evidence, `E → C(B)`. That shared substructure is a functional
group, not an isomer: the group says "uncertainty claim", the qualifier (ground-bonded or
stake-bonded) says which member. So there are two defensible levels:

| level | claim | status |
|---|---|---|
| weak | both are members of one functional group of uncertainty claims | `general`, triangulated (Fig 12 :124 + the public notation) |
| strong | over the full shared atom set they are isomers whose bond decides the substance | `specified`, falsifier stated above |

The strong reading is the one worth testing, because it is the one that predicts where the
difference will show up: in the bond, not in the wording.

### Q4 - What does refusal add, and what does negation buy?

Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Operators table :58 (not a
figure line): *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only
accumulate."* A society whose only move is to add more "uncertainty" statements to the record has
a vocabulary without negation: it can be corrected in principle and cannot carry the correction.
The three-valued output in `01` adds the missing move: value 2 is a refusal of a term's standing,
not the minting of another term. Fig 6 :42-43 (noted in the audit): what is recorded only grows;
what is reachable can fall because something was added.

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* The credential that appears across
many unrelated controversies is a catalyst in this sense: spent and regenerating across uses, and
nothing in the public record says which a term is. That is a second reason to keep the output
provenance per claim, not per speaker.

### Q5 - What order is the union, and where is the honest stop?

Fig 9 :4-5 splits the compound before searching it. Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the
ground decides"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134:
*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

So the output of this derivation is a kind of thing (a checkable bond pattern), not a verdict on a
campaign. The conjunction in `01` narrows; a ground reading decides. Where no ground instrument
exists, the third value is recorded. That is a verdict, not a silence.

## CONCLUSION

The dependency order is perception → vocabulary → objective → trend → independence and stake.
Running it in reverse produces a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). Over the full shared atom set
the two kinds of uncertainty are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond,
different substance, invisible in the public formula and decisive in behaviour. In the weaker
public notation they are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :124). The falsifier is stated in
advance and is not yet fired.

This framing rescues what is structural in the question without naming any real campaign: the
difference is in the bond, so a test must inspect the bond (falsifier, independence, ground
contact, trend), not the wording of the claim. One strain remains visible: Fig 12 :121 says the
*sign* of the loop decides whether it closes, but no instrument in this derivation measures that
sign in a social campaign. It is a prediction, not a measurement.
