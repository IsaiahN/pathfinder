# Expert verification packet: the Tether on legitimate uncertainty and manufactured doubt

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in uncertainty and doubt studies can check. Mode,
derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and
what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the checks, not `grounded`.** No named campaign is
adjudicated. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A society can distinguish legitimate uncertainty from strategically manufactured doubt by a
three-valued, per-slot, ground-referenced test. Name the slot and its positive mass; require the
claimant to state in advance what would settle the question, so that doubt becomes a bound term
rather than an unbound preference; keep the ground out of a two-sided "balance" format; count
independent frames rather than voices drawn from one pool; track whether the asserted residual
shrinks under effort and split compounds that do not shrink; declare funding stakes without
treating them as refutations; and enumerate the habitat instead of keeping a permanent list of
good and bad actors. The output is `legitimate`, `manufactured`, or `undecidable at this budget`.
The figures license the shape of this test and its falsifiers, not a verdict about any named case.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The first discriminator is a support gate on a named slot (framework-internal and field-facing)

- **Statement:** legitimate uncertainty is a live residual on a named slot with positive mass;
  manufactured doubt typically presents as a global aggregate in which a settled ground is hidden.
  A doubt-producing output is not itself evidence that a residual exists.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :21 *"the gap has real mass somewhere"*; Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is
  indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live
  signal disappears into an average."*; Fig 5 :48 *"Its own output is never a measure of success.
  A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*
- **Field check:** does the claim identify a specific unresolved quantity, or only a general state
  of "the science is unsettled"? Does the volume of doubt statements track any measurement?
- **Refuter:** a manufactured-doubt case with a named slot, positive residual and a ground-predicting
  term; or a legitimate-uncertainty case that is a pure aggregate with no slot.

### C2 - An unbound preference cannot be checked; the falsifier must be stated in advance (framework-internal)

- **Statement:** "there is still uncertainty" used as a preferred shape is not a term. It predicts
  nothing and accrues no residual, so no check in the pipeline can see it. The remedy is a
  discipline: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :62-63 *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds
  that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it
  accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."*; Fig 5 :65 *"make the sought-for shape an
  ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual
  like anything else."*
- **Field check:** can the claimant name an observation that would make them concede? If they
  cannot, is the claim functioning as a preference rather than a finding?
- **Refuter:** a doubt claim that does state a falsifier, predicts and is checked, yet still
  functions as manufactured doubt. Then preference-versus-term is not the discriminator.

### C3 - Agreement is evidence only at high independence, and the ground is not a party (both fields)

- **Statement:** many "skeptical" voices sharing one funding or evidence pool are one witness under
  many names. A balanced format that puts the ground on trial converts the anchor into another
  frame. Both frames must be ranged against the ground separately.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames
  share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :47 *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a
  frame."*; Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*;
  Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
- **Field check:** would the "experts" still agree if one funding pool were removed? Did the
  presentation give the settled record the same standing as a contrarian claim, without a
  measurement to range them both against?
- **Refuter:** a manufactured-doubt campaign whose experts are genuinely low-overlap and convergent,
  or a symmetric presentation against a non-updating ground that still manufactures doubt. Then
  common cause and false balance are not the mechanisms claimed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict on any named person, campaign, industry or country.
- Not that a funding source, prestige or institutional affiliation makes a claim false. Fig 10
  :65-66: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the
  ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
- Not a permanent list of good and bad actors. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors
  survives a change of residual."*
- Not that a flat residual proves manufacture. It may be genuinely unresolvable, or unreachable at
  this budget.
- Not a single-line test. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment in a specific case.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading of a specific case: a source-discriminating instrument for that claim, its funding
and publication provenance, the independence history of its experts, and the residual trajectory
under inquiry. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color instrument *"cannot comment on the
source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. Pre-registered falsifiers of the
shape are in `01` §3 and `07` §4; the first is the most useful to test.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 5 :21, :48, :62-65 and Fig 1 :19, :22 (six short passages).
2. Check C1 and C2 against one case you know well: was there a named slot, positive mass and an
   advance falsifier, or only a general claim of uncertainty?
3. Check C3: count independent evidence pools behind the "experts", not the number of experts. Did
   the format give the ground a separate standing?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate an honest residual from a funded one in that case,
   or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
