## What the wider literature contributes, and its limits

The world sources corroborate the figure verdict but do not decide it. They also fall into clearly
separated fields, which is why they are worth keeping apart.

1. **History of science / agnotology.** Oreskes and Conway document the deliberate manufacture of
   doubt by a small, organised network whose output is delay, not a residual. Proctor's agnotology
   names the same class: culturally cultivated ignorance serving a product or an opinion. This is
   a *ground-facing* confirmation that the manufactured-doubt class exists in the world. It does
   not tell us how to classify a new case.
2. **Media studies / false balance.** The false-balance literature shows the mechanism F3 names:
   a norm of equal airtime makes a settled question look contested and creates doubt without any
   new evidence. Liz Spayd's line that the doctrine *"masquerades as rational thinking"* is the
   public-language version of Fig 2 :34.
3. **Decision theory / uncertainty.** The uncertainty literature supplies the other side: legitimate
   uncertainty is quantified, indexed to states and probabilities, and often resolvable by
   acquiring knowledge. This is the world-facing twin of Fig 1 :19 and Fig 5 :21. Note the
   asymmetry: "uncertainty" in the public square is often interpreted as ignorance, so the same
   word carries two different structures.
4. **Human cognition / source discrimination.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* explains why
   the content of a claim cannot settle its provenance. Two different causes can produce the same
   reading; behavior is a lossy projection. The same public claim "we are uncertain" can come from
   an honest residual or from a funded doubt machine. An instrument that cannot distinguish the
   cases demonstrates its own inadequacy.
5. **Search / shape.** *The Library of Babel* gives the stop rule: a narrowed descriptive shape is
   enough, and a bundle should be split before further search. It also supplies the honest warning
   that my own derivation is simulated by an LLM and cannot self-validate.

Overlap declaration: sources 4 and 5 and the RSI essay share an author with the figures, so their
agreement is expected and is weak corroboration. Sources 1 to 3 are independent fields. No source
was cited that was not actually opened; the two framework documents are local and were read.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the whole
  framework before a recipient has described what they would count as a failure sends the interior,
  not the test.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not
  hold the interiors. A reader who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined
  the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what they would expect to see if the checklist were true before giving them the essays.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an
  evidence pool"* A list selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 11 :51** - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Do not send
  this to a permanent list of "skeptics" or "believers"; send it to venues whose work touches the
  residual.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the three-valued test and its four refuters; (2) ask what
they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then offer the links, noting that
the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
