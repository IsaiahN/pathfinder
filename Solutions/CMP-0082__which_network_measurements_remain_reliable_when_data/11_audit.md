## 1. Independent domain census

I worked from the residual line alone before reading the primary's §1. The residual line is: the gap between a measurement on an observed network and that measurement on the true network, per slot, under incomplete, uneven, or biased collection.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: one residual per slot with the channel and ground as parameters; two graphs at the whole-network scale. Symmetry: exchangeability holds under random incompleteness and fails when inclusion probability depends on node attributes or a platform rule. Scale: the channel acts before the statistic, so the honest scale is the slot. A global average is a different object at a different scale, not a finer version of the same reading.
- **Domain 1, measurement.** Actors: nodes, edges, slots, named measures. Currency: graph statistics. Anchor: an independently enumerated true network. Without one, the anchor is owed and the ranking is unreached.
- **Domain 2, channel.** Actors: sampler, platform, sensors. Currency: observed edges. Anchor: none of its own. The platform's output moves with its ranking rule, so it is a frame. This is the common-cause collapse: the platform metric and the measured statistic share a hidden source.
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: independent enumerators, a second channel, direct survey. Currency: per-slot residuals. Anchor: a ground that does not update. This domain is nameable in principle, and is where the ranking would be settled.
- **Union, not partition.** The three defects overlap; the intersections are categorically different from the single conditions. Four loops are owed. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Dependency order.** Perceive the slot, then find a per-slot term, then state the objective, then plan, then verify and carry. Work above a broken perception link cannot arrive.

My census matches the primary's three domains, its union reading, and its dependency order. I reached it from a different starting point: the primary began with Fig 1's per-slot residual and Fig 11's capability claim, while I began with Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 3's dependency chain. ## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually sends.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` §2 and `08` §1: report per-slot residuals, range platform metrics against a ground, split the three defects, measure the gap, require independent channels.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete study that computes a named measure on observed data and reports it as the value for the true network, often using the platform's own ranking as confirmation.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing**, which a reported global average drops;
- the **non-updating anchor**, which a platform-published metric silently replaces with a frame;
- the **union split**, which a single correction collapses into one condition, leaving the intersections standing.

One thing survives cleanly: the requirement to measure the observed-to-true gap at all. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no enumerated ground exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
