## Wider literature that bears on the residual

- **Cognitive linguistics, conceptual metaphor theory.** Lakoff and Johnson, *Metaphors We Live By* (1980), reached through the SEP entry and the Zibin and Solopova article, both read. Predicts a shared sensorimotor basis for many mappings and cultural elaboration on top of it; corroborates F4 and F5 at the content level, but is secondary to the figures.
- **Analogy research.** Gentner (1983), read in full, plus Gentner 1997 and the structure-mapping literature reached through that text. Predicts relational mapping and systematicity; corroborates F1.
- **Cross-cultural psychology.** Su (2020), read; the cross-cultural analogy review was only seen in search results and was not read, so it is not cited.
- **Comparative cognition.** Smirnova et al. 2015 via Phys.org, read. Predicts that relational matching does not require a human lexicon; corroborates F6.
- **Philosophy of language.** SEP Metaphor, read. Predicts that a metaphor's force depends on conventions about the secondary subject, which are tradition-relative; corroborates F5.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was not triggered. Overlap is declared in the ledger: the three framework essays share an author and an evidence pool with the figures and are not independent witnesses.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who absorbs the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the links.
- **Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."** Do not pool later agreement as corroboration, and do not select recipients for receptiveness (Fig 2 :41 makes an exception only for conventions, where agreement is the product).
- **Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending a recording (a list of conclusions) is not the same as sending the generator (the shape claim and the refuter).
- **Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."** A larger audience does not add an anchor.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) residual, shape claim and four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the verdict; (3) only then, the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) offer the formula as a method for their own problem, with their own pre-registered refuter.
