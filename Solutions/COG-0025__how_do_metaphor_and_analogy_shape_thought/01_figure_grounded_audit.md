## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question is not "what is a metaphor" and not "list metaphors in different cultures." Stated as an effect:

> When a mapping from one domain to another becomes available to a thinker, what changes in what the thinker can compose, and how does that change depend on the language and tradition the thinker sits in?

So R is **the change in a frame's reachable compositions after a metaphor or analogy is available, measured per slot, across a variation dimension.** Define it in the framework's notation: R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot, where the arrival of a mapping alters Γ (the library of what can be expressed) and therefore alters the reach, not merely the labels. Three properties:

- **Arity:** two mechanisms (metaphor, analogy) plus a variation dimension (languages and traditions). This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :53 "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A single lever that clears the mechanism leaves the variation standing, because the second condition still binds.
- **Symmetry:** the mapping is **directional**, not symmetric. Gentner (1983) maps base to target; the SEP entry distinguishes primary subject from secondary subject; Fig 6 distinguishes a frame from the external source whose closure differs. Asking "is the mapping symmetric" is an empirical sub-question, not an assumption (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- **Scale:** first order inside one mind (one mapping), second order across a population and across history (a mapping that travels between languages and is handed along traditions, Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not").

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (THE_FORMULA.md "Direction"): **a shape stating what any mechanism must do to a frame's reach, what is invariant across languages and traditions, what is lossy in the crossing, and what instrument would discriminate a shared mechanism from a shared vocabulary.** Committing to this shape before the run makes it refusable rather than a preference that no check can see (Fig 5 :65-66).

## 3. Findings F1 to F7

### F1. The mapping carries relations, not attributes (D1)

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable"

A metaphor or analogy that carries only shared attributes (Juliet is beautiful, the sun is beautiful) is the flat case. The mapping that shapes thought carries the **relations** (Juliet appears above the speaker and brings hope, as the sun rises and gives light). This is exactly the distinction the structure-mapping literature draws independently: Gentner (1983) writes that "Relations between objects, rather than attributes of objects, are mapped from base to target," and that the rules "depend only on syntactic properties of the knowledge representation, and not on the specific content of the domains."

- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 12 (bond carries the information) and Gentner 1983 (low overlap: a formalism about typed bonds versus a psychological theory of mapping). Within the figures this is single-source (Fig 12), corroborated from outside the framework.
- **Mode:** `specified` on the claim that a shaping mapping is relation-carrying; `general` on the structural point that arrangement is not recoverable from ingredients.
- **Refuter:** a controlled case in which a mapping that transfers only object attributes produces the same downstream inferential change as one that transfers relations. If that holds, the bond reading does not govern this quantity and F1 is withdrawn.

### F2. A metaphor or analogy is an import that changes reach, not a new primitive (D1)

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world."

Fig 6 :26 "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."

Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

So the effect of a mapping is not that the thinker acquires a new primitive. It is that a frame gains access to structure already held in a differently closed frame. This is why "shaping thought" is a reach claim, not a vocabulary claim: Fig 5 :43-44 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." The mapping pays as compression. It does not add an atom to the world. The *Library of Babel* essay states the same thing in the idiom of transfer: "The reason why analogies work so well to explain concepts: they are translating structural relationships and problems through language and scenarios that you are already familiar with, so that the knowledge transfers."

- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 6 (closure and what import adds) and Fig 8 (union surplus), two figures with low conceptual overlap. World corroboration from Gentner 1983 ("imported into the target") and the essay, but the essay shares the framework's author and is not an independent witness.
- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. No measurement of reach before and after a mapping was taken here.
- **Refuter:** show a case in which a metaphor demonstrably adds a primitive to the frame's generating set, so that the closure expands by an atom rather than by access. F2 then fails and the mapping is a mint, not an import.

### F3. The figures refuse the merely rhetorical mapping (D1)

Fig 11 :38 "That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice."

Fig 12 :97 "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"

The framework uses both words in order to **draw a boundary with them**. A metaphor is refused when a description of the practice is available; an analogy is refused when a structural difference that makes a behavioural difference is available. So the figures do not license a free-floating "everything is a metaphor" reading. They license a mapping only where a structure is actually carried and the refusal is by a difference that shows up in behaviour. This is a check on the question itself: the claim that metaphor "shapes thought" is only admitted where the shape is observable in what the thinker can compose or predict.

- **Verdict:** triangulated within the figures across Fig 11 (metaphor refused in favor of practice) and Fig 12 (analogy refused in favor of isomerism). Both are framework-internal, so this is internal consistency, not an independent witness.
- **Mode:** `general`, structural.
- **Refuter:** produce a figure passage in which a mapping is accepted as explanatory solely on rhetorical grounds, with no carried structure and no behavioural difference. None was found in the census; if one is shown, F3 is wrong.

### F4. The kernel is invariant; the landing is lossy and conditioned (D2)

Fig 4 :19-20 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

Fig 4 :34-35 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

A mapping that travels between languages is a **seed**, not a recording of what it did in the origin tradition. The conditions it lands in (the habitat, the available relations, the cultural elaboration) decide what it becomes. The round trip has a nonzero gap R_T: what survives the crossing is the coarse shape, not the particular mapping. This is the figure-level reason for the empirical pattern reported in the cross-cultural literature: Zibin and Solopova (2024) write that "different languages, cultures and discourses may emphasize various components, resulting in metaphor variation," while also reporting the shared bodily basis that makes broad universality plausible. Su (2020) reports a measurable cross-tradition difference on a standard analogy task: "Chinese participants produced significantly more correct answers on the SPM task, indicating more successful analogical reasoning."

Two clarifications. First, the invariance claim is about the **mechanism**, not about which metaphors exist. Second, a measured cross-tradition difference in task performance is a difference in the habitat's effect on the same kernel, not evidence of a different kernel. Fig 2 :40 warns against reading a shared pool as two witnesses; the converse error, reading a different landing as a different mechanism, is the one to avoid here.

- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 4 (kernel and seed) and Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" (the reason to state the residual in effect terms before comparing languages). Low overlap between the scale figure and the leave/arrive figure.
- **Mode:** `specified` on the lossy landing; `general` on the invariant kernel.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a language or tradition in which the mapping operation itself is absent, so that no structure is transferred at all and the kernel cannot be said to be running. A difference in direction preference, source-domain choice, or task accuracy does not fire this refuter; only the absence of the operation does.

### F5. Convergence and adoption look identical in content; only provenance separates them (D2)

Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Fig 2 :41 "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"

This is the most important finding for the "across different languages and traditions" part of the question. When two traditions use the same mapping, three different things may have happened: (a) independent convergent derivation from shared embodiment, (b) adoption through contact, or (c) a shared convention where agreement is the product. In the contents they are indistinguishable. So the apparent universality of a metaphor is **one witness when it comes from a shared evidence pool** (shared body, shared environment), however many languages display it. Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" also applies: the absence of a recorded borrowing is not evidence of independent invention. The SEP entry supplies the philosophical version: it notes that a metaphor's interpretation turns on what the secondary subject is "habitually pretend[ed]" to have, which is a community-level, tradition-relative fact.

- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 8 (provenance and surplus) and Fig 2 (correlation has valence), two figures with low overlap.
- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. Deciding which of (a), (b), or (c) occurred in any particular case requires historical and corpus evidence, which is a ground reading, not a figure reading.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where two traditions demonstrably share a mapping, share no evidence pool, share no convention, and have no contact, so that the surplus must be attributed to something other than convergence or adoption. Until such a case is produced, the provenance requirement stands.

### F6. The mechanism is not dependent on a particular natural language (D1, D2)

Fig 4 :19-20 gives the shape: the kernel does not change with size or substrate, only the actors, currency and anchor change. Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The world supplies a low-overlap test from animal behaviour. Smirnova, Zorina, Obozova and Wasserman (2015, reported by Phys.org/University of Iowa, 2014) found that hooded crows "also have the brain power to solve higher-order, relational-matching tasks, and they can do so spontaneously," after training only on identity matching. Relational (analogical) matching therefore appears in a species without human language. This corroborates the figure shape that the mechanism is a contact-and-structure operation rather than a property of a particular lexicon. It does not corroborate any claim about human metaphor content.

- **Verdict:** figure single-source for the shape (Fig 4), corroborated by a genuinely low-overlap world source (comparative cognition, authors independent of the framework). The corroboration is weak for human language specifically and is recorded as such.
- **Mode:** `general` on the domain-agnostic kernel; the animal result is `grounded` within its own field but is secondary here.
- **Refuter:** a well-controlled demonstration that relational matching requires a natural-language lexicon, which would break the substrate-independence inference. Also note the study's own caveat: the crows' earlier identity-matching training likely enabled the transfer, so "spontaneous" does not mean "with no prior structure."

### F7. Which specific mappings are universal is a gap at figure level (D2, D3)

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

The canonical usage rule is explicit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." It cannot tell us that ARGUMENT IS WAR is universal and LIVER IS THE SEAT OF EMOTION is not. Those are names, and naming them requires a source-discriminating instrument: corpora, elicitation, behavioural measures, historical records. *The Hard Problem is About Source* states the needed discipline: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information," and the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the colour case. A cross-linguistic reading of a mapping is a reading of a projection; several sources could give it. What is owed is a better discriminator, not a name from the framework.

So the honest verdict for this part of the question is **`gap`**: unreached at this budget and under these instruments, not an absence of an answer in the world. Fig 8 :39 applies directly.

- **Verdict:** `gap` at figure level. The figure set governs the *form* of any answer (a bond-preserving import, invariant kernel, lossy landing, provenance requirement) and is triangulated for that form. It does not govern the *list* of universal mappings.
- **Mode:** `general` on the gap verdict itself; no `grounded` content is claimed.
- **Refuter:** produce a figure line from which a specific universal mapping follows without a ground reading. If such a line exists, this gap was a failure of the census and should be closed. None was found.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach (Seat Map :287). The shape claim fails if any of the following holds:

1. A metaphor or analogy is shown to add a primitive to the frame's generating set, so the closure expands by an atom rather than by access (kills F2).
2. A mapping that transfers only shared attributes produces the same downstream inferential change as one that transfers relations (kills F1).
3. A language or tradition is found in which the mapping operation itself is absent, so the kernel cannot be said to be running (kills F4/F6). Mere differences in direction preference, source domain choice, or test accuracy do not fire this.
4. Two traditions share a mapping, share no evidence pool and no convention, and have no contact, so the surplus cannot be attributed to convergence or adoption (kills F5).
5. A figure line is found that yields a specific universal mapping without a ground reading (kills F7, the gap verdict).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the packet is not sent. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the mechanism shape and the refuter, not with a list of metaphors. Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
2. State the mode on every claim. An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict (Seat Map :281).
3. Keep the three domains separate. Do not average "the mechanism is universal" with "the mappings vary"; they are different quantities at different levels.
4. For any specific mapping, ask for provenance before counting cross-linguistic agreement as corroboration (F5). Shared embodiment is one witness.

## 7. Final verdict of the primary seat

The shape survives and is triangulated: metaphor and analogy act as bond-preserving imports that change a frame's reach without adding primitives; the mechanism kernel is invariant across languages and traditions while each mapping lands lossily into a habitat that decides what it becomes; and convergent derivation, adopted import, and shared convention are indistinguishable in content and are separated only by provenance. The specific question of which mappings are universal is a **`gap`** at figure level, correctly returned as an unreached verdict rather than a silence. No part of this is `grounded`. Strains after re-census: one (R3, isomer versus functional group), named and repaired. The package is ready for the second seat, and for a one-to-one ask that requests a refutation rather than agreement.
