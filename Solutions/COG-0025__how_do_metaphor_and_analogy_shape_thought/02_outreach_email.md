**Subject:** One checkable output on metaphor, analogy, and cross-language variation

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** When a mapping from one domain to another becomes available to a thinker, what changes in what that thinker can compose, and how does the change depend on the language and tradition the thinker sits in? That is a compound of two conditions (a mechanism and a variation dimension), not one hard question, so I have kept the verdicts separate.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structure, `gap` on specific contents):**
>
> 1. A shaping metaphor or analogy carries the **relations** between things, not just the shared attributes of things. If it only carries attributes, it is the flat case and does no shaping work.
> 2. The mapping is an **import**: it gives a frame access to structure held in a frame whose closure differs. It does not add a new primitive to the thinker's generating set. What changes is reach, not the inventory of primitives.
> 3. The **mechanism kernel is invariant** across languages and traditions; only the actors, currency, and anchor change. Each particular mapping lands in a habitat whose conditions decide what it becomes, so the crossing is lossy and has a measurable round-trip gap.
> 4. Two traditions using the same mapping can have arrived by independent convergence, by adoption through contact, or by sharing a convention. The three look identical in the mapping itself. Only the record of provenance separates them, so agreement about a mapping is not automatically two witnesses.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** I am not naming which metaphors are universal. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which particular mappings are universal is a `gap` for me here; it needs corpora, elicitation, and historical evidence, not more argument. I am also not claiming that a shared bodily basis makes two cultures independent corroborators of each other.
>
> **What would refute the output, written down before this message:**
> 1. A metaphor that demonstrably adds a primitive to the frame's generating set, so the closure expands by an atom rather than by access.
> 2. A controlled case where a mapping transferring only shared attributes changes downstream inference exactly as much as one transferring relations.
> 3. A language or tradition where the mapping operation itself is absent, not merely different in direction or source-domain preference.
> 4. Two traditions sharing a mapping with no shared evidence pool, no convention, and no contact.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
