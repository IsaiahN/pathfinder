## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question, a **shape** is the statement of what any mechanism of metaphoric or analogical shaping must do (carry relations, change reach without adding primitives, be invariant in kernel and lossy in landing, require provenance for agreement), stated before we can name which particular mappings are universal. "A mapping carries the relations between domains, not just the attributes" is a shape. "ARGUMENT IS WAR is universal" is a name. The first constrains; the second fills. The same essay gives the stopping rule: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place."*

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: start with the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30 "Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" and :31 "rather than the cause." This is why §0 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` states R as a change in reach rather than as a list of metaphors: the words for the latter are borrowed from a particular tradition and would make any frame we picked seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).
- The same essay gives the reason analogies are the right instrument for this bootstrapping: *"The reason why analogies work so well to explain concepts: they are translating structural relationships and problems through language and scenarios that you are already familiar with, so that the knowledge transfers."* Note the precise claim: what transfers is **structural relationships**, which is the figure's bond and not the figure's atom. This is an internal corroboration from the framework's own author, not an independent witness.

## 3. What licenses a name here

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For this question the relevant grounds are corpora, elicitation, behavioural measures, historical records. A figure cannot supply them.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. If one tradition's mapping is taken into the analysis from another, the record of where it came from is part of the term, not metadata.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens this into an instrument requirement. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* The same reading could come from one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Applied here: a mapping observed in two languages is a projection. Shared body, borrowing, and convention can all give the same reading. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.

## 4. Why the "universal mechanism" claim must stay a shape

It is tempting to read the invariance of the kernel (Fig 4 :19-20) as a claim about particular metaphors. That would invert the figure's own rule. The kernel is what does not change with size; the actors, currency and anchor do change. So the invariant is the **mapping operation**, not its content. The sentence to keep is Fig 4 :34-35: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* A metaphor that travels is the seed. The language and tradition are the conditions. The growing is not in the seed.

This is also the reason D1 and D2 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` must keep separate verdicts. "The mechanism is invariant" and "the mappings vary" are not in tension and cannot be averaged into "mostly universal."

## 5. The three consequences for this question

1. **The shape is answerable; the list is a gap.** The figures decide that a shaping mapping carries relations (Fig 12 :5), is an import (Fig 6 :25-26), and needs provenance for apparent agreement (Fig 8 :50-52). They do not decide which mappings are universal. F7 records this as `gap`, and Fig 13 :134 says plainly: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."
2. **A metaphoric redescription is not a ground reading.** Fig 11 :38 "That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice." A metaphor applied to the framework (the bridge, the membrane, the seed) is a stand-in for the practice it describes; it is not itself evidence. The figures use the words in order to refuse them where a description is available.
3. **Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* A well-cited metaphor theorist's agreement does not settle whether a mapping transfers relations; only a case that pays against a ground does. This governs the one-to-one outreach in `02_outreach_email.md`.

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too."* But *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 7. Correction to my own §3 (visible)

I initially wrote that the "shape, not a name" rule forbids naming any particular structure in this question. That is too broad. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. Relations and bonds are already in the framework's vocabulary (Fig 12 :46), so saying that a mapping carries relations is a binding onto held terms, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains forbidden is naming a *specific universal mapping* as if the framework returned it. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost. This matches the exemplar's own correction about naming Gradient: the narrow form survives, the broad form does not.
