## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: mapping is bond-preserving import; kernel invariant; landing lossy; agreement needs provenance; specific universal mappings are a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim such as "this mapping is universal in all languages" or "this tradition reasons by a different mechanism."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **atom set** required before Fig 12 :117 can be applied. The shorthand says "isomers"; the concrete case may only support "functional group" (Fig 12 :124);
- the **provenance distinction** among convergence, adoption and convention. The shorthand says "agreement needs provenance"; the concrete case may have no record at all, in which case the correct report is one witness with unknown source, not two;
- the **ground requirement**. The shorthand's "gap" can be read as "the answer does not exist"; the figures say unreached at this budget, not absent (Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence").

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a shaping mapping carries relations rather than attributes, changes reach without adding a primitive, and runs the same kernel while landing differently. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
