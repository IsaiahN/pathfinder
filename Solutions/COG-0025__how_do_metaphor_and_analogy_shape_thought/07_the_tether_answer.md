# The Tether's answer: how metaphor and analogy shape thought across languages and traditions

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism shape, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which specific mappings are universal. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The change in what a frame can compose when a mapping from one domain to another becomes available, measured per slot, across a variation dimension. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: a mechanism condition and a variation condition overlap in every actual case (Fig 9 :53 "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"). The verdicts are therefore kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **What is carried is the relation, not the attribute.** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." A mapping that transfers only shared attributes is the flat case and does the shaping work only when it transfers the relational structure. Gentner (1983) independently reports that "Relations between objects, rather than attributes of objects, are mapped from base to target."
2. **The operation is an import that changes reach, not an added primitive.** Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :26 "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access." Metaphor and analogy give a frame access to structure held in a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23). The measured question is therefore not "what new word did the thinker learn" but "what can the thinker now compose that was not composable before."
3. **The mechanism kernel is invariant across languages and traditions.** Fig 4 :19-20 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The crow relational-matching result (Smirnova et al. 2015, via Phys.org 2014) corroborates the substrate-independence shape at low overlap; it says nothing about human metaphor content.
4. **Each mapping lands lossily into a habitat.** Fig 4 :34-35 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." The round trip carries a nonzero gap R_T (Fig 4 :30), so what survives a crossing between languages is the coarse shape, not the particular mapping. Zibin and Solopova (2024) report precisely this mixture of a shared bodily basis and culture-specific emphasis.
5. **Apparent agreement needs provenance before it counts as two witnesses.** Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Three cases must be distinguished: independent convergence, adoption through contact, and a convention where agreement about a shared meaning is the product (Fig 2 :41). A shared bodily basis is one witness however many languages show the mapping.
6. **Which particular mappings are universal is a gap, not an omitted answer.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Answering that requires corpora, elicitation, behavioural measures and historical records, which are grounds, not figure lines.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular metaphor (ARGUMENT IS WAR, LIVER IS THE SEAT OF EMOTION) is universal or culture-specific. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that languages share a mechanism because they share a vocabulary. Those are different quantities; shared vocabulary may be a convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that the crow result settles human language. It bears on the substrate-independence shape only.
- Not that two traditions using the same ingredients have the same mapping. Under Fig 12 :117 they may be isomers; under Fig 12 :124 they may be a functional group; the atom set must be shown (`06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not that the mapping is symmetric. Base to target is an ordered bond; swapping operands need not preserve systematicity (`06` Q3).
- Not grounded. No measurement of reach before and after a mapping was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, and what is owed

The ground, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; a reading that several paths can produce is "a projection with lossy information," and the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. Here the corresponding discriminators are:

- **Transfer test:** does a case in which a mapping transfers only shared attributes produce the same downstream inferential change as one that transfers relations? If yes, F1 fails.
- **Reach test:** does the mapping change what can be composed, and can a new primitive be ruled out? If a primitive is added, F2 fails.
- **Kernel test:** is the mapping operation present but differently directed, or absent altogether? Only absence fires the refuter; difference in direction or source-domain choice does not (F4/F6).
- **Provenance test:** for a mapping shared by two traditions, is there evidence of contact, of independent derivation from shared experience, or of convention? Without one, agreement is not two witnesses (F5).
- **Standing refuters:** from `01` §4, any of the five fires withdraws the corresponding claim.

What is owed for the `gap` specifically: a source-discriminating instrument at the level of cross-linguistic data, not a further argument from the figures. Fig 6 :34 "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there": corpora, elicitation and behavioural tasks already return something that fails to resolve the convergence/adoption question, so the instrument can be extended from them.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached; content unreached.** The six-term claim in Fig 13 is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes," not nature's own list. Declaring a set makes a choice visible, not neutral. The derivation stops at the limit of what the figures plus the opened sources can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." No last room: an eventual answer to which mappings are universal would become an atom for further questions, not a final one (Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of universal metaphors, and it does not claim a single mechanism that erases cultural difference. It says that a metaphor or analogy shapes thought by carrying the **relations** between two domains rather than their shared attributes; that the mapping is an **import**, giving a frame access to structure it did not compose without adding a primitive to the world; that the **mechanism kernel is the same** across languages and traditions while each mapping lands in a habitat whose conditions decide what it becomes, with a measurable round-trip loss; and that two cultures using the same mapping cannot be counted as two witnesses until provenance separates independent convergence, borrowing, and convention. It refuses to name which particular mappings are universal, because that is a name where the framework returns a shape. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of discriminator that would close the remaining gap. The gap stays open until the ground settles it.
