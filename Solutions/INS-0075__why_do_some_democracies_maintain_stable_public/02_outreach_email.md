**Subject:** one checkable shape about democratic stability, and what would refute it

> Hello,
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of a narrow question in democratic stability: why
> some democracies keep stable public support through severe disagreement while others become
> vulnerable to breakdown. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you
> to agree with anything. I have one shape and three refuters. If the shape does not survive, there
> is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** we observe two outcomes under the same stressor.
> Some polities keep a registered capacity to remove a government by non-violent means while
> citizens disagree sharply. Others lose that capacity. The question is not how much disagreement
> there is. It is what keeps a system's contact with its ground intact while its internal frames
> disagree, and what closes that contact. I am measuring this as a slice, not a national average,
> because an average can hide one live group.
>
> **The shape I get from the figures:** stable support under disagreement needs five things at
> once. (1) An anchor that settles who governs and does not change its answer because a party or
> media frame claims it should. (2) Frames that draw on different evidence, so their agreement
> means something. (3) A selection step between successive governing "rooms" against the residual
> the last one surfaced, rather than the same closure handed along. (4) A channel that still
> registers the ground per group, because the ground does not decay but the channel does.
> (5) A working way to refuse a failed claim, not merely absorb it.
>
> What I am explicitly **not** claiming: that any named country is stable or doomed; that a single
> institution, such as an independent judiciary or a particular voting rule, *is* the anchor; that
> disagreement itself causes breakdown; that more civil society is always better; or that this is
> an empirical result. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which institution fills the slot
> is a claim from your field, and it needs its own provenance. I have left the slot empty.
>
> **What would refute the shape. I wrote these down before running anything:**
> 1. A polity keeps stable, independently measured support while its legitimacy arbiter visibly
>    updates on what frames claim, over a period long enough to exclude a transient.
> 2. Breakdown occurs while the channel is demonstrably reachable: independent elections, free
>    per-group measurement, courts that do not update on the governing frame, and genuinely
>    low-overlap opposing frames.
> 3. The disagreement is one irreducible question whose residual shrinks under a single common
>    mechanism, and stability obtains with no selection step between rooms.
>
> If any holds, I withhold the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me
> than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary, are
> the authority. They are listed with line numbers so you can check each quote yourself:
> - Figures text with line numbers (local canonical extract, `Fig N :line`):
>   `C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule, branch `general-agent`:
>   https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - WATSON note on the figures as metalaws (local):
>   `C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem
> you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before*
> you run it, and state whether your mode is general, specified or grounded. The framework's own
> limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an unwritten refuter
> leaves the preference unfalsifiable. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported
> success.
>
> Thank you for reading this far.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter before theory | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Refuters written before the run | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| No country named, no institution named | Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; the canonical README's *"a shape, not a name."* |
| Per-group measurement instead of a national average | Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* |
| Disagreement amplitude not treated as the cause | Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* |
| Links only after stage 1 | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |
| No "bombshell", no "true answer", no request for trust | Prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it; only the ground settles it. |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask each user to write their own refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check what it is looking for. |

## The two-stage offer, explicit

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape and R1 to R3. Ask what the reader would count as a
   failure. Record their verdict before showing any figure.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three links. Their later agreement is
   never pooled as corroboration, because a reader who has absorbed the framework has joined the
   frame being checked (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user
   writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
