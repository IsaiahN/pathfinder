## Relevant wider literature, held as secondary

- The literature on democratic backsliding is not a single voice. The backsliding page reports
  Bermeo on promissory coups and executive aggrandizement, Mettler and Lieberman on four
  precondition clusters, and Haggard and Kaufman on the pernicious effects of polarisation, party
  realignment and incremental derogations. These are corroborating descriptions of the failure
  modes in `01` F2 and F4, not figure lines.
- The Journal of Democracy homepage lists 2026 articles by Lilliana Mason (titled *Polarization Is
  Not the Problem*), Jeffrey Kopstein (*When Polarization Turns Violent*), and Saskia Brechenmacher
  (*How Protest Can Halt Backsliding*). Their existence supports the correction that disagreement
  amplitude is not the decisive variable. Only the titles were read; no claim is made about their
  contents.
- V-Dem's own homepage states that it provides *"a multidimensional and disaggregated dataset that
  reflects the complexity of the concept of democracy as a system of rule that goes beyond the
  simple presence of elections."* This is the closest world analogue to Fig 1's per-slot rule and
  is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- The echo-chamber and motivated-reasoning pages both caution that effects are weaker or more
  contested than popular accounts suggest. That caution is kept visible. It is the reason the
  findings in `01` are `specified` and not `grounded`.

## Overlap warning

All world sources are encyclopedia or institutional pages. Several draw on the same underlying
studies, especially V-Dem data and Levitsky and Ziblatt. By Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a
verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool,"*
their agreement is one pool talking. They corroborate the figure-derived shape only in the weak
sense that a different literature finds compatible failure modes. No world source conflicts with
the figure verdict after the civil-society re-census in `01` section 4.4; the apparent conflict was
resolved by re-census, not by choosing one side.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework
  before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape
  claim and its three refuters; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who
  has absorbed the entire architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being
  checked. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded first.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the figures.
- **Fig 2 :40** again: a list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not
  corroboration.

**Order in a reply:** (1) the residual and `STABILITY(???)BREAKDOWN`, (2) the three refuters and
the question of what they would count as failure, (3) record the verdict, (4) only if asked, the
three public links with the note that the figures and not the summary are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, with its precondition

Stage 1 is the output check. Stage 2 is opt-in reading of the theory. Stage 3 is reuse of the
formula as a method. The order follows Fig 10 :52 (*"Bring a characterised residual, never the
source"*) and Fig 2 :45 (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).
Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: once a reader has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops
being evidence, so it is never pooled as corroboration. Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted,
but it still needs a ground per problem and a self-written refuter.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. It is currently a
`specified` shape with no ground reading, so no one should be told it is an empirical result.
