## The question as an effect

Restate the question before choosing any figure line. Two outcome classes are observed under the
same stressor (severe public disagreement):

- Option A: a polity keeps stable public support and remains able to remove a government by
  registered, non-violent means.
- Option B: a polity becomes vulnerable to breakdown, meaning support collapses or the capacity
  to remove a government by those means is degraded.

What is being decided is not whether disagreement is bad. It is what keeps a system's contact with
its ground intact while the frames inside it disagree strongly, and what breaks that contact. The
residual is the persistent gap between a polity's prediction (support continues) and what its
ground returns (support registers, or does not) at specific slots, not as one aggregate. The
figures are used to constrain the shape of that condition. They are not used to name a country,
a party, or a single institution.

## What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term

We bind the sought-for shape before the run, so it can accrue a residual like anything else:

> `STABILITY(???)BREAKDOWN` = an anchor that does not update on what frames claim, frames with
> low evidence overlap, a selection step between frames against the residual the last one
> surfaced, a channel that registers the ground per slot, and a working operator for refusal.
> The parentheses are the institutional name, which the framework cannot supply.

This is the "Direction" discipline from `THE_FORMULA.md`: write down what an answer would look
like, bind it, and let it be refusable. The pre-registered refuter is in
`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 and is repeated in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

## Deliverable headline

The figure-derived shape survives. The specific names do not. Disagreement magnitude is not the
decisive variable. The decisive variables are whether the legitimacy anchor updates on frames
(Fig 2 :33-34), whether frames are independent (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :48), whether the polity selects
between rooms rather than handing the same closure along (Fig 7 :35-36), whether the channel to
the ground is still readable per slot (Fig 10 :24-25, Fig 1 :19), and whether wrong claims can be
refused at all (Operators table :58). Every world source read is secondary corroboration. The
verdict comes from the figures.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. First, agreement is not an anchor: Fig 2 :35 says
"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients
for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies their agreement
(Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing
frames share an evidence pool"). Second, a verifier who holds the interiors cannot verify:
Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Third, a room does not
upgrade a label; more agreeing readers raise the vocabulary and do not add an anchor. Legal
constraints reinforce the structural one: unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM
style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted.
