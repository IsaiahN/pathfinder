# Isomers and order: same constitution, different bond

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground
reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two polities can hold the same written constitution and still diverge sharply in stability. The
written text records the offices and powers; it does not record the relations between the people
who hold them. The residual is the part of the outcome that the ingredient list cannot explain.
Two questions are owed: (1) are such polities **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) which
link of the dependency chain actually stops first?

## ATOMS AND BONDS

Atoms considered here are the formal items a written constitution can name: the offices, the
division of powers, the electoral rule, the rights list, the term limits. Bonds considered here are
the unwritten relations that decide how those atoms are used: mutual toleration, institutional
forbearance, acceptance of an adverse result, willingness to refuse a failed claim. No new
primitive is available without an import from political science, and none is claimed.

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the
half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :14-16: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can
tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost"*

That is exactly the constitution case. Two documents can list the same powers; nothing in the text
signals which norms are present, and nothing downstream of the text can recover them from the
ingredient list alone.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They
are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is / carried
entirely by the bond."**

If two polities share the formal atom set and differ only in the unwritten relation between those
atoms, they are Fig 12 isomers. The difference is invisible in the constitutional text and decisive
in behaviour. This is the structural reading of the world claim in `01` section 3: Levitsky and
Ziblatt's mutual toleration and forbearance are not extra atoms; they are the bond. The book's own
definition of forbearance, quoted in `04_links_and_distribution.md`, is a description of a relation
between actors, not a new office.

The weaker reading is the **functional group**: Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose
presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* A
non-updating anchor is a functional group; an independent judiciary, an electoral commission or a
constitutional court are candidate qualifiers. Selecting a qualifier is an import with provenance,
not a derivation. The group claim survives without the import; the qualifier claim does not.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two polities sharing the formal atom set show no
systematic difference in the stability outcome after a ground reading controls for per-slot support
and channel reachability, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.
Equally, if a stability difference appears without any difference in the unwritten relation, the
bond is not doing the work. **Status: `specified`, unmeasured.**

### Q2: What does the bond cost?

Fig 12 :99-101: *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."* and *"A bond that costs
nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."* Operators table :66: *"A bond that
costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."*

An unwritten norm costs bits to state but its absence is not recorded anywhere. Fig 12 :16 says
nothing signals the loss. That is why a constitution can look complete and still be the flat case:
it lists ingredients and no arrangement. Fig 1 :27 adds the second half: *"They are frame-internal.
A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A government's own report on its
observance of norms is frame-internal; the behaviour of an opposing frame after an adverse result
is the ground reading.

### Q3: Cycle, catalyst, and the sign of the loop

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes,
and a chain cannot close by construction."*
Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave
differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*

Elections are a cycle: the output (a government) re-enters as input to the next election. The sign
of the loop decides whether it closes. If the government changes the conditions of the next
election in its own favour, the loop is a mirror chain and Fig 7 :36 applies: *"A chain of mirrors is
one long room."* If it does not, the next room can differ from the last and be selected against the
residual. The distinction is not in the written rule; it is in the bond.

A norm can also be a catalyst: forbearance survives its own use when the actor who refrains is not
weakened by the restraint, and is spent when restraint is read as weakness and punished later. Fig
12 :119 says nothing records which a term is, so this has to be measured, not inferred from the
text. **Strain named, not hidden:** the figure's catalyst definition comes from chemistry; whether
a political norm survives use is an empirical question about that polity.

### Q4: Which link stops first?

Fig 3 :25-27: *"The middle link is where chains usually break."* and *"Perception has obvious
failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them,
having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has
neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Apply the five links of Fig 3 to INS-0075:

1. **Perception:** can the polity register what is in front of it? Elections, polls, courts, free
   media. Obvious failures, obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary:** does it have terms for what a disagreement affords? Which claims can be refused,
   which are negotiable, which are not political at all. This is the middle link.
3. **Objective:** can it say what would count as stability, in its own terms? Removal capacity
   intact? Support registered per slot? This is also part of the middle link.
4. **Planning:** a route to that objective under incomplete information. Reform design.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem.

Most reform effort lands on link 1 (more polls, more transparency) or link 4 (more reform
packages), because those are visible. Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which
link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* If links 2 and 3 have
no instrument, work above them arrives at a step that cannot use it. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose
input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the middle link is shown to have a working instrument in a
given polity and strengthening it does not change the stability outcome, the "break is in the
middle" claim fails for that polity. This is a local, refutable claim, not a universal law.

## CONCLUSION

The two polities are **isomers** if they share the formal atom set and differ only in the unwritten
bond: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The weaker and safer claim is the
**functional group**: a non-updating anchor, low-overlap frames, selection between rooms, a readable
per-slot channel and a working refusal are a group whose qualifier is not derived by the framework.
The written constitution records the ingredients and not the arrangement, which is why the
ingredient list alone cannot predict the outcome.

The dependency order puts the vocabulary and objective links first. Reform above a broken middle
link cannot rescue it, and a link with no instrument is not a link that works. `01` F3 and F6 are
the figure-grounded content of those two links; this file supplies only the order.
