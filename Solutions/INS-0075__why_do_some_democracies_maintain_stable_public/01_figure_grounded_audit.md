## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question compares two outcome classes under the same stressor:

- A: stable public support despite severe disagreement, with the capacity to remove a government
  by registered, non-violent means intact.
- B: vulnerability to breakdown, meaning support collapses or that removal capacity is degraded.

Let `R` be the persistent gap between a polity's prediction (support continues, the government can
be replaced at the ballot) and what its ground returns (support registers at specific slots, or
does not), while the frames inside the polity disagree strongly. The residual is a **slice**, not a
single national number. Symbols table :38-40: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is
always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

What is owed is the shape of the condition that keeps the channel to the ground open under high
disagreement, and the shape of the condition that closes it. We do not owe a list of countries, a
party name, or a single institutional fix. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer
itself."*

We bind the sought-for shape now, before searching, so it can be refused:

> `STABILITY(???)BREAKDOWN` = an anchor that does not update on what frames claim, frames with low
> evidence overlap, a selection step between frames against the residual the last one surfaced, a
> channel that registers the ground per slot, and a working operator for refusal. The parentheses
> hold an institutional name that the framework cannot supply.

## 1. Figure census before interpretation

Each domain is described by arity, symmetry and scale, then by actors, currency and anchor. A
candidate without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain. The partition/union question and the
dependency order (Fig 3) are stated before any figure line is used to decide.

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least two conditions. Support stability and breakdown are not two values of one
  scalar measured by one poll; they are different regimes produced by different loop closures.
  Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on
  a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than
  search."* So this is a **union compound** until shown otherwise, with its own loop and ground.
- **Symmetry:** severe disagreement is relational among frames, not a symmetric join of two
  operands at the same level. Adding another agreeing frame does not add evidence. Fig 2 :40:
  *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames
  share an evidence pool."*
- **Scale:** first order inside each frame (a party, a court, a news outlet, a citizen), second
  order at the polity level where frames are selected against the ground. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled
  molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level
  and never about the world."*

## 2. Findings, one loop per domain

### F1 (D1) The anchor must not update, or the system enters coherent drift

Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because
of what any frame thinks."* and *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything
drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."*

In effect terms: a democracy stays stable under disagreement while there remains something that
settles the question of who governs and does not change its answer because a party or media frame
claims it should. When the arbiter starts updating on the claims of the parties it is supposed to
arbitrate, the correction channel becomes a mutual-update loop. The drift is coherent, so the
actors experience it as normal politics, which is why it is not noticed from inside. Fig 2 :45:
*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

**Refuter:** show a polity sustaining stable, independently measured public support while its
legitimacy arbiter demonstrably moves when frames push on it, over a period long enough to rule
out a transient. If that holds, the anchor requirement is false. **Verdict: triangulated** within
the framework (Fig 2 and the Symbols table definition of the ground), still ungrounded politically.

### F2 (D1) Selection between rooms, not a mirror chain

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the
ground votes."*
Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and
match the residual it surfaced."*
Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
Fig 7 :37: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*

Elections, leadership turnover and open contestation are the political analogue of selecting the
next room against the residual the last one surfaced. If successive "rooms" are the same closure
with a different label (ρ near 1), handing the question along produces motion without progress:
agreement compounds, evidence does not. This is the figure-grounded reason why a polity can look
stable, be internally unanimous, and still be vulnerable. High internal agreement is a property of
one room, not a verdict about the ground.

**Refuter:** demonstrate stability maintained over time with low between-room difference and no
mechanism that selects a new room against the surfaced residual. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 7
plus Fig 2 :40 on correlation), ungrounded.

### F3 (D1, D3) The ground does not decay; the channel does

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 10 :25: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*

Public support does not evaporate by itself. What fails is the channel that registers it: polling
that saturates, local signals pooled into a national average, transient protests dropped, election
administration captured, courts that no longer read as independent. The output of channel failure
is not a negative verdict about support; it is "you have lost touch," which is a different claim
and is much harder to see from inside. This is the strongest single figure-grounded point for the
diagnosis half of the question: vulnerability to breakdown begins as a measurement failure, before
it becomes a collapse.

**Refuter:** find a case where the channel is demonstrably intact (independent measurement, per
slot, with a real possibility of removal) and support collapses anyway. That would move the cause
to the ground or to an exogenous shock, not to the channel. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 10 plus
Fig 1 :19 and Fig 3 :27), ungrounded.

### F4 (D2) Severe disagreement may be a union, not one hard question

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on
a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than
search."*
Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you
search again."*
Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So
searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

Severe disagreement is often treated as one national malady. The figure says to test that first.
If different rules work well on different subsets, the residual is a compound of overlapping
conditions, not a single large disagreement. A single lever clears the region where only one
condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The political analogue is that a single
reform (a voting rule, a civics campaign, an economic transfer) can improve one subset while the
compound persists. The first instruction is to unbundle, not to search harder.

**Refuter:** show that the disagreements do shrink with effort under one common mechanism and that
the conditions are separable rather than overlapping. Then the union reading fails and the single
question reading stands. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 8 :48), ungrounded.

### F5 (D2) Low overlap is the resource; agreement from one pool is not corroboration

Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement
means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*
Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
Fig 10 :80: *"And divergence regenerates unevenly. Frames whose independence is renewed by
separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be
rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."*

A polity with many institutions that all read the same evidence pool has one frame wearing many
names. Its agreement is the pool talking. Stability built on that agreement is fragile precisely
because rotation cannot refresh a correlation fixed at origin. Fig 10 :80 also explains why
"more voices" is not automatically more independence: independence is renewed by separate
experience, not by count.

**Refuter:** provide a case where two frames with demonstrably low evidence overlap agree, and
that agreement predicts stability better than the high-overlap alternatives. That would support,
not refute, this finding. To refute it, show that low overlap adds no predictive surplus over
pooled agreement for the stability outcome. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 2, Fig 8, Fig 10),
ungrounded.

### F6 (D3) Aggregate metrics hide the live slot

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*
Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

A national approval average near zero residual can coexist with one live slot (a region, an ethnic
group, a cohort) where the signal is strongly positive. Fig 1 :13 says that is a legal state, not
an inert one. The framework's rule is to measure per slot and disallow a frame scoring itself with
a quantity it produces: Fig 1 :27, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a
quantity it also produces."* A government's own popularity metric is frame-internal; an
independent, disaggregated instrument is the ground reading. This finding is the figure-grounded
reason to prefer the V-Dem style multidimensional disaggregation to a single index (section 7).

**Refuter:** show that a single aggregate index predicts breakdown as well as a per-slot reading
across a set of cases, so the slot indexing carries no information. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 1
plus Fig 10 :25 on pooled readings), ungrounded.

### F7 (D4) Capability lives in the agent-and-habitat, not in the agent alone

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system
capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it
improves."*
Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Replacing a leader, or "improving" a single institution in isolation, changes nothing unless it
changes contact with the ground. Civil associations are not good or bad by kind; their valence is
relative to the residual. This figure resolves an apparent conflict in the world sources: Putnam's
bridging social capital and Berman's finding that civil society helped destroy Weimar are not
contradictions once valence is made relative. Bonding associations can deepen the very cleavage
that is the residual; bridging associations can renew the independence Fig 10 :80 requires.

**Refuter:** show an agent-only improvement (a leader, a single agency) that moves the ground and
sustains it, with contact unchanged. Fig 11 :71-72 already names this as the standing refuter of
the capability claim. **Verdict: triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 10 :80), ungrounded.

### F8 (D4) Refusal is a separate operator; a vocabulary that cannot refuse only accumulates

Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only
accumulate"*
Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

A polity needs a way to say that a held claim is false, not merely that an operand is absent.
Elections, votes of no confidence, impeachment, judicial review and a free press are political
instances of the negation operator: they refuse a claim rather than subtract an actor. Without such
an operator the system can accumulate grievances and corrections but cannot carry the correction,
because carrying it requires refusing the term that failed. Fig 10 :47 adds the companion rule:
a convention (for example, a norm of mutual toleration or forbearance) that decides an outcome has
stopped being a convention and has become a authored constant. That is the precise point at which
a soft guardrail becomes a captured instrument.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a polity that sustains refusal of failed claims over time with no separate
negation path, where all correction is absorption into existing terms. **Verdict: triangulated**
(Operators table and Fig 12; corroborated by Fig 10), ungrounded.

### F9 (D4) Introduction is available, exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for
another."*
Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"*

Institutional design can add an actor, a court, a commission, a transparency rule. It cannot
subtract the habitat: contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the designer. Trying
to isolate a polity from a cleavage substitutes one habitat for another and inherits new actors
that were not chosen. What was failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails; what was
unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts. This is why managed political systems are
permanent work rather than a solved state, and why the honest output of this derivation is a set of
constraints and refuters, not a design bill.

**Refuter:** show a case where an institution was successfully removed from a polity's habitat and
the removal, not a substitution, produced durable stability. **Verdict: single-source** for the
asymmetry (Fig 11), corroborated in the world by the backsliding literature on imported reforms
that worsen erosion; ungrounded.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

### 4.1 "Public support is the ground" (initial reading, corrected)

The first reading treated public support itself as the anchor. That strains Fig 2 :29-30, because
a captured or unfree "public support" reading moves when frames push on it. Re-census: the anchor
is whatever settles the question and does not update on frames, which in a political system is the
registered capacity to remove a government under conditions the frames cannot alter at will. An
approval poll is a **reading**, not the anchor. Correction recorded in section 8.

### 4.2 "Disagreement causes breakdown" (initial reading, corrected)

The obvious reading is that more disagreement causes more breakdown. Fig 7 :35 and Fig 2 :40 make
that a category error: agreement within a room is variation, not a verdict, and correlation
disqualifies. What matters is the relation between frames and the ground, not the amplitude of
disagreement. Correction recorded in section 8.

### 4.3 "Independent judiciaries prevent breakdown" (world claim, held as corroboration only)

The backsliding page reports a 2011 study of 163 countries finding that established independent
judiciaries prevent drifting to authoritarianism, while newly formed courts are associated with
regime collapses. This is a world claim, not a figure line. Fig 2 :36 says an anchor is legitimate
when the question is constitutively about it; Fig 10 :47 says a convention that decides an outcome
has stopped being one. The world finding is consistent with an anchor that has settled and become
non-updating, and with a newly authored "court" that is still a frame. It corroborates but does not
decide. Held as secondary.

### 4.4 "Civil society helps" versus "civil society killed Weimar" (world conflict, resolved by re-census)

The Bowling Alone page reports Putnam's bridging versus bonding distinction; the backsliding page
reports Berman's finding that robust civil society helped scuttle Weimar by strengthening social
divisions in filter-bubble-like associations. This is a genuine conflict inside the world pool.
The rule is that a conflict sends us back to the census, never flips the verdict. Re-census: Fig 11
:49 makes valence relative to the residual, so "civil society" was always two domains masquerading
as one. Bonding associations have valence relative to a cleavage residual; bridging associations
have valence relative to an isolation residual. Both world claims are retained, neither averaged.

### 4.5 Fig 13 six-term substrate: a declared gap

An earlier pass tried to read the political system as a composition of the six physical terms
(space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network). That reading only works by replacing "spectrum"
with "pluralism" and "gradient" with "polarisation" by analogy. Fig 13 :75 says *"Two states never
bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only
through a state."* That is a rule about the six primitives, not a selector of political
institutions. Applying it to parties and polls is a strain, and a strain means the line governs
another quantity. Re-census moves D5 to `gap`.

What survives from Fig 13 is only the `general` structural caution: the six are *"a human's careful
guess at reality's compression axes"* (world source), any minimum set is a visible choice, and
naming one of its members as *the* political answer would turn a visible choice into an invisible
one. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (recorded without its trailing
comma). This is recorded as a gap, not as a failure to find a line.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

Required before any outreach: write what would refute the shape claim before the run. The shape
claim is `STABILITY(???)BREAKDOWN` as bound in section 0. It is refuted if any of the following
holds:

1. **Anchor refuter (R1).** A polity maintains stable, independently measured public support and
   an intact removal capacity while its legitimacy arbiter demonstrably updates on what frames
   claim, sustained over a period long enough to exclude a transient. That breaks F1.
2. **Channel refuter (R2).** Breakdown occurs while the channel is demonstrably reachable:
   independent elections, free per-slot measurement, courts that do not update on the governing
   frame, and low evidence overlap between opposing frames. That breaks F2 and F3.
3. **Union refuter (R3).** The severe disagreement is shown to be one irreducible question whose
   residual shrinks under a single common mechanism, and stability obtains with no
   selection-between-rooms step. That breaks F4 and the dependency order in section 1.2.

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. If any fires, the shape claim is withheld
or withdrawn. None has been run, because no political ground reading was taken. This is a
simulation, so the refuter is a criterion offered to a checker, not a test performed here.

## 8. Strains, gaps and what is left open

- **Live strain, named:** a real polity's constitution is amended by legitimate procedure. Is an
  amended anchor an updating anchor? The figures distinguish a convention that decides an outcome
  from one that does not (Fig 10 :47), but they do not adjudicate constitutional amendment versus
  capture. This needs a political ground reading, not a further argument. It is not smoothed.
- **Live strain, named:** Fig 10 :80 says independence is renewed by separate experience. Whether
  a given media reform actually produces separate experience is an empirical question about that
  media system. Not settled here.
- **Gap:** D5, the six-term substrate, governs physical composition and returns no verdict for
  this question. Recorded as `gap`, per the rule that unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
- **Gap:** the framework returns no institutional name. Which arrangement fills the parentheses in
  `STABILITY(???)BREAKDOWN` is owed to a ground reading and a provenanced import, not to this
  derivation.
- **No averaged verdict.** D1 to D4 are separate. A checker may accept D3 and reject D2 without
  contradiction; that is a useful result, not a failure of the package.
