# The Tether's answer: how these six attributes affect whose claims are believed

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separations, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on any ranking of the six. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The residual is the per-slot gap between the belief a claim receives and what the claim's ground would pay if the claim were read without the claimant's attributes. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The question bundles two anchors, so it is a union compound with its own loop, not one question with six dials (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The shape, stated as three separate verdicts

**Verdict 1, claim truth (D1).** The six attributes cannot settle whether a claim is true. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Credibility is frame-produced: Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Where an audience uses a marker as the answer to the claim, the anchor has moved into the room and drift becomes coherent (Fig 2 :34). **Mode `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 1 :27 and Fig 2 :29, low overlap between the agent loop and the ground figure.

**Verdict 2, belief reception (D2).** The six attributes are habitat actors with valence relative to the residual, not a standing credibility list. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." They change contact, that is who gets heard, and in the framework's own terms they give an action a budget to be tried. They do not pay for the claim. Fig 9 :51: "what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it". **Mode `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 9 :51.

**Verdict 3, verification (D3).** Endorsement chains compound agreement, not evidence. Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A credential that certifies a reusable method adds an atom and can move the wall; a badge that certifies only rank reweights existing terms and adds none (Fig 6 :22). **Mode `specified`, single-source on the closure vocabulary of Fig 6 and Fig 7 together, with world corroboration from the credentialism and Matthew effect pages.**

**Verdict 4, the ranking.** There is no figure line that orders gender, class, race, ethnicity, status or credentials by causal weight. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". So the requested ranking is `gap`, of the unreached-at-this-budget kind. An instrument is owed, not a number invented. **Mode `gap`.**

The four verdicts are kept separate and are never averaged into one "identity matters by x percent" claim.

## 3. The structure, in brief

- **Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Blinding a name leaves a title; blinding a title leaves an accent.
- **Broken middle link.** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The missing instrument is a way to say, per slot, what would count as belief for ground reasons.
- **Isomers.** Blind review (subtraction of an operand), open review (conjunction), credential gating (sequence) and a citation loop (cycle) are different bonds over the same atoms, hence different substances (Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour.").
- **Two currencies.** Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A marker is cheap to describe and may be worthless to settle; they do not add.
- **Direction not given by association.** The word "influence" is ordered; the measured association is symmetric. The arrow must be imported from an identification frame with provenance and a shadow test, or read from an intervention.

## 4. What this answer explicitly does not say

- Not that any of the six is more important than another. That is the declared `gap`.
- Not that the effect size is universal, stable across audiences, or additive across attributes.
- Not that removing one marker removes the compound. It is a union.
- Not that a credential is either a pure signal of competence or a pure badge of rank. Both bonds exist; the provenance decides.
- Not that belief and truth are the same anchor. They are different domains with different anchors (Fig 2 :36-37).
- Not that a correlation establishes the direction of influence (Fig 12 :59, Operators table).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

The ground, not agreement. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The owed instrument is:

1. A factorial vignette varying gender, class, race, ethnicity, status and credentials independently, with claim content held fixed.
2. A checker who reads the claim's ground blind to all markers, so evidence bearing only on the maker never reaches the verdict (Fig 9 :51).
3. Per-slot residuals reported without aggregation (Fig 1 :19).
4. An anchor that does not update on the audience's own report (Fig 2 :33).
5. The eight refuters in `01` section 4 registered before the run, with the equivalence test for R1 specified in advance.

Standing falsifiers: a masked-identity equivalence result (R1); an attribute with fixed positive valence across all residuals (R2); a method-free badge that changes ground contact (R3); a ground that moves when belief moves (R4); aggregation that loses no slot signal (R5); a single lever that clears all intersections (R6); a symmetric association that determines the arrow (R7).

**Honest stop.** The six are a declared list read off the question, not nature's own set (Fig 11 :48). The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Indiscernibility is not identity. Where the instrument is absent, the derivation stops and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. It does not fill the slot with an attribute name in order to look complete.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The six attributes do not form one dial. They are habitat actors whose effect is relative to the residual at each slot, so a marker that buys a hearing where a claim is hard to check can be irrelevant or harmful where the ground is directly readable. They cannot make a claim true: credibility is a quantity the audience's own frame produces, and a frame cannot score itself with what it produces. "Who is believed" and "is the claim true" are two questions with two anchors and must not be averaged. Blind review, open review, credential gating and citation loops are different bond arrangements over the same actors, hence different substances, not different amounts of the same thing. No figure line ranks the six, so the framework returns a shape, not a ranking: a per-slot separator between attributes that settle claims and attributes that only change contact, with a pre-registered refuter and a blind-ground instrument owed to fill it. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
