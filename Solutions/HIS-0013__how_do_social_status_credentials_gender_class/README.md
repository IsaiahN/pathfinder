# Solution HIS-0013: whose claims are believed

**Question:** How do social status, credentials, gender, class, race, and ethnicity influence whose claims are believed?

**Source:** `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 18, section 2 "Trust, authority, and expertise".

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain separations, `general` on the structural shape, and `gap` on any ranking of the six attributes by causal weight. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive file states that this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

**Field for public contact points:** sociology of knowledge research ( Track A, institutional and opt-in only).

## The residual in effect terms

The residual is not "how much bias is there". It is the per-slot gap between the belief a claim receives and what the claim's own ground would pay if the audience read it without the claimant's attributes. The six named attributes are candidates for the channel that fills that gap. They are not averaged into one credibility score, because the effect is indexed per claimant, claim and audience slot (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). An answer would look like a separator: for each slot, which attributes are verdict-bearing, which are contact-changing only, and what instrument would discriminate them.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures rank gender above class, or credentials above race. They do not, and the request for such a ranking is refused as a shape-not-a-name gap (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

The reason for the first three is structural, not merely legal. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Legally, unsolicited bulk mail to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions.
