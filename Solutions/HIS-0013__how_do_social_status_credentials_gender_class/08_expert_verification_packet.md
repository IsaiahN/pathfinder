# Expert verification packet: the Tether on whose claims are believed

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in sociology of knowledge, science studies or social epistemology can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separations, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the ranking of the six. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed by this packet.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Whose claims are believed" bundles two questions with two anchors. "Who is believed" is constitutively about people, so recorded belief is a legitimate anchor for it. "Is the claim true" has an anchor that does not change because an audience thinks otherwise. The six attributes are habitat actors whose valence is relative to the residual at each slot, not a standing credibility list; they change contact, that is who gets a hearing, and cannot settle a claim. The question is a union compound, so one lever leaves the intersections standing. Blind review, open review, credential gating and citation loops are different bonds over the same actors, hence different substances. No figure line ranks the six, so that ranking is a `gap` with a described instrument owed, not a number to be invented.

Either fires C1.

### C2 - World-facing; checkable against the empirical literature

- **Statement:** the six attributes have residual-relative valence, and the compound is a union rather than a partition, so a pooled effect size cannot be promoted into a standing ranking.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." ### C3 - Framework-internal derivation with a declared limit

- **Statement:** the institutional arrangements are isomers over the same atoms, and the requested ranking is a `gap`, not a silence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. - **What to check:** is the bond mapping faithful? Blind review removes an operand (subtraction); open review holds claim and marker together (conjunction); credential gating requires the credential before admission (sequence); a citation loop re-enters its own output (cycle). - **Refuter:** show that two of these arrangements have the same bond once operand sets are matched, so their behavioural differences are only parametric; or produce a figure line that assigns an order or weight to the six attributes. Either fires C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of the six. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not a universal effect size, and not an additive model of six effects.
- Not that any marker makes a claim true, and not that belief is the ground for truth.
- Not that removing one marker clears the compound.
- Not that a credential is only a badge or only a method. Both bonds exist; provenance separates them.
- Not that a correlation establishes the direction of influence.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

Two live type questions are stated rather than hidden: (S3) maker position is claim-bearing evidence when the claim is constitutively about the maker, for example where an observer was standing, under Fig 2 :36; (S4) "influence" is ordered while the measured association is symmetric, and the arrow requires an identified intervention or an imported frame.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried from the exemplar, not re-opened): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". Here:

1. A factorial vignette varying the six markers independently with claim content fixed.
2. A checker who reads the claim's ground blind to all markers, so maker-only evidence cannot reach the verdict.
3. Per-slot residuals reported without aggregation, with R1 specified as an equivalence test in advance.
4. An anchor that does not update on the audience's own report.
5. An identified intervention for the direction of influence, imported with source gate and shadow test.
