# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live strains S3 and S4 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7 are stated in the message, not hidden. The recipient must know where the separator stops.

---

**Subject:** One check on a claim about whose claims get believed

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the separations, `general` on the shape):** the question "how do status, credentials, gender, class, race and ethnicity influence whose claims are believed" bundles two questions with two different anchors. "Who is believed" is constitutively about people, so recorded belief is a legitimate anchor for it. "Is the claim true" has a different anchor, whatever settles the claim and does not change its answer because of what an audience thinks. Mixing them produces the appearance that a marker which buys a hearing also makes a claim true.
>
> The six attributes are then not a standing list of credibility weights. Their effect is relative to the residual at each slot, so the same marker helps where the ground is remote and hurts where the ground is directly checkable. A pooled number across claim types throws away the only signal that says where to look. A credential that certifies a learned method and a credential that certifies only rank look identical in the record; only their provenance separates them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any one of the six is the main driver, that the effect size is universal, that removing one marker removes the compound, or that a correlation alone tells us the direction of influence. I am not asking you to trust the framework. I am asking you to check whether the separator fails.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. a masked-identity study in which belief allocation is unchanged across systematic variation in all six attributes, at several claim domains, with a pre-registered equivalence test;
> 2. an attribute whose valence is positive for every residual at every scale, including where the ground is directly checkable;
> 3. a badge that adds no method to the checker's vocabulary but changes ground contact in a source-discriminating measurement;
> 4. a measurement in which the ground's answer moves because the audience's belief moved;
> 5. aggregate statistics that fully determine the per-slot allocation with no live slot left;
> 6. one manipulation that removes all six channels and their intersections at once.
>
> There is one honest limit I want on the table before you answer. When a claim is constitutively about the maker, for example where an observer was standing, the maker's position is evidence about the claim, not merely about the maker. The separator treats that as a different slot with its own anchor. If you think that exception is larger than I have allowed, say so; that is a failure worth having.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. **Please record what you would count as a failure before reading any framework material below.**
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: local file `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project.
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
