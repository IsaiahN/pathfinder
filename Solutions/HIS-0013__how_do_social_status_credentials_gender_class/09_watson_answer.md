## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that six attributes influence whose claims are believed. Option A: return one ranked list of the six with effect sizes. Option B: split the residual into its domains, separate the two anchors, and return a per-slot separator with the ranking recorded as `gap`. What each leads to: A delivers a number that aggregates across slots, promotes a frame-internal quantity into a verdict, and asks outside checkers to endorse a ranking the framework never derived; B delivers a checkable shape, eight written refuters, and a described instrument that would fill the slot if a ground ever pays for it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot gap between belief B(c,p,a,t) and what the claim's ground would pay if the claim were read without the claimant's attributes. Arity: six attributes, one claim, one claimant, one audience, one time, with strong overlap among the attributes, so a union of conditions rather than six independent causes. Symmetry: "influence" is ordered (attribute to belief) while the measured association is symmetric, so the arrow needs an import or an intervention. Scale: individual encounter, institutional room chain, population-level credential inflation. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Domain 1, claim truth.** Actors: the claim, the instrument or record that could settle it, the audience as checker. Currency: R per slot. Anchor: whatever settles the claim and does not change its answer because of any frame's opinion. Nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: partition at the closure boundary.
- **Domain 2, belief reception.** Actors: claimant, audience, institution supplying the credential. Currency: "credibility accrued", a frame-internal quantity. Anchor: recorded belief, legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people. Nameable as a replicable distribution, so this is a domain. Question type: union.
- **Domain 3, verification and gatekeeping.** Actors: reviewers, editors, committees, prize panels. Currency: agreement, which is a budget filter and not a witness. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: checking arrangement. Maker-only evidence is denied to the verifier except where the claim is constitutively about the maker.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the marker, have vocabulary for credibility, say what would count as belief for ground reasons, plan a route, learn and carry. The third link has no instrument (Fig 3 :27), so effort on links one, two, four and five lands above the break.
- **Strain test.** The physical six of Fig 13 do not map onto the six social attributes; forcing that mapping was a strain and was re-censused into habitat actors under Fig 4 :19. Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment the right anchor only for a question about people; re-census gives one anchor per domain. Two live type questions remain: testimony about the maker's own position, and the direction of influence from a symmetric association.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The six attributes influence **who is believed**, by changing contact and weighting, and they do not influence **whether the claim is true**, because credibility is frame-internal and the ground is the only metric. Their valence is relative to the residual, so no standing credibility list survives a change of domain. The question is a union compound, so one lever leaves the intersections standing, and the effect is per slot, so a pooled effect size is the wrong object. Institutional arrangements are isomers over the same actors, distinguished by bond type, and provenance separates a method-adding credential from a rank badge. The ranking of the six is `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134), with a factorial blind-ground instrument owed. Mode `specified` on the separations, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the ranking, ungrounded, candidate. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** a false separator would misallocate authority in both directions, either dismissing claims that should be heard or crediting claims that should be checked. Because the answer is mode-labelled, carries eight refuters and records the lineage, that error is locatable rather than invisible. The avoidable extra cost is spending outside checkers' independence by sending a recording or a ranking instead of the shape and its refuter (Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. The two anchors, the per-slot rule and the refused ranking can be corrected without withdrawing the habitat-actor claim. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet; no term has to be deleted from the record, and a refusal can only fall out of reach when something new is settled.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A factorial vignette with independently varied markers, a checker blind to the markers, a non-updating anchor and a pre-registered equivalence test would name the per-slot weights; an attribute with fixed positive valence across all residuals would break Fig 11 :51; a ground that moved when belief moved would break Fig 2 :33; a figure re-census that assigns weights would clear the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
