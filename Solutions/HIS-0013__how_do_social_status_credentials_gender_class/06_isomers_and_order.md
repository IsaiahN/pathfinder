# Union structure, order and isomers in the belief question

**Mode: `specified` on the separations, `general` on the structural claims.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for one influence weight, but the six attributes overlap and the three domains have different anchors. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) what is the dependency order, so effort lands on the broken link rather than above it; (2) are the institutional arrangements that use these attributes isomers or merely variants of one thing?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is this a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

The attributes overlap. A name carries race and ethnicity signals; a title carries credentials and status; an institution carries credentials and class. Blinding names leaves titles. Blinding titles leaves accents. The compound is a union. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

**Falsifier:** one manipulation that removes all six channels and their intersections at once, with zero residual at every slot. If that succeeds, this is a partition with a single common cause and Q1 is refuted.

### Q2 - What order does the chain have, and where is the break?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The five links for this question are: (1) perception of the marker, (2) vocabulary for credibility, (3) objective, that is what would count as a claim being believed for ground reasons, (4) planning a route from marker to belief, (5) learn and carry.

Fig 3 :25-26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Link 3 is the break. Both the perception side and the planning side have obvious instruments (audit studies, citation counts). The middle link, "what would count as believing for ground reasons", has none in most settings. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Adding more vocabulary about bias or more vocabulary about credentials lands above the break.

**Falsifier:** exhibit an instrument in current use that states, per slot, what would count as belief for ground reasons and that a checker blind to the markers can read. If such an instrument exists and is used, the break is not at link 3 and Q2 is refuted.

### Q3 - Are the institutional arrangements isomers?

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Consider two arrangements over the same atom set {claimant, marker, audience, ground, institution}:

- **Open review:** the marker and the identity are bonded to the claim by a conjunction (Fig 12 :55-59, + conjunction, "both hold at once", symmetric). The audience reads the claim and the marker together.
- **Blind review:** the identity is removed from what the checker sees. This is not negation of the claim; it is subtraction of an operand, Fig 12 :78-83: "− A with B removed or absent", with the test "is the second operand's absence the point? Yes." The absence of the marker is the design.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. They are isomers under Fig 12 :117. Their behaviour differs decisively: one lets maker-only evidence reach the verdict, the other denies it (Fig 9 :51). Their ingredient lists look almost identical, which is why a policy that says "we changed the review process" can hide the bond change.

A third arrangement, credential gating, is a **sequence** bond (Fig 12 :69-73: "A then B; B needs A to have happened"), because the credential must be checked before the claim is admitted. A fourth, a citation cartel, is a **cycle** (Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."). These four arrangements are not degrees of one dial; they are different terms.

**Falsifier:** show that two of these arrangements have the same bond when the operand sets are matched, so that their behavioural differences come only from a parameter. That would collapse the isomer claim into a variant claim and Q3 is refuted.

### Q4 - Is there a functional group?

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The recurring substructure in D2 is:

> marker -> audience inference about the maker -> belief allocated to the claim

The group fixes the kind of term: a credibility channel. The qualifier says which member: gender, class, race, ethnicity, status or credentials. This is why a pooled "identity effect" is a functional group reading, not a list of six unrelated causes, and why swapping the qualifier changes the member without changing the kind.

Fig 12 :124: "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The group is stable across slots only in this structural sense. Its valence is not stable (Fig 11 :49-51); that is the correction carried from `01` F3.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a slot where the substructure is absent but the marker still changes belief. That would break the functional-group reading and require a different atom.

### Q5 - Do the two currencies add?

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A credential is cheap in bits: a title is one short string. It may be expensive or cheap in actions: checking whether it certifies a method requires a different instrument. A direct demonstration of the claim is expensive in bits and may be one action to refute. The two readings come apart in both directions (Fig 12 :128-129). Summing "credential strength" and "evidence strength" into one score is the error Fig 12 names, not the fix.

This dissolves an apparent conflict in the world sources. The credentialism entry describes a degree used as a screening mechanism, which is a bits-saving device. The Bertrand and Mullainathan result shows callbacks changing with a name, also a bits device. Neither measures the action cost of settling the claim. They are not in conflict with the claim that credentials cannot settle truth; they are measuring a different currency.

**Falsifier:** show a setting where a single number in one unit fully prices both the description cost of a marker and the action cost of checking the claim, with no shortfall in either direction. That would refute the two-currency claim.

### Q6 - Catalyst and spent parts

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A credential that certifies a reusable method survives its own use: it is a catalyst in the audience's closure. A prestige badge spent on one hearing does not regenerate; it is a spent part. The visible marker is the same. Fig 12 :119 adds that "nothing records which a term is", which is exactly the provenance gap Fig 8 :51 names. The practical instruction is to record which kind of credential is being used, before the hearing, not after the outcome.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate that a badge which confers no reusable method nevertheless regenerates its effect across independent hearings with independent grounds, so that the catalyst/spent distinction does not track the behaviour.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound with a broken middle link, not a partition with a ranking. Institutional arrangements over the same six atoms are isomers (blind review as subtraction, open review as conjunction, credential gating as sequence, citation loops as cycle), so the meaningful variable is the bond type, not the amount of identity information. The credibility channel has a functional group with six qualifiers. Two currencies, bits and actions, do not add, which is why a marker that is cheap to read can be worth nothing to settle. Each claim has a stated falsifier written before the conclusion.
