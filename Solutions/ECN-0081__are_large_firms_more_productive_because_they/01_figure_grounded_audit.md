## R. Residual in effect terms

The question asks for a causal attribution of one observed association. State the residual before any figure is chosen:

> **R:** We observe that larger firms display both higher measured productivity and more market power. The question wants one cause for both. In prediction terms: given a model of firm size, the observation that is not predicted is the joint presence of a cost-side advantage and a price-side advantage. What is owed is a term that says which mechanism produced the observation, and an instrument that would discriminate them.

- **Arity:** two conditions, not one. A cost-side condition (subaddible cost, engineering and division-of-labour advantage, bulk purchasing) and a price-side condition (markup, foreclosure, entry deterrence). This is a **union compound** under Fig 9, not a single hard question.
- **Symmetry:** the two are not interchangeable. A real cost advantage can *produce* market power (it is itself an entry barrier), and market power can *masquerade* as productivity when productivity is read off revenue. Swapping them changes the causal story. The relation between them is a threshold comparison or a feedback cycle, not a symmetric join.
- **Scale:** firm level (plant, production function), market level (entry, concentration, price), and policy level (competition authority). A settled firm-level cost advantage becomes a market-level entry barrier, which becomes an industrial structure. Composition is recursive across these levels.
- **Slot indexing:** the residual must be tracked per market and per mechanism, never as one global "productivity" figure. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term, Formula step "Direction"):** a per-market statement of the form "in market m, the cost-side mechanism is present or absent, the price-side mechanism is present or absent, the intersection is present or absent, and the anchor that settles each is named." A single global answer of the form "scale" or "power" fails this specification.

| domain | actors | currency | nameable anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Production / cost** | plant, production function, engineers, inputs | cost per unit, output per unit of input, cost-side TFP | measured long-run average cost curve; a cost-function or subadditivity test (Baumol's test) | one condition of the union |

**Union, not partition.** The two conditions overlap: economies of scale are themselves classified as an entry barrier and as a source of market power in the IO literature. The overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either condition.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).**
1. Perception: observe the size-productivity association.
2. Vocabulary: split "productive" into cost-side and price-side readings. This is the middle link, the one with no obvious instrument, and therefore the one where this question usually breaks.
3. Objective: say what would count as progress, namely a reading that discriminates source.
4. Planning: choose the cost-frontier study, the markup decomposition, or the natural experiment.
5. Learn and carry: record the attribution per market, with the route and the date, so a later reader can locate the error.

Effort spent at step 4 cannot rescue a missing step 2. Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

## 2. Findings

### F1: The question is a union compound, not a partition (decisive on framing)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55-56:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Fig 9 :57-58:

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The "or" in the question presumes a partition. It is not one. A cost advantage can exist without strategic foreclosure, foreclosure can exist without a cost advantage, and the overlap (a scale advantage used as an entry barrier) is a third, specific case. Asking which one explains large firms is asking a single lever to clear a union. Fig 9 :55 says the intersection survives the lever.

The Library of Babel essay names this exact failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Verdict mode:** `specified` on the compound shape. **Triangulated** across Fig 9 (split/union) and Fig 1 :19, :22 (per-slot residual, never aggregate), two figures with different jobs and low overlap. **World corroboration:** the IO sources below treat scale economies and entry barriers as separate but overlapping categories, never as a forced choice. **Refuter:** show that the two mechanisms are one quantity occupying one slot, or that a single lever clears the intersection as well as the two exclusive regions.

### F2: "Productive" is a bundled term and must be split before measurement

Fig 3 :22-23:

> **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."**

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 9 :30-31:

> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

The question's own key word, "productive", is measured in two incompatible ways: cost-side (units of output per unit of input) and price-side (revenue or profit per unit of input). A revenue-based reading goes up when a markup goes up, with no change in engineering efficiency. Arguing option A versus option B before splitting the term is a reading taken below the break.

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Running more regressions on an unsplit measure optimises above the break.

**Verdict mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated** across Fig 3 (dependency order) and Fig 9 :30-31 (describe the effect), low overlap. **World corroboration:** Syverson 2019 connects markups, prices, costs, scale elasticities and profits in one measurement problem; the TFP article calls TFP a residual dependent on estimates of its components. **Refuter:** show a single measure of productivity that does not move when price is separated from cost, and that both mechanisms load on it identically.

### F3: What changes with firm size is the cast, not the rule

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Fig 4 :33-35:

> **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

"Economies of scale" does not license the claim that size changes the production rule. Under the figures the loop and the naming rule are invariant; what changes is the cast of actors, the currency, and the anchor (indivisible plant, spread fixed cost, bulk purchasing terms, division of labour, the relevant demand). Equally, "large" is not a property that carries a fixed valence: the same size is a cost benefactor to one residual and a foreclosure malfactor to another. Fig 11 :49-50: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

**Re-census note (strain named, then resolved).** An earlier reading took Fig 4 to deny economies of scale. That was a strain: Fig 4 governs the loop and the naming rule; the economics of scale governs the cast and the habitat conditions. Different objects, so no contradiction. Kept visible rather than silently corrected.

**Verdict mode:** `specified`, structural. **Triangulated** across Fig 4 (membrane) and Fig 11 (habitat), low overlap. **World corroboration:** the economies of scale article lists physical, managerial, purchasing and learning determinants, all of which are changes in the cast, not a new production law. **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the same production loop at larger size obeys a different composition rule with the same actors, currency and anchor.

### F4: The two mechanisms form a feedback cycle, so one lever cannot clear the intersection

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 9 :55-56 (quoted in F1) says the intersection binds. The IO literature supplies the sign: cost advantage lowers the entrant's viable scale, which raises the incumbent's share, which deepens the cost advantage (a positive loop); market power raises price above cost, which funds foreclosure, which protects the markup. The question's "A or B" is a chain reading of a cycle.

Fig 12 :125-126:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Describing the compound in bits and settling it in actions are different accounting. A cheap story about both mechanisms can still be ruinously expensive to test.

**Verdict mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated** across Fig 12 :121 (cycle) and Fig 9 :55-56 (single lever), low overlap. **World corroboration:** *Natural monopoly* (scale economies are grouped with the reasons for natural monopoly), *Barriers to entry* (economies of scale listed as an antitrust barrier), *Market power* (increasing returns to scale listed among the sources of market power). **Refuter:** demonstrate in a well-specified market that a cost advantage produces no entry deterrence and a markup produces no cost advantage, with both loops measured and flat.

### F5: Measured productivity is a projection that cannot comment on its source

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 1 :27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

A revenue-based productivity number is produced by the same price system whose market power it is supposed to diagnose. Push on the markup and the reading moves, so by Fig 2 :29-30 it is not an anchor for the cost-side question. It is an instrument that can be wrong on an axis it does not measure. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and the instrument *"cannot comment on the source."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the analogous case.

Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* The discriminator must be described in effect terms before a field is chosen.

**Verdict mode:** `specified` that a discriminator is owed; `gap` on which discriminator works in a given market. **Triangulated** across Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 1 :27 (frame-internal quantity), low overlap. **World corroboration:** TFP is explicitly a residual dependent on its component estimates; concentration measures use revenue and carry no cost or profit information; Syverson 2019 documents the incongruence between macro markup evidence and micro views. **Refuter:** show revenue TFP and a cost-frontier efficiency measure moving together under an instrument that shifts markups only.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Compound refuter.** A single lever is demonstrated to clear the scale-only region, the power-only region, and their intersection, so Fig 9 :55-56 is wrong about the binding intersection.
2. **Slot refuter.** The cost-side and price-side mechanisms are shown to be one quantity with one residual slot, so the arity claim of F1 fails.
3. **Cost-side refuter.** A credible natural experiment expands output under unchanged technology and input prices with no decline in average cost, and the size-productivity association vanishes once markups are removed.
4. **Power-side refuter.** In a market with free entry, no foreclosure conduct and no measurable markup, a persistent large-firm cost advantage remains, so the price-side mechanism is not part of the compound there. If this generalises, F4's cycle is broken.
5. **Measurement refuter.** Revenue TFP and a cost-frontier efficiency measure move together under an instrument that shifts markups only, so F5's projection claim fails.
6. **Vocabulary refuter.** A single measure of "productive" is found whose price and cost components cannot be separated even in principle, so F2's dependency break is not repairable and the chain cannot be continued.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` must be reissued. Until a ground reading exists, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.
