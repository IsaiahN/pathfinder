## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can settle it. It constrains; it does not fill. For ECN-0081:

- **Not a shape:** "scale", or "market power", or a percentage attribution such as "70 percent scale, 30 percent power".
- **A shape:** "a union compound of two conditions with a binding intersection; the productivity term split into cost-side and price-side readings; each side ranged against its own anchor; a source-discriminating instrument owed at the measurement step; verdicts kept per market and never averaged."

The Library of Babel essay names the defect in the original question directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That line is the single most on-point world sentence for this question. It is corroboration only; the figure authority for the split is Fig 9 :54-58.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, then look for new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the closest held vocabulary is "cost advantage" and "markup"; the new terms that narrow the space are "subaddibility", "entry barrier", "revenue TFP" and "source discriminator".
- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Describe the missing thing as "an instrument that moves when markup changes but not when cost changes", not as a named technique borrowed from one school.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* is the canonical source for the rule that when contact is not possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you would expect to see. I did not re-open it this session, so I state the rule but do not quote it here; the figure equivalent is Fig 9 :30-31.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* The stop here is the shape above, not an empirical verdict.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The figures README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For ECN-0081, a name such as "scale economies dominate in utilities" is licensed only by a cost-function study or a natural experiment in that market.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A claim imported from a different discipline must say where it came from and which gap it explains.

Important narrowing, kept visible: the shape-not-a-name rule governs a **missing primitive**, not every act of naming. In this question what is missing is an **instrument**, not a term already in the library. Naming a mechanism in a specific market after a ground reading is allowed. Naming one as the universal answer before that reading is the error. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; the honest report is `gap`, not a guess.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."* Several underlying causes produce identical readings, and *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. The color case was resolved *"not by argument"* but by the arrival of a better instrument, the spectrometer. The essay's own summary: *"What the Spectrometer Example Reveals: The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Apply that to ECN-0081 without straining it:

- A revenue-based productivity reading is a projection. A real cost advantage and a markup both raise it. The reading cannot say which source produced it.
- Concentration ratios are the analogous green reading in IO. The *Market power* article notes they require only revenue data and *"includes no information about costs or profits."*
- What is owed is the discriminator, not a better argument about which option sounds more plausible. Fig 2 :29-30 defines the requirement: if the reading moves when you push on the markup, it is not an anchor for the cost-side question.

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* The price system that yields the markup also yields the revenue productivity number. One frame, two labels, one witness.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What shape must any answer to ECN-0081 have?** It must be:

1. **A compound, not a choice.** Two conditions plus an intersection. Fig 9 :54-58.
2. **Split before measurement.** Cost-side and price-side readings tracked in separate slots. Fig 1 :19, Fig 3 :22-23.
3. **Relational, not a property of size.** The same size has different valence for different residuals. Fig 11 :49-50.
4. **Cyclic, not a chain.** Cost advantage and entry deterrence re-enter each other. Fig 12 :121.
5. **Anchored separately.** Cost data for the cost side, prices and entry for the power side, a discriminator for the measurement side. Fig 2 :29-30.
6. **Per-market and never averaged.** A global "large firms are productive because X" repeats the aggregation error of Fig 1 :22.
7. **Honest about the stop.** The figures supply items 1 to 6. They do not supply the empirical weight. That is `gap`, and the stop is recorded with the lineage.

**Does the framework overstep if it says this?** No. All seven items are constraints on the form of an answer, derived from figure lines. It oversteps only if it asserts which mechanism dominates in a named industry without a ground reading.

## 6. Consequences for contact

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A list of well-known IO economists assembled to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is for an instrument and a refutation.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01`, `06`, `07` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the economics claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on.
