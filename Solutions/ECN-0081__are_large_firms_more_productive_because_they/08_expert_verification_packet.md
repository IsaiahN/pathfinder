# Expert verification packet: the Tether on scale economies versus market power

**What this is:** a claim in a form an industrial organisation specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `gap` on empirical dominance. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This packet cannot settle the question it reports on; it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Large firms showing both high measured productivity and market power is not evidence that one of those causes the other to the exclusion of the second. It is a union compound: a cost-side condition (subaddible cost, spread fixed cost, bulk purchasing, division of labour), a price-side condition (markup, foreclosure, limited entry), and an intersection where a cost advantage itself operates as an entry barrier. The word "productive" must be split into a cost-side and a price-side reading before measurement, because a revenue-based reading cannot comment on which source produced it. A settled cost advantage can re-enter as a source of power, so the structure is a cycle with a sign, not a chain, and a single lever leaves the intersection standing. Which mechanism dominates in a named market is `gap` here; a source-discriminating instrument is owed and is named in effect terms below.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: The framing: union compound, not a partition (checkable by reading Figure 9)

- **Statement:** the question's "or" is invalid. There are two conditions plus a binding intersection, and one lever cannot clear all three.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Also Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** in a market you know, can you name a case where a cost advantage exists without foreclosure, a case where foreclosure exists without a cost advantage, and a case where the cost advantage is itself the barrier? If the third case cannot be named, the intersection claim fails.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that a single lever clears the scale-only case, the power-only case and the intersection, or that the two mechanisms are one quantity occupying one residual slot.

### C2: The vocabulary: "productive" must be split before measurement (checkable against measurement practice)

- **Statement:** a cost-side reading and a price-side reading of productivity move differently when markup changes, and a revenue-based reading cannot discriminate them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- **What to check:** is there a published case where revenue productivity rose with markup while cost-frontier efficiency was flat, or the reverse? Syverson 2019 (JEP) and the TFP measurement article are the entry points. A corroborating world source is *Market power*: concentration ratios require only revenue data and carry no cost or profit information.
- **Refuter:** show that revenue productivity and cost-frontier efficiency move together under an instrument that shifts markups only, or that the two components cannot be separated even in principle.

### C3: The structure: a cycle, not a chain, with a named owed instrument (checkable by reading Figure 12 and by inspection of a market)

- **Statement:** cost advantage and entry deterrence re-enter each other; the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. Over the shared atom set they are isomers; as the question states them they are a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- **What to check:** does the market show a loop (cost advantage lowers the viable entrant scale, which deepens the cost advantage) or a one-way chain? Is the description cheap while the test is expensive, as Fig 12 :125 predicts?
- **Refuter:** measure both loops in one market and show one is flat while the other closes, so a single lever works there; or show the relationship passes none of the seven bond tests and is not a cycle, which would remove the compound's internal structure.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that economies of scale are unimportant. They are a real condition in the cost domain.
- Not that limiting competition raises real productive efficiency. The X-inefficiency literature says lack of competition can let productive inefficiency persist. The price-side mechanism explains a markup, not an engineering gain.
- Not that one mechanism wins in all industries. No global attribution is offered.
- Not a competition-policy or welfare ruling. The figures do not supply welfare weights.
- Not `grounded`. No cost function, price series, dataset, natural experiment or market observation was used in this derivation.
- Not that the figures name the dominant mechanism. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 5. What would settle it

1. A cost-frontier or subaddibility test that isolates the cost-side mechanism after markups are removed.
2. A price and entry study that isolates the price-side mechanism without using revenue concentration as its own evidence.
3. A source-discriminating instrument: a reading that moves when markup changes but not when engineering cost changes, or the reverse. In effect terms, described before a field or method is chosen (Fig 6 :30: *"Cast it in effect terms."*).
4. Any of the five standing falsifiers in `01` §3 and `07` §4.
5. Agreement is not on this list. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-58 and Fig 1 :19, :22 and :27, three short passages. Decide whether the union reading or the forced "or" better fits a market you know.
2. Check C2 against your own measurement practice: does the productivity number you use change when price is separated from cost?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a cost advantage from a markup in that market, or say why none is possible.
4. Try the intersection case: can a scale advantage be an entry barrier in your market? If yes, the single-lever objection is live.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
