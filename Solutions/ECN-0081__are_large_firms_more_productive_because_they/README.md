**Question:** Are large firms more productive because they exploit economies of scale, or do they become powerful by limiting competition?

The adjacent questions 82 to 85 are about rents versus innovation, mergers versus competition, natural monopoly, and what competition policy protects, so the field for the public contact points in `03_public_contact_points.csv` is industrial organization research.

**Mode:** `specified` on the structural verdict, `gap` on the empirical dominance question. Nothing here is `grounded`: the figures return a shape, not an empirical weight, and no measurement was taken in this derivation.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending. The registry is maintained outside this folder and was not touched.

## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not a definition but a causal attribution. We observe a statistical association between firm size and two different readings: (A) a cost-side advantage (lower unit cost, higher cost-side total factor productivity), and (B) a price-side advantage (a markup, restricted output, limited entry). Option A says the cost-side advantage causes the size and the power. Option B says the power causes the appearance of advantage. What each leads to:

- If A alone were right, breaking up a large firm would destroy a real cost advantage measured in units of input per unit of output, and consumer prices would rise because costs rise.
- If B alone were right, breaking up the firm would remove a markup without raising real cost, and measured productivity would fall only because the price-side reading was removed.

An answer would look like a per-market attribution: which mechanism is present, which anchor settles it, and what a refuter would see. This package binds that shape as an ordinary term and lets it accrue a residual, per `THE_FORMULA.md` step "Direction".

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures settle which mechanism dominates in a real market. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; the figures narrow the shape and name the owed instrument, they do not supply the measurement.

Three figure-grounded reasons for the omissions:

1. **An anchor is not a vote.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Agreement selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make it worthless (Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention"*).
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Sending the framework before asking for a verdict converts a checker into a participant.
3. **Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* The aim is to give the shape claim a budget to be tried, not to gather endorsements.

## Status of the whole derivation

Every substantive file states that this is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading**. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* This package cannot settle the economics question it reports on. It can say what shape any answer must have, what is owed, and what would reverse it.
