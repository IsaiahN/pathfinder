# The Tether's answer: scale economies or limiting competition?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `gap` on which mechanism dominates in a given market. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We observe that larger firms show both higher measured productivity and more market power. The question asks which one causes the other, as if they were mutually exclusive. Under Fig 9 the observation is a **union compound with a binding intersection**, not one hard question:

> Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

The residual must also be indexed per slot, not averaged into one global productivity number. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 2. The shape

1. **The "or" is refused.** Two conditions overlap: a cost-side advantage and a price-side advantage. Their intersection, a scale advantage used as an entry barrier, is its own case. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

2. **"Productive" is split before it is measured.** Cost-side (output per unit of input) and price-side (revenue per unit of input) readings move differently when a markup changes. Measuring the bundled term is a reading below the break. Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

3. **Size changes the cast, not the rule.** Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Large-firm advantage is a property of firm-and-habitat, not of the firm alone, and its valence is relative to the residual. Fig 11 :49-50: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

4. **The mechanisms are coupled in a cycle.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A cost advantage lowers the viable scale of an entrant and can thereby create power; a markup funds foreclosure that protects the cost position. Over the shared atom set they are isomers (Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."); as the question states them they are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.").

5. **Measured productivity is a projection, not an anchor for the cost side.** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Push on the markup and a revenue productivity reading moves, so it is not an anchor for the engineering-efficiency question.

6. **The verdict is per domain and never averaged.** Where cost is subaddible, a real cost-side mechanism exists. Every market with restricted entry has a price-side mechanism. Which dominates in a named market is `gap`: no measurement was brought. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that economies of scale are unimportant. They are a real condition in D1, and the framework does not deny them.
- Not that limiting competition raises real productive efficiency. The X-inefficiency source says the opposite: lack of competition can let productive inefficiency persist. The price-side mechanism explains a markup, not an engineering gain.
- Not that one mechanism wins universally. No global percentage attribution is offered.
- Not a welfare ruling. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Competition-policy standards are not derivable from these figures.
- Not `grounded`. No cost function, price series, natural experiment or market observation was used.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. Here:

- **Cost-side test:** does output expand under unchanged technology and input prices with a decline in average cost, measured by a cost frontier or subadditivity test, after markups are removed?
- **Power-side test:** does price exceed marginal cost, and does entry respond to that gap, measured by prices, entry rates and merger retrospectives rather than by revenue concentration alone?
- **Discriminator:** does a measure exist that moves when markup changes but not when engineering cost changes, or the reverse? That instrument would name the source.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a single lever clearing all three regions; one residual slot instead of two; a flat cost curve with the association vanishing after markup removal; a free-entry market with no foreclosure and no markup that still shows a persistent large-firm cost advantage; co-movement of revenue TFP and cost-frontier efficiency under a markup-only instrument.

Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, kept distinct.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable-in-principle.** The framework contains no market, no cost function and no price series. It can describe the instrument that is owed and the shape the answer must have; it cannot supply the empirical weight. Figure 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The record stops here with the lineage intact. The three essays re-opened this session share the figure author, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose between economies of scale and limiting competition, because the question's "or" is a bundle, not a choice. It says the two conditions overlap: a real cost advantage can itself be an entry barrier, and a markup can make a firm look productive when the productivity number is read off revenue. So "productive" has to be split into a cost-side and a price-side reading before anything is measured, the two mechanisms have to be tracked per market and never averaged, and their causal order is a feedback cycle rather than a chain, which is why one lever leaves the overlap standing. A revenue-based productivity reading cannot say which source produced it, so a source-discriminating instrument is owed. The figures supply that shape and five refuters written in advance; they do not say which mechanism dominates in any industry. That part stays open until the ground settles it.
