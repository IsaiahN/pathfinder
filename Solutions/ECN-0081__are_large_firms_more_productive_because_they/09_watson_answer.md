## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that large firms are more productive because they exploit economies of scale, or become powerful by limiting competition. Option A: the cost-side mechanism is the answer. Option B: the price-side mechanism is the answer. Option C, derived here: the observation is a union compound with a binding intersection; the mechanisms must be split and ranged separately against separate anchors, and the empirical dominance question is a `gap` until a source-discriminating instrument is brought. What each leads to: A destroys a real cost advantage and raises cost; B removes a markup but is contradicted by the X-inefficiency evidence on real efficiency; C sends a checkable shape, five written refuters and an honest stop.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The joint presence of high measured productivity and high market power in large firms. Arity: two conditions plus an intersection, so a **union compound** (Fig 9). Symmetry: not interchangeable. A cost advantage can produce power; power can masquerade as productivity in a revenue reading. The comparison is ordered (`⋛` is a threshold relation), not a symmetric join. Scale: firm, market, policy levels; a settled cost advantage becomes a market-level entry barrier, which becomes an industrial structure.
- **D1, production / cost.** Actors: plant, production function, inputs, engineers. Currency: unit cost, output per unit of input, cost-side TFP. Anchor: a measured long-run average cost curve or a cost-function / subaddibility test. One condition of the union.
- **D2, strategic / market power.** Actors: incumbent, entrants, consumers, competition authority. Currency: price-cost margin, market share, entry and exit counts. Anchor: observed prices, entry rates, merger retrospectives, antitrust findings. The other condition of the union.
- **D3, measurement / identification.** Actors: researcher, estimator, instrument. Currency: the measured productivity residual. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument or identifying assumption, not yet supplied. The frame in which D1 and D2 are confounded.
- **D4, policy / welfare.** Actors: authority, courts, legislators. Currency: consumer welfare, price, choice, innovation. Anchor: a statutory standard and the evidence admitted under it. Not decidable by these figures; recorded as `gap`.
- **Partition or union:** union. Scale economies are themselves classified as an entry barrier and as a source of market power in the IO sources, so the overlap is a compound with its own loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perceive the association; split "productive" into cost-side and price-side (the middle link with no instrument); name progress as a source-discriminating reading; choose the design; record per-market attributions with lineage. Effort on design cannot rescue a missing vocabulary link.

Strain test: the apparent conflict between Fig 4 (kernel invariant with size) and economies of scale was re-censused. Fig 4 governs the loop and the naming rule; scale economics governs the cast, the currency and the anchor. Different objects, so no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The forced "or" fails. The observation is a union compound: a cost-side condition, a price-side condition, and an intersection where scale itself operates as an entry barrier. "Productive" must be split into cost-side and price-side readings before measurement; the two mechanisms form a cycle with a sign rather than a chain, so one lever leaves the intersection standing; and a revenue-based productivity reading is a projection that cannot comment on its source. Over the shared atom set the mechanisms are isomers; as stated they are a functional group. Which mechanism dominates in any named market is `gap`: no cost function, price series or discriminator was brought. D4 is `gap` outright. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result with five pre-registered refuters, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** a wrong attribution could justify breaking up firms whose cost advantage is real (raising cost and prices), or could let a genuine markup persist behind a scale-efficiency defence. Both errors are public-policy errors, which is why independence is worth buying here. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost of the *shape* being wrong is smaller and locatable: the compound claim states its own refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape claim adds no primitive and claims no import, so nothing has to be withdrawn to correct it. A wrong dominance claim would be replaced by a market-specific attribution after a cost study and a price study. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one with the derivation intact. The only thing not undoable is a spent independent reviewer who was shown the framework before recording a verdict, which is why stage 1 comes first.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that moves under markup changes but not engineering cost, or the reverse, would name the mechanism; a cost-function study showing flat average cost after markup removal would break the cost-side half; a free-entry market with no foreclosure and no markup that still shows a persistent large-firm cost advantage would break the power-side half; a demonstration that one lever clears all three regions would break Fig 9 :55-56; a demonstration that the two mechanisms occupy one residual slot would break the arity claim. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
