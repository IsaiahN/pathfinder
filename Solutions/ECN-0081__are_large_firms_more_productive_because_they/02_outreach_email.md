# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the stage-1 material below. The verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any framework link is shown. The claim is `specified` on the shape and `gap` on which mechanism dominates, so do not present it as a settled economic result.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about firm size and competition before you decide whether it is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** we observe that larger firms show both higher measured productivity and more market power. The usual question asks which one causes the other, as if they were alternatives. The claim is that they are not alternatives. They are a union of two overlapping conditions, and the overlap (a cost advantage used as an entry barrier) is its own case. A single lever that removes market power does not remove a real cost subaddibility, and a cost advantage can itself create an entry barrier.
>
> A second part of the claim is that the word "productive" has to be split before it is measured. A cost-side reading (output per unit of input) and a price-side reading (revenue per unit of input) move differently when a markup changes. A revenue-based reading cannot comment on which source produced it.
>
> **What is explicitly not claimed:** that scale economies are unimportant, that limiting competition improves real efficiency, or that one mechanism wins in every industry. Which mechanism dominates in a given market is a `gap` here. The figures supply the shape an answer must have, not the empirical weight. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before this message was sent:**
> 1. A single lever is shown to clear the scale-only case, the power-only case, and the overlap, so the overlap does not bind.
> 2. The cost-side and price-side effects are shown to be one quantity with one residual slot, not two conditions.
> 3. Output expands under unchanged technology and input prices with no decline in average cost, and the size-productivity association disappears once markups are removed.
> 4. In a market with free entry, no foreclosure and no markup, a persistent large-firm cost advantage remains.
> 5. Revenue productivity and cost-frontier efficiency move together under an instrument that changes markups only.
>
> These were written in advance. If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part of the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** what instrument in your field would discriminate a real cost advantage from a markup in the same market? That is the thing this claim is owed and does not yet have. Please answer that before looking at any framework material.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - The public figure text extract and formula (local framework files, paths supplied on request)
> - The public figure repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The worked derivation of this question: the attached `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [name]
