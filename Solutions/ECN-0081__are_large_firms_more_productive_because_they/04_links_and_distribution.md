## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Scale and power are not disjoint categories.** The *Natural monopoly* article gives Baumol's subadditivity test and notes that scale economies are sufficient but not necessary for it, and in the multiproduct case neither necessary nor sufficient. The *Market power* article lists "increasing returns to scale" among the sources of market power. The *Barriers to entry* article lists economies of scale as a barrier and classifies it as a "contentious example" and an antitrust barrier. Together these are one witness with three faces, not three independent corroborations: all are tertiary, share the same platform, and describe the same established IO taxonomy. They support the union reading of F1 and the cycle of F4, but they do not supply an empirical dominance result.
2. **Limiting competition tends to reduce real productivity.** The *X-inefficiency* article says competition pushes firms toward lowest unit costs and that "with market forms other than perfect competition, such as monopoly, productive inefficiency can persist." This **conflicts with option B as worded** (that large firms are more productive *because* they limit competition), while corroborating the existence of the price-side mechanism. Under the figures a conflict sends us back to the census, never flips a verdict: the re-census in `01` §5 keeps the power mechanism in the compound and refuses the claim that it raises real productive efficiency.
3. **The measurement is the hard part.** Syverson 2019 names the joint estimation of markups, prices, costs, scale elasticities and profits, and reports conflicting evidence between macro and micro views. The *TFP* article calls TFP a residual dependent on its components. These corroborate F5: the reading cannot comment on its own source without a discriminator.
4. **The Cournot dilemma is the same shape as the union compound.** The *Economies of scale* article records that scale economies do not by themselves determine firm size or market structure, because capabilities, transaction costs, product differentiation and demand heterogeneity counterbalance them. This is a second witness from a different concept cluster, but still within the same tertiary pool.

**Overlap warning.** Six of the seven web sources above are Wikipedia articles. They are one evidence pool wearing six names. Fig 2 :31-32: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* The three framework essays share an author and carry high overlap with the figures. Syverson 2019 is the only source from a distinct venue and author, so it is the only genuinely low-overlap world witness here, and it is corroboration only. The figure verdict stands on the figures.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full derivation before anyone has characterised the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what instrument would discriminate cost from markup before giving them a literature list.
- **Seat Map :163**: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not a check.
- **Fig 2 :42**: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost of being wrong here is a wrong competition policy conclusion, so two low-overlap frames are worth more than ten agreeable ones.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual, the compound shape and the five refuters; (2) ask what instrument they would count as discriminating cost from markup, and record the answer; (3) only then the public links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only on request, the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure and what instrument would settle it. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure text, the formula and the derivation. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3, on request:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter first, per the "Direction" section of `THE_FORMULA.md`.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. As written it does survive as a *shape*, but it explicitly reports `gap` on dominance. Do not send it as though it settled which mechanism makes large firms productive. That would breach its own mode and would be the output-layer defect the exemplar records against its own draft.
