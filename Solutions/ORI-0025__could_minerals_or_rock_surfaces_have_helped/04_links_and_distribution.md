## 4. Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four slots and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if surface templating were real before giving them the Erastova or Ferris paper.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Do not pool later agreement as corroboration, especially with readers drawn from the Ferris/Bernal lineage.
- **Fig 2 :42** — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One refuter from asymmetric catalysis or geochemistry is worth more than ten agreements from the same mineral-catalysis pool.
- **Fig 11 :61** — *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Adding a reader to the conversation is cheap. Removing a conflicting condition from the claim is not available.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four-slot shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the figure text and essay links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* — and Fig 2 :45 — *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
