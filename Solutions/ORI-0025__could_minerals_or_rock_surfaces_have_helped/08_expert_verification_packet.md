# Expert verification packet — the Tether on mineral-assisted prebiotic assembly

**What this is:** a claim in a form a mineral catalysis researcher, prebiotic chemist or geochemist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural constraints, not `grounded`.** No measurement of the Hadean Earth is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a historical result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "could minerals or rock surfaces have helped assemble and organize prebiotic molecules?" is a union of at least four overlapping conditions: concentrating dilute monomers, aligning them into an order, forming bonds with a part that survives its own use, and protecting or releasing the product. The figures license that shape and refuse the name. Capability belongs to the whole habitat, so a mineral is one actor beside wet-dry cycles, UV, feedstock and gradients. "Organize" is a bond claim: products differing only in monomer order are different substances. Every cited laboratory result is a substituted habitat that proves a mechanism is wired, not that the early Earth ran it. What is offered is the shape, four refuters written in advance, and the discriminating instrument that would close the shape. No named mineral is asserted as the answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12

- **Statement:** a catalyst is a role (a part that survives its own use), and arrangement carries the information. A mineral surface can hold these roles, but the figures do not name a material.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119 — *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :94 — *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **Refuter:** show a figure re-census in which a consumed part holds the catalyst role, or in which the framework derives a specific mineral name from its primitives. Either breaks C1.

### C2 — chemistry-facing; checkable against the product data

- **Statement:** reported product distributions are arrangement results, not just yield results. Ferris 1996 reports oligomers up to 55 monomers on mineral surfaces versus about 10 in solution. Rimoldi 2025 reports AGG:GAG:GGA = 8:47:45 on colemanite. Li 2024 reports 14.5 to 42.4% D-amino-acid excess on natural pyrite. Under Fig 12 :117 these are isomers or enantiomers, not a single product with a bigger yield.
- **What to check:** is each characterization faithful? Are the sequences correctly assigned (co-elution, derivatization bias)? Are the enantiomeric excesses robust to vortex and stirrer-field effects (Li 2024 ran those controls)? Does the mineral change the *distribution* or only the *amount*?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 — *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123 — *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **Refuter:** a surface that changes total yield while leaving sequence and enantiomer distributions unchanged breaks the "organize" half of C2. A misassigned peak or an uncontrolled chiral bias breaks the specific citation.

### C3 — the actor-holding question; checkable by experiment, not by argument

- **Statement:** the catalytic and concentrating roles may be held by ions released from a mineral rather than by the solid face. Rimoldi 2025 states this against their own positive result, saying the finding *"puts in the background the structure of the mineral surface, even though the latter could still play a role in concentrating aa monomers from the aqueous environment."* Erastova 2017 measured concentration, alignment and templating on layered double hydroxide surfaces by simulation. These are compatible different actors holding different slots, not a contradiction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :70 — *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :61 — *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
- **Refuter:** a four-arm experiment (intact surface, mineral-derived ions in solution, no mineral, wet-dry cycle without mineral) at matched feedstock and conditions that shows no difference in length, sequence or ee breaks the claim that any mineral-derived actor is doing work in that setting. A field trace tying the product to a specific assemblage would move C3 toward `grounded`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything and no final room; the residual remains a compound with its own slots.
- Not that montmorillonite, pyrite, colemanite or a layered double hydroxide is *the* answer. These are candidate occupants with provenance, and the framework returns a kind of thing, never a name.
- Not that minerals were necessary; a mineral-free pathway closing the four slots is a live alternative, and its absence from the literature is not proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that a flask result proves a Hadean setting (Fig 11 :70).
- Not that the solid surface rather than dissolved ions did the catalysis in a particular experiment.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement of the early Earth.
- Not a claim that the six-term substrate derives mineral behaviour. The bindings of spectrum, gradient and network onto mineral ordering, dehydration and scaffolding are hypotheses from the mineralogy frame, adding no atom.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"* — the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Run the four-arm experiment above and report length, sequence and ee per arm. That names the actor holding each role.
2. Test escape: can the oligomer leave the surface and serve as a substrate at the next level under plausible conditions? Lambert's question in Erastova 2017 is the standing version.
3. Find a geological trace tying a product distribution to a specific mineral assemblage in a setting whose conditions are independently established.

A failure in any of these is more useful than an endorsement. If a reported alignment or ee turns out to be a vortex, stirrer-field or derivatization artefact, say so and I will retract that citation.
