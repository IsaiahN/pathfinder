# Outreach draft — check the output before you decide about the story

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 claim below survives. If the four-slot shape or its refuter is wrong, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output on prebiotic mineral surfaces before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "could minerals or rock surfaces have helped assemble and organize prebiotic molecules?" is not one question. It is at least four conditions that overlap on a surface: (1) concentrating dilute monomers, (2) aligning them into an order, (3) forming bonds with a part that survives its own use, and (4) protecting or releasing the product. A surface can hold any subset of those roles, and mineral-derived ions in solution can hold some of them. The claim I am offering is the **shape** with those four slots and the requirement that an instrument, not an argument, decide which actor holds each slot. It does not name a mineral. It does not claim this is what happened on the early Earth.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that montmorillonite, pyrite, colemanite or a layered double hydroxide is the answer; that the solid surface rather than a dissolved ion did the catalysis in any particular experiment; that a flask result proves a Hadean setting; or that minerals were necessary. Those are bindings from the experimental frame, and until provenance is shown they carry no evidentiary weight here. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a ground-visible experiment that concentrates, aligns and polymerizes the same monomers to the same lengths and sequence distribution with no mineral contact at any stage, in a setting whose conditions are independently established;
> 2. a measurement showing mineral-bound products cannot leave the surface under plausible early-Earth conditions, so no part survives its own use and no product composes further;
> 3. a re-census of the framework showing that arrangement is not the substance, or that a catalyst is not a part that survives its own use;
> 4. a source-discriminating instrument showing that a reported alignment or enantiomeric excess is an artefact of the setup rather than surface structure.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local extract; cite as `Fig N :line`)
> - Canonical figures README: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight essays by Isaiah N, 2026: `https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-library-of-babel-locating-the` and the linked set in `05_what_shape_means.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get (`general`, `specified`, `grounded`). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
