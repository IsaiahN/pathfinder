# Roles, order and isomers in mineral-assisted prebiotic assembly

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The word "assemble and organize" in ORI-0025 hides three different claims: a surface may **concentrate** monomers, **align** them, or **catalyze** bond formation, and these may be held by different actors (solid surface, dissolved ion from that mineral, wet-dry cycle). A second question follows: are the products related as **isomers** or only as members of a **functional group**? These are structure and order questions, and each needs its own falsifier.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are concentrator, template and catalyst three roles or one?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- Fig 11 :49-50, world frame: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries" (Fig 11 :49, apostrophe normalized). A mineral is a benefactor to the concentration residual and possibly inert to the catalysis residual.
- A **concentrator** need not survive its use: adsorption can fill sites and stop.
- An **aligner/template** must hold orientation long enough for a bond, but may not change the chemistry (Erastova 2017 measured templated C-termini alignment and six-fold diffusion on layered double hydroxides).
- A **catalyst** must survive its own use (Fig 12 :119). In Ferris 1996 the surface is reused across successive feedings. In Rimoldi 2025 the catalytic role is held largely by borate ions released into solution, not by the crystal face.
- ⇒ Three roles, three checks, no averaging. A single "minerals help" verdict would aggregate slots and lose the signal (Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** one instrument and one variable that close all three roles simultaneously, with no residual left on any slot, would collapse the distinction. If none is reported, the roles stay separate.

### Q2 — What does "order" mean here?

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- The Operators table :29-33 defines → as sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*.
- In prebiotic assembly, a chain is a sequence: monomer n must be added before monomer n+1 can be judged. A surface that aligns C- and N-termini (Erastova 2017) is a candidate sequence-generator, not merely a state.
- Fig 12 :98: **"Same atoms, different bond, different term."** The Gly-Ala isomers AGG, GAG and GGA reported by Rimoldi 2025 are exactly this: same atoms, different order, different substance. The 8:47:45 ratio is a measure of which sequence the mineral environment favours.
- **Falsifier:** a surface that changes only the total yield and leaves the sequence distribution unchanged does not generate order. That is a cheap HPLC-MS check.

### Q3 — Are the products isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The three Gly-Ala sequences AGG, GAG and GGA share the same atoms, so under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: different bond order, different substance, invisible in a flat formula. Rimoldi 2025 reports they are present in different proportions on colemanite, so the surface environment discriminates among isomers.
- The longer Gly-rich products (G7AG, G7GA) found by Rimoldi 2025 recur in proteins of *Cyanobacteria* and *Thermoproteota*. Under Fig 12 :123 they are members of a **functional group**: the Gly-rich substructure fixes the kind (flexible loop), and the qualifier (AG versus GA) says which member.
- Ferris 1996's 55-mers and Erastova 2017's modeled 24-mers are longer members of the same kind (oligomer of amino acids or nucleotides) with different atoms in the slots. Across different feedstocks they are a functional group, not isomers. Within one feedstock and different order, they are isomers.
- **Falsifier:** if analysis shows the reported sequences are not actually distinct (co-elution, derivatization artefact, misassigned peaks), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim may still stand. Li 2024's controls on vortex and magnetic stirrer field are the right model for this kind of control.

### Q4 — What is the order of the union of conditions?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Dependency order for ORI-0025: feedstock supply → concentration (adsorption) → alignment (template) → bond formation (catalyst) → sequence selection → release and further composition → settlement by instrument. Each step consumes the previous one's product.
- A long oligomer that is settled as a product can become an atom for the next level (a protocell compartment, a ribozyme-like function). Fig 12 :104 licenses this recursion without a new primitive. Ferris 1996's repeated feedings are a laboratory instance of a settled product serving as a substrate for the next round.
- **Falsifier:** a measurement showing the product cannot be released from the surface and therefore cannot serve as an atom at the next level would break the recursion. Lambert's question, quoted by Erastova 2017, is exactly this: *"If the initial steps of life really occurred on surfaces, how then did life escape surfaces at a later stage?"* It is recorded here as a live question, not smoothed over.

### Q5 — Analogues, and why they are not evidence

Three lower-overlap analogues were considered for the residual "conditions that concentrate and organize dilute monomers":

| analogue | what it does for the residual | why it is not evidence for minerals |
|---|---|---|
| Wet-dry cycles in hydrothermal ponds (Pérez-Fernández 2022; Song et al. 2024 as a lead) | concentrate monomers by evaporation and drive condensation | an alternative actor for the concentration slot; its success would weaken the necessity of mineral contact |
| Ice eutectics and meteorite delivery (listed in Rimoldi 2025's introduction) | concentrate or supply feedstock | a supply-side actor, not an organizing surface |
| Alkaline hydrothermal vents and iron-sulfide chimneys (Erastova 2017 discussion; NASA Q and A) | provide gradients, microcompartments and mineral surfaces together | a habitat in which minerals are one condition among several, exactly Fig 11 :61's asymmetry |

- Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* These analogues are recorded as imported frames, not as corroboration drawn from the same pool.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The residual was described before these analogues were opened.
- **Falsifier:** an analogue that closes all four slots of the residual without any mineral actor, in a ground-visible experiment, removes the mineral necessity claim. It does not remove the possibility claim; both are recorded.

## CONCLUSION

"Assemble and organize" resolves into three distinct roles (concentrator, aligner, catalyst) and one type question (isomer versus functional group). The figures license the roles and the distinction; the world sources show that different actors can hold them, including dissolved ions released from a mineral. Products that differ only in order are isomers under Fig 12 :117; products that share a substructure across different feedstocks are a functional group under Fig 12 :123. The union is ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20), and escape from the surface is a live, unclosed question. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no naming of a seventh term is needed for any of this.
