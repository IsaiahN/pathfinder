## 0. The residual, in effect terms

In bulk aqueous solution, condensation of prebiotic monomers competes with hydrolysis. Products stay short, dilute, unoriented and dominated by unwanted variants. A genetic or catalytic system needs the opposite: concentration, alignment, lengths in the tens of monomers, reproducible sequence, and protection from UV and dilution. The residual **R** is the gap between those two states.

- **Option A (minerals helped):** contact with a mineral surface closes R by concentrating monomers from dilute solution, aligning them, lowering the activation cost of bond formation, and sometimes selecting one enantiomer or sequence.
- **Option B (they did not):** the same four jobs are done by other habitat conditions, and the mineral is a spectator or a late complication.

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55-58: *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing."* Concentration, alignment, catalysis, protection and chirality are separate conditions that overlap on a surface. A demonstration that a surface catalyzes bonds does not close the concentration condition, and a demonstration that wet-dry cycles concentrate does not close the alignment condition. Each gets its own loop and its own ground.

**What an answer would look like, bound before the run:** a shape with four named slots and an instrument. (1) a condition that concentrates monomers from dilute solution; (2) a structure that aligns them; (3) a part that survives its own use as a catalyst; (4) an instrument that discriminates which actor did the work. The sought-for shape is made an ordinary term here so it can accrue a residual and be refused like anything else (THE_FORMULA.md, "Direction").

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |

| **D1 Surface / mineral catalysis** | montmorillonite, layered double hydroxides, pyrite, colemanite, quartz, silica; adsorbed monomers; metal ions | adsorption energy, surface free energy, catalytic rate, product distribution | laboratory measurement: adsorption isotherms, HPLC-MS, yields, TPD, DFT | partition boundary: which compositions happen *in contact with* a mineral versus in solution |

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Supply of monomers (D3) → concentration and alignment (D1/D2) → bond formation and sequence (D1) → escape from the surface and inheritance (D2) → settlement by an instrument (D4). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A claim about catalysis cannot rescue a missing feedstock, and a claim about feedstock cannot rescue a missing discriminator. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.

**Arity / symmetry / scale of R.** Arity: four conditions (concentration, alignment, catalysis, protection), plus a fifth (chirality) that appears only on some minerals. Symmetry: a surface is asymmetric, it breaks rotational freedom and can break mirror symmetry; that is precisely why it can impose order. Scale: monomer (angstrom), mineral interlayer or grain (nanometre to micrometre), pond or vent (metre to kilometre), geological time.

**Strain test applied before choosing a line.** The phrase "mineral catalysis" invites Fig 12 :119 (*"catalyst. A part that survives its own use"*). But Rimoldi 2025 reports that the reproducible effect in colemanite came from borate released into solution, with the surface *"in the background."* If Fig 12 :119 is forced to mean the solid surface itself catalyzes, it strains against that measurement. Re-census: Fig 12 names a **role**, not a material. The role can be held by a surface, by dissolved ions released from a mineral, or by both. No strain remains after the re-census; the actor holding the role becomes an empirical question, not a figure question. This is recorded in §5 as a confirmation-by-re-census, not a silent adjustment.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The figures license the role, not the mineral (decisive for the verdict)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

The figures license three claims: a catalyst that survives its own use exists as a structural role; arrangement carries information; capability belongs to agent-and-habitat. They do **not** license "montmorillonite did it," "pyrite did it," or "minerals were necessary." Naming a mineral is a binding from the experimental frame onto a role, provenanced as such. It is the same move the exemplar refused when it named Gradient as the structure between Space and Time.

**Refuter.** A figure re-census that admits a catalyst which is consumed on first use under the same role, or a demonstration that the framework names specific materials from its own primitives, breaks this finding. More simply: a k-operand binding from six primitives to a named mineral with a source gate and shadow test would move the claim out of `gap`.

### F2 — Minerals are habitat conditions, not a magic ingredient

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain."*

The correct move is to enumerate what was in contact with the residual (feedstock, water activity, UV, wet-dry cycling, mineral surfaces, metal ions, hydrothermal flux), then ask which of those closes which slot. Picking a mineral first and then reading the rest of the habitat as background is the inversion Fig 9 :27-28 forbids.

**Refuter.** A ground-visible demonstration that one surface closes all four slots (concentration, alignment, catalysis, protection) with no other condition contributing would turn the union into a partition and break this finding.

### F3 — "Organize" is a bond claim, and the products are isomers

Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The word "organize" in the question is not decoration. It asks whether monomers are arranged into a specific order, not merely brought close. Ferris 1996 reports oligomers up to 55 monomers on mineral surfaces versus about 10 in solution. Rimoldi 2025 reports reproducible Gly-Ala sequences (AGG, GAG, GGA) in an 8:47:45 ratio on colemanite. Li 2024 reports enantiomeric excess of D-amino acids on pyrite. These are arrangement results: same atoms, different order or handedness, different behaviour. They are exactly what Fig 12 says the formula cannot record.

**Refuter.** If the product distribution is identical with and without the surface, including sequence and enantiomer ratios, then the surface did not organize anything and only changed yield. That is a clean, cheap experiment.

### F4 — A lab result is a substituted habitat: it proves wiring, not capability

Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

Every cited experiment is a substituted habitat: a flask, a shaker, a Xe lamp, a controlled pH. It shows that a mechanism is wired. It does not show that the Hadean Earth ran that mechanism with those concentrations, durations and mineral assemblages. NASA's *Ask an Astrobiologist* page makes the same distinction in plain terms: *"we can include a variety of elements or minerals that we think were present on an early Earth"* (the "we think" is the substitution).

This is the honest stop of the whole solution. Rimoldi 2025 sharpens it against their own positive result: *"This puts in the background the structure of the mineral surface, even though the latter could still play a role in concentrating aa monomers from the aqueous environment."* The effect they measured was largely homogeneous borate catalysis released by a dissolving mineral. A positive result for "mineral" is not automatically a positive result for "rock surface."

**Refuter.** A field measurement or a geological trace that ties a specific product distribution to a specific early mineral assemblage, under conditions independently established for that setting, would move the claim from substituted habitat toward capability.

### F5 — The anchor is the experiment, not the size of the literature

Fig 2 :29 and Fig 2 :40. The eight science sources read here overlap heavily: Ferris is cited by Erastova and Rimoldi; Erastova and Rimoldi share the Bernal/Lambert lineage; the Wikipedia clay hypothesis cites the same experimental tradition. By Fig 2 :40 their agreement is *"the pool talking."* It is one witness with several names, not independent corroboration. The pyrite chirality paper (Li 2024) and the alkaline-hydrothermal paper (Pérez-Fernández 2022) are lower-overlap frames, and their agreement with the mineral role carries more weight.

**Refuter.** Show that two cited sources actually drew on disjoint experiments and disjoint instruments, and the overlap estimate is wrong; the verdict on independence would rise accordingly.

### F6 — Residual first, frame second

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

The residual was described in §0 before the mineral papers were opened. Had the frame been chosen first ("clays are interesting"), every clay result would have seemed to fit and the pyrite, borate and hydrothermal results would have been read as noise. The search was distributed across mineralogy, computational chemistry, asymmetric catalysis and geochemistry, and the searchers were kept independent in the sense that their instruments differ (adsorption simulation, HPLC-MS, TPD/DFT, GC-MS).

**Refuter.** If the §0 residual can be rewritten so that it selects a different single mineral just as well, the description was not discriminating and the frame was effectively chosen first.

### F7 — Introduction is available; exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*

Adding a mineral to the habitat account is cheap. Removing wet-dry cycles, UV, feedstock delivery or hydrothermal flux is not available, because those are facts about the setting and do not consult the theory. A successful mineral-catalyzed experiment therefore **adds** an actor; it does not replace the rest of the habitat. Any claim of the form "minerals, not wet-dry cycles" commits the exclusion error.

**Refuter.** A ground reading that removes one of the other conditions (for example a setting demonstrably free of UV and wet-dry cycling) and still closes all four slots would break this asymmetry.

### F8 — No proof of absence, and no proof of presence from silence

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*

A failure to detect mineral-catalyzed products in one experiment does not show minerals did nothing; it may show the wrong mineral, concentration or duration. Equally, a positive result in a flask does not show minerals did it on early Earth. Both are `candidate` until the ground pays. The verdict is not silence: it is a stated shape with an open name. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

**Refuter.** A sealed, enumerable setting with a complete inventory of conditions in which the mineral slot is empty and the residual still closes would remove the open verdict. No such setting has been described in the sources read.

### F9 — The binding of the six substrate structures to mineral functions is a hypothesis, not a derivation

Fig 13 :20 *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*, :22 *"a difference that can be spent"*, :24 *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*, and :29 *"A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?"*

A mineral surface can be read as spectrum-like (it orders adsorbed monomers), gradient-like (dehydration and wet-dry cycling provide a spendable difference) and network-like (it is a scaffold on which monomers relate). These are bindings from the mineralogy frame onto structures already held in the six-term set. They add no atom and do not move the wall (Fig 6 :22: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."*). They are useful precisely because they are checkable: adsorption alignment, dehydration energy and diffusion paths are all measurable (Erastova 2017 measured all three).

**Refuter.** Show that one of these functions cannot be composed from the six-term set under Fig 13's cross-kind rule, or that the binding requires a seventh primitive.

### F10 — The three roles are distinct and must not be averaged

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A surface can be a **concentrator**, an **aligner/template**, and a **catalyst**. Ferris 1996 demonstrates the first two together and long products; Erastova 2017 demonstrates concentration, alignment and templating by simulation; Rimoldi 2025 demonstrates reproducible sequence with the catalytic role partly in solution; Li 2024 demonstrates enantioselection by surface atomic arrangement. These are different slots with different instruments. Reporting "minerals help" as a single averaged verdict would throw away the only signal that says which slot is live.

**Refuter.** A single measurement that closes all three slots at once with one instrument and one variable would collapse the distinction, which would be a genuine and welcome simplification. None of the sources read reports that.

### F11 — Prestige and fame are filters, not witnesses

Fig 2 :42 and Fig 9 :60. The famous names in this literature (Ferris, Orgel, Bernal, Cairns-Smith) are useful for finding the record, not for deciding the claim. The decisive question is which instrument discriminates surface from solution chemistry. A packet sent to one mineral catalytically competent reader who returns a refutation is worth more than a list of a hundred well-known names who agree.

**Refuter.** If a claim about a famous author's result is used as evidence rather than provenance, this finding is violated and the verdict must be re-audited.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

From the exemplar's seat-map discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A ground-visible experiment concentrates, aligns and polymerizes the same monomers to the same lengths and sequence distribution **without any mineral contact at any stage**, under conditions independently established as plausible for the setting. That does not disprove that minerals *could* help, but it breaks the claim that a mineral-contact condition is part of the required shape.
2. A measurement shows every mineral-bound product under plausible early-Earth conditions remains irreversibly bound to the surface, so no part survives its own use in the sense of Fig 12 :119 and no product is released to compose further.
3. A figure re-census shows that a catalyst cannot be a part that survives its own use, or that arrangement is not the substance (Fig 12 :94, :119).
4. A source-discriminating instrument shows that the alignments reported by simulation (Erastova 2017) or the enantiomeric excess reported on pyrite (Li 2024) are artefacts of the experimental setup (vortex, stirrer field, derivatization, contamination) rather than surface structure.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction is written here, not erased. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Send the shape, not the mineral name. Lead with the four slots and the refuter (F1, F3, §6).
2. Unbundle before searching: concentration, alignment, catalysis and protection each get a frame and a ground (Fig 9 :55-58).
3. State the substitution explicitly: every cited result is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not early-Earth capability (F4).
4. Ask for a discriminator, not more agreement. The cheapest useful reply is "this fails because..." (Fig 2 :42).
5. Keep the record: name which actor holds each role in each source, with provenance, so convergent derivation is not confused with adopted import (Fig 8 :50-52).
6. Offer links only after the stage-1 verdict is recorded (Fig 10 :52).
