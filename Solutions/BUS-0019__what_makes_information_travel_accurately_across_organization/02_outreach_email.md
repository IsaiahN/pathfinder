# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails of named individuals.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output below must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. If it does not, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about information across levels before you decide if it is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about organizational communication. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** accurate information transfer across organizational levels is not a matter of sending more or clearer messages. It requires three conditions, and distortion is what happens when any one of them fails:
>
> 1. What crosses a level boundary must be a re-applicable method going up and a head start going down, with the loss across the boundary measured. A recording of one occasion looks like knowledge but is only a description of one success; replayed downward it produces repetition, not a new decision.
> 2. Each handoff must go to a genuinely different receiver that is still under the pressure of the same non-updating ground, with the decision-relevant detail intact. If each level only re-summarizes the last, agreement compounds while evidence does not.
> 3. The receiving level must be able to check its picture against a ground that does not move when anyone pushes on it, using evidence independent of the source it is checking. Agreement that comes from one shared dashboard is one evidence pool talking, not two confirmations.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a single accuracy score, a best medium, or a universal number of layers. The work returns a shape and a refuter, not a name. It does not say communication is bad for organizations, and it does not say shared meanings are a corruption. Agreement about what words mean is the product; agreement about facts drawn from one pool is what fails.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. A team shows its received picture matches the operational ground on every decision-relevant slot, with the loss measured and bounded, and the ground unmoved by the reporting relationship.
> 2. Two genuinely independent instruments, not two people reading the same report, agree against that ground.
> 3. A one-off success story, retold down the hierarchy, still generates a valid new decision with the ground paying for it.
> 4. One reporting change clears all three conditions at once with measured ground contact.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding condition. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README (canonical usage rule): see the local copy in `FRAMEWORK/reference/`
> - The framework's own account of drift and shared evidence: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** take any one of your own problems, write down what would refute your answer *before* you run it, and check the result against a ground you can name. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage offer, explicitly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the three conditions and the refuter. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If and only if stage 1 survives and the recipient asks, show the derivation and the figures, with the note that the figure text, not the summary, is the authority. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in.** Offer the loop as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user state their own ground and refuter before running it. That is a head start crossing, not a recording (Fig 4 :22).
