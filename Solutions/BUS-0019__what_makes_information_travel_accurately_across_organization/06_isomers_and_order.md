## DERIVATION

### Q1. Union, not partition, and why the intersections matter

Fig 9 :54-55: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

D1 (transfer fidelity), D2 (chain independence) and D3 (verification against ground) overlap. A reporting change can fix fidelity while leaving verification broken; a verified fact can arrive through a mirror chain; independent frames can disagree because the channel decayed. These are not three versions of one problem. Each intersection is a specific compound with its own loop.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one lever clears all three conditions at once, with ground contact measured per slot, the union reading fails and a single-cause partition is owed instead. This is refuter item 4 in `01` §5.

### Q2. The order is dependency, not importance

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

Apply the chain of Fig 3 in order: (1) perception: can the reporting level tell what is in front of it and distinguish it from before; (2) vocabulary: does it have terms for what the situation affords, including the bonds or arrangement, not just the ingredients; (3) objective: can it say what would count as progress; (4) planning: is there a route to that objective chosen under incomplete information; (5) learn and carry: name what worked, verify it and carry it. The break usually sits at the middle link (Fig 3 :24-27), because perception and planning have obvious tests while vocabulary and objective do not.

**Falsifier:** an organization that repairs the middle link (adds affordance terms and an explicit progress criterion) shows no improvement in per-slot residual when measured against the ground, while repairing a link above the break does. If so, the dependency order does not bind in that case.

### Q3. Are two reporting structures isomers or one functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 12 :46-47: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

Two organizations with the same chart, the same number of layers and the same set of reporting atoms can differ only in which bond holds between a report and its source: a method (re-applicable) versus a recording (one occasion), an independent measurement versus the same dashboard read twice, a non-updating anchor versus a target that moves with the report. By Fig 12, they have the same atoms and different bonds, hence different substances. They are **isomers**, and the difference is invisible in the org chart (the "flat formula") and decisive in behaviour. Fig 12 :14-16: "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost."

If the two structures do not share the same atom set, for example one has an independent instrument the other lacks, they are not isomers; they are two members of the same **functional group** (a handoff with a ground) differing in which atom occupies a slot. The isomer claim is therefore conditional on a fixed atom set, exactly as in the sibling solution BUS-0012. This condition is a live strain and is named, not hidden.

**Falsifier:** if a reporting structure can be written without an atom the other requires (no non-updating anchor, or no independent frame), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q4. Catalyst, cycle and functional group as checkable analogues

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."

- **Catalyst.** A shared definition or method that survives its own use is a catalyst; a saturated metric is a spent part that looks identical in the chart. Fig 12 warns that nothing records which a term is. Checkable question: does the same reporting element still discriminate after repeated use, or has it saturated (Fig 10 :26)?
- **Cycle.** A feedback loop in which the output re-enters as input can close. Fig 2 :13-15 shows the negative case: two levels updating on each other close a coherent drift loop that never touches ground. Fig 12 :121 says the sign of the loop decides whether it closes; a linear chain cannot close by construction. Checkable question: does the reporting loop range against an external anchor, or only against itself?
- **Functional group.** "a handoff under a non-updating ground" is a functional group; the qualifier (which method, which instrument) says which member. This is the honest level of generality the figures support without naming a medium.

**Falsifier for the analogues:** a spent metric that still discriminates after repeated use, or a self-referential reporting loop that nonetheless tracks an external ground, would break the catalyst or cycle reading. Then the analogue is decorative and should be dropped.

### Q5. Bond visibility: why the org chart cannot settle accuracy

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :14-16: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost"
Fig 13 :127-129: "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable. Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."

An org chart lists ingredients: layers, roles, channels. It does not record whether a given report crossed as a method or a recording, whether the receiving frame was independent or a mirror, whether the anchor moved. The bond is the missing half. This is why the expert packet asks for the bond to be named for a specific signal rather than asking for the chart. Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."

**Falsifier:** an org chart from which the bond type of each cross-level signal can be read directly, with the ground confirming it. If such a chart exists for a case, the bond-visibility claim is too strong for that case and should be narrowed to charts that omit bond records.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of overlapping conditions with an ordered dependency chain, not a partition and not a single lever. Under a fixed atom set, two reporting structures that differ only in the bonds between their signals are isomers: same chart, different substance, invisible in the formula. Without a fixed atom set, the honest claim is a functional group. The catalyst and cycle analogues are checkable only as questions about saturation and loop closure, not as decorations. The isomer claim carries one live strain (the fixed-atom condition), recorded in §3 and in `11_audit.md`.
