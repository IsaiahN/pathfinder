# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the frame

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker can see the residual and its refuter without first being shown the framework. If the output cannot be checked on its own, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about independent agencies, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can independent agencies protect long-term public interests without weakening democratic control?" is not one dial with two ends. It is two conditions that must hold at once, plus a compound at their intersection. From that it follows that:
>
> 1. the long-term outcome the agency serves has to work as a reference that does not change when a political frame pushes on it. If the "public interest" means whatever the current majority says this quarter, it is not a reference and the agency and its principals drift together in a way nobody notices;
> 2. democratic control is not the agency doing what the current executive wants. It is the agency and the elected principals being corrected by the same outcome separately, with neither able to move it;
> 3. insulation is not removal of democratic control. It substitutes one habitat for another, so the real question is which contacts were reproduced and which unchosen actors came in;
> 4. what must be designed is the channel to the outcome and an outside seat that maintains it, not the title "independent." A seat that can reconstruct the agency's own claim cannot check it.
>
> This is a shape and a separator, not a design. I am deliberately not naming fixed terms, appointment rules, budgets, or an oversight architecture. The framework I am using returns a shape, not a name, and no measurement here settles which institution occupies the slot.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a case where the long-term outcome can legitimately be settled by a reading that moves when a political frame pushes on it;
> 2. a case where lawful democratic correction requires the agency to adopt the principal's preferred answer rather than be corrected by a shared outcome;
> 3. an agent-only change (status, term, budget) that moves the outcome and preserves correction with no change in contact;
> 4. an insulation change that reproduces every required contact and introduces no unchosen actor, with measured outcomes;
> 5. one reform that clears both conditions with the compound explicitly tested;
> 6. oversight that reconstructs the claim, decides outcomes through its procedures, hides its own reading-interest, and stays fixed while the question moves, yet still corrects independently.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Full architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked derivation and six-term argument: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want the method:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
