# The Tether's answer on independent agencies and democratic control

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the claim that the question is a union with a compound, `gap` on the institutional recipe and on reading the long-term interest now. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05` section 7). It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether insulation that protects long-term outcomes can coexist with the polity's capacity to correct the agency. The residual is not "how much independence." It is two conditions that must hold at once, plus a compound where an independent act is also a democratic veto. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22).

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, time horizon.** The long-term public outcome must work as a reference that does not change when a political frame pushes on it. Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." If "the public interest" is whatever the current majority says, it is a frame, and agency and principals drift together coherently (Fig 2 :34). Insulation is necessary for this, but only as a way of keeping the reference non-updating, never as a substitute for it. Status: `specified`, `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 11 :40).

**D2, democratic control.** Control is not the agency complying with the current executive. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :46 "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it." An agency that refuses a politically convenient decision can be *more* democratically controlled in this sense. Electoral judgement is the legitimate anchor for the legitimacy question because that question is constitutively about people, but it is only an instrument for the outcome question (Fig 2 :36-38). Two questions, two anchor roles, no contradiction. Status: `specified`, `triangulated`.

**D3, checking arrangement.** The seat that maintains the channel must be outside the agency's frame (Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."), must declare its stake (Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."), must not let a procedure decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"), and must change when the question changes (Fig 10 :72). Oversight rooms must differ from the last (Fig 7 :36). Status: `specified`, `single-source` on the seat rules (Fig 10), corroborated at low overlap by Fig 7.

**D4, the compound.** Insulation is not the removal of democratic control; it is the substitution of one habitat for another. Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Two failures are then silent: what democratic contact was not reproduced, and what unchosen actors came in (Fig 11 :68-69). Regulatory capture is the world's name for one version of the second failure. The compound is resolved by channel separation (Fig 2 :46), not by more insulation or more oversight. Status: `specified`, `single-source` for the substitution law, `triangulated` for the union itself (Fig 9 plus Fig 3).

**Across domains.** Independence is a property of agency and habitat, not of the agent. Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Its valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51), so verdicts stay per agency and per interest. The two halves use different operators (refusal ¬ versus comparison ⋛, see `06` Q1), so the trade-off framing is a notation error. Benefit and cost are priced in two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125-126).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named agency or country is independent enough, or too independent. No case was measured.
- Not a design. No fixed term, budget rule, appointment mechanism, sunset clause, or oversight architecture is selected. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not that insulation is good or bad in itself. Its valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that democratic control means majority preference or executive compliance. That is negotiation (Fig 2 :35).
- Not that an independence index settles anything. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an outcome.
- Not that oversight fixes the channel. A room that shares the agency's closure is a mirror (Fig 7 :36, Fig 10 :42).

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another index. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read directly): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Reference test:** does the agency's operative reference move when political pressure is applied? Measure the reference, not the statute.
2. **Separate-correction test:** are the agency and the elected principals corrected by the same outcome separately, with neither able to move it (Fig 2 :46)?
3. **Substitution audit:** enumerate the democratic contacts the insulation failed to reproduce and the unchosen actors it introduced (Fig 11 :66-69). This is an enumeration, not a score.
4. **Seat test:** can the oversight body reconstruct the agency's claim? Does any of its conventions decide an outcome? Has it declared its reading-interest? Does it change when the question changes (Fig 10 :42, :47, :60, :72)?
5. **Discriminator:** exogenous variation in insulation that measures both long-term outcomes and democratic correction against a result neither side controls. Without this, commitment and capture remain metamers (see `05` section 4).

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4, all written before the run, are repeated in `08`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The reference's regime is deferred: the long-term outcome cannot be read at decision time with the instruments available for this derivation. Fig 13 :133 "reached at all. A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The six candidate bindings (terms, budgets, appointments, review periods, mandates, sunset clauses) are a `gap`; they need a source gate and a shadow test before being carried across, then a ground reading. One live type strain remains: a real checking chamber is an arrangement, while ¬ is a unary operator on a claim (`06` Q1). It is named, not hidden. Indiscernibility is not identity; the record of the route is what keeps this checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a design for an independent agency and does not say independence is good or bad. It says the question is not one dial. The long-term outcome must work as a reference that does not change when a political frame pushes on it; democratic control is not the agency doing what the current executive wants, but the agency and its principals being corrected by that same reference separately, with neither able to move it. Insulation does not remove democratic control, it substitutes a different habitat, so the real audit is which contacts were reproduced and which unchosen actors came in. What must be designed is the channel and the outside seat that maintains it, with its stake declared and its room changing when the question changes. The two halves are different operators, refusal and comparison, so they do not trade off as opposite amounts of one thing. What the framework offers is that shape, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The institution stays open until the ground settles it.
