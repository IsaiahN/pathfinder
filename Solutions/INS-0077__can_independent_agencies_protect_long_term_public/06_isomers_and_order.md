# Operators, union order, and institutional isomers

**Mode: `specified` on the operator assignment and the union order, `general` on the structural claim, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary debate treats "protection of long-term interests" and "democratic control" as opposite ends of one quantity, so that more of one means less of the other. Three questions are owed: (1) are the two operations the same kind of thing, (2) in what order must a design be assembled, and (3) are two different designs over the same institutional atoms different things or different amounts of one thing?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held at the institutional level: a reference (outcome), a channel (contact), a correction seat, a decision, a set of authorized goals, and a stake. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The six-term physical substrate is **not** being mapped onto agencies; that forced mapping is a retracted reading (see `01` section 7). What is used here is the operator set and the composition rules, which are domain-agnostic.

## Q1. Are refusal and control the same operator? No.

- The Operators table defines negation as "this does not hold" (:55), with the test "is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes." (:57) and the note "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." (:58).
- Fig 12 :135-136: "The seven are bonds, and a bond is a relation between two things. Negation is not one: it takes a term and yields a term, and joins nothing. So it is not an eighth bond, and it is not a relation that fails the six tests. It is a unary operator, outside what the tests range over, which is a different verdict from the one above and reached differently."
- The Operators table, :48-51: "⋛ / comparison / a threshold relation between two quantities / no / is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." and :52: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one".

**Derivation.** Insulation from short-term pressure is a refusal: the agency declines the claim that the politically preferred answer is the one to give. That is ¬ acting on a single claim. Democratic control is a threshold comparison: does the agency's action fall within what was authorized, and does it track the shared outcome? That is ⋛ acting on two quantities. They act on different operands with different arities, so they do not trade off against each other as opposite values of one variable. The trade-off framing is a notation error: a vocabulary holding only the symmetric bond + and the sequence → can name both ingredients and cannot record that one is a refusal and the other a comparison.

**Live strain S1 (named, not hidden).** A real oversight chamber is an arrangement of actors and conventions, while ¬ is a unary operator on a claim. The seat's "refusal capacity" is an analogue of negation, not an instance of it. Operators table :73-74: "− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim. A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong." A charter that removes a political actor is subtraction; a charter that refuses a claim is negation. Designs that do only the first can only accumulate. This strain is carried into `08` and `11`.

**Falsifier Q1:** exhibit a design where the refusal and the comparison act on the same operand as opposite values of one quantity, with no loss of meaning. Then the operator separation fails.

## Q2. What is the union order? Anchor, channel, seat, then design.

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
- Fig 2 :46: "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."

**Derivation.** The dependency order is:

1. **Name the reference in effect terms** (does not move when pushed, per slot). Without this, "independence" has no object.
2. **Separate the channels** so agency and principals are corrected by that reference separately. Without this, the compound D1 ∩ D2 stays standing.
3. **Install an outside seat** that maintains the channel, cannot reconstruct the agency's claim, declares its stake, and changes when the question changes.
4. **Only then** choose terms, budgets, appointment rules and review periods. These are bindings onto steps 1-3, not substitutes for them.

Most reform effort lands at step 4, where instruments already exist (Fig 3 :29-30: "Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."). That is why independence charters proliferate while the channel question stays unmeasured. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

**Falsifier Q2:** a design assembled in reverse order (charter first, reference later) that nevertheless keeps both the reference non-updating and the correction channel intact, with both measured. Then dependency order does not bind for this domain.

## Q3. Are two designs isomers? Yes, over a fixed atom set.

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

**Derivation.** Two agencies with the same atoms (reference, channel, seat, decision, authorized goals, stake) can differ by bond:

- Design A joins reference to decision through a **sequence** → with the seat *downstream* (ex post review).
- Design B joins reference to decision through a **threshold** ⋛ with the seat *upstream* (ex ante authorization).
- Design C joins seat to agency by **identity** ≡ (the reviewers are drawn from the same professional pool and share its history).

Under Fig 12 :117 these are not three amounts of independence. They are three substances with the same ingredients, and the difference is invisible in any ingredient list (a charter's written powers) and decisive in behaviour. Design C is the same atom set as A and B but a different bond, and it is the one most likely to be a "chain of mirrors" (Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced.").

At a coarser level, all three share a **functional group**: reference, channel, outside seat. Fig 12 :123: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The shape returned by the framework names the group; it does not choose the member.

**Falsifier Q3:** if two designs with the same atoms and different bonds behave identically on both the long-term outcome and democratic correction, with both measured, the isomer claim fails for those atoms and only the functional-group claim remains.

## Q4. What are the two currencies, and why do they not add?

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."
- Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."

**Derivation.** The benefit of insulation is an action price: it is settled only when a long-term outcome pays, and that payment is deferred and often irreversible. The cost to democratic control is partly a description price: who gets to name the goal, and whose vocabulary counts as the public interest. An "independence score" adds these two readings or, worse, replaces both with a third (expert reputation). The figures forbid the sum. The honest report keeps two ledgers and says which one is unmeasured.

**Falsifier Q4:** a single unit in which both the long-term outcome and the authorization cost are settled by the same instrument, with no loss on either axis. Then the two-currency rule does not bind here.

## Q5. What is the order of the union itself? Unbundle before searching.

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

**Derivation.** The question's "without" is the union's intersection. The first pass is to unbundle: separate the case where long-term protection fails because of electoral pressure from the case where democratic control fails because of insulation. Two levers work on two regions. The intersection (an independent act that is also a democratic veto) is not a harder case of either; it is a compound that needs channel separation (Fig 2 :46). Searching for one lever that solves all three is searching inside a closed set for an atom that is not there (Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom.").

**Falsifier Q5:** one lever that clears both regions and the compound, with the compound explicitly isolated and tested.

---

## CONCLUSION

The two halves of the question are **different operators** (refusal and comparison), assembled in a **dependency order** (reference, channel, seat, then design). Institutional designs over one atom set are **isomers**, not quantities, so the variable is type and bond, not degree. Two currencies are in play and do not add. The union must be unbundled before any lever is chosen, and the intersection is a compound requiring channel separation. No figure line names the institution; the output is the functional group plus written falsifiers.
