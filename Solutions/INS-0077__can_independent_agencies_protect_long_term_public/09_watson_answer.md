## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about insulation that is meant to protect long-term public interests while preserving democratic control. Option A: treat it as one dial, raise independence, accept the loss of control as the price. Option B: treat it as a union of two conditions with a compound at the intersection, pin a non-updating reference, separate the correction channels, and put the maintenance of the channel in an outside seat. What each leads to: A spends the budget on a filter and leaves the compound standing; B yields a checkable shape, six written refuters and an honest gap where an institution would have to be named.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Two conditions that must hold at once (long-term protection, preserved democratic control) plus a constitutive third question (what counts as the long-term public interest and who settles it). Arity: two conditions with a compound, not one variable. Symmetry: asymmetric; protection is a refusal (¬) of the short-term frame's claim, control is a comparison (⋛) of action against an authorized goal. Scale: first order inside one decision, second order in the design of the checking seat. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- **Domain 1, time horizon / commitment.** Actors: elected officials with short horizons, diffuse future beneficiaries, the longer-horizon agency. Currency: stability against the cycle versus responsiveness to the moment. Anchor: the realised long-term public outcome, nameable as a kind and not readable in full now. Union side: one half. Dependency: first, because independence has no object until the reference is stated.
- **Domain 2, democratic control / authorisation.** Actors: citizens, legislature, elected executive, parties, interest networks. Currency: votes, authorisation, the power to correct or remove. Anchor: electoral judgement is the legitimate anchor for the legitimacy question (constitutively about people) and only an instrument for the outcome question. Union side: other half; overlap with D1 is D4. Dependency: second.
- **Domain 3, checking arrangement / seat.** Actors: oversight committees, courts, auditors, inspectors general, professional peers, sunset reviews. Currency: conventions, verdicts, provenance. Anchor: the reachable ground, maintained but never authored. Question type: separate domain at the next scale. Dependency: third.
- **Domain 4, the compound D1 ∩ D2.** Actors: the case where the same input is both direction and pressure, where an independent act is also a democratic veto. Currency: ambiguous input. Anchor: present but unable to correct both frames separately until channels are separated. Question type: a specific compound with its own loop. Dependency: resolved by channel separation.
- **Not a domain:** "the public interest" as one aggregate number. No nameable anchor across plural interests; verdicts stay per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22). Reported as a gap.

Order by dependency: name the reference, separate the channels, install the outside seat, then choose terms and budgets (Fig 3 :19, :20). Strain test: the non-updating anchor rule and the legitimacy-is-about-people rule govern different questions, so there is no internal strain after re-census. One live type strain remains: a real checking chamber is an arrangement while ¬ is a unary operator on a claim (`06` Q1).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union with a compound, not a trade-off. Long-term protection requires a reference that does not update on the political frame. Democratic control requires that the agency and the elected principals be corrected by that reference separately, with neither able to move it, and that an outside seat maintain the correction channel, declare its stake and change when the question changes. Insulation does not remove democratic control; it substitutes a habitat, so the audit is which contacts were reproduced and which unchosen actors came in. The two halves are different operators, refusal and comparison, so they do not consume one another. Independence is a property of agency and habitat, never of the title. No figure line selects a term, budget, appointment rule or oversight architecture; that is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, six refuters and the lineage before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recipe or a score instead of a shape. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter and by seeking reviewers from different closures.
- **undoable:** yes. The candidate bindings are separable from the shape in `07_the_tether_answer.md`. A defective binding can be withdrawn without touching the derivation, no primitive has to be removed, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. The substitution audit is an enumeration, not a permanent institutional commitment.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A reference test showing the operative reference moves when pushed would break D1; a separate-correction failure would break D2; an agent-only improvement that moves the outcome with unchanged contact would break the capability claim (Fig 11 :39-40); a single reform clearing both conditions and the compound would break the union reading; a source-discriminating instrument separating commitment from capture would move the gap from `unreached` to settled; and a figure re-census allowing a real seat to be identical with the unary negation operator would close the live type strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
