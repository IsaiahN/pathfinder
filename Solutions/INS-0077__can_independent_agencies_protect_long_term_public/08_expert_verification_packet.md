# Expert verification packet: independent agencies and democratic control

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of independent agencies can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the union claim, `gap` on the institutional recipe. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

The question is a union of conditions with a compound at their intersection, not a single dial. Long-term protection requires a reference that does not update when a political frame pushes on it. Democratic control requires that the agency and the elected principals be corrected by that reference separately, with neither able to move it, and that an outside seat maintain the correction channel, declare its own stake and change when the question changes. Insulation does not remove democratic control; it substitutes a habitat, so what must be enumerated is which contacts were reproduced and which unchosen actors came in. The two halves are different operators, refusal (¬) and comparison (⋛), so they do not trade off as opposite amounts of one quantity. The framework returns this shape and the refuters, not an institution.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The reference must not update, and control is separate correction (D1 and D2)

- **Statement:** if the "public interest" is defined by whatever the current majority says, it is a frame, not an anchor; control is not compliance but separate correction by a shared reference.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :46 "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."
- **What to check:** does the agency's operative reference stay fixed under political pressure, and are agency and principals corrected by it separately? Or does "independence" simply replace one frame's preference with another's?
- **Refuter:** a case where the long-term outcome can legitimately be settled by a reading that moves when a political frame pushes on it; or a case where lawful democratic correction requires the agency to adopt the principal's preferred answer rather than be corrected by a shared outcome.

### C2. Insulation substitutes a habitat; the union needs channel separation (D4 and form)

- **Statement:** insulation changes contact and imports unchosen actors. A single lever clears only the region where one condition binds; the intersection needs the channels separated.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- **What to check:** for one real agency, enumerate the democratic contacts cut by insulation and the new actors introduced (industry peers, internal culture, professional norms). Is the compound case, where an independent act is also a democratic veto, left standing by the reform?
- **Refuter:** an insulation change that reproduces every required contact and introduces no unchosen actor, with measured outcomes, or one reform that clears both conditions with the compound explicitly tested.

### C3. The checking seat must be outside, declared, and changing (D3)

- **Statement:** oversight that shares the agency's closure is a mirror; a procedure that decides an outcome is a verdict, not a convention; a fixed room is the failure when the question moves.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :72 "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom." Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- **What to check:** can the oversight body reconstruct the agency's own claim? Do any of its procedures fix outcomes? Is its reading-interest declared? Does it change with the question?
- **Refuter:** an oversight body that fails all four tests yet still produces independent correction, measured against an outcome neither side controls.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named agency, country or reform is independent enough or too independent. No case was measured.
- Not a design: no term, budget rule, appointment mechanism, mandate, sunset clause or oversight architecture is selected (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that independence is intrinsically good or bad; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51).
- Not that democratic control means majority preference or executive compliance (Fig 2 :35).
- Not that an independence index is a verdict (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not that oversight repairs a broken channel (Fig 10 :42, Fig 7 :36).
- Not `grounded`. One live type strain is carried: a real seat is an arrangement, while ¬ is a unary operator on a claim (`06` Q1).

## 6. What would settle it

An exogenous variation in insulation that measures both long-term outcomes and democratic correction against a result neither side controls. Three shorter instruments that can fire sooner: a reference test (does the operative reference move when pushed?), a separate-correction test (Fig 2 :46), and a substitution audit (Fig 11 :66-69). A source-discriminating instrument is needed because commitment and capture currently read the same green; see *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Standing falsifiers are in section 2 and `01` section 4. If any fires, the claim is narrowed or withdrawn.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, :35, :46 and Fig 11 :66-67 (four short passages).
2. Check C2 against one agency you know: list the democratic contacts insulation cut and the unchosen actors it introduced. This is the decisive check.
3. Check C3: can the relevant oversight body reconstruct the agency's claim, and does any of its conventions fix outcomes?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish commitment from capture, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
