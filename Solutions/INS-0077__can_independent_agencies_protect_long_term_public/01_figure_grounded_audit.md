## 0. The residual in effect terms

Option A: treat the question as a single dial, raise "independence," and expect more long-term protection at the price of less democratic control. Option B: treat it as a union of conditions with a compound at the intersection, pin a long-term outcome that does not update on political pressure, keep the correction channel from the polity intact, and put the maintenance of that channel in an outside seat.

What each leads to: A spends the budget on a filter and leaves the compound standing, because one lever clears the region where only one condition holds. B yields a checkable separator, five written refuters, and an honest `gap` where a specific institution would have to be named and then measured.

**Ordinary-term binding (formula step, "Direction").** The sought-for shape is bound now so it can accrue a residual like any other term: *a governance arrangement protects long-term interests without weakening democratic control iff (i) the reference the agency uses does not change when a political frame pushes on it, (ii) the agency and the elected principals are corrected by that reference separately, and (iii) a seat outside the agency maintains the correction channel and declares its own stake.* That is the claim the refuter in section 4 is written against. It is a shape, not a recipe.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale:**
- Arity: two conditions that must hold at once (long-term protection; preserved democratic control), plus a constitutive third question (what counts as the long-term public interest and who settles it). Not one variable with two ends.
- Symmetry: asymmetric. Protection is a refusal of the short-term frame's claim on the decision; democratic control is a comparison of agency action against an authorized goal and a threshold. Refusal (the unary operator ¬) and comparison (the bond ⋛) are different operators, so they can act on different objects without trading off (see `06`).
- Scale: first order inside one agency decision (does this decision track the long-term outcome rather than the cycle); second order in the design of the checking arrangement (who corrects the correctors). Ordered by dependency, not by importance.

A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds" (Fig 9 :55). Most reform effort lands at the last step because that is where the measurements already exist, which is exactly Fig 3 :29-30's warning.

**Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.** "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" (Fig 1 :19). "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" (Fig 1 :22). Any composite independence score is an aggregate; it can be a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 3. Findings

### F1. The long-term interest must be pinned as a reference that does not update on the political frame (D1, decisive)

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

The time-consistency problem is exactly the anchor-updating failure. If "the long-term public interest" means whatever the current majority says it means today, it is not an anchor; it is a frame, and the agency and the elected principals drift together coherently, which is why the drift is invisible from inside. Insulation is the structural attempt to keep the reference from updating on the electoral frame. Its necessity follows from the anchor rule, not from a preference for technocracy.

**Refuter:** show a case where the long-term outcome can be settled by a reading that legitimately changes when a political frame pushes on it, and where the change does not make the "public interest" a frame. Or show that the outcome is constitutively about the political judgement itself, in which case D2's anchor governs and this finding narrows.

Triangulation: `triangulated` across Fig 2 (anchor and non-updating) and Fig 11 :40 (capability as contact), two different figures with low overlap. Status: `specified`.

### F2. Democratic control is triangulation, not agreement with current officeholders (D2, decisive)

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."

So "democratic control" cannot be operationalised as the agency doing what the current executive wants. That is negotiation; the agency becomes a mirror of the strongest influence. Control is preserved when the agency and the elected principals are each corrected by the same outcome separately and neither can move it. An agency that refuses a politically convenient decision can be *more* democratically controlled in this sense than one that complies.

**Refuter:** produce a case where lawful democratic correction requires the agency to adopt the elected principal's preferred answer rather than to be corrected by a shared outcome; then the triangulation reading fails for that slot and D2 must be re-censused.

Triangulation: `triangulated` across Fig 2 :35/:45/:46 and Fig 11 :40 (contact), low overlap. Status: `specified`.

### F3. The two anchor roles are different questions, not a contradiction (D1 and D2, strain dissolved)

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

Fig 2 :37-38: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

This is the key move that prevents a false conflict. Electoral judgement is the legitimate anchor for the legitimacy question ("is this an acceptable answer for a polity"), because that question is constitutively about people. It is only an instrument for the outcome question ("did the decision track the long-term public outcome"), and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. Long-term insulation is therefore compatible with democratic legitimacy when each is anchored to its own question, and it is corrosive when the electoral instrument is promoted to settle the outcome question or when the outcome reference is used to settle legitimacy.

**Refuter:** show that the legitimacy question is not constitutively about people, or that an electoral reading does discriminate long-term outcomes on the axis in question; then the two-role separation collapses.

This resolves the apparent strain between F1 (outcome must not move with politics) and F2 (control must be democratic). Different objects, same independence rule. Status: `specified`, `triangulated` (Fig 2 :36-38 plus the figures README's re-census rule).

### F4. Independence is a property of channel and habitat, not of the agent alone ("without weakening" half)

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does".

Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings".

A charter that changes only the agency's legal status, without changing its contact with the long-term outcome or the channel by which the polity can correct it, changes nothing on the axis the question asks about. What degrades under political interference is the channel: metrics saturate, readings get pooled, transients are dropped. The ground does not decay. So the object to design and to measure is the channel, not the title "independent."

**Refuter:** an agent-only change (status, term length, budget) that demonstrably moves the long-term outcome and preserves correction without any change in contact would refute the capability claim (Fig 11 :39-40). This is the standing falsifier from Fig 11 :71-72.

Triangulation: `triangulated` (Fig 11 and Fig 10, different figures and different levels). Status: `specified`.

### F5. Insulation by isolation substitutes a habitat, and that is precisely how control is weakened (D4, decisive for the "without" clause)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."

Making an agency "independent" by cutting its electoral moorings does not remove democratic control; it substitutes a different habitat (expert peers, regulated industry, internal culture). The agency inherits actors it did not choose, and two failures are silent: the democratic contact it failed to reproduce, and the new actors it unintentionally introduced. Regulatory capture is the world's name for one version of the second failure. This is why a formal independence score cannot answer the question: the insulation may have removed the wrong contact and imported the wrong actors at the same time.

**Refuter:** exhibit an insulation change that reproduces every contact the goal required and introduces no unchosen actor, with measured outcomes on both sides. Then the substitution warning does not bite for that case.

Triangulation: `single-source` for the substitution law (Fig 11), corroborated at the design level by the world source on regulatory capture (Stigler 1971, via the public summary) but not independently derived. Status: `specified`, unmeasured.

### F6. A union with a compound: one lever leaves the intersection standing (governs the question's form)

Fig 9 :54-57 (quoted in section 1) plus Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

"More independence" clears the region where only short-term pressure binds. "More oversight" clears the region where only unchecked discretion binds. At the intersection, the case where the same input is both direction and pressure, one lever leaves the compound standing because the other condition still binds. The compound needs a specific operation: separate the channels so that each frame is corrected by the same outcome separately (Fig 2 :46). An independence index, a term length, or an oversight count is a filter for where to spend attention; none is a witness for the verdict.

**Refuter:** a single reform that clears both conditions in a measured case, with the compound explicitly tested, would refute the union reading and restore the trade-off model for that domain.

Triangulation: `triangulated` (Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 3 dependency order, low overlap). Status: `general` on the form, `specified` per domain.

### F7. The checking seat must be outside the agency's frame, declare its stake, and change with the question (D3)

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Oversight that shares the agency's evidence pool is one room wearing two names. A procedural rule that actually decides outcomes is no longer a convention; it is a constant the seat authored and therefore a verdict (Fig 10 :47-49). Reviewers and agency staff have unavoidable interests in outcomes; what is disqualifying is an interest in the reading, so stakes are declared. And a review arrangement fixed while the underlying question moves is the named failure, which is the figure-grounded case for sunset and adaptive review, not for a permanent charter.

**Refuter:** an oversight body that reconstructs the agency's claim, decides outcomes through its procedures, has an undeclared interest in the reading, and remains unchanged while the question moves, yet still produces independent correction, would refute this finding.

Triangulation: `single-source` for the seat rules (Fig 10 is one framework document); the "rooms must differ" half is `triangulated` with Fig 7 at low overlap. Status: `specified`.

### F8. Agreement and prestige cannot substitute for a ground in this question (outreach consequence)

Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

A list of agencies or scholars selected because they already agree that independence works is a convention, not corroboration. The cost of being wrong here is high and often irreversible (environmental and financial harms), so the figures say to buy independence in proportion: seek reviewers from different closures (administrative law, institutional economics, reliability science, political philosophy) rather than a larger sample of the same public-administration pool. No personal emails, no receptiveness rank, no bulk send.

### F9. Independence is not good in itself; its valence is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

The same insulation that protects a financial regulator from an election cycle can protect a captured regulator from the public. There is no standing list of "good" insulation devices. The question is always which residual the device acts on, and contact cascades beyond first order: domestic institutional choices affect international and market actors three steps out (Fig 11 :52-54). Verdicts must therefore be per agency and per interest, never a global "independence is good" or "democracy requires control."

### F10. What the framework returns is a shape, not an institution (honest stop)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

Fig 13 :133: "reached at all. A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,".

No figure line selects fixed terms, appointment rules, budget insulation, sunset clauses, or a particular oversight architecture. Those are candidate bindings from the institutional-design frame onto the held structure "outside seat maintaining a channel to a non-updating ground." They require a source gate and a shadow test before being carried across (Fig 6 :23-24, Fig 8 :50-52), and then an empirical check against an outcome neither side controls. The shape is: pin the outcome, separate the channels, maintain the channel from an outside seat, declare the stake, change the room when the question moves. The institution is a `gap`, reported as a verdict and not as silence (Fig 13 :134).

### F11. Two currencies are being conflated in the usual debate (supporting, see `06`)

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."

The benefit side (long-term outcomes) is an action price settled at the ground; the cost side (democratic responsiveness) is partly a description price about who gets to name the goal. Summing them into one "independence score" is the named error, not the fix. Over a shared atom set, high-insulation and high-control designs are isomers (Fig 12 :117), so type, not degree, is the variable.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any world search was used to select a verdict. Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor failure:** a case where the long-term public outcome can be settled by a reading that legitimately moves when a political frame pushes on it, and where that does not make the reference a frame (breaks F1).
2. **Triangulation failure:** a case where lawful democratic correction requires the agency to adopt the elected principal's preferred answer rather than be corrected by a shared outcome (breaks F2).
3. **Agent-only success:** a change limited to the agency's legal status or resources that moves the long-term outcome and preserves correction with no change in contact (breaks F4, Fig 11 :39-40).
4. **Substitution success:** an insulation change that reproduces every contact the goal required and introduces no unchosen actor, with measured outcomes on both sides (breaks F5).
5. **Single-lever success:** one reform that clears both conditions with the compound explicitly tested (breaks F6).
6. **Seat failure:** an oversight body that reconstructs the claim, decides outcomes through procedure, hides its reading-interest, and stays fixed while the question moves, yet still produces independent correction (breaks F7).
7. **Ground reversal:** exogenous variation showing that more insulation monotonically reduces democratic correction with no compensating long-term gain, or the reverse, in a setting where both are measured against an outcome neither side controls.

If any fires, the union shape is withdrawn or narrowed for that domain. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Reframe the question as a union with a compound (F6) before choosing any lever. Stop asking "how much independence?"
2. State the long-term outcome as a non-updating reference per slot, and say which question it anchors (F1, F3). Do not aggregate into one "public interest" score (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. Redefine democratic control as separate correction by a shared reference, not as compliance with current officeholders (F2).
4. Design the channel and the outside seat before the charter terms: non-access, declared stakes, no outcome-fixing conventions, rooms that change with the question (F7).
5. Treat insulation as habitat substitution; enumerate what democratic contact was reproduced and what unchosen actors came in (F5, F9).
6. Publish the five written refuters with the shape claim, and seek checkers from different closures (F8). Use the public venues in `03`, one to one.
7. Mark the institutional recipe `gap`. Do not name a design as the answer without a source gate, a shadow test, and a ground reading (F10).
