## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Two classical results point in opposite directions, and that is the answer's shape, not a contradiction to be smoothed away.** Albert et al. 2000 find that a scale-free network is robust to random failure but fragile to the removal of a few high-degree nodes. Liu et al. 2011 find that the nodes needed to control a directed network *"tend to avoid the high-degree nodes."* Both are figure-consistent once the residual is named: valence is relative to the goal (Fig 11 :49-51). "Influential" changes meaning between attack, immunization and control. This is the single most useful thing to tell a reader, and it comes from the world literature, not from the framework.
2. **The concentration assumption is empirical, not given.** Broido and Clauset 2019 find strongly scale-free structure rare across 928 networks. So the condition C1 (live mass concentrated) has to be measured in the system at hand; it cannot be assumed from a generic network shape. Artime et al. 2024 make the same practical point for dismantling: techniques have an *"optimal range of applicability"*, not universal superiority.
3. **Small interventions have a phase boundary.** Sanhedrai et al. 2022 find that a single node can reignite a failed network only within a recoverable phase and after structural reconstruction. This maps onto C3 and C4: the habitat must hold, and the target must be reachable from a closure that already contains the right structure. A viewer who tries one-node targeting outside that phase will conclude targeting fails; the figure says the condition was absent.
4. **A low-overlap field corroborates the hard part.** MathisonSlee et al. 2022 report the ecology *"rule of hand"*: three to five variables may govern critical change, the proposition is under-tested, and it has been critiqued as reductionist. That is the shape (a small set can matter) plus the instrument gap (identifying the set is where the work is). It is a different evidence pool from network physics, so it is a genuine witness for the mechanism shape, not a second copy of the same model.

## 4. The two-stage offer

The proposition is: **(1)** validate the stage-1 shape against the ground, **(2)** only if curious, read the theory that produced it, **(3)** use the formula recursively on other problems.

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors; once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded first and is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and label the mode of what they get.
