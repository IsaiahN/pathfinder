# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the four conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated plainly in your own words. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one conditional claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the claim that "influential node" is not a property a node carries. It is a role relative to the question and the dynamics. Figure 11 says an actor's valence is relative to the residual, and the same actor can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. From the per-slot residual rule and the support guard, targeting a small set has an object only where the live residual mass is concentrated; where it is diffuse, a distributed action is the one with a signal to act on. Two further conditions have to hold: the measured influence must actually track the outcome (bracket fidelity), and the habitat must be stable enough that the identified influence persists. A fourth condition is reach: you cannot identify a target your closure does not contain.
>
> So the claim is not "targeting wins" or "distributed wins". It is a conjunction: targeting wins when live mass is concentrated, the bracket is faithful, the habitat holds, and the target is reachable. Each condition has its own failure signature and its own instrument. I have written down what would refute the claim below, before sending this.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular centrality measure is the answer, that hubs are always the right targets, that targeting is always cheaper, or that this framework settles which nodes you should act on. It returns a shape, not a name. Degree, betweenness, eigenvector centrality and PageRank are different functions of the same adjacency, so their agreement is one witness, not four.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a node whose influence rank is invariant across a change of goal on the same network, measured against the outcome in both cases;
> 2. a targeted intervention that wins where live residual mass is demonstrably diffuse;
> 3. a targeted intervention that wins while the influence map is measured as stale and the outcome barely depends on the cascade;
> 4. a direct-contact target that still wins where the decisive effect is three or more steps out;
> 5. a single lever that restores all four conditions across failure types with no categorically different residue left over.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Full architecture (13 figures): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it: the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the architecture post warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah
