# The Tether's answer: when targeted intervention beats distributed intervention

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two policies are compared: `a_T` acts on a small chosen set of nodes; `a_D` spreads the same effort across many. The question is when `R_T = |Γ(b,a_T) − o′|` is smaller than `R_D = |Γ(b,a_D) − o′|`, measured per system and per node. The outcome o′ is the anchor; the centrality score is an instrument (Fig 2 :29). The comparison is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: concentration, bracket fidelity, habitat stability and reach are overlapping conditions, each with its own loop and ground (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 2. The shape

1. **"Influential" is a role, not a property.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same node is the right target for containing an infection and the wrong target for preserving a keystone species (Fig 11 :50). Without naming the goal and the residual, the question has no object.
2. **Targeting needs concentrated live mass.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Formula step 3 requires support, `|R⁺_s| > 0 for some s`. If live mass is diffuse, there is no small set to aim at; if one slot carries the signal while the global average looks quiet, distributed action averages it away (Fig 1 :22).
3. **The bracket must be faithful and the ground must pressure the room.** Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" followed by Fig 7 :38 "the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff" and Fig 7 :39 "the ground’s pressure on the room". A stale influence map targets a phantom (Fig 10 :26).
4. **The habitat must hold and contact cascades.** Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A direct-contact centrality can miss the residual that matters; this is the concrete case where broad intervention can win.
5. **The target must be reachable.** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." You cannot identify a target your closure does not contain; contact with a differing frame is required (Fig 6 :23).
6. **The intervention acts on agent-and-habitat, with asymmetric power.** Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." "Targeted removal" is still an introduction of change into a habitat; you cannot subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :61-62).
7. **The framework does not name the nodes.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Which specific set wins needs a source-discriminating instrument or a provenanced import. Agreeing centrality measures are one evidence pool, not corroboration (Fig 2 :31, :40).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal rule for targeting, and not a universal rule for distributed action. Both are condition-bound.
- Not that high degree is the same thing as influence. Liu, Slotine and Barabási 2011 find control driver nodes tend to avoid hubs; this is a different residual, and the valence flips as Fig 11 :50 predicts.
- Not that cost decides the winner. Cost is a budget filter (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a centrality ranking, an isomer reading or any binding is `grounded`. No measurement pays for them here.
- Not which nodes to act on. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, declared rather than filled.
- Not that the framework validates itself. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself."*

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Concentration test:** does live residual mass actually concentrate in a few slots in the system at hand, measured per node and never averaged? Broido and Clauset 2019 show concentration cannot be assumed from a generic network shape.
- **Bracket test:** does the measured influence rank prospectively predict the post-intervention outcome, or is it a pooled reading of the same adjacency (Fig 10 :26)?
- **Cascade-depth test:** is the decisive residual first order, or several steps out (Fig 11 :52)?
- **Phase test:** is the system inside the recoverable phase in which a microscopic intervention can work at all? Sanhedrai et al. 2022 say that phase must be established first.
- **Goal-flip test:** does the target set change when the goal changes, as Fig 11 :50 requires?
- **Standing falsifiers:** the eight refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions are stated in effect terms and each has an instrument that would close it. The specific node set is not named, because the source-discriminating instrument is not held here. Declaring the conditions makes the choice visible, not neutral (*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*). The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that targeting influential nodes always wins. It says that "influential" is a role relative to a goal, not a property a node carries, so the question only has an object once the goal and the residual are named. Targeted intervention wins when four conditions jointly hold: live residual mass is concentrated rather than diffuse; the measured influence actually tracks the measured outcome; the habitat is stable enough that the influence persists through the cascade, including effects several steps out; and the target is reachable from the intervener's own closure. It loses when the mass is diffuse, the influence map is stale, the decisive effect is three steps away, or the goal flips the node's valence. The two strategies are arrangements of the same ingredients, so under the bond rule they can be isomers, and the framework stops at the shape rather than naming the node set. What it offers is the conjunction of conditions, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close each one. The specific nodes stay open until the ground settles them.
