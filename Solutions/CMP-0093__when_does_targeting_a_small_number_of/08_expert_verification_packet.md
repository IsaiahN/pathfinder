# Expert verification packet - the Tether on targeted versus distributed intervention

**What this is:** a claim in a form a network scientist or intervention researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No per-system intervention measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Influential node" is a role relative to a goal and a residual, not a property a node carries. Targeted intervention on a small node set outperforms broad distributed intervention only when four conditions jointly hold: live residual mass is concentrated rather than diffuse; the measured influence actually tracks the post-intervention outcome (bracket fidelity); the habitat is stable enough that the identified influence persists through the cascade, including effects several steps out; and the target is reachable from the intervener's own closure. The two strategies are arrangements of the same ingredients, so under the bond rule they can be isomers. The framework returns the conjunction of conditions and the falsifiers written in advance; it does not name which nodes to act on.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 7 and 11

- **Statement:** influence is valence-relative, and targeting has an object only where live residual mass is concentrated and the bracket is faithful.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 7 :37-39 - *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"*, *"the ground’s pressure on the room"*.
- **Refuter:** a node whose influence rank is invariant across a change of goal on the same network; or a targeted intervention that wins where live mass is demonstrably diffuse; or a targeted intervention that wins while the influence map is measured as stale and the outcome barely depends on the cascade.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against the intervention literature

- **Statement:** the same node ranks differently under different tasks, and small-node intervention is phase-bound. Albert, Jeong and Barabási 2000 report that scale-free networks are *"extremely vulnerable to attacks"* on a few nodes. Liu, Slotine and Barabási 2011 report that controllability driver nodes *"tend to avoid the high-degree nodes."* Sanhedrai et al. 2022 identify a *"recoverable phase"* in which a system can be *"reignited by microscopic interventions, for instance, controlling just a single node"*, after topological reconstruction. Broido and Clauset 2019 find *"strongly scale-free structure is empirically rare"* across nearly 1000 networks.
- **What to check:** do these results really range different tasks, rather than contradict each other? Is the concentration condition measured, not assumed, in the system at hand?
- **Refuter:** a single target set that is simultaneously optimal for attack, immunization and controllability across several real networks, with concentration measured and held fixed. That would collapse the task-relative reading.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a stated type limit

- **Statement:** targeted and distributed intervention are arrangements of the same atoms, so they can be isomers under Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If they share only the network and differ in which nodes occupy the action slots, the weaker functional-group claim holds (Fig 12 :123): *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **Refuter:** two interventions with identical atoms and identical bonds that nevertheless diverge in measured outcome with no difference in ground contact or imported atom. That moves the explanation out of the arrangement and into the habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal rule for targeting, and not a universal rule for distributed action.
- Not that degree, betweenness or any other centrality is the framework's answer. Their agreement is one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31, :40).
- Not that cost decides the winner. Cost is a budget filter (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the specific node set is named. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a theory of everything, not self-validating, and not `grounded`. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself."*

## 5. What would settle it

A ground measurement, not agreement. In order:

1. **Concentration test:** measure live residual mass per node, never as a global average. Is it concentrated in a few slots?
2. **Bracket test:** does the influence rank prospectively predict the measured outcome, or is it a pooled reading of the same adjacency?
3. **Cascade-depth test:** is the decisive residual first order, or several steps out?
4. **Phase test:** is the system inside the recoverable phase in which a microscopic intervention can work?
5. **Goal-flip test:** does the target set change when the goal changes?
