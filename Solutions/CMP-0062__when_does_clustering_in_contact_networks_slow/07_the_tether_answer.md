# The Tether's answer on clustering in contact networks

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical flip point and on which arrangement is present in a given network. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A contact pattern (structure) plus a state-transition regime (immunity) together predict an infection outcome, but the same scalar clustering coefficient is reported with opposite signs. Observed incidence and persistence, per cluster and per time, do not match a single "clustering effect". The contact is a **union compound**, not one hard question with two opposite answers: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). It has two anchors and needs two instruments.

## 2. The shape

1. **Clustering is an arrangement, not an ingredient count.** The global clustering coefficient records ingredients (closed triples) and drops the bond (how triangles share edges, how cliques bridge). Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Two networks with the same coefficient can be isomers with different epidemic behaviour (`06` Q1).
2. **Slow-down condition (C1): contacts are spent on already-reached slots.** When two of a node's contacts are themselves connected, infection arriving there finds them already exposed. The effective number of new susceptible slots per infection falls, so early growth and final size fall. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Anchor: observed incidence per cluster.
3. **Reservoir condition (C2): the closed path re-enters.** If recovery returns the host to susceptibility, or immunity wanes, the triangle becomes a cycle. The cluster keeps infection alive even as global incidence falls. Fig 11 :49: "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Anchor: observed fade-out versus endemicity and disease lifetime.
4. **They are two members of one functional group, not a dial.** Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The group is local cluster and correlated exposure; the qualifier is permanent removal versus re-entry. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.
5. **The flip is a threshold comparison, with a live type strain.** Persistence is decided at a comparison (⋛, threshold between two quantities), not by a smooth function of a coefficient. But the operands in the derivation are arrangements, and the Operators table restricts ⋛ to quantities. That strain (S2) is disclosed, not smoothed. No figure line returns a numerical flip point.
6. **Degree heterogeneity can move the two quantities in opposite directions.** Holding transmissibility fixed, clustering can slow an epidemic and still increase final size when the variance of the degree distribution also rises (Volz et al. 2011). The degree sequence is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal sign for clustering. The same structure has opposite valence under different immunity regimes.
- Not a numerical coefficient threshold, and not a claim that a transmission tree recovers the arrangement. Welch 2011 says it cannot do so independently of the degree sequence.
- Not a claim that group size is the cause. The lower-overlap ecology witness says spread is driven "by the social organization of interactions in a group and not its size" (Sah et al. 2018).
- Not a claim that one lever clears both conditions. A single intervention leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55).
- Not a derivation of epidemiology from the six physical terms. Fig 13 is used only for the network structure and the honest stop, after re-census of strain S1.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settled any claim here.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The questions are:

- **Regime test:** with degree sequence and transmissibility held fixed, does the same arrangement reduce early growth under permanent removal and lower the threshold under documented reinfection, as predicted? This is an experiment or a natural comparison, with observed incidence as anchor.
- **Arrangement test:** does a measure of motif structure (shared-edge triangles, clique-size distribution, community bridges) predict outcomes better than the scalar coefficient, prospectively and per cluster?
- **Re-entry test:** is reinfection or waning immunity actually present in the cluster reported as a reservoir? Without it, the cycle claim is unmeasured.
- **Instrument test:** can a published method recover the arrangement from outbreak data with calibrated error, confirmed by an independent contact census? This would close the declared `gap`.
- **Standing figure-level falsifiers:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five; admit a state-state or structure-structure composition that cannot be refused; or show that the ⋛ operands are bare scalars at every level (which would close S2 against this reading).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The separator says what an answer must look like and what would refute it. It does not name a flip-point number and does not identify the arrangement in a given observed network. The blog essay *The Library of Babel* gives the stop condition: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The record of the route is what keeps the result checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for "how much clustering is good". It says that clustering is an arrangement, not a scalar, so a single coefficient is a flat formula that drops the part that matters. It separates two outcomes with different anchors: clustering slows an outbreak when contacts are spent on neighbours the infection has already reached, depleting the local susceptible pool; and clustering keeps a disease alive when the same closed path becomes a cycle through reinfection or waning immunity, so the cluster acts as a reservoir. Those are two members of one functional group, distinguished by the immunity regime, not ends of one dial. The flip is a threshold comparison, and the question of what kind of thing can be an operand there is a live, disclosed strain. What the framework returns is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the gap. The number stays open until the ground pays for it.
