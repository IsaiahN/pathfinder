## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 12 :5/:94/:117/:121 (arrangement, isomer, cycle), Fig 11 :49-52 (valence, habitat), Fig 9 :54-57 (union), Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07`: clustering is a bond, slow-down is spent contacts, reservoir is a cycle, the two are a union with two anchors, no number is returned.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete answer about a given contact network: a coefficient value predicts an outbreak; a cluster is a reservoir; a policy should break up clusters.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **arrangement** behind the measured coefficient (disjoint triangles versus shared-edge motifs versus communities with bridges);
- the **immunity regime**, which is the sign of the loop and is not carried by the word "clustering";
- the **fixed-degree condition**, without which the measured clustering parameter is a proxy for degree variance;
- the **operand type of ⋛** (S2): the threshold comparison is between quantities, while the objects compared in the abstraction are arrangements.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: (i) a structure must be described by its bonds, not its ingredient count, and (ii) re-entry of infection is what turns a depleted cluster into a reservoir. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
