# Expert verification packet — the Tether on clustering in contact networks

**What this is:** a claim in a form a network epidemiologist or disease ecologist can check. Mode, derivation, strain and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical flip point and on arrangement identification. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Clustering in a contact network is an arrangement (a bond), not a scalar ingredient, so a global clustering coefficient is a flat formula that drops the arrangement and cannot by itself predict an epidemic. Two outcomes form a union with different anchors. Slow-down: when contacts are spent on neighbours the infection has already reached, the local susceptible pool is depleted and early growth and final size fall; anchor, observed incidence per cluster. Reservoir: when the same closed path re-enters infection through reinfection or waning immunity, the triangle becomes a cycle and the cluster persists; anchor, observed fade-out versus endemicity and disease lifetime. The two are members of one functional group distinguished by the immunity regime, not ends of one dial. The flip is a threshold comparison whose operand type is a live strain. No numerical flip point or arrangement identification is claimed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and network-science-facing; checkable by reading Fig 12 and by your own knowledge of contact-network models

- **Statement:** the scalar coefficient is a flat formula; the arrangement carries the epidemically relevant information. Two networks with the same ingredient list but different bond arrangement are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 — "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94 — "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117 — "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is it true in your field that two networks matched on degree distribution and global clustering coefficient can give materially different SIR outcomes? Rolls et al. 2015 and Volz et al. 2011 both say yes; Welch 2011 qualifies the effect size when degree sequence is held fixed.
- **Refuter:** matched networks (degree sequence, triangle count, clique-size distribution) give statistically indistinguishable growth rate, final size and disease lifetime across many runs. That breaks the isomer claim.

### C2 — epidemiology-facing; checkable against SIR-style results

- **Statement:** with the degree sequence held fixed, clustering slows spread because contacts are spent on already-reached susceptible slots.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 — "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 1 :22 — "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 12 :121 — "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What to check:** Volz et al. 2011 state "Clustering always slows an epidemic" and find longer time to peak; the 2025 pairwise review says regular contacts "reduce transmission due to local depletion of susceptibles" and clustering "may result in localized outbreaks before propagation through the broader network." Does that match what you see when degree variance is actually held fixed?
- **Refuter:** a fixed-degree, permanently removing system in which higher transitivity fails to reduce early growth or final size across an ensemble. That breaks C2.

### C3 — epidemiology/ecology-facing; checkable against SIS, SIRS and wildlife reservoir results

- **Statement:** the same triangle becomes a reservoir when infection re-enters; the two outcomes are a union, not one dial, and the immunity regime is the sign of the loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 — "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 — "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 9 :57 — "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **What to check:** Nadini et al. 2018 report that "while the presence of tightly connected clusters inhibits SIR processes, it speeds up SIS phenomena" and that "communities act as a reservoir for the disease and favour the contagion process pushing the epidemic threshold to smaller values." House and Keeling 2010 report more infectious-infectious pairs at a given endemic burden. Benton et al. 2016 and Marescot et al. 2021 give a lower-overlap ecology corroboration. Is the immunity regime the discriminator in your system?
- **Refuter:** a clustered SIS-like system with documented reinfection that shows no threshold shift and no longer lifetime than a degree-matched control; or a clustered SIR-like system that sustains endemicity without re-entry. That breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numerical clustering threshold or a universal coefficient value.
- Not that a transmission tree recovers the arrangement. Welch 2011 says it does not, independently of the degree sequence.
- Not that group size is the cause; spread is driven primarily by "the social organization of interactions in a group and not its size" (Sah et al. 2018).
- Not that one lever clears both conditions.
- Not that clustering is always bad or always good. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :51).
- Not that the operand type of the threshold comparison is settled. The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities"; here the operands are arrangements. Strain S2 is live and disclosed in `01` F4, `06` Q5, `09` and `11`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Regime test:** hold degree sequence and transmissibility fixed; does the same arrangement reduce early growth under permanent removal and lower the threshold under documented reinfection?
2. **Arrangement test:** does a motif-level measure predict outcomes prospectively and per cluster better than the scalar coefficient?
3. **Re-entry test:** is reinfection or waning immunity actually documented in the cluster called a reservoir?
4. **Instrument test:** can a published method recover the arrangement from outbreak data with calibrated error, confirmed by an independent contact census? This closes the declared `gap`.
5. **Type test:** show either that ⋛'s operands are quantities at every level (closing S2 against this reading) or that a settled arrangement can legitimately occupy one.
