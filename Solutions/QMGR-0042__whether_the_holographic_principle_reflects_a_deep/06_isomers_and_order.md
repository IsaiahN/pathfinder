# Is bulk-boundary duality an isomer? Order, bonds and level in the information-geometry question

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the per-domain claims, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit (`01`) leaves three support questions. (1) What is the dependency order of the two halves of the question, and where does the chain break? (2) Is the relation between the bulk and boundary descriptions an isomer, an identity, or a functional group under Fig 12's definitions? (3) What licenses a name for the mediating structure, and what is the falsifier?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Dependency order, and the broken link

Fig 3 :20-23, :25-27:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

For this question the chain runs: perception (we have entropy, area, Hilbert-space dimension as readings) → vocabulary (we have terms for cross-kind composition, membrane transform, level) → **objective** (what would count as settling "deep"?) → planning → learn and carry. The break is at the objective link. We can measure areas and entropies, and we can name the structural shape, but there is no instrument that distinguishes the ontological from the epistemic reading of the bound (Adlam 2023), and no instrument that certifies a bottom level (Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."*). Effort spent on further formal duality proofs lands above the break; it cannot use a ground that has not been asked.

- **Consequence:** report `unreached` at the objective link, not `unreachable-in-principle`. Fig 8 :40-43 separates the two, and importing a new primitive against a budget limit would cost more than starting from primitives.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit an instrument that discriminates the ontological and epistemic readings of the bound. Then the objective link works and the chain closes.

### Q2 - Isomer, identity or functional group?

Fig 12 :117, :124, :65-69:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
> **"two names, one referent"** and **"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."**

Three candidate readings, kept separate:

1. **Identity (≡).** If the bulk and the boundary are dual in the physicists' sense, they are two names for one physical referent, and ≡ applies. But Fig 12 :69 requires that substituting one for the other lose nothing, whereas Fig 4 :27 says the transform between scales is lossy and Fig 4 :31 calls the gap *"the only honest report of how much was given up."* So ≡ strains against the membrane transform.
2. **Isomer.** If bulk GR and the boundary CFT are treated as two *theories as written*, over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing in which structure mediates which state pair, then they are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is the checkable structural claim.
3. **Functional group.** As the two are usually written, GR's core (geometry over a region) and the CFT's core (ordering over a spectrum of states) share the same `state - structure - state` skeleton but different atoms in the slots. That is a functional group, not an isomer: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

**Re-census, not a smoothed strain.** The apparent identity-versus-loss conflict dissolves once the objects are separated. Fig 12's ≡ is a bond between two names *at one level*. Fig 4's transform is a mapping *between levels*, and its loss is the round-trip gap R_T. Bulk-boundary duality is a physics claim about equivalence of Hilbert spaces; the framework's ≡ is a claim about substitution at a level. Different objects, so no contradiction. But the framework does **not** certify the physicists' duality: it can only say that at the level where the relation is settled, the two descriptions are composable as one atom, and that the gap across the membrane must be measured (F3).

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if an exact invertible bulk-boundary map with zero round-trip gap is demonstrated, the isomer reading collapses into ≡ and F3's nonzero-gap prediction is refuted. If the two theories can be written without a shared atom set, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 - What names the mediating structure?

Fig 9 :30-32:

> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**
> **"→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

The physics frame offers three names, each with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`: entanglement (Van Raamsdonk 2010), minimal area surfaces (Ryu-Takayanagi 2006), and quantum error-correcting codes (Almheiri et al. 2015). The framework does not select among them. That selection is a binding from the physics frame onto held atoms or compositions, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). The honest status is `gap`: the framework returns the shape (a structure must mediate, and the relation has a measurable round-trip gap) but not the name.

- **Falsifier:** a source-discriminating instrument that shows which of the three (or a fourth) actually occupies the slot. Until then, naming one carries zero verdict weight.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The two conditions are (i) the existence of a lawful relation and (ii) the depth verdict. They have different anchors: (i) is anchored in the convergence of two independent physics frames (Bekenstein thermodynamics and AdS-CFT), (ii) is anchored in the figures' no-bottom rule. They cannot be merged by one lever. Showing the relation is generic in lattice models clears part of (i) and leaves (ii) untouched. Showing irreducibility is level-relative refuses (ii) and leaves (i) standing. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## CONCLUSION

The dependency chain for this question breaks at the objective link: we have readings and structural vocabulary, but no instrument that discriminates the ontological from the epistemic reading of the bound or certifies a bottom level. The bulk and boundary descriptions are best treated as **isomers over a shared atom set when read as two theories as written**, and as **one settled atom when read as a duality at the level where the relation is settled**; the identity reading is valid only at that level and must not be stretched into a claim about the membrane transform. The name of the mediating structure is a `gap`, filled by bindings with provenance and zero verdict weight until an instrument pays for one. The whole derivation is `general` on structure and `specified` per domain, not `grounded`.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the strong "deep fact" claim (a lawful, cross-kind, boundary-indexed relation with a measurable gap) without what fails (absolute irreducibility, a privileged boundary description, and a named microscopic source).
