## Question as an effect

Two options, stated by what each leads to.

- **Option A, "yes, a deep fact":** the entropy-area relation (S = A/4 in Planck units) discloses an irreducible, level-independent fact that information and geometry are the same underlying thing. This leads to a verdict of absolute depth, to the claim that geometry reduces to counting, and to the expectation that the relation must appear at the bottom of any final description.
- **Option B, "no, a contingent regularity":** the relation is a derived residue of a particular composition at a particular scale, perhaps generic to any local system with finite correlation length, and carries no warrant for absolute depth. This leads to a level-relative verdict, a shape claim instead of a name, and an honest stop at the instruments available.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a shape stating the *form* of the information-geometry relation (which kinds compose, across what boundary, with what round-trip gap), and a separate verdict on whether "deep" can mean level-independent irreducibility. See `01` section 3 (pre-registered refuter) and `07` section 6.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. A room does not upgrade the label; correlation is disqualifying for a verdict; and a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 2 :35, Fig 2 :40, Fig 10 :42, Fig 7 :35). Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, which is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
