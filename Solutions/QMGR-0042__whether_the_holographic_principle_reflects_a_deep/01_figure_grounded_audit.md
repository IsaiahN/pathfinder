## 0. The claim under audit

> Whether the holographic principle reflects a deep fact about how information relates to geometry.

Stated in effect terms, with the two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, yes:** the area law ( entropy of a region proportional to boundary area, S = A/4 in Planck units ) discloses an irreducible, level-independent fact that information and geometry are one underlying thing. Leads to: an absolute-depth verdict, a reduction of geometry to counting, and a bottom-level description.
- **Option B, no:** the relation is a derived residue of a particular composition at a particular scale, possibly generic to local systems with finite correlation length, and carries no warrant for absolute depth. Leads to: a level-relative verdict, a shape instead of a name, and a stop at the instruments available.

The physics content of the area law is NOT a figure line. It comes from the world sources actually read: Bousso 2002 (*"A universal relation between geometry and information is thus uncovered. It has yet to be explained."*), 't Hooft 1993 (*"Boolean variables defined on a two-dimensional lattice"*), Maldacena 1998 (bulk-boundary duality), Ryu and Takayanagi 2006 (entanglement entropy equals minimal area), Van Raamsdonk 2010 (*"the emergence of classically connected spacetimes is intimately related to the quantum entanglement of degrees of freedom"*). The figures decide only what form a lawful relation may have and what "deep" can mean. They do not derive the area law and are not presented as doing so.

**Term to bind before the run (direction discipline).** "Deep fact about how information relates to geometry" is the sought-for shape. Formula step "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question because that shape is not a term it holds. So I bind it as an ordinary term now: *a cross-kind composition whose relation is irreducible at the level where it is settled and composable as an atom above that level, with the boundary-indexing of its residual stated and its round-trip gap recorded.* That bound term is then refusable like anything else.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The figures name holography as a known case of triangulation (decisive for the relation, retrodiction only)

Fig 8 :56-57 (verbatim, the sentence spans two extracted lines and is quoted here as one):

> **"The triangulation claim fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward: Wiles and Frey, Montgomery and Dyson, Bekenstein and holography, the relativization barriers in complexity theory."**

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> **"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."**

Derivation. The framework itself lists "Bekenstein and holography" among four cases where two low-overlap frames converged on one result. The physics record matches: black-hole thermodynamics (Bekenstein, Hawking) and string theory / AdS-CFT (Maldacena, Susskind, 't Hooft) reached the area-entropy relation from different closures. By Fig 2, that convergence is evidence only if the frames are independent, which in this case they are: one came from thermodynamics of horizons, the other from large-N gauge theory and branes. So the existence of a lawful information-geometry relation is not a frame-internal artifact of the Tether framework; it is a case the framework acknowledges from outside itself.

**But** the same line says these cases *"has not been tested forward"* and Fig 4 :25 warns that *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The framework did not predict holography. It fits it after the fact. The corroboration is therefore a retrodiction and must be labelled as such. It cannot be used as if the framework had derived the area law.

- **Mode:** `general` on the existence of a relation, `specified` that the two frames are low-overlap.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 8 triangulation case, Fig 2 independence rule, with the physics record as a secondary witness).
- **Refuter:** show that the Bekenstein line and the AdS-CFT line draw on one evidence pool (for example, that one was derived from the other rather than reached independently), or show the framework's claim to fit the case is a post-hoc labelling with no structural match. If either holds, F1 falls to single-source.

### F2 - The area law is a boundary-indexed residual, and the framework indexes residuals per slot, not by aggregate

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Derivation. The Bekenstein-Hawking result says the information content of a region is bounded by its *surface area*, not its volume (Bousso 2002: *"the area of any surface limits the information content of adjacent spacetime regions"*). A naive volume count aggregates over bulk cells and yields an extensive entropy; the area law says that aggregation throws away the live signal. This is a structural agreement with the framework's per-slot indexing rule: what is finite is indexed on the boundary, and the boundary is where the count terminates.

**Strain check and honest narrowing.** The framework's "slot" is a tracked object in an agent's perception loop. Equating a Planck-area cell with a framework slot is a binding from the physics frame, not a derivation. I keep the shape-level claim (indexing matters, aggregation hides the signal) and I do **not** assert that the framework's R is literally the entropy density. The line governs *how a residual must be indexed*, not the numerical coefficient 1/4.

Fig 13 :68 (*"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"*) and :59 (*"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"*) corroborate at the substrate level: counting configurations requires the network structure, and finiteness in a finite region requires energy. Information here is a composition over held atoms, not a new primitive.

- **Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` for the physics binding, ungrounded.
- **Status:** single-source on the figure side for the indexing rule (Fig 1), triangulated with Fig 13 for the counting substrate.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where aggregating over slots preserves a residual that per-slot indexing loses, or show that counting configurations does not require the network atom. Either breaks the mapping.

### F3 - Bulk to boundary is a transform across a scale membrane, and the round trip has a gap

Fig 4 :27, :30, :31-32, :33:

> **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
> **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**
> **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**

Derivation. The holographic principle relates a d-dimensional bulk to a (d-1)-dimensional boundary. That is a transform between scales in the framework's sense. Fig 4 says such a transform is lossy and that the loss is measured, not assumed away, by a round trip: carry a description up, bring it back down, and measure R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). The live physics problem of **bulk reconstruction** (Almheiri, Dong and Hartnoll 2015: *"the question of whether bulk operator reconstruction works only in the causal wedge or all the way to the extremal surface"*) is exactly the measurement of that gap. The framework therefore makes a checkable prediction: the bulk-boundary map has a nonzero, measurable round-trip gap, and the substance of holography is the structure of that gap, not an identity of the two descriptions.

Honest caveat, not smoothed over: in AdS-CFT the bulk and boundary are believed to be *dual*, that is, to encode the same information. Calling the map "lossy" is the framework's claim about what a coarser description cannot hold at a level, not a claim that information is destroyed in the duality. The type of the gap, whether it is a loss of information or a difference of descriptive variables, is a real question, and it is the question the bulk-reconstruction program is answering. I record it as an open measurement, not a settled identity.

- **Mode:** `general`, with a `specified`, ungrounded prediction of a nonzero R_T.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 membrane plus Fig 13 :104-105 level-relative irreducibility), with the physics as corroboration only.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate an exact invertible bulk-boundary map with zero round-trip gap for all states and all subregions. That would refute the nonzero-gap prediction. It would not by itself refute the area law.

> **"So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

> **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**
> **"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."**
> **"infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

Derivation. Option A asks for "deep" as an absolute, level-independent property: the holographic principle would be *the* bottom fact, irreducible in every description. The framework structurally refuses that object. Irreducibility is assigned by a level, there is no bottom and no top, and a terminal all-explaining frame is ruled out definitionally (canonical README, section "This is not a Theory of Everything"). So the question "is it a deep fact?" is malformed as stated. The re-grounded, answerable question is: at what level is the relation settled, what composes over it there, and what would a lower level have to look like? That is a `specified` question with a shape answer. The absolute-depth reading is a `gap`, reported as a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).

This is the key move of the whole audit: the *yes/no* is refused, and the refusal is derived, not rhetorical.

- **Mode:** `general` structural refusal, `specified` for the re-grounded level question.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 recursion plus Fig 4 no-top plus canonical README no-ToE).
- **Refuter:** derive the six-term set from a genuinely deeper primitive that is not itself a composition at some level, or show a frame can certify its own bottom. Fig 8 :37 says *"A frame cannot certify its own limit"*; a demonstration that it can would break this finding.

### F5 - Discreteness and the area law are composition residues, not a seventh primitive

Fig 13 :124-125 and :115-116:

> **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."**
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Derivation. The "bits per Planck area" feature looks like an added primitive: information as a new fundamental ingredient alongside space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network. Fig 13 says no. Discreteness is a remainder that a composition leaves, like smoothness and chaos, and the six-term set is closed under arrangement. So the holographic principle does not require importing a seventh kind to accommodate information. It is a composition over the held atoms: space (a region), energy (finiteness makes the count terminate), network (things relate and configurations are countable), and spectrum (ordering, so more and less can be said).

This also explains a world-source tension without flipping the verdict. Wolf et al. 2008 (PRL, abstract obtained via the search integration, direct fetch 403) report that an area law *"not only emerges in the search for new Planck-scale laws but also in lattice models of classical and quantum physics"* and is *"generally implied by a finite correlation length when measured in terms of the mutual information."* If area laws are generic to local lattice systems, the area law is not special to quantum gravity. The framework predicted exactly this: a residue that appears at many levels is a *composition* residue, not a primitive (Fig 13 :124). The world result therefore corroborates Fig 13 rather than threatening the verdict. It does, however, weaken any claim of *uniqueness* for the holographic principle as a deep fact.

- **Mode:** `general` structural, with the Wolf result as `specified`, unmeasured corroboration, single witness.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 13 :124 plus Fig 13 :115, and THE_FORMULA substrate section).
- **Refuter:** derive one of the six from information-theoretic discreteness alone (which would make discreteness a primitive and the set non-minimal), or show the area law is genuinely unique to gravity and absent in finite-correlation-length lattice models. The second is a physics question, not a figure question.

### F6 - Information and geometry are different kinds and cannot bond by direct identity

Fig 13 :74-75 and :35-37:

> **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
> **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
> **"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network."**

Derivation. Geometry in the six-term set is space, a **state** (Fig 13 :9-10: *"near, far, between, contained"*). Information, as counting configurations and ordering more and less, is built from **structures**, network and spectrum. The two are different kinds, so by Fig 13 :74-75 they cannot stand in a direct identity; a term must mediate the composition. The framework returns the **shape** of that relation, a structure between the geometric state and the informational structure, but it does not name the structure. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The physics frame names candidates: entanglement (Van Raamsdonk 2010), minimal surfaces (Ryu-Takayanagi 2006), quantum error correction (Almheiri et al. 2015). Those names are bindings from D2 onto held atoms, with provenance to be recorded (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*).

- **Mode:** `general` that a structure must mediate, `specified` on the shape, no name asserted.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 13 cross-kind rule plus Fig 9 shape limit).
- **Refuter:** show a direct state-information identity is composable under the six-term set, or show that information is neither a structure nor built from one. Either breaks the cross-kind shape.

### F7 - Two world frames disagree about whether the principle is "deep", and both dissolve back into the census

Three world sources conflict on the interpretation, and the conflict rule sends each back to the census rather than flipping the verdict.

1. **De Haro, Cinti and Corbeel 2026** (arXiv:2608.26282,forthcoming in a World Scientific festschrift) argue that information is *"a substantive guiding principle"* and that holographic bounds *"function as theory selection criteria that constrain viable quantum gravity theories"*, and that *"holography functions as a principle of theoretical equivalence: the bulk and boundary theories must describe the same physical content"*. This reads as a strong "deep" claim.
2. **Fields, Glazebrook and Marcianò 2022** (*Quanta* 11, 72-96, arXiv:2210.16021) argue the holographic principle is *"a straightforward consequence of the quantum information theory of separable systems"* and that *"Principles equivalent to the HP appear in both computer science and the life sciences, suggesting that the HP is not just a fundamental principle of physics, but of all of science."* This also reads as "deep", but in a domain-agnostic sense: the principle is wide, not necessarily bottom-level.
3. **Adlam 2023** (*Philosophy of Physics*, arXiv:2303.10781) distinguishes *"ontological and epistemic interpretations of the covariant bound"* and explicitly declines to advocate either. She quotes Smolin's reading that *"nature is fundamentally discrete"* and that *"geometry must turn out to reduce to counting"*, while also observing that an epistemic reading is possible. This is an open direction-of-explanation question.

Re-census. The apparent conflict is between "deep as bottom-level" (source 1) and "deep as cross-domain generality" (source 2), with source 3 refusing the Direction question. Figures 4 and 13 dissolve it: domain-agnostic recurrence across many fields is exactly what a recursive metatheory predicts (Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*), and it says nothing about a bottom level, which Fig 12 :104 and Fig 4 :33 rule out. So source 2 corroborates the *recursive* reading, not an absolute-depth verdict; source 1's "theory selection criterion" is a filter for the budget of theory choice, never a witness for truth (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*); source 3's open Direction question is precisely the limit Fig 9 :30-31 names: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*

No verdict was flipped. All three sources are secondary and share the physics evidence pool to some degree; they are one discussion, not three independent witnesses. The philosophy sources (1 and 3) are genuinely lower-overlap with the physics papers in *frame*, but not in underlying evidence.

- **Mode:** `general` on the dissolution, `specified` per source reading.
- **Status:** single-source per world claim; the figure-side dissolution is triangulated.
- **Refuter:** a world source that shows the principle is *both* domain-specific and bottom-level with a certificate of irreducibility. That would require a frame to certify its own limit, which Fig 8 :37 forbids.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written here before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One of the six terms is derived from the other five, or a seventh member is derived from information-theoretic discreteness alone (breaks Fig 13 minimality and closure).

6. A source-discriminating instrument measures the area law to be extensive in a regime where the framework predicts boundary-indexing (breaks F2 at the ground).
