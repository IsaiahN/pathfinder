# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding what the debate is about

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter below survive the reader's own check. The two-stage offer collapses if stage 1 fails on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on the cognition versus culture question in religion

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms:** why do religious and spiritual beliefs recur across cultures in recognizable forms, and why do the same beliefs vary so sharply in content and prevalence? One literature explains the recurrence with general cognitive tendencies. Another explains the variation with institutions and histories. The output says the question is not a contest between them.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the rest):** this is a case in two conditions, not one harder question. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. A cognitive only account predicts the recurrent forms but leaves the local content unexplained. An institution and history only account predicts the local content but cannot derive the recurrent forms without borrowing them. The explanation has to be a typed relation between the two, not a list of factors that both matter. Which relation holds is left open until a cross cultural reading can discriminate it.
>
> Capability is a property of agent and habitat, never of the agent alone. On this reading, the cognitive tendency is the agent and the institution and history are the habitat. Improving the agent in isolation does not change contact, and an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a particular cognitive mechanism is the cause, that a particular institution is the cause, that the two factors contribute equally, or that I have measured their weights. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Agreement between cognitive scientists and historians who read the same ethnographic corpus is one evidence pool talking, not two confirmations. If your evidence and theirs are disjoint and you each predict the other's residual, that would be corroboration. Otherwise it is a convention.
>
> **What would refute the output** (written before this message):
> 1. one model with a single parameter predicts both recurrence and local content on held out cultures, with no residual left on either;
> 2. a cognitive only model with no transmission parameters predicts the distribution of specific contents on held out populations;
> 3. a history only model with no cognitive parameter predicts the recurrent forms;
> 4. the union of the two frames adds no predictive surplus over the better single frame on held out data.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The formula and the mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public figures repository (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
