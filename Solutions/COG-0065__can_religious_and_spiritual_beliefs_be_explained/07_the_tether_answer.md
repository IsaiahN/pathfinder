# The Tether's answer on cognitive tendencies versus institutions and histories

**Mode: `general` on the structural refusal of the either/or, `specified` on the shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Religious and spiritual beliefs recur across cultures in recognizable forms and vary sharply in content and prevalence. General cognitive tendencies predict the recurrence; institutions and histories predict the variation. R is measured per slot, one belief form in one population at one time, and is never averaged into one global score. The two conditions overlap, so this is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

## 2. The shape, per domain, verdicts kept separate

1. **The either/or is refused.** D4, `general`, triangulated. Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The question asks which candidate does more work on a bundled outcome. The figures require unbundling the union before search. This is a refusal of the question form, not a choice of side.

2. **Cognitive tendencies are necessary for the recurrent forms and insufficient for the local content.** D1, `specified`, triangulated. Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." A cognitive-only frame cannot derive a particular historical content from inside its own closure.

3. **Institutions and histories are necessary for which variants persist and insufficient for the recurrent forms.** D2, `specified`, triangulated. Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." A historical frame reads its actors off the world; it needs the cognitive shape imported from a closure that differs (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs.").

4. **The explanation is a typed bond, not a list of factors.** D4, `specified`, triangulated. Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." "Cognition and culture both matter" records the ingredients and loses the relation that does the explanatory work.

5. **Which bond holds is a `gap`.** D4, `general` shape only. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" Operators :68 "A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet." The figures license the shape of a typed cross-kind composition. They do not select the bond, and no measurement here discriminates them. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.

6. **Consensus between the two literatures is not a verdict.** D3, `general`, triangulated. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not" Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Agreement is corroboration only if the two frames draw on disjoint evidence and each predicts the other's residual.

7. **The numerical weighting is unreachable at this budget.** D3, `general`. Fig 9 :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides" Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Nothing in the figure set returns a percentage for cognition versus history.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that either side wins. No figure line selects a winner, and a consensus narrative is not a substitute for an anchor.
- Not that cognition and history contribute equally. Fig 13 :4 "The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward," governs physical primitives, not two research frames.
- Not a specific cognitive mechanism as *the* cause, and not a specific institution as *the* cause. These are bindings from the world frame and need provenance (Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ.").
- Not `grounded`. No cross-cultural instrument was run here. This is a shape and a route.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

An anchor neither literature can move. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Bond discrimination.** Does recruitment run from cognitive bias to institutional form, or from institutional selection to cognitive variant? A held-out cross-cultural design with disjoint evidence pools could discriminate the two bonds. Until then the slot stays `???`.
- **Union surplus test.** Does the two-frame union predict recurrence and local content better than the better single frame on held-out cultures? If not, F5's shape falls (Fig 8 :12-15 blend collapse).
- **Isomer test.** Over the full shared atom set, do the two accounts differ only in bond? If either can be written without an atom the other needs, the isomer reading falls to a functional-group reading (Fig 12 :117, :123).
- **Standing refuters.** The six pre-registered conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3, written before the conclusion. A single-parameter model covering both slots, a cognitive-only model covering content, a history-only model covering recurrence, no union surplus, a bond the seven tests reject, or a discriminating instrument that contradicts the shape, each refutes a specific finding.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable at any price.** A universal claim about "religion" is itself a compression whose axes were chosen by a particular sensorium. *They're Scanning Books*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Declaring primitives "does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot." The answer stops at the limit of the instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between cognitive tendencies and institutions. It says the question is a case in two conditions, not one harder question: cognitive mechanisms are the agent and institutions and histories are the habitat, and capability is a property of agent and habitat together. A cognitive-only account predicts why supernatural concepts recur but not which ones took hold here; a history-only account predicts what persisted here but has to borrow the recurrent forms from the cognitive frame. What a complete explanation needs is a typed bond between the two sets of atoms, not a list in which both factors matter. Which bond holds is left open until a cross-cultural reading with independent evidence pools discriminates it. The framework returns that shape, the refuters written in advance and the instruments that would close it. It does not return a percentage, and it does not count agreement between the two literatures as a verdict.
