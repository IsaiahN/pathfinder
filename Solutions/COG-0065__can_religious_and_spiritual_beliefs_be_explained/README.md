# Solution COG-0065

**Question:** Can religious and spiritual beliefs be explained through general cognitive tendencies, or do institutions and histories do more explanatory work?

**Source:** cognitive science.txt line 80

**Section:** 7. Morality, religion, and meaning

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural refusal of the either/or framing. Nothing here is `grounded`: no cross-cultural instrument was run in this session.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Question restated in effect terms.** Two candidate explanations are offered for the same observed thing: religious and spiritual beliefs recur across cultures in recognizable forms, and the same beliefs vary sharply in content and prevalence across times and places. Option A (general cognitive tendencies) predicts the recurrence; option B (institutions and histories) predicts the local variation. The decision is not which option wins but what shape a complete explanation must have if it is to predict both the residual of recurrence and the residual of variation. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a typed composition of cognitive operands and institutional-historical operands that predicts recurrence and variation per slot, with the specific bond left open until the ground settles it.

**Boundary.** Nothing in this folder changes any file outside it. Figure quotes come from the local verbatim extract `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`. World sources were fetched and are listed with author, venue, year, access date and a quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with either side of the debate.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No verdict on which side of the real academic debate is correct. The figures decide the shape of the answer, not the empirical weighting of cognition versus history. "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." (Fig 9 :60)

The first contact carries the residual and its refuter. The framework is offered only after a reader has recorded a verdict on stage 1. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
