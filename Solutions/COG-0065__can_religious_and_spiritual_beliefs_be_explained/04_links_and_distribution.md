## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Atran and Norenzayan (2004)** do not claim religion is a single adaptation. They call it a "recurring cultural by-product" set by "cognitive, emotional, and material conditions." That is a two-condition shape from a source independent of the framework. It corroborates the union reading, but it is a secondary witness and its evidence pool overlaps with the cognitive science literature generally.
2. **The Wikipedia field entry on dual inheritance** states that "cultural traditions have recruited such beliefs to motivate prosocial behaviour." This is the bond claim in the vocabulary of the field: a typed relation (recruitment) rather than an additive list. It corroborates F5's shape while leaving the specific bond unmeasured.
3. **DiMaggio and Powell (1983)**, from organizational sociology, show that institutions make their members alike through "coercive, mimetic and normative" pressure. This is a genuinely low-overlap witness for the institutional frame: it explains local uniformity without appealing to individual cognitive mechanisms. It does not explain the recurrent forms, which is exactly F3.
