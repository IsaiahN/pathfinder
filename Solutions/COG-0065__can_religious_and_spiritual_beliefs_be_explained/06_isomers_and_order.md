# Isomers and order: the two frames as bonds over a shared atom set

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three structural questions open:

1. Is the compound a union or a partition, and what is its order (Fig 9 :54-57)?
2. Can a cognitive-dominant and an institution-dominant explanation be written as isomers over the same atom set, or are they only members of a functional group (Fig 12 :117, :123)?
3. Does the comparative phrase "do more explanatory work" name a bond the figures license, or a quantity nobody has measured (Operators :49)?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the world frame, not in the framework's six-term library: evolved agency detection, folkpsychological inference, memory advantage for minimally counterintuitive material, transmission chains, ritual and textual institutions, contingent historical events. Bonds held: the seven, including + conjunction, → sequence and ⋛ comparison. No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :22-23). Per Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed," the institutional atoms are read off the world rather than composed from the cognitive ones.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- The two conditions overlap: cognitive biases are transmitted through institutions, and institutions recruit biases. Asking which does "more" work treats the overlap as noise. **Union**, with a bond to be identified. Not a partition. `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 8 :45.

### Q2. Are the two accounts isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As usually written, the cognitive account holds {agency detection, memory bias} and the institutional account holds {transmission chain, ritual institution, historical event}. They share almost no atoms. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a broader family with different contents.
- Stronger form: over the full shared atom set {agency detection, folkpsychology, memory bias, transmission chain, ritual institution, historical event}, a "cognition recruits institutions" account and an "institutions select cognitive variants" account differ only in which typed bond connects the same atoms. Under Fig 12 :117 they **are** isomers, and the difference is decisive in behaviour even though the ingredient list is identical.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either account can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, an institutional account that needs no cognitive mechanism, or a cognitive account that needs no transmission term), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the same test pattern used in the exemplar's isomer derivation.

### Q3. What order does the compound have?

- Fig 12 :30-31 defines → sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened." Fig 12 :55-57 defines + conjunction: "both hold at once."
- "Cognitive tendencies produced institutions" is a → claim. "Both are present in every case" is a + claim. They are different terms with the same ingredients, and the choice is not recoverable from the ingredients (Fig 12 :46).
- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A general cognitive method can be carried up and reapplied; a particular historical occasion cannot. That gives a direction to the *method* crossing between scales, but not to the causal order of a given belief's formation. Do not read one against the other.
- **Strain named, not hidden:** the phrase "do more explanatory work" reads as ⋛, and Operators :49 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities." Explanatory weight is not yet a measured quantity here. The comparison is a `gap` until an instrument produces weights, not a Figure-licensed operator on unmeasured terms.

### Q4. What would close the order question?

- Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- A held-out cross-cultural prediction from each frame separately, with disjoint evidence pools, would supply the discriminating reading. Convergence under those conditions is "corroboration at maximum independence" (Fig 8 :50). Shared corpora would not.

## CONCLUSION

The two frames form a **union compound** with a typed bond whose identity is left open. As usually written they are a functional group with different atoms; over the full shared atom set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The comparative "more work" is a ⋛ on an unmeasured quantity and is marked as a `gap`, not admitted as a figure-licensed verdict. This rescues what is defensible in the common answer (both sets of atoms are needed) without the part that fails (an unmeasured winner and an unnamed bond).
