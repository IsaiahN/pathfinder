# Solution ECO-0010: figure-grounded package

**Question:** How much biodiversity is required to maintain essential ecosystem functions under rapid environmental change?

**Source:** ecology.txt line 13

**Section:** 1. Biodiversity, stability, and resilience

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions. No measurement settles the binding here, so nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this work.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §0 and `05_what_shape_means.md` §6.

## What this folder decides, in effect terms

Two options were carried through every file.

- **A:** there is a universal quantity of biodiversity (a species count, a diversity index, a percentage of a baseline) sufficient to maintain essential ecosystem functions under rapid change, and it can be stated before the functions and the change scenarios are named.
- **B:** the required diversity is a per-function, per-place, per-change quantity, obtained by enumerating the habitat in contact with each named function's residual and by keeping the number reversible as the residual moves.

The figures decide for B as a shape and refuse A as a framing. They do not supply a number. No universal minimum is asserted anywhere in this folder.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No universal species number, no single diversity threshold, and no claim that the figures can produce one.

Reason, in the figures' own words: Fig 1 :22 says "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average", and Fig 9 :60 says "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A number selected for agreeableness is a filter promoted to a verdict.
