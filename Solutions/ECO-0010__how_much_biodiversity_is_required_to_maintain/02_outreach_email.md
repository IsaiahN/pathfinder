# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the refuter below have survived the recipient's own check. The offer collapses on contact if the first stage fails.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about "how much biodiversity is enough"

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one claim I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the predictions; not `grounded`):** the question "how much biodiversity is required to maintain essential ecosystem functions under rapid environmental change" bundles several different questions. A single portable number cannot carry the answer. What can be stated is a shape:
>
> 1. name one function and an instrument that reads it;
> 2. name the change scenario you are asking about;
> 3. enumerate what is in contact with that function, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering;
> 4. state what observation would reverse the resulting requirement.
>
> The reason is structural: residuals are indexed per slot and averaging across slots discards the signal that says where to look. Also, an actor's value is relative to the function being tracked, so a list of "essential" species does not survive a change of conditions. This is a claim about the form an answer must have, not a claim that biodiversity is unimportant and not a number.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is no relationship between diversity and stability; that any particular count is right or wrong; that functional redundancy is absent; that the framework can name which species matter. Those are ground questions for your field. The literature I read reports that diversity often stabilises ecosystem processes, and that the strength and the threshold depend on the function, the place, the time and the change scenario. I take that as evidence for the shape, not against it.
>
> **What would refute this claim, written before I sent it:**
> 1. one number predicts maintenance of every named function under every rapid-change scenario at the same threshold, regardless of which species are present;
> 2. a fixed roster of species keeps the same signs across a change of residual;
> 3. a measured under-change result shows a fixed roster, not response diversity in contact, maintained the function;
> 4. a universal target computed before any function measurement predicts the outcome at the ground.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three short sources: the verbatim figure text with line numbers, the canonical usage note, and the insurance-hypothesis paper that first formalised the dependence of saturation on species responses. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]
