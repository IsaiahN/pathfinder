## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is order a property of the kinds or of the composition?

- Fig 13 :4-5: "The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other." The six have no rank.
- Fig 13 :12 defines time as "the ordered succession of differences". The ordering belongs to the succession, not to a privileged kind.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*, Rule 1 demonstration: at the level of the kinds "we cannot say which state composed first, and we don't know if the order mattered", because "at this level of the set order is only introduced as a concept when it comes to composition".
- Operators table: `→` is sequence, "A then B; B needs A to have happened", an ordered bond. `⋛` is comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities", also ordered.
- Conclusion: a first moment would be a property of a *composition*, not of the substrate. The substrate supplies order only when kinds cross. So the question "did time begin" is a question about the first term of a composition, which is where the no-bottom rule bites. `general`, triangulated (Fig 13 plus the derivation narrative, low overlap).

### Q2. Are the four accounts isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Read over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}`, the four accounts use the same six atoms and differ in which structure mediates which state pair and in what happens at the boundary. They are therefore candidates for Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different observable behaviour.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any account requires an atom the others do not (for example a new primitive at the boundary, rather than a different arrangement of the six), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands instead. An import that adds an atom moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom"), which is a different verdict from an isomer.
- Status: `specified`, unmeasured. The figure definition is triangulated (Fig 12 :117 and :124); the physics classification is single-source from the world search and remains unmeasured.

### Q3. What is the order of the union of accounts?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Each account is settled to a different degree: the GR description is well confirmed in its domain, the quantum accounts are not. Only settled molecules can become atoms at the next level, so the four cannot simply be stacked as peers.
- Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." Any account that writes a bare time boundary with no mediating structure is illegible at first order; it must pass through a structure.
- Conclusion: the union is **second order and comparative**. The comparison of "first element present" versus "first element absent" is a `⋛` threshold relation between two quantities that are themselves second-order molecules. Type strain remains live: the Operators table restricts ⋛ to "a threshold relation between two quantities", and whether settled molecules count as quantities at that level needs an instrument. ### Q4. Does a first moment break the lockstep?

- Fig 13 :4-5 says the two kinds are in lockstep and no member is senior. A first term of the succession would be a privileged stratum, which is the same violation F2 names in `01` via Fig 12 :104's "there is no bottom".
- So "a first moment" is not a smaller version of "an early moment". It is categorically different: it would be a boundary of the recursion rather than a term inside it. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- `general`, single-source for the figure rule, confirmed by the no-bottom line.

## CONCLUSION

Order enters the six-term set only at composition, so "time has a first moment" is a claim about a first element of a composition, and the no-bottom rule governs it. The four early-universe accounts are candidate isomers over the shared atom set, with a falsifier stated in Q2. Their union is second order and comparative, with a live type strain on ⋛.
