# Outreach draft: check the shape before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the split and the no-bottom claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F2 and F4 are stated plainly. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the Big Bang boundary, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the reading, `gap` on the physical question).** A framework I am testing defines time as "the ordered succession of differences" rather than as a container. Its composition rule has no first stratum and no last stratum. From that rule it follows that a *first moment* in the ordered succession cannot be a licensed term of the framework: a beginning of time, taken as a first level of the composition, would be a bottom, and the framework has no bottom. What it can license is a boundary of composition, a limit at which the structures that supply the differences stop composing, with no primitive first element.
>
> I also keep one question separate. Whether the universe's classical description has a past boundary is a physics question with a real anchor (the cosmic microwave background, light elements, geodesic incompleteness). The framework has no figure line that settles it, so I mark that question `gap` for the framework and let the instruments answer it. The singularity as *encountered by a theory* looks like a saturated channel, not a beginning of the ground. That part is `specified`, not measured.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework proves the universe began, that it proves there was a bounce or a prior phase, that it names what happened at the Planck scale, or that it replaces general relativity. "A structure must sit between space and time" is a structural constraint; what sits there is a binding from physics, not a derivation. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a first or privileged stratum of the composition is admitted and cannot be refused;
> 2. time is derived from the other five terms, or shown not to be an ordered succession of differences;
> 3. a measurement returns a reading from beyond the past boundary that itself has a first moment with no predecessor;
> 4. the physical boundary question and the first-moment question are shown to be one condition, so the split fails;
> 5. a turn of the composition is shown that leaves no remainder re-entering the next.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
