## 0. The claim under audit

Two readings are in play, and the question bundles them.

> **Reading A (ground boundary):** the Big Bang singularity is a real first boundary of the world. The scale factor goes to zero, curvature and density diverge, and time itself begins at that boundary. There is a first moment with no predecessor.
>
> **Reading B (channel limit):** the singularity is where the describing frame, classical general relativity plus the FLRW model, stops returning well-defined readings. The ground does not begin there; the channel saturates there. Whether the ordered succession of differences has a first element is a separate, structural question.

The audit does not pick between these by preference. It states the residual, censuses the domains, quotes the figure lines that actually bear, and keeps the verdicts separate.

### The bound sought-for shape (Direction discipline)

The formula warns that "the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds" and that a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question". The remedy is to write the shape down as an ordinary term. Bound here, before the run:

- **S1 (first moment):** a first element in the ordered succession of differences, with no earlier element. This is the shape Reading A asserts.
- **S2 (boundary of composition):** a limit at which the structures that supply the differences stop composing, so the succession has no further term, and no primitive first term either. This is the shape Reading B asserts.
- **S3 (channel saturation):** a state of the describing frame in which its quantities diverge and its readings lose resolution, while the ground is untouched.

These three are bound and will accrue residuals below. S1 is the preference most readers bring to the question; binding it makes it refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Residual and domain census

### 1.1 The residual R in effect terms

R is a divergence at the past boundary of the current description: the FLRW scale factor tends to zero, energy density and curvature grow without bound, and past-directed timelike and null geodesics are inextendible. The effect is that the frame's own quantities stop paying. Two things are bundled in the single question: (i) a geometric divergence in the model, and (ii) a topological claim about time having a first moment. R is therefore a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

- **Arity:** two conditions (divergence, temporal firstness), overlapping.
- **Symmetry:** the boundary is directional, not a symmetric join. It is a past edge, reachable in one time orientation only, so it behaves like the ordered bond `→`, not the symmetric bonds `+`, `∥`, or `≡`.
- **Scale:** first order inside the GR/FLRW model; at the limit, the composition itself ceases to be definable, which is a structural question about the six-term set, not a larger first-order quantity.

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Verdicts are therefore kept per domain and never averaged.

## 3. Findings

### F1. Time is a state defined by succession, not a container (governs S1)

Fig 13 :12 names time as "the ordered succession of differences". The Symbols table calls it "The ordered succession of differences." A first moment is therefore not an extra item placed inside a container; it would be an element of the ordering with no earlier element. The shape S1 is statable and now accrues a residual: nothing in the six-term set supplies a first element. Fig 13 :29-32 says each turn "leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next", so the succession is fed by what the previous turn discarded. A first term of that succession is not licensed. Mode: `general`, structural. Triangulated across Fig 13 :12, :29-32 and the Symbols table definition (low overlap: substrate definition versus recursion description). Refuter: derive a first moment from the six-term set, or show time is a primitive that does not require differences.

### F2. There is no bottom (decisive against S1 as a structural claim)

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom". Fig 4 :33: "Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress." A beginning of time, taken as a first stratum of the composition, is exactly a bottom. The framework forbids both a bottom and a top definitionally. So S1 cannot be a conclusion of this framework: it would require a privileged first level that the recursion rule removes. S2, a boundary of composition with no primitive first term, survives. Mode: `general`. Triangulated across Fig 12 :104 and Fig 4 :33 (different figures, different ends of the chain, low overlap). Refuter: admit a privileged first stratum that cannot be refused, or derive one of the six from the other five.

### F3. The channel, not the ground, loses contact (governs S3)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". The signature of a channel in trouble is given at Fig 10 :25-28: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings", with the output being "you have lost touch". A Big Bang singularity, as encountered by a physical theory, is a saturated metric and a dropped transient in this sense: the description's quantities diverge and cease to resolve what happens next. Fig 9 :40-41 requires the two cases to be kept apart: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions." A divergence in the model is not, by itself, a beginning of the ground. Mode: `specified` (a reading of the observation, not a measurement). Single-source for the figure mechanism (Fig 10), corroborated by world sources in `09` (SEP Philosophy of Cosmology: GR "comes to an end", which is a channel claim). Refuter: a reading returned from beyond the boundary that itself has a first moment of the ordered succession.

### F4. The bundle must be split before either half is searched

Fig 9 :55 and :57 (quoted in section 1.1) govern D1 and D3. Asking one question "did time begin at the Big Bang" mixes a physical boundary condition with a topological claim. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Once split, D1 is answered by instruments and proofs (world anchor), D2 by the figures, D3 by argument, and D4 by an outside frame. Mode: `general`. Triangulated across Fig 9 :54-57 and the world distinction recorded by the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* entry on Time (universe beginning versus time beginning). Refuter: show the two conditions are one, so that settling the physical boundary settles the topological firstness without remainder.

### F5. Strain test: the "nothing returns" line does NOT govern the singularity

An early draft of this audit applied Fig 6 :34-35 to the Big Bang: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." That reading strains. At the Big Bang something is already returning readings: the cosmic microwave background, light-element abundances, the Hubble flow. The singularity is not a place where nothing ever registered; it is the limit of an instrument that already works. Strain test rule: a line that fits only with a strain governs another quantity, so re-census. Re-census result: Fig 6 :34-35 governs the search for a *proto-instrument* where no reading exists at any resolution. It does not govern a boundary at the edge of a working instrument. The correct inward track here is Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms." No strain after re-census. Kept visible as F5 rather than silently dropped.

### F6. No closure certifies its own boundary

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Applied here: the fact that GR's equations stop returning values past the boundary is not a proof that the world begins there. It is a report of the reach of GR, and it must be stated as a verdict (unreached with this frame), not smuggled in as an ontology. Mode: `general`. Triangulated across Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :44, Fig 13 :134. Refuter: a sealed frame whose completeness can be certified from inside.

### F7. An import needs a source gate and a shadow test

When a physics claim ("spacetime comes to an end") is carried into the framework, it is an import from D1 into D2. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8's import rules require provenance and a shadow test: the imported term must explain a gap the receiver already has. "Time has a first moment" does not explain a gap in the six-term set; the six-term set already has a definition of time and a no-bottom rule. So the import is not licensed by the figures. What can cross is the characterised residual: "a prediction diverges here and the observations stop resolving". Mode: `specified`, single-source for the figure gate. Refuter: show the imported term explains a live gap in closure(Γ) and record its provenance.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the world search and before the conclusion, per the seat rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim (S2 plus F3) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Bottom admitted.** A first or privileged stratum of the composition is admitted by the framework and cannot be refused (breaks Fig 12 :104 "there is no bottom" and Fig 4 :33).
2. **Time derived or primitive differently.** Time is derived from the other five terms, or shown not to be an ordered succession of differences (breaks Fig 13 :12 and the six-term minimality).
3. **Ground reading with a first moment.** An instrument returns a reading from beyond the GR past boundary, and that reading itself contains a first element of the ordered succession with no predecessor (breaks F3 at the ground).
4. **Bundle resists splitting.** The divergence condition and the first-moment condition are shown to be one condition, so that no remainder is left after the physical question is answered (breaks Fig 9 :54-57).
5. **Remainder fails.** A turn of the composition is shown that leaves no remainder re-entering the next (breaks Fig 13 :29-32).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn and the earlier reading is recorded, not erased.
