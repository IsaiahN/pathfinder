# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape below survives the reader's own check. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about observing-system gaps before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about Earth-system observing. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the observations that most limit understanding of interactions among atmosphere, ocean, ice, land and ecosystems are not the ones inside those five boxes. They are the ones at the interfaces between them, ranked per slot rather than added into one global priority number, and they have to be found by enumerating the contact cascade outward, not by scoping to the compartment first named. A second or third-order effect can be the limiting one. That is how biomagnification reached the eagles: first-order enumeration missed it and the residual that mattered was three steps out.
>
> For each slot there are three different questions, and they are not the same question: (1) is the datum already held in an archive and simply unfound; (2) is there a proto-instrument already returning something that fails to resolve; (3) does another observation frame already hold it, with a different evidence pool. More search, a better instrument and an import are three different remedies. Buying more of the wrong one spends the budget.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the single most limiting observation. The method returns a shape and a ranking rule, not a name. Candidate slots drawn from the published literature (sub-canopy and ocean-interior variables, snowfall water content, glacier ice volume, interface fluxes) are recorded as imports with provenance, not as results of the method. I am also not claiming that agreement among modelling groups identifies the priority. Model spread is a residual; an instrument that resolves the coupling is the anchor.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before I sent this:**
> 1. a compartment-by-compartment observing schedule closes the interaction residual as well as a bond-first schedule;
> 2. a single global priority score preserves a small live slot that a global average would erase;
> 3. a sensor for something that never registered at any resolution is specified from a description alone and built without a proto-instrument;
> 4. agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool settles which observation is limiting, with no instrument resolving the residual;
> 5. the method, without an import, names the specific missing observation and the ground settles it.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, with line numbers (public, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The observation-gap landscape I used as world corroboration (GCOS Essential Climate Variables): https://gcos.wmo.int/site/global-climate-observing-system-gcos/essential-climate-variables/about-essential-climate-variables
> - The cryosphere gap paper with the instrument argument: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/climate/articles/10.3389/fclim.2021.689823/full
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an observing problem you know well and can check against an instrument. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Stage order for the reply

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim, the three reachability questions and the five refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each reader writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A Stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A Stage 1 failure is kept in `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat, as if it came from any other source.
