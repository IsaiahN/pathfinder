# Expert verification packet - the Tether on missing Earth-system observations

**What this is:** a claim in a form an earth-observation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** No measurement in this derivation closed a slot. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The observations that most limit understanding of interactions among atmosphere, ocean, ice, land and ecosystems are at the interfaces between those compartments, not inside them. The limiting residual must be ranked per slot and never aggregated into one global priority score, because a global average erases a live interface. The habitat of a limiting observation must be enumerated outward through the contact cascade, because the effect that matters can be three steps from the first-framed cause. Each slot then owes three distinct questions: is the datum already held and unfound, is a proto-instrument already returning something that fails to resolve, or does another observation frame hold it with a different evidence pool. The resulting priority order is a filter for the budget, not a verdict; an instrument that resolves the coupling is the anchor. The framework does not name the single winner.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus observation-facing; checkable by reading Fig 12 and any flux dataset

- **Statement:** a compartment inventory is not an interaction inventory. The bond carries information the compartments do not. Interface fluxes are the observations that bear on interactions.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - **"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Also Fig 12 :46-47 - **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."** And Fig 12 :111 - **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- **What to check:** take any coupled Earth-system dataset. Does a model using only the internal states of atmosphere, ocean, ice, land and ecosystems predict the cross-component fluxes as well as one that includes the flux observations themselves? IPCC AR6 Chapter 9 says *"limitations in understanding feedback mechanisms involving the ocean, atmosphere and cryosphere, which are not fully represented in the current generation of climate models, generally limit our confidence in future projections."* The Critical Zone common measurements list *"Event-based and continuous fluxes across CZ interfaces"* as a distinct measurement class.
- **Refuter:** a compartment-only observation schedule closes the interaction residual as well as a bond-first schedule. If that holds, the problem is a partition and this claim fails.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 1, Fig 9, Fig 11

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot, the problem is a union compound, and contact cascades matter. A single global priority score and a first-order-only scoping both lose the signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 9 :55 - **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 11 :52-53 - **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- **What to check:** find one case in your own field where the observation that mattered was second or third order in contact distance. The DDT case is documented independently: *"the link between DDT and the eggshell thinning that caused reproductive failure in these birds was not initially recognized"* (US EPA, 2016). GCOS monitoring principles give priority to *"data-poor regions, poorly observed parameters, regions sensitive to change, and key measurements with inadequate temporal resolution"* (GCOS, 2024), which is a multi-slot rule.
- **Refuter:** a single global scalar priority score preserves a small live slot that a global average would erase, or every limiting observation in the domain is strictly first order. Either breaks this claim.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 9

- **Statement:** three reachability questions are distinct, and the priority rank is a budget filter, not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :34 - **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."** Fig 8 :39 - **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 9 :60 - **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
- **What to check:** for one slot you know well, can you name (a) a held-but-unfound datum, (b) a proto-instrument already returning something too coarse, and (c) another frame holding the observation? If they collapse into one remedy, the split has no practical content. Pritchard (2021) is a clean INWARD instance: *"New instruments have recently been developed to overcome these difficulties, and a key priority now is to deploy them widely"* for snowfall and ice thickness. WMO iClimateAction is a clean OUTWARD-shaped gap: *"global observations do not always translate directly into information that supports local decisions."*
- **Refuter:** all three remedies have the same effect on the residual; or agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool settles the rank with no instrument resolving the residual (Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a single named "most important" observation. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that more satellite resolution closes the gap. Independence requires frames with different closures.
- Not that model consensus sets the priority. Model spread is a residual; an instrument is the anchor.
- Not that an absent observation is proven to be the limiting one. SEARCHED is asked first.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this run.
- Not that the candidate slots listed in `01` F10 are derived. They are provenanced imports from the published literature.

## 5. What would settle it

A measurement programme at the interfaces, not more argument. In priority order from the shape:

1. Digitise and search held archives before buying new instruments (SEARCHED).
2. Deploy instruments already returning something too coarse: representative snowfall water content and glacier ice thickness; in-situ profiles beneath the ocean surface and within canopies; interface fluxes across soil, vegetation and atmosphere (INWARD).
3. Import local and Indigenous knowledge as a frame with a different closure, with provenance and a shadow test (OUTWARD).
4. Register the five refuters from `01` section 5. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the shape.

A single instrument that resolves one coupling names one slot at the ground. Until several do, the ranking stays open.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :55, Fig 11 :52-53, Fig 6 :34, Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :60. Seven short passages.
2. Check C1 against one coupled dataset you know: do the fluxes carry information the compartment states do not?
3. Name one case where the limiting observation was second or third order in contact distance, or say why none exists in your field.
4. For one slot, separate the three remedies. If they collapse, say so.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
