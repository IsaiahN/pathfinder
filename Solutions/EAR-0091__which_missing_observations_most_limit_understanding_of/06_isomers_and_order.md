# Bonds, isomers and order in the Earth-system observation question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Two supporting questions are owed after `01`: (1) are the five compartments a set of ingredients or a molecule, and what does the bond carry that the compartments do not; (2) what order governs the observation problem, and is that order dependency or preference.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms named in the question: Atmosphere, Ocean, Ice, Land, Ecosystems. Bonds available: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. Candidate observations are not atoms of the question; they are readings of the bonds between these atoms. No new observing primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Ingredients or molecule?

Fig 12 :5: **"A bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :46-47: **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."** Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

The question's phrase *"interactions among atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, and ecosystems"* is a request for the bonds. An inventory of the five boxes is the flat formula. The observed quantities that matter are typed relations: air-sea heat and momentum flux (symmetric in the sense that both sides exchange, though the flux has a direction), ice-ocean freshwater (ordered →), land-atmosphere evapotranspiration (ordered →), ecosystem state transitions driven by soil moisture and temperature (ordered → and threshold ⋛), and feedback loops where output re-enters input (Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input"*).

⇒ The five compartments are ingredients; the interactions are the molecule. **Falsifier:** if a complete catalogue of the five compartments' internal states predicts all cross-compartment behaviour without any flux or coupling term, the bond claim fails. This is testable in a model ablation.

### Q2 - Are different coupling arrangements isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two Earth-system configurations can hold the same five compartments and differ only in which coupling is active and with what sign. Example: a landscape where vegetation-atmosphere evapotranspiration is coupled to soil moisture, versus one where the same five compartments are present but the coupling is severed by land-use change. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a compartment inventory and decisive in regional climate and water balance. The critical-zone literature names this directly: *"the interactions and feedbacks among geologic, pedologic, hydrologic, chemical, biologic, atmospheric, and anthropogenic processes are coupled"* (Guo and Lin, 2016).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two configurations sharing the same five compartments but differing in a coupling bond behave identically under the same forcing, the isomer claim fails and the compartments are sufficient. A second form: if the coupling difference is already visible in the compartment inventory, then the bond is not carrying information the operands do not, and Fig 12 :5 does not apply.

### Q3 - The functional group

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Across all five compartments the recurrent motif is `state → flux/structure → state`: a state (ocean heat, soil moisture, ice mass) crosses through a structure (gradient, network, spectrum) into another state. This motif is the functional group; the qualifier (which state, which flux) says which member. It explains why a single lever that observes only one compartment fails (F3): it clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the compound at the interface standing.

**Falsifier:** if a coupling-observing schedule performs no better than a state-observing schedule across members of the group, the functional-group reading has no practical content, even if it is structurally true.

### Q4 - Dependency order, not importance order

Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The observing chain is perception (1), vocabulary for what a coupling affords (2), objective (what counts as reduced residual) (3), planning the observing system (4), learn and carry (5). This question sits at link 1. A better model (link 4) cannot rescue a dead sensor (link 1). Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

This is also why the question's own numbering matters: EAR-0092 asks how to combine satellites, field measurements, historical records and local knowledge; EAR-0093 asks which processes are poorly represented in models. These are downstream links. EAR-0091 is the upstream one. Reversing the order would be reading below the break.

**Falsifier:** show a case where improving link 3 or 4 reduced the observation residual while link 1 was unchanged and dead. Then the dependency order is wrong for this domain.

### Q5 - Cascade order: first, second, third

Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Order here is not preference; it is distance in contact. The observation that matters may be at the third step. The DDT case is corroborated independently: *"With several concentrating steps in the food chain below the level of fishes... very slight environmental contamination can be turned into a heavy pesticide load in birds at the top of the food chain"* (Stanford, DDT and Birds), and *"the link between DDT and the eggshell thinning that caused reproductive failure in these birds was not initially recognized"* (US EPA, 2016). The first-framed mechanism (adult lethality) was wrong; the cascade was three steps out.

**Falsifier:** a domain where the limiting observation is always a direct-contact variable, with no second or third-order case. Then enumerating the full cascade is wasted budget, and the practical rule should be scoped to direct contact.

### Q6 - Competing explanations, kept separate

Three explanations of "why this observation is missing" are often conflated. They must not be:

| explanation | shape | remedy | figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| already held, unfound | datum in an archive or dataset | distribute SEARCHED, independent searchers | Formula step 7; Fig 8 :39 |
| instrument too coarse | proto-instrument already returning something that fails to resolve | INWARD, sharpen from a worse instrument | Fig 6 :32, :34 |
| held in another frame | different closure, different evidence | OUTWARD import with source gate and shadow test | Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :9-13 |

**Falsifier:** show an instance where all three remedies have the same effect on the residual. Then the three-way split is a distinction without a difference and should be collapsed.

## CONCLUSION

The five compartments are ingredients; their couplings are the molecule, and differently coupled configurations sharing the compartments are isomers in Fig 12's sense, with a recurring `state → flux → state` functional group. The governing order is dependency (Fig 3), not importance, and contact distance (Fig 11), not a preference ranking. Three competing explanations of a missing observation are kept separate because their remedies differ. Each claim has a falsifier above. None is `grounded`.
