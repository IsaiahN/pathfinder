## 1. Residual

State R in effect terms before choosing any figure line.

- **Arity.** R is per object slot (Fig 1 :19) and the question bundles five compartments plus the bonds among them. The residual is therefore a vector over slots and interfaces, not a scalar over "Earth understanding." Symmetry: some couplings are symmetric exchanges (atmosphere-ocean heat, ice-ocean freshwater), others are ordered cascades with a direction and a threshold (permafrost thaw to carbon release, sea-ice loss to albedo to ocean heat uptake). Both a symmetric bond and an ordered bond are needed to write the residual (Fig 12 bond table). Scale: the same coupling is read at satellite, in-situ, historical and local-knowledge resolutions, with a lossy transform between them (Fig 4 :27-32).
- **Anchor test (Fig 2 :29).** What settles the question of which observation is most limiting? Not agreement among modelling groups. An instrument that resolves a previously unresolved coupling, or a dataset that closes a budget. If the proposed anchor moves when people push on it, it is not an anchor. The GCOS monitoring principles name exactly this: reference observations *"traceable to SI and/or community standards with robustly quantified uncertainties"* (GCOS, 2024).
- **Slip condition.** The question is constitutively about what cannot yet be perceived. Fig 1 :11 and the Symbols table say R is always a slice: *"what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."* So the answer cannot be a name read off the world; it can only be a shape and a route to a name.

## 4. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The question is about bonds, not the five boxes (decisive for framing)

Fig 12 :5: **"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :46-47: **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

The question names five ingredients (atmosphere, ocean, ice, land, ecosystems) but asks about their *interactions*. A list of the five compartments is a flat formula. The missing observations that most limit understanding of interactions are observations of the couplings: air-sea fluxes, ice-ocean freshwater, land-atmosphere energy and water, ecosystem state transitions. Observing a box better does not observe the bond.

World corroboration, low overlap with the framework: IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 9 says *"limitations in understanding feedback mechanisms involving the ocean, atmosphere and cryosphere, which are not fully represented in the current generation of climate models, generally limit our confidence in future projections"* (Fox-Kemper et al., 2021). The Critical Zone common measurements list *"Event-based and continuous fluxes across CZ interfaces; including bedrock-soil; soil-atmosphere; soil- vegetation; vegetation-atmosphere; landscape surface-soil-water"* (SERC/Carleton CZO measurements). These are bond observations, not box inventories.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 plus two independent world fields: climate assessment and critical-zone instrumentation). **Refuter:** show that a compartment-only observation schedule reduces the interaction residual as much as a bond-observing schedule, i.e. the union is actually a partition. If that holds, F1 fails and a ranked variable list is the right answer.

### F2 - Index per slot, never average into one priority number

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :20-21: **"Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one."**

A single "most important missing observation" collapses a vector into a scalar. A global residual near zero with one live interface is a legal state. The right output is a per-slot ranking with the live slots marked, not a single winner.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated (Fig 1 plus the Symbols definition of R as a slice). **Refuter:** exhibit a single scalar priority score that preserves a small live slot a global average would erase. If it can, F2 fails. Note the world analogue: GCOS monitoring principles give priority to *"data-poor regions, poorly observed parameters, regions sensitive to change, and key measurements with inadequate temporal resolution"* (GCOS, 2024), a multi-slot rule, not one number.

### F3 - This is a union compound, not five separate gaps

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57-58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

An observation schedule that treats atmosphere, ocean, ice, land and ecosystems as five independent boxes clears the single-condition regions and leaves the intersections standing. What remains is not a harder atmosphere problem; it is a specific compound at the interface. Library of Babel (Isaiah N, 2026-10-02) names the same failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the essay, low overlap in wording but shared author, so weak corroboration). **Refuter:** show the interaction residual closes under compartment-wise observing. Then the split is a partition and F3 is withdrawn.

### F4 - Enumerate by contact cascade; the residual that matters is often third order

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Fig 11 :54: **"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."**

The figures contain an earth-systems case in their own text. Direct-contact enumeration missed a third-order effect. The same shape appears in the TERN OzCZO description: the information collected *"will fill essential knowledge gaps about interactions of under- and above-ground environmental processes and their responses to disturbance and change"* (TERN, 2021). US EPA's retrospective on DDT says *"the link between DDT and the eggshell thinning that caused reproductive failure in these birds was not initially recognized"* (US EPA, 2016). The limiting observation was not the obvious one, and the mechanism was not the one first tested.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus two independent world sources: ecology and environmental regulation). **Refuter:** show a case where all limiting observations are strictly first-order contacts and the cascade boundary adds nothing. Then F4 is a needless expense.

### F5 - Three reachability questions, not one

Formula step 7 asks SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 8 :40-41: **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."**

For each candidate missing observation:

1. **SEARCHED.** Is the datum already held in an archive or dataset and simply unfound? Remedy: distribute the search and keep searchers independent (Fig 6 :49-50). World instance: the 2021 GCOS Status Report notes that *"a vast trove of observations held in various international libraries and archives still require digitization"* (IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 1 first-order draft text). This is budget-unreachable, not absent. Importing a new instrument against it would add an atom not needed.
2. **INWARD.** Is there a proto-instrument already returning something that fails to resolve? Fig 6 :34: **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."** Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** Pritchard (2021) is the direct world instance: *"New instruments have recently been developed to overcome these difficulties, and a key priority now is to deploy them widely to collect representative observations"* of snowfall and ice thickness. The new methods were built on pluviometers and snow pills already returning biased readings.
3. **OUTWARD.** Does another frame already hold the observation? Fig 6 :23: import from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test. Local and Indigenous knowledge is a candidate frame, not a data layer to be assimilated into the same closure. The WMO iClimateAction assessment says *"global observations do not always translate directly into information that supports local decisions"* (WMO, 2026-09-21), which is the shape of an OUTWARD gap.

Keep the three distinct. Fig 6 :31: *"Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*

**Mode:** `general` structural shape, triangulated (Formula, Fig 6, Fig 8, plus independent world instances for each branch). **Refuter:** show a case where a sensor for something never registered at any resolution was specified purely from a description and then built successfully without a proto-instrument. That breaks the INWARD rule. Note the standing caveat: Fig 6 :37-38 says theory routinely precedes its instrument (Neptune), so this refuter is about *building the instrument*, not about predicting where to look.

### F6 - "Most limiting" is a per-slot ranking by persistent, flat residual, and the rank is a filter, not a verdict

Fig 5 :18: **"seeks the gap that is large and compressible"** (the curiosity drive seeks high residual mass that an instrument could actually compress). Formula step 2 ranks candidates by *"the residual that PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN."* Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

"Most limiting" therefore means: large live mass at that slot, persistent and not trending down, and tractable by an instrument already returning something. The resulting priority order decides what gets funded first. It does not decide what is true. Only the instrument that resolves the coupling does that.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 5 plus Fig 9). **Refuter:** a ranking produced purely by expert agreement with no instrument that resolves the residual. By Fig 2 :35 that is a mirror, not an anchor, and the verdict would be missing. World cross-check: WMO (2026-11-10) quotes GCOS Chair Thelma Krug, *"Across the air, ocean, ice, and land, the lights are starting to go out,"* and Celeste Saulo, *"Coverage is uneven too, from parts of Africa to the deep ocean. The basic weather network the world agreed on is less than 40 per cent in place."* These are per-slot live residuals, and they are reported by instruments, not by a vote.

### F7 - Independence is bought across instrument types, not across reports about the same retrieval

Fig 2 :31: **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** Fig 2 :32: **"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."** Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Satellite, in-situ, paleo archive and local knowledge count as separate frames only when they draw on different evidence. Two satellite products sharing a retrieval algorithm are one witness. GCOS Monitoring Principle 2 requires *"both in-situ and remote sensing platforms as appropriate based on their respective strengths and limitations"* (GCOS, 2024), and the iClimateAction assessment says in-situ observations are needed *"to calibrate and validate satellite measurements and to observe variables and environments that satellites cannot adequately capture, including beneath the ocean surface or within complex vegetation canopies"* (WMO, 2026-09-21). That is a statement about independence, not about redundancy.

**Mode:** `general` for the rule, `specified` for the earth-observation binding, triangulated (Fig 2 plus GCOS/WMO). **Refuter:** show two "independent" streams drawing on the same calibration or assimilation product. Then they are one searcher wearing many names and their agreement carries no weight.

### F8 - Models are frames, not anchors; model spread is a residual, not a verdict

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Disagreement among Earth-system models about which observation matters is a room, not a ground. IPCC AR6 Chapter 9 reports *"deep uncertainty arising from limited process understanding, limited availability of evaluation data, uncertainties in their external forcing and high sensitivity to uncertain boundary conditions and parameters"* for ice-sheet processes. That spread is evidence that a residual exists; it does not rank the observations. Pritchard (2021) shows the same: thirteen models gave Greenland mass losses from 1066 to 6034 Gt, and the spread was due to snowfall-dominated surface processes, so what was owed was an observation of snowfall water content, not another model intercomparison.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the figure rule (Fig 7), triangulated with the world instances. **Refuter:** a case where model consensus correctly identified the limiting observation before any instrument resolved it. That would weaken F8 as a practical warning, though not the structural claim that consensus is not the anchor.

### F9 - What the framework cannot do: name the single most limiting observation

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** The canonical figures README says the framework returns *"a shape, not a name."*

The framework returns a ranked set of slots with their reachability classes and the instruments that would close them. It does not return "the one most important observation," both because that would be a name where it returns a shape and because valence is relative to the residual as stated. A specific missing primitive would have to be imported from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :11 IMPORT), with provenance and a shadow test, and recorded as an import rather than a derivation.

**Mode:** `general` limit, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the canonical README plus the Symbols definition of R). **Refuter:** a case where the framework, without an import, named the specific missing observation and the ground settled it. That would break the shape-not-a-name limit.

### F10 - The shape that survives

Assembling F1 to F9, the answer has five parts:

1. **Bond-first.** Observe the interfaces (air-sea, ice-ocean, land-atmosphere, ecosystem-state), not just the five compartments (F1, Fig 12 :5).
2. **Per slot.** Rank by live residual mass, persistent and not trending down; never aggregate into one global winner (F2, Fig 1 :19).
3. **Cascade-enumerated.** Enumerate actors, conditions and relations outward until the cascade stops mattering; a third-order effect can be the limiting one (F4, Fig 11 :44).
4. **Triaged.** For each slot ask SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, keeping the last two separate (F5).
5. **Ground-settled.** Priority order is a budget filter. The verdict waits for an instrument that resolves the coupling (F6, Fig 9 :60), with independence bought across genuinely different instruments (F7).

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 6, Fig 9, Fig 11 and Fig 12. **Refuter:** the pre-registered set in section 5.

**Illustrative imports, NOT figure-derived.** To make the shape usable, world sources supply candidate slots. These are recorded as imports with provenance, not as results. They are: ocean interior and sub-canopy variables satellites cannot capture (WMO iClimateAction, 2026); snowfall water content and glacier ice volume (Pritchard, 2021); interface fluxes across bedrock/soil/atmosphere/vegetation (CZO common measurements, 2015; TERN OzCZO, 2021); ice-sheet forcing and evaluation data (IPCC AR6 Ch.9, 2021); geographical gaps in Africa, South America, Southeast Asia, Southern Ocean and polar regions (GCOS, 2024; WMO, 2026). None of these is claimed to be "the" most limiting observation. They are the slots a figure-derived triage would rank.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A compartment-by-compartment observation schedule closes the interaction residual as well as a bond-first schedule (breaks F1 and F3; the problem is a partition, not a union).
2. A single global scalar priority score preserves a small live slot that a global average would erase (breaks F2 and Fig 1 :19).
3. A sensor for something that never registered at any resolution is specified from a description alone and built successfully without a proto-instrument (breaks F5 and Fig 6 :32/:34). This is the narrow INWARD claim, not the separate claim that theory may precede detection.
4. Agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool settles which observation is limiting, with no instrument resolving the residual (breaks F6, F7 and Fig 2 :35/:40).
5. The framework, without an import, names the specific missing observation and the ground settles it (breaks F9).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Reframe the question from "which single observation" to a per-slot, bond-first ranking with the three-way triage (F1, F2, F5).
2. Enumerate the habitat by contact cascade across satellite, in-situ, archive and local frames, not by the five named boxes (F4, F7).
3. Treat priority scores as filters for the budget. Record the instrument that would close each slot. Never let the filter hand you the verdict (F6).
4. Keep candidate slots as provenanced imports. Do not present them as figure-derived names (F9).
5. Lead outreach with the shape and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what would refute it before showing any theory (Fig 10 :52).
