# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are checked against the figures by the recipient. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable output on prediction in changing-component systems

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Can an emergent pattern be predicted from interaction rules when the components themselves are changing? There are two readings that must be kept apart. Predicting the *kind* of pattern (its shape) and predicting the *name* (the exact macro configuration) are not the same question, and one lever does not clear both.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** From the framework's minimum set and its membrane rule: (1) the kernel, the loop plus the rule for naming what worked, does not change with size, while only actors, currency and anchor change; (2) the set of kinds is closed while the arrangements over it are not, so there is always another pattern to generate but no new kind to manufacture by composition; (3) what is recorded only grows while what is reachable is derived and can fall, so a prediction indexed to a fixed cast does not transfer unchanged when the habitat updates; (4) a residual that compounds through an amplifying chain is chaos, with discarded detail returning as noise. Together these give a shape, not a bare yes or no: prediction of the kind is licensed under component change; prediction of the exact configuration is not licensed from the rules alone.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a general closed-form shortcut from rules to the exact macro state; that the components can be ignored; that agreement among many predictors settles the question; or that no measurement could ever close this. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. What is predictable is the shape any candidate must have plus the route to closing it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. a closed-form derivation from rules to exact long-horizon configuration in a positive, amplifying residual regime, with no new atom and no import;
> 2. a system where the loop and naming rule change when only size or cast changes;
> 3. a macro prediction that transfers unchanged from one cast to a different cast with no lossy transform;
> 4. two frames with genuinely different closures deriving the same macro *name*, not just the same kind, with no import and no shared evidence pool.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public figure repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Regards,
> [Your name]
