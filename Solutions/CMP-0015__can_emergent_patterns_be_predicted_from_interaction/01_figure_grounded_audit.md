## 0. The claim under audit, and the residual in effect terms

> **Question.** Can emergent patterns be predicted from interaction rules when the components themselves are changing?

**Effect statement.** There are three relata: the interaction rules Γ (the micro rules), the components (the cast, or atoms(Γ)), and the emergent pattern φ* (the macro term whose prediction is asked for). "Predict" means derive φ as a term in closure(Γ) and have the ground settle it (Fig 12, formula steps 3 and 5). "Components changing" means the atoms and the cast are not held fixed across the derivation.

- **Option A (unqualified yes):** composition inside closure(Γ) reaches φ*, so the macro pattern follows from the rules alone even as components change. This leads to a closed-form prediction and requires atoms to remain stable long enough for closure and settlement.
- **Option B (unqualified no):** component change destroys every invariant, so no macro statement is predictable. This leads to a claim that nothing survives turnover.
- **What the figures actually license (the split kept here):** prediction of the *shape or kind* survives component change, because the kernel does not change with size (Fig 4) and the set of kinds is closed while the arrangements are not (Fig 13). Prediction of the *particular configuration* does not survive, because composition never adds an atom (Fig 6, Fig 8), the reach can fall when components change (Fig 6 amendment, Fig 11), and a compounding residual gives chaos (Fig 13 :31).

**Bind the sought-for answer as an ordinary term before the run.** An answer to this question should look like: (i) a statement of which quantity is invariant under component change; (ii) a shape for the predictable part, not a name for the macro configuration; (iii) a route, namely SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD (formula step 7), after the residual is described in effect terms; (iv) a pre-registered refuter. That is the shape checked below.

### Domain census (done before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** three relata, interaction rules (a composed molecule), components (atoms and cast), emergent pattern (a term φ* at a higher level). The question is a reachability question: is φ* ∈ closure(Γ)?
- **Symmetry:** the prediction relation is directed, not symmetric. Composition runs from rules to pattern (sequence and production bonds, Fig 12), and settlement runs from ground to claim. "Predict from" is not invertible: the pattern does not derive the rules (Fig 8 :34 to :38, Gödel, Tarski, Chaitin).
- **Scale:** the rules operate at the one-agent or micro stratum, the emergent pattern at the population or macro stratum (Fig 4 membrane). Component change is a change at the atom stratum that propagates upward through the membrane.

**One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor).**

1. **Domain 1, reachability and composition (framework-internal).** Actors: atoms(Γ), the seven bonds and negation, the candidate term φ (the emergent pattern). Currency: composition inside closure(Γ), then settlement against the ground. Anchor: the figures as metalaws, and the ground for any settlement. No nameable anchor would make this a non-domain; it has two (the figure text and the ground). Type: reachability, not computation.
2. **Domain 2, changing components and levels (dynamics).** Actors: agents or components, the habitat (conditions, relations), the membrane between scales. Currency: residuals compounding across turns, settlement, methods carried up and head starts sent down. Anchor: the ground (does not decay) and the kernel (does not change with size). Type: level and partition question.
3. **Domain 3, the verification of "predict" (the claim itself).** Actors: the predicting frame, outside frames, the ground. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2). Type: verification question.

**Partition or union.** Domains 1 and 3 touch at the ground (both involve settlement). Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The overlap is a compound with its own loop: reachability plus verification at one ground. I keep the three verdicts separate and name the compound at the seam rather than averaging them. Domain 2 is a genuine partition boundary (what changes and what is invariant).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual in effect terms before selecting any frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Refuse before mint (formula step 2). Settle the invariant at level one before predicting the term at level two (Fig 12 :104). Only then ask SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD (formula step 7).

## 2. Findings

### F1: The question is a reachability question, and composition cannot reach what the closure lacks

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
Fig 13 :91: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*
Fig 13 :113: *"So the array is unbounded and the reach is not. More compositions never add a member, and no chain reaches outside what the six and their bonds already span."*

Predicting an emergent pattern from interaction rules is exactly the move "compose inside closure(Γ) and ask whether φ* is in it." The figures license a bounded version of that move. Arrangements are unbounded, so there is always another pattern to generate; but the reach is bounded by the atoms and bonds held, so no amount of composition manufactures a new kind. If the emergent pattern requires an atom the rules do not hold, the prediction is **unreachable by composition** and needs an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23). Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* This is a `general` structural result, triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 13 (three low-overlap routes: closure idempotence, union surplus, set-of-kinds).

**Refuter:** exhibit a composition inside a fixed closure that produces a genuinely new atom, or show that the distinction between depth and genuine absence collapses (that the two are not different facts).

### F2: Changing components move the reach downward, not merely the cast

Fig 6 amendment :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*
Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*

"Components themselves are changing" is not a footnote to the prediction; it is the central effect. The figures treat the record and the reach as two separate quantities. Adding a refusal lowers the reach even though nothing is deleted. In habitat terms, the actors and conditions in contact with the residual update, so a prediction indexed to a standing cast is indexed to something that no longer exists. In level terms, a changed component is a settled molecule becoming an atom for whatever composes over it, so the irreducible unit is a fact about a level and the prediction must be re-run at the new level. This yields a `specified` prediction: a macro prediction made under cast C is not transferable to cast C' without re-derivation or a provenanced import.

**Refuter:** show that the reach is monotone in the cast (that changing components cannot remove the standing of any term), or that a prediction under cast C transfers unchanged to a different cast with no lossy transform.

### F3: The kernel is what survives component change, so only shape prediction is stable

Fig 4 :19: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand."*
Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
Fig 4 :34: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*
Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Fig 4 is the direct figure for this question. Scaling from one agent to a thousand, or replacing the cast over time, changes only actors, currency and anchor. The kernel (the loop and the naming rule) persists. So what can be predicted robustly under component change is the *form* the emergent pattern will take, not its materialisation, because the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes. Fig 11 sharpens it: capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, so a prediction based on the agent alone fails when the habitat changes. This is triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 11 (different domains: scale membrane versus field of contact). Mode `general` for the kernel claim, `specified` for its application to a given system.

**Refuter:** show a system where the loop and naming rule change when the size or cast changes, while the actors stay the same, or show an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (Fig 11 :72 names that as the standing refuter of the capability claim).

### F4: Residual compounding gives chaos, so exact long-horizon prediction fails even with fixed rules

Fig 13 :31: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*

This is the figure's own statement of the limit on prediction. Each turn leaves a remainder that re-enters the next; when the chain amplifies, that remainder is chaos, and detail discarded at a crossing returns as noise above it. So even with the interaction rules held fixed, an exact macro configuration at a long horizon is not obtainable from the rules by composition: the remainder compounds. This is the barrier that the world literature on computational irreducibility independently describes (see `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD), but the figure line is primary and decides. Mode `specified` when applied to a given amplifying chain; `general` as a structural statement.

**Refuter:** exhibit a general shortcut from the rules to the exact long-horizon configuration in a regime whose residual is positive and amplifying, with no new atom and no import.

### F5: "Predict the emergent pattern" is a union and must be unbundled before search

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

The phrase "predict the emergent pattern" bundles two different questions. (i) Predict the *kind* of pattern, a shape: licensed under F1 and F3. (ii) Predict the *name*, the exact configuration: not licensed under F1, F2 and F4. Treating them as one hard question is the union error of Fig 9 :54. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing; what remains is a specific compound with its own loop. The honest output is therefore a shape plus a route, not a bare yes or no, and the budget (how much search) is a filter while the ground is the witness.

**Refuter:** show that predict-kind and predict-name are the same question (that deriving the kind entails the name, or vice versa) in a case where the residual is positive and compounding.

### F6: The anchor for "predict" is the ground, not agreement among predictors

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

A crowd of predictors who agree because they share the same model and the same data is one evidence pool wearing many names (Fig 2 :40). The question of whether it can be predicted is not settled by how many people predict it. It is settled by an anchor that does not update: a measurement, a dataset, an experiment, a board. The ground does not decay; the channel to it does. So the verification domain's verdict is: a prediction claim is `candidate` until the ground pays, and the cost of being wrong determines how much independence to buy in the check.

**Refuter:** produce a case where agreement between two frames sharing an evidence pool counts as a verdict, or where a ground that moves when frames push on it still settles the question.

### F7: The verification domain cannot certify its own completeness

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 7 :30: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"*

If a predictor searches and fails to find the macro term, that failure is not proof that the term is absent: the frame is not a sealed room, and a metaframe cannot be certified from inside (Fig 8 :34 to :38). Conversely, a chain of more powerful predictors is a room chain: agreement inside a room is variation, and progress comes from selecting the next room against the residual the last one surfaced, not from adding more agreeing rooms. So the honest report for "not predictable yet" distinguishes underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-this-budget (formula step 7) and issues the verdict `unreachable` rather than silence. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

**Refuter:** show a frame that searches itself to completion and proves from inside that a term is absent, or show that underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-budget are the same fact with the same instruction.

### F8: The round trip must be measured, not assumed

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
Fig 4 :31: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."*

Condensing the figures into the answer "shape yes, name no" loses information. The loss is not an error to hide; it is R_T, the round-trip gap, and it is enumerated in `11_audit.md`. Treating the condensation as identical to the law set would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error this figure names.

**Refuter:** show that the condensation preserves every constraint of the figure set (that R_T is zero), which would require the gap enumeration in `11_audit.md` to be empty.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion and before any outreach)

The shape claim (predict the kind, not the name; the invariant is the kernel and contact structure; the reach can fall when components change) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Shortcut refuter.** A general closed-form derivation is given from interaction rules to the exact long-horizon macro configuration in a regime whose residual is positive and amplifying, without adding an atom and without importing a frame. This would falsify Fig 13 :31 and Fig 8 :32 as applied here.
2. **Kernel refuter.** A system is exhibited in which the loop and the rule for naming what worked change when only the size or cast changes, with actors, currency and anchor held fixed. This would falsify Fig 4 :19-20.
3. **Invariance refuter.** A macro prediction made under cast C transfers unchanged to a different cast C' with no lossy transform and no re-derivation, in a case where the habitat changed. This would falsify Fig 11 :39-40 and Fig 6 :42.
4. **Shape refuter.** Two frames with different closures derive the same macro *name* (not merely the same kind) with no import and no shared evidence pool. This would falsify Fig 9 :32.
5. **World refuter.** A low-overlap source shows that the quantity predicted by a universality class depends on the microscopic components within that class, or that coarse-graining preserves exact configuration detail. This would send the derivation back to the census, not flip the verdict by itself.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this solution is labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Split the question before answering it (F5). Ask separately: predict the kind, and predict the name. Do not average the two verdicts.
2. State mode on every claim (F9). The only `general` claims are the structural ones; the system-facing ones are `specified` until the ground pays.
3. Lead with the residual and the refuter in any outreach (F6, F8). Offer links after the stage-1 verdict is recorded. The figures, not this summary, are the authority.
4. Ask for the failure, not the agreement. Fig 7 :35: a room's agreement is variation, not a verdict.
5. Report `unreachable` plainly where the search fails; do not convert absence of evidence into a claim about the world (F7).
