# Expert verification packet: prediction under changing components

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complex-systems researcher, statistical physicist or collective-behaviour scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "can emergent patterns be predicted from interaction rules when the components themselves are changing?" is a union and must be split. What survives component change is the kernel (the loop plus the rule for naming what worked) and the closed set of kinds, so prediction of the **kind** of pattern is licensed and the arrangements never run out. What does not survive is the exact macro configuration: a compounding residual gives chaos, composition never adds an atom, and the reach can fall when the habitat updates, so a prediction indexed to one cast does not transfer unchanged to another. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and reports `unreachable` rather than silence when a search fails. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, the dependency order of the questions, and refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 13

- **Statement:** the invariant under component change is the kernel and the closed set of kinds, not the cast. Therefore predict-kind is licensed; predict-name is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19 *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand."* Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 13 :91 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*
- **Refuter:** derive a macro *name* from the rules alone in a case where the residual is positive and amplifying, or exhibit a system where the loop and naming rule change when only the size or cast changes with actors, currency and anchor held fixed.

### C2: dynamics-facing; checkable against any system with turnover

- **Statement:** changing components lower the reach even though the record only grows; predictions tied to a fixed cast do not transfer unchanged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :42 *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."* Fig 11 :73 *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*.
- **What to check:** take a documented case of component turnover (a flock whose density changes, an ecosystem with species replacement, a multi-agent system with agent churn). Does the macro descriptor survive while the exact configuration changes? Does the prediction have to be re-derived at the new level, with a lossy transform?
- **Refuter:** a macro prediction that transfers unchanged from cast C to cast C' with no lossy transform and no re-derivation, in a case where the habitat demonstrably changed.

### C3: verification-facing; checkable by any researcher who has tried to predict a critical transition

- **Statement:** "predict" is settled by a ground and by low-overlap frames, never by agreement inside one evidence pool. A failed search is reported as `unreachable`, not as proof of absence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** are the claims of predictability in your field anchored to a measurement, or to agreement among models trained on the same data? Do failed predictions distinguish underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-budget?
- **Refuter:** a case where agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool counts as a verdict, or a frame that proves from inside that a term is absent.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not an unqualified "yes" (a general closed-form rule-to-configuration shortcut) and not an unqualified "no" (nothing survives turnover).
- Not that the components can be ignored. Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- Not a derivation of any specific emergent pattern, and no new primitive.
- Not that the macro configuration is named. It is a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that agreement among predictors corroborates the answer (Fig 7 :35).
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulation checked for internal consistency, not a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument and a low-overlap frame, not a further argument.

1. **Shortcut test:** a general derivation from rules to exact long-horizon configuration in a positive, amplifying residual regime, with no new atom and no import.
2. **Invariance test:** re-run the macro prediction after documented component turnover; it must transfer with no lossy transform for C2 to fail.
3. **Kernel test:** change only size or cast, hold actors, currency and anchor fixed, and see whether the loop and naming rule change.
4. **Ground test:** measure rule set and macro pattern independently; an instrument that discriminates several rule/cast pairs giving the same reading would name the source.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

2. Check C2 against one system you know with component turnover. Does the kind survive while the name changes?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate the rule or cast that produced a given macro reading, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
