# Solution CMP-0015

**Question:** Can emergent patterns be predicted from interaction rules when the components themselves are changing?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 20

**Section:** 2. Emergence, self-organization, and collective behavior

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Nothing here is `grounded`: no measurement settles the prediction claim, and this folder reports simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures, not ground readings.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual in effect terms (what is being decided and what each option leads to).** Option A, "yes, emergent patterns are predictable from interaction rules even when components change", leads to a closed-form derivation from the rules to the macro configuration. Option B, "no, nothing is predictable once components change", leads to a claim that no invariant survives component turnover. The figure-governed split is different from both: what survives component change is the kernel and the closed set of kinds, so prediction of the *shape or kind* of the emergent pattern is licensed (Fig 4, Fig 13); what is not licensed is prediction of the *particular configuration*, because composition never adds an atom (Fig 6, Fig 8), the reach can fall when components change (Fig 6 amendment, Fig 11), and a compounding residual gives chaos (Fig 13 :31). Bind the sought-for answer as an ordinary term before the run: the answer should look like a kind of thing plus a route plus a refuter, not a name.

**What is deliberately not here:** no personal emails of named individuals, no ranking of people by likelihood of agreement, no bulk-send plan. Per Fig 2 :35, alignment is triangulation, not negotiation; per Fig 2 :40, agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not two; per Fig 10 :42, a verifier who can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.
