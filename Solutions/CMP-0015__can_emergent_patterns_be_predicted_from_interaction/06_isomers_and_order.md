## DERIVATION

### Q1: Same pattern, or same kind of pattern?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 13 :124: the ingredient/bond distinction is already there: *"the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*
- Two macro patterns built from **different component sets** are not isomers under Fig 12: they do not share the same atoms. They can still share a **functional group**, a substructure such as `state → structure → state` that fixes the kind of outcome while the qualifier (which component fills which slot) decides the member. This is the weakest reading of "same emergent pattern across component change", and it is the one the figures license without import.
- The stronger reading: if two changing-component systems are written over a **shared atom set** and differ only in which structure mediates which state pair, they **are** isomers, same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is a real, checkable structural claim, not a metaphor.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a pattern appears in a system that uses no atom the other system requires (for example, a macro form that needs a structure the first system does not hold), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands instead. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 + Fig 13); single-source for its application to a given system until measured.

### Q2: What is the order of the derivation?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Fig 9 :27: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: refuse before mint (a refutation is a negation, ¬, which is not a bond).
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 7: SEARCHED first (cheapest, the other two assume its answer), then INWARD, then OUTWARD. INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and never read against one another.
- Applied order for this question:
  1. Describe the residual in effect terms: rules, changing components, macro pattern, and what fails to resolve.
  2. Split the union (Fig 9 :54): predict-kind and predict-name get separate loops.
  3. Refuse the unlicensed reading (a closed-form rule-to-name derivation) before minting any new term (formula step 2).
  4. Ask SEARCHED: is the macro term already in closure(Γ) and unfound? Same atoms, same wall, more of the space visited.
  5. Ask INWARD: extend an instrument already returning something, sharpen in our own terms.
  6. Ask OUTWARD: characterise the residual in effect terms, then find a frame whose closure predicts its shape, with a source gate and a shadow test.
  7. Settle at the ground; a gate is not the ground (formula step 5).
- **Falsifier:** if any later step can be run before its predecessor and still produce a checkable result, the dependency order claimed here is wrong (Fig 3 :20 to :23).

### Q3: What is the analogue, and where does it stop?

The low-overlap world analogue for "same macro form despite different components" is the **universality class**: Zinn-Justin (Scholarpedia, 2006/accessed 2026-11-22) writes that continuous phase transitions show universal *"macroscopic or large distance properties"* independent of the specific system within a wide class; Scheffer et al. (Nature, 2009) write that near a critical point the dynamics have generic properties *"regardless of differences in the details of each system."*

- The analogue supports the **kind** prediction: the macroscopic descriptor survives component change.
- The analogue does **not** support a **name** prediction: critical exponents and early-warning signals are class quantities, not the exact trajectory of one system. Scheffer et al. also state that the actual moment of transition remains difficult to predict, and that early-warning signals can fail.
- Wolfram's computational irreducibility (MathWorld, accessed 2026-11-22) is the complementary analogue: "pockets of reducibility" let coarse properties be predicted while exact behaviour still requires running the system.
- Fig 8 :50 to :52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* The analogue is a corroborating import only if its provenance and shadow test are recorded. It never decides the figure verdict.
- **Falsifier for the analogue:** show a universality-class quantity that depends on microscopic composition within the class, or a coarse-grained predictor that recovers the exact configuration. That would break the analogy and send the derivation back to the census, not flip the verdict by itself.

### Q4: The level of the prediction

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :34, the seed carries no small tree; the conditions decide what it becomes.
- A macro pattern predicted at level n becomes an atom for level n+1 only after it settles. When components change, the level is remade (Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."*), so the prediction must be re-run. Round-trip gap R_T is nonzero and is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.
- **Falsifier:** show a settled macro term that composes at the next level with no lossy transform and no re-derivation after the cast changes.

## CONCLUSION

Across component change, the licensed form of "same emergent pattern" is a **functional group** (same kind of arrangement), and the stronger **isomer** claim is available only when the systems share an atom set. The derivation order is fixed by dependency: describe, split the union, refuse, SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD, settle. The world analogues corroborate the kind prediction and the limit on exact prediction; they do not decide it. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall and no exact-name claim survives this derivation.
