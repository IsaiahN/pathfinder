## Wider literature that bears on the four channels

Grouped by field so an overlap pool is visible.

- **Political science and money in politics:** the campaign finance article records studies on contributions and committee assignments, on access, on tax rates and lobbying, and the counterfinding of Fowler, Garro and Spenkuch. These are per-channel anchors. They do not by themselves settle the compound.
- **Political economy and professional networks:** the revolving door article names bureaucratic capital and regulatory capture; the interlocking directorate article documents small-world connectivity among boards, foundations and policy groups. These support the cascade and cycle shape but, because they describe connected institutions, they share the same evidence pool as the political science sources.
- **Media economics:** the concentration article gives the pluralism mechanism and explicitly names the shared-wire-service problem. That is a direct instance of Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool", reached independently.
- **Animal behaviour:** the dominance hierarchy article separates coercive dominance from prestige-based influence. This is a low-overlap analogue for treating money, access, framing and trust as different currencies rather than one scalar.
- **Ecology:** the trophic cascade article supplies the indirect, multi-step effect and the warning that real food webs are not clean chains. Low overlap with the political sources.
- **Mathematics and statistics:** Clauset, Shalizi and Newman supply the discipline that a heavy-tailed concentration claim is a fit to be tested, not an assumption. Low overlap with all of the above.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. Where sources disagree with each other, as Fowler and Spenkuch versus the access studies, the disagreement is recorded as a live empirical question rather than resolved by the framework.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the residual and the refuter. Send the links only after the recipient records a verdict.
- Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before judging has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them anything to read.
- Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." A send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." If the cost of a wrong answer is high, seek low-overlap checkers (an economist, an ecologist, a network scientist), not more people who study money in politics.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim; (2) the question "what would count as a failure?"; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the three public links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter and states a mode.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, before theory.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the four pre-registered refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict first.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the local figure extract, the canonical figures README and the OpenSecrets data portal. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, method only.** Offer the eight-step loop as a reusable generator. It is a method, not an answer, so giving it away does not give away a result. Each user must name their own ground and write their own refuter before running it.
