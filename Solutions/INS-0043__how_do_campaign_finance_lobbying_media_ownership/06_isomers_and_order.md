# Which bonds join the four channels, and in what order

**Mode: `specified` on the bond assignments, `general` on the order rule.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The four channels are named, but "interact" is not a bond. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Two questions are owed: (1) which of the seven bonds is claimed at each junction, and (2) is the arrangement a chain, a cycle, or a compound with its own loop?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: the four channels as institutional actors (money, access, framing, trust), plus their overlaps. Bonds available: the seven typed relations of Fig 12, plus negation as the eighth operator which is not a bond. No new primitive is available without an import, and no import is made here. The operands are institutions at one level; settled molecules becoming atoms at the next level is a separate question, left open.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the four channels a flat conjunction?

A flat formula would write `money + access + framing + trust`, one symmetric bond. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92-93), so all orderings collapse and the bond carries nothing. The price would be zero and the term would record only ingredients. That is exactly the failure Fig 12 names as the flat formula. **Refused.** A flat conjunction cannot name a route to concentration.

### Q2: Are they a functional group or isomers?

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

- **Weak reading:** each channel is a different member of a functional group, "an influence channel into a policy outcome". Different qualifiers, same kind. This is licensed by the figure and costs no new atom.
- **Strong reading:** two polities hold the same four channels but connect them with different bonds, for example one where media ownership is a `⇒` production of finance (owners create friendly coverage) and one where it is a `∥` disjunction (either channel suffices to carry the message). Then they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance, and the difference is invisible in a list of the four channels. This is the checkable structural claim worth taking to a checker.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two cases with the same four channels but different connecting bonds produce the same concentration against a ground, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3: Which bond at which junction?

Assignments are `specified` and unmeasured. They are proposals to test, not findings.

| junction | proposed bond | reason from the figure | what would refute it |
|---|---|---|---|
| finance → friendly coverage | `⇒` production, "B did not exist before" | coverage is an output produced by funding, not merely concurrent with it | coverage exists before the money and is unchanged by it |
| committee assignment → contributions | `→` sequence, "B needs A to have happened" | the world source reports contributions rise for new committee members and fall for departing ones | contributions are simultaneous with assignment, with no lag |
| official → private lobby role | `→` sequence plus `catalyst` | the person survives their own use and carries access across the boundary | the person's access does not survive the crossing |
| money ⋛ outcome | `⋛` comparison, a threshold relation | money matters above a threshold, not linearly at every level (this is a hypothesis) | the response is linear with no threshold |
| cross-boundary subsidy | `+` conjunction with an external atom | two conditions must both hold for the compound to bind | either condition alone suffices |

Two cautions. First, `⋛` is defined as a threshold relation between two quantities; applying it to institutions is a type question that needs an instrument, not an argument. I record it as strained, not hidden. Second, the table is a census of candidate bonds, not a discovery. Fig 12 :90: *"Six tests, and they separate the seven bonds."* Each row must pass its test against the world.

### Q4: Chain, cycle, or compound?

A chain is the degenerate case, one bond type, no branching (Fig 12 :48). A chain cannot close by construction. The shape claim in `01` is that the four form a cycle whose output re-enters as input. Fig 12 :121: *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

- If the loop is positive, concentration amplifies and a single lever is insufficient. This is the claim.
- If the loop is negative, the same structure damps and a single lever may be sufficient. The shape would survive but the recommendation would flip.
- If the arrangement is a tree with no re-entry, it is a chain, not a cycle, and F3 is withdrawn.

**This is the live open item.** The figures supply the distinction; they do not sign this particular loop. The world sources describe mutual reinforcement but do not measure the sign against a ground.

### Q5: What order does the dependency chain impose?

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

1. Perception: can each channel be observed? Yes. Filings, votes, ownership registries, board seats.
2. Vocabulary: do we have terms for what each affords? Partly. The bond table supplies typed relations, but which holds is unmeasured.
3. **Objective: what counts as concentration?** No instrument. This is the break (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).
4. Planning and learning come after step 3 and cannot be assessed while step 3 is unmeasured.

So the order of work is: characterise the residual, enumerate the habitat, then build or import the discriminator. Effort spent on better money data arrives at a step that step 3 cannot use.

## ANALOGUES, AS THE QUESTION NEEDS

- **Ecology (low overlap):** a trophic cascade is an indirect effect across levels, and the ecology source warns that real food webs include omnivory and external subsidies, so discrete levels blur. This corroborates the multi-step cascade and the union reading, but it also warns against drawing a clean four-node chain.
- **Animal behaviour (low overlap):** dominance and prestige are distinct currencies. This corroborates treating money, access, framing and trust as different operand types rather than one scalar.
- **Mathematics (low overlap):** a heavy-tailed distribution of influence is a fit to be tested, not a visual impression. Clauset, Shalizi and Newman show that common fitting methods can be substantially inaccurate. This is the discipline for the missing anchor in D5.

These analogues corroborate the shape only. They do not decide the verdict; the figures do.

## CONCLUSION

The four channels are best read as a functional group of influence routes, with a stronger isomer claim available if two cases share the atoms but differ in bonds. A flat conjunction is refused because it carries no arrangement. The proposed junction bonds are a census of tests, not findings, with one type strain (`⋛` over institutions) named. The arrangement is a cycle only if the loop sign is positive, and that sign is the one live open item. Dependency order puts the missing objective first. After re-census, one strain and one open item, neither hidden.
