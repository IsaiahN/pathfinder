# WATSON answer: how misinformation and partisan identity alter public judgments of institutional performance

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

Restated in effect terms: why does the gap `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` between what citizens believe about an institution and what that institution actually does stay small in some institution-slots and grow in others? Option A: explain it as one national trust deficit driven by a single bias. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, index per slot, anchor to measured performance, and split the union into two conditions with different instruments. What each leads to: A sends a `general`-mode verdict that aggregates away the live signal and treats an instrument as an anchor; B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open `???` until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and an anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** A four-place relation: belief b with weight w, an information input, institutional performance o′, and expressed judgment a, indexed by institution-slot s and ordered by time t. Arity: four, with an ordered sequence bond and a threshold comparison bond, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: "performance" is a threshold relative to expectation (Fig 12 :85-89), and partisan identity makes the comparison non-exchangeable. Scale: individual citizen loop, population media habitat, institutional stratum, and the time channel across election cycles; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :30).
- **D1 Citizen belief loop.** Actors: citizen, Γ, misinformation items, partisan cues. Currency: prediction error reduction, belief updating. Anchor: a factual benchmark about the institution that does not move with what citizens think. Admitted.
- **D2 Information and media habitat.** Actors: platforms, ranking systems, producers, party elites, fact-checkers. Currency: attention, reach, measured exposure. Anchor: measured exposure and diffusion counts. Admitted.
- **D3 Partisan identity weighting frame.** Actors: party identity, inherited priors, group norms. Currency: `w`, in-group and out-group regard. Anchor: measured party self-identification and voting behavior. Admitted.
- **D4 Institutional performance ground.** Actors: institution, outputs, audits, oversight bodies. Currency: actual performance. Anchor: the performance measurement itself. Admitted.
- **D5 Measurement frame.** Actors: survey designers, experimental researchers, auditors. Currency: instrument validity. Anchor: the experiment that settles a causal claim. Admitted.
- **X1 "The misinformed voter" as a type.** No anchor independent of the frame that uses the label. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 "Partisan bias" as a single scalar.** Already inside D1 and D3 plus their overlap. Absorbed.

Union, not partition: D1/D3, D2/D3 and D1/D4 form overlapping compounds, and D5 overlaps all (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). Strain test: applying Fig 13's physical six-term substrate to citizen belief only fit by renaming identity as energy, so Fig 13 governs the honest-stop line only (strain S1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

Field, author and venue, year, quote, prediction, and whether it corroborates or conflicts. All accessed 2026-11-23.

- **Computational social science / platform selection.** David E. Clementson, HKS Misinformation Review, 2026. Quote: "Feed algorithms allocate scarce attention, reward successful message variants, and give producers immediate feedback about what to make next." Prediction: the habitat selects the message pool even when short-term persuasion is null. **Corroborates** D2. Overlap: low with the other world sources on method, but cites the same Vosoughi result.
- **Ecology / information ecosystems.** Alicia Wanless, Claire Wardle and David A. Scales, HKS Misinformation Review, 2026. Quote: "Structure is both a precondition and a partner for understanding dynamics." Prediction: name units and boundaries before dynamics. **Corroborates** the middle-link method, not the social fact. Overlap: low, genuinely different field.
- **Practitioner studies / institutional measurement.** Gregoire Darcy and Sacha Altay, HKS Misinformation Review, 2026. Quote: "likes are not beliefs, and beliefs are not actions". Prediction: institutions count what they can see and mistake activity for effect. **Corroborates** D5. Overlap: high with Altay et al. 2025 and Kuru and Zhang 2026 through shared authors and venues.
- **Health communication / identity moderation.** Ozan Kuru and Siyu Zhang, HKS Misinformation Review, 2026. Quote: "Among individuals high in scientific populism, pseudoscientific content combined with censorship accusations was associated with lower vaccination and GMO consumption intentions." Prediction: identity-like moderators change the effect of the same content, provisionally. **Corroborates** D3's weight shape, with the binding provenance still unproven. Overlap: high with the other Misinformation Review sources.
- **Human cognition / intervention.** Sacha Altay, Emma Hoes and Magdalena Wojcieszak, Nature Human Behaviour, 2025. Quote: "The treatment enhanced current affairs knowledge ... as well as trust in the news. The treatment had no significant effects on ... affective polarization." Prediction: one lever moves the belief axis and not the identity axis. **Corroborates** the union split. Overlap: moderate, shares Altay with two other sources.
- **Misinformation harms / exposure tails.** Ceren Budak, Brendan Nyhan, David M. Rothschild, Emily Thorson and Duncan J. Watts, Nature, 2024. Quote: "a pattern of low exposure to false and inflammatory content that is concentrated among a narrow fringe with strong motivations to seek out such information". Prediction: an average hides the fringe. **Corroborates** per-slot indexing. Overlap: moderate, shares Nyhan with the intervention literature.
- **Framework essays, canonical reading only.** Isaiah N, The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap, The Hard Problem is About Source, The Library of Babel, 2026, fetched and read 2026-11-23. Key quote from the second: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Predicts that a judgment reading cannot name its source. **Corroborates** the honest stop. Overlap: maximal with the figures, same author, so zero independent witness weight.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict after re-census. The one apparent conflict, treating public opinion as the ground, was returned to the census and re-mapped as strain S2. Agreement among the Misinformation Review sources is one pool talking, not four witnesses (Fig 2 :40).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives: public judgments of institutional performance are corrupted along two overlapping but distinguishable routes, a false input selected by the information habitat and a weight toward inherited prior supplied by partisan identity. The gap is indexed per institution, anchored to measured performance, and kept separate per domain; which claim or cue occupies any slot is a declared gap. The single-scalar and poll-as-ground readings are refused. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled and states refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than deleted (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An experiment that discriminates a false input from a biased weight per named institution would close the `???` slot; an averaged statistic that prospectively predicts per-slot gaps, a performance record that moves with opinion, a single correction with no leftover, or a habitat substitution with no effect would each break a stated finding. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
