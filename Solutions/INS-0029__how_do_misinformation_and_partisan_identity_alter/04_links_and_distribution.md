## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The two-condition shape is visible in the intervention results.** Altay et al. (2025) improved knowledge, discernment and trust by adding reliable news accounts, with no detectable change in affective polarization. That is a clean separation of a belief-axis effect from an identity-axis effect, and it is the single most checkable world bearing on F3 and F5.
2. **The supply-side and demand-side disagreement is itself a census warning.** Darcy and Altay (2026) find practitioners rank institutional distrust low while researchers treat it as central. Clementson (2026) argues ranking changes the message supply even when short-term persuasion is null. These are two different frames of the same residual; their disagreement is why the derivation must census actors, currency and anchor before choosing a figure line, not evidence that one frame wins.
3. **Per-slot indexing is supported by the tail result.** Budak et al. (2024) find exposure concentrated in a narrow fringe. A national trust average discards exactly that structure.
4. **The identity effect is conditional, not universal.** Kuru and Zhang (2026) find effects that depend on scientific populism and censorship accusations, and label their own moderation findings provisional. This corroborates treating identity as a weight with a measured provenance, not as a universal scalar.
5. **Two frameworks that rhyme are still two witnesses only if their evidence pools differ.** Wanless et al. bring physical ecology; Altay and colleagues and Darcy and Altay share authors, venues and a largely Anglophone platform-centric evidence pool. The latter count as one witness for corroboration purposes (Fig 2 :31, :40).

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict after re-census. The one genuine apparent conflict, treating public opinion as the ground, was returned to the census and re-mapped in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7, strain S2.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**  -  *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**  -  *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**  -  *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to observe if the two-condition shape were true before giving them the source list.
- **Fig 2 :40**  -  *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A send selected for people already sympathetic produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figure text, not the summary, is the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the per-slot two-condition shape and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that answer before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure text and formula links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they hold the interiors their agreement is the pool talking.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As currently drafted the shape is internally consistent with the figures; what is not yet available is a ground reading that discriminates the two conditions per institution. Until such a reading exists, the verdict stays `specified` and the `???` slot stays open.
