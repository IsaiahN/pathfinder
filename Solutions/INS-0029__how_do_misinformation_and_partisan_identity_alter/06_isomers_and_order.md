# Supporting derivation: why this is a union, what order the bonds carry, and which analogues hold

## RESIDUAL

The audit leaves three questions open: (1) is the two-condition split a genuine union or a single condition with two labels; (2) what order is carried by the bonds among input, weight and judgment; (3) do the figure's analogues (isomer, functional group, catalyst, cycle) apply here, and which must be refused. This file answers those three and states a falsifier for each.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Is the union real? CORRECTION TO MY OWN EARLY READING

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The Library of Babel essay, fetched 2026-11-23: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

An early reading treated "misinformation" and "partisanship" as two strengths of one attitude, a partition of a single bias dimension. That reading fails the strain test: it requires calling a false input and a weight the same quantity measured at two levels. Re-census: C1 (false atom) and C2 (biased weight) act on different operands and need different instruments (Fig 9 :54-57). The union is real.

**I narrowed my own earlier claim.** I initially wrote that the two conditions are independent. That overstates it: D1 and D3 overlap inside the citizen, so the conditions are *overlapping*, which is exactly what makes it a union rather than a partition. Independence between instruments is still owed (Fig 2 :31), but independence between the conditions is not claimed. Triangulated (Fig 9 + the essay), mode `general`.

### Q2  -  What order do the bonds carry?

- Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** defines `→` as ordered.
- The loop is: false or true claim enters from D2 → it is weighted by `w` in D3 → it updates b in D1 → the citizen compares b against expectations with `⋛` → a judgment a is expressed.
- The comparison `⋛` is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48). Here it compares perceived performance against an expectation, not two institutions. Fig 12 :87: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."**
- Party identity does not enter at the same point as the false claim. It sets the weight before the comparison, which is why the same claim can produce different judgments under different identities. Writing the two as a symmetric conjunction would erase the order the figure requires.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if an experiment shows that changing the weight after the comparison produces the same judgment shift as changing it before, the ordered-bond reading is false and a flat formula would have been adequate. Strained-for-now until that instrument exists.

### Q3  -  Are the two conditions isomers, or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- C1 and C2 do not share the same atoms. C1 is a false proposition entering the belief; C2 is a weighting of evidence. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two members of a **functional group**: both are substructures that fix the judgment outcome, with different qualifiers.
- The stronger isomer reading would require writing both conditions over one shared atom set, for example the same claim and the same evidence under two different bonds (input vs weight). That is a checkable structural claim, but it is not the claim the world literature makes, and I do not assert it.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either condition can be shown to operate without an atom the other requires, the isomer reading fails outright and the functional-group reading stands. This is already the case as written, so the isomer reading is refused here, not kept.

### Q4  -  Do the catalyst and cycle analogues hold?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A media actor that keeps producing reachable claims is catalyst-like; a one-off rumour is a spent part. Nothing in the claim's text records which it is. This is a `specified` analogue, triangulated with Fig 11 :49-51.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** The judgment a feeds back into b and into the habitat's rewards (Clementson 2026: producers retain what worked). This is a real cycle, not a chain, and the sign is decided by whether the feedback increases or decreases contact with the ground.
- **Refused analogue:** treating the citizen as a single convex believer whose "total bias" is the sum of C1 and C2. Fig 12 :125-127 warns that two currencies, bits and actions, do not add; the analogous warning here is that a false input and a weight do not add into one scalar. This is why there is no "bias score" in the answer.

**Falsifier:** a measured case where the feedback sign is demonstrably zero over repeated uses and the judgment still drifts in the same direction. That would break the cycle analogue.

## CONCLUSION

The two conditions are a genuine **union of overlapping conditions**, not a stronger single condition and not isomers. They form a **functional group** that fixes the judgment outcome with different qualifiers. The bond order runs claim then weight then comparison then judgment; part of that order is figure-derived and part is a `specified` binding that needs an instrument. A catalyst analogue holds for persistent producers, a cycle analogue holds for feedback into the habitat, and the additive "bias score" analogue is refused. The record's useful residue is the union plus the order plus the refused scalar.
