# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the shape is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the audit findings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 are applied. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about judgments of institutional performance, with its refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** public judgments about how well an institution is performing should not be treated as one national number. The gap between what citizens believe and what an institution actually does is indexed per institution and per issue, and it grows along two distinguishable routes. The first is a false input about performance, supplied by a media habitat that selects which claims get reach. The second is a weighting of the same evidence toward an inherited prior, supplied by partisan identity. These are two conditions, not one stronger condition. A correction that clears the first does not by itself clear the second. The framework's own rule for this is Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that public opinion is the ground for institutional performance. It is an instrument, and Fig 2 :37-38 warns that instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* I am not claiming a single "bias" score explains the gap, not claiming that one correction closes both routes, and not naming which particular false claim or party cue occupies the gap. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an averaged national statistic that predicts, prospectively and per named institution, which gap will grow next;
> 2. a measured performance record that changes because frames changed their opinion of it;
> 3. a correction that removes the identity-driven part of the gap with no categorically different residue;
> 4. removal of amplification structures that leaves the distribution of judgment gaps unchanged.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The wider literature I actually read is listed with links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]
