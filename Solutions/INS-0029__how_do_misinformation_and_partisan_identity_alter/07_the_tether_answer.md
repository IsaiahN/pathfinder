# The Tether's answer: how misinformation and partisan identity alter public judgments of institutional performance

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Why does the gap between what citizens believe about an institution and what that institution actually does stay small in some institution-slots and grow in others? The gap is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot s, indexed by institution and issue, never aggregated into a national trust score (Fig 1 :19, :22). The ground for `o′` is measured performance and experiments with a factual benchmark, not polls (Fig 2 :29-30, :37-38). The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Index every judgment to a named institution and issue.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A national average throws away the only signal that says where contact with the ground is failing.
2. **Measured performance is the anchor; public opinion is an instrument.** Fig 2 :29 names a measurement or an experiment as what settles a question. Fig 2 :37-38 warns that an instrument *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* A poll reading cannot by itself settle correspondence to performance.
3. **There are two conditions, not one stronger condition.** A false input about performance is selected by the media habitat (Fig 11 :39, :49-51). A partisan weight sets how far belief leans on inherited prior (Symbols table :23). Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* They form a functional group (Fig 12 :124), not isomers and not a single scalar.
4. **The bond order is claim, then weight, then comparison, then judgment.** `→` is ordered (Fig 12 :72). `⋛` is a threshold comparison (Operators table :48). Party identity acts before the comparison, which is why the same evidence maps to different judgments under different identities.
5. **Corrections act on items, not on rewards or weights.** Fig 10 :26 names *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"* as channel failures; Fig 10 :29 says *"keep the anchor reachable."* The world reports the same separation: adding reliable news improved discernment and trust without moving affective polarization (Altay et al. 2025).
6. **The specific claim or cue stays open.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The slot is marked `???` until a source-discriminating instrument names it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that public opinion is the ground. It is the thing to be explained.
- Not "bias is one number". The two conditions do not add into a single scalar (Fig 12 :125-127).
- Not that a correction closes both conditions. A single lever clears one region and leaves the intersection (Fig 9 :55-56).
- Not which false claim or party cue occupies the slot. That is `gap`, not silence.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder discriminates the two conditions per institution.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Per-slot benchmark:** for a named institution, does the judgment gap track measured performance, or does it persist when performance is held constant?
- **Two-condition test:** does a factual correction remove the false-input residue while leaving a categorically different residue attributable to the weight, or does one lever clear both?
- **Weight test:** do judgments shift with party cue while the measured weights on private history versus evidence are unchanged? If yes, F3 is wrong.
- **Habitat test:** does removing amplification structures change the distribution of judgment gaps? If no, F4 is wrong.
- **Standing falsifiers from the audit:** an averaged statistic that prospectively predicts per-slot gaps; a performance record that moves when frames change their minds; a single correction with no leftover; a frame-internal metric that settles a correspondence dispute.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The vocabulary of "misinformation" and "partisan identity" is a visible choice from the world frame, not nature's own list. Declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where the available instruments can no longer distinguish a false input from a biased weight in a single judgment reading, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer one national "trust" score, and it does not claim a single bias explains public judgment. It says the judgment gap belongs to a named institution and issue, that the anchor is measured performance rather than poll agreement, and that the gap grows along two overlapping but distinguishable routes: a false claim selected by the information habitat, and a partisan weight that makes the same evidence map to a different comparison. These form a union with a functional-group structure, not one stronger condition and not a single number, so a correction that clears the first route can leave the second standing. The framework refuses to name which claim or cue occupies any particular slot; it returns the shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the slot. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
