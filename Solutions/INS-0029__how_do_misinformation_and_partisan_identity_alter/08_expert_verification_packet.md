# Expert verification packet: the Tether on institutional judgment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a misinformation or polarization researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No institutional-performance measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Public judgments of institutional performance must be indexed per institution and issue, never averaged into a national trust score. The anchor for those judgments is measured performance and experiments with a factual benchmark, not public opinion, which is an instrument that can be wrong on the axis of correspondence. The judgment gap grows along two overlapping conditions: a false input selected by the information habitat, and a weight toward inherited prior supplied by partisan identity. They are a union with a functional-group structure, not a single scalar, so a correction that clears the false input can leave the identity residue standing. The specific claim or cue occupying a given slot is not named; the framework returns a shape, a set of refuters written in advance, and the kind of source-discriminating instrument that would close the slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal and methodological; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** the gap is per institution-slot, and the anchor is measured performance, not poll agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :37-38, *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
- **Refuter:** an averaged national statistic that predicts prospectively and per named institution which judgment gap will grow next; or a measured performance record that changes because frames changed their opinion of it.

### C2  -  the union claim; checkable against intervention results

- **Statement:** the false-input condition and the weight condition are overlapping but distinct; one lever can clear one and leave the other. Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57, *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **What to check:** Altay, Hoes and Wojcieszak (Nature Human Behaviour, 2025) report that following reliable news accounts improved knowledge and discernment and trust, with no detectable effect on affective polarization. Does that pattern hold in your data, or does one intervention shift both? Kuru and Zhang (HKS Misinformation Review, 2026) report conditional, provisional identity moderation; does the conditionality survive in your setting?
- **Refuter:** a single correction that removes the false-input residue and the identity-weight residue with no categorically different leftover. That collapses the union into a partition and falsifies C2.

### C3  -  the mechanism frame; checkable against the habitat literature

- **Statement:** which false claims reach a citizen is a property of the habitat, and the same actor changes valence with the residual. Fig 11 :39, *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** Clementson (HKS Misinformation Review, 2026) writes that ranking systems *"allocate scarce attention, reward successful message variants, and give producers immediate feedback about what to make next."* Darcy and Altay (2026) report that practitioners and researchers disagree on how much weight to give institutional distrust. Does a habitat-level intervention change the distribution of judgment gaps, or only which individuals are exposed?
- **Refuter:** a controlled habitat substitution that removes the amplification structures and leaves the distribution of judgment gaps unchanged. That breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that public opinion is the ground for institutional performance.
- Not that "bias" is one additive number; the two conditions do not add into a scalar (Fig 12 :125-127).
- Not that a correction closes both conditions.
- Not which false claim or party cue occupies a given slot.
- Not that `w` is literally a measured party-identification coefficient. It is a binding from the world frame onto a held term, recorded as such, mode `specified`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. For a named institution, does the judgment gap track measured performance, or persist with performance held constant?
2. Does a factual correction leave a categorically different residue attributable to the weight?
3. Do judgments shift with party cue while measured weights on private history versus evidence are unchanged?
4. Does removing amplification structures change the distribution of judgment gaps?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 2 :29, :37-38, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one intervention you know: did it move both the belief axis and the identity axis, or only one?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a false input from a biased weight in a single judgment reading, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
