## 0. The residual in effect terms

**Question as effect.** A system holds a library Γ with closure(Γ). A situation X has never been an operand of that system's perception, so no reading of X is held in Γ. The question asks whether there is a molecule φ that (a) predicts what X will do, and (b) pays when the system acts. "Useful" is not a feeling of understanding. It is a ground relation: the model changes contact in a way the ground pays.

**Two options and where each leads.**

- **A, yes without qualification.** Any system with a compositional library can produce a fluent account of an unencountered X. This leads to treating coherence as evidence, and to sending a recording that looks like knowledge (Fig 4 :25).
- **B, no without direct contact.** Only direct exposure to X can produce a model of X. This leads to forbidding prediction before observation, which the figures explicitly permit: "theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to." (Fig 6 :38), and "Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point" (Fig 6 :38).

**Shape bound as an ordinary term, written before the run.** Per the formula's "Direction" rule, the sought shape is made bindable so it can accrue a residual like anything else. The bound is: a useful model of an unencountered X requires three conditions, (i) the residual of X is describable in effect terms from contact the system already has, (ii) the needed atoms are either in closure(Γ) or reachable by import from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test, and (iii) the ground settles φ when contact eventually occurs. This is a **shape**, a set of constraints, not an inventory of models. It is refutable at section 4.

## 1. Domain census

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: at least two conditions, construction and settlement, so R is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Symmetry: "constructible" and "useful" are ordered and asymmetric. Constructible is necessary but not sufficient for useful; useful implies settled, settled does not imply constructed by this system. Scale: construction is first order inside closure(Γ); usefulness is settled at the ground; only methods cross back down a scale (Fig 4 :22).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** A domain needs a nameable anchor or it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 composition inside one closure | atoms of Γ, seven bonds, negation, candidate φ | cost(φ) and left(R,φ) in bits; reachability | closure(Γ) as wall, ground for any later settlement | partition boundary: what composes across the two kinds |
| D2 import between frames | frame A holding the residual, peer frame B with different closure, the effect description | atoms crossing back, provenance, shadow test, independence debit | the ground, and the difference of closures | union surplus: two frames reach what neither reaches alone |
| D3 settlement and usefulness | the acting system, instrument, experiment, board, outcome | residuals that pay; contact | the ground, which does not update | verdict domain: nothing here is decided by agreement |
| D4 habitat and capability | actors, conditions, relations, seat, inherited methods | contact cascades | the ground, which settles, and the habitat, which updates | enumeration, never composition |

**Partition or union.** Union. D1 and D2 are both routes to a candidate; D3 is a different quantity that sorts the candidate. Averaging them would hide the load-bearing fact that D3 can fail while D1 and D2 succeed. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Describe the residual in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28) before choosing a frame; settle each candidate before composing over it (Fig 5 :69-70); refuse before mint when a held term is false (formula step 2); only then promote a method across a scale (Fig 4 :22). Optimising a link above the break cannot rescue one below it (Fig 3 :19).

**Strain test applied before conclusions.** Fig 6 :23 says the bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. Fig 6 :38 says theory routinely precedes its instrument. Read literally these strain against each other. Re-census: the theory's *atoms* were acquired in other frames (the history of physics and mathematics), and what precedes the instrument is a *shape*, not a settled model. Different quantities, so no strain survives the re-census. The strain is recorded at section 7 rather than smoothed away.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Construction is genuinely possible inside a closure, but novelty is library-relative (decisive for "can construct")

Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Fig 5 :28: "keep the gap, look for an import". Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."

A system can compose a candidate for a situation it has not met, because the arrangements over held atoms do not run out. But "new" means not already in the library. The model of X may be a rearrangement of things the system already had. That is still construction, and it is not the same as having met X. The curiosity drive makes this reachable from a cold start: Fig 5 :47: "So a system that starts with nothing can still be shaped, by seeking the gap that is large and compressible, which is the same quantity the acceptance test scores."

**Refuter.** Show a composition over held atoms that is claimed to be a model of X while none of its atoms or bonds has any bearing on any reading X returns. It is not a model; it is a poem. If the system cannot tell the difference, the reachability claim fails at the support gate.

### F2 — The bound moves through contact with a different closure, not through direct contact with X

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :54: "The atom cannot be derived from inside A. But the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

This is the direct answer to the question's hard half. A system does not need to have met X to have a model of X. It needs a residual described well enough that a frame with a different closure can be recognised as already holding the needed shape. World analogue: Gentner's structure-mapping theory, where analogy is "the mapping or alignment of the elements of source and target" and a known source yields a predicate for the target. That is secondary corroboration only; the figures decide.

**Refuter.** Produce a case where a model of X is constructed with no direct contact, no held atom predicting X, and no lineage of contact with any frame whose closure differs. That breaks Fig 6 :23.

### F3 — Theory may precede the instrument, but the instrument still settles

Fig 6 :38: "theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to." Fig 6 :39: "instruments do not have to improve by small steps. A jump in resolution is fine". Fig 6 :40: "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."

The figures license Neptune-style prediction. They also bound it. The prediction can be a shape that says where to point; it cannot be a reading of something that has never registered anywhere at any resolution. So a model of a wholly unencountered situation is reachable *as a prediction*, not as a measurement. The three-condition shape in section 0 is exactly this: conditions (i) and (ii) can be met before the instrument, condition (iii) cannot.

**Refuter.** Show a system deriving a ground-paying model of an X for which nothing, at any resolution, ever registered, and doing so without importing an axis from another frame. That breaks Fig 6 :40, not merely the budget.

### F4 — Constructible is not useful; settlement is a separate quantity

Fig 5 :69: "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient." Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one." Fig 5 :72: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

This is the load-bearing limit. A fluent, self-consistent model of X can be perfectly wrong. Internal coherence scores the mechanism, not the direction of the question. The framework returns "a kind of thing" (Fig 9 :32), and "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides" (Fig 9 :42). "Useful" is not a property the model carries; it is a relation between the acting system and an anchor that does not update.

**Refuter.** Show a model of an unencountered X that pays at the ground with no settlement event at all, construction alone sufficing. That breaks Fig 5 :69-70 and Fig 1 :23.

### F5 — Agreement among frames cannot substitute for the ground, and shared evidence is one witness

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Ten cognitive scientists agreeing that a model "feels right" is a convention, not a verdict. This also governs this very document: the second seat in `11_audit.md` reads the same figures and the same essays, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

**Refuter.** Show two frames that draw on exactly the same evidence settling a model by agreeing. That breaks Fig 2 :40.

### F6 — "Never directly encountered" does not mean "out of contact"

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three". Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

The habitat is enumerated, not composed, and contact extends outward until the cascade stops mattering. A model of a situation with no *direct* contact can still be built from methods and head starts inherited through a habitat, and from second-order contact. This dissolves an apparent paradox in the question: the system need not have met X to be in contact with things that determine X.

**Refuter.** Show an agent-only improvement that moves the ground for a situation it never contacted. Fig 11 :72 already names this as the standing refuter for the capability claim.

### F7 — A substituted habitat proves wiring, not capability

Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

The machine-learning source "World Models" reports training an agent inside a hallucinated dream and transferring the policy back into the actual environment. That is a real result, but on re-census it is a substituted habitat: the model was trained on the same environment it is returned to. It is not evidence for a model of a situation never encountered. The framework-author essay "The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap" says "This is why world models will not work. You cannot recreate our reality. It is combinatorically impossible." The conflict is recorded, not used to flip the verdict; re-census shows the two claims address different objects. A genuine test would transfer to an environment whose ground the model has never contacted.

**Refuter.** Show a policy trained purely in a generative model transferring to a genuinely novel environment with a ground the model never registered, with no import from that environment. That would move this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

### F8 — The method is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 7 :34: "the truth is where the schedule is heading". Fig 3 :13: "a route to that objective, chosen under incomplete information".

A model of X can be useful *provisionally* to steer a probe, without being settled. Fig 3 licenses planning under incomplete information. But provisional steering is a budget use. It is not a verdict that the model is right. Keeping these apart is what prevents a useful search heuristic from being promoted into a claim about the world.

**Refuter.** Show a system counting a budget filter as a settlement and producing a correct verdict without a ground reading. That breaks Fig 9 :60.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." Written before the conclusion of this audit.

The shape claim in section 0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A system produces a ground-paying model of X with no direct contact, no different-closure frame in its lineage, and no held atom predicting X. Breaks Fig 6 :23 and Fig 11 :40.
2. A model of an unencountered situation pays at the ground with no settlement event, construction alone sufficing. Breaks Fig 5 :69-70 and Fig 1 :23.
3. A system derives a ground-paying model of an X for which nothing ever registered at any resolution, without importing a new axis. Breaks Fig 6 :40.
4. Two frames drawing on the same evidence settle the model by agreement. Breaks Fig 2 :40.
5. For the isomer claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md`: two models with identical atom sets and different provenance behave identically in every ground test. Breaks the Figure 12 :117 difference.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

2. Keep the two conditions separate in every sentence: constructible is a reach claim, useful is a ground claim. Never say "can construct" when the evidence only supports "can compose".
3. Put the derivation before any mapping to cognitive science. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them (Fig 8 :50).

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Constructible at all?** Yes in shape. Fig 13 :115 and Fig 12 :104. The arrangements over held atoms do not run out.
2. **Must the system have met X?** No. Fig 6 :23 moves the bound on different-closure contact, and Fig 6 :38 licenses theory before instrument.
3. **How is the frame found?** By describing the residual in effect terms first (Fig 9 :27-28; Fig 8 :53). Matching is a one-pass check, not a search (Fig 9 :29).
4. **Is the result a name?** No. Fig 9 :32 gives a kind of thing; Fig 13 :133 gives a shape rather than a name.
5. **Is it an import?** Only if it crosses from a closure that differs, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 10 :34-35). If the atom was already held, it is a binding and adds no atom (Fig 6 :25).
6. **Is it useful?** Not until the ground pays (Fig 5 :70). No measurement here, so `candidate`.
7. **What would reverse it?** A settlement event, or one of the five refuters at section 4. Agreement is not one of them.

**Updated verdict.** The question's yes/no splits. Yes to constructibility of a shape, through composition and through different-closure import. Not yet to usefulness, which requires settlement and cannot be conferred by coherence, analogy, number of agreeing readers, or a synthetic test habitat. Mode `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structural shape. Strains after re-census: none live inside the figures; one live scope conflict between a world ML source and a framework-author essay, named above and used only to sharpen the test.
