# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 4 are checked against the verbatim figure text. A two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output is not stable.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about models of situations a system has never met

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like checked against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output, `specified` not `grounded`:** a system can construct a model of a situation it has never directly encountered, but "construct" and "useful" are two different things. I am claiming three necessary conditions for a useful one, written down before I ran anything:
>
> 1. the gap the situation produces has to be describable in effect terms from contact the system already has;
> 2. the parts of the model are either already in the system's vocabulary, or arrive from another frame whose vocabulary differs, with the source recorded;
> 3. the situation has to eventually settle the model. An instrument, an outcome, a board that pays. Until that happens the model is a candidate, however coherent it feels.
>
> Condition 2 is the part I most want checked. The claim is that a system does not need to have met a situation to model it, but it does need contact of a different kind: another frame that already holds the missing part. Condition 3 is the part most often dropped. A fluent model and a useful model are not the same thing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that analogy always transfers, that a generated simulation is the same as capability, that a large agreement among researchers settles anything, or that this note is a measurement. I am also not claiming that a model must be named before it can be useful. The framework returns a shape, and the shape here is the three conditions above, not a list of models.
>
> **What would refute it, written before the run:** a system producing a ground-paying model of a never-encountered situation with no direct contact, no different-vocabulary source, and no held part that predicted anything; or a model that pays at the ground with no settlement event at all; or two groups drawing on the same evidence settling the model by agreeing. If any of those holds, I withdraw the shape.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. What would you count as a failure here? If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The thirteen figures and the formula, figures folder, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - How Intelligence Actually Works, including the definition of general intelligence as answering problems never seen before: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Decoding Tacit Knowledge, on contact and what can travel without it: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether what you got is general, specified, or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1.** The reader checks the three conditions and the refuter without seeing the figures. Record their verdict before any theory is shown. A reply that says "this fails because..." is the useful outcome.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the interiors.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and labels their own mode.

No stage asks for trust. No stage asks for belief. The only thing requested is a failure the author did not see.
