**Question:** Can systems construct useful mental models of situations they have never directly encountered?

**Source:** reasoning.txt line 58

**Section:** 5. Memory, representation, and world models

**Mode:** `specified` on the conditions for constructibility and usefulness, `general` on the structural shape. Nothing here is `grounded`: no measurement was taken, and this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual in effect terms.** A system holds a library Γ with a closure. A situation X has never been an operand of the system's perception, so no reading of X is held. What is at issue is whether there exists a molecule φ in the system's vocabulary that (a) predicts the behaviour of X, and (b) pays when the system acts. Option A: yes, construction plus a ground test suffices. Option B: no, only direct contact with X can produce such a model. A sends a fluent composition that may be perfectly wrong. B forbids prediction that precedes its instrument, which the figures explicitly license. What an answer would look like is written as an ordinary term before the run, per the formula's "Direction" rule: a useful model of an unencountered X exists iff the residual is describable in effect terms, the needed atoms are held or reachable through a frame whose closure differs, and the ground settles φ when contact eventually occurs. That shape is bound and allowed to accrue a residual; see `01` section 0.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, no purchased or scraped lists.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan. All venues in `03` are public or opt-in.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not upgrade the label. "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." (Seat Map :283).
2. Selecting for agreement is disqualifying. "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." (Seat Map :163).
3. Showing a reader the full framework before they judge the output spends their independence. "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42).
