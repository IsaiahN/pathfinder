## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is "constructible" symmetric with "useful"? No, the relation is ordered

- Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one."
- Fig 5 :69: "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."
- Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides".
- Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

Therefore: constructible is necessary for useful but not sufficient; useful implies that settlement occurred; settlement does not imply that this system did the constructing (another frame's settled atom can be imported). The relation is a one-way order, not an equivalence. Writing it as `constructible → useful` is a claim about conditions, not about time: the ground can settle a model long after construction.

The word "useful" is reserved for ground-paying.

### Q2 — Are two models with the same atoms the same substance? No, if their origin differs

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".
- Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."

Two models of an unencountered situation can be built from the same held atoms. One arrives by convergent derivation from the system's own residual; the other is imported from a frame whose closure differs. In the artefact they look identical. Under Fig 8 they are different substances because the surplus exists only where closures differ. Under Fig 13 the lineage is literally part of the term, so they are not the same object. The provenance is not a footnote; it is the difference.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If two models with identical atom sets and different recorded provenance behave identically in every available ground test, and no instrument can discriminate them, then the isomer claim for this question is false and the weaker reading stands: they are one term with two stories. The test must be run against a ground, not against agreement between the two authors.

### Q3 — What order do the operations run in?

- Fig 9 :43: "The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
- Fig 5 :69-70: settle before composing over; Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

Order: describe residual in effect terms → decide whether the parts are in closure (SEARCHED, the cheapest question) → if not, look up a different-closure frame (OUTWARD) → import with source gate and shadow test → compose the candidate → settle at the ground → promote only the method across a scale. Inward instrument refinement and outward import are opposed tracks and must not be read against each other (formula step 7).

A vocabulary that skips the order can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it. Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

### Q4 — Does the mirror chain help? No

- Fig 7 :34: "the truth is where the schedule is heading".
- Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Adding more systems that describe the same residual from the same evidence does not add a ground. It adds vocabulary. This is the structural reason the outreach in `02_outreach_email.md` asks for a failure and never for agreement.

## CONCLUSION

Constructible and useful are ordered, not symmetric, and they are different substances in Fig 12's sense: one is priced in bits inside a closure, the other in actions at a ground. Two models over the same atom set differ by provenance, and the lineage is part of the term. The operational order is residual first, frame second, import before composition, settlement before promotion. The isomer claim is checkable and has a falsifier; it is not asserted as settled.
