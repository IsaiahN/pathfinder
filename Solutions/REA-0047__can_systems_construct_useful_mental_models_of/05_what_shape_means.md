## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README gives the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133: "A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the answer itself. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is not a catalogue of mental models. It is a three-part constraint: residual describable in effect terms, parts held or reached across a closure boundary, and ground settlement. That constraint can be written and refuted before any model is proposed.

## 2. How the shape is obtained here

- *Library of Babel* on vocabulary: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." The searcher starts with the vocabulary already held and looks for recurring new terms that overlap with it.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." This is the reason condition (i) is stated as an effect description: a description in private shorthand cannot be matched to another closure.
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place", because by then "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

Applied here, that means the shape is not read off a list of famous mental-model experiments. It is derived from what a system with a closure can and cannot do, then checked against what the experiments report.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA step 5: "SETTLE — the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 10 :34-35).

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source", and several underlying causes can produce the same reading. "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." So a fluent account of an unencountered situation names a source from a projection. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.

## 4. The exact error this question invites

The question says "useful mental models of situations they have never directly encountered". It is tempting to answer by pointing to analogy, imagination, or a trained world model and declaring success. That skips condition (iii).

*How Intelligence Actually Works* defines the target: "The definition of what we call General Intelligence is that a system can answer questions to problems it's never seen before and in environments it has no training data for." The same essay immediately warns that the recursion stops "when nothing you have registers a difference from it", and that "indiscernibility is not identity." A model that produces no further residual is not thereby in contact with the ground.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the error: "General intelligence is not reliable success on the novel, nobody has that. It is the right kind of failure: making progress through search-and-adaptation rather than confabulation." A human facing novel structure "builds a partial model, tests it, notices it is wrong, and revises, even in failure, the mind moves; the failure is a search." A model that cannot be surprised has no way to register that it is wrong.

So the specific forbidden move is treating **constructibility** as if it settled **usefulness**. Fig 5 :72: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."

## 5. Q answered: what does "shape" mean for this question?

**It is the constraint set, not a model inventory.** Concretely:

- the model's residual must be described from contact that exists, even if that contact is indirect (Fig 11 :52);
- its parts must be held, or cross from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23);
- its usefulness is a later relation to a ground, not a property of the artefact (Fig 5 :70, Fig 1 :23);
- the answer permits "reachable as a shape" while refusing "settled as useful" (Fig 13 :134).

**Does any source licence a stronger answer?** No. The human-cognition sources document construction and transfer; the instrument-discrimination essay explains why construction alone cannot settle; the learning-theory sources separate adaptation from stored skill. None supplies a ground reading here.

## 5b. Correction to my own section 3

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any component of the model. That overstates the rule, and the same correction was made in the exemplar. The README phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". It governs a primitive not held in Γ. Here the atoms are held: space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network, plus the seven bonds. Naming them is a binding from a frame, not the invention of a new primitive.

What remains true, and is **not** retracted: naming the parts does not certify the model. *They're Scanning Books* warns that "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." A visible choice can be revised; a certified model needs a ground. Both statements are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous cognitive scientists who like the shape gives the claim a budget, not a verdict. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` has no personal addresses and no likelihood-of-agreement column.

**This document is a simulation.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." And the canonical article adds that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and cannot settle anything it reports on.
