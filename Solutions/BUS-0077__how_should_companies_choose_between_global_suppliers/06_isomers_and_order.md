## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the order, and why is it not a ranking by importance?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Applying the five links of Fig 3 to the sourcing decision:

1. **Perception.** Can the firm tell what is in front of it and distinguish it from what was there before? Concretely: is the supplier map current, and are the sub-tier actors visible at all? Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the firm have terms for what the configuration affords: what can be moved, qualified, switched, buffered, blocked? Without these, "dual source" is a label, not a term with a test.
3. **The objective.** Can it say what would count as progress here, in its own terms? This is where the anchors are named. Fig 12 :112: "A goal is a comparison."
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information. This is where the configurations are compared.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it forward. Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."

The break is most likely between 3 and 4. Fig 3 :25-27: a link with no instrument "is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." In sourcing, most analytical effort goes into step 4, optimizing a cost model, because cost models have instruments. The objective step, where an immovable anchor should be named, often has none. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** show a firm whose objective step has no instrument and whose planning step nevertheless changes the ground in the intended way, measured on the same anchors. If that holds, the dependency order is not required here.

### Q2: Are two sourcing configurations isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :124: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Take two configurations over the same bill of suppliers, lanes, inventory and qualification records:

- **Configuration G (global).** One distant source, one lane, low inventory, qualification recorded once at selection.
- **Configuration R (regional).** Same supplier set relocated into a nearer habitat, same lane type shortened, higher inventory, qualification repeated locally.

Case 1, written over the **full shared atom set**, the two share every atom and differ only in the bonds between them (single source becomes dual source, direct becomes sub-tier, qualification once becomes qualification repeated). They are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a cost rollup that records only the ingredient list, and decisive in behavior when a disruption arrives. This is the strong claim worth testing.

Case 2, written as the firm usually writes them, G and R share only the product and the objective. They are **not isomers**. They are two members of the same **functional group**, the structure "configuration over a supply habitat," with different atoms in the slots (Fig 12 :124). The weaker claim is then: both belong to a kind whose presence fixes what counts as a decision, and the qualifier says which member.

**Triangulation status:** the isomer definition is single-source for the figure (Fig 12). The mapping of real configurations onto shared atoms is `specified` and unmeasured. It is not corroborated by the world sources, which do not use this vocabulary.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either configuration can be written without an atom the other requires, with no change in behavior on the anchors, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3: Do catalyst and cycle appear in the habitat?

Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

- **Catalyst.** A qualification record, a test harness or a trusted relationship that survives its own use is catalyst-like. It is spent and regenerating in different ways, and a flat cost record does not say which a term is. A supplier relationship that only worked once is not a catalyst.
- **Cycle.** The recovery loop is a cycle when the output of a disruption response re-enters as input to the configuration: the firm redesigns, and the redesign changes what the next disruption will test. The sign matters. A reinforcing loop that removes all slack closes into brittleness; a balancing loop that restores slack closes into resilience. A linear bill of materials cannot close at all, which is why a cost-only model cannot represent recovery.

These are analogues, not new figure lines. They are `general` on the structure and `specified` on the mapping to a particular firm. **Falsifier:** show a supply configuration whose recovery behavior is fully captured by a chain with no re-entrant output, on the same anchors. If so, the cycle analogue adds nothing here and should be dropped.

### Q4: What is the union of global and regional, and is it a final configuration?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room." Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."

The union of global and regional is not a third, final configuration to be adopted once and for all. It is a compound with its own loop. A portfolio that holds both is a different molecule, not a compromise between two opinions. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Selection, here the ground's verdict on cost, recovery and capability, decides which arrangement survives.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a sourcing arrangement after which no further question about cost, recovery or capability can be asked. That would be a last room, and it would break Fig 7.

## CONCLUSION

The order is dependency order, with the likely break at the objective step: before a firm can plan a configuration it must be able to say what would count as progress and name an anchor that will not move with its preference. The two configurations are isomers only under the stronger writing where they share the full atom set and differ in the bonds; as usually written they are members of one functional group. Catalyst and cycle are legitimate `general` analogues for surviving qualification and re-entrant recovery, with stated falsifiers. The union of global and regional is a compound with its own ground, not a final configuration.

Nothing here requires a new primitive, an import that moves the wall, or a country name. The one strain is the operand type of ⋛ when both configurations are settled and serve as atoms at the comparison level; the Operators table defines it as a threshold relation between two quantities, and whether a settled configuration counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument.
