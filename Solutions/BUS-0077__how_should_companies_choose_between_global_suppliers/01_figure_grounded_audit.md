## 0. The claim under audit

The practical question arrives as a binary: global supplier, or regional or domestic production. Naive Option A says choose the global supplier because the unit price is lower. Naive Option B says choose domestic because it is closer and safer. Both are answers given before the residual is described, and Fig 9 :27-28 warns what happens then: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

**The residual in effect terms.** For one product slot s, the residual R is the gap between the configuration the firm predicted would perform and what the ground returns when realized landed cost, lead-time variance, a quality escape, or a disruption arrives. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single company-wide "source globally" or "source locally" rule aggregates away the only signal that says where to look.

**The bound answer term.** Written down before the run, the sought-for shape is a *figure-licensed sourcing decision*: a procedure that unbundles the decision into conditions, anchors each against a ground that does not move with belief, counts second-order contact, treats relocation as habitat substitution, prices in two incommensurable currencies, triangulates low-overlap frames, and keeps cuts ranked and reversible. It returns a shape and a route, not a country name. Fig 5 :65-66 makes this a discipline rather than a step: write the sought-for shape as an ordinary term and "let it accrue a residual like anything else."

## 1. Figure census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** The residual has at least five conditions in play at once: landed cost, lead time and its variance, quality and conformity, disruption exposure and recovery, and the coordination or tacit-knowledge burden. It is not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." This is a **union compound**, with its own loop and its own ground, not a list of separate questions to be averaged.
- **Symmetry.** Distance and direction are not interchangeable. A supplier three steps up a sub-tier chain is not equivalent to one in the next region. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." So "global is bad, local is good" is not a symmetry the framework licenses; the valence flips with the residual.
- **Scale.** First-order contact is the direct supplier. Second-order contact is the sub-tier and the lane. Third-order contact is the regulatory, climatic or institutional condition several steps out. Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A decision that prices only the direct supplier is scoping to direct contact.

### 1.2 Domain census: actors, currency, anchor

A domain requires a nameable anchor. If there is none, it is not yet a domain.

| # | domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable, does not move with belief) | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Landed economics | purchase price, freight, duty, inventory, rework, opportunity cost | money | realized total landed cost under actual conditions, not the quoted unit price | one condition of the union |
| D2 | Disruption and recovery | supplier sites, lanes, backup capacity, inventory, alternate sources | time to recover, service level | observed time-to-recover in a real disruption, or a tabletop test with a fixed failure | one condition of the union |
| D3 | Capability and habitat | engineers, suppliers, institutions, conditions, relations | contact and adaptation | realized capability of the firm plus its supply habitat, which cannot be improved by tuning the firm alone | one condition of the union |
| D4 | Verification and governance | decision makers, internal frames, outside checkers | agreement versus residual | the ground, never agreement; an external frame with a different closure | the checking loop over all three |

**No domain is admitted without an anchor.** "Brand reputation" and "strategic fit" were considered and rejected at this stage: neither has a nameable anchor that fails to move when the firm changes its mind, so they are not domains yet. They are preferences, and Fig 5 :62-63 warns that a preference held as a shape predicts nothing and accrues no residual.

### 1.3 Partition or union

The four rows are not a partition of one decision. D1, D2 and D3 are overlapping conditions of one union compound; D4 is the checking loop over them. Fig 9 :54-55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing." A cost-only lever clears the region where only cost binds and leaves the cost-and-disruption intersection standing. That intersection is a specific compound, not a harder cost problem.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perceive and slot the residual (Fig 1 :19). Without this, no later link has an input.
2. Unbundle the union (Fig 9 :54) before searching.
3. Describe each condition in effect terms and name its anchor (Fig 2 :29-30); "describe it before you go looking" (Fig 9 :27).
4. Range each frame against its anchor separately (Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it.").
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), then keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41).

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :23: "It is a reading of nothing." A sophisticated cost model applied at step 5 when step 3 never named an anchor is a reading of nothing.

## 2. Findings

### F1: The decision is a union compound, so unbundle before searching (decisive for the question's form)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

**Reading.** "Global versus local" is a single lever applied to a bundle. It clears cost-only cases and leaves the cost-and-disruption intersection standing. The correct move is to split into D1, D2 and D3, then recombine only after each condition has its own anchor. Verdict for D1, D2, D3: `triangulated` (Fig 9 and Fig 1 are low-overlap: one governs union structure, the other governs per-slot indexing).

**Refuter.** Show that all five conditions reduce to one scalar that every frame accepts as its own anchor, or show that the intersection of cost and disruption behaves exactly as the harder cost case. Either breaks the union reading.

### F2: No figure line names a country, and that is a gap, not a silence

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

**Reading.** After a genuine re-census of all 13 figures, the Operators table and the Symbols table, no line governs the choice of a specific supplier, country or region. Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." The framework returns a shape, not a name. Per the canonical rule, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). So the specific-choice sub-question is recorded as `gap`. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." This is a limit of what the figures govern, not proof that no sourcing decision exists.

**Refuter.** Produce a verbatim figure line that selects among configurations by a named property of geography or scale, with no import and no strain. Until then the gap stands.

### F3: Each condition needs an anchor that does not update with belief

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

**Reading.** For D1 the anchor is realized total landed cost, not the quoted unit price or a consultant's spreadsheet, because the latter move when the firm pushes on them. For D2 the anchor is observed or testable recovery, not a manager's confidence. For D3 the anchor is realized capability of the firm-and-habitat pair, not an internal maturity score. The world source on total cost of ownership corroborates D1: "TCO recognises that ownership costs are significantly greater than the cost of purchasing or acquiring a product." The world source on supply chain resilience corroborates D2: "Relying on a single supplier, or on suppliers concentrated in one location, can leave an organization exposed when supply is interrupted." World sources are secondary; they do not decide.

Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 1 are low-overlap: one defines the anchor, the other disqualifies self-scoring).

**Refuter.** Show a named anchor for one condition that changes its answer when a frame's belief changes, and show the decision still settles. That collapses the anchor clause.

### F4: Second-order contact must be counted; first-order price is not the ground

Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."

**Reading.** A global configuration buys a first-order price improvement. If that improvement does not change the firm's contact with the conditions that actually settle D2 and D3, it changes nothing that matters. The sub-tier, the lane and the institutional condition are three steps out. The ECB world source corroborates the cascade: "the 'bullwhip-effect', a standard amplification channel phenomenon whereby firms build up their inventories because they are expecting robust demand amid a shortage of key inputs in the production process." The engineering world source on single points of failure corroborates the concentration case: "A single point of failure (SPOF) is a part of a system that would stop the entire system from working if it were to fail." Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 11 and Fig 4 govern different objects: contact cascade versus scale filter).

**Refuter.** Demonstrate a configuration in which a single point of failure has no second-order contact effect under any disruption, or a case where an improvement that leaves contact unchanged moves the ground. Either breaks the clause.

### F5: Relocation is habitat substitution, not removal of risk

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails." Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

**Reading.** Reshoring or nearshoring does not delete the global habitat. It substitutes a different one. The firm inherits local actors it did not choose, and the capabilities the distant configuration sustained are lost invisibly until the goal fails. The offshoring world source corroborates with a named failure: Otis Elevators "failed to consider the consequences of the new location and tried to do too much at once." The total-cost-of-ownership source corroborates the hidden-cost side: "TCO includes a variety of cost of doing business items, for example, ship and re-ship, and opportunity costs." The Ivanov operations-research source corroborates the structural point: a viable supply chain needs "multiple structural SC designs for supply–demand matching and, most importantly, establishment and control of adaptive mechanisms for transitions between the structural designs." (Quoted from the source opened; the publisher version uses standard typography.) Verdict: `single-source` for the substitution law itself (Fig 11 alone), with world corroboration from three independent fields, which is secondary and is not counted as a second figure witness.

**Refuter.** Show a relocation that removes, rather than substitutes, the habitat: nothing new is inherited, nothing is lost, and the goal cannot fail for a reason created by the move. That breaks the substitution clause.

### F6: Two currencies, bits and actions, do not add

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :127: "They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them." Fig 12 :128-129: "A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute."

**Reading.** The cost of *describing* a configuration (coordination, documentation, supplier qualification records, systems) and the cost of *settling* whether it holds (running a qualification, a pilot, a tabletop disruption test) are different units. A single money-only TCO number merges them and will not notice the shortfall. This is not a refusal to use money; it is a refusal to add incommensurable readings. Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

**Refuter.** Show a conversion that makes description cost and settlement cost commensurable without losing either reading, and show a decision that is improved by the sum. Otherwise the two-currency clause stands.

### F7: Triangulation needs low overlap, not more agreement

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

**Reading.** A finance cost model, a resilience engineering frame and a habitat or capability frame draw on different evidence pools, so their agreement about a configuration means something. Three internal managers using the same spreadsheet are one frame wearing three names. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost of a wrong sourcing decision is high enough to buy at least two genuinely different frames. Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 8 are low-overlap: one defines independence, the other locates the surplus).

**Refuter.** Show that two frames drawing on one evidence pool count as two witnesses for this decision, or that a larger room supplies an anchor. Either breaks the clause.

### F8: Filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 9 :41: "so keep every cut ranked and reversible." Fig 6 amendment :42-43: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."

**Reading.** "This supplier is easy to work with" and "this region is politically aligned" are budget filters: they decide what gets tried first. They are not witnesses for the verdict. A shortlist must keep every cut ranked and reversible, because adding a refusing term lowers reach by addition, not deletion: a decision to "never single-source" removes the standing of that option without striking it from the record. Fig 9 :40: "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up."

**Refuter.** Show a filter that is also a legitimate witness for this decision, with a nameable anchor that does not move with the firm's preference. Until then the filter or witness separation stands.

### F9: The comparison is second-order and settleable only at the ground

Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it."

**Reading.** Each candidate configuration is first-order and must be settled on its own before the comparison between them is meaningful. The comparison ⋛ is a threshold relation between two quantities, not a merge into a single supplier. The union of global and regional options is not a theory of everything: Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room," and Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Verdict: `single-source` for the second-order rule (Fig 12), `triangulated` for "no last configuration" (Fig 7 and the canonical README are low-overlap).

**Refuter.** Show that a settled configuration cannot serve as an atom for a higher comparison, or that a final sourcing configuration closes further questions. Either breaks the clause.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A heterogeneous sourcing decision is settled by a single scalar metric that moves only with the firm's belief (breaks F3 and the anchor test, Fig 2 :29-33).
2. A configuration with one supplier, one lane or one region is shown to have no second-order contact effect under any disruption (breaks F4, Fig 11 :52).
3. Relocating production is shown to remove rather than substitute the habitat: nothing new inherited, nothing lost, and the goal unaffected by the move (breaks F5, Fig 11 :66-69).
4. Two frames drawing on one evidence pool are shown to count as two independent witnesses for this decision (breaks F7, Fig 2 :40).
5. Description cost and settlement cost are shown to be commensurable by a conversion that preserves both readings and improves the decision (breaks F6, Fig 12 :125-127).
6. A verbatim figure line is produced that selects a country or region with no import and no strain (closes the F2 gap).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until a real firm's cost, recovery and quality data are read against these anchors, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. State the residual per product and supplier slot before naming any option (F1, F2).
2. Unbundle into landed economics, disruption and recovery, and capability and habitat; name an immovable anchor for each (F3).
3. Count second-order contact explicitly; price first-order unit cost in its own currency only (F4, F6).
4. Treat any move as habitat substitution; list what must be reproduced and what will be inherited before the move, not after (F5).
5. Use at least two low-overlap frames; never pool agreement from one evidence pool as corroboration (F7).
6. Use preference and political alignment as budget filters only; keep every cut ranked and reversible (F8).
7. State the mode on every sentence; this is `general` on the shape and `specified` on the predictions, not a grounded verdict (F10).
8. Report the gap at F2 honestly. The figures do not name a winner; a decision still has to be made in the world.

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the procedure and the refuter, not a country recommendation.

### Round-trip note

Condensing the figure set into the seven-clause answer term loses at least three constraints: the type of ⋛ at second order, the per-slot indexing of R, and the fact that the anchor must not update. The condensation is a lossy generator, not an identity. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. ## 6. Verdict of the audit

The shape is internally consistent and survives the re-census. It is `general` on the procedure shape and `specified` on the per-domain predictions, and it is not `grounded`. The specific question "which supplier or which country" is a `gap` at the level of names, reported plainly rather than filled with a preference. One strain remains live, the operand type of ⋛ when both options are settled configurations now serving as atoms. It is named, not hidden. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict; the only recorded conflicts are with naive readings that the figures already refuse.
