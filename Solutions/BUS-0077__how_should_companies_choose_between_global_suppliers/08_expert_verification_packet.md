# Expert verification packet: the Tether on global versus regional or domestic production

**What this is:** a claim in a form a sourcing or operations researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.** No real firm's data is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The sourcing decision is a union compound, so it must be unbundled before search. Each condition requires an anchor that does not move with the firm's belief: realized landed cost, observed recovery and realized capability of the firm plus its habitat. First-order price is not the ground; second-order contact is. Relocation substitutes a habitat rather than removing a risk. Description cost and settlement cost are two currencies that do not add. Frames that agree must draw on low-overlap evidence, and every cut must stay ranked and reversible. The framework does not name a country. It returns the shape any defensible decision must have, with falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Structure of the decision, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 2

- **Statement:** The decision is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question, and the conditions must be anchored separately before any configuration is compared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **What to check:** Does treating landed cost, recovery and capability as separate anchored conditions change the decision relative to a single weighted score? If not, say so.
- **Refuter:** a single scalar that every condition accepts as its own anchor and that moves only with the firm's belief, with the decision still settling. That breaks the anchor test.

### C2: Contact and habitat, checkable by a sourcing or resilience researcher

- **Statement:** A first-order price improvement that leaves contact unchanged changes nothing that matters, and relocation substitutes a habitat rather than removing risk. Effects that matter are often three steps out.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** From your own cases, did a move to a nearer or domestic base inherit actors you did not choose, or lose a capability only when the goal failed? Did the disruption that mattered sit beyond the first tier? Name a case where it did not.
- **Refuter:** a configuration with a single supplier, lane or region that has no second-order contact effect under any disruption, or a relocation that removes rather than substitutes the habitat with nothing lost and nothing inherited.

### C3: Pricing and evidence, checkable by an operations or accounting researcher

- **Statement:** Description cost and settlement cost are incommensurable, and agreement from one evidence pool cannot corroborate a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- **What to check:** In your cost models, are the cost of describing and qualifying a configuration and the cost of settling whether it holds recorded separately, or summed? Does your evidence for a sourcing choice come from independent frames or from one shared benchmark?
- **Refuter:** a conversion that makes the two costs commensurable while preserving both readings and improving the decision, or a demonstration that two frames sharing one evidence pool count as two witnesses here.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a supplier, country or region. No figure line governs that choice; this is a `gap` reported as a verdict.
- Not that offshore is always worse or domestic always safer. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not a resilience score added to a cost score. Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- Not a last or final configuration. Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is asserted as settling any of this.
- Not a claim that the figures certify their own completeness. Fig 8 :37: "A frame cannot certify its own limit."

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Specifically:

1. Realized total landed cost per slot under actual conditions, not a quoted price.
2. Observed time to recover in a fixed-failure test or a real disruption, including propagation beyond the first tier.
3. A before-and-after capability map showing what had to be reproduced and what was inherited after a move.
4. A verbatim figure line naming a country, if one exists; none was found after a full census.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :57, Fig 2 :29, Fig 11 :52, Fig 11 :66 and Fig 12 :125. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against one sourcing move you know: did the move substitute a habitat, and was the effect that mattered more than one step out?
3. Check C3 against your own cost model: are description and settlement priced in one number or two?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the source of a low quoted price, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
