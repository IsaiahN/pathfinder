# WATSON answer: how should companies choose between global suppliers and regional or domestic production?

**Mode: `general` on the decision shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This run synthesizes `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself") it cannot settle the question it reports on. No real firm's cost, recovery or quality data was measured.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the choice between a global supplier and regional or domestic production. Option A: treat the choice as one hard question and pick whichever option has the lower unit price or the shorter distance. Option B: unbundle the decision into anchored conditions, count second-order contact, treat relocation as habitat substitution, keep the two cost currencies apart, triangulate low-overlap frames, keep cuts reversible, and report the specific choice as a gap. What each leads to: A delivers a single-number verdict with a preference presented as a witness, and it averages away the per-slot signal. B delivers a checkable decision procedure with falsifiers written in advance and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** For one product at one supplier slot, the gap between the configuration the firm predicted would perform and what the ground returns when realized landed cost, lead-time variance, a quality escape or a disruption arrives. Arity: at least five conditions, so a union compound, not one hard question. Symmetry: none; distance and direction are not interchangeable and valence is relative to the residual. Scale: first order is the direct supplier, second order the sub-tier and lane, third order the institutional or climatic condition. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, landed economics.** Actors: purchase price, freight, duty, inventory, rework, opportunity cost. Currency: money. Anchor: realized total landed cost under actual conditions, which does not move with the firm's belief. Question type: one condition of the union.
- **Domain 2, disruption and recovery.** Actors: supplier sites, lanes, backup capacity, inventory, alternate sources. Currency: time to recover and service level. Anchor: observed time to recover in a real event or a fixed-failure test. Question type: one condition of the union.
- **Domain 3, capability and habitat.** Actors: engineers, suppliers, institutions, conditions and relations. Currency: contact and adaptation. Anchor: realized capability of the firm-and-habitat pair. Question type: one condition of the union.
- **Domain 4, verification and governance.** Actors: decision makers, internal frames, outside checkers. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: checking loop over the three conditions.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive and slot the residual, unbundle the union, name anchors, range frames against their anchors separately, buy independence, keep cuts reversible. Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- **Rejected as domains.** Brand reputation and strategic fit: no nameable anchor that fails to move when the firm changes its mind, so not domains yet.

Strain test applied: a cost line that strains when applied to recovery governs money, not the whole decision. Re-census resolves it by Fig 12's two currencies. No averaging.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and the specific choice is a gap. The decision is a union of anchored conditions, not one hard question. First-order price is not the ground; second-order contact is. Relocation substitutes a habitat rather than removing risk. Description cost and settlement cost are two currencies and do not add. Frames that agree must draw on low-overlap evidence, and every cut stays ranked and reversible. The figures do not name a country or a supplier. Per-domain verdicts are D1 triangulated, D2 triangulated, D3 single-source for the substitution law, D4 triangulated, and the specific choice `gap`. This is `general` on the shape and `specified` on the predictions. It is ungrounded and its status is `candidate`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, its refuters and its lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a single-number verdict or a country recommendation instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The decision procedure is revisable, every cut is ranked and reversible, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent recommendation can be superseded by a corrected packet once a ground reading arrives.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Realized total landed cost per slot, an observed time to recover with propagation beyond the first tier, or a before-and-after capability map showing what was lost and inherited after a move would settle the per-domain predictions. A verbatim figure line naming a country without import or strain would close the gap. A demonstration that a single belief-moving scalar settles all conditions, or that relocation removes rather than substitutes the habitat, would break the shape claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
