## Wider literature that bears on the claim

These were read through the sources above, not opened as independent pages. They are recorded here as leads for a checker and are not counted as separately read world sources.

- Wieland and Durach, "Two perspectives on supply chain resilience", *Journal of Business Logistics* 42(3):315-322, 2021, doi 10.1111/jbl.12271. Distinguishes engineering and socio-ecological resilience. Relevant to the anchor and habitat conditions.
- Wieland, "Dancing the supply chain", *Journal of Supply Chain Management* 57(1):58-73, 2021, doi 10.1111/jscm.12248. Relevant to adaptation rather than return to a fixed state.
- Tukamuhabwa, Stevenson, Busby and Zorzini, "Supply chain resilience: definition, review and theoretical foundations", *International Journal of Production Research* 53(18):5592-5623, 2015, doi 10.1080/00207543.2015.1037934. A systematic review of 91 studies grouped into flexibility, redundancy, collaboration and agility. Relevant to F1's unbundling.
- Simchi-Levi, Wang and Wei, "Increasing supply chain robustness through process flexibility and inventory", *Production and Operations Management* 27(8):1476-1491, 2018. Relevant to the two-currency clause.
- Christopher and Peck, "Building the Resilient Supply Chain", *International Journal of Logistics Management* 15(2):1-14, 2004, doi 10.1108/09574090410700275. Relevant to collaboration and visibility.
