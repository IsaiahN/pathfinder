# The Tether's answer on global versus regional or domestic production

**Mode: `general` on the decision shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For one product at one supplier slot, the residual is the gap between the configuration a firm predicts will perform and what the ground returns when realized landed cost, lead-time variance, a quality escape or a disruption arrives. It is indexed per slot, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19). The decision is a union compound, not one hard question: Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

## 2. The shape

1. **Unbundle before searching.** The conditions are landed economics, disruption and recovery, and capability and habitat. A single "global or local" lever clears the cases where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-56).
2. **Name an immovable anchor for each condition.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Realized total landed cost, observed time to recover, and realized capability of the firm plus its habitat qualify. A quoted unit price, a manager's confidence and an internal maturity score do not.
3. **Count second-order contact.** Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing that matters (Fig 11 :40).
4. **Treat relocation as habitat substitution, not risk removal.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." You inherit actors you did not choose, and you lose things invisibly until the goal fails (Fig 11 :67-69).
5. **Price in two currencies that do not add.** Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds. A money-only score merges two incommensurable readings.
6. **Triangulate low-overlap frames.** Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." A cost model, a resilience engineering frame and a capability frame can agree meaningfully. Three readers of the same spreadsheet are one frame with three names (Fig 2 :40).
7. **Keep every cut ranked and reversible.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Preferences and political alignment are budget filters, not evidence.

The answer is a decision procedure, not a country. Over the full shared atom set, two configurations that differ only in their bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; as usually written they are members of one functional group (`06_isomers_and_order.md`). Their union is a compound with its own ground, not a final configuration (Fig 7 :6).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- It does not name a supplier, a country or a region. No verbatim figure line governs that choice; the F2 gap is reported as a verdict, not filled with a preference.
- It does not say offshore is always worse or domestic always safer. Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Valence is relative to the residual.
- It does not add resilience to a cost score. Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- It does not treat agreement as a witness. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- It is not `grounded`. No real firm's cost, recovery or quality data was measured here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; a spectrometer resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Landed cost.** Does a supplier's quoted price remain lowest after freight, duty, inventory, rework and opportunity cost are counted under realized conditions? This is the D1 anchor.
- **Recovery.** In a fixed-failure tabletop or a real event, what is the observed time to recover for each configuration, and does the failure propagate beyond the first tier? This is the D2 anchor.
- **Capability.** After a move, what had to be reproduced, what was inherited unchosen, and did the goal fail for a reason created by the move? This is the D3 anchor. Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails."
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a single belief-moving scalar settles the decision; a single point of failure has no second-order effect; relocation removes rather than substitutes the habitat; two frames sharing one pool count as two witnesses; description and settlement cost are commensurable; a figure line names a country without import or strain.

Until one of these fires, the verdict is `candidate`, not `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." The figure set returns the shape of a decision procedure and stops. It cannot say which configuration is right for a firm whose ground has not been read. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell a company whether to source globally or domestically, and it does not claim a final configuration. It says the decision is a bundle, so it must be unbundled before searching; that each condition needs an anchor that does not move when the firm changes its mind; that first-order price is not the ground, because the effect that matters is often three steps out; that moving production substitutes a habitat rather than removing a risk, so the things you lose stay invisible until the goal fails; that description cost and settlement cost are two currencies that do not add; that the frames which agree must draw on different evidence; and that every cut must be kept ranked and reversible. What it offers is the shape any defensible sourcing decision must have, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific choice stays open until the ground settles it.
