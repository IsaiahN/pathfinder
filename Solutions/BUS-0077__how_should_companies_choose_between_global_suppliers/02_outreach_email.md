# Outreach draft: check one decision output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `08_expert_verification_packet.md` has been checked against your own knowledge of sourcing decisions. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Would this count as a failure in a sourcing decision?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about one specific output, not about the framework that produced it. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to read the theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** For one product at one supplier slot, the gap is between the configuration a firm predicts will perform and what the ground returns when real landed cost, lead-time variance, a quality escape or a disruption arrives. That gap is indexed per slot. It is not one company-wide number. A single "source globally" or "source locally" rule averages away the only signal that says where to look.
>
> **The output for you to check.** The framework says it does not name a country or a region. It returns the shape of a decision procedure:
>
> 1. Unbundle the decision into its separate conditions before searching. A single lever clears the cases where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing.
> 2. Name, for each condition, an anchor that does not change its answer when the firm changes its mind. Realized total landed cost, observed time to recover, and realized capability of the firm plus its supply habitat qualify. A quoted unit price, a manager's confidence and an internal maturity score do not.
> 3. Count second-order contact. A first-order price improvement that does not change contact with the conditions that actually settle the decision changes nothing that matters. The effect that matters is often three steps out.
> 4. Treat a move from global to regional or domestic as a substitution of habitat, not a removal of risk. You inherit actors you did not choose, and you lose things invisibly until the goal fails.
> 5. Price description cost and settlement cost in separate currencies. They are in different units and do not add.
> 6. Use low-overlap frames. A cost model, a resilience engineering frame and a capability frame can agree meaningfully. Three people reading the same spreadsheet are one frame with three names.
> 7. Keep every cut ranked and reversible. Preferences and political alignment are filters for what gets tried first, never witnesses for the verdict.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is `general` on the procedure and `specified` on the per-condition predictions. Nothing here is `grounded`, because no real firm's data was measured.
>
> **What I explicitly am not claiming:** that the framework selects a particular supplier, country or region; that offshore sourcing is always worse; that domestic sourcing is always safer; that resilience can be added to a cost score; or that a panel of agreeing managers supplies an anchor. The figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this, per the framework's own rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument:
>
> 1. A sourcing decision is settled by a single scalar metric that moves only with the firm's belief.
> 2. A single supplier, lane or region has no second-order contact effect under any disruption.
> 3. A relocation removes rather than substitutes the habitat, with nothing lost and nothing inherited.
> 4. Two frames drawing on one evidence pool count as two independent witnesses.
> 5. Description cost and settlement cost are commensurable by a conversion that preserves both readings.
> 6. A figure line is produced that names a country or region without an import and without a strain.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then,** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Local figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public verified discussion of supply chain resilience: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain_resilience
> - European Investment Bank, Navigating supply chain disruptions: https://www.eib.org/en/publications/20240179-navigating-supply-chain-disruptions
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a sourcing decision you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Distribution discipline

One recipient at a time. Record the verdict on stage 1 before showing any theory. A later expression of interest is a conversation, never corroboration. Do not pool it into the result. Public institutional submission routes are in `03_public_contact_points.csv`; none of them require a personal email from you or from the recipient.
