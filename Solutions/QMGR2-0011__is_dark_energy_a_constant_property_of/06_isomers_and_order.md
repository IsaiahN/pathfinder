## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- The three conditions do not share an anchor. Smallness is a QFT-versus-observation mismatch; constancy is a measurement of w(z); coincidence is a selection or anthropic question. A lever that clears one leaves the others binding.
- ⇒ The question is a **union compound**, not a partition of independent subquestions. Three separate loops are owed. This is not a claim that the conditions are unrelated; it is a claim that one instrument will not settle all three. Triangulated (Fig 9 :54, :59 plus the *Library of Babel* passage on bundled problems, opened 2026-11-23).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a single observation or argument that simultaneously fixes the vacuum energy scale, returns w(z) at all z, and explains the present matter-density coincidence. If produced, the union reading fails and a single loop suffices.

### Q2 - What kind of thing is "constant"?

- "Constant" is not a substance and not a bond. It is a symmetry: invariance of the composed term under a shift in cosmic time.
- Fig 13 :65 (when time is removed): **"no time-translation symmetry, and no finite operations to leave a remainder"** - the framework's own treatment of time is expressed in symmetry terms, so describing R by symmetry is the correct move (Fig 9 :21-22, **"arity · symmetry · scale"**).
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** The bond vocabulary describes arrangements, not their invariances. No eighth bond is needed to state constancy; the symmetry is a property of the arrangement.
- ⇒ "Constant" belongs to the **symmetry** column of the census, not to the atoms or the bonds. Whether the symmetry obtains is `gap` for the figures and is handed to the ground (Fig 9 :42, **"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"**). Single-source for the figure definition, triangulated with Fig 13 :65 for the treatment of time.

### Q3 - Are constant and evolving dark energy isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Under the shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}` the two candidates read:
  - Λ (constant): a composed term whose symmetry is time-translation invariance; the structure slot is open.
  - Dynamical DE (evolving): a composed term over the same atoms whose symmetry is broken; the structure slot is filled by a field-like binding from the physics frame.
- Same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour (acceleration history). Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, and the difference is invisible in the ingredient list. The weaker reading is that they share a **functional group**: both are `state - structure - state` first-order cores with a time state, the qualifier (constant versus evolving symmetry) picking the member.
- The physics-facing check is not performed here: whether the two models truly use the same atom set depends on whether the dynamical case requires an extra field atom that the Λ case does not. If it does, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands. Mark `specified`, unmeasured.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a formulation of dynamical dark energy that requires an atom absent from the Λ formulation, or vice versa. Then the two are not isomers over a shared set.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** Both the Λ term and the measured expansion history are settled against the ground to good precision, so each can serve as an atom at the next level.
- Fig 3 :18-21: the chain is ordered by dependency, not importance; a reading below a broken link is a reading of nothing.
- Order: (1) refuse or repair "property of space" as a primitive; (2) settle the first-order composition `space - (structure) - energy`; (3) ask the symmetry question of that molecule at the ground; (4) only then compare constant versus evolving formulations as second-order arrangements of settled molecules.
- Comparison ⋛ is defined as **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** (Fig 12 :87). At second order the operands are settled molecules acting as atoms; whether they count as quantities at that level is a type question that needs an instrument, not an argument. Named as a strain, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound of three conditions, not one hard question. "Constant" is a symmetry of a composed term, not an atom and not a bond. Constant and evolving dark energy are isomers over the shared six-atom set in the strong reading, or members of one functional group in the weaker reading, with a stated falsifier. The dependency order is category, composition, symmetry, comparison. Nothing here requires a new primitive, an import that moves the wall, or a naming of the structure slot. Whether the symmetry is actually time-translation invariance remains a `gap` at the ground.

This framing **rescues** what is defensible in the question without taking a position cosmology has not settled: it rejects "property of space" as a primitive, keeps the structure slot open, and puts the constant-versus-evolving decision where it belongs, with the instruments.
