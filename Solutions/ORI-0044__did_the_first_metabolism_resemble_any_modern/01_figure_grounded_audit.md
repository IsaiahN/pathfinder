## 0. The question under audit

> "Did the first metabolism resemble any modern pathway, or was it fundamentally different?"
>
> Source: `origins of life.txt` line 62, section 5 (Metabolism-first and chemical networks).

**Restated as an effect.** We are asked to decide the relation between an ancestral chemical network and present-day metabolic pathways across roughly four billion years of descent. Option A (resembled a modern pathway) asserts continuity. Option B (fundamentally different) asserts discontinuity. Each option leads somewhere different: A implies that a contemporary pathway can be used as a model of the origin state; B implies that no contemporary pathway is a usable model and that a bridge has to be built.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** The shape being searched for is not a term the figures hold, so it is written down here and allowed to accrue a residual:

- **S1 functional resemblance:** the ancestral network and a modern pathway do the same job (for example, reduce CO2 with H2 and conserve energy), whatever their parts.
- **S2 compositional identity:** they share the same atoms under the same bonds, which under Fig 12 would make them the same substance or isomers; different bonds over the same atoms make different substances.
- **S3 lineage continuity:** the ancestral network is the ancestor of the modern pathway, established from a record rather than inferred from resemblance.

An answer to the original question is only well formed once it says which of S1, S2 and S3 it is answering. The pre-registered refuter in section 3 tests these three, not a vague yes or no.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: the question presents two options but the effect analysis finds at least three conditions (S1, S2, S3), so it is a **union compound**, not a single hard question. Symmetry: "resembles" is not an equivalence until the relation is named; functional resemblance, compositional identity and descent are different relations with different symmetry properties (identity is symmetric and reflexive, descent is neither). Scale: the origin network is molecular and geochemical; modern metabolism is cellular and enzymatic. The two are separated by the membrane between scales (Fig 4), so a transform is required and it is lossy.

**Partition or union.** Union. S1, S2 and S3 overlap but are not the same condition. ## 3. Findings (verbatim figure lines before conclusions)

### F1 - The question is a union compound and must be split (triangulated)

Fig 9 :54 and :57, quoted in section 1. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

A single "yes, it resembled" or "no, it was different" averages three slots. Under Fig 1 that is exactly how the live signal disappears. **Verdict:** split into S1, S2, S3 and keep the per-domain verdicts separate. Triangulated: Fig 9 (leave/arrive procedure) and Fig 1 (per-slot residual), low overlap between them.

### F2 - Functional resemblance does not imply compositional identity (single-source, with a declared overlap)

Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Fig 13 :123: *"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms"* Fig 13 :124: *"the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."* Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

So a "yes" at S1 and a "no" at S2 can both be true of the same pair of networks. The offered binary is underdetermined until the relation is named. **Verdict:** S1 and S2 are distinct quantities; neither option of the original question decides the other.

**Triangulation status: single-source.** Fig 12 :117 and Fig 13 :121 to :124 reuse the same 1824 to 1830 Liebig/Wöhler isomerism precedent. They are two statements of one witness, not two independent figures. World sources in `09_watson_answer.md` corroborate the distinction, but several share the same acetyl-CoA evidence pool (declared there).

### F3 - The first network is a seed, not a miniature modern pathway (triangulated)

Fig 4 :34: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*

If "resembled a modern pathway" means the origin network contained a scaled-down acetyl-CoA pathway, Fig 4 :34 forbids it: the seed does not contain the tree. What can cross the scale boundary is a method (a reusable catalytic move such as metal-centred redox carbon fixation or thioester energy coupling), not the recording of a particular pathway. Fig 12 :104 permits the modern pathway, once settled, to become an atom at the next level; that is the direction of composition. **Verdict:** S1 is licensed only as method-or-function resemblance, never as miniature replay. Triangulated across Fig 4 (membrane, scale) and Fig 12 (bond, level), low overlap.

### F4 - The modern artefact cannot reveal the route; the lineage is the proof (single-source)

Fig 13 :129: *"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*

Comparing a modern pathway with a reconstructed primitive network compares two artefacts. It cannot by itself decide S3. A lineage (isotopic, phylogenetic, geological, or a continuous laboratory series) is required. **Verdict:** S3 is out of reach from the artefact alone. Single-source for the figure line; Fig 12 :130 (*"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them"*) is a near restatement inside the same framework and is not counted as a second witness.

### F5 - The anchor for the historical fact is empirical and is not in the figures (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

The anchor here is a geochemical or phylogenetic measurement, not agreement among reviewers and not a figure line. The figures return the shape of an answer and name what is owed; they do not return the date, the pathway, or the historical fact. **Verdict:** on the empirical question the honest status is `gap`, reported plainly (Fig 13 :134). Triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor theory), Fig 13 (reach and verdict) and Fig 9 (shape), low overlap.

### F6 - "Fundamentally different" is not licensed by absence of evidence (triangulated)

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*

Option B gains no support merely from failing to find a modern analogue in the ancient record. Depth (instrument not yet adequate) and genuine absence (no analogue existed) look identical from inside the frame. **Verdict:** option B remains `candidate`, not `grounded`. Triangulated across Fig 8 (search) and Fig 6 (reachability), low overlap.

### F7 - What may be shared is a functional group, not a whole pathway (single-source)

Fig 12 :123: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The world sources report that metal-sulfide centres in modern anaerobic carbon-fixation enzymes resemble vent minerals, and that metals can replace more than a hundred enzymes for the same overall transformation (see `09_watson_answer.md`). Under Fig 12 that is a shared **functional group** (metal-centred redox carbon fixation) with different qualifiers (enzyme-bound cofactors versus bare mineral surfaces). **Verdict:** the defensible continuity claim is at the functional-group level. Single-source for the figure definition.

### F8 - Parsimony is a frame-internal preference, not an anchor (single-source)

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

One public-facing source argues that a metabolism completely unknown today "seem[s] less plausible" because it would have had to be lost without trace (Brunk and Marshall, 2024, opened 2026-11-23). That is a legitimate scientific heuristic, but under Fig 1 it is a preference held inside the frame, not an anchor that settles the question. **Verdict:** do not promote parsimony to a verdict. Record it as a preference.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the standing instruction, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. These four are written now and govern the shape claim, not the historical fact:

1. **R1, breaks S1.** A controlled geochemical experiment showing that the metal-centred redox carbon-fixation functional group cannot arise under plausible early-Earth vent conditions. Then there is no shared functional group to point at.
2. **R2, breaks S2.** A lineage reconstruction showing that the ancestral network used the identical full atom set and bond arrangement of a named modern pathway, with a continuous recoverable series. Then the "compositional difference" half of the shape is false.
3. **R3, breaks S3.** A demonstration that the ancestral route is recoverable from the modern artefact alone, with no lineage record. Then Fig 13 :129 as applied here is wrong and the lineage requirement is withdrawn.
4. **R4, framework-internal.** A figure re-census in which functional resemblance and compositional identity turn out to be one partition rather than two conditions. Then the split in F1 collapses and the question is one question after all.

If any fires, the corresponding half of the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

2. Say plainly that the historical fact is a `gap` from the figures and belongs to geochemistry and phylogenetics (F5). That is a verdict, not a silence.

## 7. Verdict

The figures do not decide whether the first metabolism resembled a modern pathway. They constrain the shape of any defensible answer:

- the question is a union of functional resemblance, compositional identity and lineage continuity, and must be split before it is searched (Fig 9 :54, :57; Fig 1 :19);
- what can be shared across the scale boundary is a method or a functional group, not a miniature pathway (Fig 4 :22, :34; Fig 12 :123);
- the same function can be reached by different routes, and the same ingredients under different bonds are a different substance (Fig 13 :121, :124; Fig 12 :117);
- descent cannot be read off the modern artefact, and neither absence of evidence nor parsimony settles it (Fig 13 :129; Fig 8 :39; Fig 6 :27; Fig 1 :27);
- the empirical fact is a `gap` here and is owed to the ground (Fig 2 :29; Fig 13 :134).

After re-census: one named strain (Fig 13 :75 misapplied to networks, withdrawn), one collapsed false triangulations (F2), and no figure line forced to fit. The shape survives; the yes/no verdict is not available from the figures.
