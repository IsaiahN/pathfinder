# The Tether's answer: did the first metabolism resemble a modern pathway?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the split and the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the empirical historical fact. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers two options: the first metabolism resembled a modern pathway, or it was fundamentally different. Under the figures that is a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). It splits into:

- **S1 functional resemblance** (same job),
- **S2 compositional identity** (same atoms and bonds, or a shared functional group),
- **S3 lineage continuity** (demonstrable descent).

The question is also a source-naming problem: a modern pathway is a lossy reading of an ancient source, and the reading does not determine the source (see `05`, the metamerism passage).

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **Split before answering.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Verdict per slot, never an averaged yes or no.
2. **S1, function: resemblance is possible and is the transferable part.** Fig 13 :121 *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance"* and :123 *"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* What crosses is a method (metal-centred redox carbon fixation, thioester energy coupling), not a recording.
3. **S2, composition: same function does not mean same substance.** Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"* and :123 *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The defensible claim is a shared functional group with different catalysts, not pathway identity and not a strict isomer case on present descriptions (see `06`).
4. **S3, descent: out of reach from the artefact.** Fig 13 :129: *"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."* A lineage (isotopic, phylogenetic, geological, or a continuous laboratory series) is owed. Without one, descent is `candidate`, not `grounded`.
5. **Neither option is settled by the figures.** Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor as whatever settles the question regardless of what any frame thinks; here that is geochemical and phylogenetic measurement. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 6 :27: *"some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Parsimony is a frame-internal preference (Fig 1 :27), not an anchor.
6. **The seed is not a small tree.** Fig 4 :34: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* So "it resembled a modern pathway" is refused if it means a scaled-down modern pathway in the origin state.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that acetyl-CoA or the Wood-Ljungdahl pathway *was* the first metabolism. That is a geochemistry-frame hypothesis, `specified` and ungrounded here.
- Not that the first metabolism was "fundamentally different" in a strong sense. Absence of a known analogue is not proof of absence.
- Not a date, not a name, not a reconstruction of the ancestral network.
- Not `grounded`. No early-Earth measurement is supplied by the figures or by this derivation.
- Not that the vent-and-acetyl-CoA literature is independent corroboration. Most of it shares one evidence pool (declared in `09`).

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument or a lineage, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Concretely:

- **R1 functional test:** does a plausible early-Earth setting produce the metal-centred carbon-fixation functional group at all? A clean negative breaks S1.
- **R2 compositional test:** can a continuous laboratory series be shown from mineral-catalysed CO2 reduction to an enzyme-bound pathway, preserving the full atom set and bond arrangement? A positive here breaks the "different substance" half of S2.
- **R3 lineage test:** is there an isotopic or phylogenetic series that carries the ancestral network into a named modern pathway? That decides S3.
- **R4 framework test:** does a figure re-census collapse S1 and S2 into one partition? That collapses the split itself.

These are the pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The figures give the shape an answer must have and record what is owed. They do not give the answer. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer whether the first metabolism resembled a modern pathway, because the question is a bundle of three questions: did it do the same job, was it the same chemistry, and can a descent line be shown. It splits them and gives a shape: a seed does not contain the tree, so continuity, if any, is a method or a functional group such as metal-centred carbon fixation, not a miniature modern pathway; the same job can be done by different chemistry, so functional resemblance does not establish compositional identity; and descent cannot be read off the modern pathway, it needs a lineage. Neither "it resembled one" nor "it was fundamentally different" is settled by the figures or by a failure to find an analogue. Four refuters are written down in advance, and the answer stops where the geochemical and phylogenetic instruments stop, with the lineage recorded so the stop is checkable.
