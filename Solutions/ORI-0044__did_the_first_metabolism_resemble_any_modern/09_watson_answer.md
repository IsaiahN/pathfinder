## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, between option A (the first metabolism resembled a modern pathway) and option B (it was fundamentally different). What each leads to: A licenses a modern pathway as a model of the origin state; B says no modern pathway is a usable model and a bridge must be built. The finding is that A and B are not exhaustive: they bundle functional resemblance, compositional identity and lineage continuity, and the historical fact itself is a gap from the figures.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the yes/no verdict is not available. The question is a union of functional resemblance, compositional identity and lineage continuity and must be split. Continuity, if any, is a method or functional group (metal-centred redox carbon fixation), not a miniature modern pathway. The same function is reachable by different routes and the same ingredients under different bonds give a different substance, so function-level resemblance and chemistry-level difference can both hold. Descent cannot be read off the modern artefact and needs a lineage. Neither option is settled by the figures, by absence of an analogue, or by parsimony. This is a `general` shape and `specified` split, with the empirical fact at `gap`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, split and four refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the split and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The functional-group and method claims are separable from the historical fact. A geochemical or phylogenetic result can move S1, S2 or S3 independently without withdrawing the split or the shape. Nothing has to be declared finally true here.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating geochemical experiment would break S1; a continuous series showing identical full atom set and bond arrangement would break the compositional-difference half of S2; a lineage reconstruction would settle S3 and retire the `gap`; a figure re-census collapsing S1 and S2 into one partition would collapse the split. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
