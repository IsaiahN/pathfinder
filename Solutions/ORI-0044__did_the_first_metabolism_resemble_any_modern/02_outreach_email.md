**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about early metabolism. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as posed is a bundle, not one question.** "Did the first metabolism resemble a modern pathway, or was it fundamentally different?" conflates three different things: (1) did it do the same job, (2) was it the same chemistry under the same bonds, and (3) can a descent line be shown. Under the framework I use, a case in three conditions is not a harder case of either. It has to be split before it is searched.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the split, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the historical fact):** what can cross from an origin network to a modern pathway is a method or a functional group, not a miniature of the pathway. The same function can be reached by different routes, and the same ingredients under different bonds are a different substance. So a "yes" at the function level and a "no" at the chemistry level can both be true. The descent claim cannot be read off the modern pathway; it needs a lineage. This is a shape, not a measurement, and I am not claiming to date or name the first metabolism.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the acetyl-CoA pathway *was* the first metabolism, that the first network was a scaled-down modern pathway, that absence of a known analogue proves fundamental difference, or that a parsimony argument counts as evidence. Parsimony gives a claim a budget to be tried; it does not settle it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a geochemical experiment showing the metal-centred carbon-fixation functional group cannot arise under plausible early-Earth conditions;
> 2. a lineage reconstruction showing the ancestral network used the identical full atom set and bond arrangement of a modern pathway, with a continuous recoverable series;
> 3. a demonstration that the ancestral route is recoverable from the modern artefact alone, with no lineage record;
> 4. a figure re-census in which functional resemblance and compositional identity turn out to be one partition, so the split collapses.
>
> If any one holds, that half of the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula, with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local extract) and `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures`
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: `https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether`
> - The figure-grounded derivation for this question: `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` (local files)
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an early-metabolism problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
