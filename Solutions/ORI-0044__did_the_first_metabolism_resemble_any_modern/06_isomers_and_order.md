# Is the first metabolism an isomer of a modern pathway? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural comparisons, `gap` on the historical fact.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

## RESIDUAL

Given two chemical networks, an ancestral one and a modern one, are they (a) the same substance reached by different routes, (b) isomers, (c) members of one functional group, or (d) unrelated substances? And what is the order relation between them, given that one is a seed and the other is a settled, composed molecule?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the discussion (not primitives of the six-term substrate): H2, CO2, formate, acetate, pyruvate, thioesters, phosphate, Fe/Ni sulfides, pH and redox gradients, cofactors such as pterins and CoA. Bonds held: redox reduction, condensation, carbonylation, thioester formation, ligation to a mineral surface, later enzyme-bound catalysis. No new primitive is available without import. Actors, currency and anchor are as in domains D1 and D2 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Same substance by a different route?

- Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*
- Fig 13 :123: *"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms"*
- Fig 13 :124: *"the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*

These are compatible. A vent network and a modern pathway can do the same job (functionally identical terms) while being different substances (different bonds). Fig 13 :121 does **not** license the claim that they are historically the same network; that is S3 and needs a lineage (F4 of `01`). **Verdict:** same function is possible; same substance is not established. Single-source, with the declared shared 1824 to 1830 isomerism example noted in F2.

### Q2 - Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Strict Fig 12 isomerism requires the same atoms under different bonds. As the literature describes them, the modern acetyl-CoA pathway uses enzyme-bound cofactors (pterins, CoA, transition-metal clusters inside proteins), while the proposed prebiotic analogue uses bare metal sulfides and simple thioesters. The atom sets are not identical, so the pair is **not** a clean isomer case as currently described. What they share is a substructure, metal-centred redox carbon fixation, which fixes the outcome. Under Fig 12 :123 that is a **functional group** with different qualifiers (mineral surface versus enzyme active site). **Verdict:** functional-group claim survives; strict isomer claim does not, on present descriptions.

### Q3 - What is the order between them?

- Fig 4 :34: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*
- Operators table: `→` is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, an ordered bond; the algorithm section of the Formula notes that *"a plan is a sequence, a goal is a comparison, progress is a subtraction."*

The origin network is the seed. It does not contain the modern pathway in miniature. The modern pathway is a settled molecule (centuries of experimental settlement at the ground), and as Fig 12 :104 permits, it can serve as an atom in later compositions. Direction of descent is `→` if a lineage is shown; it is not `≡` (identity) and not a free conjunction. **Verdict:** the order is seed to settled molecule to next-level atom, and the arrow is licensed only by a lineage, not by resemblance.

### Q4 - A named strain, re-censused

Fig 13 :75 says *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* An early reading tried to apply this to chemical networks as "two networks cannot bond directly." That is a **strain**: the six-term substrate governs physical states and structures (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network), not reaction networks. Applying the rule to networks is a category error. The figure was sent back to the census (canonical README: a reading that makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain) and is **withdrawn as a binding constraint here**. No verdict in this file depends on it. This is the one live strain recorded in `01` and `11`.

### Q5 - Analogues and the shadow/echo order

- Chemical analogue: Liebig and Wöhler's 1824 to 1826 result, cited in Fig 12 :98 and Fig 13 :123, forced structure into chemical notation because same composition gave different properties. It is a *shadow* for the compositional distinction, not a claim about origins.
- Deposit analogue: the artefact does not record its route (Fig 13 :129), as a sediment does not record every step of its deposition. This is a shadow for the lineage requirement, not an assertion about geology.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The residual was described in effect terms (S1, S2, S3) before the acetyl-CoA vent frame was read. Frame second, residual first.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a clean case is shown in which the ancestral network and a modern pathway share the identical full atom set and differ only in bond arrangement, the functional-group reading upgrades to a strict isomer reading. If they share neither atoms nor a function, both readings fail and the pair is simply unrelated.

## CONCLUSION

The strict isomer claim is not supported on present descriptions; the functional-group claim is. Same function does not imply same substance, and the seed does not contain the tree. The order is cross-scale (seed to settled molecule), and the arrow expressing descent requires a lineage. One strain (Fig 13 :75 applied to networks) was found, re-censused and withdrawn; it binds nothing.
