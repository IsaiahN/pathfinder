## Three things in the literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The field's own version of the split.** Lauber, Flamm and Ruiz-Mirazo (2021) write that *"there is an open, ongoing debate on whether prebiotic reactions to produce the first biomolecules... should resemble current metabolic pathways or be completely different,"* and immediately add that *"The question may not have an 'all-or-none' answer."* That is a world-source corroboration of F1, arrived at from systems chemistry and philosophy of biology rather than from the figures. Use it when explaining why the binary is being refused.
2. **Same function, different composition.** Mrnjavac et al. (2024) report that Fe, Co and Ni in metallic form replace the function of *"over 120 enzymes"* in the conversion of H2 and CO2 to pyruvate. Martin and Russell (2006) say carbon *"might have entered early metabolism via reactions hardly different from those in the modern Wood-Ljungdahl pathway."* Read together with Fig 12 :117 (*"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*) and Fig 12 :123 (functional group), these support the function-or-group continuity claim without supporting pathway identity. **Overlap warning:** both papers sit in the same vent-and-acetyl-CoA evidence pool. They are one witness on that point, not two.
3. **A live dissent inside the same pool.** Nitschke and Russell (2013) argue that the Wood-Ljungdahl pathways are themselves *"derived from an even earlier type of biomass-accumulating metabolism."* So even the camp that treats acetyl-CoA as ancient allows an earlier, different metabolism. This corroborates the open-lineage verdict and conflicts with any simple "it resembled a modern pathway." Marked as a conflict in `09_watson_answer.md` where relevant.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the split, the shape and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A mass send selected for people already inclined to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not negotiate the verdict; ask for the refutation.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, restated

**Stage 1.** Validate the output against the ground. Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* and Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the split and the shape without holding the framework's interior.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, read the theory that produced it. Their verdict on stage 1 is already recorded and is never pooled as corroboration afterward (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because the Formula's "Direction" section says a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question."*

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The shape in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is stated with its mode and refuters; it is not a claim to have settled the historical fact.
