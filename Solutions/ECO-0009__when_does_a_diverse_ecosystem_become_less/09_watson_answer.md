# WATSON answer: when does a diverse ecosystem become less stable through amplified fluctuations?

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No ecosystem was measured.

## QUESTION

When does a diverse ecosystem become less stable because interactions among species amplify fluctuations? Restated as an effect: why does the per-species fluctuation residual R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| grow after a perturbation in some diverse communities rather than decay, when R is indexed per species and per time and never aggregated (Fig 1 :19)? Option A: species count is itself the governing quantity, so more diversity means less stability as a direct law. Option B: the governing quantities are the arrangement of interaction bonds, the correlation of species responses, and the choice of stability metric; diversity enters only through those. What each leads to: A predicts a monotone diversity-instability law that no interaction data is needed to state; B predicts amplification only when a signed return loop, overlapping responses and a masking metric coincide, and keeps a declared gap where the number would be.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: many-body, at least an interacting pair and usually a return loop. Symmetry: signed and directed, the test is the sign of the loop, not the number of links. Scale: per-species slot versus ecosystem aggregate, with a lossy membrane between (Fig 4). This is a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55).
- **D1 Community habitat.** Actors: species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) (Fig 11 :47). Currency: abundance, biomass, cascading contact. Anchor: field measurement, experiment, time series. Admitted.
- **D2 Interaction-bond structure.** Actors: species as atoms, typed interaction relations as bonds. Currency: the arrangement, which bond joins which pair. Anchor: the interaction matrix or its field estimate. Admitted.
- **D3 Fluctuation / amplification.** Actors: states and structures in alternation, the remainder re-entering the next turn. Currency: residual compounding per turn. Anchor: a measured exponent or growth versus decay of a perturbation. Admitted.
- **D4 Response correlation / overlap.** Actors: species as response frames, the environmental signal each reads. Currency: independence versus overlap. Anchor: the perturbation signal and each species' response. Admitted.
- **D5 Detection / metric.** Actors: object slots, the aggregating metric, the observer's instrument. Currency: per-slot residual versus pooled average. Anchor: the ground as the only metric (Fig 1 :23). Admitted.
- **X1 Diversity count as an independent cause.** Actors: a number. Currency: count. Anchor: none nameable once arrangement is held. Not a domain yet; absorbed into D2 and D4.

Union, not partition: D1 and D2 form one compound; D3, D4 and D5 are two conditions and a detector whose intersections are categorically different from any single one (Fig 9 :57). Dependency order: perception (per-slot time series) -> vocabulary (interaction bonds) -> objective (damping versus growth) -> planning (intervention). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Strain test: Fig 2 / Fig 7 / Fig 8 govern overlap of evidence; mapping that onto species response signals is a specified cross-kind application allowed by Fig 4 :20 (only actors, currency and anchor change). The strain is named in `01` section 4. If the mapping is rejected, D4 becomes a gap; it does not flip to "diversity count governs".

## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates|conflicts)

**Theoretical ecology and mathematics, first witness.**

- **Theoretical ecology / complex systems.** Robert M. May, "Will a Large Complex System be Stable?", *Nature* 238, 413-414, 1972. Opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "large complex systems which are assembled (connected) at random may be expected to be stable up to a certain critical level of connectance, and then, as this increases, to suddenly become unstable." Prediction: at fixed random structure, size and connectance push a system across a stability boundary. **Corroborates** D2 (arrangement matters) and the existence of a crossover; it supplies a candidate number that the figures withhold. Full text paywalled; abstract read.

**Statistical physics of ecosystems, independent pool from field ecology.**

- **Statistical physics / complex systems.** Emmy Blumenthal, Jason W Rocks and Pankaj Mehta, "Phase transition to chaos in complex ecosystems with non-reciprocal species-resource interactions", *Physical Review Letters* 132(12):127401, 2024 (PMC open manuscript read 2026-11-23). Quote: "such ecosystems generically undergo a phase transition to chaotic dynamics as the amount of non-reciprocity is increased." Prediction: non-reciprocal (directed, non-symmetric) interaction produces chaos; the boundary depends on the ratio of surviving species to non-depleted resources. **Corroborates** D2 and D3 strongly: the sign/direction of interaction, not the species count, governs the regime. Overlap with May is bibliographic (it cites the same literature), so these two are related witnesses, not fully independent.

**Network science, moderate overlap with theoretical ecology.**

- **Network science / theoretical ecology.** Samuel Johnson, Virginia Dominguez-Garcia, Luca Donetti and Miguel A Munoz, "Trophic coherence determines food-web stability", *PNAS* 111(50):17923-17928, 2014 (PMC read 2026-11-23). Quote: "trophic coherence, a hitherto ignored feature of food webs that current structural models fail to reproduce, is a better statistical predictor of linear stability than size or complexity." Prediction: structural ordering of interactions predicts stability better than species number or connectance. **Corroborates** D1 and D2. It also reports runaway fluctuations in species abundances as the instability signature, which corroborates D3.

**Reference overviews, tertiary, used for vocabulary not for the verdict.**

- **Ecology reference.** Wikipedia contributors, "Ecological stability", Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23. Quote: "dynamical stability is limited by diversity, and the strictness of this tradeoff is related to the magnitude of fluctuations in interactions." Prediction: diversity can be destabilising, with interaction fluctuation magnitude as the coupling. **Corroborates** D2 and D3. Also: "The effect of diversity on stability in food-web models can be either positive or negative, depending on the trophic coherence of the network," which corroborates the conditional verdict and refuses a universal law. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict is condition B, kept per domain and never averaged. Diversity count is not an anchor. Stability at fixed species count is carried by the arrangement of interaction bonds (Fig 12 :117, isomer); amplification requires a signed return loop (Fig 12 :121, Fig 13 :31); overlapping responses remove the independent insurance diversity would otherwise supply (Fig 2 :31, Fig 7 :13, Fig 8 :49); and an aggregate stability metric can hide the live slot (Fig 1 :22). The question is a union of these conditions, each with its own ground. The numeric crossover is a declared gap: figures give a shape, not a number. World sources corroborate the shape, with the systems/control source the least overlapping witness, but they never decide it. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** a field season spent measuring the wrong axis. If the count were the true anchor, an interaction-matrix study would be unnecessary work, and a checker's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and locatable, because the packet names the refuters in advance. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; the refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than removed (Fig 6 amendment). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). A published packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the pre-registered refuters in `01` section 5 reverses the corresponding verdict, in particular (1) amplification with all loops damping and all correlations near zero, (2) monotonic stability change with count at fixed bonds, (3) a non-masking aggregate that stays flat while a slot amplifies, or (4) a genuinely different evidence pool showing the response sign independent of reciprocity, coherence and correlation. A measured threshold with provenance would close the gap, not reverse the shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
