# The Tether's answer: how small galaxies form, and why some stop forming stars

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an astrophysical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A low-mass halo may or may not become a luminous galaxy, and a galaxy that formed stars may stop, at a mass-dependent time that depends on environment and history. One frame that predicts the first does not thereby predict the second. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* This is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. One loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

**D1, halo assembly and gas supply.** Actors are haloes, the cosmic web, accretion and expansion; currency is mass and gas delivery; anchor is the halo mass function and large-scale structure. Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* A gas supply that can be spent is a flow, and a gradient over the halo's lifetime is a different term from a gradient over its radius. **Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 13 plus the world halo census in Primack 2024).** The figures license the flow shape; they do not compute a mass function.

**D2, baryonic conversion.** Actors are cold gas, cooling, stars and stellar feedback; currency is gas mass and star formation rate; anchor is resolved star formation histories and H I measurements. Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent"*. A spendable gradient exists in cold gas; whether it is spent into stars or removed is decided by contact with the habitat, not by the substrate. **Verdict: `general` on the spendable-gradient shape, `specified` that the discriminator is a gas measurement, single-source for the figure line (Fig 13).**

**D3, shutdown and environment.** Actors are hot halo gas, ram pressure, tides, neighbours, reionization, backsplash orbits and the cosmic web; currency is gas removal or heating; anchor is quenched fractions, H I deficiency and quenching epochs. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The same ram pressure strips one dwarf and compresses another; the sign of the returned-energy loop decides whether star formation stops. Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* The mechanisms are isomers over a shared atom set, distinguishable by their behaviour, not by their names. **Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 valence plus Fig 12 cycle and isomer).** Which mechanism fired is `gap` until an instrument settles it.

**D4, verification.** Actors are simulations and surveys; currency is residual between prediction and observation; anchor is the ground only. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* **Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 10).**

The four verdicts are not averaged. A strong result in D1 says nothing about which actor fired in D3.

## 3. The shape, stated once

1. **Split the question.** Assembly and shutdown are a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).
2. **Enumerate the habitat.** The actor list is read off the world and stays open at the instrument edge (Fig 11 :43-44). The framework supplies the rule for reading it, not its contents.
3. **Quenching is a threshold comparison and a sign-flip cycle.** A spendable gas gradient is removed or spent; the feedback loop either recloses or opens. The figures give the comparison shape (Operators table :48, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*), not the numeric mass.
4. **Mechanisms are isomers or functional-group members.** Same atoms, different bond, different substance; where atom sets differ, the group fixes the kind and the qualifier fixes the member (Fig 12 :124).
5. **An instrument names the mechanism.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The discriminator is sharpened from something already returning a signal: resolved histories, H I, FUV, spatial structure.
6. **The slot stays open.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Figure 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that ram pressure, reionization, tidal stripping or stellar feedback is *the* answer for dwarf galaxies. Those are world names with provenance; the figures do not select among them.
- Not that the transition near 10^7 solar masses is a figure result. That number is world-grounded in Slater and Bell (2014), Sales et al. (2022) and Simpson et al. (2018), and is reported as such.
- Not that more simulation inside one physics frame adds a mechanism. Fig 8 :32: *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*.
- Not that agreement among simulation suites is corroboration. They share subgrid physics and calibration data (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the chain's breaking point is known. That is `gap` in `01` F10.
- Not `grounded`. No new measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the questions are:

- Does a given quenched dwarf retain a CGM or H I reservoir that could reignite star formation, and with what spatial distribution? Hai et al. (2026) show how this is measured on one object.
- Does the quenching epoch correlate with infall time, with stellar mass, or with neither? Weisz et al. (2015) found no correlation with current proximity inside the virial radius, which eliminates the simplest distance-only story.
- Do two mechanisms over the same atoms produce distinguishable H I, star formation history and structural signatures? If not, the isomer claim is refused.
- Is there a transition mass at which the dominant mechanism changes, and does it appear in independent samples? Slater and Bell (2014) combine Local Group and SDSS samples precisely to avoid one evidence pool.
- Standing pre-registered refuters from `01` §4: union split unnecessary, actor valence fixed, isomer discrimination impossible in principle, smooth single-mechanism fit, or an instrument built from pure description.

## 6. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in mechanism.** The figures decide what any answer must look like: split, enumerated, thresholded, sign-sensitive, isomer-aware, instrument-bound. They do not decide which actor fired in a particular dwarf, or which mechanism dominates at a given mass. The actor list is read from a world that keeps changing as instruments improve, and the ground is the only thing that settles a name. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (figures README). The lineage above is the record of why the stop is here.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name one cause of dwarf-galaxy quenching. It says the question is a union of two conditions, formation and shutdown, that must be split before either is searched. The list of actors that can strip or heat a dwarf's gas must be read off the world and kept open: hot haloes, ram pressure, tides, preprocessing, stellar feedback, reionization and the cosmic web. An actor's valence is relative to the residual, so the same ram pressure can strip gas from one dwarf and reignite star formation in another; the sign of the feedback loop decides whether star formation stops. The mechanisms are isomers over a shared set of atoms, distinguishable by H I content, star formation history and structure, not by agreement inside a simulation frame. The figures give the threshold shape, not the transition mass; the world supplies the number near 10^7 solar masses and the instruments that could tell the mechanisms apart. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the honest statement that the slot stays open until a source-discriminating observation closes it.
