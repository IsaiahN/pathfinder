# Expert verification packet: the Tether on small-galaxy formation and quenching

**What this is:** a claim in a form a dwarf-galaxy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not an astrophysical result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how do small galaxies form, and why do some stop forming stars?" is a union of two conditions that must be unbundled before either is searched. The list of actors that can remove or heat a dwarf's gas is enumerated from the world and remains open at the instrument edge. Shutdown is a threshold comparison on a spendable gas supply and a feedback cycle whose sign decides whether it closes, so the same actor can strip gas in one galaxy and reignite star formation in another. Distinct mechanisms over the same atoms are isomers, distinguishable by H I content, star formation history and spatial structure, not by agreement inside a simulation frame. The figures give that shape; they do not name which mechanism fired, and the slot stays open until a source-discriminating observation closes it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the two conditions form a union, not one hard question; the quenching actor list is enumerated from the world, not composed from the substrate; an actor's valence is relative to the residual.
- **Sources, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
  - Fig 11 :43 — *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
  - Fig 11 :49 — *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
  - Fig 11 :50 — *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check:** do these lines actually say what the claim says, and does the dwarf-galaxy case meet their conditions? In particular, is there a real dwarf case where one actor flips valence? Hai et al. (2026) report that cosmic-web ram pressure can both strip gas and reignite star formation.
- **Refuter:** one continuous mechanism with one parameter fits formation and shutdown with no residual (kills the union split); or an actor is measured with fixed valence for every residual it touches (kills the valence claim).

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable by a dwarf-galaxy researcher against the literature

- **Statement:** shutdown is a sign-sensitive cycle plus a threshold comparison; mechanisms over the same atoms are isomers, and a current instrument can in principle distinguish them.
- **Sources, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :121 — *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
  - Fig 12 :117 — *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 12 :124 — *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
  - Operators table :48 — *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (the shape only; no numeric value is claimed).
- **What to check:** are the two characterisations faithful? Do resolved star formation histories (Weisz et al. 2015), H I masses and FUV rates (Hai et al. 2026), and quenched-fraction statistics with interloper corrections (Slater and Bell 2014) actually discriminate candidates? Or do current measurements leave the candidates observationally degenerate?
- **Refuter:** two mechanisms over the same atoms are indistinguishable in H I, star formation history and spatial structure even in principle (kills the isomer claim and leaves only the functional-group claim); or a smooth single-mechanism fit with no transition mass reproduces both low-mass satellite and higher-mass field fractions (kills the threshold shape).

### C3 — verification-frame; checkable against any published dwarf-galaxy analysis

- **Statement:** more simulation inside one physics frame adds no new mechanism; a mechanism name is licensed only by a source-discriminating instrument, and agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is not corroboration.
- **Sources, verbatim:**
  - Fig 8 :32 — *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*
  - Fig 8 :39 — *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
  - Fig 6 :32 — *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
  - Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
- **What to check:** in a paper you know, was a mechanism established by a discriminating measurement, or by the convergence of simulation suites sharing subgrid recipes? Is a null H I detection treated as proof that no reservoir exists, or as a limit to be sharpened?
- **Refuter:** a genuinely new mechanism derived inside one simulation frame with unchanged physics and no new observational input (kills the import rule); or an instrument designed purely from a description, with no ancestor instrument returning a signal, settles the question (kills Fig 6 :32).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*.
- Not that ram pressure, reionization, tidal stripping or stellar feedback is *the* mechanism. Those are world names with provenance; the figures do not select among them.
- Not that the transition mass near 10^7 solar masses is a figure result. That value is world-grounded in Slater and Bell (2014), Sales et al. (2022) and Simpson et al. (2018).
- Not that a quenched dwarf with an old stellar population and no detected H I is definitively quenched rather than briefly quiescent. Hai et al. (2026) explicitly cannot rule out a low-mass reservoir without deeper follow-up.
- Not that the chain's breaking point is known. `01` F10 records `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Which mechanism operates in a given mass and environment regime, measured through resolved star formation histories, H I distribution, FUV rates and spatial structure (Hai et al. 2026 show the template on one object).
2. Is there a real transition mass, and is the dominant mechanism the same on both sides of it (Slater and Bell 2014; Sales et al. 2022)?
3. Does the actor's valence flip with the residual, as C1 predicts, or is it fixed?
4. The five pre-registered refuters in `01` §4. Any one firing retracts the affected finding.
