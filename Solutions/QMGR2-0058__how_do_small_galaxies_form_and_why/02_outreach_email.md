# Outreach draft: check one claim about dwarf galaxies before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send a single-mechanism story. The claim below is a shape plus four refuters. If it is rewritten as "ram pressure is the answer", it fails the figures' own shape-not-a-name rule and the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output on dwarf-galaxy quenching before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how do small galaxies form, and why do some stop forming stars?" is not one hard question. It is a union of at least two conditions, assembly and shutdown, and Fig 9 says to split such a bundle before searching it. What the figures then constrain is a shape, not a mechanism:
>
> 1. The list of things that can act on a dwarf galaxy's gas must be read off the world, not generated from the framework. For this field that list includes the host halo's hot gas, ram pressure, tidal interactions, preprocessing in low-mass groups, stellar feedback, cosmic reionization, backsplash orbits and the cosmic web. The list is open at the edge: what current instruments cannot measure is itself the residual.
> 2. An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same ram pressure or cosmic-web interaction that strips gas from one dwarf can compress gas and reignite star formation in another. The sign of the feedback loop, not the presence of the actor, decides whether the loop closes.
> 3. Quenching is a threshold comparison on a gas supply, not a single cause. Distinct mechanisms over the same atoms (gas, stars, halo) are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different observable substance. Which one fired in a given galaxy is an instrument question.
> 4. More simulation inside the same physics frame adds no new mechanism. A new atom enters only from a frame whose closure differs, meaning a different observational probe or a genuinely different physical input, with provenance recorded.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any one mechanism, ram pressure, reionization, tidal stripping or stellar feedback, is *the* answer for dwarf galaxies. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that the six-term substrate decides which mechanism operates at 10^7 solar masses. That is a measurement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. A single continuous mechanism with one parameter fits formation and quenching across the full dwarf mass and environment range with no residual left, so the union split is unnecessary.
> 2. An actor is shown to have fixed valence for every residual it touches, stripping only, never reigniting or compressing.
> 3. Two mechanisms over the same atoms give indistinguishable distributions in H I content, star formation history and spatial structure, even in principle, so the isomer claim is empty.
> 4. An instrument built from a pure description, with no prior instrument already returning a signal on that quantity, settles the mechanism question without an observational ancestor.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
