## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What order are formation and shutdown?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Formation and shutdown are a union of overlapping conditions, not a sequence with one arrow.
- Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Inside shutdown the relation is a cycle: gas forms stars, stars return energy, the gas either cool-reaccretes (loop closes) or is expelled or heated (loop opens).
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* A galaxy whose star formation history is settled against the ground becomes an operand at the next level: the group, the cluster, the quenched-fraction statistic.
- ⇒ The union is not ordered by a single arrow. Each condition has its own loop, and the two loops contact at the gas reservoir. A figure that writes `formation → shutdown` as a chain has already lost the cycle.
- **Falsifier:** a case where shutdown precedes any star formation (a "failed" or dark halo) and is fully explained by the same atoms without a gas-reservoir loop. Then the union is a sequence and this derivation is wrong for that case. Note that reionization fossils (Weisz et al. 2015; Hai et al. 2026) are a real candidate for exactly this case, so the falsifier is live, not rhetorical.

### Q2 — Are the mechanisms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Over the full shared atom set {cold gas, halo, stars, pressure/heating, orbit, UV background}, ram-pressure stripping, tidal stripping, reionization suppression and stellar-feedback outflows share atoms and differ in the bond. They are **isomers**: same formula, different observable behaviour in H I content, star formation history and spatial structure.
- If the atom sets are not shared (for example, reionization operates where no hot halo exists), the mechanisms are not isomers. They are members of one **functional group**: a `state → structure → state` shutdown module with different qualifiers. The group fixes the kind (quenched), the qualifier fixes which member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two mechanisms over the same atoms produce indistinguishable distributions in H I mass, quenching epoch and spatial structure even with an ideal instrument, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives. Cortese et al. (2021) write that *"different physical mechanisms may act simultaneously and cannot always be easily separated"*; that is a warning about current instruments, not proof of in-principle indistinguishability.

### Q3 — Catalyst: the part that survives its own use

- Fig 12 :119: **"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A hot circumgalactic medium, a concentrated dark matter halo, or a dense stellar component can hold onto gas or resist stripping across many passages. It is a catalyst in this sense: it survives the interaction and keeps deciding outcomes. Hai et al. (2026) report that a dwarf's CGM is stripped quickly and does not shield the ISM, while hydrodynamic work on stripping finds that the mass distribution changes the response (Slater and Bell 2014). Which part is catalytic is a measurement.
- **Falsifier:** a candidate catalyst is consumed in its first use yet still used to explain repeated episodes. Then it is not a catalyst and the term is misapplied.

- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes a residual whose content is "a regularity we hold is false" to REFUTED, and says *"what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*. Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*.
- The held term in this field is "one mechanism explains dwarf quenching". The pre-registered refuters in `01` §4 are the refusal. Only after one of them fails or survives does a multi-mechanism term get minted.
- Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* The shape bound in `README.md` is that ordinary term.
- **Falsifier:** the refusal is issued after the new term is already in use, so it functions as decoration. Then the order is reversed and the mint is not licensed.

## CONCLUSION

Formation and shutdown are a **union with a cycle inside one branch**, not a chain. The shutdown mechanisms are **isomers over the full shared atom set**, or **functional-group members** where the atom sets differ; the isomer claim has a stated falsifier. A hot halo or concentrated core may be a **catalyst**, a part surviving its own use, subject to measurement. Refusal of the single-mechanism term precedes the multi-mechanism mint, and the sought-for shape was bound in advance so it can be refuted by the same test as everything else.

This rescues what is checkable without pretending to name the mechanism. It needs no seventh primitive, no figure-derived threshold value, and no claim that the framework selects among the mechanisms that the world has already named.
