## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Measurements that discriminate.** Weisz et al. (2015) use resolved star formation histories; Hai et al. (2026) combine CMD fitting, archival FUV and H I upper limits on a single isolated quenched dwarf. These are the kinds of instruments Fig 6 :32 requires: improved from a worse instrument already returning something.
2. **Population statistics.** Slater and Bell (2014) combine Local Group dwarfs with the NASA-Sloan Atlas, two samples with *"very little in terms of potential observational biases"* in common, and correct for redshift interlopers. This is a genuine independence argument, not two readings of one catalogue.
3. **Simulation plus ground, not simulation versus simulation.** Simpson et al. (2018) use 30 Auriga zoom simulations; Sales et al. (2022) argue that models and numerical accuracy must both improve. Agreement among several simulation suites that share subgrid recipes is one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
4. **The shape of the open problem.** Primack (2024) lists the diversity of dwarf galaxies and the puzzles small scales pose to ΛCDM. That is a census of residuals, which is what a figure-grounded answer should attach to, not a target to be explained away.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Lead with the refuter and the shape claim. A recipient who has absorbed the full framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before offering any paper.
- **Fig 2 :35** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Never ask for agreement, never ask for trust.
- **Fig 2 :42** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* For a published, checkable claim about dwarf galaxies the cost is real, so spend the effort on genuinely independent readers, not on a larger list.
- **Fig 8 :50** convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. Keep the provenance of each world claim visible.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which instrument would discriminate two mechanisms; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then, if they are curious, offer the figure text and formula links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, stated once more

1. **Stage 1:** send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` (claim, three checkable sub-claims, verbatim figure lines, refuters, non-claims). Ask for a refutation, not an endorsement.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** offer the three local framework links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

**Hard precondition:** stage 1 carries the shape and its refuters. If a rewrite turns it into "ram pressure is the answer", stages 2 and 3 must not be offered, because the first stage no longer survived (see `02_outreach_email.md`).
