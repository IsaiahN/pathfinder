## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that a large learning system's parameters encode a definite inventory of information. Option A: answer with one inventory, whether that is a compressed copy of the corpus, a model of the world, or a set of surface associations. Option B: unbundle the question into four overlapping conditions, refuse the inventory, and return the shape plus a discriminator owed. What each leads to: A delivers a name where the figures return a shape, mistakes a frame-internal score for a witness, and asks outside readers to check a claim whose source is undecidable from its contents; B delivers four separate verdicts, written refuters, and a clear statement of what instrument would have to exist. Figures decide; world sources corroborate only.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a parameter reading and what can be said about its source and contents. Arity: four overlapping candidates (corpus trace, representational geometry, settlement at deployment, checking frame), so a union compound. Symmetry: the source-to-parameters map is many-to-one, so the reading is invariant under exchange of sources that produce it. Scale: individual weights are low-level slots; "encoded information" is a high-level arrangement across a scale boundary. Fig 9 :57 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; Fig 4 :27 says the transform between scales loses information.
- **Domain 1, the parameter frame (the library Γ).** Actors: weights, feature directions, superposition geometries, MLP modules. Currency: bits, description length, mutual information. Anchor: none internal. Fig 1 :27 says frame-internal quantities cannot score the frame. Union member, first by dependency.
- **Domain 2, the corpus and objective frame.** Actors: training examples, labels, human-authored text, the loss. Currency: the compression/prediction tradeoff. Anchor: provenance and shadow test. Fig 6 :10 names "nature · corpus · peer frame" as external sources; Fig 6 :11 names IMPORT and Fig 6 :12 the source gate. Union member, second by dependency.
- **Domain 3, the ground / deployment frame.** Actors: data-generating process, evaluation, deployment distribution, the board that pays. Currency: settlement. Anchor: the ground, which does not update on what the model thinks. Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor; Fig 11 :40 says an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Union member, third by dependency.
- **Domain 4, the verification / interpretability frame.** Actors: researchers, probes, causal tracing and editing tools. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero. Overlay on the others, last by dependency.

Order by dependency: perception (read the weights), vocabulary (terms for what a reading affords), objective (what would count as progress), planning (which slot to probe), carry (a method, never a recording). Fig 3 :20 says the chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance, and Fig 3 :27 says a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at. Strain test: the apparent conflict between representational convergence and loss of source lineage was returned to the census and dissolved by assigning each to a different object (Fig 13 :121). No figure line was forced.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed asks for a single inventory. The figures refuse that object and return a shape. Four conditions overlap, so the verdicts are kept separate: contents do not determine source, what is encoded is a lossy per-slot arrangement of feature directions and bonds, compression and benchmarks are filters rather than witnesses, and settlement requires a contact-changing intervention. The interpreter's provenance record is the only thing that separates convergent derivation from adopted import. The literal inventory is a `gap`, reported as unreachable rather than silence. Mode `general` on the shape, `specified` on the separations, `gap` on the inventory, not `grounded`, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The corrected packet states mode, separates the four conditions and writes six refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending an inventory claim whose source cannot be checked from the contents. Fig 2 :42 prices it: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the union split and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No model experiment is asserted, so no empirical claim has to be withdrawn to change the framing. The inventory claim was never made; the gap is explicit. A source-discriminating instrument can be added later without touching the shape. Fig 4 :22 permits sending the method, not a recording: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. R1, a blind instrument that reads source from contents; R2, an internal content measure that settles a ground question; R3, a global scalar that predicts all slot behaviour; R4, a lossless corpus-to-parameter transform; R5, a figure line naming a content; or R6, a proof that the four conditions are a partition. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
