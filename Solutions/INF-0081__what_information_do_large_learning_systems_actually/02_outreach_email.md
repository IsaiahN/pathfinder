**Subject:** One checkable claim about what model parameters encode

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the separations, not `grounded`):** the question "what information do large learning systems actually encode in their parameters?" bundles at least four overlapping things, the corpus trace, the representational geometry, what pays at deployment, and who can check. These are not a partition, so a single inventory is the wrong object. Two consequences follow and you can check both without my theory:
>
> 1. **The contents do not determine the source.** Two training histories can produce the same parameter geometry. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the weights; only the record of where each came from separates them. That is a figure line, but it is also just an observation about identifiability that you can test with two independently trained models.
> 2. **What is encoded is per feature slot, not a global property.** The claim "the model encodes language" aggregates across slots and throws away the only signal that says where to look. Causal tracing and model editing have already shown that specific factual associations are localised and editable. That is the shape of a per-slot result.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that parameters are a lossless copy of the training corpus; that a model reconstructs the data-generating process; that a compression ratio or a benchmark score tells you what is encoded. Those are filters for what gets tried, not witnesses for what is true. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge, and a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an instrument that reads the source of a parameter set uniquely from its contents, with no provenance and no ground contact, and passes a blind test across independently trained systems;
> 2. a purely internal measure of parameter content that settles a contested ground question without intervention or benchmark;
> 3. a single global scalar that predicts ground behaviour across all feature slots and domains, so per-slot indexing is unnecessary;
> 4. a demonstration that the four candidate sources are a genuine partition rather than a union.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
