## 4. Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- **T_A, going up:** carry the primary's four-condition shape to a one-sentence abstraction. Result: *source attribution needs a discriminator, independence, a fixed anchor and per-case reporting.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that abstraction into a specific study. Result: *add a contrast, compare two papers, pick a significance threshold, and report firm-level results.*

**R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)).** The gap is what the abstraction cannot hold:

1. The one-sentence form loses the union rule. Coming back down, "add a contrast" is applied as a single lever to a four-condition union, which is effort above the break (Fig 3 :29-30). Fig 9 :57 says a case in two conditions is categorically different.
2. It loses the anchor rule. "Fixed anchor" does not say that the market return anchors payment and only a pre-registered contrast anchors source (Fig 2 :29-30). Coming back down, a researcher can use an in-sample threshold as its own ground (Fig 1 :27).
3. It loses the independence rule. "Compare two papers" does not say whether they share a pool. Coming back down, two teams on one dataset look like two witnesses (Fig 2 :31, :40).
4. It loses the gap on luck. "Report per case" has no place for the honest declaration that luck is the residue the instruments cannot source (Fig 8 :39, Fig 13 :134-135).

This gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). It is also why the outreach packet sends the generator, not the one-sentence version (Fig 4 :25). The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such.
