## Question restated in effect terms

The decision is between three options and what each leads to.

- Option A: preserve expertise by insulating experts (closed peer guild, restricted access, validation only from inside the discipline). This preserves the comfort of the frame and risks severing the channel to both the discipline's ground and public need, with failures that are invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :68-69).
- Option B: preserve public contact by treating public opinion as the arbiter of expertise. This risks a coherent drift in which the technical anchor updates on whatever the public currently approves, and nobody notices (Fig 2 :34).
- Option C, the figure-derived shape: keep two distinct non-updating grounds, keep the frames independent and low-overlap, build the institution as the mediating structure between expert and public, and spend the design effort on keeping the channels reachable and the conventions refusable.

What an answer would look like has been written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run, per the formula's "Direction" step: a structure that (1) leaves the expert's technical ground pinned, (2) leaves public need and human judgment as a separate anchor, (3) lets public criticism range against the expert frame without updating it, and (4) keeps an instrument for progress at the broken middle link. That shape then accrues a residual like any other term, so it is refusable by the same test as everything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words.

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :41 makes the same correlation *"efficient for a convention."* A list chosen for likely agreement produces a convention, not a verdict.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The outside frame must not hold the interiors of the claim it is asked to refute.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
