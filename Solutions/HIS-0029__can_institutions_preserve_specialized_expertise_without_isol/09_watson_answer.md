## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that institutions can preserve specialized expertise, that is scarce, hard-won, ground-checked competence, without isolating the experts who hold it from public needs and criticism. Option A: preserve expertise by insulating experts, so validation stays inside the discipline. Option B: preserve contact by letting public opinion arbitrate expertise. Option C: the figure-derived third structure, keeping two distinct non-updating grounds, independent low-overlap frames and a reachable critic channel. What each leads to: A substitutes a habitat and drops the contact that made the expertise capable, with failures invisible until the goal fails; B turns the technical anchor into another frame so everything drifts coherently and nobody notices; C returns a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and an open mechanism.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between an institution's prediction that insulation protects expertise and what arrives: drift from the discipline's ground, loss of legitimacy, unregistered needs. Arity: at least two conditions with different anchors, so a union compound (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), not one dial. Symmetry: asymmetric and directed, because criticism flows toward the expert and expertise is answerable to need (Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."). Scale: individual expert, institution, public, with a directional filter between scales (Fig 4 :21). R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), not aggregated into an institution-wide trust score (Fig 1 :22).
- **Domain 1, the expertise or specialty frame.** Actors: expert, apprentice, peer reviewer, the discipline's library. Currency: validated results, competence, credentials, what pays inside the closure. Anchor: the discipline's ground, measurement, experiment, proof, the phenomenon (Fig 2 :29). Genuine domain. Question type: which compositions keep the anchor reachable.
- **Domain 2, the public or need frame.** Actors: affected communities, users, practitioners, journalists, extended peers. Currency: lived needs, values, criticism, extended facts, material consequences. Anchor: human judgment about what is good for people plus material consequences (Fig 2 :37). Genuine domain, different anchor. Question type: which frames hold facts the expert closure lacks.
- **Domain 3, the institutional design and verification frame (the seat).** Actors: governance, funders, oversight bodies, engagement officers. Currency: conventions, rules, channels, instructions. Anchor: two anchors held reachable at once, D1 and D2 (Fig 10 :24, :29). Genuine domain. Question type: composition over both, so a compound with its own loop.
- **Order by dependency.** Keep the two grounds pinned before choosing a frame (Fig 2 :33); describe the residual before choosing the engagement mechanism (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse a fixed-outcome convention before minting a new one (formula step 2); settle each slot before composing over it (Fig 12 :104). The chain usually breaks at the objective link, where there is no instrument for progress per slot (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 gives public judgment the anchor for questions about people and only an instrument for technical questions; Fig 11 makes contact necessary for capability. These do not strain after re-census. They are two anchors with two channels, and the design seat keeps both reachable. The mapping of the six-term substrate onto expert and public as states is a separate, live strain, named in `06` and not hidden here.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only. Figures decide. A conflict sends us back to the census, never to a flipped verdict.

- **Sociology, social networks, genuinely low overlap.** Mark Granovetter, "The Strength of Weak Ties," American Journal of Sociology 78(6), 1973 (full text opened at MIT reproduction URL). Quote: "The fewer indirect contacts one has the more encapsulated he will be in terms of knowledge of the world beyond his own friendship circle; thus, bridging weak ties (and the consequent indirect contacts) are important in both ways." Prediction: an expert guild with only strong internal ties is encapsulated; bridging ties carry what the closed frame lacks. Corroborates Fig 11 :40 and Fig 8 :49. Conflict recorded: a 2023 Annual Reviews review, "What Makes Weak Ties Strong?", challenges whether weak ties are bridges and whether they transmit well enough for the structural claim; author not captured from the search snippet, abstract only, reduced weight. Re-census: the challenge narrows the claim to "the channel must actually transmit," which is Fig 10 :24. Verdict not flipped.
- **History and philosophy of science.** Michael Polanyi, "The Republic of Science: Its Political and Economic Theory," Minerva 1:54-74, 1962 (full text opened at the Polanyi Society). Quote: "Consider by contrast the effect which a complete isolation of scientists would have on the progress of science." Prediction: isolation stops the production of new problems; coordination is by mutual adjustment of independent initiatives (chains and networks of overlapping neighbourhoods). Corroborates Fig 6 :23 and Fig 7 :36. World conflict with Funtowicz and Ravetz: Polanyi also says the scientist needs "a secluded place among like-minded colleagues who keenly share his aims and sharply control his performances." Re-census: his seclusion is from public direction of research questions and is paired with mutual peer control, a different object from Fig 11 isolation from the ground. Both require continuing criticism; they differ over who counts as a peer and for which ground. Verdict not flipped.
- **Science studies, post-normal science.** Silvio Funtowicz and Jerome Ravetz, "Science for the Post-Normal Age," Futures 25(7):739-755, 1993, read through Silvio Funtowicz, "A quick guide to post-normal science," i2Insights, 2021 (both opened; the 1993 publisher page returned 403, so partial read flagged in the ledger). Quote: "In such contexts of policy making, normal science (in the Kuhnian sense, see Kuhn 1962) is still necessary, but no longer sufficient." Prediction: extend the peer community without abandoning quality assurance; quality depends on open dialogue with all those affected. Corroborates Fig 8 :45 and Fig 10 :24, and explicitly corroborates the two-anchor split by refusing to replace certified expertise.
- **Public understanding of science.** Brian Wynne, "Misunderstood misunderstanding: social identities and public uptake of science," Public Understanding of Science 1(3):281-304, 1992 (abstract opened via IOPscience and SAGE; direct article fetch 403, partial read flagged). Quote: "The case study shows laypeople capable of extensive informal reflection upon their social relationships towards scientific experts, and on the epistemological status of their own 'local' knowledge in relation to 'outside' knowledge." Prediction: affected publics hold knowledge the expert frame lacks, so they are a differing closure, not an audience to be educated. Corroborates Fig 8 :45 and Fig 6 :23.
- **Human cognition and social psychology, genuinely low overlap.** Irving Janis's groupthink literature, read via the full Wikipedia reference opened this session, a tertiary source at reduced weight. Quote: "the mode of thinking that persons engage in when concurrence-seeking becomes so dominant in a cohesive ingroup that it tends to override realistic appraisal of alternative courses of action." Prediction: an insulated expert room drifts coherently; prevention by outside experts, independent groups and devil's advocacy is the low-overlap import of Fig 8 :45 and the refusal operator of the Operators table. Corroborates Fig 7 :36 and Fig 2 :34. The empirical record is mixed, with cohesion itself not reliably predictive while insulation and homogeneity generally are, which agrees with the figure's emphasis on overlap rather than friendliness.

Overlap declaration: Granovetter and the groupthink literature are genuinely low-overlap witnesses with each other and with the framework. Polanyi, Funtowicz and Ravetz, and Wynne all belong to the broad history and social studies of science pool, share part of the same debate and cite some of the same work, so they are **one witness with internal disagreements**, not three independent confirmations. The eight framework essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent witness weight. The figure verdicts stand without them.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the either/or does not. Insulation is habitat substitution, so it does not preserve the capability whose contact it drops. The two conditions carry different anchors, so correctness stays settled by the discipline's ground and legitimacy and need stay settled by human judgment, with neither anchor updating on the other frame's approval. Public criticism earns its place only when it draws on different evidence and the specific residual was described before the mechanism was chosen; otherwise the institution and its "public" are one frame with two names. The institution is the required mediator between expert and public, and over a fixed atom set the insulating and porous forms are isomers, so the variable is type and not degree. The ground does not decay, but the channel does, and a consultation whose outcome is fixed has stopped being a consultation. No figure line returns the mechanism, so that is a `gap`, reported as a verdict rather than filled. Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the mechanism, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the refuters are used. The real cost is asking outside readers to endorse a shape before they have tested it, which spends the independence that makes their check worth having (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). Declaring a `gap` instead of inventing a mechanism avoids the larger cost of a false name. A smaller cost is the live strain on the substrate mapping, carried visibly in `06` rather than smoothed over.
- **undoable:** yes. Conventions carry no truth value and can be rewritten (Fig 10 :45-46); a channel can be reopened, a fixed-outcome consultation can be withdrawn, and a provenance record can be added to existing criticism. No primitive has to be withdrawn to correct this package. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet in `08`.
- **reversed by:** a per-slot ground reading, not agreement. A measured contact-free capability increase would break Fig 11 :40; a demonstration that public judgment is the right anchor for technical correctness, or that the anchor can update and still settle, would break Fig 2 :33/:37; a real surplus from high-overlap frames would break Fig 8 :49; a fixed-outcome consultation yielding a checkable verdict would break Fig 10 :47; and a derivation of the specific mechanism from the figures alone would falsify the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
