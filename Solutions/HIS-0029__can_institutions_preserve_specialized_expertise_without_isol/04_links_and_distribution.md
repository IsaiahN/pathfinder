## What the wider literature actually contributes

These are **secondary corroboration only.** The figures decide. None of these sources overrules a figure line, and a conflict sends us back to the census.

1. **Sociology, genuinely low overlap: Granovetter 1973.** *"The fewer indirect contacts one has the more encapsulated he will be in terms of knowledge of the world beyond his own friendship circle; thus, bridging weak ties (and the consequent indirect contacts) are important in both ways."* Predicts that a closed expert guild is encapsulated and that bridging ties carry what the closed frame lacks. Corroborates Fig 11 :40 and Fig 8 :49. His conclusion that *"strong ties, breeding local cohesion, lead to overall fragmentation"* is the same shape as Fig 7 :12-13. Caveat recorded honestly: a 2023 Annual Reviews review, *"What Makes Weak Ties Strong?"*, raises two challenges, whether weak ties are actually bridges and whether they transmit information well enough for the structural property to matter. That is a **conflict between world sources**, not with the figure verdict. Re-census: it narrows the claim to *the channel must actually transmit*, which is exactly Fig 10 :24 (*"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). The verdict is not flipped. The 2023 review was read as an abstract via search only and its author was not captured, so it carries reduced weight.
2. **History and philosophy of science: Polanyi 1962.** *"Consider by contrast the effect which a complete isolation of scientists would have on the progress of science."* And: *"the whole of science will be covered by chains and networks of overlapping neighbourhoods."* Predicts that isolation stops the production of new problems and that coordination runs through mutual adjustment, not central direction. Corroborates Fig 6 :23 and Fig 7 :36. **World conflict with Funtowicz and Ravetz:** Polanyi also writes that *"For such work the scientist needs a secluded place among like-minded colleagues who keenly share his aims and sharply control his performances"* and that the soil of academic science must be exterritorial. That seems to favour insulation. Re-census (see `09` WORLD and `11`): Polanyi's seclusion is from *public direction of research questions* and is paired with *mutual peer control*, which is a different object from Fig 11's isolation from the ground. Both sources require continuing criticism; they differ over who counts as a peer and for which ground. No flip.
3. **Science studies: Funtowicz and Ravetz 1993, and Funtowicz's 2021 guide.** *"In such contexts of policy making, normal science (in the Kuhnian sense, see Kuhn 1962) is still necessary, but no longer sufficient."* And: *"For the problems addressed by post-normal science, quality depends on open dialogue between all those affected."* Predicts the extended peer community: add frames without abandoning quality assurance. Corroborates Fig 8 :45 and Fig 10 :24. It also explicitly refuses replacement of certified expertise, which corroborates the two-anchor split in F2.
4. **Science studies / public understanding: Wynne 1992.** *"The case study shows laypeople capable of extensive informal reflection upon their social relationships towards scientific experts, and on the epistemological status of their own 'local' knowledge in relation to 'outside' knowledge."* Predicts that affected publics bring knowledge the expert frame does not hold, so they are a differing closure rather than an audience to be educated. Corroborates Fig 8 :45 and Fig 6 :23.
5. **Human cognition and social psychology: Janis's groupthink literature.** *"the mode of thinking that persons engage in when concurrence-seeking becomes so dominant in a cohesive ingroup that it tends to override realistic appraisal of alternative courses of action."* And *"Insulation of the group: This can promote the development of unique, inaccurate perspectives on issues the group is dealing with, which can then lead to faulty solutions to the problem."* Predicts exactly the mirror-chain failure of an insulated expert room (Fig 7 :36, Fig 2 :34). Prevention in that literature (outside experts, independent groups, devil's advocacy) is the low-overlap import of Fig 8 :45 and the refusal seat of Operators :58. The empirical record is mixed, and the Wikipedia page reports that cohesion itself is not reliably predictive while insulation and homogeneity generally are. That agrees with the figure's emphasis on overlap rather than on friendliness.

**Overlap declaration.** Granovetter (sociology) and the groupthink literature (social psychology) are genuinely low-overlap with the framework and with each other. Polanyi, Funtowicz and Ravetz, and Wynne are all in the broad history and social studies of science pool and partly share citations and a common debate about expertise and democracy, so they count as **one witness with internal disagreements**, not three independent confirmations. The framework essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent witness weight.

- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would count as a failure before showing the literature.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Do not rank recipients by likely agreement; a mass send selected for receptiveness produces a convention.
- **Fig 7 :35:** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Prefer a venue where a public record is made and a refusal can be entered, not a private exchange of encouragement.

(3) Only then the three framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
