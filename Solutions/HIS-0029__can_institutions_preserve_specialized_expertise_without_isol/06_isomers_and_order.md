### Q1: Can an expert and a public be bonded directly? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST READING

- Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*
- Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*

At the institutional scale, an expert and a public are two states. A structure must sit between them. That structure is the institution's methods and channels, acting as a filter with a direction: methods cross up, head starts come down (Fig 4 :22). So "the institution mediates" is not a preference for bureaucracy; it is the cross-kind requirement at this scale.

**Strain, named and not hidden.** This maps the six-term physical substrate onto institutional actors. Institutions are not made of space, time and energy. What carries over is the *rule* that two states need an intervening structure and that the transform between scales is lossy. The README's re-census rule says a reading that makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain; here the domain is institutional composition at a different stratum, which is permitted because the rules are recursive and domain-agnostic (Fig 12 :104 and the figures README on recursion). I keep this as a **live strain on the mapping**, not a correction to the figure. It is triangulated for the mediator requirement (Fig 13 :75 plus Fig 4 :21) and `single-source` for the specific state labeling.

**Falsifier, written before the conclusion:** show a direct expert-public bond with no mediating structure that still settles both grounds. That breaks Fig 13 :75.

### Q2: Are the insulating and porous forms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Define the fixed atom set: the same experts, the same affected publics, the same needs, the same methods, the same channels available in the environment. Then:

- **Insulating form:** expert bonded to a closed peer room; public bonded to a communications office; the two bonds run inside separate closures. The institution records the ingredient lists (staff, offices, procedures) but not the difference in bond, which is exactly the half nothing was recording (Fig 12 :5).
- **Porous form:** expert bonded to the public through a described residual, with an independent critic room and a channel that can carry a refusal. Same ingredients, different bond, different substance.

Under Fig 12 :117 those are **isomers**, not degrees of one thing. The variable is **type, not amount**. Both share the functional group *"expert-and-habitat in contact with a ground"* (Fig 12 :123); the qualifier decides which member. This dissolves the trade-off reading: adding a public-relations office to the insulating form does not move it along a dial toward the porous form; it leaves the bonds in place and adds an ingredient.

Because actor valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), there is no standing list of which form is good. The porous form is better only for residuals where public need and external criticism are live at the slot. For a residual entirely inside the discipline's ground (a proof check, a measurement replication), the closed peer room can be the right room, provided the technical anchor is not moved by opinion and the channel to the public need is not needed for that slot. Verdicts stay per slot.

**Live strain, narrowed.** The isomer claim requires a genuinely fixed atom set. In practice a porous form often adds an atom (a new critic, a new dataset, an extended fact). When it does, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism, and the import needs a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23-24, Fig 8 :51). I keep this as a live condition on the claim, not a hidden caveat. **Status: `single-source` on the figure side for the isomer mapping** (Fig 12 :117 definition only), with world corroboration from Granovetter's overlap result being about a different object and therefore not counted as a second witness for this specific mapping.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either form requires an atom the other lacks, the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import. If a form can be shown to preserve the same capability with no contact change, Fig 11 :40 fails and the whole shape goes with it.

### Q3: What order does the institutional chain have, and where does it break?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :25-27 (the middle link): having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

The five links for this question:

| link | can it be read here? | typical instrument | failure signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | yes | complaint records, published critiques, field reports | stale percepts, dropped transients (Fig 10 :26) |
| 2 vocabulary | yes | an "engagement" office, a stated mission, a participation policy | a declared meaning that is not grounded (Fig 10 :49) |
| 3 objective | **usually no** | none in most institutions | no term for what counts as progress at this slot |
| 4 planning | reached only if 3 is | governance routes, review boards | a plan aimed at a goal nobody can state |
| 5 learn and carry | reached only if 4 is | named what worked, verified, carried | a recording of one success replayed downward (Fig 4 :25) |

So the chain usually breaks between 2 and 3. An institution can name its stakeholders and publish its engagement policy and still have no instrument that says what would count as progress on a specific unmet need. Adding more vocabulary at link 2 (a new office, a new statement) cannot rescue link 3. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

**Falsifier:** show an institution whose link-3 objective is measured per slot and whose public criticism actually enters that measurement without the technical anchor updating. That would move the chain past the break and would be a real ground reading, which this derivation did not take.

### Q4: What crosses the scale membrane, and what is the round-trip gap?

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

From the individual expert to the institution, what crosses up must be a **reapplicable method**: how to characterise a residual, how to keep a channel reachable, how to enter a refusal. What comes down is a head start, not a replay of one successful consultation. A single case study publicised as "our engagement model" is a recording; replayed at the next slot it produces the form of participation without the contact (Fig 11 :70: a substituted habitat proves wiring and not capability).

The round trip from the verbatim figures, through the institutional shorthand, back to a concrete institution, loses at least three constraints, enumerated in `11_audit.md` as R_T: the two-anchor distinction, the low-overlap requirement, and the refusable-convention rule. The gap is nonzero and must be recorded, not assumed zero (Fig 4 :31-32).

## CONCLUSION

The institution is the required structure between expert and public (Fig 13 :75, Fig 4 :21). Over a fixed atom set the insulating and porous forms are isomers (Fig 12 :117), so the variable is type, not degree, and the valence of either form is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49). The institutional chain usually breaks at the objective link, where there is no instrument for progress per slot (Fig 3 :25-27), and no amount of added vocabulary at the link above rescues it. What crosses scales is method, not recording (Fig 4 :22-25), and the round-trip gap R_T is nonzero. Two strains are kept live: the mapping of the six-term substrate onto institutional actors, and the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim.
