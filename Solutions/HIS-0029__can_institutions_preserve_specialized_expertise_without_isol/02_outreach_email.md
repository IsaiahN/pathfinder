# Outreach: check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been checked by someone in a different closure and the refuter has been read by them first. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about expertise and public criticism before you decide whether the argument is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm working on a question in the history and organization of knowledge: can an institution keep deep, scarce expertise without cutting the experts off from public needs and criticism? I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the claim is a shape, not a policy. It says four things.
>
> 1. Capability belongs to the expert and the habitat together, not to the expert alone. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves. So insulating experts does not preserve their expertise. It substitutes a different habitat and drops the contact that made the expertise capable, with two failures that are invisible until the goal fails: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced.
> 2. There are two grounds, not one. The discipline's phenomena settle whether a result is correct. Human judgment settles whether an answer is good for people. Neither should update on what the other frame currently approves. If public approval sets technical validity, everything drifts together and nobody notices. If the discipline's convenience sets what counts as a public need, the "public" channel becomes a mirror.
> 3. The two frames must differ at the evidence level to produce anything. A public engagement exercise that draws on the same reports as the institution is one frame wearing two names. The surplus is real but conditional: describe the specific unmet need or criticism first, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. A single loud voice imported as "the public" is a wrong atom, and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives.
> 4. The ground does not decay; the channel does. Stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings are the real failure. The honest reading of a failed channel is "you have lost touch," not "the public was wrong." And a consultation whose outcome is already fixed has stopped being a consultation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not naming the governance mechanism, the ratio of insulation to exposure, the number of public seats, or any participation ritual. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that public opinion decides technical truth, and I am not claiming that expertise is a substance that insulation can store. I am not claiming to have measured any institution. This is a `specified` derivation, checked for internal consistency against a set of figure texts, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a competence improvement that changes no contact with either ground and still increases capability at the slot;
> 2. a demonstration that public judgment is the proper anchor for technical correctness, or that the technical anchor can update on frame opinion and still settle anything;
> 3. a homogeneous expert room drawing only on its own evidence producing a genuine surplus against the ground;
> 4. a consultation whose outcome is fixed still yielding a checkable verdict about the world.
>
> I wrote these down before drawing the conclusion and before showing any theory to anyone. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. Their canonical README says they outrank the docs and the rulings in their own repository.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative and the shape limit: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a preference that is not bound as an ordinary term is invisible to every check it runs. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]
