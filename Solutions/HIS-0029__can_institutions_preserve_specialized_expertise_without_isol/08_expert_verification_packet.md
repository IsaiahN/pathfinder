# Expert verification packet: the Tether on expertise and public criticism

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of expertise, public engagement or institutional history can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the mechanism, not `grounded`.** No institution was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the historical question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Capability belongs to the expert and the habitat together, so an institution that preserves expertise by insulating experts substitutes a habitat and silently drops the contacts that made the expertise capable. The two conditions in the question are not one dial: the discipline's phenomena settle correctness, human judgment settles what is good for people, and neither anchor should update on the other frame's approval. Public criticism adds something only when it draws on different evidence and the specific unmet need was described before the engagement mechanism was chosen. The institution is the structure that must sit between expert and public, the insulating and porous forms are isomers over a fixed atom set, and a consultation whose outcome is fixed has stopped being a consultation. No figure line returns the specific mechanism; that is declared as a `gap`, not filled.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - habitat and contact; checkable by reading Figure 11
- **Statement:** insulating an expert does not preserve the capability. It substitutes one habitat for another and inherits actors that were not chosen, with two failures that are invisible until the goal fails. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** is capability in your field a property of the person alone, or of the person and their contact arrangement? Look for a case where a competence improvement with no contact change moved a real outcome.
- **Refuter:** one measured case of a contact-free competence improvement increasing capability at the slot. That breaks Fig 11 :40.

### C2 - two anchors and low overlap; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 8
- **Statement:** public judgment is the right anchor for whether an answer is good for people and only an instrument for whether a result is correct. An institution and its own communications office overlap almost completely, so their union adds no surplus. A criticism that is merely adopted, and one reached separately, look identical in the content and differ only in provenance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 8 :49 *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did the public criticism draw on evidence the institution did not hold, and was the specific need named before the participation format was chosen? Or did the "public" channel simply re-report the institution's own material?
- **Refuter:** a high-overlap channel producing a genuine surplus at the slot, or a mechanism chosen before the residual was described remaining checkable. That breaks Fig 8 :49 and Fig 9 :27-28.

### C3 - the channel, the convention and the gap; checkable by reading Figures 10, 12 and 13
- **Statement:** the ground does not decay but the channel does, so the design task is channel maintenance. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. The institution is the required mediator between expert and public; the insulating and porous forms are isomers over a fixed atom set. No figure line names the mechanism, which is reported as a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 13 :75 *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **What to check:** can you name a public engagement exercise whose result was fixed in advance and that still produced a checkable verdict? Can you show a direct expert-public bond with no mediating structure? Can you derive a specific governance mechanism from the figures alone?
- **Refuter:** yes to any of those breaks C3. The isomer claim also fails if either form needs an atom the other lacks, in which case the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a trade-off dial, an optimal ratio of insulation to exposure, or a number of public seats.
- Not that public opinion decides technical truth. The technical anchor does not update on approval (Fig 2 :33).
- Not that all insulation is bad. Closed peer rooms can be the right room for a residual entirely inside the technical ground.
- Not that any engagement creates a surplus. High overlap yields none (Fig 8 :49).
- Not a governance mechanism, participation ritual or org chart. That is the declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument, and no institution was measured.
- Not that expertise is a sixth primitive or a substance. It is a label for a lineage that met its ground (Fig 11 :36).
- Not that the six-term substrate maps literally onto institutions. That mapping is kept visible as a live strain in `06`.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot ground reading, not agreement. The discriminator needs four readings at a minimum:

1. Contact test: did capability move with contact, or without it (C1)?
2. Anchor test: did the technical ground stay pinned while legitimacy and need were settled separately (C2)?
3. Overlap and provenance test: did the critic hold different evidence, and is the source of each criticism recorded (C2)?
4. Refusal test: could a criticism change the course, or was the outcome fixed (C3)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :40 and :66-69 (four short passages). Decide whether the habitat claim describes your field.
2. Read Fig 2 :37 and Fig 8 :49. Name one case where a public criticism changed an expert judgment and one where it merely echoed the institution.
3. Read Fig 10 :24 and :47. Name one consultation whose outcome was fixed, or say why none exists in your area.
4. Check the gap: try to derive a mechanism from the figures alone. If you can, the `gap` verdict is wrong and should be retracted.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record the verdict before opening the framework links.
