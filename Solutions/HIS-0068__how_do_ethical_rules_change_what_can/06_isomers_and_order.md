# Supporting derivation: structure, order, and analogues

**Mode:** `specified`, not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit established that an ethical rule is a refusal and that two outputs (reachable set, admissible methods) must be kept apart. Three further questions are owed:

1. Is the historical material one problem with several instances, or several conditions that overlap? If the latter, it must be unbundled before search.
2. Are successive ethical codes isomers (same atoms, different bond) or members of a functional group (same structure, different atoms)?
3. What is the order: which compositions must settle before which, and what bond carries the change?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available in the library: the six substrate terms, plus the discovered terms of this question (subject, risk, consent, review, record, habitat). Bonds available: the seven, plus the unary negation. No new primitive is available without import, and none was imported. See Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-58: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

Take the case "a method is banned and it was the only instrument for a slot". Two conditions hold: the method is inadmissible (D3) and the slot loses its only route to the ground (D1). Clearing only the admissibility condition leaves the instrument gap standing. The residue is not a harder version of either condition; it is a compound. So the historical material is a **union with a compound centre**, not one problem with many examples. Fig 1 :19 (quoted in `01`) requires the union to be indexed per slot.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if every historical rule change can be fully described by either the reach predicate or the admissibility predicate alone, with no case where both bind, the union reading fails.

### Q2 — Isomers or functional group?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

As the codes are usually written, Nuremberg (consent, risk, purpose), Helsinki (consent, risk, review, publication), Belmont (persons, beneficence, justice) and the 3Rs (replacement, reduction, refinement) do **not** share an atom set. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are members of one **functional group**: a refusal attached to a subject, a risk comparison, a review seat, and a record. The group fixes the kind; the qualifier fixes the member.

The stronger claim is available. If each code is rewritten over the full shared atom set {subject, risk, consent, review, record, habitat}, differing only in which atom is bonded to which and with what weight, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. That is the checkable structural claim.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any code requires an atom that another does not, or if two codes over the identical atom set differ only in name and not in bond or weight, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 — What is the order?

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

The chain for this question:

```
perception of a failure
    -> vocabulary for what it affords (consent, risk, vulnerability, review)
    -> objective (knowledge versus protection, and how to weigh them)
    -> plan (protocol, review, registration)
    -> carry (code, regulation, guideline becomes a method for the next case)
```

The middle link is the one Fig 3 :24-27 says usually breaks, because it has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests. The Belmont weighting gap recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md` is an instance: the terms exist, the objective is not fixed by the report, and the review board supplies the weighting. A link with no shared instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27).

Carry is licensed by Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* A code that has settled in a community becomes an operand for the next code. This is recursion, not import: it adds no new primitive. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

**Falsifier:** if a later code can be shown to work without any earlier code having settled, the recursion claim fails.

### Q4 — Which bonds carry the change?

- **¬ negation**, not a bond. Operators table :73: *"Negation is the refusal of a claim."* Fig 12 :112: *"A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* This is the rule itself.
- **→ sequence**, ordered. Operators table :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. This is the historical cascade in Q3. Because it is ordered, swapping two links produces a different thing.
- **⋛ comparison**, ordered and threshold-like. Operators table :48: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. The risk-benefit test in the codes is a comparison bond: benefits ⋛ risks. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A standard that sets the threshold at zero risk is not a stricter version of the same comparison; it is a comparison no real occupant can satisfy (Fig 10 :53-54).
- **− subtraction** is explicitly *not* the rule's operation. Operators table :73: *"− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand."* My first reading used −; it was retracted in `01` §6.

A vocabulary holding only ingredients cannot write the rule. It can name the subject, the risk and the review, but not that one of them was refused or that one compares against another. Fig 12 :112-113: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

### Q5 — Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as *"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down"*, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 5 :55, Fig 6 :42, Fig 9 :54 and :51, Fig 10 :42-54, Fig 11 :66-69.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `02_outreach_email.md`: a refusal lowers the reach per slot; admissibility is a separate convention; legitimacy depends on the kind of question; habitats are substituted.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the historical claim that a named code changed a named field at a named date.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19), which the historical narrative flattens into "ethics restricted the field";
- the **anchor condition** (Fig 2 :36-37), which the narrative drops when it treats a code as simply true;
- the **two silent substitution failures** (Fig 11 :68-69), which a before-and-after code comparison does not record.

One thing survives cleanly: a refusal leaves a record and lowers a reach, and admissibility is a different loop from reachability. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading was taken here. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the condensed formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## CONCLUSION

The historical material is a union with a compound centre, not one problem. The codes form a functional group as written and a genuine isomer claim when rewritten over a shared atom set. The order is dependency order, with the objective link the one most likely to break, and carry is recursion rather than import. Three bonds do the work (¬, →, ⋛), and subtraction was the wrong reading and is retracted. The round trip to a named historical cause loses at least three constraints, so the honest artifact is the shape plus its refuters, not a causal history.
