## 0. The claim under audit

**Question as effect.** A community holds a library of studyable objects and admissible methods. An ethical rule is introduced. Afterwards, some objects that could be studied are no longer studyable by that community, some methods that would have been admitted are no longer admitted, and new methods and new objects sometimes become available. The residual R is the difference between what the community's own rules predicted it could do and what it can actually do after the rule is in force. We want the operation that produces that difference, and the condition under which the rule is a legitimate settlement rather than a frame that has appointed itself an anchor.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction").** Bind the sought-for shape as a term S:

> S = "an ethical rule is a refusal that lowers the reach of studyable slots and re-sets method admissibility, with legitimacy depending on whether the question is constitutively about those the rule touches, and with habitat substitution as the route by which the physical reading can change."

S is written down before the run so that it can accrue a residual like anything else. Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"*. Fig 5 :62: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."*

**Dependency order of the question itself (Fig 3).** Settle what a rule changes in the reachable set before asking whether the rule is good; settle what counts as an acceptable method before designing a protocol; describe the residual before choosing a historical case. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*.

## 1. Residual and domain census

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: three coupled outputs (reachable objects, admissible methods, material contact), so this is not a single residual and must not be averaged. Symmetry: the rule is a unary refusal, not a symmetric join and not a subtraction; the direction of the question matters, so the operation is ¬ rather than −. Scale: it acts at the level of a single object slot, at the level of a community's closure, and at the level of a habitat that updates. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

One line per domain, with actors / currency / anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, reachable set | researchers, topics, instruments, library Γ | compositions and reachability | the ground: what would settle a claim if it were studied | partition by slot, becomes a union compound when one method is the only route to a slot |
| D2, the rule and its authors | codes, committees, regulators, prohibitions | conventions and refusals (¬), not evidence | constitutively the people the rule touches for about-people questions; otherwise none, so it is an instrument | convention until it decides an outcome |
| D3, review and admissibility | review committee, protocol, applicant | approval or refusal of a method | the ground for the world-question, never the committee's agreement | partition of admissible from inadmissible methods |
| D4, material contact | subjects, tissues, data, models, environment | contact and its cascades | the ground: the reading the experiment returns | union, because substitution carries unchosen actors |

**Partition or union.** D1 and D3 are partitions by object slot and by method class. D4 is a union: moving a residual to another habitat does not remove the original conditions, it substitutes a different set and inherits actors nobody chose. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Where a banned method was the only instrument for a slot that also depends on another condition, the overlap is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception of a failure (a study that should not have run) precedes vocabulary for what the failure affords (consent, risk, vulnerability, review); vocabulary precedes an objective (knowledge versus protection, and how to weigh them); the objective precedes a plan (protocol, review, registration); the plan precedes carrying the method to the next problem (codes, regulations, guidelines). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

## 3. Findings

### F1 — A rule is a negation; the reach falls by addition, not deletion (decisive for D1)

Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Operators table :73: *"Negation is the refusal of a claim."* Fig 12 :112: *"A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

Fig 5 :55: *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."*

Fig 6 :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*

A rule of the form "this may not be done to that subject" does not strike the topic out of the record. The topic stays, with a refusal attached, and the set of protocols composable over it shrinks. That is why the history of research ethics looks cumulative, with codes, declarations, regulations and guidelines piling up, while the set of permissible experiments narrows. The two observations are not in tension: they are the monotone record and the non-monotone reach.

This also corrects a tempting arithmetic. Adding a rule is not subtracting a freedom from a fixed total. It is adding a term whose consequence is computed by derivation over everything currently held. Fig 6 :45: *"A term is reachable if it is derivable under everything currently held"*.

**Mode:** `general` for the operation, `specified` for any particular rule. **Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 5, Fig 6 and the Operators table, three low-overlap figure lines inside one framework. **Refuter:** a rule that erases a topic from the record rather than refusing its standing, or a reach that fails to fall when a standing is refused.

### F2 — The effect is per slot, never global (D1)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

"Ethics restricts science" is an aggregate that throws away the only signal. A consent requirement closes the slot for non-consensual human challenge trials and leaves observational epidemiology open. A reporting requirement changes method admissibility without closing a topical slot at all. The claim "ethics narrows what can be studied" is therefore only true slot by slot, and the slots must be named before any verdict.

Fig 9 :54 (quoted in §1) adds the compound case: where two conditions overlap and only one lever is applied, the intersection remains, and what remains is categorically different rather than merely smaller.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 and Fig 9). **Refuter:** a rule whose effect on the reachable set is uniform across every object slot at every scale.

### F3 — Two outputs, two loops, never averaged (D1 versus D3)

Fig 9 :6: *"the verdict: is φ* in here at all?"* Fig 9 :51: *"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*

(a) What can be studied is a reachability question. It is answered with the wall and the closure. (b) What counts as an acceptable method is an admissibility question. It is answered with a convention about what evidence may enter and what a protocol may do. A rule can shrink (a) without touching (b), change (b) without shrinking (a), or do both. Averaging them produces a single number that cannot tell you which link moved.

This is the reason the two halves of the question must be reported as separate verdicts in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`, never as one score.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 and Fig 10). **Refuter:** a single measurement that settles both reach and admissibility with no distinguishable mechanism for either.

### F4 — Legitimacy is anchor-dependent, not rule-dependent (D2, the strain test)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*

Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

For the question "may this procedure be done to these people", human judgment and the consent it expresses are the legitimate anchor. The rule is not interfering with science there; the question is constitutively about the people. For the question "does this intervention work", the same rule is an instrument, and an instrument can be wrong on axes it does not measure. When an ethics convention is promoted to decide the world-question, Fig 10 :47 applies: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*, and Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

This is the re-census point of the whole audit. My first reading (see §6) treated "ethics is an anchor" as a single claim. The figures force the split: legitimate anchor for one question, instrument for another. No figure has to be strained once the domains are separated.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 and the anchor law in the local Seat Map, plus Fig 10 on the collapse). **Refuter:** an ethical convention shown to settle a world-question without ground contact, that is, an anchor whose answer moves when a frame pushes on it.

### F5 — The review seat cannot author or reconstruct what it reviews; a rule no real occupant can meet is an idealisation (D3)

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :46 and :47 (quoted in F3 and F4). Fig 10 :49 (quoted in F4).

Fig 10 :53: *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* Fig 10 :54: *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."* Fig 10 :56: *"An idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support"*.

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*.

An ethics review works only while the reviewer is outside the slice being graded, has no gain from a particular reading, and sees what agreement hides. A committee that wrote the protocol it approves has collapsed the check into the thing checked. A standard demanding zero risk, or fully informed consent in a condition where consent cannot be given, describes an idealisation rather than a position a real laboratory can occupy; the inferences it then licenses are drawn by whoever finds them useful. The local Seat Map states the same law in its own vocabulary: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"* and *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Seat Map :149, :171).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` within the framework (Fig 10 and Seat Map); the world instances are secondary, not a second witness. **Refuter:** a review body that authors the claim it reviews and still produces a sound verdict, or a rule no real occupant can meet that nevertheless protects subjects.

### F6 — Habitat substitution changes contact, so it can change the reading itself (D4)

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*. Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

When human experimentation is restricted, the residual does not vanish. It is moved to an animal model, then an organoid, then a simulation. Each move introduces actors the researcher did not choose, and each loses conditions silently. The two failures are invisible until the goal fails or until the introduced actor acts. This is also the enabling half of the answer: oversight is an introduced actor, and introduction is available even though exclusion of the underlying habitat is not.

Low-overlap world corroboration. The NC3Rs (UK National Centre for the Replacement, Refinement and Reduction of Animals in Research) states on its public 3Rs page: *"Evidence suggests that pain and suffering can alter an animal's behaviour, physiology and immunology. Such changes can lead to variation in experimental results that impairs both the reliability and repeatability of studies."* An ethical refinement here changes the ground reading, not only the convention. That is a different field and a different evidence pool from the figures.

**Mode:** `specified` for the structural claim, `grounded` only for the NC3Rs statement taken on its own page. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus an animal-welfare science source, low overlap). **Refuter:** a substitution that reproduces every condition the goal required, introduces no unchosen actor, and leaves all ground readings unchanged.

### F7 — The historical cascade is a dependency chain, and the middle link lacks an instrument

Fig 3 :19 (quoted in §1). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The cascade from Tuskegee to the National Research Act to the Belmont Report to the Common Rule, or from Baumrind's critique to revised psychological ethics standards, is a dependency chain, not a single lever. Perception precedes vocabulary, vocabulary precedes an objective, the objective precedes a protocol, and the protocol is carried forward as a method. The middle link, saying what counts as progress, is where the weighting dispute lives: the Belmont Report's Wikipedia entry records that *"the Belmont Report does not specify how its three ethical principles should be weighted or prioritized."* That link has an institution (the review board) but no shared instrument, which is why the same protocol can pass one board and fail another. More committees and more codes add rooms, not an anchor.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` for the chain structure (Fig 3 and Fig 7); `single-source` for the specific historical weighting gap (one Wikipedia report, not independently checked against the primary report). **Refuter:** a case where adding review rooms demonstrably moved the ground reading rather than the admissibility decision.

### F8 — Admissibility decisions carry provenance, and provenance is not correctness

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

Fig 10 :62: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."* Fig 10 :65-66: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

A concrete instance is in the WMA Declaration of Helsinki: *"Reports of research not in accordance with the principles of this Declaration should not be accepted for publication."* That is a convention deciding an outcome, so it has stopped being only a convention. Its provenance is public and detailed (adopted 1964, amended at successive General Assemblies through 2024), and that provenance makes the decision auditable without making it correct. A wrong admissibility ruling with a clean record is still to be corrected.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 8 and Fig 10 plus the WMA text, low overlap). **Refuter:** an admissibility decision with no recorded provenance that is nevertheless checkable and correct.

### F9 — The rule rearranges a closed set; that is why it can enable as well as restrict

Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 13 :113: *"So the array is unbounded and the reach is not. More compositions never add a member, and no chain reaches outside what the six and their bonds already span."*

An ethical rule does not add a seventh primitive to the world. It adds an arrangement inside the held set: a consent procedure, a registration requirement, a reporting standard, a 3Rs refinement. Because the arrangements do not run out, the same refusal that closes one composition opens others. Informed consent closes non-consensual work and opens a legitimate channel to populations previously unreachable by that community. This is the strongest reason not to describe ethics only as a brake.

**Mode:** `specified`, unmeasured. **Status:** `single-source` for the figure rule (Fig 13). **Refuter:** a rule that genuinely adds a primitive to the substrate, or a refusal that opens no new composition whatsoever.

### F10 — What survives: a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The canonical figures README states that the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (local file, not a figure line). Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

The shape for this question is S from §0. The names (which code, which year, which committee, which field) are historical findings that need a ground reading of documents and outcomes. This derivation does not supply them and does not pretend to.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated` within the framework (Fig 9 and the canonical README, same evidence pool), so weak corroboration by the framework's own standard. **Refuter:** a derivation from these figures that yields a unique historical code, date, or causal weight without import.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before outreach. The local Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim S is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A rule is found that removes a topic from the record instead of refusing its standing while leaving it recorded (breaks Fig 5 :55, Fig 6 :42).
2. A rule is found whose effect on the reachable set is uniform across all object slots and scales (breaks Fig 1 :19).
3. An ethical convention settles a world-question without ground contact, that is, an anchor that moves when frames push it (breaks Fig 2 :29, :36).
4. A review body authors the claim it reviews and still produces a sound verdict (breaks Fig 10 :42).
5. A prohibition that no real occupant could meet is shown to protect subjects rather than license inferences no real position supports (breaks Fig 10 :53-56).
6. A habitat substitution reproduces everything the goal required, introduces nothing, and changes no ground reading (breaks Fig 11 :66-69, :40).
7. An admissibility decision with no provenance is demonstrably checkable and correct (breaks Fig 8 :50-52, Fig 10 :62-66).
8. A derivation from the figures alone yields a unique historical code, date, or causal weight (breaks Fig 9 :32).

If any fires, S is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this audit may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Report the two outputs separately, reachable set and admissibility, and never average them (F3).
2. State the mode on every substantive claim. Nothing here is `grounded` (F1 to F10).
3. Lead outreach with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of research ethics (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
