## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what changes when an ethical rule enters a research community, and what each option leads to. Option A: treat the effect as one global restriction on science and report it as a single verdict. Option B: split the effect into a reach change and an admissibility change, add the material-contact change and the historical cascade, and give each its own verdict with a stated anchor condition and a pre-registered refuter. A aggregates across slots and hands a filter a verdict (Fig 9 :60). B keeps the signal that says where to look and returns a shape with a lineage.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, question type, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The mismatch between what a community's rules predicted it could study and admit, and what it can study and admit after a rule arrives. Arity: three coupled outputs, so this is not one residual. Symmetry: the rule is a unary refusal (¬), not a symmetric join and not a subtraction, so direction matters. Scale: one object slot, the community's closure, and a habitat that updates. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Domain 1, reachable set.** Actors: researchers, topics, instruments, library Γ. Currency: compositions and reachability. Anchor: the ground, what would settle a claim if it could be studied. Question type: partition by slot, with a union compound where one method is the only route.
- **Domain 2, the rule itself.** Actors: codes, committees, regulators, prohibitions. Currency: conventions and refusals, not evidence. Anchor: constitutively the people the rule touches for about-people questions; otherwise there is none and it is an instrument. Question type: convention until it decides an outcome.
- **Domain 3, review and admissibility.** Actors: review committee, protocol, applicant. Currency: approval or refusal of a method. Anchor: the ground for the world-question, never the committee's agreement. Question type: partition of admissible from inadmissible methods.
- **Domain 4, material contact.** Actors: subjects, tissues, data, models, environment. Currency: contact and its cascades. Anchor: the ground, the reading the experiment returns. Question type: union, because substitution carries unchosen actors.
- **Order by dependency.** Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); perceive the failure before naming vocabulary, vocabulary before an objective, objective before a protocol, protocol before carrying the method (Fig 3); describe the residual before choosing a historical frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: the apparent conflict between "ethics is an anchor" and "ethics is an instrument" was dissolved by re-census. Fig 2 :36 makes legitimacy depend on the question, so the two readings govern different domains (see `01` §5). No figure was strained after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the historicised version does not. An ethical rule is a negation: it leaves the topic in the record, refuses its standing to be composed over, and lowers the reach of studyable slots per slot by addition rather than deletion. Because the kind set is closed while the arrangements are not, the same refusal can open new compositions. Reachability and admissibility are separate loops and receive separate verdicts. Legitimacy is question-dependent: an anchor for "may this be done to these people", an instrument for "does this work". Review is sound only without authorship of the claim and without standards no real occupant can meet. A model change is a habitat substitution with two silent failures, and where it changes contact it can change the reading. The cascade from harm to code is a dependency chain whose middle link lacks a shared instrument, and more review rooms add agreement, not ground. The figures return this shape and its refuters, not a named code, date, or causal weight. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and pre-registered refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a historical narrative instead of a generator; Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its six refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The four domain verdicts can be narrowed independently without touching the derivation. A refuted anchor claim does not collapse the reach claim. A corrected substitution case can be re-recorded with provenance, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A message already sent can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A rule-by-rule slot audit that shows erasure instead of refusal, or globally uniform effects, would break the reach claim. An ethics convention that settles a world-question without ground contact would break the anchor condition. A review body that authors the claim and still gets a sound verdict, or an unsatisfiable rule that protects, would break the admissibility claim. A substitution that reproduces everything and changes no reading would break the habitat claim. A figure re-census that returns a unique code or date would break the shape limit. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
