# The Tether's answer: How do ethical rules change what can be studied and what counts as an acceptable method?

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A community holds a library of studyable slots and admissible methods. An ethical rule is introduced. The residual R is the mismatch between what the community's own rules predicted it could do and what it can do afterwards. R arrives in three places, so it is indexed and split, never aggregated. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

The sought-for shape was bound as an ordinary term in `01` §0, so the preference can accrue a residual and be refused: a refusal that lowers reach per slot, a separate admissibility convention, a legitimacy condition, and a habitat substitution.

## 2. The shape

### Domain 1, what can be studied: a reach that falls by addition

Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Fig 5 :55: *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* Fig 6 :42: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*

An ethical rule does not delete a topic. It attaches a refusal, the topic stays in the record, and the compositions that can be built over it shrink. Codes accumulate while permissible experiments narrow; those are the same event read as record and reach. The effect is per slot (Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*), so no single percentage of "science restricted" is meaningful. Because the kind set is closed but the arrangements are not (Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*), the same refusal also opens compositions: a consent rule closes non-consensual work and opens a legitimate channel to a population previously unreachable by that community. **Verdict: `triangulated` across Fig 5, Fig 6 and Fig 13; `specified`; unmeasured.**

### Domain 2, what counts as an acceptable method: a convention, not a verdict

Fig 9 :51: *"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*. Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.

Method admissibility is a different loop from reachability. It decides what may enter as evidence and what a protocol may do. Legitimacy is not a property of the rule alone but of the question: Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

So for "may this be done to these people", consent and review are the anchor and the rule is legitimate. For "does this treatment work", the same rule is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Fig 10 :53: *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* Fig 10 :54: *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."* **Verdict: `triangulated` across Fig 2, Fig 9 and Fig 10; `specified`; unmeasured.**

### Domain 3, material contact: substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*. Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Restricting one population moves the residual to a model, an organoid, a simulation. Nothing is subtracted from the world; a different habitat is installed with actors nobody chose. If the substitution changes material contact, it can change the reading itself. The NC3Rs page supplies low-overlap corroboration: refinement alters animal behaviour, physiology and immunology and can impair reliability and repeatability. **Verdict: `triangulated` across Fig 11 and the NC3Rs source (low overlap); structural claim `specified`, NC3Rs statement `grounded` on its own page.**

### Domain 4, the historical cascade: a dependency chain of rooms

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Perception of harm, vocabulary for it, an objective, a protocol, and a carried method form an ordered chain. The objective link is where the weighting dispute lives and has no shared instrument. More codes and more committees add rooms, not an anchor. **Verdict: `triangulated` across Fig 3 and Fig 7 for the structure; `single-source` for the specific historical weighting gap from one Wikipedia entry.**

**The four verdicts are kept separate. They are not averaged into one claim about "ethics and science".**

### What the answer does not name

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The figures give the shape. They do not give a unique code, year, committee, or causal weight. Those require an import with provenance and a ground reading.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that ethical rules are always right, nor that they are always a brake. They are refusals inside a closed set; the same refusal can open new compositions (F9 in `01`).
- Not that ethics settles whether a theory or treatment is true. For a world-question it is an instrument (Fig 2 :37), and an instrument can be wrong on axes it does not measure.
- Not that a code's text reveals its cause. Several sources can produce one reading; the spectrometer, not the argument, resolved the color case (*The Hard Problem is About Source*).
- Not that review committees corroborate a result. A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict (Fig 7 :35).
- Not that a standard is better because it is stricter. A rule no real occupant can meet is an idealisation (Fig 10 :54).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement, archive visit, or protocol audit was performed in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating record, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Rule-by-rule slot audit.** For each code, list the object slots that lost standing, the methods that became inadmissible, and the habitat that was substituted. That is a reading of documents and protocols, not a derivation.
2. **The per-slot prediction.** Encode a rule as a negation in a library and check that the record grows while reach falls, slot by slot. This is a simulated test unless it is run against real protocol outcomes.
3. **The anchor test.** Find a case where an ethics convention settled a world-question without ground contact. If one exists, F4 is refuted.
4. **The substitution test.** Compare readings before and after a refinement or a species/model change. The NC3Rs citation gives one domain where such a comparison is already possible.
5. **The standing falsifiers** from `01` §4, written before this answer: erasure instead of refusal, globally uniform effects, a movable anchor, a reviewer who authors the claim, an unsatisfiable rule that protects, a perfect substitution, or a figure derivation that names a unique historical cause.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the same figure adds *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The figure-level shape is reachable and recorded. The historical names are not supplied by this method, and the method says so. The canonical README calls this *"a shape, not a name"* and requires the lineage to be recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

The round trip to a named historical cause drops at least three constraints: per-slot indexing, the anchor condition, and the two silent substitution failures (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q5). R_T is nonzero and was not numerically measured. This is the record of what was given up.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not say that ethics simply restricts science. It says an ethical rule is a negation: the topic stays in the record while its standing to be composed over is refused, so the reach of studyable slots falls by addition and slot by slot, never as a global average. What can be studied and what counts as an acceptable method are two different loops and get two verdicts. The rule is a legitimate anchor only when the question is constitutively about the people it touches; for a world-question it is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. A review body checks only while it does not author or reconstruct what it reviews, and a standard no real lab can meet is an idealisation, not a protection. When a population is replaced by a model, the habitat is substituted rather than removed, with unchosen actors and two silent failures. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not a named code or a causal history; those need a ground reading that this simulated derivation did not perform.
