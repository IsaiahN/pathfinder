# Expert verification packet: ethical rules, studyable slots, and acceptable methods

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science or research ethicist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No archival or protocol measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a historical result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An ethical rule is a refusal, not a subtraction. It leaves the topic in the record and removes that topic's standing to be composed over, so the set of studyable slots falls by addition rather than deletion, and it falls per slot rather than globally. Two outputs need separate verdicts: what can be studied (a reachability question) and what counts as an acceptable method (an admissibility question). A rule is a legitimate anchor only when the question is constitutively about the people the rule touches; applied to a world-question it is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Moving a study to another habitat is a substitution, not a removal, with two silent failure modes. The framework returns this shape, its refuters, and the instruments that would close it, not a named code or a causal history.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and historical; checkable by reading Figures 1, 5, 6 and 13

- **Statement:** a rule is a negation; the record grows while the reach falls, per slot. Because the kind set is closed and the arrangements are not, the same refusal can also open new compositions.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :55 *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* Fig 6 :42 *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **What to check:** pick one documented rule (for example a consent requirement). Can you name the slots that lost standing and the compositions it opened? Does the code's own text remain in the record after the rule arrives?
- **Refuter:** a rule that erases a topic from the record rather than refusing its standing; a rule whose effect is uniform across all slots; a refusal that opens no new composition.

### C2 — normative and institutional; checkable by a research ethicist against real review practice

- **Statement:** admissibility is a convention, and its legitimacy depends on the kind of question. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. A review body can check only while it does not author or reconstruct the claim, and a standard no real occupant can meet is an idealisation rather than a protection.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 10 :53 *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* Fig 10 :54 *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."*
- **World instance to check:** the WMA Declaration of Helsinki states *"Reports of research not in accordance with the principles of this Declaration should not be accepted for publication."* That is a convention deciding an outcome. Its provenance is public (adopted 1964, amended through 2024) and that does not make it correct.
- **Refuter:** an ethics convention shown to settle a world-question without ground contact; a committee that authors the protocol it approves and still produces a sound verdict; an unsatisfiable rule that demonstrably protects subjects.

### C3 — cross-field; checkable by an animal-welfare researcher or methodologist

- **Statement:** an ethical restriction on a population does not remove the residual but substitutes a habitat. The change is asymmetric (introduction available, exclusion not), it carries unchosen actors, and it has two silent failures. If it changes material contact, it can change the reading itself.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* Fig 11 :69 *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **Low-overlap corroboration:** NC3Rs, public 3Rs page: *"Evidence suggests that pain and suffering can alter an animal's behaviour, physiology and immunology. Such changes can lead to variation in experimental results that impairs both the reliability and repeatability of studies."*
- **What to check:** name one model substitution in your field, the conditions that failed to transfer, and the actor that was unintentionally introduced. Is there a before-and-after reading?
- **Refuter:** a substitution that reproduced every condition the goal required, introduced nothing unchosen, and left all readings unchanged.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything, and not a unique historical cause. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.
- Not that an ethical code was right because it was influential. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*
- Not that ethics determines scientific truth. For a world-question it is an instrument (C2).
- Not that stricter is better, or that every review board is independent in fact.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement, archive visit, or protocol audit was performed here.
- Not a claim that the four essays cited below independently corroborate the figures. They share an author and an evidence pool with the framework.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Rule-by-rule slot audit.** For a named code, list the slots that lost standing, the methods that became inadmissible, and the habitat that was substituted.
2. **Anchor test.** Find a case where an ethics convention settled a world-question without ground contact. That refutes C2.
3. **Substitution readings.** Compare before-and-after readings for a model change; the NC3Rs domain already permits this. That tests C3.
4. **The six standing refuters** are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and are written before this packet. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 5 :55, Fig 6 :42, Fig 1 :19, Fig 13 :115 (four short passages). Do you accept that a rule adds a refusal and lowers reach without deleting the record?
2. Read Fig 2 :36-37 and Fig 10 :42, :47, :53-54. Do you agree that legitimacy is question-dependent and that an unsatisfiable standard is an idealisation? Name one real protocol that contradicts either claim.
3. Read Fig 11 :66, :68-69, :40 and the NC3Rs sentence. Name one substitution in your field with its silent failures.
4. Send the refutation, not an endorsement. If you think the shape is wrong, say which of C1, C2 or C3 breaks and why.
