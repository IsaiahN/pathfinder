## DERIVATION

### Q1 Are the three levers a sequence?

Fig 12 :30 defines `→` as "A then B; B needs A to have happened", with test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes" (Fig 12 :74). The offered menu, read as `price → choice → innovation`, commits to an order: prices first, then choice, then innovation. Nothing in the figure text or the world sources licences that order. Aghion et al. find an inverted-U between competition and innovation (World W3), which is evidence against a monotone ordering. The order is declined as an import of an ordering the derivation does not hold.

**Reading:** `→` is refused for the three levers. Mode `specified`, unmeasured. Single-source for the definition (Fig 12 :30), corroborated on the economics by W3.

### Q2 Are they a symmetric conjunction?

Fig 12 :57-59 defines `+` as "both hold at once", symmetric, with test "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." If the levers combined by `+`, then price plus choice would be the same term as choice plus price. The ecological sources find that combined effects often cannot be predicted from individual effects and that five of fifteen stressor interactions deviated significantly from additive predictions (World W5, W6). That is evidence that arrangement matters and the bond is not symmetric.

**Reading:** `+` is refused as the bond for the union. "All three" cannot be a flat, order-free list of ingredients. The bond is a priced, non-additive relation whose type is still open. Mode `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 12 :57-59, :92 with W5, W6, low overlap).

### Q3 Are they a comparison?

Fig 12 :87: `⋛` is "a threshold relation between two quantities", test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes" (Fig 12 :89). Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." The word "best" in the question is a goal, so a vocabulary without `⋛` cannot even state the question.

Fig 12 :111-113: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A flat list of three levers names a state of the policy menu; it cannot name a route to consumer welfare. The comparison is legitimate at first order, where the operands are welfare readings per axis (quantities), and it must be made per slot (Fig 1 :19). At the compound level it is a comparison of a case in two conditions with a case in one, which Fig 9 :57 says is categorically different, not merely larger.

**Reading:** "best" is `⋛` over per-slot readings, not a sum and not a sequence. Operand type is sound at first order and strained at the compound level, where the operands are compounds rather than quantities. The strain is named, not hidden. Triangulated (Fig 12 :87, Operators :51, Fig 9 :57).

### Q4 Are the three levers isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

- Hold the habitat atoms fixed: same firms, same entrants, same consumers, same authority. Vary only the intervention bond: a price control, a choice-preserving remedy, an innovation incentive. Then the three policies are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different consumer outcome. The difference is invisible if you record only the list of actors and not the intervention bond.
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." A policy memo that lists the actors but not the bond has recorded half of itself.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one lever requires an atom the others do not (for example an innovation policy that needs a patent office absent under a price policy), the isomer claim fails. The levers then fall under Fig 12 :123-124, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member": all three restore the competitive process (the group), and the qualifier says which member. Which reading holds is an empirical question about the habitat, not a matter of taste.

**Reading:** isomer claim `specified`, with a stated falsifier; functional-group fallback recorded. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12 :117, :123-124).

### Q5 What is the order of composition across scales?

Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

Once a lever settles against the ground in one slot, it becomes an atom that the next slot's policy composes over. So "all three" at market scale is a recursion of settled per-slot molecules, not an addition of three scores. The kernel does not change with size: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." (Fig 4 :20). The transform between slots is lossy, so the round trip must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

**Falsifier:** if a lever that settled in one slot cannot serve as an atom in another slot without re-settlement, the recursion claim fails and each slot must be derived independently.

## CONCLUSION

The three levers are not a sequence and not a symmetric conjunction. "Best" is a comparison (`⋛`) over per-slot readings; the union is a compound whose bond is priced and non-additive, and whose type at the compound level is still strained. Under a fixed habitat the levers are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier; if one lever needs an extra atom, the functional-group reading stands instead. Across scales the composition is recursive, not additive. This preserves what is checkable in "all three" without turning it into a composite score, a fixed order, or a name the framework cannot supply.
