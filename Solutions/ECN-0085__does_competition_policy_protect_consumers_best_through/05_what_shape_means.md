## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The local canonical README says the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So for this question a **shape** is the form of a defensible answer that can be stated before a lever is named. The shape here is: consumer welfare is the anchor; the levers are partial instruments per slot; the levers form a union; a general ranking is unavailable. "All three" as a single composite score is a name where a shape is owed. "Lower prices" as the one true lever is also a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it; "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." The same essay warns about the exact failure mode of this question: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" That is the offered menu, which presents a union as a list.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: when contact isn't possible, the best that can be sent is a plain description of what you would expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world. That is why the four refuters in `01` are written as checkable expectations, not as theoretical objections.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place." The paradox is that by the time you can name the right book, you have composed the answer through the search.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things only, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The canonical README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." Applying that here: declaring price, choice and innovation as the three relevant axes makes a choice visible. It does not make the choice the ground's own list. A fourth axis (quality, sustainability, worker welfare) could be introduced from a differing closure, and would have to be debited against that frame's independence.

## 4. Why "all three" is not the same as a composite index

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The reading does not determine the source; only the spectrometer resolved which source was present.

A composite consumer-welfare score reads one number and cannot comment on which axis produced it. That is the metamerism error: the same score can arise from low price with poor choice, or high price with strong innovation. What is owed is a better instrument (a dashboard with per-axis, per-slot readings), not a single name. Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a lever as best?

No, and the distinction matters. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing. Price, choice and innovation are not missing primitives; they are already in the habitat and the instruments. Selecting one as a lever is a binding from the competition-policy frame onto a term already held, not the invention of a seventh primitive. What is forbidden is claiming that the framework itself names the winner, or that the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." This binding adds none).

The honest form is therefore: the framework returns the union shape; which lever wins for a given slot is a binding that needs a ground reading and provenance. F8 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` records this as a gap, not a hidden answer.

## 6. Two consequences for this answer

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A list of famous antitrust scholars agreeing that "all three" is correct is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the shape against a ground.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** The same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
