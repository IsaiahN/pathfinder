# Outreach draft: check the shape before deciding about the answer

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 through F4 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated plainly and the refuter below is written down. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one claim about competition policy before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the application, not `grounded`):** competition policy does not choose one lever for consumers in general. Consumer welfare is the anchor, because the question is about people; lower prices, greater choice and more innovation are three instruments that return partial readings, indexed per consumer slot and never aggregated. The three are a union of overlapping conditions, not a menu and not a score to be summed. Where two conditions bind, a case in both is categorically different, so a single lever clears the one-condition region and leaves the intersections standing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a general ranking of the three levers, and I am not claiming that "all three" means a composite welfare index. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which lever helps which slot, under what interaction, is a declared gap until an instrument can read the three axes separately for a named market. I am also not claiming that agreement among competition specialists settles any of this. A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one instrument that returns consumer welfare on all three axes per slot with no loss, verified at the ground;
> 2. one intervention that clears the intersections, improving all three axes in every slot with no categorically different residue;
> 3. a ground reading showing welfare is invariant to one axis across all named slots;
> 4. a composite index whose weights are shown at the ground to be compensable for every slot.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The public figure text and the formula are at the links below.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local copy in the solution folder): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as Fig N :line.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a competition question you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
