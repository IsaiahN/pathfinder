## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per slot), Fig 2 :36-38 (anchor and instruments), Fig 9 :55-57 (union compound), Fig 12 :125-130 (two currencies), Fig 11 :61-63 (seat asymmetry).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: consumer is anchor, levers are partial instruments per slot, union not sum, dashboard not composite, seat introduces and cannot subtract, no general ranking.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete competition-policy decision: choose one enforcement lever for a national market, report a single consumer-welfare score.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** is lost when a national score is reported (Fig 1 :19, :22);
- the **intersection conditions** are lost when one lever is chosen (Fig 9 :55-57);
- the **two-currency separation** is lost when the axes are summed (Fig 12 :125-130).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the consumer is the anchor and the three axes are instruments. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating a formula condensation as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
