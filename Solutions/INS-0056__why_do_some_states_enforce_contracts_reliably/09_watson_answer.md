# WATSON answer: why reliable contract enforcement coexists with failed personal protection

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the checker-position shape, not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesizes `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself") it cannot settle the question it reports on. No measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, why one state enforces private contracts predictably and fails to protect people from coercion or discrimination. Option A: it is one apparatus working well in one domain and badly in another, so more of the same law and more of the same courts should fix both. Option B: the two are different domains with different anchors and different checker positions, so a lever that improves contract enforcement need not move the personal-protection residual. What each leads to: A spends reform budget above the dependency break and produces more declared rights with no instrument; B states the shape, names the refuters, and stops where the instrument runs out.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The differential between a live contract slot and a dead personal-protection slot inside one state. Arity: two overlapping conditions plus the shared state apparatus. Symmetry: asymmetric, because one case has an external anchor (payment, seizure) and the other, as formally constituted, has only a declared meaning. Scale: first order inside a dispute, second order where the checker is the state and must itself be checked. Fig 9 names a union compound: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :58).
- **Domain 1, contract enforcement.** Actors: two private parties, court, enforcement officer, market. Currency: enforceable claims, damages, recovery. Anchor: yes, payment or seizure, which does not move with anyone's opinion. Question type: partition boundary (which compositions cross kinds).
- **Domain 2, personal protection.** Actors: person, coercer (private or state), formal rights regime, inspectorate, press. Currency: rights on paper versus effective remedies. Anchor: no, if the offered anchor is the state's own declaration; yes, if an independent check is named. Not yet a domain until that check is named, per the census rule.
- **Domain 3, verification and measurement.** Actors: observers, regulators, journalists, index builders. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, actual outcomes, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero and agreement is one pool talking.
- **Order by dependency.** Register the harm, then bind a term that can travel into a case, then state an instrumented objective (the break in Domain 2), then plan with a checker outside the case, then settle and carry (Fig 3 :20 to :21). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28).

Strain test: an early reading said "rights protection has no anchor". Re-census under Fig 2 :29 and Fig 11 :39 showed this was a claim about the frame, not the world. Corrected; correction kept in `01` §6.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the institution is not named. A state can enforce contracts reliably and fail to protect persons because the two cases have different anchors and put the checker in different positions: outside the private disputants in one, potentially inside the case in the other. The residual is per slot, the conditions are a union with a real intersection, the rights chain breaks at an uninstrumented objective, and accumulation of rights language cannot carry the refusal of the state's own act. Over the shared institutional atoms the two regimes are isomers in the strong reading, a functional group in the cautious one. The differential is `specified` and ungrounded. Which particular office would occupy the outside position is a `gap` in the framework's output, not a silence to be filled by assertion.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded but real. The packet carries a mode, five refuters and a provenance record, so an error in the shape is findable. The larger cost is spending reform budget above the dependency break (Fig 3 :19) or spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished institutional blueprint before they have checked the output (Fig 2 :42). Both are avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape is a constraint, not a policy; the specific institutional bindings are imports and can be swapped without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove every named office. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A matched internal-versus-external checker study, a legal vocabulary that carries a refusal of its own state's act without an outside seat, a per-slot dataset that collapses the differential, an agent-only reform that moves the coercion residual, or a re-census that admits a different checker operator at second order. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
