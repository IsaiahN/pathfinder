**Subject:** One checkable shape about contract enforcement and rights protection

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The observation in effect terms.** Some states enforce private contracts predictably, and the same states fail to protect people from coercion or discrimination. A missed payment produces a court order and a recovery. A coercive act by an employer, a landlord or an official often produces no effective remedy. The puzzle is the differential inside one apparatus, not the existence of either outcome.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The two cases occupy different checker positions. In contract enforcement the state stands outside two private parties, so it can act as a verifier that is not a player. In personal protection the state is itself a potential party, so any checker inside it is a party checking its own case. The framework's own rule for a sound checker is: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A second rule is that "a declared meaning is not a grounded one", so writing a right into a statute does not install an anchor. What follows is a shape, not a policy: reliable protection requires a checker positioned outside the case it decides, with an instrument that can register harm and a vocabulary that can refuse the state's own act. The framework does not name which institution does this.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that commercial courts are the answer, that a particular commission or inspectorate is required, that a cross-country index settles anything, or that the shape is `grounded`. I am not claiming the two conditions are separate; they overlap wherever a contracting party is also a person needing protection. Agreement among internal reviewers is variation, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a state's own internal checker returning verdicts against the state at the same rate as an external checker, under comparable caseloads;
> 2. a legal vocabulary that carries a standing refusal of its own state's act without any external check;
> 3. evidence that contract enforcement and personal protection form a partition, with no overlapping cases;
> 4. an agent-only reform (judicial training alone, with no change in complaint channels, press, inspection or standing) that measurably moves coercion outcomes;
> 5. a dataset in which the per-slot residual always collapses into the aggregate score.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or rewrite the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local extract; public repo `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures`)
> - Institutional economics, Douglass North's 1993 Nobel lecture: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/1993/north/lecture/
> - Judicial independence and the checker-position problem: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_independence
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
