# The Tether's answer: contract enforcement versus protection from coercion

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the checker-position shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A state enforces private contracts predictably while failing to protect persons from coercion or discrimination. The question is not "why do rights fail" but why **one apparatus produces a live signal in one slot and a dead one in another**. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The two conditions overlap, so this is a union compound with its own loop, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape

1. **The aggregate is not the witness.** A state can score well overall while the personal-protection slot is dead. Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Indices and scores are filters for where to look, never witnesses for the verdict.
2. **The anchor differs between the two cases.** Contract enforcement is settled by payment or seizure, which does not move when a frame changes its opinion. Formal rights are settled by the state's own declaration, which does. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." An external check (independent court, inspectorate with subpoena, press, measured outcome) is a genuine anchor; the state's own statute is not.
3. **The load-bearing variable is the checker's position.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :43: "Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking." In contracts the state stands outside two private parties and can verify. In coercion cases the state is a party or the author of the rule, so an internal checker is a party checking its own case. This is a structural difference, not a difference in the quantity of law.
4. **The rights chain breaks at the objective link.** Commercial enforcement has an instrumented objective (recovery, delay, cost). "rights protected" on paper is a declared meaning until something can be violated and measured. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Effort spent above the break cannot rescue it.
5. **Accumulation is not correction.** The bond set can write breach, damages and comparison, but not the refusal of the state's own act; that needs the unary negation supplied by a seat outside the case. Adding statutes and commissions adds terms and cannot carry the refusal.
6. **The two regimes share atoms and differ in bond.** Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Under the strong form they are isomers; under the cautious form, members of one functional group whose qualifier is the checker's position. The overlap (worker, tenant, debtor, migrant) is a specific compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
7. **No institution is named by the framework.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Any named checker is a provenanced import with a shadow test, not a derivation.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that rights law is useless. It says a declared meaning is not a grounded one until installed where it can be violated and measured.
- Not that commercial courts solve coercion. That is a recording carried across domains, not a generator.
- Not that the two conditions are separate. They are a union with a real intersection.
- Not that the checker must be any particular office, nor that it must be external in every possible world. It must be external to the case it decides.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by an instrument. No cross-country test has been run.
- Not that low complaint counts prove low coercion. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading does not determine its source, and the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case.

1. **Checker-position test.** Compare a state's internal checker with an external checker on matched caseloads. If the internal checker returns verdicts against the state at the same rate, F2 and F3 fail.
2. **Refusal test.** Find a legal vocabulary that carries a standing refusal of its own state's act without an outside seat. If one exists, the negation claim fails.
3. **Per-slot test.** Publish independently audited rates of substantiated coercion complaints with a fixed denominator, separated from contract-recovery rates. If the slots always move together, F1 fails.
4. **Habitat test.** An agent-only reform (training judges alone, with no change in complaint channels, press, inspection or standing) that moves the coercion residual refutes F8. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
5. **Ground for the shape itself:** a measurement of outcomes in states with and without an external checker, where the checker was installed before the measurement and the slot signal was pre-registered.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the institution.** Six low-overlap figure lines govern the checker-position constraint, so the shape is triangulated within the framework. No figure line names the checking institution, and Fig 9 :32 limits the output to a kind of thing. That is a `gap` for the design question, stated plainly rather than filled. The six-term read of institutions is a visible choice, not nature's own list, and the framework's own rule is indiscernibility is not identity. What is recorded here is the lineage, the refuter and the instrument that would close it.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not offer a new theory of institutions. It says the puzzle is per slot, not global: a state can have a live contract-enforcement slot and a dead personal-protection slot at once, and averaging the two hides the signal. It says the difference is the position of the checker, not the quantity of law. Contract enforcement has an anchor outside the disputing parties, payment or seizure, and a state that can verify without being a party. Coercion and discrimination put the state inside the case, so the rule it writes is not an anchor and its own inspectors cannot verify its own conduct. The rights chain also breaks earlier, at the objective link, because it has a declared meaning where the commercial chain has an instrument. The two regimes share atoms and differ in bond, and their overlap is a compound with its own loop. The framework returns a shape: protection requires a checker outside the case, an instrument that registers harm, and a way to refuse the state's own act. It does not name the office. Five refuters were written before this paragraph, and the ground, not agreement, decides.
