# Expert verification packet: contract enforcement and protection from coercion

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in contract enforcement research can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the checker-position shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A state can enforce private contracts reliably and still fail to protect persons from coercion or discrimination because the two cases put the checker in different positions. In contract enforcement the state stands outside two private parties, so it is not a player and its verdict is anchored by payment or seizure. In personal protection the state is itself a potential party or the author of the rule, so an internal checker reconstructs the very claim it is asked to verify, and the state's written guarantee moves when the state moves. The residual is per slot, and the two conditions form a union with a real intersection, so a lever that fixes commercial enforcement leaves the worker or tenant who is also a victim standing in the second condition. The framework returns the shape (external checker, registered harm, a refusal operation) and not the name of the office. Five refuters were written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10
- **Statement:** the load-bearing difference is the position of the checker relative to the case, not the amount of law on the books.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :43: "Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** show a state's own internal checker returning verdicts against the state at a rate statistically indistinguishable from an external checker under comparable caseloads, or show the per-slot residual always collapsing into the aggregate.
- **What to check:** is the parallel between a contract dispute (state outside) and a coercion case (state potentially inside) faithful to how the offices actually work?

### C2 - law and measurement facing; checkable against the literature
- **Statement:** the personal-protection chain breaks at the objective link, where the commercial chain has an instrumented objective. Adding rights language is accumulation, and a vocabulary without a refusal operation cannot carry a correction of its own state's acts.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Operators table :58 (not a figure-line quote): "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- **What to check:** against North's 1993 Nobel lecture ("Measuring and enforcing agreements in political markets is far more difficult") and the judicial-independence literature, does the instrument gap hold? Is there a rights regime with a published, independently audited complaint rate that still fails at the same level as one without?
- **Refuter:** name such a regime with a genuinely instrumented objective and the same or worse outcomes, or exhibit a legal vocabulary that carries a standing refusal of its own state's act without any external checker.

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12
- **Statement:** the two conditions are a union with a genuine intersection, and the two regimes are the same institutional atoms under a different bond (isomers in the strong reading, a functional group in the cautious one). A lever that helps the contract-only region leaves the intersection standing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- **Refuter:** show the two conditions form a partition with no overlapping cases, or show that the rights case requires an atom with no counterpart in commercial enforcement (which kills the isomer reading and leaves only the functional-group reading).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a particular court, ombudsperson, inspectorate or commission is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that rights on paper are worthless; only that they are a declared meaning until installed somewhere a real outcome can violate them.
- Not that commercial courts solve coercion, or that cross-country indices settle anything. Indices are filters for the budget.
- Not that the two conditions are separate. They are a union with a real intersection.
- Not that the state must always be the coercer; a private coercer can be inside the case too when the checker is captured by the same local power structure.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by measurement, and this derivation cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Checker-position test:** matched internal versus external checker on comparable caseloads.
2. **Refusal test:** a legal vocabulary carrying a standing refusal of the state's own act without an external seat.
3. **Per-slot test:** independently audited coercion complaint rates with a fixed denominator, separated from contract recovery rates.
4. **Habitat test:** an agent-only reform (training alone, no change to complaint channels, press, inspection or standing) that moves the coercion residual.
5. **Conflict rule:** if a world source conflicts with the figure verdict, the census is reopened. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not two.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :42 to :43, Fig 2 :29, Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :56 and Fig 12 :117. Five short passages.
2. Name one case in your own field where the state is inside the dispute and its own checker still returns adverse verdicts at a normal rate. That is the refuter for C1.
3. Say whether the rights regime you know has an instrumented objective, not a declared one. That decides C2.
4. Say whether a worker, tenant or debtor in that state is both a contracting party and a person exposed to coercion. That decides C3.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
