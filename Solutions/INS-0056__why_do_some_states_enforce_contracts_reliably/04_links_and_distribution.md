## What in the wider literature bears on the question

Three findings from fields outside the framework corroborate the figure-derived shape. They are secondary and do not decide it.

1. **Institutional economics (North, 1993).** The distinction between transacting in economic markets and "measuring and enforcing agreements in political markets" is the anchor asymmetry of F2. North's "It is the polity that defines and enforces property rights" names why the state is inside the second case. Corroborates, low overlap with the figures.
2. **Law and political science (rule of law, judicial independence).** The distinction between rule of law and rule by law, where "the law is a mere tool for a government", and the requirement that courts be free of "improper influence from those branches or from private or partisan interests", are the checker-position condition of F3 in the vocabulary of the field. Corroborates, moderate overlap with the general principle but independently derived.
3. **Behavioural and cross-cultural science (third-party punishment).** Norm enforcement is carried by a third party "not directly affected by the transgression", and "victims are more protected if the third-party observer belongs to same group as them". That last finding is a sharp empirical analogue: an internal checker drawn from the coercer's own group is lenient in exactly the way Fig 10 :42 to :43 predicts. Corroborates, low overlap. Note that the same page reports second-party punishment is often stronger than third-party punishment, which is a genuine qualification, not a suppression: the point borrowed here is the position of the checker, not its severity.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. The three framework essays share an author with the figures and carry little independent weight; they are recorded because they define "shape" and the simulation limit, not because they corroborate the verdict.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the differential and the shape claim; (2) the five refuters and the question "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) only after their stage-1 verdict is recorded, the three public links; (4) on request, the formula as a reusable method with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source", and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it.
