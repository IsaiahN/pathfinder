## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :22 and :25-26 (method versus recording), Fig 10 :24-26 (channel decay), Fig 7 :37-39 (two factors) and Fig 11 :49-51 (valence).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, namely: preserve when a method crosses and the habitat pays with a readable channel; corrupt when a recording crosses, the pool is shared, the channel decays or the habitat flips the sign.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete field claim, for example: overimitation is a recording failure; the guppy long-route tradition is a mirror-chain failure; Rogers's result is a no-ground-filter case.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **exact measurement of bracket fidelity**; the shorthand says "grounding travels with it", but the figure gives no instrument that discriminates a method from a replayed recording in a named habitat;
- the **operand type of the refusal**; a negation operator is licensed by the Operators table, but what counts as a refusal in a transmission chain, and who may author it without becoming a participant, is not settled;
- the **independence of the world witnesses**; the cultural-evolution sources share authors and a field pool, so their agreement with the figures is one pool talking, not corroboration.

One thing survives cleanly: a method can cross and a recording cannot do its work, and a chain that hands along the same closure does not add evidence. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no habitat-ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
