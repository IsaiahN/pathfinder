# Expert verification packet: the Tether on social learning and useful knowledge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on cultural transmission or social learning can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No habitat measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Social learning preserves useful knowledge when two conditions hold together: the item that crosses between people is a reapplicable method whose grounding travels with it, and the receiving habitat still pays for that method through a channel the receiver can read. It spreads ineffective or harmful practices when the item crossing is a recording of one occasion, when all receivers draw on a single evidence pool, when the receiver's channel to the payment decays, or when the habitat changes so that a formerly useful practice becomes a malfactor without changing at all. These are two conditions with different instruments, not one lever. The framework does not name which practice wins in a named habitat; that is a declared `gap` with a described but unheld discriminator.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 12

- **Statement:** a generator can cross a transmission boundary; a recording of one occasion cannot do the work of a method.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25-26, *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."* Fig 12 :119, *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **What to check:** does the distinction between a rule and a replay of visible actions actually predict which copied practices survive a habitat change?
- **Refuter:** a practice transmitted as a verbatim replay with no reapplicable rule and no re-derivation that keeps paying across a documented habitat change.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10 and 7

- **Statement:** quick corruption is failure of the channel to the ground, not decay of the ground; persistence requires both bracket fidelity and ground pressure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; :26, *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 7 :37, *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*; :38, *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"*; :39, *"the ground’s pressure on the room"*.
- **What to check:** in your study system, can you measure the two factors separately, and do the failure cases split accordingly?
- **Refuter:** a tradition that becomes harmful while the habitat's payment is unchanged and receiver readings measure fresh, unsaturated and unpooled. If both factors stay high, C2 is not the cause.

### C3 - framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading Figures 7, 2 and 11

- **Statement:** a single-source chain compounds agreement without adding evidence; a practice's valence flips with the residual; a chain without a refusal operator can only accumulate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :12, *"same closure, handed along;"*; :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*; Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Operators table :58, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.
- **What to check:** do the widely copied cases in your data come from independent sources or from one demonstrator? And can you find a practice whose sign changed when the habitat changed?
- **Refuter:** a single-demonstrator chain that produces new, ground-verified discriminations absent in the demonstrator, or a practice whose payoff contribution is invariant across a documented habitat change.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that social learning is good or bad in general. The sign is conditional on C1 and C2.
- Not that fidelity is the defect. Recording fidelity without provenance is a C1 failure; method fidelity with provenance is the success case.
- Not that "culture" is a substance with a valence. It failed the anchor test and is not admitted as a domain.
- Not that any named bias (payoff bias, conformist transmission, copy when uncertain) is the framework's derivation. They are bindings from the world frame, recorded as such.
- Not which practice wins in a named habitat. That is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The five falsifiers are in `01` section 4. The most field-ready instrument already described is in *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"watch the trend in correction size, not the count of lessons given."* A shrinking correction trend is a ground-visible signal that a method is being rebuilt; a flat or growing trend under repeated teaching is evidence of a recording being replayed.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :22 and :25-26, Fig 10 :24 and :26, Fig 7 :37-39 and Fig 11 :49. Six short passages.
2. Name one case from your own study system where the item crossing was a recording rather than a method, and say how you would tell.
3. Say whether your data can separate bracket fidelity from ground pressure, or whether the two are pooled in one "success" measure.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a method from a replayed recording, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
