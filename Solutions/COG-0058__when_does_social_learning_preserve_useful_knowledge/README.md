## Restatement in effect terms

The question is not "is culture good or bad". It is: for a given practice, source and receiving habitat, why does the gap between what the transmitted practice predicts and what the habitat actually returns stay small across handoffs in some cases and grow in others? R is indexed per practice and per habitat, never averaged into a "culture works" score (Fig 1 :19, :22). The answer is bound in advance as a shape: a pair of conditions whose conjunction keeps R small across a chain of transmissions, plus an instrument that distinguishes a failure of one condition from a failure of the other (Formula, "Direction").

## Boundary and honesty

This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output is *"simulacra"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* It cannot settle the question it reports on. Figures are primary and decide; world sources are secondary corroboration only. Figure quotes are cited `Fig N :line` against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reasons, in the figures' words: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention"* (Seat Map :163); *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"* (Fig 10 :42); and *"alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you"* (Fig 2 :35). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the very correlation that makes their agreement worthless. Public opt-in channels also avoid the GDPR/CAN-SPAM failure of unsolicited bulk mail.
