**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** Check one output about social learning before you decide whether the account is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** social learning is a change in how much a belief leans on inherited prior versus private history. Measured per practice and per habitat, its effect does not have one sign. It preserves knowledge when what crosses between people is a reapplicable method whose grounding travels with it, and when the receiving habitat still pays for that method with a channel the receiver can read. It spreads ineffective or harmful practices when what crosses is the recording of one occasion, when every receiver draws on the same source, when the receiver's channel to the payment has decayed, or when the habitat has changed so that a formerly useful practice has flipped sign.
>
> These are two conditions, not one lever. A failure of the first needs a provenance and method test. A failure of the second needs a habitat and channel test. A single intervention that fixes teaching fidelity will clear only the cases where the first condition failed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named tradition is good or bad in itself, that copying is always adaptive, that "culture" is a substance, or that a practice that worked once will keep working. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It says what any candidate answer must be able to explain; it does not say which practice wins in a named habitat. I have marked that question as open rather than filling it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a practice transmitted as a verbatim replay, with no reapplicable rule and no re-derivation, that keeps paying across a documented habitat change;
> 2. a tradition that becomes harmful while the habitat's payment is unchanged and receiver readings measure fresh, unsaturated and unpooled;
> 3. a chain in which all receivers observe the same single demonstrator and yet produce new, ground-verified discriminations the demonstrator never had;
> 4. a practice whose measured contribution to payoff is invariant across a documented change of habitat or residual;
> 5. one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, canonical extract with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula as SVG (public repository): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Cultural Evolution Society: https://www.culturalevolutionsociety.org/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a transmission problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
