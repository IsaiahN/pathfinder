# Expert verification packet: the Tether on habitat corridors

**What this is:** a claim in a form a landscape geneticist, movement ecologist or conservation biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed by this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A corridor changes contact, and its effects arrive in orders. First comes movement. Second an atom (an individual, a pollen grain, a seed, a propagule) crosses into a previously closed population and an interaction or demographic rate can change. Third, where a pathogen, invasive species or fire can use the same path, a contagion can spread with the opposite sign. The same strip is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so its value is relative to the species and the question, not a property it carries. Presence inside the strip does not establish functional connection; a genetic assay or an incidence map is the discriminator. The four conditions (contact, import, interaction change, contagion) are a union, need separate anchors, and must not be blended into one connectivity score. Which design wins in a named landscape is not claimed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal and field-facing; checkable against Fig 11 and the corridor literature

- **Statement:** the three named effects are successive orders of a cascade, not three views of one effect; and a corridor's valence is relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** in a system you know, can gene flow, an interaction and a disturbance be ranked by order of effect, and does the same strip change sign when the focal residual changes? Tewksbury et al. 2002 demonstrates the second-order interaction link; Christie and Knowles 2015 find species interactions can outweigh corridor design; Firth et al. 2025 report pathogen and fire risks.
- **Refuter:** first-order movement counts alone predict third-order pathogen or fire incidence across taxa with no carrier term; or a corridor's measured valence is invariant and positive across a documented change of residual.

### C2: genetics-facing; checkable against a genetic dataset

- **Statement:** connection requires an import into a closure; waiting inside an isolated patch cannot add an atom, and presence or movement alone does not establish functional connection.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*.
- **What to check:** does F_ST or assignment change only when individuals, gametes or propagules actually cross? Horskins, Mather and Wilson 2006 report a corridor with breeding populations of two rodent species but the same significant genetic differentiation as isolated habitats. That is the discriminator working.
- **Refuter:** gene flow or diversity changes with no individual, gamete or propagule crossing a previously closed boundary; or presence and movement alone predict F_ST and recolonization prospectively, per species and per patch.

### C3: framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Fig 9 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** the conditions form a union (not a partition), and over the shared base of patch, corridor and carrier the three effects are an isomer claim with the same atoms arranged differently; as measured with different carriers, a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **Refuter:** one lever restores all four conditions in all failure types with no categorically different residue; or any one effect can be written without the corridor atom or without a crossing (which breaks the isomer claim and leaves only the functional-group reading).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that corridors always help, and not that they always spread disease. Valence is relative to a named residual.
- Not a winning corridor width, species or landscape. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the disease-risk literature is settled. Johnson and Haddad 2011 (foliar fungal pathogen, edge effects) and Firth et al. 2025 (chytrid, African swine fever, landscape scale) concern different slots; both are recorded in `01` F8.
- Not that corridor presence proves functional connection, and not that a single connectivity index witnesses all four conditions.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument per order, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. For this packet:

1. **Contact:** mark-recapture, telemetry or fluorescent pollen and seed marks, per species and patch pair.
2. **Import:** F_ST, heterozygosity, assignment tests, with a before/after design, to show an atom crossed the closure.
3. **Interaction:** fruit set, seed rain, predation or parasite incidence, and effective population size.
4. **Contagion:** mapped incidence and time-to-first-detection, with the pathogen's or fire's carrier biology as an explicit term.
5. **The union test:** an intervention that clears one condition and leaves the others standing, showing the residue is categorically different (Fig 9 :57).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :52 and :49-51, Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32, Fig 9 :57 and Fig 12 :117 and :124. Six short passages.
2. Name one system you know where contact, gene flow and a disturbance have been measured separately. Do the three verdicts agree, or does one fail while the others hold?
3. Name one instrument in your field that distinguishes corridor *use* from functional *connection*. If none exists, say so: that is the gap, not a defect in the question.
4. Try refuter 2 or 3 in `01` §4 against your system. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
