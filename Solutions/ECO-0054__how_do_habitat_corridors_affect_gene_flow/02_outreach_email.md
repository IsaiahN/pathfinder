# Outreach draft: check one shape before you decide about corridors

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are kept separate in the text. A blended "connectivity works" claim collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about corridor effects, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from corridors and gene flow. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree that corridors are good or bad. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the three effects in the question, gene flow, species interactions and disturbance spread, are not three views of one effect. They are successive orders of a cascade. First a corridor changes contact. Second it can import an atom into a previously closed population (an individual, a pollen grain, a seed, a propagule), and it can change an interaction such as pollination or seed dispersal. Third, where a pathogen, an invasive species or a fire can use the same path, the sign of the effect can reverse. The claim is that these need four separate measurements, because a breeding population inside a strip does not by itself establish functional connection, and a movement count does not by itself predict disease or fire.
>
> **What the framework says about the strip itself:** the same corridor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. There is no standing list of good corridors; the value is relative to the species and the question you ask. So "corridor quality" is a relation, not a number the strip carries.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a particular width always wins, that corridors always increase gene flow, or that they always spread disease. No field measurement here pays for those statements. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The four measurements are: a crossing count, a genetic assay (F_ST, assignment), an interaction or demographic rate, and an incidence map for the disturbance of interest.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. A corridor that removes contact without substituting another habitat and without importing any actor.
> 2. A corridor whose measured valence stays positive across a documented change of residual.
> 3. A case where first-order movement counts alone predict third-order pathogen or fire incidence, with no term for the carrier's biology.
> 4. A genetic change with no individual, gamete or propagule crossing a previously closed boundary.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, local canonical set): `FRAMEWORK/figures/` in the Project Pathfinder workspace, with the verbatim text extract at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
