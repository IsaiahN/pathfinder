# The Tether's answer on habitat corridors

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A corridor decision asks what will happen to gene flow, species interactions and disturbance spread. The loop rewrites this as per-slot residual: the gap between what a resistance or least-cost model predicts about connection and what genetic, demographic and incidence data actually return, indexed by species pair, patch pair, interaction and disturbance event (Fig 1 :19). The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). It has four overlapping conditions, each with its own anchor.

## 2. The shape

1. **A corridor is an introduction to the habitat, not the removal of isolation.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Building it substitutes a new habitat and you inherit actors you did not choose (Fig 11 :67). Introduction is available; exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). Hence a managed corridor is *"permanent work rather than a solved state"* (Fig 11 :63).
2. **Connection happens by an import across a closure.** An isolated patch is a closure; waiting cannot add an allele (Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*). What moves the wall is a disperser, pollen grain, seed or propagule from a closure that differs (Fig 6 :23). The genetic assay is the anchor that tells whether an atom actually crossed.
3. **The effects are ordered, not simultaneous.** Contact is first order; gene flow and interaction change are second order; disturbance spread is third order, where the loop can close with the opposite sign (Fig 11 :52; Fig 12 :121: *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*). Pollination and seed dispersal are the experimentally demonstrated second-order link (Tewksbury et al. 2002).
4. **The same strip has different valence for different residuals.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Gene flow for one species, chytrid transmission or fire spread for another. Corridor quality is a relation, not a number the strip carries.
5. **Use is not function.** A breeding population inside the strip does not establish connection (Horskins et al. 2006). Fig 10 :26 names the failure mode: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Presence and movement are filters for where to look, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
6. **The four conditions are a union and keep separate verdicts.** Contact (C1), import (C2), interaction or demographic change (C3), and contagion (C4). Over the shared base of patch, corridor and carrier they are an isomer claim; as measured with different carriers, a functional group (see `06`). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55).
7. **Which design wins is not stated.** The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). No width, species or landscape is named here.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that corridors always help or always harm. The valence is relative to a named residual.
- Not that any particular corridor width is optimal. Christie and Knowles (2015) find width effects in a model; that is a finding in one evidence pool, not a figure-derived name.
- Not that presence in a corridor proves functional connectivity, and not that gene flow is the only anchor.
- Not that the disease-risk literature is settled. Johnson and Haddad (2011) and Firth et al. (2025) are about different slots; both are recorded (see `01` F8).
- Not grounded. No genetic, demographic or incidence measurement was taken by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument per order, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **C1:** does a marked individual, pollen grain or seed actually cross, per species and per patch pair? Mark-recapture, telemetry, fluorescent marks.
- **C2:** does an allele enter the deme? F_ST, heterozygosity, assignment tests. This is the discriminator that Horskins et al. show is missing from presence-only inference.
- **C3:** does fruit set, seed rain, predation rate or effective population size change? Demographic and interaction measurement, not a movement count.
- **C4:** does pathogen, invader or fire incidence change along the strip, and for which pathogen? Incidence mapping, with the carrier's biology as an explicit term.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §4:** a corridor that removes contact without substitution or import; invariant positive valence across a change of residual; first-order movement alone predicting third-order spread; genetic change with no crossing; one lever restoring all four conditions with no residue.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The connectivity frame's own primitives (patch, matrix, corridor, resistance) are a visible guess, in the sense of *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these. The answer stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The gap is not a hidden answer.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say corridors are good or bad. It says a corridor changes contact, and its effects arrive in orders: first movement, then the import of genes, pollen or propagules into a previously closed population and a change in interactions, then, where a pathogen, invader or fire can use the same path, a third-order spread whose sign can reverse the benefit. The same strip is a benefactor to one species and a malfactor to another, so "quality" is a relation to a named residual, not a property of the strip. Presence of animals in a corridor does not establish functional connection; the genetic assay is the discriminator. The four conditions need four anchors and must not be blended into one connectivity score. What is offered is that shape, four measurements, and refuters written in advance. Which design wins in a particular landscape stays open until the ground settles it.
