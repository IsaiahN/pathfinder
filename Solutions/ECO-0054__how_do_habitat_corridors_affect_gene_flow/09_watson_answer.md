# WATSON answer: ECO-0054, habitat corridors and connectivity

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No field measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

How do habitat corridors affect gene flow, species interactions, and the spread of ecological disturbances? Restated in effect terms: why does R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| stay small in some corridor-slots and grow in others, and why does it grow at a different rate for gene flow than for pathogens or fire? R is indexed per species pair, patch pair, interaction and disturbance event, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Option A: treat the three effects as one connectivity question and answer with a single score. Option B: unbundle into four conditions and keep separate verdicts. A delivers a scalar that pools the signal away (Fig 10 :26). B delivers a shape with separate refuters. The anchor that settles either is genetic, demographic and incidence data, named in Fig 2 :29.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale: a four-place relation (patch pair, species or process, action, time), ordered rather than symmetric, with a threshold comparison for "connected" and three nested scales (individual, population, landscape). The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

Union, not partition: D1/D2, D2/D3 and D3/D4 overlap, so the whole is a union of contact, import, interaction change and contagion (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: perceive contact, name what the strip affords to this species, state what would count as progress, plan, then learn and carry (Fig 3 :20, :27). Second-order order follows the cascade: movement, then genes and interactions, then disturbance (Fig 11 :52). Strain test: mapping gene flow to Fig 13's network and fire to gradient only fit by calling alleles energy, so Fig 13 is restricted to the network-as-relation reading and the honest-stop rule (strain S1 in `01`).

## TETHER (primary, verbatim, per domain)

Cited against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Quotes are verbatim from the SVGs.

**D1 Contact and movement, status triangulated (Fig 11 habitat and Fig 4 membrane, low overlap: insertion versus scale filter).**

- Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." -> movement is first order; a direct-contact scope misses the effect that matters.
- Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." -> a corridor substitutes a habitat and imports actors unchosen.
- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." -> a crossing count at one scale is a head start, not a recording of the population-level result.
- Reading: the corridor changes contact, and contact is the first link. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a corridor that removes contact without substituting a habitat or importing any actor.

**D2 Population genetics, status triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13, distinct roles: import, idempotence, closure).**

- Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." -> an isolated patch cannot gain an allele by waiting.
- Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." -> a genetic change requires a carrier crossing.
- Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." -> the import is the atom, not a new kind.
- Reading: connection is an import of an atom into a closure. Mode `general` with a `specified` ecology consequence. Refuter: gene flow changing with no individual, gamete or propagule crossing.

**D3 Species interactions, status triangulated (Fig 11 valence and Fig 12 catalyst/cycle, low overlap: relations versus structures).**

- Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." -> the same strip helps one species and harms another.
- Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." -> corridor quality is a relation, not an intrinsic number.
- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." -> a corridor survives use by pollinators and pathogens alike, and its description records neither sign.
- Reading: valence is relative to the residual; the loop sign is not in the strip's description. Mode `specified`. Refuter: invariant positive valence across a documented change of residual.

**D4 Disturbance spread, status triangulated (Fig 12 cycle and Fig 11 cascade, distinct roles).**

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." -> contagion is a third-order loop whose sign can reverse the benefit.
- Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." -> a fire front or pathogen is a benefactor or malfactor only relative to a named residual.
- Reading: disturbance uses the same contact path with the opposite sign. Mode `specified`. Refuter: movement counts alone predicting third-order spread with no carrier term.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates or conflicts)

- **Landscape ecology, empirical experiment.** Joshua J. Tewksbury, Douglas J. Levey, Nick M. Haddad et al., PNAS 99(20):12923-12926, 2002; full text via PMC, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "corridors not only increase the exchange of animals between patches, but also facilitate two key plant-animal interactions: pollination and seed dispersal". Prediction: second-order interaction effects are real and additive to movement. Corroborates F3 and D3. Overlap: one pool with the other corridor studies, so one witness, not several.
- **Landscape genetics, agent-based model.** Mark R. Christie and L. Lacey Knowles, Evolutionary Applications 8(5):454-463, 2015; full text via PMC, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "The ABM also revealed that species interactions can play a greater role than corridor design in shaping the genetic responses of populations to corridors." Prediction: design alone will not predict genetic outcome; the interaction term can dominate. Corroborates F2 and D3.
- **Landscape genetics, field test of use versus function.** Kerrilee Horskins, Peter B. Mather and John C. Wilson, Landscape Ecology 21:641-655, 2006; abstract read 2026-11-23. Quote: "connectivity between connected populations will not always be achieved by the construction or retention of a corridor and that connectivity cannot be inferred solely from the presence of individuals, or breeding populations, within the corridor." Prediction: presence and movement will sometimes fail to predict F_ST. Corroborates F5 and C2.
- **Population genetics, method review.** Stephanie Manel, Michael K. Schwartz, Gordon Luikart and Pierre Taberlet, Trends in Ecology and Evolution 18(4):189-197, 2003; abstract via US Forest Service public record, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "Understanding the processes and patterns of gene flow and local adaptation requires a detailed knowledge of how landscape characteristics structure populations." Prediction: the genetic assay is the appropriate anchor for the import condition. Corroborates D2.
- **Conservation and disease ecology.** Josh Firth et al., Nature Reviews Biodiversity 2025, as reported by the University of Oxford Department of Biology, news page read 2026-11-23. Quote: "Connectivity often helps populations persist, yet it can also create pathways for pathogens." Prediction: the third-order sign can be negative for specialist pathogens and fire. Corroborates F2 and D4. The linked paper itself was not fetched, so this is one reported source, not two.
- **Conservation practitioner synthesis.** Conservation Corridor, "Corridor Concerns", 2026, read 2026-11-23. Quote: "For the most part, researchers have not encountered negative effects of corridors in conservation. Yet, work is still needed to understand when and where corridors can have negative effects." Prediction: benefits dominate but harms are real in named slots. Corroborates the union reading and the honest stop.
- **Plant pathology, genuine conflict.** Brian L. Johnson and Nick M. Haddad, Ecology 92:1551-1558, 2011; abstract read via search integration and the Conservation Corridor evidence table; publisher page returned 403. Quote: "Our results show that worries over the potentially harmful effects of conservation corridors on disease dynamics are misplaced, and that, in a conservation context, many diseases can be better managed by mitigating edge effects." Prediction: for a facultative foliar pathogen, edge effects rather than connection govern incidence. Conflicts with the Firth et al. pathogen-pathway claim as a single general statement. The conflict was returned to the census (re-census R2 in `01`): different pathogen biology, carrier and scale, so different slots. No verdict flipped.

No other conflict was triggered. The six corroborating sources draw on adjacent ecological fields and share methods and literature, so by Fig 2 :40 ("agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool") they are one witness for the mechanism shape, not six independent confirmations.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: a corridor changes contact, and its effects are ordered (movement, then the import of genes, pollen or propagules and a change in interactions, then disturbance spread whose sign can reverse the benefit). The same strip has valence relative to a named residual. Presence is not function, so a genetic or incidence anchor is required. The four conditions form a union with separate instruments and separate verdicts, never averaged. Which design wins in a named landscape is a declared gap. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. The world pool corroborates the mechanism shape after re-mapping the apparent disease conflict to different slots; it did not decide the verdict.

- **cost if wrong:** a manager applies one lever to a union. Widening every corridor clears the region where contact is the only failing condition and leaves the genetic and contagion intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). The avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a scalar "connectivity works" claim instead of four checkable conditions; Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. No atom is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was struck out (Fig 6 amendment). The shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). A built corridor is less undoable in the physical world: introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61), which is why the pre-registered refuter matters before construction rather than after.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-species genetic assay showing F_ST change with no crossing; a corridor with invariant positive valence across a change of residual; first-order movement alone predicting third-order spread; one lever restoring all four conditions with no categorically different residue; a figure re-census that admits an ecological flow as a seventh substrate member; or a demonstration that the habitat itself settles the argument rather than the genetic and incidence data. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
