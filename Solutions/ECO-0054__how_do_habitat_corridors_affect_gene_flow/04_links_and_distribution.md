## Two things in the wider literature that bear on this question

1. **Use is not function.** Horskins et al. 2006 is the decisive check on any claim that a corridor works because animals were seen in it. The genetic anchor can disagree with the observation. This is the field's own version of Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*: the channel (a movement record) degrades or saturates while the ground (F_ST, assignment) keeps returning its own answer.
2. **The conflict is a union.** Johnson and Haddad 2011 found *"worries over the potentially harmful effects of conservation corridors on disease dynamics are misplaced"* for a foliar fungal pathogen, while Firth et al. 2025 report that connectivity creates pathogen pathways for chytrid fungus and African swine fever. Different pathogens, carriers and scales. Under Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*, so this is not one dispute to be averaged into "corridors are probably fine." It is two slots needing two instruments. The conflict was returned to the census (R2 in `01`), and no verdict was flipped.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded first.
- **Fig 9 :27**: *"describe it before you go looking"*: ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true, before giving them the links.
- **Seat Map :163**: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A list selected for people already inclined to like or dislike corridors manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in their own system; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links above, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** validate the shape against a ground the recipient knows, before showing theory. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the figures. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (Formula, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. The claim as currently written does not assert a universal corridor effect, so it can be tested; but if a recipient shows that the four conditions collapse into one, stop and repair before offering anything else.
