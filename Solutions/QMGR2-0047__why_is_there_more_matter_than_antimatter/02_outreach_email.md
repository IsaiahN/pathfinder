**Subject:** A checkable shape for the matter-antimatter asymmetry, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about baryogenesis. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Running the hot Big Bang with the physics we measure today gives matter and antimatter in near equal amounts. The observable universe instead has a net baryon excess, eta about 6 x 10^-10. Something selected matter over antimatter. The question is what shape any answer to that must have.
>
> **The output (specified, not grounded).** A framework I use has a structural rule: a difference with a sign needs a directed source; a merely conserved, directionless background cannot generate it. From that rule, any answer to the asymmetry must have four parts: (1) refusals of held symmetries, because baryon number, C and CP cannot all be exact; (2) a spendable directedness, which in physics is departure from thermal equilibrium; (3) a carrier that is not in the Standard Model's closure, because the measured CP violation is too small; and (4) the three together as a compound, since each alone is insufficient. This is a shape and a route, not a measurement and not a named mechanism.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming the framework names leptogenesis, Affleck-Dine, electroweak baryogenesis, or any other specific model. It returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming a ground settled any of this. The eta value is measured (CMB and BBN agree), but the carrier is not.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. the excess reproduced in exact thermal equilibrium with baryon number, C and CP all conserved;
> 2. a calculation wholly inside the Standard Model that reproduces eta of order 6 x 10^-10 with no new physics;
> 3. one Sakharov condition alone shown sufficient;
> 4. the CMB and BBN eta determinations shown to share one evidence pool.
>
> If any of these holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README and THE_FORMULA.md (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Background on the open problem (Wikipedia, open page): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baryogenesis
> - Review of candidate mechanisms (Riotto and Trodden, 1999): https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9901362
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]
