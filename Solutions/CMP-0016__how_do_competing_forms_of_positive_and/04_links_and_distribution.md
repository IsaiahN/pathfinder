## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The label problem is real, not a wording quibble.** The Feedback article records that the same example can be called positive or negative depending on how values are referenced, and that systems with several loops "cannot be shoehorned into either type". This is direct world corroboration for Fig 11 :49 and for the union reading in F4. It comes from control theory and terminology history, not from the framework author.
2. **Ashby's limit on decomposition.** The article quotes Ashby: with four parts all affecting each other, twenty circuits can be traced and knowing all twenty does not give complete information about the system. This is why the objective link, not the gain measurements, is the governing break (F6).
3. **Stability is a threshold, not a smooth dial.** May 1972 reports a critical connectance beyond which a random system becomes suddenly unstable. That supports the regime split and warns against a single net-gain parameter.
4. **Arrangement changes regime.** Watts and Strogatz show that changing coupling topology changes propagation and synchronizability, even with the same kind of node. This is the world analogue of Fig 12's claim that the bond arrangement carries information the components do not.
5. **The third regime is contested.** Edge of chaos describes bounded instability between order and disorder, but the article also records that the generality of the idea has been questioned. Use it as an analogue, not as a settled result, and say so.
6. **Negative feedback as stabilization is a biological fact with a moving set point.** Homeostasis shows that effectors counter deviations yet the regulated value need not be constant. This supports the view of stability as an ongoing loop against an anchor, not a frozen state.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. Where sources overlap with each other, the overlap is declared in `10_source_ledger.csv`; agreement from a shared pool is one witness, not two.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Validate the shape claim against a ground. This is Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If curious, read the framework that produced it. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded first; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :35, Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. This is permitted because a method is a generator, not a recording (Fig 4 :22). Each user must name their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question" (Formula, Direction).

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It survives internal consistency checks in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, with one open strain (⋛ operand type) and the per-system regime marked `gap`. It has not been settled at a ground.
