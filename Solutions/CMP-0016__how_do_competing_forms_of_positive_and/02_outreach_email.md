**Subject:** One checkable claim about feedback and pattern fate, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about feedback and pattern formation that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `gap` on any particular system):** a feedback process is a cycle, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. A chain cannot close by construction, so a self-reinforcing or self-correcting pattern needs a loop, not just a gain. "Positive" and "negative" are not kinds of process. They are valences relative to the deviation you choose to track: the same loop can be positive for one residual and negative for another. Growth, stability and collapse are therefore three regimes to be separated, not three values of one dial. Which regime a given system enters is not decided by the framework. It is settled by a measurement of that system.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one net feedback gain determines all three fates; that positive feedback always grows and negative feedback always stabilizes regardless of what is tracked; that a stronger loop on the same components produces a genuinely new kind of pattern; or that the framework picks the regime for any named system. I am not claiming the framework is validated or that it is the only one that could say this.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a cycle that closes with no sign deciding whether it closes, or a chain that closes by construction;
> 2. a loop whose positive or negative valence is invariant under a change of the tracked residual;
> 3. a single monotone feedback parameter that determines growth, stability and collapse without splitting the question into three conditions;
> 4. a new regime manufactured by search inside a fixed set of components and rules, with nothing imported;
> 5. an experiment that varies amplification and damping independently and finds regimes that do not match these three structural readings.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The verbatim figures are the authority, not my summary of them, and I would rather you check the lines than take my word for the reading.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Recording rule

Stage 1 asks for a verdict on the shape claim and its five refuters. Record that verdict **before** any framework material is shown. A later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration, because by then the reader holds its interiors (Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."; Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
