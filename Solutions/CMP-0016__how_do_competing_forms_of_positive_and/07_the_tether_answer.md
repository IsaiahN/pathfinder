# The Tether's answer: how competing feedback determines a pattern's fate

**Mode: `general` on the structural claims, `specified` on their application to feedback regimes, `gap` on which regime any particular system enters.** Not `grounded`. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A pattern is subject to an amplifying and a damping process. We want a rule mapping their competition to growth, stability or collapse, and we want to know what could settle which fate a given system takes. The contact is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Feedback requires a cycle, and its sign governs closure.** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A chain cannot self-correct or self-amplify. The structure and its sign, not the gain magnitude alone, decide whether the relation closes.

2. **Positive and negative are valences, not kinds.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." The same loop can amplify one deviation and damp another. Two loops are competitors only relative to one tracked residual; with different residuals they are not competitors at all.

3. **Growth is a compounding remainder; stability is a correction ranged against an anchor.** Fig 13 :31 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Collapse is the regime in which compounding dominates until the pattern loses the contact that sustained it, or damping removes the deviation that constituted the pattern. These are three structural readings, not three values of one dial.

4. **The three fates must be split before search.** Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." A pattern with both loops is a specific compound with its own ground. There is no single net-feedback parameter that decides all three without losing what is specific to each. May 1972 corroborates from ecology: stability holds up to a critical connectance and then becomes suddenly unstable, a threshold rather than a dial.

5. **A genuinely new regime needs an import, not more search.** Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall." Stronger amplification on the same components produces more of the same pattern, not a new kind of pattern. A new kind requires a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test.

6. **The bond arrangement carries information the components do not.** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two networks with identical actors and strengths but opposite bond signs are isomers and can have different fates. The order matters too: Fig 12 :73 "A then B; B needs A to have happened" for the ordered → bond, against Fig 12 :59 "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." for the symmetric + bond. A symmetric coexistence does not record a closing return path.

7. **The framework does not name the regime for a system.** It returns a shape, not a verdict. Which loop dominates, by how much, and whether the pattern crosses a threshold are settled by measurement. Observing that a pattern grew does not identify the source, because several sources give the same reading (*The Hard Problem is About Source*: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source").

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that positive feedback always grows and negative feedback always stabilizes. Valence is residual-relative (claim 2).
- Not that one net gain determines all three fates. The fates are a union compound (claim 4).
- Not that a stronger loop on the same components creates a new regime (claim 5).
- Not which regime any named system enters. That is `gap` here, not a hidden verdict.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, and no instrument discriminating among sources was supplied.
- Not a claim that the framework is validated or unique. A frame cannot certify its own limit.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating intervention, not a further argument. The corresponding questions are:

- Vary amplification and damping independently while holding the tracked residual fixed. Record which of the three regimes appears. This fires refuter 5 in `01`.
- Measure whether the observed growth comes from a returning cycle or from an external drive producing the same time series. An instrument that reads the pattern alone "cannot comment on the source".
- **Threshold Test:** does a ⋛ relation hold between the amplifying and damping loops at the critical value, or demonstrably not? This also tests the operand-type strain in `06` Q4.
- **Objective Test:** define growth, stability and collapse in the system's own terms before measuring gains. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Standing falsifiers: a cycle closing with no sign; a residual-invariant valence; a single monotone gain covering all three regimes; a new regime manufactured by search in a fixed closure.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is stated, the refuters are written, the union is split, and the one open strain (⋛ operand type at loop level) is named. The per-system regime is not answered because no ground reading exists in this package. The line to use is Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." Agreement among modellers sharing a model is not such a reading (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). The record stops at the limit of the available instruments and keeps the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you that a pattern will grow, stabilize or collapse, and it does not reduce the answer to one net feedback gain. It says that a feedback process is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes, that positive and negative are valences relative to the deviation you track rather than kinds of process, and that growth, stability and collapse are three regimes that have to be separated before you search for one. A bigger amplifier on the same components gives you more of the same pattern, not a new one; a new regime needs a source from outside the closure. It offers the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which regime a particular system enters stays open until a measurement, rather than an agreement, settles it.
