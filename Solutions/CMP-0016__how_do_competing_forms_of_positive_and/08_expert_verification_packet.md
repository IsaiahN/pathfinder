# Expert verification packet: feedback and pattern fate, CMP-0016

**What this is:** a claim in a form a feedback or network-dynamics specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural claims, `specified` on their application to regimes, `gap` on which regime any particular system enters.** No measurement is claimed. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A feedback process is a cycle whose output re-enters as input; the sign of that loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction. Positive and negative are not kinds of process but valences relative to the deviation being tracked, so the same loop can amplify one residual and damp another. Growth is a remainder compounding through an amplifying turn; stability is a correction ranged against an anchor that does not update on the frame; collapse is the loss of the contact that sustained the pattern. These are three regimes that must be split before search, not three values of one net-feedback gain. A genuinely new regime requires an import from a frame whose closure differs, not more search on the same components. The framework returns this shape and its refuters; which regime a given system enters is settled by measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading the figures
- **Statement:** a feedback process must be a cycle, and its sign governs closure. A chain cannot close by construction. An amplifying turn compounds a remainder.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Also Fig 13 :31 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a cycle that closes with no sign deciding whether it closes; or a chain that closes without an output-to-input return; or an amplifying turn that leaves the remainder unchanged.

### C2 — dynamics-facing; checkable by a feedback or network-dynamics researcher
- **Statement:** positive and negative are residual-relative valences, not intrinsic kinds. Two networks with identical actors and strengths but opposite bond signs are isomers and can have different fates.
- **What to check:** is a given loop's sign invariant when you change the quantity you track? If not, the label is relational. Do two signed networks over the same nodes behave differently? The Feedback article records that the same example can be called positive or negative depending on reference, and that multiple loops cannot be shoehorned into either type. Watts and Strogatz 1998 show that changing coupling topology changes propagation and synchronizability without changing the nodes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a loop whose valence is invariant under a change of tracked residual; or two networks with identical atoms and identical bond signs whose fates still differ for reasons the arrangement does not record.

### C3 — method-facing; checkable against modelling practice
- **Statement:** growth, stability and collapse form a union compound that must be split before search; a stronger loop on the same components cannot create a new regime without an import.
- **What to check:** does a single net-feedback parameter actually predict all three regimes, or does each regime need its own instrument? Does a model that raises gain on the same interactions ever produce a genuinely new kind of pattern, rather than more of the same one? May 1972 reports a critical connectance followed by sudden instability, a threshold, not a dial.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall."
- **Refuter:** show one monotone feedback parameter determining all three regimes without splitting the question; or manufacture a new regime by search inside a fixed set of components and rules with no import.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that positive feedback always grows and negative feedback always stabilizes. Valence is residual-relative.
- Not that one net gain determines growth, stability and collapse. The fates are a union compound.
- Not that a stronger loop on the same components makes a new pattern kind.
- Not which regime any named system enters. That is `gap` here.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, and no source-discriminating instrument was supplied.
- Not a validated or unique framework. A frame cannot certify its own limit.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating intervention, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."

1. Vary amplification and damping independently with the tracked residual held fixed; record which regime appears.
2. Measure whether a growth reading comes from a returning cycle or from an external drive that produces the same time series. Distinguishing these needs an instrument, not a model comparison.
3. **Threshold Test:** does a ⋛ relation hold between the two loops at the critical value? The operand type at this level is an open strain, recorded in `01` section 5 and `06` Q4.
4. **Objective Test:** define growth, stability and collapse in the system's own terms before measuring gains. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :121, Fig 11 :49-50 and Fig 9 :5. Three short passages.
2. Check C2 against a system you know: change the tracked residual and see whether the loop's sign changes.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a returning cycle from an external drive producing the same growth curve, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
