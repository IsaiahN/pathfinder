## 0. The claim under audit

> How do competing forms of positive and negative feedback determine whether a pattern grows, stabilizes, or collapses?

Stated as an effect: a pattern is under two opposing processes. One amplifies deviations, the other damps them. We want a rule that maps the competition to one of three fates, and we want to know what could settle which fate a given system actually takes.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run.** Following Formula step 8 ("Direction"), I write down the sought-for shape so it can accrue a residual like anything else:

> **SHAPE CLAIM:** a feedback process is a cycle, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes; positive and negative are valences relative to a residual, not intrinsic kinds; growth, stability and collapse are a union of regimes that must be split rather than searched as one monotone quantity; and which regime obtains in a given system is settled by a ground reading, not by a frame's internal consistency.

This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. If it survives, it constrains what any answer must look like. It does not name a number for any system.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R in effect terms

- **Arity.** The residual is not one quantity. It is at least two feedback processes acting on one pattern, and the outcome has three regimes. Arity: compound, two processes, three possible fates.
- **Symmetry.** Feedback sign is not a symmetric join. Amplification and damping are ordered and directed, and the cycle's sign is a threshold relation rather than a conjunction. Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities" is the operator that decides which process dominates.
- **Scale.** A local loop closes at one scale; its remainder compounds across scales (Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."). So the residual is first order locally and second order at the pattern level.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | verdict boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, framework-internal composition | the six terms, seven bonds, negation, the residual | composition inside `closure(Γ)` then settlement | the figures as metalaws, plus the ground for any measurement | partition: which compositions cross the two kinds |
| D2, the dynamical system being modelled | local interactions, the pattern, amplifying and damping loops | amplification and damping of deviations | a measurement of the pattern over time | union: both loops bind, so a case with both is a compound |
| D3, the network-dynamics research frame | modellers, instruments, datasets, simulation | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | triangulation only where closures differ |

A nameable anchor exists for all three. D2's anchor is a time series or an experiment, not a modeller's preference. If there were no such anchor, D2 would not yet be a domain and I would stop.

### Partition or union

Fig 9 :5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Grow, stabilize and collapse are three regimes with different governing loops, so the question is a **union compound**, not one monotone feedback strength. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." A pattern with both loops is the specific compound, not a harder case of either loop alone.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Perception of the deviation first; terms for what feedback affords second; a statement of what counts as growth, stability or collapse third; an instrument fourth; carrying a settled regime to the next problem fifth. The chain usually breaks at link 3, because the objective has no instrument: Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." For this question the missing instrument is the operational definition of "growth" versus "stability" versus "collapse" in the system's own terms. Effort spent above that break cannot rescue it.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — A feedback process is a cycle, and its sign decides whether it closes (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

This is the governing line. A feedback loop is not a chain with a stronger gain. It is a cycle: output re-enters as input. A chain cannot close by construction, so self-reinforcement and self-correction require a loop, and the sign of that loop governs whether it closes into a stable relation or runs. The sign is a structural property of the bond arrangement, not a property of the components.

Triangulated at low overlap: Fig 12 governs the composition structure (a bond and its sign), while Fig 13 :31 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise" governs what an amplifying turn leaves behind. Different objects, one conclusion: sign matters and amplification compounds the remainder.

**Refuter:** show a cycle that closes with no sign to decide it, or show a chain closing by construction. Either breaks the line. Showing an amplifying process that leaves the remainder unchanged breaks the triangulation.

### F2 — Positive and negative are valences relative to the residual, not intrinsic kinds (`general`, single-source in the figures)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

So "positive feedback" and "negative feedback" are not two species of process. They are labels applied relative to the deviation we choose to track. Change the residual and the same loop changes sign. This is why the question's phrase "competing forms" is a union compound: two loops compete only relative to one and the same residual, and if the residuals differ they are not competitors at all.

Corroboration from a different pool: the Wikipedia Feedback article notes that "even within a single discipline an example of feedback can be called either positive or negative, depending on how values are measured or referenced." That is a separate witness to the relativity claim, not a second figure.

**Refuter:** exhibit a loop whose valence is invariant under a change of tracked residual. That would falsify Fig 11 :49 as applied here.

### F3 — Growth compounds; stability ranges against an anchor (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 13 :31 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

Growth is what an amplifying turn leaves behind when the remainder re-enters and compounds. Stability is not the absence of a loop; it is a damping loop whose correction is ranged against an anchor that does not update on the frame. Collapse is the regime where the compounding dominates until the pattern loses contact with what sustained it, or where the damping loop removes the deviation that constituted the pattern. These are three structural readings, not three strengths on one dial.

**Refuter:** show a compounding loop that does not change the residual, or a correction that is not ranged against a non-updating anchor. Either breaks the distinction.

### F4 — The three fates are a union compound that must be split (`general`, single-source in the figures)

Fig 9 :5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

A single parameter called net feedback gain cannot settle growth, stability and collapse at once without losing what is specific to each. The three regimes need separate loops and separate instruments. A pattern with both an amplifying and a damping loop is a specific compound with its own ground; it is not an intermediate point on a line between the other two.

World corroboration from a different field: Robert May, Nature 1972, found that "large complex systems which are assembled (connected) at random may be expected to be stable up to a certain critical level of connectance, and then, as this increases, to suddenly become unstable." A threshold, not a smooth dial, is what the literature actually reports.

**Refuter:** show one monotone feedback parameter that determines all three regimes in a single model without splitting the question into three conditions. That would break Fig 9's instruction here.

### F5 — A genuinely new regime needs an import, not more search (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall."

Inside a fixed set of interactions and rules, more search visits more of the same reach. It cannot manufacture a new regime. A collapse that introduces a regime the components never held requires an import from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test. This is the structural reason a stronger positive loop on the same components gives more of the same pattern, not a new kind of pattern.

**Refuter:** manufacture a new atom or a new regime by search alone inside a fixed closure. Closure is definitional, so this refuter is unlikely to fire; if it did, the framework's own barrier claim fails.

### F6 — The governing break is the definition of what counts as progress (`specified`, single-source)

Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

For feedback questions the least-instrumented link is the objective: what would count as growth, stability or collapse in this system's own terms? Most effort lands on gains and couplings, where measurements already exist, and the regime label is assumed rather than measured. A reading taken below that break is a reading of nothing.

World corroboration: the Feedback article quotes Ashby that "when the parts rise to even as few as four, if every one affects the other three, then twenty circuits can be traced through them; and knowing the properties of all the twenty circuits does not give complete information about the system." More loop measurements do not supply the missing objective.

**Refuter:** show a system in which all three regimes are operationally defined and measured without any instrument for the objective link. That would falsify the claim that this is where the chain breaks.

### F7 — Which regime obtains is settled at the ground, not by agreement (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

A set of simulations that agree because they share the same model and priors is one witness. The ground is a time series, an intervention, or an experiment that the loops cannot move. This is why the outreach material asks for a refuter before a theory: a checker who has absorbed the framework has joined the frame being checked.

**Refuter:** produce a measurement that independently varies amplification and damping and records the pattern's fate. That fires the refuter in the useful direction: it ends the dispute at the ground and this shape claim becomes either settled or withdrawn.

### F8 — Round-trip gap is nonzero (`specified`, internal consistency)

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Condensing these findings into "positive feedback grows, negative feedback stabilizes" loses three constraints: sign is relative to a residual, the regimes are a union to be split, and the ground decides. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set sets `R_T` to zero and is the dead-reckoning error. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap. See `11_audit.md` section 4.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Fig 9 :60 discipline and the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A cycle closes with no sign deciding whether it closes, or a chain closes by construction (breaks F1, Fig 12 :121).
2. A loop's valence is invariant under a change of the tracked residual (breaks F2, Fig 11 :49).
3. A single monotone feedback parameter determines growth, stability and collapse without splitting the question into three conditions (breaks F4, Fig 9 :5).
4. A new regime is manufactured by search inside a fixed closure without an import (breaks F5, Fig 8 :32).
5. A measurement independently varies amplification and damping and the observed regimes do not match the three structural readings (breaks F3 at the ground).

If any fires, the shape is wrong or needs a new domain census. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Strains, re-census and corrections

- **Strain A, resolved by re-census.** I first tried to read Fig 12 :121 as saying that negative feedback closes (stabilizes) and positive feedback does not (grows or collapses). That forces a dynamical verdict onto a structural line and needs a strain. Re-census: Fig 12 :121 governs whether a cycle closes as a composable term; the dynamical regime is settled at the ground (F7). I withdraw the direct mapping. The line still governs the *shape*: a loop, and its sign, are required.
- **Strain B, resolved by re-census.** I first treated "positive" and "negative" as intrinsic properties of processes. Fig 11 :49-50 contradicts that. Re-census: the labels are valences relative to a tracked residual. The question's "competing forms" is therefore a union, not two fixed kinds.
- **Strain C, not hidden.** Fig 9 :5 says split; the question is phrased as one "how do" question. I keep the split verdict and record the tension rather than smoothing it.
- **Strain D, open.** The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities". At the pattern level the compared operands are loops, not bare quantities. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world" permits the demotion to atom, but whether a loop counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument. Named, not smoothed.

### Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and its refuter, not with a theory of feedback.
2. State the mode of every sentence. The regime of any particular system is `gap` until measured.
3. Split the three regimes before searching; do not look for one gain that explains all three (Fig 9 :5).
4. Define what counts as growth, stability and collapse in the system's own terms before measuring gains. That is the broken link (Fig 3 :27).
5. Use the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, ask for a refutation, and record the verdict before showing any theory (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
