## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can settle a case

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." The stop condition is paradoxical: "the finish line will also be the starting place," because by then "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

For HIS-0057 the shape is therefore **not** a verdict about DDT, tobacco, climate or vaccines. It is a constraint on what any such case would have to look like if the figures govern it:

- agenda change is **per slot** and arrives through **changed contact**, never as one global public-mood scalar;
- proof standards have **two layers**, a negotiable convention layer and a non-negotiable ground layer;
- the honest change operator on the ground layer is a **refusal**, not an accumulation of agreement;
- a controversy is useful when it selects between low-overlap rooms and empty when it chains mirrors.

That is a narrowed form of an answer. It is not a historical finding.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: the obstacle is the searcher's vocabulary, not the size of the library. "Your ignorance, of not knowing what you don't know, is the actual obstacle between you and the answers, not the vastness of the library." Use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap with it.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So the residual was described as "which slots carry live residual" and "what would settle a slot," not as "public pressure" or "political interference," which are borrowed cause-words.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." That is why `02_outreach_email.md` sends the shape and four refuters rather than the architecture.
- *They're Scanning Books*: "Discovery under consequence = a random walk plus a model that can be surprised." The surprise here is a case that violates the two-layer claim, and the pre-registered refuter records what that would look like before the search.

## 3. What licenses a name (and why this question needs no new primitive)

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).

**Correction to my own §1 reading.** The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ. For this question, "object slot" and "convention versus ground" are **already held** distinctions (Fig 1 :19; Fig 10 :45-49). Selecting them is a **binding** from the history-of-science frame onto held terms, not the invention of a new primitive. So "shape, not a name" does not forbid saying "per slot" or "two layers"; it forbids claiming the framework has discovered a seventh kind of social force. What remains forbidden is naming *which* public actor or *which* institution occupies a slot as if the derivation chose it. The earlier, blanket reading is withdrawn and this narrowed one kept.

*They're Scanning Books* is explicit about the status of the held set: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." Declaring that R is per slot and that proof has two layers is the same kind of visible choice, and is auditable for exactly that reason.

## 4. Why public controversy is the wrong thing to put in the anchor slot

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices." And: "Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources." It then names the three real safeguards: independent replication, apprenticeship with two-way correction, and "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move." The last is the closest world analogue to Fig 2's anchor.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "You may all agree and be wrong." And the prestige rule: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A public controversy is a dense carrier of prestige signals. Under this rule they are a **budget** for an inquiry (a legitimate agenda effect, Fig 11 :61-62) and never a **settlement** (F2 of the audit).

*How a Society of Agents Would Work*: "A group is only worth having while its members stay different." "Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times." "The group's agreement is never the verdict." Its four rules for keeping a shared store honest map directly onto this question: votes from outcomes rather than popularity; independent discoveries counted most; things faded so they can be retried; "Bad news travels in the same lane as good news." The last is why a refusal, not an endorsement, is the requested output in the outreach.

## 5. What "alter" can honestly mean

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator. Metamerism: a color instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source." "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument," and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

Applied here: "the public is no longer satisfied with the old standard" is a reading that cannot comment on its source. Three very different sources produce it: a genuine refutation recorded, a convention renegotiated in the open, or an anchor pushed by agreement. They read the same from the outside. What is owed is a discriminator (which layer moved? did the record gain a refusal? can the anchor still be pushed?), not a name. This is the metamerism point from the exemplar's `05`, kept and applied to a new residual.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* supplies the lineage requirement: "often, very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it," so "the lineage has to be recorded rather than reconstructed." "The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect." A historian's account of a controversy must record which route produced a changed standard, because the changed standard alone cannot tell you.

## 6. Honest stop: this is a simulation, not a reading of any controversy

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"; "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *Arrival of the Fittest* records the demo caveat: outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of any historical case, and it cannot settle the question it reports on. Mode: `specified` on the two-part shape, `general` on the structural claim that proof sits upstream of agenda. The four refuters were written before the conclusion, which is the only design protection available against fitting the answer to the method.

## 7. Consequences for outreach

- **Send the shape, not a case verdict.** Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
- **Ask for a failure, not agreement.** *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: "Bad news travels in the same lane as good news." A refuter is more useful than an endorsement.
- **Never pool later agreement as corroboration.** *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "Long, close contact pulls the frames together, so the process that makes communication easy slowly uses up that extra reach."
- **Offer the formula as a method, with a fresh refuter per problem.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Each user must state their own mode and ground.
