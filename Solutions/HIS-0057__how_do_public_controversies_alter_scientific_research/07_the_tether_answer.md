# The Tether's answer: how public controversies alter agendas and standards of proof

**Mode: `specified` on the two-part shape, `general` on the structural dependency claim. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No historical case was settled here.

## 1. The residual it answers

A public controversy is a change in the habitat in contact with R and a change in the frames bearing on R. The question bundles two effects: which object slots carry live residual (agenda), and what counts as settling a slot (proof). Fig 9 :54-58 names a case in two conditions a specific compound, so the two effects are split, then ordered by dependency.

## 2. The shape

1. **Agenda: per slot, through contact.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." A controversy adds actors (funders, regulators, instruments, audiences) to the habitat; those put previously unwatched slots under measurement. The effect is not a global public-mood scalar. Aggregation is the named way the signal about where to look disappears (Fig 1 :28).

2. **Proof: two layers, one immovable.** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 10 :45-46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." A proof standard has a convention layer, which is shared meaning and may legitimately be renegotiated (Fig 2 :41), and a ground layer, which cannot be moved by agreement. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

3. **The honest change operator is a refusal, not an agreement.** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Fig 12 :112 "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Negation is unary (Operators table :70-78), so no number of agreeing voices composes a refutation. Fig 6 :42: a refutation adds a term, the record grows, and the reach falls because something was added.

4. **Proof sits upstream of agenda.** Fig 3 :10 "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"; Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The objective link is third, planning is fourth. So a change in the proof standard propagates down to the agenda, while publicity and funding applied above the break cannot repair an unmeasured objective link (Fig 3 :19). This is why the question's 'and' is a dependency, not a parallel.

5. **The failure modes are named.** Fig 2 :4 "collapse 1: mutual update (coherent drift)" and Fig 2 :6 "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)." When a controversy appears to change a standard of proof, the diagnostic asks whether the anchor moved (it was never an anchor), whether a convention was pushed across the seam, or whether a genuine refusal was recorded. Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not" is the mirror-chain case.

6. **The useful kind of controversy is selection between different rooms.** Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Independence must be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), and the residual must be described before the frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that public pressure settles scientific questions. It changes contact and budget; the ground settles (essay: "Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action").
- Not that consensus is evidence. Fig 2 :40 makes agreement worthless for a verdict when frames share an evidence pool.
- Not that every controversy is manufactured doubt. Shwed and Bearman's "flat" controversies (coffee, vaccines) have public heat without scientific contestation, and their "cyclical" case (tobacco) is real contestation reopened by interested funding. The shapes differ; the framework diagnoses rather than smears.
- Not that this settles any actual historical case. DDT, tobacco, climate and vaccines are world corroboration, not figure derivations.
- Not a claim that any particular actor occupies a slot. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this package.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

The instrument exists in the world literature and is lower-overlap than the essays. Shwed and Bearman (American Sociological Review, 2010) measure community salience in citation networks: modularity falls as consensus forms, and the trajectories are spiral, cyclical or flat. Applied here:

- **Agenda test:** does a controversy change which specific slots carry live residual, with new contact enumerable three steps out (the DDT cascade: spraying, biomagnification, eggshell thinning)? A pure volume shift with no new contact falsifies it.
- **Proof test:** which layer moved? A convention change should be visible as a rule about evidence format or who evaluates quality; a ground change should be visible as a measurement or outcome that now returns a different answer. If the answer to "can the settler still be pushed?" is yes, it was not a ground.
- **Refusal test:** was a term recorded as refused, with the reach falling because something was added? If the standard changed with no new evidence and no refusal, F4 fails.
- **Independence test:** can convergent derivation be separated from adopted import in the record (Fig 8 :50-52)? If not, the common-cause diagnosis is not licensed.
- **Round-trip test (Fig 4 :30):** carry the shape down to a named case and back up; measure what the coarser description dropped. That gap is the honest report, not an error to hide.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: a moved ground, a global pressure scalar with no slot change, funding rescuing an unmeasured objective link, agreement producing a refusal, a subtracted actor, and indistinguishable convergence versus import.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The relevant instruments (citation-network modularity, source-discriminating measurement) exist and return something, so this is not a search for a proto-instrument. But no case was adjudicated here, and the record cannot yet distinguish all convergences from imports. The answer stops at the limit of what these instruments currently distinguish and records the lineage. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (*How Intelligence Actually Works*); a standard that reads the same under three different sources is still a projection until a discriminator is applied.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that public pressure settles science, and it does not pick winners in any real controversy. It says that a controversy changes a research agenda the way a habitat changes: by putting new actors in contact with specific unanswered questions, so the effect is per question and often arrives two or three steps out, never as one global mood. It says that a standard of proof has two layers, a convention that a community may legitimately renegotiate and a ground made of measurements and outcomes that cannot be talked into moving; the failure is to let the first decide an outcome while calling it the second. It says the honest way a proof standard changes is a recorded refutation, which is a refusal and not an accumulation of agreement, and that the proof layer sits upstream of the agenda layer in the dependency chain. A controversy is useful when it selects between genuinely different rooms and empty when it chains mirrors. What this offers is the shape and the falsifiers, not a verdict on DDT, tobacco, climate or vaccines. Those remain for the ground.
