# Union, partition and dependency order in a public controversy

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles two effects: a controversy changes a research agenda and changes a standard of proof. Are these one question, two independent questions, or a union with its own loop? And if they are ordered, which comes first? Getting this wrong decides whether an outreach claim is checkable at all.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (recap from `01`, redone for this derivation)

Atoms held: an object slot with live residual R⁺_s (Fig 1 :19), a ground that does not update (Fig 2 :29-30), a convention about shared meaning (Fig 10 :45-46), a refusal (Fig 5 :53), an actor in contact with the residual (Fig 11 :44). Bonds held: sequence `→`, comparison `⋛`, production `⇒`, conjunction `+`, negation `¬` (unary, not a bond). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is this a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :54-55: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

- Agenda slot and proof layer are **different quantities**: one is where to look, the other is what settles. That is a **partition**.
- But the *controversy mechanism* can enter both at once: new public actors change contact (agenda) while the same actors press on which convention counts (proof). A case in both conditions is a **compound** with its own loop.
- ⇒ Answer: **partition at the level of quantities, union at the level of the controversy mechanism.** Do not search them as one problem and do not pretend the overlap is a harder agenda case.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case can be found in which changing the proof layer leaves every slot's contact unchanged *and* changing contact leaves the proof layer unchanged, the union reading is wrong and the two are independent partitions. That would simplify the shape and should be recorded.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"

Fig 3 :26-27: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." And: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Ordering:

```
habitat contact and vocabulary  →  objective (standard of proof)  →  planning (agenda)  →  carry
```

The **objective** link is Fig 3's third link. Planning is the fourth. So the proof standard is **upstream** of the agenda. A change at the proof link changes what counts as a reason to fund a slot; extra funding or publicity applied at the planning link cannot repair an unmeasured objective link (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not").

This is the structural answer to the question's 'and': the two halves are not parallel effects of a controversy. One is a dependency of the other.

**Falsifier:** a case where agenda shifted first and a proof standard changed only afterwards, with the objective link demonstrably unmeasured throughout and no new contact at the habitat step. That reverses Fig 3's order for this domain and must be reported, not reinterpreted.

### Q3 - Are the two effects isomers, or something weaker?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- **As the residual is currently written**, agenda change uses `{slot, actor, contact}` and proof change uses `{claim, ground, convention}`. They share no atoms in common except "frame." They are **not isomers**; they are two members of a **functional group**, the general pattern `residual → operator → settlement`.
- **The stronger form**: if both are written over the full shared atom set `{slot, ground, convention, refusal, actor, contact}`, differing only in which operator binds which atoms, then they **are** isomers under Fig 12 :117, same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that the same vocabulary can describe both halves and that the difference lies entirely in the bond.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either half requires an atom the other does not (for example, a proof change with no actor in contact, or an agenda change with no live slot), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - What analogues does the question actually need?

Three, each with its own failure mode:

1. **Room chain (Fig 7).** A controversy is a chain of rooms. Useful when the next room differs from the last and matches the residual it surfaced; empty when it is a mirror chain. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." The analogue predicts that a controversy with many agreeing publics and no new room produces motion, not progress.
2. **Habitat cascade (Fig 11).** Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The analogue predicts that agenda effects appear at second or third order, which is why first-order scoping misses them. The figure's own example is a public controversy, so this is the tightest analogue in the set.
3. **Membrane (Fig 4).** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Between the research frame and the public frame only methods may cross. A recording carried across (a slogan, a headline, a copied conclusion) is not knowledge at the new scale. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The analogue supplies the round-trip test used in `11_audit.md`.

The three analogues are not interchangeable. Room chain governs *which* external frame is selected, habitat governs *what* enters contact, membrane governs *what* may cross between scales. Folding them into one "public engagement" term would be the flat-formula failure, "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not" (Fig 12 :14).

### Q5 - What order do the inquiry steps take?

The derive-first order from Fig 9 and the formula:

1. **SEARCHED first.** Is the relevant atom already in the frame and merely unfound? Fig 6 :49-50: "more search at the same reach... Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent." Cheapest, and the other two assume its answer.
2. **INWARD.** Extend the instrument from one already returning something. For standards of proof, this is the modularity instrument of Shwed and Bearman: an existing measurement that returns a contestation level, sharpened rather than invented.
3. **OUTWARD.** Characterise the residual in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. Here: post-normal science, the politicization literature, and the SSK frame are candidate outside frames (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and are never read against one another (Formula step 7). A filter such as relevance or venue prestige decides the budget only; Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a **partition of quantities with a union at the mechanism level**, and the two quantities are **dependency-ordered**: the standard of proof is the objective link upstream of the agenda-planning link. The two effects are not isomers as written (they share almost no atoms) but can be written as isomers over a full shared atom set, with a stated falsifier. Three analogues do distinct work: room chain selects the frame, habitat enumerates contact, membrane governs what crosses scales. The inquiry order is SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, with each kept separate.

This rescues the defensible part of the question's 'and' without merging the two effects: agenda change is a contact cascade at the planning link; proof change is a convention renegotiation or a refusal at the objective link; the latter is upstream of the former.
