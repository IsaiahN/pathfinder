# Expert verification packet - the Tether on public controversy and research standards

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or sociologist of science, a science-policy researcher, or a scientist who has lived through a controversy can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the dependency claim. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a verdict about any historical case.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A public controversy changes a research agenda by changing which specific questions carry live residual, through new actors entering contact with those questions; the effect is per slot and often arrives two or three steps out, never as one global pressure scalar. It changes a standard of proof only in two honest ways: a convention about shared meaning is renegotiated in the open, or a held claim is refused on evidence. The failure mode is letting agreement move the ground, that is, the measurement, experiment, counter, market or patient outcome that is supposed to settle the question. The proof layer is upstream of the agenda layer in the dependency chain, so a change there propagates downward while extra publicity cannot repair an unmeasured proof standard. Useful controversy selects between genuinely different frames; empty controversy chains mirrors.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal and history-facing: agenda change is per slot, through contact

- **Statement:** a controversy moves attention by changing which slots carry live residual, via changed contact and cascades, not by a single global pressure value.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** take a controversy you know. Can you name the specific questions whose residual changed, and the actors that entered contact with them? Or did attention shift as one undifferentiated public mood? Is the effect first-order or three steps out?
- **Refuter:** a well-instrumented case where attention redistributed with no change in any specific question's residual and no new contact, that is, pure volume shift. Also, a showing that Fig 1 :19 and Fig 11 :44 govern the same quantity, which would demote this to single-source.

### C2 - Framework-internal: proof standards have a convention layer and an immovable ground layer

- **Statement:** agreement is legitimate for a convention about shared meaning and disqualifying for a verdict about the ground. If the settler moves when frames push it, it was never the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 10 :45-46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."
- **What to check:** in the case you know, which part of the changed standard was a rule about evidence format, who evaluates quality, or what threshold a panel uses (convention), and which part was a claim about what the measurement or outcome returns (ground)? Was a convention quietly made to decide the outcome?
- **Refuter:** a case where a proof threshold changed, the ground was untouched, no refutation was minted, and the change was neither a convention about shared meaning nor a moved anchor. That falsifies the two-layer claim.

### C3 - Framework-internal: the honest change operator is a refusal, and proof sits upstream of agenda

- **Statement:** a proof standard changes honestly when a term is recorded as wrong and a refusal is added; the record grows while the reach falls. Because the objective link is upstream of planning, this change propagates to the agenda.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 6 :42 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." Fig 3 :10 "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms."
- **What to check:** did the case produce a recorded refusal of a held claim, or only more agreement? Did the proof change precede the agenda change as a dependency, or were they independent? Try to reverse the order and see what breaks.
- **Refuter:** a composition of agreement bonds yielding a refusal without new evidence; or an agenda change that precedes proof change while the objective link is demonstrably unmeasured and no new contact enters the habitat. Either breaks the claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that public pressure settles scientific questions. Prestige and publicity can fund an inquiry; they cannot settle it (essay, *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it").
- Not that consensus is evidence (Fig 2 :40), and not that every controversy is manufactured doubt.
- Not a verdict about Silent Spring, tobacco, climate, vaccines or any other case. Those are world corroboration only.
- Not that the framework names which actor occupies a slot. It returns a shape.
- Not a derivation of one effect from the other in the world. The order claim is structural, from Fig 3.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more agreement. The world source already available is Shwed and Bearman's citation-network modularity, which returns a contestation level and distinguishes spiral, cyclical and flat trajectories. Three concrete tests:

1. **Slot test:** does a controversy change specific slots' residual with enumerable new contact, or only global volume?
2. **Layer test:** which layer moved, convention or ground? Can the settler still be pushed?
3. **Refusal test:** was a term recorded as refused, with the reach falling because something was added?

A case that fails any of these is reported in `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated as if it came from any other source.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read six short passages: Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 3 :10, Fig 5 :53, Fig 10 :45-47, Fig 11 :44 and :52-53.
2. Pick one controversy you know well. Name the slots whose residual changed and the actors that entered contact. Mark whether the effect was first order or three steps out.
3. For that case, separate a changed convention from a moved ground. Ask whether the settler can still be pushed.
4. Say which of C1, C2 or C3 fails, or write the one-line refutation you would expect from a colleague.
5. Send the failure. A refutation is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement expressed after reading this packet is not pooled as corroboration.
