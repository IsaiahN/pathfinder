## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks how a *public* controversy changes two things: (a) a research agenda, meaning which object slots carry live residual and therefore attract work; (b) a standard of proof, meaning what counts as settling a slot.

Stated as an effect, not a cause: a public controversy is a **change in the habitat in contact with R** plus a **change in the set of frames that bear on R**. The residual R is the gap between what the scientific frame's vocabulary predicted would settle the question and what the public sphere now treats as settlement. What is being decided is whether that gap is closed honestly (new contact, new terms, honest refutations) or by moving the anchor (agreement, pressure, prestige).

Three things follow before any figure is chosen:

1. **R is per slot, never aggregate.** "Public pressure" as a single scalar is exactly the averaging the figures forbid.
2. **The two outputs are different quantities.** Agenda is about where to look; proof is about what settles. They need separate loops.
3. **The word "alter" is ambiguous between legitimate and illegitimate change.** The figures give a test for each, so the audit must keep them apart rather than average them.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** two outputs (agenda, proof standard). This is a **union compound** if a case can be in both conditions at once: Fig 9's rule applies, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :54-58). It must be unbundled before search.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Actors can be introduced into the habitat but the habitat cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :61-62). Public pressure travels one way into the research frame more easily than the reverse.
- **Scale:** multi level. The same loop runs at bench, institution, public and policy strata; the membrane between them carries methods up and head starts down, not recordings (Fig 4 :16-25).

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor, then partition or union, then dependency order.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Research agenda** (where to look) | researchers, funders, institutions, journals, publics, media, regulators, litigants | attention, funding, object-slot residuals R⁺_s | yes: the live residual per slot, and the ground that fails to resolve | partition boundary: which slots are live |
| **D2 Standards of proof** (what settles) | the scientific frame, the public frame, measurements, experiments, markets, patient outcomes, courts | what pays; refutations (negation); conventions about shared meaning | yes: the ground, which must not update when frames push it | partition: convention layer vs ground layer |
| **D3 Controversy mechanism** (how the change propagates) | interested groups, common-cause sponsors, mirror chains, extended peer communities, prestige | agreement, influence, credibility | yes: the ground still decides; independence decides whether agreement means anything | union: D1 and D3 overlap when publicity changes funding; D2 and D3 overlap when a convention is mistaken for an anchor |
| **D4 Verification / outreach frame** (us and the checker) | primary seat, second seat, outside expert | internal consistency, residuals | yes: the verbatim figures and the ground, never agreement | one evidence pool in this simulation, so independence is zero |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** All four have one. D3 would fail the anchor test if "public opinion" were treated as the settler: opinion moves when pushed, so it is a frame, not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30).

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 partition cleanly (different quantities). D1+D3 and D2+D3 are unions, so an overlap gets its own loop and is not folded into either.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Habitat contact and vocabulary first (what can be perceived and named); the **objective** link second (what would count as progress here, in its own terms); planning third (which slots get work); learning and carrying last. Critically, the standard of proof is the *objective* link, so it sits **upstream** of the agenda, not downstream of it. Fig 3 :24-27 says this is the link that usually breaks because it has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests. That is the structural reason the two halves of this question cannot be answered together.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A controversy alters the agenda by changing which slots are live, not by adding a global pressure (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

A public controversy enters the agenda exactly where new contact enters: a new audience, a new funder, a new regulator, a new measurement technology puts a previously unwatched slot under an instrument. The effect is **per slot**. "The public is worried about X" is a claim about a slot, not about the whole field. Aggregating controversy into a single public-mood scalar destroys the only signal that says where to look.

Fig 11 :52-53 makes the cascade concrete: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The public controversy over DDT did not just add a voice; it moved the residual three steps out, from spraying to biomagnification to eggshell thinning. That is an agenda change in the figure's own example.

**Triangulated** across Fig 1 :19/:28 (residual bookkeeping) and Fig 11 :44/:52-53 (actor contact cascade). Low overlap: one governs how R is indexed, the other governs how contact propagates. World corroboration: Shwed and Bearman's citation-network instrument distinguishes a *cyclical* controversy (tobacco, where the field was "answered and reopened in different formulations") from a *flat* one (coffee, vaccines) in which public heat exists without scientific contestation; that is a measured per-issue difference, not a global mood.

**Refuter:** a well-measured case in which a public controversy redistributed attention without changing any slot's R⁺ (pure volume shift, no new contact) would collapse the agenda claim into "more of the same search." Fig 6 :49-50 already prices that as budget spent on a swept region. Alternatively, show that Fig 1 :19 and Fig 11 :44 govern the same quantity, which would demote this finding to single-source.

### F2 - The anchor cannot move; a proof standard has a convention layer and a ground layer (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 10 :45-46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

This is the cleanest result in the audit. A standard of proof has two layers:

- **Convention layer.** What format counts as evidence, what threshold a panel uses, how a peer community is constituted. This is shared meaning, and Fig 2 :41 explicitly says agreement is "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Public controversy can legitimately renegotiate this layer (post-normal science's "extended peer community" is a real instance: who evaluates quality, who has the ear of policy).
- **Ground layer.** Whether the claim pays against the measurement, experiment, counter, market or patient outcome. This layer "does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." If a controversy changes *this* layer, the thing doing the settling has stopped being an anchor.

The failure has a name in the world literature: the "Scientific Certainty Argumentation Method," demanding more certainty before action, and Freudenburg's description of politicization as "an attempt to shift the burden of proof." In figure terms, that is a convention being pushed across the seam until it decides an outcome. Fig 2 :43 imports Fig 10 :47 as a guard for exactly this.

**Triangulated** across Fig 2 :29-30 (triangulation geometry) and Fig 10 :45-49 (seat/convention rules). Low overlap: one governs what an anchor is, the other governs what a convention may author.

**Refuter:** produce a case where a proof threshold changed, the ground was untouched, no refutation was minted, and the change was neither a convention about shared meaning nor a moved anchor. That would falsify the two-layer claim. Equally, a re-census showing Fig 2 :41 (agreement efficient for a convention) actually governs the ground layer would break the partition.

### F3 - Two named collapses explain manufactured controversy (single-source on the figure side)

Fig 2 :4: "collapse 1: mutual update (coherent drift)"

Fig 2 :6: "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)"

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not"

Collapse 1 is frames updating on each other instead of the anchor: coherent drift, "which is why nobody notices" (Fig 2 :34). Collapse 2 is two frames that never talk and still agree because they draw on one hidden source: "the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other" (Fig 2 :20-21).

*Merchants of Doubt* is the world case for collapse 2: the same small group of contrarian scientists and funders appeared across tobacco, acid rain, DDT, ozone and climate, with the shared sponsor hidden. The tobacco memo "Doubt is our product... It is also the means of establishing a controversy" is a deliberate manufacture of the appearance of contestation. Shwed and Bearman's modularity measure gives an instrument for the same distinction: public controversy over coffee or vaccines is *flat* (low scientific contestation), while tobacco is *cyclical* (real contestation, reopened by interested funding). Public heat and scientific contestation are different measurements.

**Marked `single-source` on the figure side.** Fig 2 governs both collapses; Fig 7 :13 corroborates but shares the agreement-versus-evidence pool with Fig 2, so its agreement is not a second witness (Fig 2 :40). The *world* sources are genuinely low-overlap with the framework, but two Wikipedia articles that both recount Oreskes and Conway are one pool, counted once.

**Strain, named:** Fig 2 :4/:6 describe two failure modes of a *verdict*; the world literature describes public controversy as a *political* process. They align only if "standards of proof" is read as the objective link of Fig 3, not as the whole political process. That is the re-census, and it is recorded rather than hidden. After re-census, no strain remains.

**Refuter:** a case where the apparent common cause was in fact two independent instruments converging (Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence"). If the record cannot separate adopted import from convergent derivation, the collapse diagnosis is not licensed.

### F4 - The honest way a proof standard changes is a refusal, and that is not an agreement operation (triangulated)

Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"

Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

Fig 6 :42: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."

When controversy genuinely changes a standard of proof, the figure-grounded description is that a held term was refuted: a refusal was added to the record, and everything composed over that term lost its standing. This is why the SST/Silent Spring case matters historically: the attacks on Carson did not settle anything; the President's Science Advisory Committee report of 15 May 1963 largely backed her scientific claims, and the mechanism (biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning) was established over the following decade. The ground moved because evidence accumulated and a refusal was recorded, not because a crowd agreed. Fig 11 :54 cites this exact case.

Negation is unary, not a bond (Operators table :70-78). So a refutation cannot be manufactured by joining more voices: no number of `+` bonds produces a `¬`. This is a barrier result, not a rhetorical preference.

**Triangulated** across Fig 5 :53 (routing of a gap) and Fig 12 :112 / Operators table :70-78 (operator arity), with Fig 6 :42 as the reach consequence. Low overlap.

**Refuter:** exhibit a composition of agreement bonds that yields a refusal of a held term without any new evidence. The figures say this is impossible; a worked counterexample would break F4.

### F5 - Introduction is available, exclusion is not (triangulated)

Fig 11 :61-62: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."

Fig 11 :65 (ecosystem engineering example) and :52-53 (DDT cascade) show the consequence. A public controversy can bring a new actor into contact with a research agenda: funding, litigation, a regulator, a new instrumentation programme. It cannot un-bring the existing contact. So the effect of controversy on agendas is permanently additive in *contact*, even when the funding is later withdrawn. Post-normal science names the same structural move as an "extended peer community," with the honest caveat that such spaces contain "power differentials... in a context of inequalities and conflict."

This is the mechanism behind F1, and it is why agenda changes outlive the controversy that produced them.

**Triangulated** across Fig 11 :61-62 (asymmetry rule) and Fig 11 :52-53/:65 (cascade and engineering examples). The overlap is the same figure, so strictly this is **single-source at the figure level** with an internal example; the world corroboration (extended peer community literature) is a different field. I record it as triangulated across the rule and its worked example, and note that the two share an author pool.

**Refuter:** a managed habitat from which an actor was successfully and permanently subtracted would falsify the asymmetry rule. Fig 11 :66-70 says isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one; a case that distinguishes those would be needed.

### F6 - The proof link is the dependency bottleneck (single-source)

Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Fig 3 :24-26 describes the middle link, between having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, as having "neither" obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts "the least attention and the fewest instruments." A standard of proof is exactly this objective link. Public controversy almost always lands there: what counts as enough evidence, who may certify it, how much uncertainty is tolerable. Because the link is upstream of planning (Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"), a change at the proof link propagates down to the agenda, while extra funding or publicity applied *above* the break cannot rescue it.

This is the structural answer to "why agendas and proof standards move together but are not the same thing." One is downstream of the other.

**Single-source** (Fig 3). No second figure governs the objective link with low overlap. World corroboration from post-normal science (disputes "as often on the process of science - who gets funded, who evaluates quality, who has the ear of policy - as on the facts of science") is a different field and is counted as corroboration only.

**Refuter:** show a case where the agenda changed while the objective link stayed fixed and no new contact entered the habitat. That would be a pure above-the-break change, and Fig 3 :19 says it cannot rescue a broken link; if it did, Fig 3 is wrong about dependency order.

### F7 - Stakes must be declared; prestige funds a trial but cannot settle it (single-source at figure level)

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"

Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A public controversy is dense with prestige signals: famous names, institutional reputations, media credibility. These can buy an investigation (a legitimate effect on agenda via F5) but they cannot settle the standard of proof. When a journal, agency or panel protects its own reputation, Fig 10 :93 disqualifies it as a ground keeper.

**Single-source** (Fig 10). The prestige rule comes from an essay, not a figure, and is recorded as such, not cited as `Fig N :line`.

**Refuter:** a case where prestige alone settled a contested standard and the decision survived later contact with the ground would falsify F7. It would also break Fig 2 :29-30, since a reputation is not a measurement.

### F8 - Useful controversy is selection between low-overlap rooms, not amplification of agreement (triangulated with declared overlap)

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A controversy is epistemically useful when it brings rooms whose closures differ and whose members were selected against the residual the last room surfaced (Fig 7 :36). It is epistemically empty when it chains mirrors: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :13). The operational rule is Fig 9 :27-28: describe the residual in effect terms *before* choosing which public or discipline to listen to, or any frame will seem to fit.

**Triangulated** across Fig 7 :35 (selection between rooms) and Fig 9 :27-28/:60 (residual-first method and filters), with Fig 2 :40 as the shared-pool warning. Fig 7 and Fig 2 overlap on agreement-versus-evidence; I declare the overlap, so this is weaker than F1/F2.

**Refuter:** a case where a chain of agreeing frames (no new room, no new ground contact) genuinely increased a proof standard's reliability would break F8 and Fig 7 :35.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The whole shape in `README.md` is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor test fails.** A public controversy changes a standard of proof by moving the ground itself, where "ground" means a measurement, experiment, counter, market or patient outcome that then changes its answer because frames pushed on it. Under Fig 2 :29-30 that thing was never an anchor, so the two-layer claim collapses.
2. **Per-slot test fails.** A well-instrumented case shows that controversy changed a research agenda by a single global pressure scalar with no change in any slot's live residual and no new contact. That would mean Fig 1 :19 does not govern this domain.
3. **Dependency test fails.** Extra funding or publicity applied above Fig 3's break rescues a broken objective link while that link itself stays unmeasured. That would reverse Fig 3 :19's dependency order.
4. **Refusal test fails.** A composition of agreement bonds produces a genuine refusal of a held term without new evidence, or a case shows a standard of proof raised purely by adding voices. That breaks the unary-negation claim (Fig 12 :112, Operators table :70-78).
5. **Asymmetry test fails.** An actor is permanently subtracted from a habitat rather than the habitat being substituted. That breaks Fig 11 :61-66.
6. **Independence test fails.** The record cannot distinguish convergent derivation from adopted import in the controversy cases, so the collapse diagnosis in F3 cannot be applied (Fig 8 :50-52).

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Enumerate before acting.** For any controversy, list everything in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44). Do this before deciding what to fund or which public to answer.
2. **Keep the layers labelled.** Publish which part of a proof standard is convention (negotiable, Fig 2 :41) and which is ground (not negotiable, Fig 2 :29-30). The failure to label them is what lets a convention decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
3. **Declare stakes.** Researchers, funders and public actors state their interest in the outcome; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59-60). A reputation-protecting ground keeper steps aside (Fig 10 :93).
4. **Treat publicity as contact, not as verdict.** New publics are new actors in the habitat, so they legitimately change agendas (F1, F5). They do not change what pays against the ground (F4).
5. **Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong** (Fig 2 :42). Seek low-overlap rooms selected against the residual (Fig 7 :35); do not chain mirrors.
6. **Lead with the residual and the refuter** in any outreach (Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source"). The figures are the authority, not this summary.

### Six-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is this one question or two?** Two, a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58), because agenda slot and proof standard are different quantities.
2. **Which governs agenda?** Fig 1 :19/:28 plus Fig 11 :44/:52-53, per-slot and contact-based. Aggregation is the named failure.
3. **Which governs proof?** Fig 2 :29-30 plus Fig 10 :45-49, two layers. Agreement is legitimate for convention, fatal for the ground.
4. **What is the dependency order?** Fig 3 :10/:19/:27: objective before planning; the objective link is the unmeasured bottleneck.
5. **What is the legitimate change operator?** Refusal (Fig 5 :53, Fig 12 :112, Fig 6 :42), not aggregation of voices. Negation is unary and cannot be composed from `+`.
6. **What would reverse it?** The six pre-registered tests in section 4; none has been run here.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a controversy changes agendas through per-slot contact and cascades, changes proof standards only through refusal or convention renegotiation, and fails when it moves the anchor or chains mirrors. The proof layer sits upstream of the agenda layer in the dependency chain. The verdict is `specified` and ungrounded, with F1 and F2 triangulated across low-overlap figures, F3, F6 and F7 single-source, and no hidden strain after re-census. It is a shape and a route, not a history of any particular controversy.
