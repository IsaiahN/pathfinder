## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (opened this session): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name its occupant. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is:

> Efficient simulation is a per-slot, per-currency relation between a simulator frame and a physical ground that does not update on what the simulator reports. It is composed inside a declared closure, priced in bits and actions, lossy across scales, and stoppable by one of five conditions.

A universal "yes, any process" or "no, some always resist" is not that shape. It would name an occupant, a universal verdict, before any ground settled it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel* (opened this session): use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, then "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." It is a bootstrapping convergence process. Applied here: start from the words people actually use, "computable", "efficient", "chaotic", "hard", and narrow until the five conditions separate.
- The same essay on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." Also the direct hit on this question: "If the final book in your exhaustive search doesn't answer the question, then there are only two possibilities: the nature of your question was unanswerable, or malformed. Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" That is the union verdict in the essay's own words.
- *The Library of Babel* on the tether: "When traversing through an infinite library, you need something to tether and anchor you to reality, the same way a mountaineer needs a tether when rappelling or climbing." Here the anchor is the physical process, not the simulator's internal consistency.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is why the outreach asks what a failure would look like before sending theory.

## 4. The decisive essay on this exact question

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened this session) contains the framework's most direct statement about simulation:

> "You cannot recreate humanity. The exact lineage of events is lost. You would need to know every natural event and interaction with every other species, all the way back, and even then you would need the exact sequence. The combinatorial possibility exceeds exponential. Even if you had the rules, even if you had a computer that could run the simulation, you would not get the same result without the exact events playing out. At best you will have a facsimile, but it will never be aligned with humanity."

> "This is why world models will not work. You cannot recreate our reality. It is combinatorically impossible. The best you could do is take traces of patterns."

This is the computable-in-principle versus useful-in-practice distinction stated inside the framework, without needing any outside source. It licenses the shape's requirement that a simulation name which feature of the ground it must reproduce. A facsimile can be useful for a statistic and useless for an exact trajectory; which one is wanted is the residual's slot.

The same essay supplies the validator limit: "The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality. Anything else is drift." That is Fig 1 :27 and Fig 10 :42 in prose, and it is why a running simulation does not settle its own accuracy.

## 5. Why the universal question is malformed, not merely hard

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried) gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source". One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered, all give the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

Applied here: a runtime measurement is a reading of one instrument. It "cannot comment on the source" of the difficulty. The same runtime can come from a description that is too large, a settling action that is too expensive, a channel that has decayed, or an underivable property. Asking for one efficiency number is the metamerism error: naming the source from a reading where several sources give the same reading. What is owed is a discriminator, not a number.

*The Library of Babel* (opened this session) says the same thing in its own vocabulary: an exhaustive search that fails means the question was "unanswerable, or malformed", and often "a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one".

## 6. Correction to my own reading: "shape, not a name" governs missing primitives

**I overstated this earlier and the correction is kept visible.** The canonical phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Classical and quantum simulators, precision and instruments are all already available options in the physics and engineering frames. Choosing among them is a binding from those frames onto held atoms, provenance-bearing and refutable. It is not the invention of a seventh primitive, and it is not forbidden by the shape rule.

What remains wrong in a naive universal answer is not that it names an option but that it names **one** option as the settled answer without a ground reading and without provenance. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost. Compare the same correction in the exemplar's `05` section 5b and `06` Q1.

A second correction, specific to this question: the essay's "world models will not work" must not be inflated into "no simulation is ever useful". The exact claim is that exact recomputation of a full lineage is combinatorically impossible, so the best obtainable object is a trace of patterns. That is precisely the trajectory-versus-statistics distinction that Corless 2025 establishes numerically. The full claim and the narrowed claim are both kept.

## 7. Consequences for verification and outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened this session): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly. ... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous simulation experts who agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the physical process settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too ... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

**What would settle it.** A source-discriminating instrument: an experiment that tells which of the five conditions is doing the work in a claimed simulation failure, plus a ground that does not update on the simulator's report. Agreement among simulators sharing an evidence pool is one witness, not five (Fig 2 :40).
