## The residual in effect terms

`R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` is measured per object slot, against a ground that does not update on what the simulator thinks. The question asks whether, for a given physical process P, a simulator frame S can keep its prediction paying at the ground within a budget. That is a relation between a simulator, a process, a slot and a budget, not a property of an algorithm in isolation. "Computable in principle" is a reachability question. "Efficiently" is a question in two currencies, bits for the description and actions for finding out whether it holds. "Resists useful simulation" is a union of distinct failures: a closed set that cannot add an atom, a budget that cannot pay for precision, a channel that has decayed, and a property that is underivable in principle. What an answer would look like is a per-slot, per-currency verdict with its own refuter and its own anchor, not a universal percentage or a named winning algorithm. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons: Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."; Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; and Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A universe-wins-or-loses campaign would manufacture the disqualifying correlation and would spend the independence of exactly the readers whose check is wanted.

Legally: public opt-in and institutional channels avoid the practical failure of unsolicited bulk outreach as well as the structural one.
