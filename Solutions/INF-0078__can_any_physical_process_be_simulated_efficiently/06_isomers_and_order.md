## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are a classical and a quantum simulator isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

If the two simulators are written over a shared atom set (the same target Hamiltonian, the same observable slot, the same ground), and differ only in which substrate carries the state, then by Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms under a different arrangement, different substance, decisive in behaviour. This is the strong form and it is a checkable structural claim.

If instead the classical simulator's closure does not contain the qubit or entanglement at all, then the quantum simulator is not a different arrangement of the same atoms. It is an **import** of a new atom from a physics frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :51). The gain is a moved wall, not a faster run of the same program.

Both readings are licensed depending on how the atom set is declared. What is **not** licensed is calling the gain "optimisation" while silently importing the substrate: Fig 8 :51 says convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, and only the record separates them. The provenance is the whole issue.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a single primitive set containing both classical and quantum simulation with no substrate imported, and show the performance difference is produced solely by a different bond arrangement with a recorded lineage. If that succeeds, the import reading fails and the isomer reading stands alone. If it fails, the import reading is the one that survives.

### Q2: What is the order of the five conditions?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The dependency order is:

1. **Describe the residual and its slot** (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"). Without a slot, "hard to simulate" is unreadable.
2. **Reachability** (D1). Ask SEARCHED first: is the simulator already in the closure and merely unfound? Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."
3. **Price both currencies** (D2, D3). Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." A budget in one currency cannot rescue a shortfall in the other.
4. **Check underivability** (D5). Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions." This is asked after SEARCHED, because a budget answer is wrong if the property is underivable.
5. **Let the ground settle** (D4). `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."

The order is not a ranking of importance. A cheaper algorithm cannot rescue a missing anchor, and a larger budget cannot rescue an undecidable question.

**Falsifier:** show a case where pricing the currencies changes the reachability verdict, or where a decision procedure changes which slot is being simulated. That would collapse the dependency order and require a re-census.

### Q3: Does the framework owe a refusal before minting "efficient"?

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes a residual into TRANSFERRED, NOVEL, BROKEN with rebinding, BROKEN with a mint owed, or REFUTED. Fig 5 :53 names the fifth kind: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". The common term "efficient simulation" is bound and predicting, so it is an enumerable candidate for refusal (Fig 5 :58-60). Its residual persists and does not trend down across the five domains, which is exactly the ranking signal the pipeline uses to point a refusal.

The refusal owed is not a new theory of efficiency. It is a negation: the claim that efficiency is a single property of an algorithm is refused. Fig 12 :112 says "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Negation acts on one operand and joins nothing; it does not enter the bond price.

**Falsifier:** show a single scalar quantity, measured by one instrument, that ranks simulation difficulty identically across reachability, description cost, settling cost, channel decay and underivability, and that predicts an intervention in each. That would make the refusal unnecessary.

### Q4: Is a shadow orbit an answer or a different object?

- Corless 2025: "Every computed orbit is shadowed by a true reference orbit, for all time." And: "every single one of those computed orbits is periodic because the set of floating-point numbers is finite. That means that the statistics of the orbits must be wrong, and cannot give the correct probabilities, because almost all true orbits are aperiodic and dense in the interval [0,1)."
- Fig 13 :32: "comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."

A shadow orbit is a **different object** from the exact trajectory. The numerical solution is the exact solution of a nearby problem, with a persistent disturbance εv(t). That is a successful simulation of the perturbed system and a failed simulation of the exact one, depending on which slot is being tracked. The framework has a name for this: the transformed problem is a composition with a different operand, not a recording of the original. Refusing to distinguish them is the flat-formula collapse of Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

**Falsifier:** show that the shadow orbit and the exact reference orbit are the same term under the declared code, with identical statistics on every observable slot and no discarded detail. Corless's Gauss map example is the standing counterexample against that claim.

## CONCLUSION

Over a shared atom set, a classical and a quantum simulator are isomers; where the classical closure lacks the quantum atoms, the relation is an import, and provenance decides which. The five conditions are ordered by dependency, not importance, and SEARCHED is asked first. The common phrase "efficient simulation" owes a refusal before any new concept is minted, because its residual persists across domains. A shadow orbit is a different object from the exact trajectory, and conflating them is the flat-formula error. The fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim is a **live strain**, named here rather than hidden: it depends on how the atom set is declared, and no instrument in this folder settles the declaration.
