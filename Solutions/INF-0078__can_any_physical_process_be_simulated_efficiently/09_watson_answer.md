## QUESTION

Can any physical process be simulated efficiently, or do some systems resist useful simulation even when they are computable in principle?

Restated as an effect: for a simulator frame Γ_S, a physical process P, an object slot s and a budget B, `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` is indexed per slot against a ground that does not update on what the simulator reports (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Option A: efficiency is a property of an algorithm and a universal yes or no is findable. Option B: the question is a union of five conditions with different anchors, the universal yes/no is a `gap`, and what survives is a per-slot, per-currency shape with seven refuters. A leads to one ruler applied to every failure and to mistaking budget facts for underivability; B leads to discriminators and instruments.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The universal yes/no is a `gap`; the shape survives. Computability is closure membership, and search in a fixed closure adds no atom. Efficiency is two currencies that do not add, so a single runtime cannot be the verdict. A simulation is a substituted habitat: it proves wiring and never capability until its prediction is checked against a ground the simulator did not author. Chaos is an amplifying remainder, so exact trajectory tracking is budget-limited while attractor statistics can be well-conditioned; the object that resists must be named. Underivable properties are not budget problems, and a substrate change is an import with provenance, not an optimisation. There is no last simulator, so neither universal side closes. Verdicts are per domain, triangulated or single-source as marked, and never averaged. Mode `specified` on domains, `general` on shape, `gap` on universal; ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** misallocated effort and a broken checking channel. A team would buy a bigger computer for an underivable question, import a new physical assumption for a precision problem, or accept a model's self-report as validation. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible if the five conditions are separated before the budget is committed. The irreversible part is spending the independence of an outside reader by sending a recording instead of a generator.
- **undoable:** yes. Nothing is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than deleted (Fig 6). The shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded with provenance, so a later reader can locate the error. A published efficiency claim can be superseded by a corrected per-slot claim without withdrawing any primitive or any substrate.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the seven pre-registered refuters would reverse the part it touches: a simulator paying at an unauthored ground with no channel; self-verification accepted from the producing record; a new primitive from fixed-set composition; bounded-precision exact tracking over unbounded time; a uniform decision procedure for the general spectral gap problem; one term in both currencies for two simulators; or an exactly reversible cross-scale transform. A genuinely different evidence pool establishing a single scalar efficiency measure across all five conditions would also reverse it. Until one fires, the per-domain verdicts stand as `candidate` and the universal question stands as `gap`.
