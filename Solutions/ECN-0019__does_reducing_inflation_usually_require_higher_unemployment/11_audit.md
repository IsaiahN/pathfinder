## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before using the primary's findings. The residual line I worked from: the fixed-coefficient prediction for d(u)/d(pi) versus an episode record in which similar inflation reductions carried very different unemployment costs, with the question of what policy could avoid the cost.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two surface quantities, but the observed variance requires at least one hidden third variable, the regime, so the surface arity is a projection of a higher-arity object. Symmetry: ordered comparison, not a symmetric join; which quantity is set and which responds matters. Scale: one loop per episode (first order) and a second loop in which the regime itself is selected (second order). This is a **union** at both scales, not one hard question.
- **Domain A, the controller.** Actors: central bank, target, policy rate. Currency: a comparison of inflation and employment outcomes. Anchor: a fixed reference that does not move when the responding field moves. Question type: partition boundary, what the controller may set.
- **Domain B, the responding field.** Actors: firms, workers, wage setters, indexation conventions. Currency: expected inflation in nominal contracts. Anchor: a commitment that cannot be revised by the outcome. Separate frame, low overlap with A.
- **Domain C, the instrument.** Actors: price and unemployment statistics, survey and market expectations. Currency: readings. Anchor: the data. A channel exists and can decay; its failure is not the absence of a ground.
- **Domain D, the compound.** Actors: expectations, rigidity, shocks, labour-market structure. Currency: which condition binds. Anchor: identified per episode. Union.

Order by dependency: reference first, then the responding field, then the coefficient, then the measurement. A coefficient estimated before the reference is fixed consumes an input that does not exist yet.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :29, :33, :34, Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 6 :21, :23.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: the cost is indexed per slot, the anchor must not update, the compound is a union, the regime change is an import, capability is agent-and-habitat.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** an outreach reader's natural reconstruction: "the Tether says there is a specific sacrifice ratio, a specific optimal target, and a specific institution that delivers cheap disinflation."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four constraints that the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the ban on naming a magnitude, which the reader converts into a number the framework never supplied;
- the requirement that an import carry provenance and a shadow test, which the reader drops when the regime change becomes "policy";
- the level-relativity of import and irreducibility (Fig 12 :104), which the reader reads as a new atom in the world;
- the agent-and-habitat condition, which the reader collapses into a better central bank.

One thing survives cleanly: a fixed reference that does not update on the responding field, with the residual indexed per slot and the union unbundled. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists in this derivation; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
