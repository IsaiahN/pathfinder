# The Tether's answer: does reducing inflation usually require higher unemployment?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural constraint, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No measurement was run.

## 1. The residual it answers

A claimed stable cost coefficient between disinflation and unemployment is compared with an episode record in which the same inflation reduction carried very different output costs. The residual is indexed per regime, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19). The question as asked bundles four conditions into one word, "usually", and the figures require the bundle to be split (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

**D1, the policy loop.** The nominal target is the anchor: Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." and Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." If the target is revised in response to what firms and workers expect, or traded for lower unemployment, it becomes another frame and the system drifts coherently: higher inflation, no lasting employment gain (Fig 2 :34). If it is pinned, expectations range against it and the same disinflation costs less. Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" makes this checkable in the record. **Verdict for D1: `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 10).** The "exploit the tradeoff" option is refused; the "pin the anchor" option survives.

**D2, the expectations frame.** Within one policy closure, a better rule is a mint and "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." (Fig 6 :21). What moves the wall is contact with a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23): the expectations regime, indexation rules, the institutional mandate, market competition. These are imports into the policy frame and need provenance and a shadow test. **Verdict for D2: `triangulated` (Fig 6 and Fig 8).** "Better policy" is a regime change with a lineage, not a parameter tune.

**D3, the ground.** The cost must be read per episode against measured inflation and unemployment. Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; the failure mode is a saturated or pooled channel, not an absent ground. **Verdict for D3: `triangulated` for the rule (Fig 1 and Fig 10), `gap` at the numeric level.** The figures supply no coefficient.

**D4, the compound.** It is a union: Fig 9 :55 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Higher unemployment is that single lever. It is effective where excess demand is the only binding condition and ineffective, or singly costly, in the intersections. **Verdict for D4: `triangulated` (Fig 9 and Fig 8).**

The four verdicts are kept separate and are never averaged into a yes or no.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that disinflation is costless. The rational-expectations "costless disinflation" result is a finding of an economics frame, secondary corroboration at best, and is not adopted as a figure verdict.
- Not that there is no short-run tradeoff. Fig 12 :113 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." The target is a comparison; the cost is a subtraction. Both are real; their magnitudes are empirical.
- Not which target, institution or regime is best. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a number. No sacrifice ratio, NAIRU value or cross-country coefficient is asserted.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A measurement indexed per slot, not an argument:

1. **Slot indexation.** For each episode, record whether the anchor was pinned, which condition was binding, and the labour and product market setting. Report d(u)/d(pi) per slot. A single dataset-wide coefficient is the aggregation error Fig 1 names.
2. **Anchor test.** Compare the path of survey or market expected inflation before and after a credible, pre-committed target change. If expectations fall faster and the output cost is smaller than under a discretionary episode, F2 is corroborated; if not, it is refuted.
3. **Unbundling test.** Separate demand, expectations and supply components and check whether the effective instrument changes across the intersections. If it does not, F3 fails.
4. **Instrument check.** Confirm that the expectations series was already returning a reading before the model used it (Fig 6 :33), and that the channel was not pooled or saturated (Fig 10 :25-27).

## 5. The honest stop

**Reachable gap, not unreachable.** The data exists; the measurement has not been run here. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The limit is the limit of this derivation, not of the instruments. The framework returns the shape any measured answer must satisfy: indexed per slot, pinned anchor, unbundled compound, imported regime, habitat-aware. The number is owed to the ground. Until then the verdict is `candidate`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "usually" with a number, because its first figure says the residual is indexed by object slot and that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears. The cost of disinflation is indexed by the expectations regime, by whether the shock is demand or supply, and by labour and product market structure. The strongest figure-grounded point is about the anchor: a nominal target that moves when expectations move, or is traded for lower unemployment, has stopped being an anchor, and the framework predicts coherent drift, higher inflation with no lasting employment gain. A target that is pinned lets expectations come to it and lowers the cost. The compound is a union, so higher unemployment as a universal lever works only where demand is the sole binding condition and leaves the intersections standing. Regime change moves the wall only as a provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs. What is offered is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the measurement that would close it. The magnitude stays open until the ground settles it.
