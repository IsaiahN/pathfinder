# Solution ECN-0019: Tether figure-grounded package

**Question:** Does reducing inflation usually require higher unemployment, or can better policy avoid much of that cost?

**Question, restated as an effect.** Two options are offered. Option A: the tradeoff is a structural wall, so every point of disinflation must be paid for in unemployment, on average, usually. Option B: the tradeoff is conditional on the expectations regime and on which condition is binding, so a different policy frame can move the wall and avoid much of the cost. An answer would look like a statement of which quantities the cost is indexed by, which anchor must hold, and what a measurement would have to show to settle it. This is bound as an ordinary term before the run, per the formula's "Direction" rule.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Gathering a room of agreeing economists cannot settle a question about the world.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A list ranked by willingness to believe the answer is a list selected to agree.
3. **Sending the theory before the verdict spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. Use the public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, one to one.
