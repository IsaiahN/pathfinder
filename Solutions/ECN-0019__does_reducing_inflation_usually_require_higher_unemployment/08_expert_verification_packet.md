# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the cost of disinflation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural constraint, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a number.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does reducing inflation usually require higher unemployment" is malformed as a request for one coefficient. The cost is indexed by regime slots, and aggregating across them discards the signal. The figure-governed shape is: the nominal target must be an anchor that does not update on private expectations or on an employment outcome, or the system drifts coherently to higher inflation with no lasting employment gain; the cost compound is a union of expectations, nominal rigidity, supply shocks and labour-market structure, so a single leverage of unemployment clears only the demand-only region; moving the wall requires a provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs, not a better setting inside the same frame; and capability is agent-and-habitat, so a better controller alone changes nothing. The figures return this shape, not the magnitude. The magnitude is owed to measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus empirically checkable; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** the cost must be indexed per slot, and the anchor must not update. A target revised to hit an employment outcome is no longer an anchor.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
- **What to check:** is the inflation target in a given episode a fixed reference that expectations range against, or was it adjusted in response to unemployment and expected inflation? Does d(u)/d(pi) differ materially across the four slots?
- **Refuter:** one stable cost coefficient across all slots with no interaction by regime, or a pinned credible target followed by no faster fall in expected inflation and no smaller output cost than a discretionary regime.

### C2 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 8

- **Statement:** the cost is a union compound, not a partition, so higher unemployment is a single lever that leaves the intersections standing. Unbundle before choosing an instrument.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- **What to check:** do the expectations, supply-shock and labour-market conditions interact with the demand condition in the episode record, or do they behave as separate additive cases?
- **Refuter:** a partition result, with one coefficient per condition and no interaction, would kill this sub-claim.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 6, 11 and 8

- **Statement:** within a policy closure a better rule cannot add an atom; moving the wall requires an import from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and shadow test. A better controller in isolation does not change contact, so it changes nothing that matters.
- **Sources, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** did the episodes with a lower cost involve a change of regime, institution or market structure with a traceable provenance, rather than a better-calibrated rule inside the same regime? Can a shadow test be stated, that is, did the change explain a gap it was not built for?
- **Refuter:** a regime change with clear provenance and a stated shadow test that fails to reduce the measured cost, or an agent-only improvement that moves the ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that disinflation is costless. That is a model-frame result, not a figure verdict.
- Not that there is no short-run tradeoff, and not a value for any sacrifice ratio or NAIRU.
- Not that a particular target number, framework or institution is the Tether's answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No episode measurement was run for this packet.
- Not a claim that "agreement among experts" settles any of this. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

## 5. What would settle it

A measurement indexed per slot: for each episode, record whether the anchor was pinned, which condition was binding, and the labour and product market setting; then report d(u)/d(pi) per slot and test the three refuters above. A source-discriminating instrument for expectations is already available in survey and market measures; the failure mode is a pooled or saturated channel, not an absent one (Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does").

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 2 :29, :33, :34 and Fig 10 :47, six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one episode you know: was the target pinned, and did expectations fall to it?
3. Check C2: do the conditions interact in the data, or are they additive?
4. Check C3: did the lower-cost episodes involve a regime change with a visible provenance?
5. Name one measurement that would refute C1. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
