# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note for a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter are stated in your own words. The output must survive the first check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output about the cost of disinflation before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Does reducing inflation usually require higher unemployment, or can better policy avoid much of the cost? The word "usually" asks for one number across episodes. The output says that is the wrong form of the question.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The framework's first figure states that the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated, and that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. Applied here: the cost of disinflation is indexed by the expectations regime, by whether the shock is demand or supply, and by labour and product market structure. There is no single coefficient "usually". Where a single number is quoted, the slot information is already lost.
>
> **The second output, the one most worth checking.** The framework's second figure says an anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks, and that the anchor must not update. Applied here: if the nominal target is moved in response to what wage setters and firms expect, or traded for lower unemployment, the target stops being an anchor. The figure predicts the two frames then drift together, with higher inflation and no lasting employment gain. If the target is pinned, expectations range against it and the same disinflation costs less. The framework also has a rule that a convention which decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. A target revised to hit an employment outcome is no longer an anchor.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that disinflation is costless. Not that any particular target number or institution is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name: it says which quantities the cost is indexed by and which anchor must hold, not what the sacrifice ratio is and not which regime is best. Real-world results about credibility and central bank independence are secondary corroboration from another frame, not derivations from this one.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a cross-episode dataset showing one stable cost coefficient across all four slots, with no interaction by expectations regime or shock type;
> 2. a pinned, credible target followed by no faster fall in expected inflation and no smaller output cost than a discretionary regime;
> 3. unbundling demand, expectations and supply conditions that does not change which instrument is effective.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The Lucas critique, for the historical-correlation failure this bears on: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_critique
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]
