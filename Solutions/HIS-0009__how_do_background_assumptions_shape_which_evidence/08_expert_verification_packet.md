## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Background assumptions are not a filter applied to evidence after arrival; they are terms in the prediction `Γ(b,a)`, where `b` is a belief weighted between private history and inherited prior and `Γ` is the library of what can be expressed. Because the residual `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` is indexed per object slot, a datum is noticed only where the slot carries positive residual; where the prediction already explains the slot, the gap is "held" and nothing is owed, and where no slot exists the datum cannot be read at all. Whether a noticed datum survives into a record is a channel property, since the ground does not decay while the channel does. At community scale, agreement drawn from one shared evidence pool is a convention rather than corroboration, and a datum with no category in that pool is readily disregarded as artifact. The same recorded items under different assumptions can be isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Per-slot residual governs noticing (framework-internal plus slot-level history)

- **Statement:** noticing is positive live mass at a tracked slot; a held slot or an untracked one is not noticed as evidence. The assumption acts through the prediction, not after it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table, `R⁺_s`: "the part of the residual on slot s that is positive: something happened there the prediction missed." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 5 :11: "held: the model was right, nothing owed". Fig 6 :34-35: "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."
- **What to check:** in a case you know, can you identify which slots the researcher tracked, and did the datum said to be "missed" have a slot? If no slot existed, this is the no-tracker branch; if it had a slot and was sorted as explained, it is the held branch. These predict different documentary traces.
- **Refuter:** matched `b`, `w`, `Γ` and `o'` with systematically divergent noticing at the same tracked slot; or a positive live mass at a tracked slot, with an intact channel, that is still not noticed.

### C2 - Recording is a channel property (framework-internal plus instrument history)

- **Statement:** once noticed, survival into a durable report depends on the channel, not on the ground. Saturated metrics and pooled readings produce "you have lost touch", not a verdict about the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." Fig 2 :37-38: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 6 :33: "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."
- **What to check:** do raw instrument logs contain items absent from the published tables? If yes, the difference is a channel effect. Was the sharper instrument built on a residual the older one already returned, rather than deduced from a description?
- **Refuter:** a channel demonstrably saturated on an axis the ground still varies along, yet returning a record the ground pays; or an instrument specified from a pure description with no prior reading.

### C3 - A shared evidence pool makes agreement a convention, not corroboration (community scale)

- **Statement:** researchers who draw on one evidence pool can agree without talking; that agreement is good for shared meaning and worthless as evidence. A datum with no category in the pool is easily labelled artifact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :18-21: "one evidence pool / they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** in a episode of apparent consensus, did the agreeing parties draw on independent contact or on the same reports, instruments, training and funding source? If the latter, the agreement is the pool talking. Check whether the datum later treated as decisive was available to all of them before the consensus formed.
- **Refuter:** a chain of same-closure rooms that increases evidence rather than only agreement; or a standing catalogue of evidence types whose valence survives every change of residual (this would break the single-source Fig 11 sub-claim specifically).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement.

1. **Slot test.** Identify the object slots tracked in a case and check whether the allegedly missed datum had a slot. Documentary trace: protocols, instrument logs, notebook headings.
2. **Channel test.** Compare raw records with published reports to separate "not noticed" from "noticed and dropped".
3. **Pool test.** Establish whether consensus drew on independent contact or a shared source. Lines that never talked but agree point to the pool.
4. **Discriminator test.** A blind manipulation of the kind Chadwick used on scintillation readings: change a condition unbeknownst to the observer and see whether the reports track the change. This is the spectrometer move, not an argument.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 10 :24-28, Fig 2 :18-21 and :40, and Fig 11 :49. Five short passages.
2. Pick one historical or experimental case you know well. Name the slots that were tracked and say whether the disputed datum had one.
3. Say whether the record of that datum survived a channel, and whether the agreeing parties shared a pool.
4. Name one instrument or documentary check that would discriminate between "held", "no slot" and "shared pool" in that case, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
