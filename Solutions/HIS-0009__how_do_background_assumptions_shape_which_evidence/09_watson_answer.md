## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how a background assumption changes which evidence is noticed, recorded or disregarded. Option A: treat assumptions as a filter applied after evidence arrives, so the mechanism is selection among already available observation reports. Option B: treat assumptions as terms in the prediction, so they determine which object slots carry residual in the first place, and then separate record and corroborate as downstream links. What each leads to: A predicts that a better filter or a more open review culture should fix the problem, and it cannot explain a datum for which no slot exists. B predicts three distinguishable documentary traces (no slot, slot but dropped by the channel, slot recorded but neutralised by a shared pool), gives a refuter for each, and stops short of naming a historical assumption. The figures decide for B.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The difference in observation reports between frames facing the same ground. Arity: `k` object slots, not a global scalar. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symmetry: asymmetric, with a threshold between positive live mass and held; the valence of a datum is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), so direction matters. Scale: one agent's step-by-step loop and the population over time, with a directional membrane between (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
- **Domain 1, the perceptual slot.** Actors: researcher as frame, slots `s`, belief `b`, weighting `w`, library `Γ`, observation `o'`. Currency: residual `R_s`, live mass `R⁺_s`, boundary diff. Anchor: `o'`, which does not update on what the frame thinks. Question type: partition, which slots carry residual and which are held. Verdict: triangulated.
- **Domain 2, the channel and instrument.** Actors: instruments, metrics, channel, ground-maintainer seat. Currency: what can be registered at all; contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Question type: partition, what survives into a record. Verdict: triangulated.
- **Domain 3, the community and the pool.** Actors: researchers, frames, rooms, peer groups, the evidence pool. Currency: agreement and residuals; independence. Anchor: the ground via selection between rooms, never agreement. Question type: union compound, shared pool and divergent closure both bind. Verdict: triangulated for the pool claim; single-source for the valence sub-claim.
- **Order by dependency.** Notice, then record, then corroborate (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."). Inside the search: SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD (Formula step 7). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second.").

Strain test applied: an early reading used Fig 13's six-term substrate as the definition of background assumptions. Re-census showed the six are what the loop assumes, not the variable contents of `b` and `Γ`; the reading was retracted. A second early reading applied Fig 2's correlation rule to individual noticing; it was re-censused into Domain 3. Neither strain was smoothed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The mechanism shape is figure-governed and the Option B reading stands. Assumptions occupy the prediction term; because the residual is per slot, a datum is noticed only where the slot carries positive mass, held where the prediction explains it, and unreadable where no tracker exists. Recording is a channel property, with the ground fixed and the channel decaying. At community scale, a shared evidence pool turns agreement into a convention and makes a category-less datum easy to disregard as artifact. The same items under different assumptions can be isomers, same atoms and different bond, because the assumption is the bond a list of ingredients omits. The ground does not move, so this is not relativism. The specific historical instance is not answered in this package: naming which assumption mattered in a given episode requires an archive, a blind re-test or an independent line of evidence, and none was run. Result: `general`/`specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The refuters are written before the run and the three domains are kept separate, so a failure can be located in a specific slot, channel or pool rather than dissolving into a general dispute about bias. The real cost is spending a reader's independence by sending a recording before a characterised residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The three domain claims can be refused independently without touching the others. A refuted Fig 11 valence sub-claim leaves the Fig 2 pool claim standing. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a stage-1 packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A documentary check showing that a positively massed, tracked datum was still not noticed would break Domain 1; a raw log whose content survives intact into the published record under a supposedly saturated metric would break Domain 2; a same-closure chain that increases evidence would break Domain 3; and a standing catalogue of evidence types with residual-independent valence would break the single-source Fig 11 sub-claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
