## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

`FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figure-text extract at Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (carried reading): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the instance. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is a three-stage mechanism (assumption sets prediction, prediction sets residual slots, channel and pool set what persists). A name would be "the 1912 assumption that mattered" or "the 1999 attentional set". The framework cannot supply that name from its own resources.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried reading): use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Applied here: describe the effect (two frames, same ground, different records) before naming the cause ("confirmation bias", "paradigm", "institutional pressure"). The framework's own rule is that any frame you pick will seem to fit if you pick it first (Fig 9 :27-28).
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried reading): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when "the finish line will also be the starting place", and the paradox is that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The canonical README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest." For this question, that means an archive, a blind re-test, or a replicated observation, not an argument about the framework.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Assigning a specific historical assumption to a specific omitted datum is an import from the history-of-science frame into the vocabulary. It must carry its provenance.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried reading): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list; naming one as the answer to a slot turns a visible choice into an invisible one. That caution transfers directly: writing "the assumption was X" converts a frame choice into an invisible one unless the provenance is shown.

## 4. Why reading the record cannot name the assumption

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried reading) gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source" (one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered). "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

That is exactly the historian's position with a lab notebook. The recorded report is the reading. Several different assumptions could have produced the same report, so the report does not determine the assumption. Naming the assumption from the record alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. The SEP entry fetched this session supplies a real instance: Chadwick did not watch the scintillation screen and compare Pettersson's data with what he saw; he manipulated the equipment blind, alternating normal conditions with a condition in which particles could not hit the screen, and the assistants reported flashes at nearly the same rate in both. That is a source-discriminating instrument, and it settled the case where an argument could not.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (carried reading): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." So this audit cannot certify its own answer.

## 5. Q2 - ANSWERED, with the scope stated

**What exactly does the framework return for this question?** The shape of the mechanism: assumptions occupy the prediction term; the prediction is indexed per slot; positive residual is what is noticed; the channel determines what is recorded; a shared evidence pool turns agreement into a convention rather than corroboration. That is a kind of thing (Fig 9 :32), and it is checkable without the whole architecture.

**What does it not return?** Which assumption mattered in a given episode, which datum should have been noticed, or whether a particular community was right. Those require a ground reading. Naming them from inside this package would be the source-from-projection error in section 4.

**Does the package commit that error?** No, provided the three domain claims stay in `general`/`specified` mode and the specific historical instances stay in the WORLD section as secondary corroboration. `01`, `07`, `08` and `09` keep that separation. The one place where a name appears (the SEP Millikan example) is explicitly a world source with provenance, not a figure verdict.

## 5b. Correction to an earlier reading in this package

I initially wrote the mechanism as "assumptions filter evidence after it arrives". That is wrong and is corrected throughout: the assumption is a term in the prediction `Γ(b,a)`, so it acts *before* the residual exists. The record of a datum that "arrived and was filtered" is a different mechanism and is not what the figures say. Both readings are described here so the correction is visible rather than silent. Triangulated (Symbols table `b` and `R`; Fig 1 :19 and :28).

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (carried reading): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers famous historians to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the claim against a case each reader can check.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01`, `07` and `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency. They cannot settle the historical question they report on, and they are labelled as such everywhere.
