# Same evidence, different bond: isomers, order and union

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes that assumptions sit in the prediction and set per-slot residual. Two further questions are owed: (1) if two researchers hold the same recorded items, what makes their conclusions different rather than merely differently labelled? (2) in what order must the three operations (notice, record, disregard) be examined, and is the question one problem or several?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two researchers with the same records merely relabelling each other? No: they can be isomers

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."

If the "same evidence" in the question means the same set of recorded items, then the difference between two researchers is not in the items. It is in the bond the assumption imposes between them: which item is taken as a cause, which as a measurement, which as noise, which as an outlier to exclude. Under a symmetric bond the order of operands would not matter; under an ordered bond (sequence, production, comparison) it does, and Fig 12 :92 says symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. So the same ingredients under different assumptions can be different substances, with the difference invisible in a list of ingredients and decisive in what gets published. This is the figure-grounded content of the isomer claim for this question.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two frames with the same recorded items and demonstrably different assumptions produce the same behaviour at the ground in every tested case, the isomer reading collapses into a relabelling and the functional-group reading (Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.") stands instead.

### Q2 - What is the order of the three operations? Dependency order, not importance

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."
- Fig 3 :26-27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."

The order is: **notice, then record, then corroborate.** A slot never noticed cannot be recorded; a record that does not survive the channel cannot be corroborated. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." For this question that means a study of peer review (corroboration) cannot repair a failure of instrumentation (record) or a missing slot (notice), yet most effort lands at the top of the chain because "that is where the measurements already exist" (Fig 3 :29-30). The dependency order explains why the literature on research communities is larger than the literature on what never entered the notebook.

A second order governs the search itself. THE_FORMULA step 7 asks SEARCHED first (is the term in here and unfound?), then INWARD (extend an instrument already returning something), then OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs). For this question: first ask whether the datum is already in the frame and was missed; only then ask for a sharper instrument; only then bring in another field. "INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed; never read one against the other."

### Q3 - Is the question one problem or several? A union compound

- Fig 9 :54-56: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

"Notice", "record" and "disregard" are three conditions, not three severities of one failure. A better instrument fixes the record condition and leaves the held-bin condition standing; a better category scheme fixes the notice condition and leaves the shared-pool condition standing. Treating them as one problem produces a single lever and an intersection that never clears. Fig 9 :59: "the residue is finally describable" is the second-order payoff of splitting: after the split, what remains has a nameable shape. The split is made from the residual, not from a preferred frame.

**Falsifier:** if a single intervention moved all three conditions together in repeated cases, the union reading would be refuted and the three domains should be merged.

### Q4 - Which analogues transfer, and which are refused

- **Isomer, accepted** (Fig 12 :117): same records, different bond, different finding. Structurally licensed.
- **Functional group, accepted as the weaker reading** (Fig 12 :124): different records, same `state → structure → state` practice. This is the fallback if Q1's falsifier fires.
- **Catalyst** (Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use.") is worth noting for funding and credentialing: a piece of equipment or a review convention that survives its own use behaves differently across cases, and "nothing records which a term is". This is a `specified` analogy, not a figure verdict about any institution.
- **Import, refused as a verdict.** Naming a specific historical assumption as the missing primitive would be an import with no source gate or shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Allowed only with provenance.
- **Fig 13 substrate, refused.** The six terms are the substrate the loop assumes, not variable background assumptions. Using them as the answer to this question strains and was re-censused in `01` section 7.

## CONCLUSION

Two researchers can hold the same recorded items and still differ as isomers rather than as relabellers, because the assumption is the bond and the bond is the half a list of ingredients omits. The three operations run in dependency order, notice before record before corroborate, and the question is a union compound whose intersections survive any single lever. The habitat from which the cast is drawn is enumerated from the world, not composed from the framework. What this derivation does not do is name an assumption in a historical case; that requires a ground reading and a provenanced import.
