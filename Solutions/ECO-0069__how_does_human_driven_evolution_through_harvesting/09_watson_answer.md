## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question: how does human-driven evolution (through harvesting, pollution, or habitat change) alter ecosystem relationships? Option A: read it as one global force with one average effect on ecosystem health. Option B: read it as a maintained habitat in which a seat acts, with a union of overlapping conditions, per-slot residuals and cascades that cross scales. What each leads to: A produces an ungrounded average that hides the live signal and selects management targets as if they were anchors; B produces a checkable shape, four refuters written in advance, and an honest gap where a source-discriminating instrument is owed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; union or partition; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what the current library predicts about relationships and what actually persists and reproduces after an imposed human pressure. Arity: a three-way union of overlapping conditions (harvesting, pollution, habitat change), not a list (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: relations are typed and ordered, not symmetric properties; pollination, predation, competition and dependency have direction (Fig 11 :47; Fig 12 :49). Scale: first order inside a population, second order among species, third order across a substituted habitat, with cascades crossing all three (Fig 11 :52).
- **D1 evolutionary response in a population.** Actors: individuals, heritable variants, the imposed mortality or contaminant regime. Currency: trait frequencies, fitness, survival. Anchor: measured trait change and differential fitness in the ground. Admitted.
- **D2 the relationship web.** Actors: species and their interactions. Currency: interaction strength and sign, abundance. Anchor: observed persistence or loss of the interaction. Admitted.
- **D3 the human seat and management.** Actors: harvesters, regulators, managers, engineers. Currency: yield, policy, intervention, declared target. Anchor: the biological ground, not the declared target; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47). Admitted with a convention/anchor split.
- **D4 the temporal channel.** Actors: time series, monitors, instruments. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay, while the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **X1 "human-driven evolution" as a single global force.** No nameable anchor at that scale; it is a union of D1 to D4, not a further domain. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 "ecosystem health" or "progress" as the altered thing.** No nameable anchor until a residual names what failed to resolve. Not a domain yet.

**Union, not partition.** The overlap of D1 and D2 is an eco-evolutionary compound with its own loop. **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :19), and most intervention effort lands above a missing instrument (Fig 3 :27).

**Strain test applied:** Fig 11 :50 governs valence across residuals; Six and Klepzig (2021) report magnitude-only shifts within one system under varying context. Different objects, so no strain after re-census; see `01` §6. The sign-versus-magnitude discriminator is owed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the local name does not. This is a maintained habitat, so a seat acts and the biological ground, not the management convention, settles what changed. Actors' valences are relative to the residual, so what alters first is the relation and same-species-different-bond states are isomers. The change cascades outward past direct contact; habitat change substitutes rather than removes; introduction is available and exclusion is not. The three pressures are a union to be unbundled, read per slot and never averaged. Which specific trait or relation changes in a named system is a declared `gap`, unreachable at this budget and owed to a source-discriminating instrument. The earlier readings that habitat means niche, that harvesting is a subtraction bond, and that sign reversal is asserted in every case are retracted or narrowed in `01` §6. Mode `general`/`specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The verdicts per domain are kept separate above and are never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape claim carries four refuters written before the run and a provenance trail, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending the framework before their verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable version of that cost is handled by the two-stage offer in `02` and `04`.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a refuter fires; a refusing term is added and the reach falls because "something was added rather than because anything was deleted" (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its four falsifiers are recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument showing whether a given pair shifts sign or only magnitude would close part of the gap; a completed enumeration that accounts for a three-step cascade from first-order data would break F3; an intervention that removes contact rather than substituting a habitat would break F4; one lever clearing all three pressures with no categorically different residue would break F5; an ecosystem-level aggregate predicting every slot with no residual would break F8. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
