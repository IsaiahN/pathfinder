## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07`: a maintained habitat, relations re-typed, cascades, substitution, union, ground not target.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a specific claim about a named system, e.g. "in this river, harvest X caused interaction Y to flip sign by year Z."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **sign-versus-magnitude discriminator**. Fig 11 :50 says valence is relative to the residual, but no figure line says whether a given change flips a sign or only shifts magnitude. Six and Klepzig (2021) found magnitude only in one system. The shorthand "relations change" collapses that distinction.
- the **per-slot anchor**. "Per slot" is a rule for indexing, not a list of slots. The slots have to be enumerated from the world; the condensation does not carry them.
- the **cascade boundary**. Fig 11 :44 says enumerate until the cascade stops mattering; nothing in the shorthand says where it stops in a given river.
- the **convention/ground separation**. Fig 10 :47 is a rule about what a convention is not; converting it into a pass/fail management test requires a ground reading the shorthand does not contain.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a seat acts, the relation is the thing that changes, contact carries the change outward, and the ground settles. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero, the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
