## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question as asked: how does human-driven evolution (through harvesting, pollution, or habitat change) alter ecosystem relationships?

Restated as an error signal: `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per slot `s` (Fig 1 :11, Symbols table). A human seat performs an action `a` on a habitat (a harvest regime, a contaminant, a substitute habitat). The prediction `Γ(b,a)` says what the relationships will be. The observation `o'` is what actually persists and reproduces. The residual is the gap between them, and the question asks what the gap is made of and where it propagates.

**What is being decided:** whether "alters ecosystem relationships" names one mechanism or a union of distinct conditions, and what shape any answer must have before a particular case is measured.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"):** a claim of the form "a seat acts on a habitat; the action changes the residual against which each actor's valence is read; the change propagates outward through contact; the ground settles which relationships actually changed." This shape is written down here, before any figure line is chosen, so it can accrue a residual and be refused like anything else. It is a preference, not yet a term with standing.

**Per-slot warning retained:** "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19) and "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." (Fig 1 :22) There is no single global answer for "ecosystem relationships." There is an answer per species, per interaction, per place, per time.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three named pressures (harvesting, pollution, habitat change), but they are not three independent one-place facts. Each is a two-place relation between a seat action and a receiving habitat. The question is therefore a three-way union, not a list.
- **Symmetry:** relationships are typed and ordered. Predation, pollination, competition and dependency are not symmetric properties of the participants; which direction the influence travels is part of what the relation is. Fig 11 :47 lists them as relations. Fig 12 :103 names the seven bonds and distinguishes the three symmetric from the four ordered.
- **Scale:** at least three. Trait frequencies inside a population (first order), interaction strengths among species (second order), persistence of the assemblage across a substituted habitat (third order). Contact does not stop at the first scale: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." (Fig 11 :52)

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 evolutionary response in a population | individuals, heritable variants, the imposed mortality or toxicant regime | trait frequencies, fitness, survival | measured trait change and differential fitness in the ground | admitted |
| D2 the relationship web | species, interactions (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | interaction strength, sign, abundance | observed persistence or loss of the interaction | admitted |
| D3 the human seat and management | harvesters, regulators, managers, engineers | yield, policy, intervention, declared target | the biological ground, not the declared target | admitted, with a convention/anchor split |
| D4 the temporal channel | time series, monitors, instruments, sampled percepts | contact with the ground | the ground, which does not decay; the channel does | admitted |
| X1 "human-driven evolution" as one global force | none nameable at this scale | none | none: it is a union of D1 to D4, not a further domain | not a domain yet |
| X2 "ecosystem health" or "progress" as the thing altered | none | none | none until a residual names what failed to resolve | not a domain yet |

No nameable anchor means not a domain yet. X1 and X2 are excluded on that rule, not by preference.

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Harvesting plus habitat change is a compound, not a harder case of either; Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." D1 and D2 overlap (eco-evolutionary feedback), so the overlap is itself a compound with its own loop.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. perception: can the changed trait or interaction be perceived at all (Fig 3 :4-5)
2. vocabulary: are there terms for what the change affords (Fig 3 :6-7)
3. objective: can we say what would count as a change in this system (Fig 3 :9-10)
4. planning: a route to that objective under incomplete information (Fig 3 :12-13)
5. learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it (Fig 3 :14-15)

Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a link below it: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." (Fig 3 :19) Most human-driven-evolution effort lands at step 4 (regulation, restocking, engineering) while the instrument at step 1 is still missing: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." (Fig 3 :27)

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1: Human-driven evolution is a maintained habitat, not bare "survival of the fittest" (decisive for the frame)

Fig 11 :56: **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."**
Fig 11 :58: **"Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."**

The question explicitly names a human seat. So the governing panel is the maintained one, not the unmaintained one. The framework essay states the same distinction without the figure vocabulary: "Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground. When there is no one in the Ground Maintainer seat, the environment is the only selector." (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-23) And: "In order to steer evolution of a thing, you need to control what becomes the ground for the composer to range against. The 'Steering of Evolution' is unnatural selection." (*How Intelligence Actually Works*, opened 2026-11-23)

This is not a rhetorical relabel. It changes the answer: in the maintained mode, the seat chooses instruments and introductions, so "how does human-driven evolution alter relationships" is partly a question about what the seat did, and only partly a question about what selection did. The ground still settles; the seat does not author the verdict.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), applied `specified` to humans. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 10 :24, low overlap between the habitat panel and the channel panel). **Refuter:** a documented case in which the human seat is absent or inert and the same relationship change occurs by pure contact; then this finding does not govern that case and the unmaintained panel applies.

### F2: What changes is the valence of the relation, not a fixed property of the actors

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

So the direct answer to "alter ecosystem relationships" is a re-typing of the bond. The actors can stay identical while the relationship changes, because the residual against which they are read has moved. Fig 12 :117 names the structural form: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

**Mode:** `general` for the valence rule, `specified` for ecosystem application. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 valence rule plus Fig 12 isomer definition, two figures with different subject matter and low overlap). **Refuter:** a case in which the same actor keeps an invariant interaction sign across a documented change of residual that the actor demonstrably affects. A genuine partial conflict exists here: Six and Klepzig (2021), opened 2026-11-23, report in a bark beetle system that they "found no evidence that bark beetle mutualisms change in sign as conditions vary, only in magnitude." This did not flip the verdict. Re-census (see §6) shows the figure line is about different residuals, while that study is about one system under varying context; different objects, no strain after re-census. The magnitude-versus-sign question is itself a measurement question, recorded as owed.

### F3: Contact cascades; scoping to direct contact is the named failure

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

The framework itself uses the pollution case as its cascade example. A world source opened for corroboration states it independently: "The bald eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus) population in North America declined greatly after World War II due primarily to the eggshell thinning effects of p,p'-DDE, a biodegradation product of DDT." (Bowerman et al. 1995, *Environmental Health Perspectives*, opened 2026-11-23). The contaminant introduced for one purpose reached a non-target apex predator three links away.

**Mode:** `general` for enumeration and cascade, `specified` for the DDT case. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 3 :19, different figures: enumeration rule versus the impossibility of rescuing a broken upstream link). **Refuter:** a completed habitat enumeration that stops at first-order contact and still accounts for every observed relation three steps out.

### F4: The seat can introduce but cannot subtract; habitat change is substitution

Fig 11 :61-62: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
Fig 11 :63-64: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state. And in neither mode is the habitat composed: unmaintained nothing composes it, maintained the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author."**
Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :29-30: **"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"** (the two silent failure modes of a substituted habitat).

This governs habitat change directly. Moving a population, restoring a site or fencing off an area does not remove a habitat; it replaces one, and the new habitat's actors were not chosen. A management plan that treats removal as available has misidentified the operator. Fig 11 :65 names the introduced-actor version as ecosystem engineering after Jones, Lawton and Shachak (1994), and the framework essay adds the caution: "when altering a habitat by unintentionally introducing a predatory threat into the ecosystem, or disturbing the balance by adding or removing vital resource points, you can inadvertently create major unpredictable consequences downstream." (*How Intelligence Actually Works*, opened 2026-11-23)

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 asymmetry plus Fig 10 :47, the convention that decides an outcome stops being one). **Refuter:** a documented intervention that genuinely removes a habitat rather than substituting one, with the removed contact demonstrably absent from the system afterwards.

### F5: The three pressures are a union and must be unbundled before searching

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Harvesting (selective removal), pollution (introduced actor with cascading dose) and habitat change (substitution) are three different seat actions with different currencies and different anchors. A single lever, for example a harvest quota, clears the region where only harvesting binds and leaves the pollution-plus-habitat intersections standing. A compound (harvesting under a substituted habitat with a persistent contaminant) is a new object with its own loop, not an average of three effects.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 8 :51-52 surplus lives where closures differ, low overlap). **Refuter:** a case in which one intervention resolves all three conditions with no categorically different residue, which would show the problem was a partition after all.

### F6: The management target is a convention, not the ground

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Yield targets, recovery criteria and "ecosystem health" indices are conventions. They become invalid as conventions the moment they are allowed to decide the verdict, for example when a stock is declared recovered because the index says so rather than because the ground (persistence, reproduction, interaction structure) pays. The ground is what settles; managers range against it separately and cannot move it.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 2, different figures: seat rule versus anchor rule). **Refuter:** a declared outcome that survives a ground reading in which the biological relations did not recover.

### F7: Which specific trait or relation changes is a declared gap at this budget

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 6 :34-35: **"the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

No figure line yields the sign, magnitude or timing of a change in a named system. The shape is returned; the local name is owed. This is unreachable-at-this-budget, not underivable-in-principle: the term is reachable with a per-slot ground reading (a time series, a common-garden or reciprocal-transplant design, a source-discriminating measurement). Importing a general theory here would add an atom that was not needed and would cost more than starting from the instrument.

**Mode:** `general` for the gap verdict, `specified` for the owed instrument. **Status:** gap, declared after re-census. **Refuter:** any figure line that determinately predicts which trait or interaction changes under a named pressure in a named system; a re-census that finds one would remove this gap.

### F8: Per-slot indexing, never an ecosystem-wide average

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A harvest regime can select earlier maturation in one population while a contaminant selects tolerance in another, and an average "ecosystem response" erases both. World evidence: "Harvest of immature fish can skew the age composition of Chinook salmon populations towards younger, and hence smaller, individuals." (Gayeski et al. 2026, *Scientific Reports*, opened 2026-11-23) and, in a separate experiment, "populations exposed to positively size-dependent harvest, as that imposed by fisheries, evolved towards faster life history (i.e., heritable changes towards early maturation and high fecundity)" (Diaz Pauli et al. 2026, *Evolutionary Applications*, opened 2026-11-23). These are per-population readings, not a global law.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 plus Fig 13 :34-39, independence within a set, different figures). **Refuter:** an ecosystem-level aggregate that predicts every per-slot response without residual.

### F9: The temporal channel can fail while the ground does not

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :26: **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Rapid evolution can outpace the monitor. Consecutive years of the same index are one room handed along, not independent corroboration. What is needed is selection between rooms: the next observation must differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced. Fig 7 :37: **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"**

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 7, different figures: channel failure versus room chain). **Refuter:** a monitoring series in which agreement across years tracks ground change with high bracket fidelity and high ground pressure, and still fails to detect a documented change.

### F10: Prestige cannot settle which relationships changed

Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."
Seat Map :283: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."
Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

A list of authorities selected for likely agreement would manufacture exactly the correlation that disqualifies their agreement as a verdict. The ask must be for a refutation of a shape, not an endorsement of a conclusion.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source for the seat rules (the amended field guide), corroborated by Fig 2 :40 and the framework essay's prestige rule: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." **Refuter:** a case in which expert agreement, with no ground reading, correctly settled a relationship change and no amount of ground reading improved it.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. A relationship changes sign or magnitude with an invariant residual and an invariant seat action, so valence is not relative to the residual (breaks F2).
2. A habitat intervention demonstrably removes contact rather than substituting a habitat (breaks F4).
3. One lever resolves harvesting, pollution and habitat change with no categorically different residue, so the problem is a partition and not a union (breaks F5).
4. A declared management outcome survives a ground reading in which the biological relations did not recover (breaks F6).
5. An ecosystem-level aggregate predicts every per-slot response with no residual (breaks F8).
6. A figure line is found that determinately names the trait or relation that changes in a given system, which would dissolve F7.

If any fires, the shape is wrong and the affected finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: this is a maintained habitat and the thing altered is the valence of relations. Everything else is downstream.
2. Keep the three pressures unbundled (F5) and keep the verdicts per domain; never average them into an "ecosystem response" (F8).
3. Enumerate outward past direct contact (F3). The DDT case in the figure is the template, and the published record corroborates it.
4. State the mode on every claim. No measurement here settles anything (F7).
5. Separate the management convention from the biological ground (F6), and ask outside readers for refutations (F10).
6. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

### Strain test

After re-census, one live strain, named and not smoothed: Fig 11 :50 says the same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, while Six and Klepzig (2021) find magnitude shifts without sign shifts in a bark beetle system. The strain is resolved by object scope (across residuals versus within context), not by softening either statement. Whether a given human-driven change produces a sign reversal or only a magnitude shift in a given system remains an empirical question, recorded in `07` and `08`.

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives: a human seat acts on a habitat, the residual moves, valences are re-typed relative to it, the change cascades outward through contact, habitat change is substitution rather than removal, the three pressures form a union, and the biological ground and not the management convention settles what actually changed. What does not survive is any claim that the framework names which trait or relation changes, any equation of habitat with niche, any subtraction model of harvesting, and any averaging of per-slot responses. Everything is `general` or `specified`, nothing is `grounded`. Strains after re-census: one, named above. Verdicts per domain are kept separate in `09_watson_answer.md` and are never averaged.
