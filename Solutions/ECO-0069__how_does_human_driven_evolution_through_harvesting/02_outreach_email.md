# Outreach draft: check one shape before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the corrections in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 have been read. The stage-1 claim must survive on its own, without the framework's interiors.

---

**Subject:** Would this refute your account of human-driven evolution in ecosystems?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a checkable claim about how human-driven evolution (harvesting, pollution, habitat change) alters ecosystem relationships. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the three pressures, not `grounded`):** a human pressure is a seat acting on a habitat. The framework's rule is that an actor's value is relative to the current residual, not a property it carries, so what changes first is the relation, not necessarily the list of species. The same actor can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. Contact then cascades outward: the framework's own example is DDT reaching eagles three steps from the point of use. Habitat change is substitution of one habitat for another, not removal of a habitat, and a seat can introduce an actor but cannot subtract the habitat. Harvesting, pollution and habitat change overlap as conditions, so they are a union to be unbundled, not three independent effects to be averaged.
>
> **What this does not claim:** that the framework names which trait or interaction changes in your system. It returns a shape, not a name. It does not say that a relationship always flips sign; magnitude-only shifts are possible and which occurs is a measurement question. It does not equate the framework's habitat with an ecological niche. It does not treat a yield target or a recovery index as the thing that settles the question; the biological ground does that.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this message):**
> 1. a relationship changes while the residual and the seat action are invariant;
> 2. an intervention demonstrably removes contact rather than substituting a habitat;
> 3. one lever clears all three pressures with no categorically different residue;
> 4. an ecosystem-wide aggregate predicts every per-slot response with no residual.
>
> If any holds, I will withdraw the affected part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer, one question:** what would you expect to see in your system if this shape were right? I would like your verdict recorded before I show you any theory, because once you have the framework's interiors, your agreement is no longer an independent check.
>
> **If it survives, and only then,** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Full architecture and citation list: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - How intelligence actually works (steering evolution, habitat disturbance): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - The Ground Maintainer and the habitat: figures 10 and 11 in the public figure set, https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the formula on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
