# Expert verification packet: the Tether on human-driven evolution and ecosystem relationships

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or evolutionary biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the application to harvesting, pollution and habitat change. Not `grounded`.** No claim is made about which trait or interaction changes in a named system.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A human pressure is a seat acting on a habitat. What alters first is the relation, not necessarily the actor list: an actor's value is relative to the current residual, so the same actor can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. Two states with the same species and different interactions are isomers. The change propagates outward through contact, so the residual that matters can be several steps from the point of use. Habitat change is substitution of one habitat for another, not removal of a habitat, and a seat can introduce an actor but cannot subtract the habitat. Harvesting, pollution and habitat change are overlapping conditions forming a union, not three effects to be averaged. The biological ground, not a management target, settles what actually changed. The framework returns this shape, not the local name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12

- **Statement:** an actor's valence is relative to the residual; two ecosystem states with the same actors and different relations are isomers, so the relation is the thing that changes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49, "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50, "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** a documented case where an actor keeps an invariant interaction sign across a change of residual it demonstrably affects; or two ecosystem states differing in relationships while actors, bonds and conditions are all fixed. A partial conflict is already recorded: Six and Klepzig (2021) "found no evidence that bark beetle mutualisms change in sign as conditions vary, only in magnitude." This is recorded in `01` §6, not hidden.

### C2: ecology-facing, checkable against published cases

- **Statement:** contact cascades outward, so first-order enumeration misses the residual that matters; persistent pressures act like catalysts; habitat change substitutes rather than removes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44, "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52, "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 12 :119, "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- **What to check:** is the DDT example faithful to the toxicology record? Bowerman et al. (1995) report that the bald eagle population "declined greatly after World War II due primarily to the eggshell thinning effects of p,p'-DDE, a biodegradation product of DDT." Is a substituted habitat correctly described as substitution rather than removal in a restoration or translocation case you know?
- **Refuter:** a completed enumeration stopping at direct contact that still accounts for every observed relation three steps out; or an intervention that demonstrably removes contact rather than substituting a habitat.

### C3: framework-internal derivation, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the three pressures form a union that must be unbundled, and a management target is a convention that cannot be the anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 10 :47, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
- **Refuter:** one lever that clears all three pressures with no categorically different residue; or a declared recovery that survives a ground reading in which the biological relations did not recover; or an ecosystem-level aggregate that predicts every per-slot response with no residual (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a prediction of which trait, species or interaction changes in a named system. That is a declared gap. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
- Not that relationships always flip sign. Magnitude-only shifts occur.
- Not that the framework's habitat is the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping; see `01` §6.
- Not that harvesting is a subtraction from the habitat. Exclusion is not available to the seat (Fig 11 :61-62); the earlier notation was retracted.
- Not that the six-term set derives ecology. Pressures are bindings from the ecology frame, provenanced as such; they add no primitive and do not move the wall (Fig 6).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source," and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." The discriminating tests are:

1. Does the same species pair change interaction sign, or only magnitude, under a documented change of residual?
2. Does the cascade reach three links out before a first-order enumeration predicts it?
3. After a habitat intervention, what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced?
4. Does one lever clear all three pressures with no categorically different residue?
5. Does a declared recovery survive a reading in which the biological relations did not recover?
6. Does an ecosystem-level aggregate predict every per-slot response with no residual?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-52 and :61-66, Fig 12 :117 and :119, and Fig 9 :55, eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against a case you know: is the cascade and substitution claim faithful to the published record?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a sign shift from a magnitude shift in situ, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
