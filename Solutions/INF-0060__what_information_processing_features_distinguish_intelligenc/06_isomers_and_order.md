# Union, refusal order, and the lift analogue

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit (`01` F7) left three structural questions open. (1) Is "intelligence versus pattern recognition" one question to be split, or a union of conditions to be unbundled? (2) In what order do refusal, minting and settlement come, and does reversing that order change the answer? (3) What is the right analogue: are two systems with identical internals but different contact the same thing arranged differently, or the same thing with an accessory? This file answers those three.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for the structure question: the four domains D1 to D4 from `01` §1, the seven bonds plus negation, and the operation "range against a ground." No new primitive is introduced. The anchor for each domain is the one named in `01` §1: the ground for D1/D2/D3, the verifiable score for D4. Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The candidates from `01` F7 are not mutually exclusive. A system can refuse and still never range against an immovable ground; it can import and still self-score; it can generate methods and still sit in a substituted habitat. Each candidate is a condition that can hold alone.
- ⇒ The question is a **union** of conditions, not a partition of systems into two kinds. Searching for one feature F is searching for a single lever on an overlapping union: it clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56).
- **Falsifier, written before this derivation:** if the conditions turn out to be nested (for example, if every system that refuses necessarily imports and ranges against a ground), the split is a partition and a single lever would clear it. I would then withdraw the union reading. That test was not run against data; the union reading is `specified`, not `grounded`.

### Q2 - What is the order of refusal, minting and settlement?

- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: *"REFUTED a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*, and *"the fifth by what is WRONG, and no arrangement of the other four reaches it."*
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 3: *"a refusing term pays the same bargain. What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over: NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED."*
- Fig 12 :140: **"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."**
- ⇒ The order is: describe the residual in effect terms, refuse the term that is held and failing (here, "intelligence as an agent-only property"), then mint the replacement shape, then settle it against a ground. Refusing first matters: if a mint is attempted before the refusal, the new term is composed over a term whose standing should have been removed, and the reach is larger than it should be.
- This is the smallest link in the chain and the one with no instrument (Fig 3 :25-27: the link between having terms for what a situation offers and saying what would count as progress *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments"*).
- **Falsifier:** show a case where minting the replacement before refusing the held term gives a strictly smaller reach and a better ground prediction. That would reverse step 2 and break this ordering claim. Triangulated for the rule (formula plus Fig 12), unmeasured for the consequence.

### Q3 - The lift analogue

- Fig 11 :41: **"Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."**
- *How Intelligence Actually Works* calls this Cayley's move: separating lift from flapping is what made powered flight a workable problem. The architecture essay's bibliography records *"Cayley, G. (1809-1810). On aerial navigation... A parallel. Figure 11's closing move."*
- ⇒ "Is this system intelligent?" is the bird question. "What contact does this system maintain, what can it refuse, and what can it import?" is the lift question. The former dissolves as a substrate question; the latter stays answerable. This is why `02_outreach_email.md` never asks a recipient to decide whether a system is intelligent.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit an organism or machine for which the contact question is settled and the intelligence question still makes a fresh, ground-bearing prediction. That would mean the substrate question does not dissolve, and Fig 11 :41 would be narrowed. (The framework's own Fig 11 :71-72 already names the general refuter: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground.)

### Q4 - Are the two systems isomers?

- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**
- Two systems with identical internal architecture (same atoms) but different ground contact (different bond to an anchor) are **not** variants of one thing that happens to be deployed differently. Under Fig 12 they are different substances, and the difference is invisible in the architecture diagram alone. This is the structural reason a benchmark score cannot discriminate: the score is the ingredient list without the bond.
- The weaker, safer claim is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*): both a pattern recognizer and a capable system may share the substructure "maps input to output against a distribution." The qualifier, ground contact with refusal, says which member.
- **Falsifier:** show two systems with the same internals and different contact whose behavioural predictions are identical under every available instrument, including instruments that discriminate source. Then "different substance" has no ground reading and the isomer claim collapses into the functional-group claim. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12), single-source and unmeasured for the application to AI systems.

## CONCLUSION

The discriminator is a **union** of conditions, not a single feature (Q1). The correct order is: refuse the held term first, then mint the replacement shape, then settle against a ground (Q2). The analogue is the lift question, not the bird question (Q3). Two systems with the same internals and different contact are isomers under Fig 12's definition, with the weaker functional-group claim available if the source-discriminating instrument is missing (Q4). None of this mints a primitive or settles a measurement. It is a structure claim with four falsifiers, all stated before the conclusion.
