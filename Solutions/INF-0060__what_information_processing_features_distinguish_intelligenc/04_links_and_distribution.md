## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The "wrong question" move is not unique to the framework.** Turing (1950) opens *"I PROPOSE to consider the question, 'Can machines think?'"* and immediately says that seeking the meaning of the words by common usage would mean *"a statistical survey such as a Gallup poll. But this is absurd."* He replaces the question with the imitation game, and calls the original *"too meaningless to deserve discussion."* This is the same structural move as Fig 11 :35. It corroborates the **form** of the answer (replace a substrate question with an operational one), not the framework's specific claims. Fields: AI foundations and philosophy of mind. Overlap with the framework is low; the author, venue and era are independent.
2. **Contact over internal computation.** The SEP entry on embodied cognition (Shapiro, Spaulding and Dove, 2026) says *"Mental processes are not, or not only, computational processes. The brain is not a computer, or not the seat of cognition."* It records the ecological psychology claim that visual perceptual processes are *"not exclusive to the eye or even the brain but involve the whole organism as it moves about its environment."* This corroborates Fig 11 :39 (capability is agent-and-habitat) from a different field. It is a parallel witness, not a framework source.
3. **Projection does not determine source.** The same SEP entry notes that for any retinal pattern *"there exists an infinite number of possible distal surfaces capable of producing that pattern."* This is the inverse-optics case, structurally the same point the framework's essays make about the colour green and the general limits of source discrimination. It supports the refusal of benchmark scores as a discriminator: the reading does not name the source.
4. **Pattern recognition needs supplementation.** Marcus (2018) concludes that *"deep learning must be supplemented by other techniques if we are to reach artificial general intelligence."* This is a practitioner's corroboration of the claim that fitting patterns is not the whole of capability. It says nothing about refusal, import or the ground, so it is weak corroboration only.
5. **Framework-internal essays, high overlap.** *How Intelligence Actually Works* and *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* are by the framework's author and share its evidence pool. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight. They are used for the canonical reading of "shape" and for the honest status statement, not as outside witnesses.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, before any theory.** Send the residual in effect terms and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict on the shape claim before anything else is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure text and the two essays. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they have now seen the interiors (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3, method reuse.** Offer the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A reply that says "this fails because..." is the desired outcome. An endorsement is a conversation, not evidence.
