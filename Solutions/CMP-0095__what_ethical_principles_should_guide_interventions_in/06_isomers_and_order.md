# Union, order and two currencies: supporting derivation

## RESIDUAL

The question "what ethical principles should guide uneven interventions" could be one question or several bundled together. Before choosing any figure line, decide whether the residual is a partition or a union, and in what order its links must be settled. A second question: what is the cost structure of comparing principles, and can the costs be added?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting pass)

Atoms held: the per-slot residual `R_s`; the seat and the habitat; the metric and the ground; the stakes and the ruling record. Bonds held: `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, `−` subtraction, `¬` negation (Operators table). No new primitive is available without import.

- Arity: `R_s` is a vector over many slots (Fig 1 :19).
- Symmetry: valence is relative to the residual, so benefit and harm are not fixed symmetric operands (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Scale: local action, cascading to second and third order (Fig 11 :52-53).

## Q1. Is this a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :54-55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

The residual overlaps three conditions:

1. a **distributional condition**: which party carries live mass (Domain 2);
2. an **intervention condition**: what the action introduces and what cascades (Domain 1);
3. a **verification condition**: who may grade, with what stake (Domain 3).

They are not three versions of one question. A single lever that clears the distributional condition leaves the verification condition standing, because the grader's stake still binds. So this is a **union compound** with its own loop. The correct move is to unbundle, run a loop per domain, and keep the verdicts separate. Never average them. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single principle can be shown to clear all three conditions at once (for example, one rule that simultaneously makes every slot visible, enumerates every cascade and removes every grading stake), the union reading is wrong and the problem is a partition. No such principle is in the figure set.

## Q2. What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

| link | consumes | produces | figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| describe `R_s` per slot | observations | a live-slot map | Fig 1 :19, :28 |
| enumerate habitat | the live-slot map | direct, second and third-order contacts | Fig 11 :43-44, :52-53 |
| fix the ground | the contact map | a metric the intervention cannot move | Fig 2 :33-34 |
| declare stakes | the metric | eligible grading seats | Fig 10 :59-60 |
| introduce and read | all above | a settled or refused result | Fig 11 :21, Formula step 5 |

A link with no instrument is not a link that works: Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." In this derivation the distributional link has an instrument (per-slot residuals); the third-order cascade often does not. That is where a reviewer should look first.

**Falsifier:** show that the ground can be fixed before the habitat is enumerated and that this produces the same live-slot map. If so, the order above is a convention rather than a dependency, and the table is wrong.

## Q3. Are the three conditions isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance." Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The three conditions do **not** share the same atoms: distribution has slots, intervention has contacts, verification has seats and stakes. Under Fig 12's definition they are therefore **not isomers**. They share a **functional group**: each is a per-slot ranging of a frame against a ground. The group says what kind (ground-ranged check); the qualifier says which member (slot, contact or seat).

The stronger isomer claim would require all three to be written over one atom set `{slot, residual, seat, ground, contact, stake}` with different bonds. That is available as a `specified` hypothesis, not as a derivation, and it needs a falsifier: if one condition can be written without an atom another requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), triangulated for the functional-group reading with Fig 1 and Fig 10 (low overlap between residual indexing and seat rules).

## Q4. Can the two costs be added?

Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

For an uneven intervention:

- **bits** price the description of the proposed principle and its per-slot table;
- **actions** price finding out whether it actually settles against the ground, which includes enumerating cascades and running the check.

Fig 12 :128-129 "The two readings come apart in both directions." A principle that is cheap to state (one sentence, "maximise total welfare") may take a whole budget to settle, because every live slot and every third-order contact must be measured. A long per-slot table is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. Neither figure predicts the other, so both must be read.

This is where the world literature's cost-benefit dispute lands. The Precautionary principle article records the strong-form view: "You are not allowed to balance costs against benefits when deciding what to do." That is a **conflict** with naive single-currency accounting, not corroboration. Per the method, the conflict was sent back to the census. The re-census produced this section: the figures do not forbid comparing costs, they forbid **adding** incommensurable units while claiming the sum is a verdict. So the figure answer is: report both, and let the ground decide. The verdict was not flipped by the world conflict.

**Strain, named:** the Operators table defines `⋛` (comparison) as "a threshold relation between two quantities." Whether two differently-priced readings can be compared in a single threshold at all needs an instrument, not an argument. This strain is carried into `11_audit.md`. It is not smoothed.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a single-unit accounting that preserves both readings losslessly and predicts which proposals can be settled within budget. If produced, Q4 falls.

## Q5. What recurses?

Fig 12 :103-104 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." and "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

A settled intervention (one whose per-slot result has been paid by the ground) becomes an atom in the habitat for the next intervention. The next intervention composes over it, and the residual of the previous turn is what the next turn starts from (Fig 13 :88-89). So "permanent work" (Fig 11 :63) is not a rhetorical caution: it is the recursion. A managed system is never a solved state because its settled parts become operands at the next level.

**Falsifier:** show a settled intervention that cannot serve as an operand for a later intervention in the same habitat. If so, the recursion claim is wrong for this domain.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Its three conditions must be looped separately and their verdicts kept separate, ordered by dependency from per-slot description to settlement. They are a functional group, not isomers, unless the stronger shared-atom hypothesis is separately tested. Costs come in two currencies that do not add, and a settled intervention becomes an atom at the next level. The one live strain is the operand type of comparison in Q4, recorded openly.
