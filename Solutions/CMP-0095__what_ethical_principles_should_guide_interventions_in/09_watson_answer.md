## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about ethical principles for interventions where benefits and harms are distributed unevenly. Option A: name a distributive rule (equality, maximin, utility sum) as the answer and treat it as settled. Option B: derive the shape any checkable principle must have, keep the per-slot residual visible, keep the ground unmovable, declare stakes, and mark the choice among distributive rules as a `gap`. What each leads to: A sends a `general`-mode verdict the figures cannot derive, hides live slots in an aggregate, and allows the metric to be moved by the intervention it grades; B sends a falsifiable shape, a written refuter, and an honest stop with named instruments. This run takes B.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-party vector `R_s` after an intervention, relative to prediction. Arity: many slots, indexed per object, never a scalar. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symmetry: benefits and harms are not symmetric operands because valence is relative to the residual. Scale: local action with cascade to second and third order. Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9).
- **Domain 1, intervention and habitat.** Actors: the seat, the introduced actor, the enumerated habitat, affected parties as slots. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the ground, what actually happens after introduction. Union with the other two domains. Dependency: enumerate habitat before introducing.
- **Domain 2, evaluation and measurement.** Actors: slots, the metric, the evaluator. Currency: residual per slot, plus two incommensurable costs, bits and actions (Fig 12 :125-127). Anchor: the ground as the only metric, which must not update (Fig 2 :33-34). Partition question: which slot carries live mass.
- **Domain 3, verification and the checking seat.** Actors: checkers, stakeholders, the maintainer. Currency: agreement and residual, held apart. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Stakes must be declared (Fig 10 :59-60). Partition question: who may grade.
- **Domain 4, substantive distributive content.** Actors: none currently held. Currency: none. Anchor: none nameable. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain. Recorded as `gap`, not forced into a loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe `R_s` per slot, enumerate the habitat, fix the ground, declare stakes, then introduce and read. Effort at the last step cannot rescue a missing earlier link: Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Strain test applied: an early reading tried to use Fig 13's lockstep rule as an ethical symmetry principle. Re-census sent that quantity to Fig 1's per-slot rule. No figure was made to carry a quantity it does not govern. The one live strain is the operand type of comparison across two currencies (section TETHER, C3).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the substantive rule does not. Grade per affected party rather than by a net total; re-evaluate benefit and harm against the current residual; enumerate the habitat to second and third order; treat introduction as permanent work; keep the grading metric unmovable; declare every stake, including inaction; keep agreement separate from the verdict; report two cost accounts; record rulings with lineage. These are form constraints, not a moral theory. Which distribution is just is a `gap` after a genuine re-census: `Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence` (Fig 13 :133). This is a `general` / `specified`, ungrounded result, and this run is a simulation, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is sending a moral-sounding checklist that hides the live slot, drifts the anchor and spends outside readers' independence. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. These are form constraints, so a reviewer can rewrite any one of them without a new primitive and without withdrawing the others. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already strip out every substantive distributive claim. A published packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot outcome table from a real intervention, an anchor-invariance test, a cascade audit with named instruments, a stake register, and a two-account cost report would settle the constraints. A derived unique distributive rule with no added premise would close the `gap`. A figure re-census that admits comparison across two differently-priced currencies as quantities at that level would clear the live strain. A lossless aggregate, a movable metric that still settles, or a fixed valence across residuals would break the shape claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
