# Expert verification packet: CMP-0095

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ethics-of-intervention researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the individual constraints, `gap` on the substantive distribution rule. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a moral verdict. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An intervention into a system where benefits and harms are uneven should be graded per affected party, not by a net total. A global result near zero is compatible with one party carrying all the harm, so the average hides the only signal that says where to look. The grading metric must not be a metric the intervention can move: if it is, the measure becomes part of the intervention it was supposed to judge, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices. Three further constraints follow from the same arrangement: re-evaluate who benefits against the current residual instead of fixing a permanent roster; enumerate effects outward to second and third order before acting; and declare the stake of every grader, including the stake in inaction. The framework returns this shape, not a name. Which distribution is just is a `gap`, with written falsifiers and named instruments, not a finding that the question was ignored.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11
- **Statement:** evaluate per slot; valence is relative to the residual, so no standing list of beneficiaries and harmers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** is the per-slot claim actually forced by the per-slot indexing rule, or is aggregation a compatible coarse reading? Look at Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single aggregate that is a lossless transform of the per-party vector, keeping every live slot separately visible and settled; or derive a fixed actor valence that survives a change of residual. Either fires, C1 falls.

### C2: measurement-facing; checkable by an evaluator of interventions
- **Statement:** the metric that grades the intervention must not move under the intervention; second and third-order contacts must be enumerated before acting.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 11 :52-53: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** in a real evaluation, can the outcome metric be gamed by the intervention being graded? Goodhart's law ("Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes.") and Campbell's law predict yes. Are second-order effects actually in scope, or does the standard protocol stop at direct contact?
- **Refuter:** show a metric that moves under the intervention and still settles the verdict without coherent drift, confirmed by an independent anchor; or show that third-order contact cannot in principle be enumerated and the intervention still settles with all live slots visible.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a live strain
- **Statement:** two costs, description in bits and action cost, do not add; a settled intervention becomes an atom for the next level; the substantive distribution rule is a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- **What to check:** is the two-currencies rule a claim about accounting, not about moral weighting? Does `gap` follow from the absence of a selector in the six-term set, and not from an insufficient literature search?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single-unit accounting that preserves both readings losslessly and predicts settleability; or derive a unique distributive rule from the six-term set with no added premise. Either fires, C3 falls.
- **Strain, named:** the Operators table defines `⋛` as "a threshold relation between two quantities." Whether two differently-priced readings can be compared in one threshold needs an instrument, not an argument. This is open, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- No distributive rule is derived (no equality, maximin, desert or utility sum).
- Not "the greatest good for the greatest number"; that is an aggregate that can hide a live slot.
- Not that stakeholder agreement is worthless. It is "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41), just not a verdict.
- Not that the precautionary principle is an absolute rule; its strong form is self-cancelling in the world literature, and the figures answer with two accounts instead.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement:

1. A **per-slot outcome table** after a real intervention, showing each party's change against prediction.
2. An **anchor-invariance test**: the grading metric shown not to move when the intervention pushes on it, with an independent measure.
3. A **cascade audit** to second and third order with an instrument named for each link (Fig 3 :27).
4. A **stake register** for every grader, including inaction.
5. A **two-account cost report** (bits and actions) showing which proposals can be settled within budget, which also tests the `⋛` strain.
