# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reviewer has checked F1, F5 and F8 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. The output has to survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about interventions with uneven benefits and harms

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the constraints, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** an intervention into a system where benefits and harms are uneven should be graded per affected party, not by a net total. The reason is structural: a global result near zero is compatible with one party carrying all the harm, so the average hides the only signal that says where to look. A second constraint: the metric used to grade the intervention must not be a metric the intervention can move. If it is, the measure becomes part of the intervention it was supposed to judge, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices.
>
> Three more constraints follow from the same arrangement: re-evaluate who benefits and who is harmed against the current situation rather than fixing a permanent list; enumerate effects outward to second and third order before acting; and declare the stake of everyone who grades the result, including the stake in doing nothing. A stakeholder who gains from a verdict is inside the frame being graded.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming to know which distribution is just. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It gives the form any checkable principle must have and no selector among equality, maximin or a utility sum. I have recorded that as a gap, and a gap is a verdict here, not a silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single aggregate that is a lossless transform of the per-party results, keeping every live party separately visible and settled;
> 2. a grading metric that moves under the intervention and still settles the verdict without coherent drift, confirmed by an independent anchor;
> 3. a fixed classification of who benefits and who is harmed that survives a change of the situation.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source and the derivation are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :33 and :34, Fig 11 :49 to :53, Fig 10 :59 and :60.

>

## Two-stage offer, restated

**Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** any theory is shown.

**Stage 2, opt in only.** If they are curious, send the figure extract and the audit. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Their verdict on stage 1 is the evidence-bearing event.

A Stage 3 that offers the formula as a reusable method is permitted (Fig 4 :22), but each user must write their own refuter and state their own ground. The framework's own limit applies: a loop "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds" (`THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction).
