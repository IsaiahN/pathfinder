# The Tether's answer: ethical principles for uneven interventions

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the individual constraints, `gap` on the substantive distribution rule. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No intervention was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

An intervention is an action by a seat into a habitat. "Benefits and harms are distributed unevenly" means the per-party residual `R_s` has unequal components with at least one live positive slot. The question asks for principles that keep those live slots visible, correctable and settled by a ground. The decision is between (A) naming a distributive rule as the answer, and (B) deriving the form any checkable answer must have and marking the substantive question as a `gap`. This folder takes B.

The residual is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a distributional condition, an intervention condition and a verification condition overlap, and a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :54-57). Verdicts are derived per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Grade per affected party, never by a net total.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global result near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13), not a solved one.
2. **Re-evaluate who benefits and who is harmed against the current residual.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." There is no permanent roster of beneficiaries.
3. **Enumerate the habitat outward before acting.** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Second and third-order contact is in scope.
4. **Treat introduction as permanent maintenance.** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Exit is not neutral: Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
5. **Keep the grading metric unmovable by the intervention.** Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." A metric the intervention controls is part of the intervention, not a verdict about it.
6. **Declare every grading stake, including the stake in inaction.** Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying" and Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." A ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified (Fig 10 :93).
7. **Keep agreement in its own column.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Stakeholder agreement about a shared meaning is a convention (Fig 2 :41), not a verdict about the world.
8. **Report two costs, do not add them.** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A one-line principle can be cheap to state and unaffordable to settle.
9. **Record the ruling's lineage.** Fig 10 :62 "A ruling carries provenance like an atom." So a later reader "can locate the error rather than only feel it" (Fig 10 :63-64).
10. **Name no distributive rule.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :133 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The question of which distribution is just is a `gap` after re-census, not a finding that the framework failed to search.

These ten are **form constraints**. They make an answer checkable; they do not tell you which answer is just.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a normative theory. No equality, maximin, desert or welfare principle is derived, and none is smuggled in as a derivation.
- Not "the greatest good for the greatest number." That is an aggregate and fails constraint 1.
- Not that stakeholder participation is worthless. It is "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41); it is simply not a verdict.
- Not that the precautionary principle is an absolute rule. Its strong form is self-cancelling in the world literature, and the figures answer with two separate cost accounts, not an absolute (F8 of the audit).
- Not that the DDT anecdote proves anything by itself. It is the figure's illustration of cascade order; the documented cases carry the point.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any ground settled these constraints in this run.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A ground reading of a concrete intervention, not a further argument:

- A **per-slot outcome table** after the intervention, showing for each affected party what changed, against the pre-intervention prediction. This settles constraints 1 and 2.
- An **anchor-invariance test**: show that the grading metric did not move when the intervention pushed on it, using an independent measure. This settles constraint 5. Goodhart's law and Campbell's law predict what happens if it moves.
- A **cascade audit** at second and third order, with instruments named for each link. This settles constraint 3 (Fig 3 :27).
- A **stake register** for every grader, including the stake in inaction. This settles constraint 6.
- A **two-account cost report** (bits and actions) showing which proposals can actually be settled within budget. This settles constraint 8 and tests the live strain on `⋛`'s operand type.

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4: a lossless aggregate; a movable metric that still settles; a fixed valence across residuals; a genuine exclusion operator; two high-overlap frames that settle a distributional claim. If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or rewritten.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figures returned a shape and a falsifier and stopped. The substantive distribution rule would require an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), with a source gate, a shadow test and a declared provenance. This folder does not make that import, because importing a moral premise and calling it a derivation would be "a wrong atom" costing "more than starting from primitives" (Fig 6 :24). The lineage above is what makes the stop checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which distribution is just, and it does not offer a moral theory. It tells you what any checkable answer to that question must look like. Grade an intervention per affected party rather than by a net total, because an average hides the one party carrying the harm. Re-evaluate who benefits and who is harmed against the current situation instead of fixing a permanent list. Enumerate effects outward to second and third order, because the residual that matters is often three steps away. Treat the intervention as permanent work, since you can add to a habitat but cannot subtract it. Keep the grading metric unmovable by the intervention and declare the stake of everyone who grades, including the stake in doing nothing. Keep stakeholder agreement separate from the verdict and report description cost and action cost in separate accounts. The framework returns this shape, not a name: which distribution is just remains an open question, with written falsifiers and the instruments that would close it. The center stays open until a ground settles it.
