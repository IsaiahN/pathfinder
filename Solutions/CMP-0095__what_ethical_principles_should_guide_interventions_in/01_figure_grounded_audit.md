## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question is normative, so the first task is to convert "what ethical principles should guide interventions" into an effect that can register against a ground.

An **intervention** is an action `a` taken by a seat into a habitat. It introduces an actor, a rule or a constraint. It does not subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :61). A **distribution** is the vector of per-party residuals `R_s`: what each affected party or object slot expected before `a`, against what happened after `a`. "Distributed unevenly" means the vector has unequal components and at least one live positive slot. It is a statement about the **shape of R**, not about its mean.

So the residual `R` in effect terms is: **a seat must choose `a` such that the live slots in `R_s` can be seen, corrected and settled by a ground, when benefits and harms do not cancel.** The options and what each leads to:

| option | what it produces |
|---|---|
| A: name a distributive rule (maximin, equality, utility sum) as *the* ethical principle | a `general`-mode verdict the figures cannot derive; a single number that hides live slots; a metric that can be moved by the intervention it grades |
| B: derive the shape any checkable principle must have, refuse to name a distribution, state what would settle it | per-slot evaluation, full habitat enumeration, unmovable ground, declared stakes, recorded rulings, and an honest `gap` on the substantive rule |

The answer is bound as an ordinary term before the search, per the formula's "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* The bound term here is **"a checkable principle"**: one that keeps `R_s` per slot, keeps the anchor fixed, declares the seat's stake, and records its own lineage. A candidate principle that fails any of these is refused by the same test as anything else. Without this binding, the search would silently prefer the kind of answer the author already wants, which is the failure Fig 5 :61-64 names.

## 2. Domain census

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** `R_s` is indexed by **many slots** (affected parties, object slots, species, regions). Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The question's word "unevenly" is exactly a claim about the components of this vector. Averaging destroys the only signal the question asks about.
- **Symmetry:** benefits and harms are **not symmetric operands**. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same actor is a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another (Fig 11 :50). There is no standing list of good or bad interventions (Fig 11 :51). So `⋛` (comparison) and `+` (conjunction) cannot be applied as if the parties were interchangeable atoms with fixed signs.
- **Scale:** the intervention is local, but contact cascades. Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Composition is recursive (Fig 12 :103), so a settled effect becomes an operand at the next level. The residual spans at least three orders.

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**Domain 1, intervention and habitat (the action frame).**
- Actors: the seat or maintainer, the introduced actor, the enumerated habitat, the affected parties as slots.
- Currency: **contact and cascade**, which actor touches which, and at what order.
- Anchor: **the ground**, what actually happens after introduction. It is not the designer's intention.
- Question type: **union compound**, not one hard problem. Distributional pattern, intervention dynamics and verification overlap (Fig 9 :54-58: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; unbundle before searching again).
- Figure line: Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

**Domain 2, evaluation and measurement (the residual frame).**
- Actors: object slots, the metric, the evaluator.
- Currency: **residual per slot**, plus two incommensurable costs, description cost in bits and action cost (Fig 12 :125-127).
- Anchor: **the ground as the only metric** (Fig 1 :23), which must not move when pushed on (Fig 2 :33-34).
- Question type: **partition** (which slot carries live mass). The aggregate is a different quantity, not a coarser version of the same one.
- Figure line: Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

**Domain 3, verification and the ethics of the checking seat (the second frame).**
- Actors: checker seats, stakeholders, the maintainer.
- Currency: **agreement and residual**, held apart.
- Anchor: **the ground, never agreement** (Fig 2 :35, :45).
- Question type: **partition** (who may grade the intervention). A stakeholder who benefits from a verdict is inside the frame being graded.
- Figure line: Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"

**Domain 4, substantive distributive content (the forbidden fill).**
- Actors: none currently held. There is no figure term that selects maximin over equality or over a utility sum.
- Currency: none. There is no figure-defined currency in which one distribution is "more just" than another.
- Anchor: none nameable. **No nameable anchor means this is not yet a domain** (master plan, section 2 recipe). It is recorded as `gap` in section 3 F11 and in `09_watson_answer.md`.
- Question type: `gap` after re-census, not a fourth loop to be forced.

### 2.3 Partition or union

Domains 1 to 3 form a **union**: an uneven intervention touches habitat, measurement and verification at once, and their overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-56). Domain 4 is not a member; it is the slot the other three leave open. Treating the four as a single hard question would be the bundling error Fig 9 :54 names. I search the three domains separately and record the fourth as unreachable.

### 2.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Describe the per-slot residual before choosing any frame or intervention (Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
2. Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-44).
3. Fix the ground so it cannot be moved by the intervention (Fig 2 :33-34).
4. Declare the stakes of every seat that may grade the result (Fig 10 :59-60).
5. Only then introduce an actor and read the ground. Habitat contact is at Fig 11 line 21, and the formula's step 5 says: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."

Effort spent at step 5 cannot rescue a missing step 2: Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

### 2.5 Strain test applied

An early reading tried to use Fig 13's "two kinds are in lockstep" as a direct ethical symmetry principle (equal treatment of states and structures). That is a strain: Fig 13 governs the six-term substrate, not the moral standing of parties. Re-census sends it back to Domain 2, where the correct figure is Fig 1's per-slot rule, not Fig 13's lockstep. No figure was forced to carry a quantity it does not govern. The only live strain after re-census is the comparison operator's operand type in F8, which is recorded, not hidden.

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

### F1. Evaluate per slot, never by a net aggregate (decisive for evaluation)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"

An ethical principle that grades an intervention by total benefit minus total harm commits exactly the error Fig 1 names. A global sum near zero is compatible with one party carrying all the harm; the figures call that a **legal state**, not a solved one. The correct form is a vector of per-party residuals with the live slots marked, then a separate decision about each live slot. This is the figure-grounded reason why "the greatest good for the greatest number" is not a sufficient principle here: it is an aggregate that can average away the only signal.

**Refuter:** show an intervention evaluated by a single aggregate that still keeps every positive slot visible, correctable and separately settled against a ground, with the aggregate shown to be a lossless transform of the vector. If such a transform exists, F1 falls.

**Status:** `triangulated`, low overlap between Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 11 (valence relative to residual, no standing list). Both govern evaluation but from different quantities. World corroboration from SEP Distributive Justice: the utilitarian objection that it "fails to take seriously the distinctness of persons." One evidence pool in the world sources on this point (SEP is one witness), so the figure pair carries the verdict.

### F2. No standing list of good or bad actors (decisive for the intervention rule)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A rule of the form "always protect actor X" or "always intervene against Y" is forbidden. Not because it is politically unattractive, but because valence is a relation between an actor and a residual. When the residual changes, the same actor changes sign. The principle must therefore be stated as a procedure for re-evaluating valence against the current residual, not as a permanent roster.

This also rules out a subtle version of "fairness": fixing a party's moral status once and carrying it into a different intervention. The figure says no standing list survives a change of residual.

**Refuter:** derive, inside the framework, a fixed valence for an actor that holds across two different residuals without importing a new primitive. If that is possible, F2 falls.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 1 :18 (support evaluated per slot). Low overlap: one concerns actor identity, the other slot mass.

### F3. Enumerate the habitat; the residual that matters is often three steps out

Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

An ethical principle must require enumeration of direct and indirect contact before the intervention, including second and third-order effects. A principle that evaluates only the intended first-order effect is a first-order enumeration, which is the documented DDT failure. Fig 3 :27 adds the instrument requirement: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

**Refuter:** demonstrate a case where second-order contact cannot in principle be enumerated outward from the residual and the intervention still settles against a ground with all live slots visible. If so, the enumeration requirement is too strong and F3 falls.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 (cascade) and Fig 3 (instrument at the broken link). World corroboration from Perverse incentive (Cobra effect) and from the Precautionary principle, both of which are documented cases of effects contrary to designers' intentions. Overlap between those two world sources is high (both in the unintended-consequences pool), so they count as one witness. The figure pair decides.

### F4. Introduction is available, exclusion is not; an intervention is permanent work

Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

An intervention cannot be "done" and handed over. Because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the designer, the seat can add actors but cannot remove the habitat. The ethical consequence is a duty of continuing maintenance and a prohibition on treating exit or relocation as neutral: isolation substitutes a habitat and inherits unchosen actors. A principle that promises a one-off fix with no maintenance budget is the named failure.

**Refuter:** show a genuine exclusion operator inside the framework, one that removes a habitat rather than substituting one, with a ground reading that confirms the removal. Fig 11 :61 says there is none; if one is produced, F4 falls.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 :61-67 and Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Same figure family but different quantities (asymmetry of introduction vs contact and capability). World corroboration from the Precautionary principle's irreversibility argument. Low overlap between ecology and law/philosophy, so this pair is the strongest world support in the folder.

### F5. The metric that grades the intervention must not move when the intervention pushes on it

Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric."
Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

If "benefit" is defined by a metric the intervention itself controls, the metric becomes another frame. The drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices, and the evaluation is worthless. This is the figure's anchor rule applied to outcome measurement. It forbids defining success in terms the intervention can manufacture.

**Refuter:** exhibit a metric that moves under the intervention and yet still settles a verdict without coherent drift, with a second independent anchor showing the verdict unchanged. If so, F5 falls.

**Status:** `triangulated`, high confidence. Fig 2 (anchor invariance) and Fig 1 (ground as only metric) are different figures with low overlap. World corroboration from Goodhart's law ("Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes.") and Campbell's law ("The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures..."). Those two world sources share the same unintended-consequences evidence pool and cite each other, so they are **one witness**, not two. The figure pair decides.

### F6. Declare stakes; the grader must not benefit from the verdict

Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"
Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." and "an undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."
Fig 10 :93 "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
Fig 10 :61 "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."

For an uneven intervention this is acute: the parties who gain from a distribution are exactly the parties least able to grade it. The framework does not require an impossible disinterested seat (Fig 10 :58 "A seat with no stake at all does not exist."). It requires that the stake be declared and that the grader be outside the slice being graded (Fig 10 :86 "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."). Note the asymmetry: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, so it is not a disqualifier; an interest in the reading is disqualifying, so it is.

**Refuter:** produce a checker who benefits from the verdict, whose stake is undeclared, and whose grading still settles against a ground as confirmed by an independent seat. Fig 10 :93 says the keeper is disqualified; if a case survives, F6 falls.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 10 (seat rules) and Fig 2 (anchor must not be one of the parties, Fig 2 :47). Low overlap. World corroboration from SEP Distributive Justice: "to claim that we should not pursue any changes... is, by its very nature, to take a stand on the distributive justice" of the current distribution. Inaction is also a stance; declare it.

### F7. Stakeholder agreement is not a verdict

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

A participatory process whose participants share one evidence pool produces a convention, not evidence about the distribution. Fig 2 :41 explicitly says agreement about a shared meaning is "efficient for a convention", and that is a legitimate product, but it is not a verdict about the world. The right move is triangulation: frames that draw on different evidence, each ranged separately against the ground.

**Refuter:** show two frames with high evidence overlap whose agreement nonetheless settles a distributional claim against a non-updating ground, with the overlap quantified. If so, F7 falls.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 2 and Fig 7 (two figures with low overlap: static anchor geometry vs sequential room selection).

### F8. Hold two incommensurable costs apart; do not sum them into one benefit score

Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :128-129 "The two readings come apart in both directions. A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute."

Cost-benefit analysis of an intervention typically sums all harms and benefits into one figure. The figures forbid this at the level of composition: bits and actions are in different units, "so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall" (Fig 12 :126-127). The ethical consequence is that a proposal can be cheap to describe and expensive to settle, and a single score will not show which. Both readings must be reported. **Strain, named:** the comparison operator `⋛` is defined in the Operators table as "a threshold relation between two quantities." Whether two incommensurable costs can be compared at all needs an instrument, not an argument. This strain is recorded in `11_audit.md` and is not smoothed.

**Refuter:** exhibit a single-unit accounting that preserves both readings losslessly and is shown to predict which proposals can be settled within budget. If so, F8 falls.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), with a live strain on the operand type. World source: the Precautionary principle article's record of the cost-benefit dispute (Dyson: "You are not allowed to balance costs against benefits when deciding what to do.") is a conflict with naive single-currency framing, not corroboration; it sends us back to the census, which produced the two-currencies rule. The conflict did not flip the verdict.

### F9. Record the ruling's lineage

Fig 10 :62 "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."
Fig 10 :63-64 "in a record the seats below can read. Not so the ruling can be appealed, but so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it."
Fig 10 :65 "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right"

Every decision about an uneven intervention should carry the residual it answered, the reasoning, the date and the declared stake. This is what makes a later correction locatable. It is not an appeal mechanism and it does not make the ruling right.

**Refuter:** show a ruling with no recorded lineage that a later reader can still correct as precisely as one with lineage. If so, F9 falls.

**Status:** `single-source` (Fig 10), consistent with Fig 13 :126-130 "The lineage is the proof." Fig 13 is a second figure on the same quantity but shares the framework's evidence pool, so the agreement is internal consistency, not an independent witness.

### F10. The framework returns a shape, not a distribution

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :133 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

From the cross-kind composition rule (Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one.") one can derive that an intervention must sit in a habitat and that its effects compose, but one cannot derive which of several distributive rules is just. The figures contain no selector for that. This is a `gap`, and it is a verdict, not a silence. It does not license substituting a familiar moral theory and claiming the figures said it.

**Refuter:** derive a unique distributive rule from the six-term set with no import and no added moral premise. That would falsify the `gap`. Short of that, the gap stands.

**Status:** `triangulated` across Fig 9 (shape not answer), Fig 13 (unreachable is a verdict) and Fig 8 (search that finds nothing is not proof of absence). Low overlap. World source: SEP Distributive Justice lists Strict Egalitarianism, the Difference Principle, luck egalitarianism, desert-based, welfare-based, libertarian and feminist principles, with no figure-provided selector. That corroborates the gap rather than filling it.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the master plan and Fig 10's provenance rule. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not an honesty device.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A unique distributive rule is derived from the six-term set with no new moral premise and no import (breaks F10).
2. A single aggregate is shown to be a lossless transform of `R_s`, keeping every live slot separately visible and settled (breaks F1).
3. A metric that moves when the intervention pushes on it is shown to settle a verdict without coherent drift, confirmed by an independent anchor (breaks F5).
4. A fixed actor valence is derived inside the framework that survives a change of residual (breaks F2).
5. A genuine exclusion operator is produced that removes a habitat rather than substituting one (breaks F4).
6. Two high-overlap frames are shown to settle a distributional claim against a ground with quantified overlap (breaks F7).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or rewritten. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State a mode on every principle in F11. Do not deliver a form constraint as a moral verdict (F10, `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule).
2. Begin any intervention review with a per-slot table, not a total. Mark live slots explicitly (F1).
3. Enumerate the habitat to at least second order and record which links have instruments and which do not (F3, Fig 3 :27).
4. Freeze the grading metric before the intervention and check that the intervention cannot move it (F5).
5. Publish the stakes of every grading seat, including the stake in inaction (F6).
6. Keep agreement with stakeholders as a separate column from the ground verdict (F7).
7. Report bits and actions in separate accounts; never collapse them into a single benefit score (F8).
8. Record the ruling's lineage so a later reader can locate the error (F9).
9. Say plainly that the substantive distribution question is `gap`, and name the instruments that would close it (F10, `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 5).

### Honest stop

The figures narrow the **form** of an ethical principle for uneven interventions and rule out several common failures. They do not supply the substantive moral premise, and this derivation does not import one on their behalf. `Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence` (Fig 13 :133). The lineage above is what makes that stop checkable.
