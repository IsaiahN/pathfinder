# The Tether's answer on which probe combination measures the expansion history

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a ranked winner. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question conflates two conditions. "Expansion history" is partly a geometric quantity, the distance-redshift relation measured by supernovae and BAO, and partly a growth quantity, measured by lensing and redshift-space distortions, that discriminates models sharing the same background. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The residual is: which conditions make a combination a good measurer of both, with agreement that is evidence rather than a shared systematics pool.

## 2. The shape

1. **Independence, not probe count.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* A candle, a ruler and a shear draw on different physics; their agreement is triangulation. Two catalogues sharing calibration and pipeline are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48).
2. **The union spans geometry and growth.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A combination measuring only geometry or only growth answers one condition of a union.
3. **Residuals stay per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A single figure of merit is a budget instrument; the diagnostic lives in the per-bin, per-probe residual.
4. **Calibration is an anchor that does not move.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* If a joint fit re-tunes M_B or r_d to reconcile probes, the anchor has become another frame and the drift is coherent (Fig 2 :34).
5. **The probes are isomers, and adding one is not automatically a new axis.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* A new probe adds a term; only a genuinely different way of perceiving expansion adds a new dimension (They're Scanning Books).
6. **Forecasts are filters and substituted habitats.** Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a ranking. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). No claim that SNe + BAO + lensing beats SNe + BAO, or any other ordering.
- Not that more probes is better. Overlapping probes add names, not evidence (Fig 8 :48).
- Not that the Hubble tension is new physics, nor that it is proven drift. Fig 2 :34 names the failure class; only an independent anchor settles the case.
- Not that a tight forecast is a measurement (Fig 11 :70).
- Not grounded. No independent calibration or ground reading was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument and a fixed anchor, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Overlap test:** measure how much the probes draw on the same calibration, pipeline and priors. If the overlap is high, their agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
- **Anchor test:** hold parallax / TRGB / the acoustic angular scale fixed and re-fit. If the result only survives when M_B or r_d floats, the anchor has been moved (Fig 2 :34).
- **Union test:** report geometry and growth separately, per redshift bin. If one condition carries the whole constraint, the combination is answering half the question (Fig 9 :57).
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: correlation counted as corroboration; a single probe collapsing geometry and growth; an averaged statistic that hides a live bin; a drifting anchor declared new physics.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not an exhausted search.** The ranking of named combinations is a `gap`: after a genuine re-census no figure line governs it. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answers returned are the shape every good combination must have and the falsifiers written in advance. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank supernovae, galaxy clustering and gravitational lensing, and it does not claim a final best combination. It says that a combination measures the expansion history well when its probes are independent (different evidence pools and different systematics), when their union spans both the geometry of the distance-redshift relation and the growth of structure, when residuals are kept per redshift bin rather than averaged into one figure of merit, and when calibration is held fixed against an anchor that does not move when the joint fit would prefer otherwise. The probes are isomers over a shared set of atoms, candle, ruler and shear, and their surplus comes from that difference in bonds, not from their count. A forecast can rank combinations for budget but cannot say which is true. What it offers is the shape any good combination must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The ranking stays open until the ground settles it.
