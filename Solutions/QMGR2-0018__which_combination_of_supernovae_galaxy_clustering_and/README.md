## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not which probe list wins a forecast, but what makes a combination *measure the expansion history at all*. In effect terms, let the expansion history H(z), equivalently the distance-redshift relation, be the ground. Each probe is a frame: its model plus calibration predicts an observable, Gamma(b,a); the survey returns o-prime; R = |Gamma(b,a) - o-prime| is indexed by redshift bin s and by probe, never pooled into one global number (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "the best combination is the one with the most probes": leads to the prediction that information adds with count, regardless of whether the probes share systematics.
- Option B, "the best combination is the one whose probes are independent and whose union spans the residual": leads to the prediction that the gain comes from low overlap and from covering both geometry and growth, and that adding a near-duplicate probe adds almost nothing.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* stating the conditions a combination must satisfy to be a good measurer, plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one condition from a failure of another. That shape is the sought-for term; until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is now bound in `01` section 0, so the refuter there can fire on it.

The verdict in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The specific name of the winning combination is a declared `gap`, because the framework returns a shape, not a name.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles which probe combination is best, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of cosmological measurement.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). The cosmology sources in this folder all draw on the same measurement pool, so their agreement counts as one witness.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
