# Probes as isomers, and the order of the combination

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit asks which conditions make a set of probes a good measurer of the expansion history. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are supernovae, clustering and lensing **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, so that their differences are structural rather than incidental; and (2) what is the **dependency order** of the combination, so that effort is not spent above a break.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three probes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- Fig 13 :122-123: **"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms"**
- Fig 8 :50: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

Supernovae, BAO and lensing share the atoms (redshift, distance, growth) but are joined by different physical bonds: a standardized luminosity relation, a comoving standard-ruler peak, a gravitational shear kernel. Under Fig 12 they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their difference is *"decisive in the behaviour"*, because each is vulnerable to a different systematic (calibration for candles, sound-horizon calibration and tracer bias for the ruler, shape noise and baryonic feedback for the shear).

Different routes can compose functionally identical constraints on H(z). Fig 13 :121 and :122 license that two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms. But Fig 8 :50 requires the record to separate convergence from adopted import: two probes that agree because they were calibrated from the same source are not independent confirmations, they are one witness. The isomer claim is therefore:

- **Strong form:** the probes are isomers over a shared atom set, and their union surplus comes from the difference in bonds (Fig 8 :45). Status `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 + Fig 8).
- **Weak form:** the probes share a functional group (Fig 12 :124, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*), namely "a distance-related observable from a survey", with different qualifiers. Status `specified`, single-source.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two probes can be shown to share every systematic and every evidence source, yet still add independent ground-tested reach to the combination, the isomer claim fails and the probes are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49).

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Order:

1. **Perception.** Can the probe read the signal at all, at the required redshift? A lensing shear that cannot be measured below a seeing threshold is not a weak probe, it is a missing percept.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the probe's model have terms for its systematics? A candle without a term for M_B evolution, a ruler without a term for tracer bias, a shear without a term for intrinsic alignments: each is a frame whose vocabulary cannot name what affords it.
3. **Objective.** Can it say what would count as progress on H(z), per bin? A figure of merit that collapses bins to one number cannot (Fig 1 :22).
4. **Planning.** Choose the combination only after 1 to 3 are measured.
5. **Learn and carry.** Settle each probe against the ground; then each becomes an atom for the combination (Fig 12 :104).

The middle link (vocabulary and objective) is the one with no instrument (Fig 3 :24-27). Most effort in probe-combination work lands at step 4, adding another survey, because that is where the measurements already exist.

### Q3 - Competing explanations for why combinations appear to work

| explanation | figure reading | what discriminates it |
|---|---|---|
| "More probes reduce error bars" | Forecast as filter (Fig 9 :60); a budget statement, not a verdict | Does the constraint survive an independent anchor and a systematic swap? |
| "Probes are independent (F2)" | Fig 2 :31, :40; Fig 8 :45 | Measure the overlap of evidence pools and systematics |
| "The union spans both conditions (F3)" | Fig 9 :55, :57 | Test geometry and growth separately, per bin |
| "Calibration drift was mistaken for new physics (F5)" | Fig 2 :34; Fig 10 :24 | Hold M_B and r_d fixed against an independent anchor and re-fit |

These are not four degrees of one explanation. They are different compounds of a union (Fig 9 :57). A single lever that improves the forecast cannot clear the regions where only the later conditions hold.

### Q4 - Bits and actions are two currencies, and they do not add

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A combination's parameter count and covariance matrix price the *description* in bits. The telescope time, calibration campaigns and cross-checks that determine whether it holds price the *actions*. A short combination over costly data (a single supernova sample tied to a long distance-ladder campaign) is cheap to state and expensive to settle; a long combination of many correlated observables is expensive to state and may cost little to refute. Neither reading predicts the other (Fig 12 :128-129). Summing them into a single "best combination" score is the error this names, not the fix (Fig 12 :130-131).

## CONCLUSION

The three probes are **isomers over a shared atom set** in the strong figure sense, with the union surplus coming from the difference in physical bonds. Their combination is **ordered**: perception, vocabulary of systematics, objective per bin, then planning, then carry; and the middle link is the one usually unmeasured. Which combination ranks best is not derivable here: Fig 13 :115, **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** The arrangement space does not run out, so no figure line selects a winner. The honest output is the shape and the order, with the falsifier above.
