## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **Combinations are data-driven, not fixed recipes.** DESI reports different strengths of preference for dynamical dark energy depending on which supernova sample is added (2.5 sigma, 3.5 sigma or 3.9 sigma for Pantheon+, Union3 or DES-SN5YR). That is a live demonstration that the "best" combination depends on the evidence pool and the anchor, which is the figure-grounded claim in F2 and F5. It corroborates the shape, but from the same cosmology pool, so it is one witness.
2. **A probe can disagree with another probe's inference.** Planck notes that its spectra prefer higher lensing amplitudes than base LCDM, *"however, this is not supported by the lensing reconstruction or ... BAO data."* That is Fig 10 :26 (*"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*) and Fig 2 :45 observed in real data: separate frames must be corrected against the ground separately. It corroborates F4 and F8.
3. **Calibration is a live failure channel.** Camarena and Marra show that the supernova absolute magnitude used for the local H0 constraint is not compatible with the M_B needed to fit supernova, BAO and CMB data, and warn against double counting low-redshift supernovae. That is F5 in the literature's own terms: a calibration anchor that drifts when frames negotiate.
4. **A figure of merit is a strategy instrument, not a verdict.** The DETF was established to advise agencies on an observational programme, and its own abstract calls for an *"ambitious observational program"*. By Fig 9 :60 such a programme is a filter for the budget; it cannot hand you the verdict about which combination is true.

No source conflicts with the figure verdict. All six world sources share the cosmology measurement pool, so their agreement is one witness talking (Fig 2 :40), not six independent confirmations. The conflict rule was not triggered.
