## VERDICT (from the figures)

A combination measures the expansion history well when its probes are independent (low overlap, distinct systematics), when their union spans both the geometry of the distance-redshift relation and the growth of structure with residuals kept per redshift bin, and when calibration is held fixed against an anchor that does not update on probe preference. Probes are isomers over a shared atom set, so the surplus comes from differing physical bonds, not from count. A forecast ranks combinations for budget, never for truth. The conditions are kept separate per domain and never averaged. The specific winning combination is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. Mode `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected instrumentation. A growth failure would be sold more supernovae, or a calibration drift would be sold more lenses, and a single figure of merit would be applied to a union that needs two separate checks. In outreach terms an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and reversible.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers in `01` section 4, in particular (1) correlated probes counted as corroboration with a surviving ground-tested gain, (2) one probe collapsing geometry and growth at equal independence, (3) an average that predicts per-bin failure, (4) a drifting anchor declared new physics with no independent discriminator. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that combination quality is fully predicted by probe count would also reverse it. A named winner would require a ground reading, not agreement.
