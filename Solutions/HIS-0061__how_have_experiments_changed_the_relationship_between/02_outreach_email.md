**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until §§4 to 6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have been checked by the recipient. The offer collapses on contact if the shape claim does not survive.

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about experiment and explanation

> Hello,
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation in the history and philosophy of experimental method. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`):** the question "how have experiments changed the relationship between observation, intervention and explanation" is a union of three overlapping conditions, not one trend. Under the framework's own terms, what changed is this:
>
> 1. Observation became a per-slot residual ranged against a reading that does not update on what the explainer thinks. The figures name a measurement and an experiment as anchors that *"does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
> 2. Intervention became a distinct currency. Description is priced in bits; finding out whether a term holds is priced in actions, and the two do not add. So intervention is not a more careful observation.
> 3. Explanation became a settled term with a lineage, checkable by someone who cannot reconstruct it. Blinding and allocation concealment are not etiquette; they keep the verifier outside the construction of the claim.
>
> What I am **not** claiming: that experiments made observation theory-free, that there was a single decisive experiment, or that any particular historical figure or date is derived from the figures. The framework returns a shape, not a chronology. Which experiment came first is a `gap` that I am leaving to historians, not filling in.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this derivation):**
> 1. a repeated intervention leaves the supposed anchor moving with what the frame believes, and the explanation is still settled on that basis;
> 2. description cost and action cost are commensurable in a domain, so a better story buys the standing of a test;
> 3. an instrument is built from a pure description with no prior instrument already returning something, and it registers immediately;
> 4. a checker who can reconstruct the claim is used as the verifier and the agreement is counted as a verdict;
> 5. a settled experimental explanation has no reproducible lineage and later workers do not pay the deriving again.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers, local extract: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [name]

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the answer before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. The figures are the authority, not the summary.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A Track B reply is a conversation, never corroboration. Later agreement is never pooled as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
