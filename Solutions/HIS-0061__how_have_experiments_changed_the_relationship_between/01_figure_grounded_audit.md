## 1. The residual, stated as an effect

The question asks how experiments changed the relation among three things. Stated in effect terms, before any cause is named:

> What used to be settled by comparing an account with what was noticed is now settled by comparing an account with what happened under an act that the account did not choose. Something was inserted between noticing and explaining, and it changed which side of the comparison can be moved by the explainer.

This is not a question about one thing replacing another. It is a question about a **change in a relation**, so the residual is a triple with a changed bond structure. The three terms are:

- **observation**: a reading, o′, of what actually happened (Symbols :30-32);
- **intervention**: the action a that produces the reading, a bet made under a belief (formula step 1: *"bet, act, observe"*);
- **explanation**: a term φ in the library Γ that pays for the residual, *"stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before"* (Fig 5 :43).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula "Direction"): a statement of which bond between these three changed, which currency each is paid in, and what anchor each ranges against. That shape is written down here so it can accrue a residual like anything else. The sought-for shape is not allowed to hide as a preference.

## 2. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 2a. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity: three.** Observation, intervention and explanation. Fig 9 :22 names the describing vocabulary: *"arity · symmetry · scale"*.
- **Symmetry: ordered, not symmetric.** Intervention precedes its observation, and observation precedes the explanation that must answer to it. This is the `→` sequence bond, not `+` conjunction: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :70). Swapping the operands would change the claim.
- **Scale: two, joined by a membrane.** One loop at the scale of a single run (act, observe, revise), and one lineage at the scale of a tradition of instruments and methods. Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The two scales are connected by the rule at Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

### 2b. One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain requires a nameable anchor. Where no anchor can be named, no domain is yet constituted.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | anchor nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the experimental loop (one run) | experimenter, apparatus, the object slot | actions spent and residual paid per slot | o′, the reading that does not update on the account | yes, Fig 2 :29 |
| D2, the instrument lineage (many runs) | successive instruments and proto-instruments | residual from a worse instrument, sharpened | whatever registers at any resolution | yes, Fig 6 :34-35 |
| D3, the explanation or mint frame | candidate terms φ, the library Γ, the bonds | bits (description) on one side, actions (settling) on the other | the ground that settles a term | yes, Fig 12 :125-126 |
| D4, the verification frame (historical claims about experiments) | historians, referees, replicators | agreement and residuals | the documentary and experimental record, which does not move on a reading of it | yes, Fig 2 :45 |

### 2c. Partition or union

This is a **union**, not a partition. The three terms overlap and the overlaps are compounds with their own loops:

- observation ∩ intervention = the produced reading, o′ under action a, a compound whose own loop is the per-slot residual (Fig 1 :11, :19);
- intervention ∩ explanation = the test of a term, where an action pays for a claim (Fig 12 :126);
- observation ∩ explanation = the source problem, where one reading can have several sources (essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"several underlying causes that create identical readings"*).

Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*):

1. an anchor that does not update;
2. an instrument already returning something;
3. an intervention that acts on the object;
4. an observation o′ produced under that intervention;
5. a residual R indexed per slot;
6. a term minted and settled against the anchor;
7. a method carried up the membrane and a head start sent down.

Step 2 must precede step 3: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something"* (Fig 6 :32). Step 7 comes last because *"Settling is necessary for a term to be composed over and it is NOT SUFFICIENT"* (formula step 5).

The question has two possible readings. On the strong reading, experiments changed all three bonds. On the weak reading, experiments only made observation more accurate. The residual decides: accuracy is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60), not a witness for the verdict. The census keeps the union so that one lever cannot pretend to clear all three overlaps.

## 4. Findings, with verbatim lines before conclusions

### F1. The changed bond is the anchor relation

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** (Fig 2 and formula step 1, low overlap between the ground figure and the loop notation).

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :30:

> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :45:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

Fig 2 :46:

> **"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."**

Before intervention, observation and explanation could be two frames in the same closure. Fig 2 :13-15 describes that state: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* and *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."* Fig 2 :18-21 describes the other pre-experimental failure: *"one evidence pool; they never talk, and still agree; so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."*

An intervention inserts a reading from something that does not consult the account. This does not make the account true; it makes the account separately correctable. The relation changed from mutual update or shared pool to triangulation against something neither frame can move. That is the structural content of "experiments changed explanation."

**Refuter:** show a case where a repeated intervention leaves the supposed anchor moving with what the frame believes, and the explanation is nevertheless accepted as settled on the strength of that same intervention. Then Fig 2 :30 fails here and F1 is withdrawn.

World corroboration, secondary: Bogen 2026 (SEP) notes that *"philosophers of experiment had initially emphasized experimentation as epistemically superior to observation because of the capacity for control that it introduced into scientific research."* The control is the anchor, stated in a different field.

### F2. Intervention is a third term with its own currency

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** (Fig 12 currency rule and Fig 5 bargain).

Fig 12 :125:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126:

> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Fig 12 :130:

> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

Fig 5 :43:

> **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."**

Observation is the reading; explanation is a description priced in bits; intervention is an action priced in finding out whether the description holds. Because the currencies do not add, intervention cannot be reduced to a more careful observation, and accuracy of reading cannot be exchanged for cost of settling. The history of experimental method is partly the history of making the action currency visible: a claim that could once be sustained by a good story now has to be paid for by a procedure that could have failed.

Fig 1 :23-27 disqualifies the wrong currency:

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."**
> **"coverage, and compression achieved; predicates minted, and credibility accrued. They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Credibility accrued is the prestige proxy. It is frame-internal and cannot settle an explanation.

**Refuter:** show that description cost and action cost are one commensurable quantity in this domain, so that buying a better story is the same as running a test. That breaks Fig 12 :125-130 and F2 falls.

World corroboration, secondary: the Wikipedia *Experiment* article: *"Experiments provide insight into cause-and-effect by demonstrating what outcome occurs when a particular factor is manipulated."* Manipulation is the action currency; cause-and-effect is what it buys.

### F3. Instruments are improved from worse instruments, not specified from descriptions

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** (Fig 6 instrument rule and the world history of experiment, low overlap).

Fig 6 :32:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 6 :33:

> **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."**

Fig 6 :34-35:

> **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

What experiments changed was not a one-time installation of "the experimental method." It was the opening of a lineage: an instrument that already returns something produces a residual, and that residual sharpens the next instrument. The method crosses up the membrane; the recording of one success does not (Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*).

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument built from a pure description with no prior instrument returning anything, in a domain where the new instrument immediately registers a reading. That breaks Fig 6 :32-35.

World corroboration, secondary and genuinely low overlap: the *History of experiments* page reports that Galileo timed falling balls with a pulsilogon synchronised to the pulse, and that when Lavoisier's products weighed more than his reactants he *"decided to do the experiment again. This time he measured the mass of the air surrounding the experiment as well."* Each is a worse instrument already returning something, refined on its own residual.

### F4. Observation did not become pure; it became per-slot and rangeable

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** (Fig 1, Symbols table, and the SEP entry on theory and observation).

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Symbols :38-40 defines R as:

> **"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."**

The claim that experiments removed the theory from observation is refused. What changed is that the reading could be indexed per slot and held against a ground that does not move. Bogen 2026 (SEP) states: *"All observations and uses of observational evidence are theory laden in this sense."* And: *"the contemporary vantage point that attends to modeling, data processing, and empirical results may suggest a re-unification of observation and intervention under the same epistemological framework."* That is the same union the census found, reached from philosophy of science.

Essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* states: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* An instrument that cannot discriminate sources demonstrates its own inadequacy, so the honesty is to say what it cannot distinguish.

**Refuter:** show a tradition in which a well-indexed reading was still treated as neutral arbitration between theories that disagree about the instrument itself, with no source-discriminating instrument and no acknowledged residual. Then F4 is only a partial change, not a structural one.

### F5. Blinding and random allocation are seat arrangements that keep a verifier from reconstructing the claim

**Mode: `specified`. Triangulated** (Fig 10 and the RCT history, low overlap).

Fig 10 :42:

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Fig 10 :43-44:

> **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking. Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom."**

Fig 9 :51:

> **"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"**

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26:

> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

The historical device of blinding is a channel repair. The RCT page records that Claude Bernard *"recommended that the observer of an experiment should not have knowledge of the hypothesis being tested"*, in contrast to the Enlightenment attitude that *"scientific observation can only be objectively valid when undertaken by a well-educated, informed scientist."* Allocation concealment and randomisation extend the same move: the person who assigns the treatment must not be able to steer who gets it. The figure's reason is not etiquette. A checker who knows the expected answer can reconstruct the claim and therefore cannot verify it.

**Refuter:** show an experimental tradition where the observer knew the hypothesis and the allocation, the ground still paid or refused, and the agreement of the frames was nonetheless used as verdict. Then the seat reading of blinding is wrong.

### F6. The lineage became part of the explanation

**Mode: `specified`. Single-source on the figure side** (Fig 13 and Fig 12 :107-110), corroborated by CONSORT.

Fig 13 :126:

> **"The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."**

Fig 13 :127:

> **"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."**

Fig 13 :130:

> **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."**

Fig 12 :107-110:

> **"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish. Whether that cost must be paid again depends on the substrate: where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."**

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

A reproducible experiment is not a result plus a nice story. The protocol, the preregistration, the allocation sequence and the reporting standard are the lineage, and the lineage is part of what makes the term checkable. The RCT page records the CONSORT statements of 1996, 2001 and 2010 and trial registration from 2005. Those are record structures, not decorations on a discovery.

**Refuter:** show a settled experimental explanation with no reproducible route and no lineage, where later workers did not pay the deriving again. Then the provenance claim is false in this domain.

### F7. Experiments change contact, not the isolated observer

**Mode: `general` on the structural claim. Triangulated** (Fig 11 and the tacit-knowledge essay).

Fig 11 :39:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :42:

> **"The lift move is Cayley's, 1799: separating the forces from the flapping is what made powered flight a problem anyone could work on."**

The experimental method did not make the individual mind sharper in isolation. It changed the contact between a frame and a ground, and it built a habitat of instruments, protocols and replicators around that contact. Cayley's move is the figure's own historical instance: separating the forces from the flapping converted a question about birds into a problem anyone could work on. Essays *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* and *How a Society of Agents Would Work* say the same in other terms, with maximal overlap with the framework author, so they are not independent witnesses.

**Refuter:** show an improvement in experimental reasoning that leaves the frame's contact with the ground exactly as it was and still changes what gets settled. That breaks Fig 11 :40.

Fig 13 :134:

No figure line says which experiment first changed which bond, in what year, or whether intervention or observation was historically prior. The figures return the shape of the change. ## 5. Strain test and re-census

Three readings strained on the first pass. Each went back to the census.

**S1, "experiment is an anchor" versus "experiment is an action."** Fig 2 :29 literally lists *"an experiment"* among anchors, while formula step 1 treats acting as `a`. My first pass wanted to say the figure misnamed the experiment. Re-census: the anchor is the world's response under intervention, o′, and the experiment as a whole is the loop that ranges a frame against it. The figure uses "experiment" as shorthand for the response it yields. Two quantities, one word; no contradiction. **Retracted as a strain and preserved here.** The distinction is load-bearing: if the procedure itself were the anchor, it would move when the frame pushed on it, and Fig 2 :30 would fail.

**S2, "theory precedes its instrument" versus "instruments are improved from worse instruments."** Fig 6 :38 says *"theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to."* Fig 6 :32 says an instrument is improved from a worse one already returning something. Re-census: these are the two reachability instruments of formula step 7, outward and inward, and *"Neither answer tells you much about the other"* (Fig 6 :31). Neptune was predicted before it was seen; the telescope was still sharpened from a worse telescope. No strain after re-census.

**S3, better statistics versus contact.** Statistics improved inference without new physical apparatus. Fig 11 :40 says an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Re-census: statistics changes the channel (Fig 10 :24, :26), and it changes contact in the figure's sense only when it restores a reading that does not update on the frame's own expectations. Allocation concealment is exactly that; a cleverer average over pooled readings is not. This strain is **live and narrow**, with a falsifier: a statistical method that improves a verdict while leaving the anchor movable by the frame. Named, not hidden.

**After re-census: one live narrow strain, one retracted reading, one dissolved apparent conflict.**

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim in this folder is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A repeated intervention leaves the supposed anchor moving with what the frame believes, and the explanation is still settled on the strength of that intervention (breaks F1, Fig 2 :30).
2. Description cost and action cost are commensurable in a domain, so a better story buys the same standing as a test (breaks F2, Fig 12 :125-130).
3. An instrument is built from a pure description with no prior instrument returning anything, and immediately registers (breaks F3, Fig 6 :32-35).
4. A checker who can reconstruct the claim is used as the verifier and the resulting agreement is counted as a verdict (breaks F5, Fig 10 :42).
5. A settled experimental explanation has no reproducible lineage and later workers do not pay the deriving again (breaks F6, Fig 13 :126).
6. An improvement leaves contact with the ground unchanged and still changes what is settled (breaks F7, Fig 11 :40).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is rerun. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape, not a periodization. The three-term union and the anchor change are the result; the chronology is owed to a historian, not manufactured here (F8).
2. Keep the four domain verdicts separate. Never average the accuracy of a reading, the cost of an action, the settling of a term and the soundness of a check (Fig 1 :22).
3. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before any further search, as done in §1, so it can be refused (formula "Direction").
4. Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). A famous experiment is a filter for which case to study, not a witness for the structural claim.
5. Send the generator, not the recording: the residual, the census and the refuter, in that order (Fig 4 :22-23).

## 9. Conclusion (after the refuter, not before it)

**Verdict, from the figures.** Experiments changed the relation among observation, intervention and explanation by inserting a third term with its own currency. Observation became a per-slot residual ranged against a reading that does not update on the account (F1, F4). Intervention became the action that pays for a claim, in a currency that does not add to the currency of description (F2). Explanation became a settled term with a lineage, checkable by a verifier who cannot reconstruct it (F5, F6). The instrument lineage and the habitat of protocols changed contact rather than the isolated mind (F3, F7). Which experiment did this first, and in what order, is a `gap`, a verdict of unreached, not a silence (F8). Mode `specified` per domain, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`. The whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the historical question it reports on.
