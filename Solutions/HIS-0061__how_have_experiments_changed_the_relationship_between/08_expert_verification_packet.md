# Expert verification packet: the Tether on experiment, observation, intervention and explanation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of experimental science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No archival or experimental ground reading is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Experiments changed the bonds among three terms already held, not the arrival of a new faculty. Observation became a per-slot residual ranged against a reading that does not update on the account; intervention became an action priced in a currency that does not add to the price of a description; explanation became a settled term whose lineage is part of the term and whose checker cannot reconstruct it. Instruments are sharpened from worse instruments already returning something, and the verifier must be kept outside the construction. The before and after configurations are isomers over the same atom set. Which experiment came first, and in what order, is a declared `gap`, not a claim.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The anchor relation is the changed bond (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2)

- **Statement:** the reading must not move when the frame pushes on it, and the residual must be indexed per slot, not averaged away.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30 *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** does the figure actually name an experiment as an anchor, and does the per-slot rule govern the reading? Note the precision in `01` S1: the anchor is the response under intervention, not the procedure itself.
- **Refuter:** a repeated intervention leaves the supposed anchor moving with what the frame believes, and the explanation is still settled on that basis.

### C2. Intervention is a distinct currency (physics- and practice-facing; checkable against the history of experimental method)

- **Statement:** description is priced in bits, finding out whether a term holds is priced in actions, and the two do not add. Instruments improve from worse instruments already returning something.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- **What to check:** is there a historical case in which the two prices were genuinely compared in one ledger? If so, the currency claim is false there. Otherwise check the concrete instances: Galileo's pulse-timed instrument and Lavoisier's second weighing of the air.
- **Refuter:** description cost and action cost are commensurable in a domain, so a better story buys the standing of a test; or an instrument is built from a pure description with no prior instrument returning anything and registers immediately.

### C3. The verifier must not reconstruct the claim (framework-internal and historical)

- **Statement:** a checker who knows how the claim was built cannot verify it; blinding and allocation concealment are channel repairs on an anchor that does not decay.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Fig 13 :126 *"The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."*
- **What to check:** does Claude Bernard's recommendation that the observer not know the hypothesis, and the later blinded and concealed-allocation designs, fit this rule better than the Enlightenment attitude that only an informed scientist can observe objectively? Is the protocol part of what makes the term checkable, or only an appendix?
- **Refuter:** a checker who could reconstruct the claim is used as verifier, the agreement is counted as a verdict, and the ground still pays or refuses; or a settled experimental explanation has no reproducible lineage and later workers do not pay the deriving again.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that observation became theory-free.
- Not a chronology, a named "first experiment", or a causal priority among observation, intervention and explanation. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that a famous experiment, a famous experimentalist or an accumulated credibility score has verdict weight (Fig 1 :26, Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the two currencies add.
- Not `grounded`. No archival or measurement check was performed.
- Not a claim that the essays independently confirm the figures: all eight share the framework author's evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

An archival discriminator, not agreement. Dated protocols, instruments, correspondence and reporting standards that show which bond changed and where; a case where the two currencies are actually compared; a falsifier from §6 of `01` that fires. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 12 :125-126 and Fig 10 :42. Three short passages.
2. Check C2 against one experimental episode you know well: was the action priced separately from the description?
3. Check C3 against the history of blinding: did keeping the observer ignorant change what the ground paid, or only how comfortable the observer felt?
4. Name one instrument or archive that would discriminate the chronology the figures refuse to name, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
