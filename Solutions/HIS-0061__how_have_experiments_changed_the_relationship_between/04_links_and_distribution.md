## 5. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for offering, not pushing:

- **Fig 4 :22-23:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before a residual is described sends a recording, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Do not select recipients for likely agreement and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the five refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 6. The two-stage offer

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted, because a generator can be given away without giving away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user still needs a ground and a self-written refuter, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (formula, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. It is recorded as `specified` and ungrounded, with five refuters written in advance. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
