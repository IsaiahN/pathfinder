# Expert verification packet: the Tether on dark matter self-interaction and galactic structure

**What this is:** a claim in a form a dark matter researcher can check. Mode, derivation and
refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would
settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement of `sigma/m` is claimed. This is a shape and
a route, not a physics result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a
ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does dark matter interact with itself, and could that explain the structures seen in
galaxies?" is a union of two conditions that must be kept apart: a microphysical existence claim
and an explanatory-sufficiency claim. They have different grounds, and a lever that clears one can
leave the other standing. Under the six-term substrate a self-interaction between identical
particles is a symmetric bond, so the two operand orderings are one term, and the influence it
carries is the network structure rather than a new primitive. The move from a cross-section to a
halo profile crosses a membrane and loses information, so the round trip gap must be measured. The
framework cannot return a value of `sigma/m`, name a mediator, or adjudicate between
self-interaction and baryonic feedback; those are ground questions for instruments that already
exist and are already failing to resolve at the needed precision.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound, not one yes or no. The structure residual is
  indexed across conditions and must not be averaged into a single "small-scale problem".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping
  conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder
  case of either"*. Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** show that one ground settles both the existence and the sufficiency condition in a
  single step, closing every structure residual without any separate baryonic or environmental
  lever. If that holds, the union reading is false.

### C2 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 4

- **Statement:** a self-interaction between two identical dark matter particles is a symmetric
  bond, so its operand order carries no information; the particle-to-halo mapping is a lossy
  membrane with a measurable round trip gap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :23 - *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"*. Fig 12
  :92 - *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* Fig 4 :21 - *"The
  membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a
  filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it
  you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a self-interaction whose operand order changes its meaning; or show that a
  single `sigma/m` value predicts a unique density profile for all halos regardless of baryons,
  assembly history and scattering velocity, so the round trip gap is zero.

### C3 - physics-facing; checkable by a dark matter researcher against the literature

- **Statement:** the two conditions come apart in the current literature, and the instruments that
  would settle them already exist. A positive cross-section does not by itself close the structure
  residual; a baryonic mechanism can move an inner profile without settling the existence
  question. The reported cross-section bounds and preferred values depend on the probe, and a live
  disagreement between probes is recorded rather than averaged.
- **What to check:** (i) does the missing-satellites count change in SIDM? Bullock and Boylan-Kolchin
  (2017) write that *"SIDM does not, however, significantly alleviate the missing satellites
  problem"*. (ii) Is feedback core formation an ab initio prediction? The same review: *"this is
  not an ab initio LCDM prediction, and it depends on various adopted parameters in galaxy
  formation modeling"*. (iii) Do the probes disagree? Ando et al. (2025) *"decisively prefers CDM to
  SIDM"* for a velocity-independent cross-section above about `0.2 cm²/g`, while Nadler et al.
  (2025) find inner slopes *"sensitive to both the amplitude and shape of the SIDM cross section"*.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a single lever that closes both conditions across all the named
  structure problems; or show that the disagreement between the satellite-kinematics bound and the
  velocity-dependent simulation results comes from a shared evidence pool rather than from
  different probes. The first breaks C1's physics-facing half; the second removes the recorded
  conflict and strengthens the claim, so it is logged rather than treated as a disproof.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a yes or no on dark matter self-interaction. That is a ground question and no instrument was
  run here.
- Not a value of `sigma/m`, not a mediator, not a particle identity. The framework returns a shape,
  not a name.
- Not that SIDM is correct, or that CDM-only is refuted globally. The refusal branch applies only
  in regimes where the collisionless-only term keeps a flat, persistent residual.
- Not a derivation of a density profile from the six-term set. That is the lossy bridge.
- Not that agreement among SIDM simulations is corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a
  verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence
  pool."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a theory of everything. No last room.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*:
an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer, not an
argument, that resolved the case. Here:

1. **Cluster-merger offset:** does the mass peak track the galaxies at the precision required?
   Randall et al. (2008) report `sigma/m < 1.25 cm²/g` at 68 percent confidence.
2. **Velocity dependence:** does the inferred cross-section fall from dwarf to cluster scales, as
   Adhikari et al. (2025) and Nadler et al. (2025) expect, or is the satellite-kinematics preference
   for a low velocity-independent value (Ando et al. 2025) the correct reading?
3. **Baryon/discriminator:** at fixed halo mass, does rotation-curve diversity discriminate a
   self-interaction origin from a feedback origin? The 2017 review shows why the feedback
   prescription matters.
4. **Standing refuters:** those in C1, C2 and C3 above, plus the full list in `01` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-57, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :23, and Fig 4 :21 and :30. Six short passages.
2. Check C3 against your own knowledge: do the four structure problems really come apart the way

3. Name one instrument or analysis that would distinguish a self-interaction core from a
   feedback core in the same halo, or say why none is possible at present.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and the stage-1 verdict is
   recorded before any framework material is shown.
