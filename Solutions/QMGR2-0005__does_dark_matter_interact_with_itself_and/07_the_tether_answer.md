# The Tether's answer: dark matter self-interaction and galactic structure

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated
derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not
a ground reading, and it cannot settle whether dark matter self-interacts.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for two things joined by "and": a microphysical existence claim (A: a dark
matter self-interaction with some `sigma/m`) and an explanatory-sufficiency claim (B: that
interaction closes the gap between collisionless CDM predictions and observed galactic
structure). The residual is a **union compound**, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two
conditions is not a harder case of either"*. The structure side is itself four conditions, and
Fig 1 :19 says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Verdicts are therefore
kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Unbundle before answering.** A and B have different anchors. A positive cross-section can
   fail to explain structure; baryonic feedback can explain part of the structure without settling
   A. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection
   standing (Fig 9 :55-56). The admissible answer is two verdicts plus a characterisation of their
   intersection, not a single yes or no.

2. **A self-interaction is a symmetric bond.** Two identical dark matter particles are
   interchangeable operands, so `DM_1 + DM_2` and `DM_2 + DM_1` are one term (Fig 12 :23, *"this
   bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"*). Influence travelling between particles is the
   network structure under the six-term set (Fig 13 :23-24, *"that entities relate, and influence
   travels"*), not a seventh primitive. The bond's coupling strength is quantitative and is not
   derivable from the six terms.

3. **The particle-to-halo link is a lossy membrane.** Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary
   between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it."* One `sigma/m` number does not return
   one density profile: baryons, assembly history, and the velocity dependence of the cross-section
   all enter. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating
   by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap `R_T` is a measurement,
   not an error to be assumed away.

4. **The framework returns a shape, not a value.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the
   answer itself"*. It cannot output `sigma/m = 0.5 cm²/g`, cannot name a mediator, and cannot pick
   a winner between self-interaction and baryonic feedback. Any such number or name is a binding
   from the physics frame, provenanced as such, not a framework result.

5. **The ground is a measurement with low overlap between probes.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is
   whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
   Cluster-merger lensing offsets, rotation-curve inner slopes and dwarf stellar kinematics are
   candidate anchors. Agreement among simulation frames sharing an evidence pool is not
   corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless
   when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). The live disagreement between the 2025
   velocity-dependent simulation results and the 2025 satellite-kinematics constraint is recorded,
   not averaged.

6. **No final room.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection
   between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space
   that has no last route."* A completed SIDM model would be another room, not the last one.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a yes or no to "does dark matter interact with itself". That is a ground question. The
  framework has no instrument for it and does not manufacture one.
- Not a claim that SIDM is right, or that CDM-only is dead. Where the collisionless-only term keeps
  a flat, persistent residual, a refusal is owed before a mint (Fig 5 :53); where baryons close the
  residual, no refusal and no mint are licensed. Both can hold in different regimes.
- Not a value of `sigma/m`, not a mediator, not a particle identity.
- Not a derivation of a density profile from the six-term set. That is the lossy bridge, and the
  loss has to be measured.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, and no claim here is settled by experiment.
- Not a theory of everything. No last room, no final frame.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A ground reading, not further agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument
that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer that resolved the case.
Here the corresponding instruments are already returning readings that fail to resolve:

- **Cross-section anchor:** does a cluster merger such as 1E 0657-56 return an offset between the
  mass peak and the galaxy centroid at the precision needed? Randall et al. (2008) report
  `sigma/m < 1.25 cm²/g` at 68 percent confidence from this method.
- **Velocity-dependence discriminator:** does the inferred cross-section fall from dwarf to
  cluster scales? Adhikari et al. (2025) and Nadler et al. (2025) treat a mild velocity dependence
  as the shape a successful model must have; Ando et al. (2025) prefer CDM for a velocity-independent
  cross-section above about `0.2 cm²/g`. These are different probes and are kept separate.
- **Source discriminator:** can baryonic feedback and self-interaction be distinguished in the
  same halo, for example by rotation-curve diversity at fixed halo mass or by inner slopes across
  a mass range where feedback efficiency changes? The 2017 Bullock and Boylan-Kolchin review shows
  why this is hard: feedback core formation *"is not an ab initio LCDM prediction, and it depends on
  various adopted parameters in galaxy formation modeling"*.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §4:** one ground settling both conditions; a lossless bridge with
  `R_T = 0`; a numerical cross-section derived from the six terms alone; an operand-order-sensitive
  self-interaction; or agreement from a shared evidence pool counting as a verdict.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The instruments exist and are already returning readings that fail
to resolve at the required precision. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof
of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, so if no reading
existed at any resolution, the framework would record that as a verdict too. It does not do so
here. The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (They're
Scanning Books, 2026); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the
limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility
is not identity."*

> merger offsets, rotation-curve diversity and dwarf kinematics, with the live disagreements
> between them recorded. Nothing here is settled, and this derivation is a simulation checked
