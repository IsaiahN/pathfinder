# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker can see the residual and the refuter without
first seeing the framework. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the output is not
standing on its own.

---

**Subject:** One separated shape claim on dark matter self-interaction, with a refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the
> output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "does dark matter interact with
> itself, and could that explain the structures seen in galaxies?" is not one yes or no. It is a
> union of two conditions that can come apart:
>
> 1. A microphysical existence condition: a non-gravitational self-interaction with some
>    cross-section per unit mass `sigma/m`.
> 2. An explanatory-sufficiency condition: that this interaction closes the gap between
>    collisionless cold dark matter predictions and observed galactic structure.
>
> A can hold and B fail, for example a cross-section too small or with the wrong velocity
> dependence to move a halo. B can be partly met and A undecided, for example stellar feedback
> lowering an inner density with no non-gravitational dark matter physics. The shape claim is
> that these must be separated and that the bridge between them is lossy: one `sigma/m` number
> does not fix one halo profile, because baryons, assembly history and velocity dependence all
> enter what the coarse description drops.
>
> **What the figures actually license here.** Under the six-term substrate, a self-interaction
> between two identical particles is a symmetric bond, so the two orderings are one term, not two.
> Influence travelling between particles is the network structure, not a new primitive. The
> framework returns a shape, not a value: it cannot produce a number for `sigma/m`. That number is
> owed to a ground reading, not to an argument.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before writing the conclusion:
>
> 1. One measurement settles both conditions at once and closes every structure residual without
>    any separate baryonic or environmental lever.
> 2. A single `sigma/m` value predicts a unique halo density profile for all halos regardless of
>    baryons, assembly history and scattering velocity, so the lossy bridge is actually lossless.
> 3. A unique numerical cross-section is derived from the six-term set and its bonds with no
>    measurement and no imported particle parameter.
> 4. A dark matter self-interaction is found whose operand order changes its meaning, or two SIDM
>    models differ by a new primitive rather than a new bond parameter.
> 5. Agreement among simulation frames sharing one evidence pool is shown to settle a ground
>    question.
>
> If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails
> because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is
> needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them,
> are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know
> well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run
> it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the
> direction of its own question, so an unwritten refuter leaves the preference invisible.
>
> - [your name]
