## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the compound question: A, does dark matter have
a non-gravitational self-interaction with cross-section per unit mass greater than zero; and B,
could that interaction explain the structures seen in galaxies. Option A1: answer a single yes or
no and assert a cross-section or a model. Option B1: keep the two conditions and their grounds
separate, characterise the lossy bridge, and record what an instrument would have to return. What
each leads to: A1 aggregates four structure conditions into one, spends the independence of the
people checking it, and states a numerical or named result the framework cannot derive; B1 sends
a checkable shape, five refuters written in advance, and an open slot until the ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor;
partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between collisionless CDM halo predictions and observed galactic
  structure, plus the gap between a particle cross-section and a kiloparsec profile. Arity: two
  conditions in the question (A and B) and four in the structure residual (core-cusp, diversity,
  missing satellites, too-big-to-fail). Symmetry: the self-interaction bond is symmetric because
  its operands are identical; the cross-section is a quantitative coupling, not a second bond
  type. Scale: particle scale to galactic scale, so a membrane crossing. Fig 9 names a union
  compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, dark-sector microphysics.** Actors: dark matter particles, the self-interaction bond.
  Currency: `sigma/m`, momentum and energy transfer. Anchor: a cross-section bound from a cluster
  merger or lensing measurement, a nameable anchor. Question type: existence (condition A).
- **Domain 2, galactic structure and observation.** Actors: halos, subhalos, baryons, dwarf
  kinematics, rotation curves. Currency: inner slope, core radius, circular velocity. Anchor:
  rotation curves, lensing maps, stellar kinematics. Question type: sufficiency (condition B).
- **D3, the cross-scale bridge.** Actors: N-body and hydrodynamical simulations, Jeans-based
  semi-analytic models. Currency: a predicted profile returned for a given cross-section. Anchor:
  borrowed from Domains 1 and 2; no independent anchor. Recorded as a frame, not a domain.
- **D4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers and submission venues. Currency:
  agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats
  draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Partition or union:** union. Domains 1 and 2 are partitioned for settlement because their
  anchors differ, but the question asks for their intersection, and the structure residual is
  itself a union of four overlapping conditions. One lever clears one region and leaves the
  intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency:** perceive the structure discrepancy, acquire vocabulary for cross-section
  and inner slope, state what would count as progress (the broken link), range a D3 frame against
  the D2 ground, carry the method forward. Settle A before asserting B, but keep B's verdict
  separate because a negative A does not close B.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any numerical or named answer does not. The question is a union compound whose
two conditions have different grounds, so A and B are judged separately and the four structure
conditions are never averaged. A self-interaction between identical particles is a symmetric bond
and its influence is the network structure under the six-term set. The particle-to-halo move is a
lossy membrane whose round trip gap must be measured. The framework cannot return a value of
`sigma/m`, name a mediator, or decide between self-interaction and baryonic feedback; those are
ground questions for instruments that already exist and are already failing to resolve at the
required precision. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. A single yes or no would
aggregate the slots, state a name where the framework returns a shape, and spend the independence
of the people asked to check it.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, keeps the two conditions
  separate and carries five refuters written in advance, so an error is findable rather than
  invisible. The real cost is spending a checker's independence by sending a recording or a model
  preference before the shape claim and its refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in
  proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and
  the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape and the five refuters are statements, not irreversible claims. A
  ground reading updates the open slot without withdrawing the separation of conditions or the
  membrane claim. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent packet can be superseded by a
  corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A cross-section measurement from a probe
  already returning something that fails to resolve would settle condition A; a discriminator
  separating self-interaction cores from feedback cores in the same halo would settle condition B;
  a single lever closing both conditions at once would break the union reading; a lossless bridge
  with `R_T = 0` would break the membrane claim; a numerical cross-section derived from the six
  terms alone would break the shape-not-a-name limit; an operand-order-sensitive self-interaction
  would break the symmetric-bond claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
