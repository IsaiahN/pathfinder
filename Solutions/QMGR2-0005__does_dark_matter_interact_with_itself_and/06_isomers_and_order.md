## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Bond type and operand order

Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."* Fig 12 :23: *"this bond is
symmetric, so the four are one term"*. Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an
arrangement is a new thing."*

A dark matter particle scattering off another dark matter particle has two operands of the same
kind. The test for a symmetric bond is Fig 12 :59, *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change?
No."* Swapping two identical particles does not change a scattering cross-section. Therefore:

- `DM_1 + DM_2` and `DM_2 + DM_1` are **one term**, not two candidates.
- A search that enumerates both orderings overcharges by `log(k!)`, the error Fig 12 :92-93 names.
- The bond is not subtraction (the particle is not removed or absent) and not sequence (one does
  not need the other to have happened first). It is a symmetric conjunction of two co-present
  operands with a quantitative coupling.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a dark matter self-interaction is found whose operand
order changes its meaning, or if a model requires a new atom rather than a new bond parameter,
this bond reading fails.

### Q2 - What order governs the bridge

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 12 :72: sequence is
*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can
express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*.

The particle-to-halo move is **not** a temporal sequence. A cross-section does not "happen before"
a density profile in the sense of `→`; both are properties of the same halo at the same time. The
order is dependency: the vocabulary for `sigma/m` and for an inner slope must exist before the
objective "what would count as explaining" can be stated, and that objective must be stated before
a probe can be designed. Fig 3 :25-26: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to
say what would count as progress, has neither"*.

Nor is the move a comparison `⋛`. My first pass mapped `sigma/m` onto comparison, an operator
defined as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :86). Strain test: the mapping
forces a rate coefficient into a larger-than relation and governs another quantity. Retracted; see
`01` §6. The comparison that *is* legitimate is between two measured profiles or two cross-section
bounds, at the ground, not between a parameter and a profile inside the bridge.

**Falsifier:** show that the bridge is genuinely a sequence in which the cross-section must occur
before the profile in time, such that reversing the order produces a different physical object.
If so, `→` is correct and the dependency reading fails.

### Q3 - Isomer or functional group

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A
substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which
member."*

The candidate SIDM models (velocity-independent, velocity-dependent, light-mediator, composite)
do not differ in bond *type*. All use the same symmetric scattering bond; they differ in the
functional form and strength of its coupling. By Fig 12 :117 they are therefore not isomers in the
strict sense, because the bond has not changed kind. They are members of a **functional group**:
a shared scattering substructure with different qualifiers. The claim worth testing is the
stronger one: if two models could be written over a fully characterised shared atom set with
different bond arrangements, they would be isomers, and the difference would be decisive in halo
behaviour. Since the dark matter particle itself is uncharacterised, the weaker reading is the
honest one.

**Falsifier:** exhibit two SIDM models whose difference requires a different bond *type* under the
seven tests of Fig 12, with the atom set held fixed. Then the isomer claim holds; otherwise the
functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - Catalyst and cycle analogues

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave
differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output
re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by
construction."*

Both analogues are live in the physics record and both are used carefully:

- In an elastic `2 to 2` self-interaction the particles survive their own scattering. That is
  catalyst-like: the same atoms act again. The caution in the figure is that a term naming only
  the particles does not record whether they are spent or regenerating, so a model must state
  which it assumes.
- Gravothermal evolution is cycle-like with a sign. Heat transport first produces a core; later
  the core can collapse, re-feeding the inner region. The Randall et al. (2008) abstract notes that
  core *"evaporation"* and collapse are distinct regimes, and the 2025 SIDM Concerto results report
  that the core-collapsed fraction peaks where the cross-section changes scaling. The sign of the
  loop decides whether it closes. This is an analogue from the figure, not a derivation of the
  halo evolution, and it is marked `specified`.

**Falsifier:** show that a self-scattering dark matter particle is consumed by the interaction, or
that gravothermal core formation and collapse are unrelated one-way processes with no re-entrant
loop. Either breaks the analogue.

### Q5 - Refuse before mint

Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. `THE_FORMULA.md`
step 2 routes a residual whose content is that a held regularity is false to a **refusal**, not an
explanation. Negation is unary and is not a bond (Fig 12, amendment: *"a relation that fails all
six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet"*, and the Operators
table amendment: negation *"joins nothing"*).

For this question the held term is collisionless-only CDM at galactic centres. In the regimes
where the structure residual persists and does not trend down under added baryonic physics, the
figure route is REFUTED for that term and a refusal is owed before any SIDM term is minted. In the
regimes where baryonic feedback closes the residual, no refusal is owed and no mint is licensed.
Because the question is a union compound, both branch outcomes can coexist across different
halo masses. This is why a single global verdict is inadmissible.

**Falsifier:** show that a refusal can be issued without a unary negation operator, or that the
collisionless-only term persists with a residual that trends down under every baryonic model. The
first breaks the figure's operator claim; the second removes the refusal branch here.

## CONCLUSION

The self-interaction is a symmetric bond over interchangeable operands; its operand order carries
no information. The bridge is a dependency order, not a temporal sequence and not a comparison.
The competing SIDM models are a functional group of one scattering substructure, not strict
isomers, because the bond *type* is shared; the isomer claim would require a fixed, characterised
atom set that the physics does not yet provide. Catalyst and cycle are real but limited analogues
from Fig 12, each with its own falsifier. The refusal branch of the formula applies only in those
structure regimes where the collisionless-only term keeps a flat, persistent residual, which is a
further reason to keep the four conditions separate.

No new primitive is introduced anywhere in this derivation. The one strain is inherited from
`01` §6: Fig 12's bond vocabulary is discrete and typed, while the physical self-interaction is
given by a continuous cross-section function. The figure supplies symmetry and type, not the law.
