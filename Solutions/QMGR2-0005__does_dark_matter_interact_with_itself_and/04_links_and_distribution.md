## 3. What in the wider literature bears directly on this question

1. **The unbundling is already in the field's own practice.** Tulin and Yu (2018) list four small-scale problems separately and note that SIDM leaves large-scale success intact. Bullock and Boylan-Kolchin (2017) show that the missing-satellites count is almost unchanged in SIDM. One lever clears one region (Fig 9 :55-56), so the four conditions must be kept as four lines.

2. **The field has already re-censused once.** The early constant-cross-section picture was constrained by cluster mergers (Randall et al. 2008: `sigma/m < 1.25 cm²/g`) and by strong-lensing ellipticity. The modern picture is velocity-dependent: dwarf scales prefer larger cross-sections, cluster scales smaller ones (Tulin and Yu 2018; Adhikari et al. 2025; Nadler et al. 2025). That is a re-census in the figure's sense: the earlier reading was strained by a quantity it did not govern, so the community changed the quantity, not the verdict by vote.

3. **A live conflict is recorded, not smoothed.** Ando et al. (2025) prefer CDM to a velocity-independent SIDM cross-section above about `0.2 cm²/g` from Milky Way satellite kinematics. Nadler et al. (2025) find that successful models must produce *diverse* profiles, including core collapse in low-mass halos. These are different regimes and different probes. By Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"*, the disagreement is information. It is not resolved here.

4. **The baryonic alternative is a real second lever.** Bullock and Boylan-Kolchin (2017): feedback-induced cores depend on stellar mass and admitted galaxy-formation parameters, and are *"not an ab initio LCDM prediction"*. This keeps condition B's verdict separate from A's.

5. **The metamerism point applies.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."* A rotation curve reads a mass distribution; it does not by itself name the microphysical source of a core. Both baryonic feedback and dark matter self-interaction can produce similar inner profiles. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a name asserted from a projection.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a checker has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before offering the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A send selected for people already inclined to SIDM produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 1 :19** - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Ask about the four structure conditions separately, not about "the small-scale problem" as one blob.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two conditions and the lossy-bridge claim; (2) the five refuters, written in advance; (3) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (4) only then, if they are curious, the three local framework links, with the note that the figures and not the summary are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 5. The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the shape claim without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 again: the formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The output here is a shape claim with five refuters, not a numerical SIDM result. It makes no prediction that a physicist could not check without ever seeing the Tether.
