# Expert verification packet: the Tether on culture and attention

**What this is:** one claim in a form a researcher in attention or cultural cognition can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on structure, `specified` on the channel, `gap` on the named practice. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of any population is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Attention is an allocation of residual over object slots, not one global dial. What a perceiver brings to each slot is a belief mixed from lived history and inherited prior through a weighting `w` that learning can move. Two perceivers with identical architecture can therefore notice and ignore different things without any difference in perceptual hardware. What culture hands down is a head start, a vocabulary of what things afford and a sense of what counts as progress, and that is where "important" lives. The ground, not agreement inside a culture, decides which weightings fit the task. The framework returns that shape, not the name of the practice responsible. It states in advance what would refute it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** attention is per-slot and non-aggregatable; belief mixes history and inherited prior via `w`, so identical architecture with different histories and `w` diverges.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Symbols table :20-21 *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."* Symbols table :26-27 *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*
- **Refuter:** two perceivers matched on architecture, history and `w` who still attend to different slots under the same task; or a stable global attention difference with no per-slot residual anywhere.

### C2. Frame-facing; checkable by an attention or cultural-cognition researcher against their own data

- **Statement:** "important" is the vocabulary/objective link, and culture transmits a head start rather than a recording or a new primitive. If the objective shifts, the slot allocation should shift with the scene held fixed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :7 *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*; Fig 3 :10 *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*; Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*; Fig 4 :24 *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*
- **What to check:** in a matched-task or instruction-shift paradigm, does changing what counts as progress move which scene elements are fixated or reported, with the stimulus unchanged? Does any reported cultural effect require a new perceptual primitive, or is an affordance-vocabulary difference sufficient? Note the genuine conflict in the literature: Park and Huang (2010) report cultural differences in attention and neural function but also replication failures for some gaze measures and state that some effects were *"processing biases rather than immutable circuitry changes"* and were overridden by instructions to attend to the object.
- **Refuter:** an importance weighting that survives removal of all terms for what things afford; or a transmission in which the child's allocation is a replay of the parent's with no new term formed.

### C3. Framework-internal derivation, with a ground consequence

- **Statement:** the ground, not agreement, decides which weightings fit, so a cultural consensus cannot serve as a verdict, and the specific practice is not nameable from the figures.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (the full line begins "disqualifying for a verdict:"). Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
- **Refuter:** a within-group agreement with no external anchor that changes the task's success measure; or a figure derivation that names a specific practice as the unique cause without a ground reading or a provenanced import.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that culture rewires perception or adds a slot.
- Not that all members of a culture attend alike. `w` is per-frame and learnable.
- Not that one culture's weighting is the universal baseline.
- Not a name for the practice responsible. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No population was measured.
- Not corroborated by agreement, prestige, or sample size. Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Instruction-shift test:** change the objective with the scene fixed; does the slot allocation move?
2. **Matched-`w` test:** hold inherited prior and vocabulary constant; does a residual cultural difference survive?
3. **Vocabulary-removal test:** remove the affordance terms; does the importance weighting survive?
4. **Isomer test:** hold atoms fixed, vary only the arrangement; does behaviour change without hardware change?
