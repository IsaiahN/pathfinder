# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 are copied verbatim into the message. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on structure, `specified` on the channel, not `grounded`):** attention is not one global dial. It is an allocation of residual over object slots. What a person brings to each slot is a belief mixed from their own history and what they inherited, with a weighting that learning can move. Two people with the same perceptual architecture can therefore notice and ignore different things without any difference in hardware. What a culture hands down is a head start, a vocabulary of what things afford and a sense of what counts as progress, and that is where "important" lives. The ground, not agreement inside the culture, decides which weightings fit the task.
>
> This is a shape, not a name. It does not say which practice, in which population, moves which slot. It says what any answer must look like and what would refute it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that culture rewires perception or adds a slot; that everyone in a culture attends alike; that one culture's weighting is the universal baseline; or that a cultural consensus makes a weighting right. I am not claiming the framework settles the question. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a set of figures, not a measurement of any population.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before sending this:**
> 1. a difference that survives when inherited prior and vocabulary are matched and the objective is held constant;
> 2. two perceivers matched on architecture, history and weighting who still attend to different slots under the same task;
> 3. an "importance" weighting that survives the removal of all terms for what things afford;
> 4. a transmission that installs a new perceptual primitive, or a replay of the parent's allocation with no new term formed in the child;
> 5. a within-group agreement with no external anchor that changes the task's success measure.
>
> If any one holds, the channel claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I can send the figure text extract and the canonical usage note, or point you to the public sources below.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** it is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Record the verdict before stage 2

Stage 1 is the only step whose result counts as a check. Record the recipient's verdict on the five refuters before showing any theory. Their later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they share the evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). A Track B style reply, if one is ever used, is a conversation and carries zero verdict weight.
