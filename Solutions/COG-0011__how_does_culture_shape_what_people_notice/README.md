**Question:** How does culture shape what people notice, ignore, or treat as important in their surroundings?

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

**Field for public contact points:** attention and cultural cognition research.

## The question restated as an effect

Two options are compared, and the choice is about what each leads to.

- **Option A:** culture changes the perceiver's built-in perceptual architecture, that is, the slots or sensors themselves. This leads to permanent, instruction-resistant differences and requires a hardware-level account.
- **Option B:** culture changes the weighting `w` and the vocabulary/objective layered over a shared architecture. This leads to instruction-sensitive, context-dependent differences that need no new perceptual primitive.

An answer would look like a specification of the channel: which quantity culture moves, what it does not move, and what instrument would settle the residual. Bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): **"a cultural difference in attention is a difference in `w` and in the inherited vocabulary, ranging against the same ground."** This term is written down before the run so it can accrue a residual and be refused.
