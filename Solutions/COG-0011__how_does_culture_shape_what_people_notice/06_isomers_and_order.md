# What is shared, what is arranged, and in what order

**Mode: `general` on structure, `specified` on the cultural-attention application.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit left one supporting derivation open. If culture changes `w` and the transmitted vocabulary over a shared substrate (F2, F3), then two questions follow:

1. What is *shared* across perceivers, and what is *arranged* differently? Are two cultural attentional profiles "isomers" under Fig 12, or only members of a functional group?
2. In what *order* must the links be settled? Culture cannot act on a link whose input never arrives.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the substrate of Fig 13 (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures) plus the loop quantities `Γ`, `b`, `w`, `a`, `o'`, `R`, `s`, and the vocabulary terms for affordances and objective. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

- Domain A, the perceiver's loop (D1 of the audit): actors = perceiver, slots, bet, observation. Currency = per-slot residual. Anchor = the environment.
- Domain B, the inherited backdrop (D2): actors = population, library, prior, `w`. Currency = head starts. Anchor = the ground contacted by practice.
- Order by dependency (Fig 3): perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are two cultural attentional profiles isomers?

Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :124 *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- Written at the level of the **shared substrate** (the same slots, the same six-term set, the same `w` mechanism), two attentional profiles share every atom. They differ only in which slot is bonded to which affordance term and which objective is composed. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different observable behaviour. This is the strong and checkable form.
- Written at the level of **specific content** (a particular practice in a particular population), the profiles may share nothing but the loop itself. They are then only members of a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): the group says "a per-slot residual with a transmitted objective", the qualifier says whose.
- The isomer claim is the one worth testing, because it makes a prediction: if the atoms are held fixed, changing only the bonding (the vocabulary/objective arrangement) should change the allocation pattern without changing the perceptual hardware.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two profiles differ by an atom the other does not have (one requires a slot or primitive the other lacks), they are not isomers and the functional-group reading stands. If the allocation pattern changes with no change in bonding and no change in `w`, the arrangement claim fails.

### Q2. What is the order of the union?

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Fig 3 :22 *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*

The dependency order for this question is fixed:

1. **Perception.** Can the slots be told apart, and told apart from what was there before? If not, there is nothing for culture to weight.
2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for what the slots afford? This is where the inherited head start enters (Fig 4 :22).
3. **Objective.** Can the perceiver say what counts as progress here, in its own terms? This is what "important" names.
4. **Planning and carry.** Only then does a route to the objective and the naming of what worked become possible.

A culture cannot install an objective for a slot it has no term for. If a cross-cultural study reports an "importance" difference without measuring the vocabulary link, it is reading below the break: per Fig 3 :23 *"a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

**Falsifier:** an importance difference that is stable when the vocabulary link is removed and the perception link is held constant. That breaks the dependency order.

### Q3. Which bond carries the cultural transmission?

Fig 12 :71 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (sequence →), and Operators table :34 *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be."*

Fig 4 :24 *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*

The tempting move is to write cultural transmission as a **sequence**: parent notices X, therefore child notices X. The operators table warns that a flat chain writes everything as a sequence whether it should or not. Fig 4 forbids the replay reading: what crosses down is a head start, not the recording of one occasion. So the transmission is better read as a **production** bond (⇒, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :77): the parent's practice produces a vocabulary in the child that did not exist there before, and the child still has to run its own loop against the ground.

**Falsifier:** a transmission in which the child's allocation is a replay of the parent's with no new term formed and no contact of its own. That would make it a recording, not a head start, and break Fig 4 :24.

### Q4. Does this need a new primitive?

Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*

Fig 13 :118 *"infinite effort inside a finite reach."*

No new primitive is needed. The difference between two cultural attentional profiles is an arrangement over a closed set of loop quantities. Searching harder inside one culture's closure will not manufacture the other's arrangement; that needs contact with a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*). This is why the outreach is to outside frames, not to more of the same frame.

**Falsifier:** a demonstrated need for a seventh substrate term, or a difference that no arrangement of the held terms can express. That would break Fig 13's closure claim.

## CONCLUSION

Two cultural attentional profiles are **isomers over the shared loop vocabulary** when their atoms are held fixed and only the bonding of slots to affordance terms and objectives differs. They are merely a **functional group** when their content is compared instead. The order of the union is fixed by Fig 3: perception, then vocabulary, then objective, then planning and carry. Cultural transmission is a production bond (a head start that forms a new term in the child), not a replay sequence. Nothing in this derivation requires a new primitive; it is a `specified`, ungrounded structural claim with four written falsifiers.
