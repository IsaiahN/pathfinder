## Independent domain census, written from the residual line alone

I redid the census from the residual line in `01` section 0 before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a prediction and an observation at an object slot in the surroundings. Arity: many, one R per slot. Symmetry: not aggregatable; a global near-zero with one live slot is a legal state and the average would hide the live slot. Scale: two, one loop inside a perceiver and one across perceivers. This is not one dial, so any answer that speaks of "how much a culture notices" is already the wrong shape.
- **Domain 1, individual perception and attention.** Actors: perceiver, slots, bet, observation. Currency: per-slot residual and boundary diff. Anchor: the environment and task that actualise o′. Nameable. Question type: partition at the boundary between broken and novel.
- **Domain 2, cultural inheritance.** Actors: population, library, inherited prior, `w`. Currency: head starts. Anchor: the ground contacted by practice. Nameable. Question type: how a frame's weighting and vocabulary change without a new primitive.
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: experimenters, instructions, measurements. Currency: residual under instruction shift. Anchor: measured attention, never agreement. Nameable. In this simulated run its independence is zero.
- **Union, not average.** All three conditions bind at once. A cultural difference in attention is not three answers to be blended; it is one compound with its own loop, and each domain keeps its own verdict.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry. I derived this order from Fig 3 :20 and :22, whereas the primary reached it through Fig 3 :7 and :10. Different starting lines, same boundaries.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per-slot residual), the Symbols table (belief and `w`), Fig 3 :7 and :10 (vocabulary and objective), Fig 4 :22 (head starts), and Fig 2 :29 and :45 (the anchor).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: attention is a per-slot allocation; belief mixes history and inherited prior; culture transmits a head start; the ground decides.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim a reader would take away, "this cultural group notices X because of practice Y".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the live slot** for any particular scene, which the abstract form does not carry;
- the **value of `w`** for any particular person, which is learnable and per-frame;
- the **specific practice** that produced the inherited vocabulary, which is a name where the framework returns a shape;
- the **source discrimination** between several practices that could produce the same allocation, which needs a better instrument (the metamerism point from *The Hard Problem is About Source*).

Conversely, three things survive cleanly: per-slot indexing, the `w` mechanism, and the ground-not-agreement rule. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the eight-step loop is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
