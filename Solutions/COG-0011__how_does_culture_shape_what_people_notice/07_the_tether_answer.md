# The Tether's answer: how culture shapes what people notice, ignore, and treat as important

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the transmission channel, `gap` on the named practice. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

People in different surroundings allocate the same finite attention differently over the same kind of scene. R is per slot: the gap between what the perceiver's belief predicts for that slot and what observation delivers. "Notice" is a slot with positive residual; "ignore" is a slot already predicted; "important" is the slot bound into the objective, that is, into what counts as progress here. The question asks what culture moves. Two options were compared in `README.md`: culture changes the substrate (A), or culture changes the weighting and vocabulary over a shared substrate (B). This answer takes B, with the mode and refuters attached.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

**D1, individual perception and attention.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* and Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Attention is not one dial. Culture changes the *pattern* of per-slot residual by changing the prediction, not by changing the number or kind of slots. **Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 1 plus the arity/symmetry description of R).

**D2, cultural inheritance.** Symbols table :26-27 *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* and Symbols table :25 *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* The belief that enters the prediction is a mixture of lived history and inherited prior; `w` sets the mixture and is learnable from outcomes. Cultural difference therefore needs no different hardware. What is handed down is a head start: Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* **Verdict: `general` for the mechanism, `specified` for this application, triangulated** (Symbols table + Fig 4 + Fig 11 :39, low overlap).

**D3, verification.** Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* and Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* "Important" is settled by an anchor the culture cannot talk into moving: task success, prediction, an experiment. Within-culture agreement is a convention, not a verdict (Fig 2 :40). **Verdict: `general`, triangulated.**

**Union, not average.** The three domains are conditions of one compound, not three independent answers to be blended. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). Keeping them separate is what prevents the collapse "culture changes attention" from hiding three different claims.

**Order.** Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Perception, then vocabulary (terms for what things afford), then objective (what counts as progress), then planning and carry. Culture enters at vocabulary and objective. An importance difference measured without the vocabulary link is a reading below the break.

## 3. What the answer explicitly does not say

- It does not say culture rewires perception or adds a slot. That is Option A, and it is unsupported by any figure line here.
- It does not name a specific practice (individualism, collectivism, schooling, a particular language) as the cause. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- It does not say all members of a culture attend alike. `w` is per-frame and learnable; cultures are backdrops, not uniform mindsets.
- It does not rank one culture's weighting as the universal baseline. The ground is not any frame's preference.
- It does not claim `grounded` status. No measurement of any population was taken.
- It does not use agreement, prestige, or sample size as a witness. Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

1. **Instruction-shift test.** If attention is `w`-driven and goal-driven, changing the objective (what counts as progress) should shift which slots carry residual, with the scene held fixed. Park and Huang (2010) report that cultural effects in object processing were *"overridden"* by instructions to attend to the object, which is the world-side version of this prediction.
2. **Matched-`w` test.** Hold inherited prior and vocabulary constant and look for a residual cultural difference. If one survives, F2 breaks.
3. **Vocabulary-removal test.** Remove the terms for what the slots afford and see whether the "importance" weighting survives. If it does, F3 breaks.
4. **Isomer test.** Hold the atoms fixed (same slots, same loop quantities) and vary only the arrangement. If the allocation changes without any hardware change, the isomer claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` is corroborated.
5. **Standing refuters from `01`:** viable perceivers matched on architecture, history and `w` who still differ; a transmission that installs a new primitive or a replay recording; an importance weighting independent of the vocabulary link; within-group agreement that changes the task's success measure.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is as far as the figures carry it. Which practice moves which slot is out of reach at this budget and needs an import from a frame whose closure differs, that is, a real experiment with a real anchor (Fig 6 :23). *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* This derivation cannot settle the question it reports on. Fig 5 :48 *"Its own output is never a measure of success."*

The world literature adds a genuine conflict rather than a clean confirmation: Park and Huang report robust East Asian versus Western differences in attention and neural function but also failures to replicate some gaze effects (Rayner and colleagues) and evidence that the effects are *"processing biases rather than immutable circuitry changes."* By the figure rule, a conflict sends the derivation back to the census; here it corroborates the instruction-sensitive, `w`-driven reading and does not flip the verdict. The sources share a literature pool, so they are one witness, not two.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that culture rewires perception. It says that attention is an allocation over object slots, not one global dial, and that what a person brings to each slot is a belief mixed from their own history and what they inherited, with a weighting `w` that learning can move. Two people with the same architecture can therefore notice different things without any difference in hardware. What culture hands down is a head start, a vocabulary of what things afford and a sense of what counts as progress, and that is where "important" lives. The ground, not agreement inside the culture, decides which weightings actually fit the task. The framework returns that shape, not the name of the practice responsible. It states in advance what would refute it: a difference that survives matched history and matched `w`, an importance weighting with no vocabulary link, or a transmission that installs a new perceptual primitive. The center stays open until an instrument closes it.
