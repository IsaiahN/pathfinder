**Subject:** A shape claim about a changing dark energy equation of state, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "is a change in dark energy smooth, temporary, or connected to a new field?" is not a three-way multiple choice. It bundles three predicates over one residual: the shape of w(z), the duration or closure of the phase, and the source of the component. A thawing quintessence model is smooth and involves a new field. An early dark energy model is temporary and involves a new field. So the first result is that the question must be unbundled before a search, and each predicate needs its own ground.
>
> From the six-term substrate, smoothness was explicitly removed as a seventh primitive: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time, and it is what a composition leaves behind rather than a member of the set. A temporary phase is a loop whose sign decides whether it closes; a chain cannot close by construction. A new field is an atom added by import, and import is the only operator that moves the wall of a frame's closure, under a product of guards (support, reachability, novelty, and a description-length bargain) with a source gate and a shadow test.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that dark energy does change. The premise is conditional. DESI DR2 reports a preference for a time-evolving equation of state at 3.1 sigma from BAO plus CMB, rising to 2.8 to 4.2 sigma with supernovae, while a 2026 distance-duality analysis finds that combining Pantheon+ and DESI DR2 mimics an evolving component and that accounting for the inconsistency restores a cosmological constant. I do not take a side in that disagreement. I am also not naming quintessence, phantom energy, or axion-like early dark energy as the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. deriving smoothness as a seventh primitive, or deriving one of the six from the other five;
> 2. an instrument that resolves w(z) across the transition and shows a discontinuity where the cross-kind composition predicts a smooth remainder, or a closing phase with no sign change;
> 3. a new field admitted with a guard at zero, no source gate, or no shadow test, that still settles at the ground;
> 4. one lever that clears shape, duration and source at once, so the predicates are not a union.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
