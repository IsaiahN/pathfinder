# The Tether's answer: reliable expertise versus persuasive claims

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the instruments, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading of any society.

## 1. The residual it answers

A society sees fluency, confidence, repetition, credentials, consensus and endorsement. It does not see whether the claim pays against something that does not move when people believe it. The residual is that gap: the appearance of reliability versus contact with a ground. It is a **union** of two conditions (Fig 9 :54), not one property, so it must be split before it is searched.

## 2. The shape, domain by domain

1. **Reliability is contact with an immovable anchor, per claim.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single national trust score averages away the signal (Fig 1 :22). World witness: scored forecasting, where per-question probabilities are graded against outcomes.

2. **Agreement is not a second way of being right.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Credibility accrued, consensus and prestige carry zero verdict weight on their own.

3. **The two failure modes are isomers, not degrees.** Fig 2 :13 describes mutual update; Fig 2 :18 describes a shared source. Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* They look the same as agreement and need opposite remedies. Cutting communication helps one and does nothing to the other.

4. **Independence is a quantity to buy, not a virtue to assert.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Ten readers of one wire story count as one frame. The effective-sample-size correction is the same arithmetic (Fig 2 :44).

5. **The verifier must not be able to reconstruct the claim.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Give a checker the residual and the refuter, not the theory or the checkable conclusion with its derivation. Fig 10 :84 names zero-knowledge proof systems as the engineering instance.

6. **Credentials are provenance, not readings.** Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* An institution that publishes a reliability score cannot also be the ground that settles it. Fig 10 :65: provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately; it never shows it was right.

7. **The framework gives the shape of the instrument, not its name.** A Brier score, a registered replication and a zero-knowledge proof are imports from other frames. They are admissible with provenance (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8), but the figures do not derive them and they do not move the wall by being named.

## 3. The operational order

Perception of the claim, then an instrument already returning a signal, then independent frames ranged separately against it, then settlement by the ground, then carry the record with refusals intact. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Most public effort lands above this break, where measurements already exist.

The instrument feeds back from the independent searchers who use it. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* If the instrument is tuned only by those it certifies, the loop closes into collapse 1. The seat that keeps it open may set conventions but never verdicts (Fig 10).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a trust score, a certification list, a fact-checking queue, or a ranking of experts. Those are frame-internal quantities and cannot score the claim.
- Not "the crowd is wise." The crowd is wise only when estimates are collected before social influence, which Lorenz et al. 2011 shows is fragile: convergence narrows diversity without improving accuracy.
- Not that credentials are worthless. They are admissible as provenance about a lineage. They are not a reading of the current claim.
- Not that more transparency always helps. Fig 9 :51 restricts what a verifier may see; evidence bearing only on the maker is withheld.
- Not that the framework settles which scoring rule a given society should adopt. That needs a ground reading of that society.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of any society was taken here.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

The pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 is the instrument for settling the shape. Concretely:

1. A controlled comparison in which consensus or a credential predicts later accuracy as well as scored predictions against outcomes, with no access to outcomes. That would break the zero-verdict-weight claim.
2. An anchor that changes its answer when belief changes. That would break Fig 2 :25, :30.
3. Frames sharing one evidence pool agreeing on claims that later fail at the same rate as frames with separate instruments. That would break Fig 2 :31-32, :40.
4. A checker who can reconstruct the claim producing sound verdicts as often as one who cannot. That would break Fig 10 :42.
5. A credential shown to register current contact rather than earned lineage. That would break Fig 10 :61, :65.

These are measurements, not arguments. Fig 11 :36 points at the same requirement: a label applied after the fact to a lineage that met its ground is doing no work until the ground has in fact been met.

## 6. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in instance.** The figures govern the quantities: anchor, per-slot residual, independence, verifier non-access, standing as stake. They do not name a policy for a particular society, and naming one would be an import requiring provenance and a shadow test. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The distinction is recorded, the route is checkable, and the refuters are written down in advance. This is what the framework can honestly deliver, and it is not a settlement of the question.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A society cannot read reliability off the signals it actually sees, because fluency, credentials, repetition and agreement are all produced inside the social frame. The Tether's answer is a shape with two conditions. First, a claim must pay against an anchor that does not change its answer when people believe it, scored per claim and per domain rather than averaged into a trust score. Second, the people checking it must be independent frames ranging against that anchor separately, with the verifier denied the claimant's standing and, as far as possible, the ability to reconstruct the claim. The two famous failure modes, a group that drifts toward whoever had the most influence and a group that never talks but shares one source, look identical as agreement and need opposite remedies. Credentials are provenance about a lineage, not a reading of the current claim, and an institution with a reputation to protect is no longer a ground. What is offered is that shape, the dependency order, the refuters written in advance, and the requirement for a better instrument. Which instrument a given society should adopt is left open until a ground settles it.
