## 1. The residual, in effect terms

A society must act on claims before it can settle them. The signals it can actually see are fluency, confidence, repetition, credentials, consensus, institutional endorsement and prestige. All of those are produced *inside* the social frame. None of them is a reading of whether the claim predicts and pays against something that does not move when people believe it.

**R (effect statement):** the gap between the *appearance* of reliability and *contact with a ground*. Operationally: which of the visible signals would still be present if the claim were false and everyone stopped believing it? Only payment against an immovable anchor survives that counterfactual.

Two further effects are part of the residual, not commentary:

- "Reliable expertise" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."* The same move applies to expertise. It is not a property readable off a person in isolation.
- The question is a **union**, not a single property. A claim can be well grounded but certified by a corrupt arrangement, or widely agreed and ungrounded. Both conditions bind.

## 2. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

### 2a. R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least two conditions bind, ground contact and independence of the checking arrangement. This is a compound. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Symmetry:** agreement is symmetric between frames (A agreeing with B is B agreeing with A), so it carries no direction and cannot range a claim. Ground contact is asymmetric: the ground ranges the frame, the frame cannot range the ground (Fig 2 :25-27, :45). Two reads of one pool are one report (Fig 2 :18-21).
- **Scale:** step (one claim, one checker), cycle (a scored track record), level (institution, market, court), seat (who may author conventions), lineage (standing that survives the outcome it was earned on, Fig 10 :61).

### 2b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Claim-ground contact** | a claim, a predictor, an outcome | residual R = \|Γ(b,a) - o′\|; payment | the world: measurement, experiment, counter, market, patient outcome | **yes**, the world |
| **D2 Social transmission** | people, claims, prestige, repetition, social identity | agreement, influence, credibility accrued | none internal to the frame | **no** on its own. It only becomes a domain when ranged against D1's ground |
| **D3 Checking arrangement** | checkers, journals, markets, courts, institutions | independence between frames | the ground, kept reachable | **yes**, the same ground, held by a seat that may not author it |
| **D1 ∩ D3 compound** | a certifying institution, its reputation | standing, provenance | the ground, if the institution does not move it | **yes, but endangered**: an institution whose standing depends on the verdict has made itself a frame |

D2 is the important negative result of the census. Fig 1 :26 lists *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* among the frame-internal quantities, and Fig 1 :27 says *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A persuasion-only domain has no anchor, so by the census rule it is not yet a domain. It is a failure mode of D3, not a second way of being reliable.

### 2c. Partition or union

**Union.** Ground contact and independence overlap: an independent panel can be wrong together if it shares one evidence pool, and a correct claim can be dismissed if the only checkers are the claimant's peers. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever, for example adding more credentials, clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the compound standing.

### 2d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the claim be held as a term that predicts anything at all?
2. **Vocabulary / objective:** is there an instrument returning something on it? This is the middle link and the one usually missing. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
3. **Planning:** can independent frames be ranged separately against that instrument?
4. **Learn and carry:** track record, provenance, and the standing of the checker.
5. **Settle:** the ground decides. Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."*

Effort spent above a broken link does not arrive. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Most "media literacy" effort lands at link 1 or 4, above the break at link 2.

## 4. Findings, per domain, with verbatim lines before conclusions

### F1 - D1: an anchor separates reliable from persuasive (decisive)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :25: **"pinned: it does not update on A or B"**
Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**
Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

The first sentence gives the test. A claim is reliable, in the only sense the figures license, when its standing survives contact with something that does not move when belief moves. Measurement, experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. A claim is persuasive-when-poorly-grounded when its standing is a function of how many people believe it or who believes it. Fig 2 :30 completes the test: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

**D1 verdict (`general`, triangulated):** reliability is contact with an immovable ground, graded by the residual on that claim. Overlap between citations is low: Fig 1 governs a single agent's metric, Fig 2 governs multi-frame alignment, Fig 9 governs when to leave a frame. Three different scopes, one verdict. World corroboration: the Good Judgment Project scores forecasts with Brier scores and finds selected forecasters beat credentialed experts (section 6).

### F2 - D2: credibility accrued is frame-internal and cannot score the claim

Fig 1 :26: **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**
Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Everything the persuasive claim has going for it, fluency, repetition, consensus, confidence, prestige, is produced by the same frame that is being asked to judge it. Fig 1 :27 is the disqualification, not a caution. Fig 9 :51 sharpens the admissibility rule for a checker: **"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"**. Credentials and prestige are evidence about the maker. They are withheld from the verifier by construction.

**D2 verdict (`general`, triangulated):** social marks of credibility carry zero verdict weight on their own. Overlap is low between Fig 1 (one agent's bookkeeping), Fig 7 (handoffs between rooms) and Fig 9 (what a verifier may see). World corroboration is strong and independent: the illusory truth effect, and Lorenz et al. 2011 on social influence narrowing diversity without improving accuracy.

### F3 - D2 mechanism: the two collapses are different failure genera

Fig 2 :13: **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**
Fig 2 :15: **"is whoever had the most influence"**
Fig 2 :18: **"one evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :20: **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

Collapse 1 is coherent drift: a group converges on whoever had the most influence. Collapse 2 is common cause: frames never talk and still agree because they read the same source. They look identical from the outside (agreement) and need different remedies. Adding more readers worsens collapse 1; adding more apparently independent outlets that syndicate one wire story worsens collapse 2. Fig 2 :44 notes the shared arithmetic: *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."*

**D2 mechanism verdict (`general`, triangulated with Fig 2 :31-32, :40 and Fig 8 :47): unmix the two collapses before prescribing a remedy.** The society essay reports Kish's correction, N / (1 + (N-1)rho), capped at 1/rho, so ten clones count as one. That is a quantified version of the same law, not a new one.

### F4 - D3: independence is the quantity to buy, and it is quantitative

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**
Fig 8 :47: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 8 :47 reads the same quantity twice: it decides both whether agreement means anything and whether a union of frames has any surplus. So independence is not a virtue to be asserted; it is a parameter to be measured and purchased. The purchase price rises with the cost of being wrong. For a claim that changes a medical treatment, buy more separation than for a claim about a restaurant.

**D3 verdict (`specified`, triangulated):** the arrangement contributes only by buying measurable independence and keeping the anchor reachable. Overlap is low between Fig 2 (alignment), Fig 8 (union/import) and Fig 9 (verifier inputs).

### F5 - D3: the verifier must not be able to reconstruct the claim

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**
Fig 10 :43: **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking. Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom. It transmits verdicts and issues none."**
Fig 10 :84: **"see also. Zero-knowledge proof systems, where the verifier's non-access to the witness is what makes the verification sound rather than a limitation it tolerates."**

This is the figure's own cryptographic analogue, and it is a real engineering practice. In a zero-knowledge proof the verifier is convinced without receiving the witness; a recording of the interaction is not even convincing to a third party, because it could have been staged. That is exactly why an outside checker must receive the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interior. World corroboration: Goldwasser, Micali and Rackoff 1985, opened 2026-11-23.

**D3 verdict (`general`, triangulated):** verification requires non-access. A society that gives its checkers the whole theory before asking for a verdict has converted them into players.

### F6 - D1 ∩ D3: standing is a stake, and a credential is provenance

Fig 10 :61: **"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."**
Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."**
Fig 10 :65: **"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."**
Fig 11 :36: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."**

An institution asked to certify reliability has a reputation to protect, so it is no longer a ground; it is a frame with a stake. An expert's standing was earned on an earlier outcome and survives it, so it is a stake that travels. Fig 10 :86 adds: **"Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."** The institution that publishes a reliability score cannot also be the subject of that score.

What survives is the record, not the rank. Fig 13 :141: **"not only what was concluded, but what could have been concluded, and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ."** A credential is admissible as provenance about a lineage. It is not a reading of the current claim.

**Compound verdict (`general`, triangulated):** credentials and institutional endorsement carry zero verdict weight; they are provenance for how a claim was reached, never evidence that it is right.

### F7 - The union must be unbundled before a remedy is chosen

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**
Fig 9 :4: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

"Reliable expertise" is two conditions: the claim pays against a ground, and the checker is independent. Policies that address only one leave the intersection standing. This is why "more credentials" and "more fact-checkers" both fail in the specific cases that matter: the credentialed panel with a shared source, and the independent panel with no instrument.

**Census verdict (`general`, triangulated with Fig 8's union/surplus law):** split before searching. Each part gets its own loop and its own ground.

### F8 - The middle link is the measuring instrument

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**

A society cannot install reliability by declaring a trust score. It can only improve an instrument already returning something: a scored forecast, a replication attempt, a prediction market, a court record, a patient outcome. Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** The instrument is the contact.

**D1/D3 instrument verdict (`specified`, triangulated):** the operational instrument is a per-claim score against outcomes, improved from a worse score already returning a signal. Without such an instrument, link 2 is missing and everything above it is wasted.

### F9 - Per slot, never a global trust score

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

A single national "trust in expertise" number averages away the live signal exactly where it matters. Reliability is per claim, per domain, per checker pair. Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** The cost of stating a reliability verdict and the cost of settling it are different currencies; summing them hides the shortfall.

**Verdict (`general`, triangulated):** score per slot and keep the two costs apart. The society essay's rule "keep the rate per kind of situation" is the same law in operational form.

### F10 - Apparent strain: human judgment as an anchor. Re-census, not strain

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**

If human judgment is the right anchor for one question, can agreement among people be the anchor for "is this reliable expertise"? Re-census: the two questions are not the same. "Is this a good answer for a person" is constitutively about people, so human judgment is legitimate there. "Is this claim true of the world" is constitutively about the world, so the world is the anchor and agreement is only an instrument, an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Fig 2 :34 warns what happens if the distinction is lost: *"the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

**After re-census: not strained.** Fig 2 :36-38 governs a different question from the one in `Questions/Information Theory.txt` line 114. Reported as a re-census, not smoothed over.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion and before outreach)

Required by the method: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is: **a society separates reliable expertise from persuasive claims only by per-claim contact with an anchor that does not move with belief, and by measurable independence of the checkers; social marks of credibility carry zero verdict weight on their own.**

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Agreement alone moves the residual.** In a controlled comparison, a social mark (consensus among alleged independent experts, or a credential) predicts later accuracy at or above the level of scored predictions against outcomes, with no access to those outcomes.
2. **The anchor moves.** An instrument is found that changes its answer when frames come to believe a different answer. This breaks Fig 2 :25 and :30.
3. **Shared pools do not degrade agreement.** Frames drawing on one evidence pool agree on claims that later fail against the ground at a rate no worse than frames with separate instruments. This breaks Fig 2 :31-32 and :40.
4. **Reconstruction does not impair verification.** A checker that can reconstruct the claim it is checking produces sound verdicts at the same rate as one that cannot. This breaks Fig 10 :42.
5. **A credential is a ground reading.** A standing or title is shown to register current contact with the claim's outcome rather than the lineage that earned it. This breaks Fig 10 :61, :65.

If any fires, the shape is wrong and this package is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Ask the question as two questions. Is the claim paying against an immovable instrument, and are the checkers independent? Do not accept a single "is this expert reliable" verdict (F7, F9).
2. Score per claim and per domain. Keep the cost of stating the score and the cost of settling the claim in separate columns (F9).
3. Build instruments from instruments. Start with whatever is already returning a signal (a forecast score, a replication, an outcome ledger) and sharpen it. Do not declare a trust score (F8).
4. Withhold the maker's standing from the checker. Give the residual and the refuter, not the CV or the theory (F2, F5).
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and measure it (F4). Ten syndicated copies are one frame.
6. Make the institution that hosts the process ineligible to score its own outputs (F6).
7. Keep the refusal in the vocabulary. A system that can only accrete endorsements cannot correct them (Operators table: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*).

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** What should be shared across society is the checking method, not a list of who was certified.

## 9. Round trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

- **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :25, :29-32, :40, :42, Fig 1 :26-27, Fig 9 :51, Fig 10 :42, :61, :65, :84.
- **Abstraction (T_A):** the usable slogan "trust the ground, not agreement".
- **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete institutional policy, e.g. "publish a panel consensus score".

**R_T is nonzero.** The slogan does not survive the trip down with these constraints intact:

1. the anchor must not move when belief moves (Fig 2 :25, :30);
2. independence is quantitative and must be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :31, :42);
3. the residual is per slot, not a global trust average (Fig 1 :19, :22);
4. the verifier must not be able to reconstruct the claim (Fig 10 :42);
5. the problem is a union and must be split before a remedy is chosen (Fig 9 :54);
6. the certifying institution is itself a stake and can be disqualified (Fig 10 :61, :93).

What survives cleanly: agreement is not a verdict, and contact with an immovable ground is. R_T was not measured numerically because no ground reading of a society was taken. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
