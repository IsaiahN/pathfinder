# Isomers and order: the two collapses, the checking order, and the analogues

**Mode: `general` on the structural claims, `specified` on the order, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures; see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6. Not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The visible output is the same in two very different situations: people agree on a claim. The census in `01` found two distinct mechanisms, mutual update and shared evidence pool. This file asks the two supporting questions the main audit left open:

1. Are the two collapses isomers under Fig 12's definition, or merely members of one functional group?
2. In what dependency order should a society's checks run, and can that order close into a cycle?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two collapses isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

The two collapses share the same visible atom: agreement. They share the cast, frames and a claim. What differs is the bond between them:

- collapse 1: frames update on each other. Fig 2 :13 *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*, Fig 2 :15 *"is whoever had the most influence"*. The bond is mutual update.
- collapse 2: frames do not talk. Fig 2 :19 *"they never talk, and still agree"*, Fig 2 :20 *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*. The bond is a shared source.

The visible formula is the same ("they agree"), the internal bond differs, and the behaviour under intervention differs: breaking a communication channel helps collapse 1 and does nothing to collapse 2; diversifying sources helps collapse 2 and does nothing to collapse 1.

**Q1 verdict (`general`, triangulated):** the two collapses are **isomers**, not degrees of one failure. The bond is charged for what it could have been, so a report that records only "consensus" has recorded half of itself. Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** World corroboration: Lorenz et al. 2011 found a social-influence effect and a range-reduction effect that respond differently to information conditions, while the confidence effect is psychological, a third genus. That is one independent witness for the isomer distinction, not three.

### Q2 - Are they also a functional group?

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Yes, at a coarser level. Both collapses belong to the group "agreement without ground contact". The group fixes the kind (a verdict-looking output produced inside the frame); the qualifier names which member (influence-driven or pool-driven). This matters because a remedy aimed at the group ("demand more agreement") makes both worse. Fig 12 :124: *"The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

**Q2 verdict (`general`, single-source for the figure definition):** isomer at the fine grain, functional group at the coarse grain. Both readings are true at their own level. Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

### Q3 - What is the dependency order, and can it close?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The order for a society's check is:

1. **Perception:** hold the claim as something that predicts.
2. **Instrument:** find an existing reading that returns something on it. Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
3. **Separation:** range independent frames against that instrument separately. Independence is meaningless without a common anchor to range against, so this step consumes step 2.
4. **Settlement:** the ground decides. Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."*
5. **Carry:** record the lineage, keep refusals, allow reach to fall. Fig 12 :106: **"Which is why a system that cannot settle has one level however large its library. Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."**

But step 2 benefits from step 3: more independent searchers make a better instrument. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** The loop is real but must stay open. If the instrument is tuned only by the people it will certify, the sign turns negative and the cycle closes into collapse 1. Keeping it open is the ground maintainer's office: it sets conventions, not verdicts.

**Q3 verdict (`specified`, triangulated across Fig 3, Fig 6, Fig 12):** instrument before separation in dependency order; separation feeds back into the instrument; the feedback must be kept open by a seat that cannot author the verdict.

### Q4 - What do the analogues actually license?

| analogue | structure / order | what it corroborates | limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-knowledge proof (Goldwasser, Micali, Rackoff 1985; Wikipedia synthesis opened 2026-11-23) | prover convinces verifier without transferring the witness; soundness rests on non-access | Fig 10 :42 and :84, verifier non-access | a mathematical guarantee under stated assumptions, not a social institution |
| Scored forecasting (Good Judgment Project; Wikipedia synthesis opened 2026-11-23) | per-question probabilities, Brier scores against outcomes, track record selects forecasters | F1 and F9: per-slot residual, ground contact beats credentials | forecasts are about events that resolve; many expertise claims are not |
| Registered Replication Reports (APS page fetched 2026-11-23) | pre-registered shared protocol, multiple labs, publish regardless of outcome | pre-registered refuter plus independence, Fig 2 :42 | costly, slow, and only for questions with a reproducible protocol |
| Honest signalling (Zahavi 1975, Grafen 1990; Wikipedia synthesis opened 2026-11-23) | costly signal is reliable when lower-quality signallers cannot afford it | the reliable/persuasive distinction | the same source says honest signals need not be costly and the principle *"remains unconfirmed by empirical evidence"*; cost alone is not ground contact |
| Structured interview (Wikipedia synthesis opened 2026-11-23) | same questions, same order, scored against performance | standardising the instrument beats impression | a business analogue, not a general proof |

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** The analogues differ in which currency they price. A zero-knowledge proof is cheap in actions once set up; a replication is expensive in actions. Summing them into one "best practice" score would repeat the error the figure names.

### Q5 - Does standing survive its own use?

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A credential behaves like a catalyst: it survives its own use. The record of a past success keeps operating after the ground has moved on. Fig 10 :61 says the same in the seat vocabulary: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Two figures with low overlap agree. What the record must add is the marker of which parts are spent and which regenerate, that is, whether the standing is being used as provenance or as a substitute for a fresh check.

**Q5 verdict (`general`, triangulated):** a credential must be labelled as a catalyst-like part, not consumed as a verdict.

## FALSIFIER (written before the run)

The isomer claim fails if the two collapses respond to the same intervention in the same way: for example, if severing communication between frames and diversifying their sources both reduce agreement-based error by the same amount, then the bond does not differ and the functional-group reading is the only one that holds.

The order claim fails if a society can buy meaningful independence before it has any instrument returning a signal on the claim, with no common anchor to range against.

The catalyst claim fails if credentials are observed to decay with the outcome that earned them and regenerate only on fresh ground contact, in which case they are spent parts and need no special warning label.
