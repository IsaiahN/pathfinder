# Expert verification packet - distinguishing reliable expertise from persuasive claims

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on expertise identification, judgment or information reliability can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the instruments, not `grounded`.** No measurement of any society is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A society can separate reliable claims from persuasive but poorly grounded ones only through two independent conditions. A claim must pay against an anchor that does not change its answer when people believe it, scored per claim and per domain rather than averaged into a trust score. The checkers must be independent frames ranging against that anchor separately, with evidence about the claimant's standing withheld from the verifier. Agreement that comes from mutual influence or from a shared evidence pool is a mark of the social frame, not of the claim, and carries zero verdict weight. Credentials and institutional endorsement are provenance about a lineage, not readings of the current claim. The framework gives the shape of the checking instrument, not its name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 7

- **Statement:** reliability requires contact with an immovable anchor; agreement is not a second way of being right; the residual is per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** show an anchor that changes its answer when belief changes; or show consensus or a credential predicting later accuracy as well as scored predictions with no access to outcomes; or show a per-domain signal surviving aggregation into a single score.

### C2 - mechanism, checkable against the judgment and decision literature

- **Statement:** the two failure genera are isomers. Collapse 1 is mutual update converging on whoever had the most influence (Fig 2 :13, :15). Collapse 2 is a shared evidence pool: *"they never talk, and still agree"* (Fig 2 :19). They look the same as agreement and need opposite remedies. Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** does the experimental literature separate social-influence effects from shared-source effects, and do interventions that help one fail to help the other?
- **Refuter:** show that severing communication and diversifying sources reduce agreement-based error by the same amount. Then the bond does not differ and the functional-group reading replaces the isomer reading.

### C3 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 10

- **Statement:** verification requires non-access, and standing is a stake. The verifier receives evidence bearing on the residual and is denied evidence bearing only on the maker.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 9 :51 - *"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"* Fig 10 :61 - *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :93 - *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
- **Refuter:** show a checker who can reconstruct the claim producing sound verdicts at the same rate as one who cannot; or show that a credential registers current contact rather than an earlier outcome; or show an institution protecting a reputation settling a claim about its own performance without degradation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a trust score, certification list, fact-checking queue or ranking of experts.
- Not "the crowd is wise." Crowd accuracy depends on estimates being collected before social influence, which is fragile.
- Not that credentials are worthless. They are admissible as provenance about a lineage.
- Not that more transparency always helps. What the verifier may see is restricted by the figure.
- Not that the framework derives a particular scoring rule. Brier scores, registered replication and zero-knowledge proofs are imports from other frames, admitted with provenance.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement of a society.
- Not a settlement of the question. The instrument for a particular domain is left open until a ground settles it.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Does an anchor exist for the claim domain that does not move when belief moves? If not, the claim is not composable in this frame.
2. Is there an instrument already returning a per-claim signal that can be sharpened rather than replaced? (Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*)
3. Can checkers be ranged against it separately, with their evidence pools distinguishable? (Fig 2 :31.)
4. Can the checker be kept unable to reconstruct the claim? (Fig 10 :42.)
5. Can the institution hosting the check be made ineligible to score its own output? (Fig 10 :93.)

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :13, :18, :29 and :31; Fig 1 :27; Fig 9 :51; Fig 10 :42 and :93. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against the judgment literature: does the influence/shared-source distinction survive?
3. Name one instrument in your field that already returns a per-claim signal suitable for sharpening, or say why none exists.
4. Say which of the five refuters in section 5 you would try first.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
