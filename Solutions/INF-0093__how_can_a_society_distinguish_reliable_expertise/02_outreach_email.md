# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Never** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked against the verbatim figure lines. If the stage-1 output does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about telling reliable expertise from persuasive claims

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: what a society would have to measure to tell a claim that pays against the world from a claim that is merely persuasive. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` instruments, not `grounded`):** a claim's credibility has two independent conditions. It must pay against something that does not change its answer when people believe it, and the people checking it must be separate frames ranging against that thing separately. Credibility that comes from influence, repetition, a shared source, or a person's standing is produced inside the social frame, so it cannot score the claim. In the figures' terms, agreement compounds while evidence does not.
>
> Three consequences follow, and these are the parts worth attacking:
>
> 1. Two different failure modes hide behind "people agree": a group that updates on itself until whoever had the most influence wins, and a group that never talks but reads one shared source. They look the same from outside and need different remedies.
> 2. Independence is a quantity, not a virtue. Ten readers of the same wire story are one frame, so their agreement is worth no more than one reader's.
> 3. A credential is a record of a lineage that met a ground on an earlier question. It is not a reading of the claim in front of you. The institution that publishes a reliability score has a reputation to protect and cannot also be the ground that settles it.
>
> **What would refute this (written before I ran the derivation):**
> 1. A controlled test in which consensus or a credential predicts later accuracy as well as scored predictions against outcomes, with no access to those outcomes.
> 2. An anchor that changes its answer when frames come to believe something else.
> 3. Checkers who share one evidence pool agreeing on claims that later fail against the ground at the same rate as checkers with separate instruments.
> 4. A checker who can reconstruct the claim producing sound verdicts at the same rate as one who cannot.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three short public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The question I would most value your judgement on is which instrument, in your field, is already returning a signal that could be sharpened rather than replaced.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** it is a loop for describing the residual before choosing a frame. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* A reader who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. |
| Refuter before conclusion | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. It lets the reader attack the claim without needing the author's vocabulary. |
| No request for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` - *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* A shape is not presented as a measurement. |
| Standing withheld from the checker | Fig 9 :51 - evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is withheld. The email carries no CV, no institution prestige, and no publication count. |
| Two collapses named separately | Fig 2 :13 and :18. A remedy for influence is not a remedy for a shared pool. |
| Independence described as a quantity | Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* |
| Ground with a reputation disqualified | Fig 10 :93. The email does not ask the recipient to endorse an institution. |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a verdict sends a recording, not a generator. |
| Method offered, never the answer | Fig 4 :22 again. The loop travels; a certified conclusion does not. |
| One-to-one, opt-in venues only | Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A list ranked for receptiveness is a list selected to agree. |

## Stage order (do not reorder)

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the three consequences and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* anything else is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the three public source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the loop as a method for their own problem, with the instruction to write a refuter first and state a mode.

## Legal and practical note

Public institutional and opt-in channels only. No personal emails, no scraped lists, no automation, no misleading subject lines, and an opt-out in every message. GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules apply to unsolicited outreach in many jurisdictions; a blacklisted sending domain is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
