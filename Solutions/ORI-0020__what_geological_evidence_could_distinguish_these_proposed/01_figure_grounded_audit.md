**Mode of this document: `specified`, not `grounded`.** It states what would have to be measured. Per `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate."*

**Status: simulated derivation, not a ground reading.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output is *"an approximate answer/output"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* No measurement was taken. This document cannot settle the question it reports on; it narrows the shape of an answer and records the stop.

## 0. The claim under audit

The question in section 2 of `origins of life.txt` follows question 18, which names the candidate settings: deep-sea hydrothermal systems, shallow ponds, volcanic land, ice, or another setting. Question 17 asks which settings lasted long enough. Question 19 asks whether life could have begun in more than one environment with only one lineage surviving.

Stated as an effect, R is:

> Several candidate settings are each internally coherent. They make different predictions about what the rock record should contain. The rock record has not yet returned an observation that settles which setting was actual. What observation would?

The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the search (Formula, "Direction"), so that it can be refused rather than silently preferred:

> A **discriminating observation** is a geological trace that (i) at least one proposed setting predicts and the others cannot produce, (ii) is read off the habitat rather than invented, (iii) is already registering at some instrument resolution, and (iv) survives diagnosis and metamorphism so that it still carries the setting's signature.

This is a **shape**, not a name. It says what a settling observation must look like; it does not say which setting won. Naming the winner would require the ground (rocks, instruments, dates), which this derivation does not hold.

## 2. Domain census before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: four named settings plus "another", so a discrimination problem over a set, not a two-way choice. Symmetry: asymmetric, because a trace can be necessary for one setting and absent from another without the reverse holding; the relation between a setting and its trace is a threshold relation in at least one place (Operators table, ⋛). Scale: from mineral and isotopic scale, through facies and stratigraphy, to duration over geological time. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :54): *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

One line per domain, actors / currency / anchor:

- **Domain 1, the candidate settings as frames.** Actors: the physical conditions each setting supplies (water, temperature, pH, ionic strength, gradients, UV, mineral surfaces, duration). Currency: prebiotic pathways each setting can host. Anchor: none yet. A setting is a frame, not an anchor, because it can be adjusted to accommodate a contrary reading. Not a domain until it ranges against the ground: Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **Domain 2, the rock record as candidate ground.** Actors: isotopic systems (C, S, Fe, O, Si), mineral assemblages (chert, barite, geyserite, sinter, REE + Y patterns), morphological traces (stromatolites, microfossils), stratigraphic relations. Currency: setting-diagnostic readings. Anchor: the measurement itself, provided it does not move when a setting-proponent pushes on it. This is a genuine domain, with an anchor.
- **Domain 3, the verification and contact frame.** Actors: geologists, geochemists and reviewers. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.

**Partition or union.** Domain 1 and Domain 2 are not a partition of one problem; the settings overlap in the conditions they supply (both a deep vent and a shallow pond can offer mineral surfaces and gradients). The combined R is a union and must be unbundled before the search (Fig 9 :54). Domain 3 is a separate loop whose output is a correction on shape, not a verdict on setting.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** First describe R in effect terms and bind the sought-for shape. Second, settle whether a setting's chain is intact at all: perception of conditions, vocabulary of affordances, objective, plan, carry (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*). Third, enumerate the habitat for each surviving setting. Fourth, ask which existing instrument already returns something that fails to resolve. Fifth, name a discriminator. Sixth, record what would reverse it. Effort spent on step five before steps one to four is where apophenia enters.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A setting is a frame; the discriminator must be an anchor (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Each proposed setting is a frame: a bounded explanation with its own library of conditions and its own list of advantages. If the "evidence" offered is that a setting's proponents agree it is plausible, the anchor has been replaced by a frame. The discriminator must be something that does not change its answer when a proponent pushes on it: a measured isotopic ratio, a mineral that can only form in one regime, a stratigraphic contact. Fig 2 :40 also disqualifies the cheap route of pooling setting-advocates: agreement within one evidence pool is one witness, not two.

**Refuter:** show that the proposed discriminator's reading changes with the interpreter's preferred setting rather than with the rock, or that two supposedly independent readings are the same measurement counted twice.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 7 selection-between-rooms rule, low overlap between an anchor test and a room-chain test).

### F2 - Residual first, frame second (triangulated)

Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

The Library of Babel essay describes the same direction: use the vocabulary closest to what you are looking for and recurse, because *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* If a field or a technique is chosen first, any setting will seem to fit it. The residual here is a discrimination failure between settings that leave different traces; that description must come before choosing isotope geochemistry, sedimentology, or experimental prebiotic chemistry as the frame.

Fig 9 :32 governs the output: a kind of observing that would discriminate, not a name of the winning setting. The canonical figures README says the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* for what is missing.

**Refuter:** produce a discriminator chosen before R was described in effect terms, then show it discriminates only because the setting was assumed in advance (echo without shadow).

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, different media but same author and evidence pool; counted as one pool, so the corroboration is weak by Fig 2 :40).

### F3 - The settings are habitats; enumerate them, never compose them (single-source for enumeration)

Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*

A "setting" is not a coordinate; it is a habitat of actors, conditions and relations. Fig 11 :46 names the conditions to enumerate: light, water, temperature, season, terrain. For the four candidates this gives contrasting but overlapping lists: deep vent (no UV, steep temperature and pH gradients, mineral surfaces, fluid flow), shallow pond (UV, wet-dry cycling, evaporation, concentration), volcanic land (heat, acid, ash, splash pools), ice (low temperature, radiation, concentration in brines). The lists are read off the world, not composed from the vocabulary of prebiotic chemistry.

Duration belongs in the habitat, not in a separate score. The Frontiers review (Westall, Brack, Fairén and Schulte, 2023) asks: *"If a certain environment is envisaged as a possible prime locale for the emergence of life, how long was it stable, how long did it last?"* That is a habitat-enumeration question, and it is why question 17 precedes question 20.

**Refuter:** show that a condition on the list was composed from the theory rather than read from a rock, or that a condition three steps out was omitted and its omission changes which setting survives.

**Status:** `single-source` for the enumeration rule (Fig 11), with the field review as secondary corroboration from a genuinely different field.

### F4 - No sensor for a trace that has never registered (general constraint, triangulated)

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
Fig 6 :40: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

This is the limit that keeps the answer honest. We cannot specify, from the armchair, an instrument for a trace that has never registered at any resolution. We can only ask which existing reading already fails to resolve between settings. Candidates that do already register: carbon and sulfur isotope ratios, REE + Y patterns, petrographic facies (geyserite, sinter, chert), and stratigraphic contacts. Fig 8 :39 means a failure to find a discriminator today does not prove none exists; it is a stop, not a verdict of absence.

**Refuter:** name a real instrument, at any resolution, already returning a reading that fails to resolve two settings; if none can be named for a pair, the discrimination for that pair is `unreachable` at this budget, not answered.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 6 instrument rule and Fig 8 proof-of-absence limit).

### F5 - Same product, different setting: a source problem (single-source figure verdict, world corroboration)

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Two settings can produce the same bulk reading. That is the isomer condition applied to evidence: the same chemical product under different arrangements of conditions. The Hard Problem is About Source gives the instrument-level version: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* The remedy in both cases is a source-discriminating instrument, not a better argument.

World corroboration from geology, genuinely low overlap with the framework: Zeichner, Fischer, Lotem, Moore, Goldford and Eiler (2024, American Journal of Science) write that *"it can be challenging to distinguish whether these low values reflect biotic formation, abiotic reactions, or post-depositional processes."* Knoll, Bergmann and Strauss (2016, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B) add that *"abiotic organic syntheses, including those thought to have played a role in the origin of life, also fractionate C isotopes"* and show experimentally that *"hydrothermal fluids can mobilize organic matter and redistribute it into spheroidal microstructures that resemble putative Archean microfossils."* A bulk carbon-isotope value or a spheroid is therefore not, by itself, a discriminator between a vent and a pond.

**Refuter:** demonstrate an instrument whose reading is invariant to setting for the trace in question, or show that the trace is produced by every candidate setting at the same value.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated but not triangulated by two independent geology sources. The corroboration is kept separate from the figure verdict.

### F6 - The decisive trace may be a cascade, not the setting itself (single-source)

Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

A discriminator need not be found at the site. The signature may be exported: organic matter formed in one setting and deposited in another, a chemical signature superimposed on a different background, a biosignature carried into a chert that does not itself record the source environment. The Frontiers review (2023) gives the field example: *"This signature is superimposed on a long-term, background signature of predominantly hydrothermal origin"* (REE + Y in situ measurements in the Josefsdal Chert). Reading only the background or only the short-term signal loses the discrimination.

**Refuter:** show that the proposed cascade can be reproduced by a first-order process in a different setting with the same end state.

**Status:** `single-source` (Fig 11), with a field example as corroboration.

### F7 - The four settings are a union, not a partition (single-source, decisive for method)

Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Deep vent, shallow pond, volcanic land and ice are not mutually exclusive boxes. Both a vent and a pond offer mineral surfaces and gradients; a volcanic field can host a pond; ice can overlie a vent. A single lever (say, "presence of a gradient") clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Question 19 already anticipates this: more than one environment could have hosted chemistry, with only one lineage surviving. The correct R is therefore a union of setting-conditions, and the discriminating evidence must be sought per unbundled condition, then recombined.

**Refuter:** exhibit a trace that separates the settings as a clean partition, with no overlap and no compound case; if one exists, the union reading is unnecessary and is withdrawn.

**Status:** `single-source` (Fig 9).

### F8 - Duration and chain integrity come before chemistry (single-source for the figure, corroborated)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

A setting that cannot supply a persistent gradient, or that is sterilised on a timescale shorter than the chemistry needs, has a broken chain; refining its prebiotic pathways spends effort above the break. The Westall et al. 2018 Astrobiology review lists the environments side by side and concludes that *"Many locations, ranging from hydrothermal vents and pumice rafts, through volcanic-hosted splash pools to continental springs and rivers, have been proposed for the emergence of life on Earth, each with respective advantages and certain disadvantages."* Barge and Price (2022, Nature Geoscience) add a fifth, intermediate class whose relevant conditions include *"wet–dry cycling, temperature variations, and influxes of both saltwater and freshwater"*, showing that the census is still open.

**Refuter:** show that a setting's chain is intact on the required timescale by a measurement, not by an assumption, and that a competing setting's chain is broken on the same axis.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure rule, with two field reviews as corroboration.

### F9 - Verdict status and the honest stop (triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The verdict from the figures is a shape: an anchor that each setting ranges against separately, low overlap between settings, enumerated from the habitat, already registering at some resolution, and source-discriminating. Naming the winning setting is `gap` from the figures alone; it needs the ground. That is stated plainly rather than filled with a plausible name. Where a pair of settings has no instrument returning a failing reading, the honest label for that pair is `unreachable` at this budget, not silence and not a tie.

**Refuter:** supply a ground reading that names the setting, or a figure re-census that admits a discriminator the figures currently forbid. Either would move the verdict.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 filter/witness rule, Fig 13 unreachable-as-verdict, Fig 2 independence price).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before any conclusion, per Fig 9 :60 and the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A proposed discriminator changes its reading with the interpreter's preferred setting rather than with the rock, so it is a frame and not an anchor.
2. Two readings offered as independent turn out to be one measurement counted twice, or two studies drawing on the same sample pool.
3. A trace is shown to be produced at the same value by every candidate setting, so it discriminates nothing.
4. The conditions of the settings turn out to form a clean partition with no compound cases, so the union reading is false.
5. A real instrument, at any resolution, returns a reading that names one setting, and the ground pays for it.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the verdict is re-run from the census. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and the refuter, not with a preferred setting (F2, F9).
2. Enumerate each setting's habitat from the literature before ranking any discriminator (F3, F8).
3. Treat each candidate observation as a claim that must pass the anchor test and the source test (F1, F5).
4. Unbundle the union before searching (F7); a single lever will not clear the intersections.
5. Name the instruments that already return failing readings (F4), and label the rest `unreachable` at this budget.
6. Keep world sources as corroboration only. Fig 2 :40 applies: geometry sources and framework essays share nothing with the geology literature, so the geology sources are genuinely independent witnesses to the field facts, while the essays are one evidence pool with the figures.

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives and is triangulated: a discriminator must be an anchor, described before the frame is chosen, enumerated from the habitat, already registering at some resolution, source-discriminating, and robust to union overlap. Everything specific that would name a winning setting is `gap` or `specified` and unmeasured. The honest stop is at the instruments: existing readings (isotopes, facies, stratigraphy, mineralogy) narrow the field but do not yet settle it. Fig 13 :134 applies directly: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result from a simulated derivation, not a ground reading and not a settlement of the question.
