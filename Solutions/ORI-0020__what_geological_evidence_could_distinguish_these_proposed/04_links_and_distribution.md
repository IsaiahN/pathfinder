## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The setting census is itself open.** Westall et al. (2018) enumerate submarine vents, pumice rafts, subaerial geysers and splash pools; Barge and Price (2022) argue for shallow-sea alkaline vents as a distinct class. Any answer that treats the four labels in question 18 as exhaustive is already stale. This corroborates the habitat-enumeration rule (Fig 11 :44) and the union reading (Fig 9 :54).
2. **A facies can exclude settings.** Djokic et al. (2017) report that geyserite and sinter terracettes cannot be accounted for by marine or lacustrine settings. That is the exact logical form of a discriminator: one setting predicts it, the others cannot produce it. It is a `specified` example, not a settlement of question 20, because it concerns Rhynie-scale hot-spring preservation at 3.48 Ga rather than the first origin.
3. **Bulk readings are not enough.** Zeichner et al. (2024) and Knoll et al. (2016) show that low carbon isotope values and spheroidal textures can arise abiotically or through later alteration. This corroborates the source-problem finding (F5) and the instrument rule (Fig 6 :40): the discriminator must resolve source, not merely reading.
4. **Duration is a first-order constraint.** The Frontiers review (2023) asks how long a candidate environment lasted, and reads that from zircon isotopes, pillow lavas, cherts and REE patterns. This corroborates the dependency-order finding (F8): a setting with a broken chain is not rescued by better chemistry.
5. **Agreement is not a witness.** None of the geology sources claims that consensus among origin-of-life researchers settles a setting. Their disputes are resolved by measurement. This is Fig 2 :45 in practice: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a reader has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if a given setting were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 7 :35** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict"* No mass send selected for people who already favour a setting.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Record the verdict on the shape before any theory is shown, and never pool later agreement as corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the discriminator and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the three evidence links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## Two-stage offer, one-to-one

Stage 1 carries the residual, the shape and the four refuters from `02_outreach_email.md`. Stage 2 is opt-in: if the reader is curious after recording a verdict, offer the figure extract and the geology sources. A Stage 2 agreement is a conversation, not evidence. Stage 3 offers the formula as a method, never as an answer, and each user must supply their own ground and write their own refuter before running it.

No packet is sent by this agent. Hosting and sending are the user's actions. If a public archive is used, choose a link that lets a recipient decline contact.
