# Outreach draft: validate the discriminator first, then decide about the settings

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive the reader's check. The output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide which early-Earth setting it would favour

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question from the origins-of-life literature: what geological evidence could distinguish the proposed settings for life's origin (deep-sea hydrothermal systems, shallow ponds, volcanic land, ice)? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is a discrimination failure, not a missing fact. Two conditions make an observation count as a discriminator. First, it must behave as an anchor: a measured ratio, a mineral, a facies, or a stratigraphic contact whose reading does not change when the interpreter's preferred setting changes. Second, it must be source-discriminating: at least one setting must predict it and the others must not be able to produce the same value. A bulk carbon-isotope value or a spheroidal microfossil fails the second test on its own, because abiotic synthesis and later alteration can make the same reading; published work documents hydrothermal fluids generating spheroids that resemble putative Archean microfossils. What survives is a shape: an anchor that ranges against each setting separately, read off the habitat, already registering at some instrument resolution, and durable through diagnosis. It is not a name of the winning setting.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this derivation tells us where life began. It cannot. No rock was measured here. It claims only the shape a settling observation would have, and it labels as `gap` the part that needs the ground. I am also not claiming that agreement among advocates of a setting is evidence. It is not: a room's agreement is variation, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a proposed discriminator whose reading changes with the interpreter's preferred setting rather than with the rock;
> 2. two readings offered as independent that are one measurement counted twice, or two studies drawing on the same sample pool;
> 3. a trace produced at the same value by every candidate setting, so it discriminates nothing;
> 4. a real instrument, at any resolution, returning a reading that names one setting and is paid for by the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Geological evidence review, Westall, Brack, Fairén and Schulte, 2023: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2022.1095701/full
> - Hot-spring facies as a discriminator, Djokic et al., 2017: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15263
> - Carbon-isotope resilience through the rock cycle, Zeichner et al., 2024: https://ajsonline.org/article/116058
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]
