# The Tether's answer: What geological evidence could distinguish these proposed settings?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and its seven constraints, `general` on the anchor and instrument rules, `gap` on the name of the winning setting. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question follows a list of candidate settings for abiogenesis: deep-sea hydrothermal systems, shallow ponds, volcanic land, ice, or another setting (question 18), plus the questions of duration (17) and multiple origins with one surviving lineage (19). The residual is a **discrimination failure**: the settings are internally coherent and make different predictions about the rock record, but no observation has been accepted as settling which was actual. It is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54): *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

## 2. The shape

1. **A discriminator must be an anchor, not a frame's preference.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* A mineral, an isotopic ratio, a facies or a stratigraphic contact whose reading does not move with the interpreter's favoured setting is a candidate. Agreement among advocates is not.
2. **Describe the residual first, choose the field second.** Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* and Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The output is a kind of observing that would discriminate, not a name of the setting.
3. **Enumerate the settings as habitats.** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* Conditions read from the geology literature include water, temperature, pH, ionic strength, gradients, UV, mineral surfaces and, critically, duration.
4. **The instrument must already return something that fails to resolve.** Fig 6 :40: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."* Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Existing readings that qualify: carbon, sulfur, oxygen, silicon and iron isotopes; REE + Y patterns; petrographic facies such as chert, barite, geyserite and sinter; stratigraphic relations; microfossil morphology.
5. **The reading must discriminate source, not just product.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* A bulk δ13C value or a spheroid is a product that several settings can produce. Zeichner et al. (2024) show low Archean δ13C can be biotic, abiotic or diagenetic; Knoll et al. (2016) show hydrothermal fluids can generate spheroids resembling microfossils. A source-discriminating combination is owed.
6. **The decisive trace may be a cascade.** Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A signature formed in one setting may be deposited in another; the Frontiers review (2023) documents a short-term terrigenous signature superimposed on a long-term hydrothermal background in the same cherts.
7. **Duration and chain integrity gate the chemistry.** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* A setting that is sterilised faster than chemistry can build up has a broken chain, and better prebiotic pathways spend effort above the break.

A concrete `specified` example of the shape in the field: Djokic et al. (2017) report that *"neither marine nor lacustrine settings can account for the preservation of geyserite, sinter terracettes or the mineralized remnants of hot spring pools."* That is a setting-diagnostic trace. It is not the answer to question 20, because it concerns preservation in a 3.48 Ga hot-spring system, not the first origin; but it shows the logical form a discriminator takes.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which setting hosted life's origin. That is `gap` from the figures and needs the ground.
- Not that the four labels in question 18 are exhaustive. Barge and Price (2022) add shallow-sea alkaline vents; Westall et al. (2018) list pumice rafts and splash pools. The census stays open.
- Not that any single isotope system, facies or fossil settles the question. Bulk readings are metamorphic and abiotic traps.
- Not that agreement among geologists, or a vote of famous researchers, is evidence (Fig 7 :35, Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken; no thin section was examined.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument whose reading names one setting and survives diagnosis, applied to rocks old enough and low-grade enough to carry the primary signature. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved that case. Here the analogue questions are:

- Does a facies exist that one setting produces and the others cannot, in a low-grade succession? Geyserite and sinter as described by Djokic et al. (2017) are a candidate; confirm or refute in the oldest rocks.
- Do multiple isotope systems in the same sample (C, S, Fe, O, Si) plus REE + Y patterns separate a terrigenous from a hydrothermal source, as the Frontiers review (2023) reports for the Josefsdal Chert?
- Can metamorphic overprint be ruled out, so that a low δ13C value is not a product of catagenesis or graphitisation (Zeichner et al. 2024)?
- Is there a real instrument, at any resolution, already returning a reading that fails to resolve two settings? If none can be named for a pair, that pair is `unreachable` at this budget, and Fig 8 :39 applies: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Standing refuters, pre-registered in `01` §4: a discriminator that moves with the interpreter; two "independent" readings from one pool; a trace produced identically by all settings; a clean partition where a union was claimed; a ground reading that actually names the setting.

## 5. The honest stop

The answer stops at the instruments. The settings are habitats, enumerated and read off the world; the discriminator is a shape with seven constraints; the winner is not named. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and the canonical README says the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* and stops where instruments stop. What remains outside is a seat's office: a geologist with a thin section, a mass spectrometer, and a declared stake. Nothing here can replace that.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell us where life began. It describes what would count as evidence that could. The four proposed settings are frames, so the first requirement on a discriminator is that it behave as an anchor: a measured mineral, isotope ratio or facies whose reading does not change when the interpreter's preferred setting changes. The settings must be enumerated as habitats, read off the rocks rather than composed from prebiotic-chemistry vocabulary, and unbundled, because they overlap. The observation must already be registering at some instrument resolution, must distinguish the source of a reading and not just the reading itself, and must survive diagnosis and metamorphism. Bulk carbon isotopes and spheroidal microfossils fail that source test on their own. A facies that one setting produces and the others cannot, such as geyserite and sinter for hot springs, is the logical form of a discriminator. The derivation stops there: which setting actually hosted life's origin is a `gap` that only rocks and instruments can close, and the refuters that would break this shape were written before the run.
