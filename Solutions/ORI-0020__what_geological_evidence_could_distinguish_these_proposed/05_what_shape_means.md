## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (the full line is *"→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and the same passage says the framework *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

*The Library of Babel* gives the operational version: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For ORI-0020 this means: a shape is a description of the **kind of observation that would settle which setting was actual**, stated before any setting is named. It constrains. It does not fill. "An anchor that only one setting can produce" is a shape. "Geyserite proves a hot-spring origin of life" is a name plus a verdict, and it smuggles in the ground reading the derivation does not have.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, proximity: use the vocabulary closest to the thing and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"* (elements 30 and 31). The effect here is a discrimination failure between settings; the cause would be a speculative narrative about which setting did it.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* The vocabulary for the settings comes from geology reviews, not from the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* In this question the finish line is an instrument reading that discriminates; until one exists, the search returns shapes, not names.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"SETTLE the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test. A geological result imported into the framework must be labelled as coming from geology, with author, venue and year, and must explain a gap the framework already described.

For ORI-0020, naming a winning setting would require an actual measurement of an early rock that carries a setting-diagnostic signature, with diagenetic and metamorphic overprint ruled out. No such measurement is in this derivation. So no setting is named.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the exact failure mode for this question. An instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. The resolution came *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

Applied here: a low δ13C value is a reading, not a source. Zeichner et al. (2024) show the same value can be biotic, abiotic or a product of catagenesis and metamorphism; Knoll et al. (2016) show hydrothermal fluids can generate spheroids resembling microfossils. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (facies, mineralogy, stratigraphic context, multiple isotope systems in the same sample), not a confident name attached to a bulk value.

This is also the Fig 12 :117 condition: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Different settings can be evidence-level isomers, identical in the bulk formula and different in the arrangement that a good instrument resolves.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What shape must a discriminating geological observation have?** It must be:

1. **An anchor.** Its reading must not move when a proponent of a different setting pushes on it (Fig 2 :29).
2. **Described before the frame.** The residual, a discrimination failure, is described in effect terms before choosing isotope geochemistry or sedimentology as the instrument (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. **Enumerated from the habitat.** Conditions, actors and relations are read off the geological literature, not composed from prebiotic-chemistry vocabulary (Fig 11 :44, :48).
4. **Already registering.** A real instrument must already return something that fails to resolve; no sensor can be specified for a trace that has never registered at any resolution (Fig 6 :32, :40).
5. **Source-discriminating.** At least one setting predicts the trace and the others cannot produce the same value; a bulk reading that several settings share is not a discriminator (Fig 12 :117; Hard Problem essay).
6. **Durable.** It must survive diagnosis and metamorphism, or be measured in a low-grade succession where the primary signature still exists (Zeichner et al. 2024; Djokic et al. 2017 on low-grade preservation).
7. **A union-aware result.** It must be sought per unbundled condition, because the settings overlap (Fig 9 :54).

That is a shape with seven constraints. It is not an answer to "which setting?" The figures forbid the framework from naming that; the ground must.

## 6. Consequences for contact and for honesty

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A list of famous origin-of-life researchers who agree with a setting is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the shape claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground, a thin section or a mass spectrometer, settle it.

**This derivation is a simulation.** Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: LLM output is *"an approximate answer/output"* whose testable form *"would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So §5 is a shape derived from the figures and checked for internal consistency, not a test of any geological claim. It cannot settle question 20 and must be labelled as such wherever it is sent.
