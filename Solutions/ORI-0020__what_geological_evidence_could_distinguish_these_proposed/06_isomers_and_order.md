# Supporting derivation: settings as isomers, dependency order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the analogues and the falsifier, `general` on the structural rules. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

The four proposed settings produce overlapping chemical products. Two of them can yield the same bulk observation (a carbon-isotope value, a spheroid, a chert). Two supporting questions are owed: (1) in what sense are the settings **isomers** at the level of evidence, and what would break that reading? (2) what **order** governs the settings, and how does duration enter before chemistry is scored?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the candidate conditions read off the geological literature, water, temperature, pH, ionic strength, gradients, UV, mineral surfaces, time. Bonds held: the eight operators of the framework, in particular ⋛ comparison, → sequence, − subtraction and ≡ identity. No new primitive is available without import. The settings are **frames** in Domain 1 (see `01` §2); the rock record is the candidate ground in Domain 2. This derivation works over the mapping between them.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the settings isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As written in question 18, the settings share many conditions but differ in which condition is bound to which. A deep vent and a shallow pond both have mineral surfaces and gradients; the difference is the arrangement: gradient over space and persistent anoxia versus gradient over time and wet-dry cycling with UV. Fig 13 :76 makes the underlying distinction: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- Therefore the settings are **evidence-level isomers**: same chemical atoms, different arrangement of conditions, potentially different preserved product. The bulk formula cannot tell them apart; the arrangement can. This is an analogue, not a geological derivation from chemistry. The figure governs composition; geology governs which products actually form.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either setting can be written without an atom the other requires, and the products are nevertheless identical in every accessible measurement, the isomer reading fails and the settings are merely a functional group of environments (same kind, different member). If a facies exists that one setting produces and the other cannot, the isomer reading stands. Djokic et al. (2017) supply a candidate: *"neither marine nor lacustrine settings can account for the preservation of geyserite, sinter terracettes or the mineralized remnants of hot spring pools."*

### Q2 - What is the order among the settings?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"** (the element continues past the portion quoted elsewhere).
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- The settings are not ranked by plausibility. They are ordered by dependency: first, is there water and a persistent energy source; second, are there conditions that afford concentration and polymerisation; third, can the system say what would count as progress (Fig 3 :9-11); fourth, does the habitat persist long enough for the chain to close; fifth, does a lineage survive to leave a trace.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Duration for a deep vent versus a shallow pond is a measured quantity in the field literature, not an assumption. Westall et al. (2018) tabulate stability and duration as first-class properties of each environment.
- **Order verdict:** dependency order, not importance order. A setting whose chain breaks at the persistence link cannot be rescued by a richer prebiotic pathway.

### Q3 - Does the union change the isomer claim?

- Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- The four labels overlap. A volcanic field can host a pond; ice can overlie a vent. So "vent versus pond" is not a symmetric two-term comparison at every site. The isomer claim holds per unbundled pair of conditions, not for the four labels as a whole.
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** A compound site is a different object, with its own discriminator.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a trace that separates the four labels as a clean partition with no compound case and no overlap. If found, the union reading in `01` F7 is withdrawn.

### Q4 - Analogues and what they license

- **Metamerism (from *The Hard Problem is About Source*, 2026):** several sources yield one reading; the reading does not determine the source; a spectrometer, not an argument, resolves it. Licenses the demand for a source-discriminating instrument and forbids naming a setting from a bulk value.
- **Contact cascade (Fig 11 :52-53):** *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Licenses looking for a trace exported from the setting rather than only at the setting.
- **Isomerism in chemistry (Fig 12 :97-98):** *"Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation. Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; named isomerism by Berzelius, 1830."* Licenses the claim that identical composition does not imply identical substance; it does not itself supply the discriminator.
- **Threshold comparison (Operators table :48):** ⋛ is **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. A discriminator often has this form: above a temperature or below a pH, a mineral forms or does not. The operands must be quantities at that level; where they are facies descriptions rather than quantities, the ⋛ reading is strained and is recorded as such, not smoothed.

## CONCLUSION

The settings are a defensible **evidence-level isomer** claim: same broad chemical atoms, different arrangement of conditions, different preserved product in principle. The claim is `specified` and carries a stated falsifier. They are **ordered by dependency**, not ranked by plausibility, and duration is a link in that chain rather than an afterthought. The union of overlapping conditions means the isomer comparison must be made per unbundled pair. One strain is named and kept: ⋛'s operand type when the operands are facies rather than quantities. No setting is named as the winner.
