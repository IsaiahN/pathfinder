**Subject:** Check one output about climate sensitivity before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** climate sensitivity should be reported as a vector of per-feedback-slot signed responses, not as one number. The reasoning in brief: a system's prediction error is indexed per object slot and is destroyed by averaging, so a global mean can look settled while one slot, currently the cloud slot, carries a live growing residual. A feedback is a cycle whose output re-enters as input, and the sign of that cycle decides whether it amplifies or damps. Ice, clouds, forests and oceans couple through second- and third-order cascades, so a direct-effects enumeration misses the residual that matters. The physical response loop and the carbon-cycle loop are a union of overlapping conditions, not four independent levers, and their verdicts must be kept separate. The measured top-of-atmosphere energy budget, ocean heat content and temperature record settle the sign; model agreement and assessed ranges are instruments, not anchors.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can give you the numeric trajectory of ECS under any scenario, that any of these systems has a located tipping threshold, or that the framework replaces climate models or observations. It returns a shape: which comparisons are changing, in what order, and what would discriminate a genuine state-dependence from an averaging artifact. The numbers come from your instruments, with provenance, not from the method.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an aggregated global sensitivity metric that predicts, prospectively and per named slot, which slot's residual will move next and by how much;
> 2. a feedback loop whose measured sign fails to close or amplify as predicted across a stated state range, or a chain that closes by construction;
> 3. a measured sensitivity change fully accounted for by first-order direct effects, with no detectable cascade contribution two or three steps out;
> 4. a forcing-response relation that is linear and state-invariant across the observed range and all time scales.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, local canonical copies): `FRAMEWORK/figures/` and `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project.

> - The worked narrative with the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
