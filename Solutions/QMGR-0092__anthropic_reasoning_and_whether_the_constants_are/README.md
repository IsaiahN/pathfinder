## The question restated as an effect

Two claims are bundled in the one question. (1) A descriptive claim: the dimensionless constants of physics fall inside life-permitting ranges that look narrow relative to the values the theories allow. (2) An explanatory claim: anthropic reasoning, the fact that we could only observe values compatible with our existence, explains why we see what we see.

What each option leads to. If the narrow ranges are real relative to a measure, an anchor is owed that fixes the measure and does not move when we push on it. If anthropic reasoning is only a selection filter, then it explains the bias in our observations but cannot settle whether the constants are fine-tuned, and no amount of filtering inside one universe can supply the missing anchor. The difference is decisive for what we should do next: keep looking for a ground, or stop.

What an answer would look like. A shape: a comparison between a constant's observed value and its life-permitting range under an independently motivated measure, with the slots kept separate and the measure left unnamed until a ground settles it. Bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula, "Direction").
