# Structure, order and analogues for the fine-tuning question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the analogy mapping. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The descriptive claim reports narrow life-permitting ranges; the explanatory claim says anthropic selection explains why we observe them. Two structural questions are owed. (1) Are the constants *isomers* under Fig 12's definition, and if not, what is their structure? (2) What is the *order* of the comparison buried in the phrase "fine-tuned for life", and where does the dependency chain stop? Analogues are then checked for valence rather than borrowed as decorations.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the constants isomers? No, and the question is a category error at first order

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

An isomer claim needs two molecules sharing an atom set and differing in the bond. The constants are not molecules; they are values that occupy slots in a theory. The fine-structure constant and the strong coupling are not two arrangements of the same six atoms, and they do not differ in bond type. So the isomer question does not apply to them as held.

The nearest figure-licensed structure is the **functional group**: Fig 12 :124, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Each constant is a member of the group "free parameter whose value is constrained by experiment", with the qualifier giving which parameter. The group is shared; the members are not isomers.

A stronger isomer claim becomes available only after settlement: if each constant's first-order molecule (value attached to a state through a spectrum-like ordering) settles and becomes an atom, then two theories over the same settled atom set could differ in arrangement and be isomers at second order (Fig 12 :104). That is a checkable structural claim, not a claim about the values themselves.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two constants are shown to share an atom set and differ only by a bond, so that Fig 12's definition applies at first order, the "category error" reading fails.

### Q2: The comparison is ordered

Fig 12 :86 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities". Operators table :49-51 gives the identifying test: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Under Fig 12 :59, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? Yes" marks an ordered bond.

"Value V lies inside permitted range P" is not the same claim as "range P lies inside value V". Swapping the operands changes the assertion. So fine-tuning is an **ordered comparison**, not a symmetric conjunction. This matters because the common phrasing "the constants and life are finely matched" hides the order: the question is whether the observed value falls inside a range fixed independently of it. If the range is fixed from the value, the comparison is circular and its operand is authored.

**Falsifier:** write the fine-tuning claim as a symmetric bond with no loss of content. If that succeeds, the ordered-bond reading fails.

### Q3: Structure: two orderings, one state, and a second-order comparison

Fig 13 :19-20: "spectrum / ordering, so more and less can be said." Both a constant's value and its life-permitting range are spectrum-like orderings. Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." A direct ⋛ between two bare orderings would join two structures, which the substrate forbids.

Re-census resolves this the same way the exemplar resolves its ⋛ case. Each ordering first attaches to a state: the measured value to our universe's energy state, the permitted range to states in the ensemble or in the deeper theory. Once each side settles, each becomes an atom for what composes over it (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"). The comparison is then second order and legal.

**Live strain, not hidden.** The Operators table restricts ⋛ to "a threshold relation between two quantities". At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded here and in `01` §7.

**Falsifier:** show that two orderings compose directly under a state-free bond and that the framework admits it, or show that a settled atom cannot be an operand of ⋛ at any level. Either would break the reading.

### Q4: Dependency order, and the break

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces." The five links for this question:

1. **Perception:** measured values and calculated ranges exist. Fig 3 :5: "the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before." This link works.
2. **Vocabulary:** terms for what a value *affords*: "life-permitting", "measure", "observer". Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford". This link has no settled instrument for "life" or "measure".
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress? Here: a narrowed per-slot residual against an anchor that does not update. Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms." Without step 2 fixed, step 3 cannot be stated sharply.
4. **Planning:** choose the lever. More parameter scans, a deeper theory, or an ensemble measure.
5. **Learn and carry:** record the lineage so the stop is checkable.

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The break is between 1 and 2. Fig 3 :22: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can." Effort at link 4 (pick a multiverse) cannot rescue link 2. A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing.

**Falsifier:** produce a working instrument for "life-permitting" that does not presuppose the answer, and the break moves; this section would then need re-census.

### Q5: Analogues, checked for valence

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." An analogue is not a witness; it is a structure carried into a new domain, and its valence must be re-read.

**(a) Spectrometer / metamerism, strongest analogue.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source", and "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Applied: the observation of life-permitting values is a reading; what is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources, not another argument. Valence: high and positive for the residual; it says what is missing (a discriminator), not what the answer is.

**(b) Leslie's firing squad, strained analogue.** SEP *Fine-Tuning* §3.2 describes a prisoner who expects execution and finds himself alive, and asks whether he should infer the marksmen intended to miss. Sober's analysis says no, because the selection effect is already built in; Leslie and others say that recommendation is implausible. Valence is **mixed**: the firing squad is a human-case analogue with an agent who has intentions, while the constants case has no agent in the frame. Under Fig 11 :49 the same actor is not automatically the same kind of thing. It is recorded as an analogue with a strain, not used as a verdict.

**(c) Weinberg's Λ bound, convergent-derivation analogue.** Fig 8 :50-51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The bound was computed before the measurement and carried an explicit falsifier (Barnes 2021). Valence: positive for the *method* (fix the prior and the refuter first), negative for any claim that this alone establishes a multiverse. The same numerical result would be a retrodiction if the distribution were fitted after.

**Falsifier for the whole analogue set:** show that an analogue's valence transfers without re-reading it against the residual, and Fig 11 :49 is broken.

## CONCLUSION

The constants are not isomers at first order; they are members of a functional group, with the comparison of value to range as an ordered ⋛ bond at second order. The two orderings compose legally only after each attaches to a state and settles into an atom. The dependency chain breaks at the vocabulary link: "life" and "measure" lack instruments. The only analogue with clean valence is the source-discriminating instrument; the firing squad is strained and the Weinberg case is a method analogue whose status depends on provenance. What survives is the structure and the order, not a value for any constant and not a verdict on whether the universe is fine-tuned.
