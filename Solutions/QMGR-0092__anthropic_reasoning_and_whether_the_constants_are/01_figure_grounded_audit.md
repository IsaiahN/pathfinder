## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question bundles two claims that behave differently under the figures.

- **D, the descriptive claim.** Measured dimensionless constants sit inside ranges compatible with life, and those ranges look narrow next to the ranges the theories permit. This is a claim about the world and has an anchor: measurement and calculation.
- **A, the explanatory claim.** We observe life-permitting values because only such values could produce observers. This is a claim about an inference under selection. Its anchor is not given inside the frame: the fact used as evidence (we exist) is the same fact the inference is trying to explain.

**R, the residual.** Per slot, the gap between (i) the observed value of a constant together with its calculated life-permitting range and (ii) an anchor that would say whether that range is narrow relative to a measure over possible values. When no such anchor is available, the residual persists and does not trend down.

**Arity:** many slots, one per constant, not one aggregate. **Symmetry:** the comparison is ordered, not a symmetric conjunction (a value and a range are not interchangeable). **Scale:** first order inside one universe, second order across an ensemble of universes or a deeper dynamical theory.

**What a refused answer would look like.** "Anthropic selection settles whether the constants are fine-tuned." That is a filter handing a verdict, which Fig 9 forbids. **What an acceptable answer would look like.** A shape: a comparison under an independently motivated anchor, with the slots kept separate and the measure unnamed until the ground pays for it. Bound as an ordinary term so it can be refused (Formula, "Direction").

## 3. Findings

### F1: R is indexed per constant, not aggregated (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The question asks whether "the constants" are fine-tuned. That collapses roughly 26 to 31 free parameters into a single score. The standard presentations already resist this: Rees lists six dimensionless numbers, and Barnes lists the individual cases (cosmological constant, Higgs vev, quark masses, force strengths) separately. But the rhetorical form "the universe is fine-tuned" re-aggregates them.

Consequence: one slot can be genuinely narrow (the cosmological constant) while another is broad or not assessable (the weak force, the baryon-to-photon ratio). A verdict must be recorded per slot.

**Refuter:** produce a single aggregate measure of fine-tuning that is figure-licensed, that does not average away a live slot, and that the ground pays for. If one is produced, this finding is withdrawn.

### F2: Anthropic selection is a filter, never a witness (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

The weak anthropic principle says we must take account of being compatible with our existence. This is a bias in which observations we can make. It tells us where to look (only life-permitting values), which is a budget/filter use. It does not tell us whether the values are fine-tuned relative to a measure, which is a verdict. The observation "we exist" moves with the frame when the frame is pushed, so it fails Fig 2's anchor test.

World corroboration, low overlap with the figures: SEP *Fine-Tuning* §3.2 records the anthropic objection and notes that on Sober's analysis "We exist, and if we exist, the constants must be right" makes both likelihoods equal to 1. Wikipedia's *Anthropic principle*: "No possible observational evidence bears on Carter's WAP, as it is merely advice to the scientist and asserts nothing debatable."

Important narrowing, written here rather than hidden: this does **not** forbid anthropic inference inside an imported ensemble. It forbids a filter from handing the verdict about the ensemble itself. See the re-census in section 8.

**Refuter:** show a case where conditioning on our existence yields a ground-settled verdict about the constants without an independently motivated measure, and where the conditioning does not merely redistribute where we look.

### F3: No anchor inside the anthropic frame (single-source for the figure, corroborated)

Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

The anthropic frame produces the selection weight from the same fact it is asked to explain. It cannot score itself with it. Declaring a reference class (all observer-moments, all universes) does not ground the class; it makes the choice visible. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is high, so an independent anchor is worth paying for.

What would qualify: a measure over possible constants that (a) is motivated independently of the observed values, and (b) does not move when our observation is pushed. Barnes's account of Weinberg's Λ bound is the candidate case: assuming a roughly uniform distribution near zero and asking what a typical observer sees. The candidate fails the source gate if the distribution was chosen after the value was known.

**Refuter:** name an anchor for the fine-tuning claim that does not update on our existence and is not authored by the frame that needs it.

### F4: From inside our closure the measure is unreachable (triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

From the standard model plus GR alone, there is no route to a distribution over values of the constants. More analysis of the same theories adds no measure. And we cannot tell from inside whether the answer is depth (a deeper theory or an ensemble we have not reached) or genuine absence (there is no fact of the matter about the distribution).

Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions." These are two different questions. If the measure is unreachable at this budget, the remedy is more search within the closure, not another atom. If it is underivable in principle from our frame, the remedy is an import. Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :42-43).

World corroboration: SEP *Philosophy of Cosmology* (Smeenk and Ellis): "there exists only one universe, so there is nothing else similar to compare it with". Barnes on the measure problem: "an infinite multiverse theory cannot justify probabilities and so cannot make predictions".

**Refuter:** derive a measure over the constants from within the empirical standard model plus GR, with no new frame and no authored prior, and have it predict a value not yet measured.

### F5: The question is a union compound, not one hard problem (triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Three loops are bundled: (i) measurement of the life-permitting ranges (anchor: experiment), (ii) inference under observation selection (filter, not witness), (iii) explanation of why the constants have the values they do (anchor: a dynamical law or a measure). A single lever cannot clear all three. Answering (ii) does not answer (i) or (iii).

The Library of Babel essay gives the same instruction in effect terms: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."

**Refuter:** show one lever that settles all three sub-questions at once without leaving an intersection standing.

### F6: The middle link has no instrument (triangulated)

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :22: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."

The word "life" lacks a settled definition, and the word "measure" lacks a settled construction. SEP *Fine-Tuning* §1.2 names the first: a common worry is the "lack of a widely accepted definition of 'life'". Barnes names the second as the measure problem. The dependency chain stops at vocabulary/objective. Most effort in this debate lands at the top (more parameter scans) or at the planning end (choose a multiverse), because those are where the measurements already exist. Fig 3 :29: "Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."

A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing. Until the middle link has an instrument, fine-tuned-for-life is not yet a settled claim.

**Refuter:** produce an instrument, not an argument, that discriminates life-permitting from life-hostile ranges across the parameter space, and that survives an independent check.

### F7: Fine-tuning is a comparison bond, not a mechanism (triangulated)

Fig 12 :86: "a threshold relation between two quantities" defines the comparison bond ⋛. Fig 12 :89: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

"Constants fine-tuned for life" is a threshold relation between an observed value and a permitted range under a measure. It is ordered: swapping value and range changes the claim. It is goal-shaped, not a mechanism. The framework can state the comparison; it cannot supply a dynamical reason for the value. SEP §2.3 records Einstein's hope that a final theory would make the constants "rationally completely determined", and notes that string theory's landscape has not delivered that.

**Refuter:** write the fine-tuning claim without any comparison bond, keeping the same evidential content, and show that nothing is lost. If that succeeds, the bond reading fails.

### F8: The framework returns a shape, not the measure (triangulated)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Fig 13 :137-138: "And it can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."

The canonical figures README (read 2026-11-23) says the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and stops at the limit of the instruments. Applied here: the shape is a comparison of a value against a permitted range under an anchor. The framework cannot name the measure. Choosing a multiverse measure, a theistic designer, or a brute given is a binding from another frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation from the six.

**Refuter:** derive the measure from the six-term set alone, with no import and no authored convention.

### F9: Absence of a mechanism is not proof of fine-tuning (triangulated)

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 6 :27 (above) says depth and genuine absence look the same from inside.

The failure of the LHC to find supersymmetry at the probed scales leaves the naturalness problem unresolved, but does not establish that the constants are fine-tuned for life. Equally, finding a narrow life-permitting range does not establish that the universe is fine-tuned: without a measure there is no reference distribution against which "narrow" is a comparison. Wikipedia *Fine-tuned universe*: "There is no empirical evidence of a multiverse and it is not considered a falsifiable hypothesis." That is a statement about the current ground, not a proof of absence.

**Refuter:** show an inference from "no mechanism found" to "the constants are fine-tuned" that the figures license and that does not require an imported measure.

### F10: Provenance decides whether the measure was predicted or fitted (single-source, live)

Fig 8 :50-51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Fig 6 :12-14 gives the import gates: "source gate: provenance", "shadow test: explains a gap", "the only operator that moves the wall".

Weinberg's 1987 anthropic bound on the cosmological constant, and its later agreement with observation, is the strongest case for an anthropic inference. Its status depends on the record: was the distribution assumed before the measurement, or chosen to accommodate it? Barnes reports the prediction before observation and states what would have falsified it: "If we had observed a value that was ten orders of magnitude smaller than the upper bound, then we would conclude that one of the assumptions in our model is probably wrong." That is a pre-registered-style refuter, and it is the thing worth sending. If the distribution is fitted after the fact, the same numerical closeness is a retrodiction with zero import evidence.

**Refuter:** show a multiverse measure whose prior and refuter were fixed before the constant was measured, and whose novel prediction then survived. If none can be shown, the import stays ungated.

### F11: Declare the stake (single-source, structural)

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Seat Map :171 (local: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md`): "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

I have an interest in the outcome: this derivation is being produced as a Solution, so I gain if it is judged sound. That interest is declared. I have no interest in which value the ground returns about the constants: whether they are fine-tuned or brute given changes nothing in my frame. The reading is therefore the part to watch. The pre-registered refuter in section 4 is the instrument for that, not a disclaimer.

**Refuter:** show a stake in the reading that has not been declared here.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Seat Map :287 (local: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md`): "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is this: R is per-constant; anthropic selection is a filter and never the verdict; an anchor is owed and is unreachable from inside our universe's closure; the owed object is an ordered comparison under a measure that the framework cannot name. Written before the conclusion, this claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor produced.** A frame-independent anchor over the constants is exhibited, does not update on our existence, is not authored by the frame that needs it, and yields a novel successful prediction of a constant value rather than a fit to an already known one.
2. **Residual dissolved.** Under an independently motivated measure, the life-permitting ranges are shown to be broad, so the per-slot residual does not register (the Adams 2019 and Stenger 2011 direction).
3. **Dynamical derivation.** A theory derives the values of the constants from principles, so the comparison has a fixed reference and "fine-tuning" dissolves (Einstein's hope, SEP §2.3).
4. **Filter promoted.** The framework is shown to admit a selection filter that settles a ground verdict without an imported measure, breaking Fig 9 :60.
5. **Census broken.** One of the six terms is derived from the other five, or the per-slot residual is shown to be genuinely aggregate, breaking the census in section 1.
6. **Bond reading fails.** The fine-tuning claim is written without a comparison bond and loses nothing.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`. These are written before the synthesis in `07` and the WATSON verdict in `09`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Unbundle first.** State the three loops before answering any of them (F5). Do not let "are the constants fine-tuned?" stand as one question.
2. **Keep the slots separate.** Report per-constant verdicts, never an average (F1).
3. **Label the filter.** Say plainly that anthropic selection tells us where to look, not what is true (F2). This is the single most important correction to the usual framing.
4. **Name what is owed.** The missing thing is an anchor over possible values: a measure, a dynamical law, or a demonstration that the ranges are broad. Describe it in effect terms; do not pick one (F3, F4, F8).
5. **Keep provenance.** Report whether any proposed measure was fixed before or after the value was measured (F10). Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the generator and the refuter, not the conclusion.

## 8. Updated verdict

The figures settle the shape of the reasoning and do not settle whether the constants are fine-tuned in the ground sense.

**Survives.** R is per-constant. Anthropic selection is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict. An anchor over possible values is owed and is unreachable from inside our universe's closure. The owed object is an ordered comparison, second order, under a measure the framework cannot name. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

**Does not survive as stated.** "The constants are fine-tuned for life" without a measure. "Anthropic reasoning explains why the constants have these values" without an imported ensemble and a gated measure. "No mechanism found, therefore fine-tuned" (Fig 8 :39). Any aggregate one-number fine-tuning score (Fig 1 :22).

**Status.** `specified` on the structural verdicts, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the ground verdict, `candidate` until an anchor pays. Mode stated on every substantive claim. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
