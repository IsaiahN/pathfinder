# Outreach draft: check the inference before deciding whether the constants are fine-tuned

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until section 5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been followed. The stage-1 output must state the unbundling and the filter/witness distinction, or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one inference before you decide whether the constants are fine-tuned

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific inference I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the inference does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, `specified`, not `grounded`.** The question "are the constants fine-tuned for life?" bundles three different questions: (1) are the life-permitting ranges actually narrow, which measurement can answer; (2) what does the fact that we exist license us to infer, which is a question about observation selection; (3) what fixes the values of the constants, which needs either a deeper dynamical theory or a measure over an ensemble. A single answer cannot clear all three. I keep the constants as separate slots; I do not average them into one score.
>
> **The inference I want checked.** Anthropic selection tells us where we can look: only at values compatible with observers. That is a filter on our attention. It is not a verdict about whether the values are fine-tuned. To make that verdict we need an anchor over possible values that does not move when we push on it. We do not currently have one. From inside our universe's closure we cannot tell whether the answer is depth (a deeper theory or measure we have not reached) or genuine absence (there is no fact of the matter about the distribution). Both look the same from here.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that the constants are fine-tuned, and I am not claiming that they are not. I am not claiming that a multiverse exists, that a designer exists, or that the values are a brute given. Those are bindings from other frames. The framework returns a shape, not a name: a comparison between a value and a permitted range under an anchor, with the anchor left open until something settles it. "Fine-tuned for life" is a threshold comparison, not a mechanism.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent this note:**
> 1. A frame-independent anchor over the constants is exhibited, does not update on our existence, and yields a novel successful prediction of a constant value rather than a fit to one already known.
> 2. Under an independently motivated measure, the life-permitting ranges are shown to be broad, so the residual does not register.
> 3. A theory derives the values of the constants from principles, so the comparison has a fixed reference and "fine-tuning" dissolves.
> 4. A selection filter is shown to settle a ground verdict without any imported measure.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I can send the verbatim figure text extract and the source list. Please tell me first whether you want them.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures link below. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a written refuter is the only way to make the output checkable. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name / role]

## One-to-one rule

Send to a single person at a time, with their name and field filled in. Do not copy a list. Do not sort recipients by how likely they are to agree: that manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Record the stage-1 verdict before showing any framework material. Do not add later agreement to the evidence column.
