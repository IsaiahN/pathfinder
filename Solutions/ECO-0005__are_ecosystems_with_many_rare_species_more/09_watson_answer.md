## QUESTION

Are ecosystems with many rare species more resilient, or can rarity make them especially vulnerable to change? Restated as an effect: does a community abundance distribution P ("many rare species") fix the sign of the per-slot residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` under a change C? Option A: P makes R shrink (resilient). Option B: P makes R grow (vulnerable). What each leads to: A invites a general pro-rarity stability law and a management rule that protects rarity as a count; B invites a general fragility law and a management rule that reduces rarity. The derivation tests whether the menu of two options is well formed before choosing from it.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The offered menu is malformed. The question is a union of overlapping conditions, and rarity is a relation-relative position rather than a kind with a fixed sign. Under D1 no universal count-to-resilience or count-to-vulnerability law is available, so the verdict is `gap`, which is a report about this closure and not a claim that the world has no answer. Under D2 a managed habitat can reach either branch because introduction is available and exclusion is not, and the work is permanent. Under D3 the sign cannot be computed until a function and perturbation are named and the threshold fixed before data. What survives is the shape: per-slot residuals, a non-updating anchor, an enumerated contact cascade, union branches kept separate, and a source-discriminating measurement to close it. Mode `general` / `specified` / `gap`, never `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** the shape could cause a researcher to split a question that actually has a single count-based answer, spending effort on anchors and definitions unnecessarily. That cost is bounded and locatable because every split is reversible and every refuter is written down. The larger cost runs the other way: accepting the menu and reporting a universal sign from a count would hide per-slot signals (Fig 1 :22) and would mistake a shared evidence pool for corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no empirical claim is relied on. The shape can be superseded by naming an anchor and running one of the refuters in section 4 of `01`. A management recommendation built on it (introduce redundancy rather than remove rarity) is reversible because it adds actors rather than subtracting habitat.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A replicated multi-ecosystem experiment with function, perturbation and rarity definition registered in advance that returns one signed response with the other branch absent; a measurement showing all rarity effects are first-order with zero cascade; a definition of rarity invariant across abundance, range, habitat specificity and functional distinctiveness that leaves the sign unchanged; two frames with non-overlapping evidence ranging against the same non-updating anchor; or a figure re-census that relocates Fig 11 :49-51 to a different quantity. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
