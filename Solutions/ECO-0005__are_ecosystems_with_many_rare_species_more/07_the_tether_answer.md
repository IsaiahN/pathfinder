# The Tether's answer: rarity, resilience and vulnerability

**Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` for the per-domain readings, `gap` for a universal count-to-outcome verdict. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No anchor was measured in this derivation.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers two answers to one question: many rare species means more resilient, or rarity means especially vulnerable. The residual is the failure of a count heuristic to predict a per-slot response: `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` indexed by species, function and interaction level (Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Aggregating across slots is the failure mode named in Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A community can gain insurance in one function and lose it in another at the same time.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

### D1: Unmaintained habitat. Verdict `gap` on the aggregate claim, triangulated

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Rare species are not a kind with a fixed sign. Their sign is set by the residual, that is, by which function and which perturbation are named. So neither "more resilient" nor "especially vulnerable" is a licensable general verdict from the count. Both are reachable: Mouillot et al. 2013 report in the same datasets both that rare species *"disproportionately increase the potential breadth of functions provided by ecosystems across spatial scales"* and that species supporting the most vulnerable functions are *"rarer than expected by chance in all three ecosystems."* The two statements are about different residuals, not contradictory results. Fig 11 :52-53 adds that the effect can be three steps out: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

**Refuter:** an invariant valence of rarity across a documented change of residual and of which species are rare.

### D2: Maintained habitat. Verdict `specified`, single-source

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Fig 11 :22: *"the ground still decides."*

In conservation and restoration, a seat can add actors but cannot subtract the habitat. That makes both branches reachable by intervention: adding functionally redundant species buys insurance; concentrating function in a few rare unique species creates vulnerability. The count is not the lever; the bond arrangement is (Fig 12 :94, *"The arrangement is the substance."*). The asymmetry means the defensible default is introduction of redundancy, not removal of rarity.

**Refuter:** a managed system in which exclusion of an actor is genuinely available and the habitat can be subtracted.

### D3: Assessment frame. Verdict `general`, triangulated

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (full line).

"Resilient" and "vulnerable" are undefined until the function and perturbation are named, and the threshold is fixed before the data are seen. Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The five ecology papers read here share authors and citations (Mouillot in three), so their agreement is one evidence pool talking, not five witnesses. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).

**Refuter:** two frames with genuinely non-overlapping evidence returning the same per-slot sign against a non-updating measurement.

### The union above the three domains

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The question is a union of overlapping conditions. The same rare species can be insurance for one perturbation and a single point of failure for another. Split by function, perturbation, scale and rarity definition before answering. Otherwise a forced choice is an artefact of the question's menu.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that rare species are always beneficial or always harmful for stability.
- Not that diversity begets stability as a general law, and not a revived diversity-stability conclusion.
- Not a measured result. No function and no perturbation was named and measured here, so nothing is `grounded`.
- Not that a higher species count is better, or that rarity should be eliminated from managed systems.
- Not that the ecology papers constitute independent corroboration. They are one partially shared pool.
- Not that a `gap` verdict means the question is unanswerable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the colour case was closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Concretely:

1. **Name the anchor.** Fix a function (primary productivity, pollination, nutrient cycling, invasion resistance, functional richness) and a perturbation (drought, fire, disease, warming, invasion) before the count.
2. **Fix the definition.** Choose and register the rarity metric and threshold before seeing the data; Jain 2014 showed the sign changes with the definition.
3. **Discriminate the source.** Measure separately whether the effect comes from functional redundancy (insurance), from a unique response trait (vulnerable function), or from a cascade two or three steps out. A single "stability" reading conflates all three.
4. **Buy independence.** At least one frame with separate evidence and a declared stake, ranged against the same non-updating anchor.
5. **Keep slots separate.** Report per-slot residuals, never a global average.

## 5. The honest stop

`gap` on the only verdict the question actually asks for (a universal signed answer from a count). That is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134, *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*). What is reachable now is the shape: any settling answer must be per-slot, anchor-named, valence-relative, contact-enumerated and union-split. The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list, and the ecology terms imported into it are bindings with provenance, not new primitives. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. `Indiscernibility is not identity`: a count reading that cannot discriminate insurance from vulnerability demonstrates its own inadequacy, not the absence of a difference.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "more resilient or more vulnerable". It says that the question is a union of two overlapping conditions that must be split before it is searched, that rarity is a position in a distribution whose sign is relative to the residual rather than a kind with a fixed valence, and that capability is a property of the community and its habitat rather than of a species count. The effect can sit two or three contact steps away from the rare species, which is why a first-order count misses it. In managed habitats introduction is available and exclusion is not, so either branch can be reached by intervention and the work is permanent. No figure line closes a universal count-to-resilience law, so the verdict here is `gap`: a report about what the held closure cannot settle, not a claim that the world has no answer. What the framework returns is the shape any settling answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it: a named function, a named perturbation, a fixed rarity definition, and a measurement that separates insurance from vulnerability. The sign stays open until the ground settles it.
