## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question: are ecosystems with many rare species more resilient, or can rarity make them especially vulnerable to change?

Effect reading. An ecosystem is a system that takes a perturbation (change) and returns a response in some function. Let the held prediction be a count heuristic: `many rare species -> outcome X`. The residual is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot `s` (Fig 1 :11, Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Here the slots are not "the ecosystem" as one global number; they are individual species, individual functions or services, and individual trophic or interaction levels. Slots matter because Fig 1 :22 says *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A system can be resilient in one slot and vulnerable in another at the same time, and a global average would hide exactly the signal the question asks about.

What is in dispute is the **valence** of the property "many rare species": whether that property is a benefactor or a malfactor with respect to a named change. Two options are offered: (A) more resilient, (B) especially vulnerable. A third option, that valence depends on the residual, is not on the menu. The derivation must test whether the menu is well formed before choosing from it.

Anchor test. Fig 2 :29 defines an anchor: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* For "resilient/vulnerable" the natural anchor is a measurement of a named function under a named perturbation. Until the function and the perturbation are named, there is no nameable anchor. Fig 4 :20 says only the actors, currency and anchor change between scales; if the anchor slot is blank, there is not yet a domain to census. This is recorded as a finding (F5), not used as a reason to skip the census.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. A line with no nameable anchor is marked provisional, not answered.

- **Arity.** R relates two operands: a community property (an abundance distribution) and a perturbation outcome. It is therefore at least two-place, and a comparison term is needed before it is one-place verdict.
- **Symmetry.** The relation is not symmetric. A perturbation is directed, the response is not interchangeable with the property, and "resilient" versus "vulnerable" is a threshold comparison, not a conjunction. Fig 12 :87 and the Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* A count plus a comparison is a different molecule from a count alone.
- **Scale.** Three scales appear in the literature actually read: local abundance within a community, regional occupancy across communities, and metacommunity or landscape insurance across scales (Mouillot 2013, Jain 2014, Leitão 2016). The membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4 :27-32), so a verdict at one scale is not a verdict at another.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Unmaintained habitat, natural ecosystem under change | species (rare and common), conditions (Fig 11 :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*), relations (Fig 11 :47 *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*) | pure contact, what the ground kept (Fig 11 :15) | the perturbation and the measured function that settle persistence | admitted, anchor to be named per case |
| D2 Maintained habitat, conservation or restoration management | the conservation seat, introduced or reintroduced actors, instruments | actions, with introduction available and exclusion not (Fig 11 :61) | the ground still decides (Fig 11 :22) | admitted |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :54 says *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* "Resilient through rarity" and "vulnerable through rarity" are overlapping conditions: the same rare pollinator can be a source of insurance in one perturbation and a single point of failure in another. The Library of Babel essay (read 2026-11-23) names the same failure: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** An anchor must be named before a relation can be compared (Fig 3 :9-11 objective, then :12-13 planning). The habitat must be enumerated before the frame can be assessed (Fig 11 :43). The frame's independence must be checked before agreement counts (Fig 2 :31-32). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A global species count is an optimisation above the break if the objective link (which function, which perturbation) was never measured.

Strain test applied before concluding: a figure line that only fits by forcing is reassigned to another quantity, and the census is redone. Two strains were found and resolved (section 6).

## 3. Findings, each with verbatim lines, refuters and mode

### F1: The question is a union compound, not one hard question (decisive for the menu)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 (as cited in the validated pilot): *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The two offered options overlap. A rare species can support a vulnerable function and also provide insurance under a different perturbation. Mouillot et al. (2013, opened 2026-11-23) report exactly this double result: the same dataset yields that *"Rare species offer more than aesthetic, cultural, or taxonomic diversity value; they disproportionately increase the potential breadth of functions provided by ecosystems across spatial scales"* and that species supporting the most vulnerable functions are *"rarer than expected by chance in all three ecosystems."* The names "resilient" and "vulnerable" are not two answers to one question; they are two residuals.

**Mode:** `general` for the union reading. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, different fields, low overlap). **Refuter:** a controlled design in which one fixed definition of rarity yields one signed response for one named function under one named perturbation, with the other branch demonstrably absent across all tested perturbations.

### F2: Rarity has no standing valence

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Rare species are not actors with a fixed sign. Their sign is decided by the residual, that is, by which function is at stake and what changed. Jousset et al. (2017, opened 2026-11-23) state the same condition in ecological terms: *"Species that are considered functionally non-relevant under a given environmental condition may become important under changing conditions by providing necessary traits or acting as partners in new interspecific interactions."* Jain et al. (2014, opened 2026-11-23): *"whether rare species offer novel functioning or are redundant depends on the way in which rarity is defined."*

**Mode:** `general` for the rule, `specified` for the ecological binding. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 habitat valence plus the ecology sources, low overlap between a metatheory figure and empirical papers, but note the ecology pool shares citations, see F8). **Refuter:** an invariant valence of rarity across a documented change of residual and a change of which species are rare.

### F3: First-order counts miss the cascading residual

Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

Counting rare species is a first-order enumeration. The effect that matters can be two or three steps out: a rare batfish identified by Mouillot et al. as a key species in reef regeneration after a phase shift to macroalgae, a role many common herbivores could not play; a rare tree whose thick bark buffers fire; a rare pollinator resource in a species-poor alpine cliff. Weisser-style indirect effects in the microbial literature (Jousset 2017) show rare microbes changing the behaviour of abundant microbes and rescuing communities from lethal stress. A count at first order cannot see these.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 cascade rule plus the DDT case inside the same figure and the empirical keystone examples, different strata). **Refuter:** a case where all reported effects of rarity on a named function are demonstrably first-order with zero cascade contribution.

### F4: Resilience is a contact property, not a count property

Fig 11 :39: *"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

The capacity to absorb change is a property of the community and its habitat together, not of the abundance distribution in isolation. Leitão et al. (2016, opened 2026-11-23) found *"a disproportionate impact of rare species extinction for the three groups, with significant reductions in levels of functional richness, specialization, and originality of assemblages"* and noted that *"the asynchrony of species responses to environmental fluctuations is an important mechanism through which biodiversity can stabilize ecosystem properties."* Stability comes from the arrangement of responses, which is a relation among actors, not a headcount.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 capability plus Fig 12 arrangement-bearing claim plus the empirical functional-structure results). **Refuter:** a named function whose response to a named perturbation is invariant under substitution of which species are rare while the count is held constant.

### F5: The anchor is blank, so the verdict is not yet decidable

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."*

"Resilient" and "vulnerable" are not defined until a function (primary productivity, pollination, nutrient cycling, invasion resistance, functional richness) and a perturbation (drought, fire, disease, warming, invasion) are named. A threshold chosen after seeing the data is a convention that decides an outcome, which Fig 10 :47 forbids: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The eight essays add the instrument rule from *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): a reading *"cannot comment on the source,"* and the case was closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

**Mode:** `general` for the anchor requirement. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 10 plus the essay, low overlap). **Refuter:** a case where the function and perturbation are named before the count is taken, a non-updating measurement settles the sign, and the sign holds across independent frames.

### F6: Agreement among studies is one witness until independence is bought

Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (the full line begins with this clause). Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

The papers read this session overlap: Mouillot appears in Violle 2017 (as co-author), in Leitão 2016 (as co-author), and is cited by Jain 2014 and Jousset 2017. That is a shared evidence pool, not five independent confirmations. Fig 2 :18-21 names the collapse: *"one evidence pool"*, *"they never talk, and still agree"*, *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 9). **Refuter:** two or more frames with genuinely different evidence bases (for example a microbial experimental-removal study and a tropical tree census) returning the same per-slot sign with declared non-overlap.

### F7: Managed habitats can reach either branch; the work is asymmetric

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Fig 11 :22: *"the ground still decides."* Fig 11 :39 (above).

In a maintained habitat a conservation seat can add actors (reintroduction, assisted migration, restoration of a mutualist), but cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. The seat can buy redundancy or can create a new dependency on a few functionally unique rare species. Both "more resilient" and "especially vulnerable" are reachable states of a managed system, and neither is settled by the count. The asymmetry means the safer intervention is introduction of redundancy, not removal of rarity.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the asymmetry and permanent-work claims (Fig 11 alone); corroborated at the empirical level by restoration literature read indirectly through the ecology sources, but not independently measured here. **Refuter:** a managed system in which exclusion of an actor is genuinely available and the habitat can be subtracted, which would break the asymmetry.

### F8: Rarity is a distribution property, not a species kind

Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"* (the spectrum entry). Fig 11 :50 (above): relations rather than kinds. Fig 10 :18 (above): a declared meaning is not a grounded one.

Rarity is an ordering over abundance, range and habitat specificity. The papers read operationalise it in incompatible ways: Rabinowitz seven forms (cited in Violle 2017 and Jain 2014), Violle's twelve forms of functional rarity, Jain's four independent metrics, Mouillot's two scales with two thresholds, Leitão's integrated rarity index. Jain 2014 reports that *"the influence of rare species on FTV depends on how rarity is defined."* A count under a threshold is a convention; changing the threshold changes the result without changing the world. This is the same failure as choosing the comparison after seeing the data (F5).

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 13 substrate ordering plus Fig 11 relation-not-kind plus the empirical definition-dependence). **Refuter:** a definition of rarity that is invariant across scale, metric and threshold and still yields the same signed verdict.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is this package's conclusion: no signed verdict is available from the count alone, and the answer must be a union split with a named anchor. It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Fixed-valence refuter.** A replicated, multi-ecosystem experiment holds the definition of rarity fixed, names one function and one perturbation in advance, and finds one signed response with the opposite branch demonstrably absent across all tested perturbations.
2. **No-cascade refuter.** All reported effects of rarity on the named function are shown to be first-order, with zero contribution from second- or third-order contacts, breaking Fig 11 :52-53 for this case.
3. **Definition-invariance refuter.** A definition of rarity is found that is invariant across local abundance, geographic range, habitat specificity and functional distinctiveness, and the signed verdict does not change under it, breaking F8.
4. **Anchor-refuter.** Two independent frames with non-overlapping evidence returns the same per-slot sign against a non-updating measurement of a named function, with the disagreement structure of Fig 2 panel 3 rather than panel 2, breaking F6.
5. **Figure re-census refuter.** A second seat shows that Fig 11 :49-51 governs a different quantity and does not apply to ecological rarity after honest re-census, with the line relocated and the strain named.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn and the count verdict may be admitted. Until one is run, nothing is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer "more resilient or more vulnerable." Split the question into per-domain, per-function, per-perturbation residuals and answer those, with mode stated (F1, F5).
2. Name the anchor before the count. Write down the function and the perturbation, fix the rarity definition and threshold before the data are seen, and register the refuter (F5, F8).
3. Enumerate the habitat rather than composing it: actors, conditions, relations, then second- and third-order contacts (Fig 11 :43-48, :52-53).
4. Keep verdicts per slot. A global average is the failure named in Fig 1 :22, not a summary.
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. The five ecology papers are one partially shared pool; add at least one frame with separate evidence before treating agreement as anything (F6).
6. In management contexts, prefer introduction of redundancy over attempts to subtract habitat; the seat cannot exclude (F7).
7. Send the generator (residual, shape, refuter) rather than the conclusion, through the public venues in `03` (Fig 10 :52, Fig 4 :22).
