# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the anchor is named in the recipient's own terms. If they cannot say which function and which perturbation they mean by "resilient" and "vulnerable", the offer collapses on contact, because there is no referent for the claim (F5 in `01`).

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about rarity and ecosystem response, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to
> read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive,
> there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** The question is often put as: are ecosystems with many rare
> species more resilient, or does rarity make them especially vulnerable? In error-signal terms,
> `R = |prediction − observation|` measured per slot, where the slots are species, functions and
> interaction levels, never one global average. The prediction being tested is a count heuristic:
> "many rare species means outcome X". What failed to resolve is the sign of X. In the figures this
> is Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
>
> **The shape claim (`general`, not `grounded`).** The pair of options is a union of overlapping
> conditions, not a partition. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a
> partition, unbundle before you search again."* Rarity has no valence of its own: Fig 11 :49,
> *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* So the answer cannot
> be read off a count. It is read off a named anchor (which function, which perturbation) and an
> enumerated set of contacts (who interacts with whom, and what happens two or three steps out).
> Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the
> eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three
> steps out."*
>
> **What this does not claim.** Not that rare species are always good, or always bad, for ecosystem
> stability. Not that diversity begets stability as a general law. Not that a longer species list is
> a better one. Not a measured result. The framework returns a shape here, not a signed answer:
> Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A replicated, multi-ecosystem experiment with the rarity definition, the function and the
>    perturbation all named in advance returns one signed response, and the opposite branch is
>    demonstrably absent across every perturbation tested.
> 2. All effects of rarity on the named function are shown to be first-order, with zero cascade
>    contribution.
> 3. A definition of rarity is found that is invariant across local abundance, range, habitat
>    specificity and functional distinctiveness, and the signed verdict is unchanged under it.
>
> If any of those holds, the count may carry a verdict after all and I will retract the shape claim.
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not
> engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Question for you, before anything else:** what would you count as a failure here? And which
> function and which perturbation are you actually ranging "resilient" against?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local path in the packet): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule and metalaw note (local paths in the packet): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, `FRAMEWORK/reference/60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`
> - Ecology sources actually read: the five papers in `10_source_ledger.csv`, with author, venue, year and quote
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link in the packet. Run it on
> a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result
> *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me
> than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual first, theory withheld | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter before conclusion | The discipline rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* |
| Mode on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Union named before options | Fig 9 :54-57; the Library of Babel essay: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* |
| Ask for the anchor, not for trust | Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* |
| Links only after the verdict is recorded | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| No "bombshell", no "true answer", no prestige request | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22-25: a generator may cross up; a recording of one success may not. |
| Ecology sources declared with overlap | Fig 2 :40: the five papers share authors and citations; their agreement is one pool talking, not five witnesses. |

## Distribution discipline

One-to-one only. No mail merge, no recipient list ranked by likely agreement, no follow-up unless the recipient opts in. A Track B style receptiveness estimate would select for the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40). If a refutation comes back, copy it verbatim into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it in a fresh census, rather than answering it from inside the frame that produced the claim.
