## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The offered question is a union of overlapping conditions, not one question with two candidate answers, so it must be split by function, perturbation, scale and rarity definition before it is searched. Rarity has no valence of its own: its sign is relative to the residual and to the bond arrangement among species. In unmaintained habitats no universal count-to-outcome verdict is available, so the verdict there is `gap`. In maintained habitats introduction is available and exclusion is not, so either branch can be reached by intervention and the work is permanent. The framework returns the shape any settling answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. It does not return the sign.

### C2: ecology-facing; checkable against the empirical literature

- **Statement:** the sign depends on which function, which perturbation, which scale and which rarity definition; the same rare species can supply insurance in one context and a vulnerable function in another; effects can sit two or three contact steps away.
- **What to check:** Mouillot et al. 2013 (PLOS Biology, opened 2026-11-23) report both branches in the same three ecosystems: rare species *"disproportionately increase the potential breadth of functions provided by ecosystems across spatial scales"* and species supporting highly vulnerable functions are *"rarer than expected by chance in all three ecosystems."* Jain et al. 2014 (Ecology and Evolution, opened 2026-11-23): *"whether rare species offer novel functioning or are redundant depends on the way in which rarity is defined."* Jousset et al. 2017 (ISME Journal, opened 2026-11-23): *"Species that are considered functionally non-relevant under a given environmental condition may become important under changing conditions."* Leitão et al. 2016 (Proceedings B, opened 2026-11-23): *"a disproportionate impact of rare species extinction for the three groups, with significant reductions in levels of functional richness, specialization, and originality of assemblages."* Fig 11 :52-53 gives the cascade rule: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **Refuter:** all reported rarity effects on a named function are strictly first-order with zero cascade contribution, and the signed verdict is unchanged across all four rarity definitions.
- **Mode:** `specified`. **Overlap warning:** these five papers share authors and citations (Mouillot in three). By Fig 2 :40 this is one evidence pool, not five independent confirmations.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the case was closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

1. Name a function and a perturbation before the count; fix the rarity metric and threshold before the data are seen.
2. Measure separately whether the effect comes from redundancy (insurance), a unique response trait (vulnerable function) or a cascade two or three steps out.
3. Range at least two frames with non-overlapping evidence against the same non-updating anchor.
