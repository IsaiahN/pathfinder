# Structure, order and isomers: what "many rare species" leaves out

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The count heuristic says that the number (or share) of rare species determines the sign of an ecosystem's response to change. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) does the species list, with its abundance distribution, carry the response, or does the arrangement (the bonds between species) carry it? (2) what is the dependency order in which an answer must be assembled?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the ecological frame: species identities, abundances, geographic ranges, habitat specificity, functional traits, and environmental conditions. Bonds held: the figure's seven (conjunction, disjunction, identity, sequence, production, subtraction, comparison) plus negation. No new primitive is needed to write the arrangements below. Currency: contact and settlement against a named perturbation. Anchor: the measured function, still to be named per case.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is a species list the substance, or is the arrangement the substance?

Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Two communities with the same species list and the same abundance distribution can have different interaction networks (who pollinates whom, who predates whom, who competes with whom, who depends on whom). By Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their responses to the same perturbation can differ in sign. This is the structural reason a count cannot decide the question: the count records the ingredients and not the bond, and Fig 12 :14-16 says *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"* and *"and nothing signals that anything was lost"*.

Empirical corroboration, secondary only: Leitão et al. 2016 found that rare species over-contribute to *functional structure* (richness, specialisation, originality), not merely to the list; Mouillot et al. 2013 found the same species can support vulnerable functions and provide insurance depending on trait context. Both are findings about arrangement.

**Mode:** `general` for the isomer rule, `specified` for the ecological binding. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 arrangement plus Fig 11 relations-not-kinds plus the empirical functional-structure results). **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two communities with identical species lists and identical abundance distributions but demonstrated different interaction networks return the same signed response for the same named function under the same named perturbation, the isomer reading fails and rarity may be treated as a count after all.

### Q2: What order does the answer have to be assembled in?

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

Dependency order for this question:

1. **Objective link.** Name the function and the perturbation. Without this there is no comparison term and no anchor (Fig 12 :52, *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*).
2. **Vocabulary link.** Fix the definition of rarity and the threshold before the data are seen (F8 in `01`). A threshold chosen afterwards is a convention deciding an outcome, which Fig 10 :47 forbids: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
3. **Habitat link.** Enumerate actors, conditions and relations outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-48). Fig 11 :52-53 gives the DDT-to-eagles case where the residual was three steps out.
4. **Frame link.** Check independence of the witnesses before agreeing (Fig 2 :31-32). Only then assess the comparison.

Reading taken below a break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). A global stability number computed before steps 1 to 3 is below the break; it cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can.

### Q3: Cascade order and the cycle structure

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 11 :52-53 (above) gives the ecological case. First-order enumeration is a chain; a cascade is a cycle because the effect changes the habitat that then changes the effect. Mouillot's batfish example (reef regeneration after a phase shift to macroalgae) and the microbial examples in Jousset 2017 (rare taxa changing the behaviour of abundant taxa, then rescuing the community) are closing loops, not linear chains. A chain model of rarity cannot represent them.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source for the cycle mapping to ecology (Fig 12 plus Fig 11, two figures with different subject matter but both framework-internal), corroborated at the empirical level by the opened papers. **Falsifier:** demonstrate that all reported rarity effects on a named function are strictly first-order with no feedback from the response back into the actor set.

### Q4: Functional groups and the "functional group" reading of rarity

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Rare species that share a response trait form a functional group: the group says what kind of disturbance response is possible, the qualifier says which species performs it. Fig 12 :119 adds *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* A rare mutualist that is regenerated after disturbance and a rare mutualist that is spent by it look identical before the perturbation. This is the metamerism point from *The Hard Problem is About Source* in structural form: the reading does not discriminate the source. Violle 2017's response-trait versus effect-trait distinction is the ecological vocabulary for the same gap.

**Falsifier:** a measurement that discriminates spent from regenerating rare actors before the perturbation, with the sign of the response predicted by that discrimination alone.

### Q5: Same atoms, different bond: the union is a compound, not a bigger pile

Fig 9 :57: *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*

"Rarity provides insurance" and "rarity creates vulnerability" are not two entries to be averaged. They are two molecules over the same atom set, distinguished by which bond (production of redundant function versus comparison against a unique dependence) holds. Under a symmetric bond the spellings collapse to one term; under an ordered bond different orders are different terms (Fig 12 :92-93). Perturbation order matters here: a species that becomes abundant after fire and then supplies a function is a sequence, not a conjunction. A count cannot see sequence.

## CONCLUSION

The supporting derivation confirms the main audit. The species list plus abundance distribution is an ingredient list, not the substance; the bonds between species carry the sign of the response. Dependency order requires an objective (function and perturbation) before vocabulary, habitat and frame. Cascades are cycles, not chains. The union branches are isomers over a shared atom set, distinguished by bond, and cannot be averaged into a global verdict. None of this requires a seventh primitive or an ecological import that moves a closure wall (Fig 6 :23 is about imports between frames, not about translocation of species).
