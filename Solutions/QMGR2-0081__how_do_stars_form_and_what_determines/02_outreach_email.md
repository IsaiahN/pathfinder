# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the mechanism

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survives the refuter in section 4. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold.

**Subject:** Four conditions on any answer about stellar masses, and what would break them

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, not `grounded`):** the question "what determines a star's final mass" is not one variable. It is a union of overlapping conditions: the mass reservoir a core starts with, the gradient that turns gravity into a flow, the environment the core lands in, the accretion and feedback that follow, and the reading we make of the resulting distribution. Four things follow from a domain-agnostic structure I use, stated as effects:
>
> 1. The residual has to be tracked per object and per mass regime, not averaged into one universal slope. Averaging is where the signal about where to look disappears.
> 2. The seed does not carry the tree. A core carries what can grow a star; the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes. This does not pick core accretion over competitive accretion. It says the endpoint is not pre-loaded.
> 3. The observed mass distribution is a projection. Several mechanisms can produce the same reading, so the distribution alone cannot name its source. That needs an instrument, not a better argument.
> 4. Feedback has no standing valence. The same outflow can limit mass in one regime and protect accretion in another, depending on what the residual is.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that these conditions yield a Jeans mass, an IMF slope, or a characteristic stellar mass. No figure line returns a number. That part is a declared `gap`, and a gap is a verdict here, not a silence. I am also not claiming that the framework settles the physics. The ground is the telescope, the survey and the simulation checked against them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before this run:
> 1. a figure line that produces a numerical mass or slope from the six-term set;
> 2. a demonstration across environments that final mass is fully set by initial core mass with no role for subsequent conditions;
> 3. per-regime residuals that all trend down under one mechanism, so aggregation preserves the signal;
> 4. an instrument that names the mass-setting source directly from the observed distribution.
>
> If any holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and their verbatim text are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local extract used for the citations above): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`

>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
