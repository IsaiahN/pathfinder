## What the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The problem is a union, with universal shapes but non-universal transitions.** Hennebelle and Grudić (2024) list several IMF regimes and say the regimes are likely universal while the transitions are not. That is the union signature in F2 of `01`: decompose before searching for one lever.
2. **The peak and the tail may have different causes.** Bonnell, Larson and Zinnecker (2006) and Larson (2002/2003) assign the characteristic mass to fragmentation and thermal physics, and the high-mass tail to continued accretion in clusters. Hennebelle and Grudić (2024) add that jets can regulate by unbinding gas. These are different compounds, not one mechanism.
3. **The endpoint is not fully carried by the core.** Myers, Dunham and Stephens (2023) report core-to-star efficiencies around 0.3 to 0.5, meaning much of a core's mass does not end in the star, and they test whether outflows can clear enough gas. This is the observed counterpart of Fig 4's "conditions decide."
4. **Universality is contested rather than settled.** Bastian, Covey and Meyer (2010) find no clear systematic variation; Kroupa and Jerabkova (2019) say evidence challenges invariance. Both were opened. The disagreement is evidence that the regularity is a convention-grade observation, not an anchor for mechanism (F8).

All astronomy sources come from the same broad research community and overlap in citations; they are one witness about the shape, not several independent witnesses. The three framework essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely and carry no independent weight. Overlap is recorded per row in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
