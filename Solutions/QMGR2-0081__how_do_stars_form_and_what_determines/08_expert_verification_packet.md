# Expert verification packet — the Tether on star formation and stellar masses

**What this is:** a claim in a form an astronomer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, `gap` on the mechanism, not `grounded`.** No mass, slope or characteristic scale is asserted. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "what determines a star's final mass" is a union of overlapping conditions, so the residual must be tracked per object and per mass regime rather than averaged into one slope. Collapse is a legal cross-kind composition: energy bonded to a gradient gives a flow, and the mass distribution is a spectrum. A core does not carry its final mass as a seed carries a small tree; it carries what can grow a star, and the conditions it lands in decide the outcome. The observed distribution is a projection, so several mechanisms can give the same reading and a source-discriminating instrument is owed. Feedback has residual-relative valence. The low-mass and high-mass channels are at minimum a functional group and possibly isomers over a shared atom set. The framework returns this shape and its falsifiers, not the mechanism.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 13
- **Statement:** the residual is indexed per slot and per mass regime, and the problem is a union of overlapping conditions, so one averaged mechanism verdict is the wrong object. Collapse is a legal first-order cross-kind flow.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- **Refuter:** show per-regime residuals all trending down together under one mechanism so aggregation preserves the signal; or exhibit a collapse requiring two states bonded directly, or two structures with no intervening opposite-kind term. Either fires against C1.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable by an astronomer against the mass problem
- **Statement:** the endpoint is not pre-loaded in the initial core; conditions, accretion and feedback decide it; and the observed distribution cannot by itself name the source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :34-35 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 6 :33 "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."
- **What to check:** is the core-to-star efficiency and its regime dependence consistent with an environment-decided endpoint rather than a fully preloaded mass? Is there an observation that separates candidate sources of the same mass reading? The opened literature (Myers, Dunham and Stephens 2023; Bonnell, Larson and Zinnecker 2006) gives concrete numbers and tests.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration across environments that final mass is fully determined by initial core mass with no role for subsequent conditions; or an instrument that names the source directly from the distribution. Either fires against C2.

### C3 — structural analogue; checkable by comparing the two mass channels
- **Statement:** over the full shared atom set the low-mass and high-mass channels are isomers (same atoms, different bond, different substance, Fig 12 :117); as currently described they share only the first-order collapse group, the weaker functional-group claim (Fig 12 :124). Feedback is a cycle with residual-relative valence (Fig 12 :121, Fig 11 :49).
- **What to check:** can both channels be written over the same six atoms, differing only in arrangement? If one requires an atom the other lacks, the functional-group reading stands and the isomer claim is withdrawn.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a channel that cannot be written without an extra atom, or a feedback actor with identical valence across all regimes and residuals. Either fires against C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a Jeans mass, IMF slope, characteristic mass or upper mass limit. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").
- Not a choice among turbulent fragmentation, competitive accretion, stopped accretion, or any other named mechanism. Those are imports from astronomy, not figure derivations.
- Not that the near-universal IMF is settled either way. It is a strong regularity, efficient as a convention, disqualifying as a verdict about mechanism (Fig 2 :40-41).
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken by this derivation.
- Not a standing list of mass-setting actors (Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual").
- Not a final theory of star formation (Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room").

## 5. What would settle it

1. An observation discriminating core-mass-set from environment-decided endpoints (core-to-star efficiencies across regimes; the opened 2023 ApJ paper is the right shape of instrument).
2. A decision on whether the two channels share the full atom set (isomer) or only the collapse group (functional group).
3. A source-discriminating signature that breaks the projection ambiguity of the IMF.
4. The standing falsifiers in `01` section 4: a figure line returning a number; mass fully carried by the core; residuals collapsing under one mechanism; a source-naming instrument.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 4 :34-35, Fig 9 :32/:57, Fig 13 :74/:76 — five short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge: is the endpoint environment-decided rather than preloaded in the core, and does the IMF alone name a mechanism? A one-line answer is enough.
3. Name one observation that would discriminate between two candidate mass-setting mechanisms, or say why none exists at current resolution.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
