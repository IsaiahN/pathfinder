# The Tether's answer on star formation and final stellar masses

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain shape claims, `gap` on the specific mechanism and any numerical mass, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05` section 7), not a ground reading. No telescope, survey or simulation was run for this package.

## 1. The residual it answers

We can observe clouds, cores, disks, outflows and a distribution of stellar masses. We cannot read, from the distribution alone, which conditions set an individual star's endpoint. R is per object and per mass regime, not a single average slope. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so one lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-57). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Track the residual per slot and per regime.** The low-mass peak, the intermediate regime and the high-mass tail are specific compounds, not degrees of one difficulty (Fig 9 :57). A universal slope is a regularity worth explaining, not a verdict about mechanism.

2. **Gravity plus a density gradient composes a flow.** Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." Cross-kind composition is legal (Fig 13 :74). Collapse is a first-order molecule, `state → structure → state`; the mass distribution is a spectrum (ordering, so more and less can be said), and the cloud is a network. These are bindings onto held atoms, provenanced, not new primitives.

3. **The seed does not carry the tree.** Fig 4 :34-35 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." A core carries a mass reservoir and what can grow a star; environment, accretion, competition, ejection and feedback decide the endpoint. This does not pick core accretion over competitive accretion. It refuses the picture of a mass fully pre-loaded.

4. **The conditions form a union with residual-relative valence.** Turbulence, thermal cooling, magnetic fields, accretion and feedback bind in intersections (F2, F11). Feedback is a cycle whose sign depends on the residual: the same outflow can terminate accretion in one regime and protect it in another (Fig 11 :49, Fig 12 :121). No standing list of mass-setting actors survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51).

5. **The observed distribution cannot name its source.** It is a projection. Several mechanisms produce overlapping readings, so a source-discriminating instrument is owed (F6, F7). Fig 6 :33 "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."

6. **The two channels are a functional group now, possibly isomers.** Over the full shared atom set they differ by bond and are isomers (Fig 12 :117); as the literature currently writes them they share only the first-order collapse group and differ by qualifier (Fig 12 :124). Which holds needs an instrument, not an argument (`06` Q3).

7. **No final theory of star formation.** Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room." Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Each model closes some of the gap; no finite set arrives.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a Jeans mass, an IMF slope, a characteristic mass, or a maximum stellar mass. No figure line returns a number. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not a choice between turbulent fragmentation, competitive accretion, or any other named mechanism. Those are world imports from astronomy, provenanced as such.
- Not that the near-universality of the IMF is false or true as a physics verdict. It is a strong regularity, useful as a convention; its agreement is within one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40-41).
- Not that the framework settles the physics. The anchor is the observation and the instrument, not agreement among readers (Fig 2 :29, :45).
- Not grounded. Nothing here was settled by a measurement taken in this run.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. The questions the instruments must answer:

- Does the core-to-star efficiency and its regime dependence discriminate a preloaded mass from an environment-decided one? The 2023 ApJ outflow study opened for this package reports efficiencies around 0.3 to 0.5 and tests whether outflows clear enough gas. That is the right shape of instrument.
- Do the IMF regimes share a set of atoms and differ only in arrangement? If yes, the isomer claim is settled; if not, the functional-group claim stands.
- Is there a direct observational signature that separates the candidate sources of a given mass reading? Until one exists, the metamerism limit holds.
- Standing falsifiers: derive one of the six from the other five; measure final mass fully carried by initial core mass across environments; show per-regime residuals all trending down under one mechanism; produce an instrument that names the source directly from the distribution.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The observations exist and the instruments are improving; the framework's limit is a limit on what it can compose, not a claim that astronomy cannot answer. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The answer stops where the available instruments cannot distinguish sources and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not deliver a theory of star formation or a number for stellar mass. It says that the question is a union of conditions, so the residual must be tracked per object and per mass regime instead of averaged into one slope; that collapse is a legal cross-kind flow of energy and gradient; that a core does not carry its final mass like a seed carrying a small tree, because the conditions it lands in decide the outcome; and that the observed mass distribution is a projection whose source needs a discriminating instrument, not a cleverer fit. Feedback has no fixed sign, so the same outflow can limit mass or protect accretion depending on the regime. The two mass channels are at minimum a functional group and possibly isomers over one shared atom set. What the framework returns is this shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of observation that would close the gap. The ground, not agreement, decides.
