## The question restated in effect terms

The question asks for two things that are usually answered together but have different anchors. The first, how stars form, asks for the route from a cold molecular cloud to a self-luminous object. The second, what determines final mass, asks which conditions, actors and relations set the endpoint of that route. Option A: answer with a single universal mechanism and one slope. Option B: describe the shape every answer must have, keep verdicts separate per mass regime and per domain, and mark the specific mechanism as `gap` until a source-discriminating instrument settles it. What each leads to: A aggregates per-slot residuals into one average and hides where to look; B keeps the residual indexed and states what would refute it.

An answer would look like a constrained shape plus a route to a ground. Under the formula's Direction step, that sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused. It is bound here as: per-slot, union-compound, seed-does-not-carry-the-tree, projection-does-not-name-the-source.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this shape.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures return a Jeans mass, an IMF slope, or a characteristic stellar mass. They return a shape, not a name.
- No claim that this package settles the physics. The ground is the telescope, the survey, the interferometer and the simulation checked against them. Agreement among readers is not that ground.

Three reasons, in figures' own words:

1. **Room is not anchor.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless (Fig 2 :40).
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not this derivation.
3. **A convention is not a verdict about the world.** Fig 2 :41 "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" is efficient for a convention, while the same correlation disqualifies a verdict. The near-universal IMF is treated as a regularity to be explained, not as an anchor that settles the mechanism.
