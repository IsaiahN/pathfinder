## RESIDUAL

Two supporting questions are owed. First, what is the legal structure of a collapse and a mass distribution under the six-term set? Second, what is the relation between the low-mass and high-mass formation channels: a partition, a functional group, or isomers? A falsifier is stated for each.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Legal structure of collapse

- Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow."
- Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"
- Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

A collapse is a first-order molecule: `state (energy) → structure (gradient) → state (energy redistribution)`, one crossing. The gradient must be bonded to a state; a gradient over space and one over time are different terms, so the collapse of a core and the secular evolution of a disk are different molecules even if both are flows. No state-state or structure-structure bond is needed. The composition is legal at first order.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a collapse that requires two states bonded directly, or two structures bonded directly with no intervening opposite-kind term. Then Fig 13 :75 fails for this object and re-census is owed.

### Q2. Order: union, not sequence

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 12 :75 "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (sequence bond →, from the Operators table)

One might write `cloud → core → protostar → mass` and treat the arrow as sequence. That is a trap. Fig 12 :75 makes → an ordered bond, and the mass is not produced by a single chain; it is the intersection of conditions that coexist. The correct order is dependency among checks, not chronological production: describe the residual per slot, then have terms for what a core affords, then state what counts as progress, then settle first-order molecules before second-order ones (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"). Sequence is legal for the *life history* of one star; it is not the structure of the *question*.

**Falsifier:** show that the mass regimes are ordered by a single production chain in which each regime is absent until the previous one fires, with no overlap. Then the union reading collapses to sequence.

### Q3. Are the two channels isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Consider the low-mass channel (fragmentation dominated, thermal physics setting a characteristic mass) and the high-mass channel (continued accretion in a cluster). If both are written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair and in the direction of the flow, they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The observed difference in slope is decisive in behaviour while invisible in a formula that lists only ingredients.

If instead one channel requires an atom the other does not, for example if the high-mass channel genuinely needs a network atom (clustering) that the low-mass isolated-core picture lacks, then they are not isomers. They share a **functional group**, the first-order `state → structure → state` collapse, with different qualifiers. The honest default is the weaker functional-group claim until an instrument establishes the shared atom set.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either channel can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If both can be written over exactly the same six atoms with different bonds, the functional-group claim is upgraded to isomer.

### Q4. Feedback as cycle and catalyst

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input."
- Fig 12 :119 "A part that survives its own use."
- Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

An outflow is produced by accretion and then alters the accretion that produced it: the output re-enters as input, a cycle. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes. A disk that persists through several accretion episodes and continues to feed and collimate the wind is a catalyst in Fig 12's sense: a part that survives its own use. Spent envelopes and regenerating disks behave differently across uses, and a formula that lists only "gas, disk, wind" cannot tell them apart.

Valence is not carried by the outflow. In a low-mass core it may remove enough envelope to terminate accretion and set the efficiency; in a massive clump it may open channels that let radiation escape and thus protect accretion. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." This is why F11 of `01` refuses a universal mass-limiter.

**Falsifier:** show that one and the same feedback actor has identical valence across all regimes and residuals, so a standing list is possible. Then the valence-relative claim fails.

### Q5. Two currencies in the verdict

- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A one-line IMF formula is cheap in bits and expensive in actions to settle; a full radiation-hydrodynamic simulation is expensive in bits and may be refuted by a single observation. Reading only one currency misprices the choice. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." The budget is a filter on how much of the mechanism space is searched; it is not a witness for which mechanism is true.

**Falsifier:** produce a case where description cost and action cost add in one unit so that a single total orders the options. The figures keep them in different units by construction.

## CONCLUSION

Collapse is a legal first-order cross-kind composition. The question's order is dependency, not production sequence. The low-mass and high-mass channels are at minimum a functional group and possibly isomers over the full shared atom set; which one holds is an instrument question, not an argument. Feedback is a cycle with residual-relative valence and catalytic parts. Description cost and action cost are held apart. None of this returns a mass or a slope; all of it is a shape with falsifiers attached.
