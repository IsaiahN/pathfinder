**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether a definition of computation is worth writing

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` per substrate, not `grounded`):** the question "how should computation be defined in analog, chemical, biological and quantum systems" asks for a definition keyed to the substrate. The derivation refuses that form. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not. What survives is one invariant loop: a bounded system whose prediction is compared with an observation, whose mismatch is indexed per slot rather than averaged, whose new terms are licensed only when they are supported, reachable and new, and whose settlement is paid by something that does not change its answer because of what any observer thinks. The four named systems are different casts of actors, currency and anchor for that one loop, not four definitions.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that analog and digital are two kinds of computation, that discreteness is a primitive, that "computation" is whatever an observer maps onto a physical process, or that one substrate is the real one. Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are what a composition leaves behind, not extra members of a minimum set. Hybrid, mixed and overlapping cases are the rule in the literature, so the four classes form a union with leaking boundaries, not a partition.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a substrate property that changes whether a process can compute while ground contact and the per-slot residual are held fixed;
> 2. two substrate classes that need different loop structure, not just different actors, currency and anchor, while both settle at the ground;
> 3. a clean partition of the four with no hybrid, no shared atom and no mixed discrete and continuous process;
> 4. a mapping-only criterion that settles a verdict against a non-updating anchor, with no frame-internal scoring;
> 5. a global computes or does-not-compute bit that predicts failure locations as well as per-slot residuals do.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Open item, stated rather than hidden:** in quantum systems the state changes on measurement, so the state cannot be the anchor. The recorded outcome is offered as the anchor. Whether a recorded outcome counts as something that does not update in the figure's sense is a type question I cannot settle from inside; I would value a correction on exactly that point.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
