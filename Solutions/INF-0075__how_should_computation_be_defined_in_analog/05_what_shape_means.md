## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name it

The canonical figures README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."

From *The Library of Babel* (carried): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question the shape is not a fourth definition of computation. It is the set of constraints any candidate definition must satisfy: a per-slot residual, a non-updating anchor, gated minting, settlement by what pays, and a cast of actors/currency/anchor. The four named substrates are not shapes. They are the places the shape is instantiated.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried): use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

Here the residual was described before any substrate frame was chosen: a verdict about a process that does not shrink when more descriptions are added. Only then were analog, chemical, biological and quantum systems cast as domains. Fig 8 :53, "Residual first, frame second."

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).

*They're Scanning Books* (opened this session) sharpens this and is the governing essay for this question: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." So naming counts, concentrations, qubits or gradients is a visible choice from a substrate literature, not nature's own list.

The same essay gives the reason per-substrate definitions cannot be imported for free: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Adding a substrate-specific primitive as a new criterion would be a new atom with no source gate and no shadow test.

## 4. Why "computation" cannot be named from a reading

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session) gives the mechanism. An instrument that detects green "is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." The same reading can come from one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or "a completely novel color scheme that humans have not yet discovered". The essay's conclusion: the case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Applied here: a process's observed regularity (symbol transitions, trajectory, product yield, measurement statistics) is a projection. Naming the source of that projection as "computation in substrate S" repeats the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator that distinguishes sources, not a name. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

This does not forbid saying that a process computes. It forbids treating the substrate reading as the thing that settles the claim.

## 5. Outputs are not generators, and a substrate list is an output

*They're Scanning Books* (opened this session): "A written sentence is a lossy compression of the reasoning that produced it." And the load-bearing move: "To exceed human reasoning you need a better engine. A pile of human reasoning's outputs is not an engine."

The four substrate literatures are outputs of a shared practice of building checkable descriptions. Collecting their definitions and averaging them would produce a list, not a generator. The generator is the contact loop plus the refuter. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

## 6. The observer question

Neither "computation is observer-relative" nor "computation is observer-independent" is the canonical reading. The observer supplies a cast: which states are slots, which currency is read, which instrument is the anchor. The ground supplies the verdict. Fig 2 :29: an anchor "does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

From *How Intelligence Actually Works* (carried): the six-term substrate is derived, but the question of what makes a system capable is the contact question, not the substrate question. Fig 11 :41 makes the same move verbatim: "The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."

## 7. Simulation notice and prestige

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (carried): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And LLM output is "simulacra"; "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and cannot settle anything it reports on.

The same essay gives the outreach rule: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous people who agree with a definition of computation would be the named collapse.

## 8. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any substrate mechanism as computation. That overstates the rule. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. Counts, concentrations, gradients, spectra and qubits are all already available in the relevant substrate literatures; naming one as the vehicle of a computation is a binding from that literature, provenanced as such. What remains wrong is treating the binding as a derivation, or as an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :26, "An import adds no atom to the world" - it adds none). Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

A second correction: §4's metamerism point is not retracted. It still holds that a substrate reading does not determine what produced it. The reason is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held atoms.

## 9. What shape means, in one paragraph for this question

A shape here is the checkable form of a definition of computation that can be written before choosing analog, chemical, biological or quantum as the substrate: a bounded frame with a per-slot residual, a non-updating anchor, gated term formation, settlement by what pays, and a cast of actors, currency and anchor that varies between substrates while the loop does not. It constrains each candidate definition; it does not fill the substrate slot. The slot is filled, if at all, by an instrument or a provenanced import, and until then the honest report is `gap`, which is a verdict and not silence (Fig 13 :134).
