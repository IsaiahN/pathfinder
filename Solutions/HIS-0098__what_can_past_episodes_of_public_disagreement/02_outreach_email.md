**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Replace the bracketed name only.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 is

**Subject:** One checkable claim about public disagreement and uncertainty

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about science communication. I
> am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A public scientific claim leaves a gap between what the audience takes it to
> settle and what the available ground actually settles. That gap is indexed per claim, not per debate. "The
> controversy" is usually several claims with several grounds. The question is what a communication practice has to
> do so that the audience's state tracks the evidence rather than the social arrangement around it.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the practice; not `grounded`).** A defensible practice has five
> properties: (1) split the disagreement into its separate slots before choosing any frame; (2) range each frame
> separately against a ground that does not change its answer when a frame pushes on it, so equal-time balance is
> treated as a convention about meaning rather than as a verdict about the world; (3) treat a communication failure as
> a failure of the channel to the ground first, not as a failure of the science; (4) declare the interest in the text
> of the communication, because an undeclared interest is modelled by the audience anyway; (5) state what would
> falsify the claim before stating the claim. What remains is reported as a shape and a named limit, not as a
> fabricated consensus and not as a fabricated absence.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that more information never helps, that laypeople should be excluded,
> that scientists should be the only speakers, or that every episode of public disagreement is the same. I am not
> claiming a single best communication format, a percentage that counts as consensus, or a verdict about any
> particular controversy. Past episodes supply constraints on a practice, not a slogan to copy. The question of which
> specific format performs best is open and needs a ground, not an argument.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these before sending this:
> 1. a controlled comparison in which balanced presentation plus additional information beats residual-first
>    presentation on correct inference about the specific uncertainty, in two independent replications;
> 2. a case where the controversy has one residual that trends down under one instrument and all frames range against
>    it, so the split does not apply;
> 3. an institution with a nameable anchor that does not move when either side pushes on it, using equal-time
>    presentation anyway;
> 4. evidence that disclosing a stake makes no difference to inference about the exact claim;
> 5. a high-overlap, single-pool room whose agreement is then treated as factual evidence and improves inference
>    against a held-out ground.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than
> agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the
> authority. If you want the method itself, I will send the formula with one instruction: write your own refuter
> before you run it and state the mode of what you get.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
