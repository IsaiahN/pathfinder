## 0. Residual in effect terms

The question is not "which past controversy was handled well". It is: given a public scientific claim whose evidence
does not yet settle the question, what does a communication practice have to do so that the audience's state tracks the
evidence rather than the social arrangement around it?

R is the gap between what a communication leads an audience to treat as settled and what the available ground actually
settles. R is indexed per claim, not per debate: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19).
The trap is to average across a controversy: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an
average." (Fig 1 :22). The phrase climate debate is not one slot.

Two candidate practices:

- **A, balance plus more information:** stage opposing voices as equal, then supply additional facts. Treat public
  agreement, or the absence of visible dispute, as the indicator that uncertainty has been handled.
- **B, ground-ranged residual first:** describe what would be expected if the claim were true, state the residual in
  effect terms, declare the interest, name the refuter, then offer the source material only on request.

What each leads to: A produces a convention that can settle a public narrative without moving any instrument. B leaves
the claim checkable and keeps the channel to the ground reachable. The derivation decides which practice stays in
contact with the effect described by R.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** multiple conditions. A public disagreement is usually a bundle of separate claims with separate grounds
  (the existence of a phenomenon, its cause, its magnitude, the appropriate response). Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that
  do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but
  several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The essay *The Library of Babel* (read 2026-11-23) names the
  same failure: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" This is a
  **union compound**, not one hard question.
- **Symmetry:** public discourse treats the two sides symmetrically (equal time, equal standing). The relation to a
  ground is asymmetric: frames are corrected by it and cannot correct it. Fig 2 :25: "pinned: it does not update on A
  or B". Symmetry of presentation is not symmetry of evidential standing. This mismatch is the central object of the
  audit.
- **Scale:** first order inside a discipline (a claim about the world), second order at the public interface (a claim
  about who is entitled to speak about the world). The second-order claim is composed over settled first-order terms
  only: Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about
  a level and never about the world." A second-order room cannot be built over an unsettled first-order term.

### One line per domain (actors, currency, anchor)

A domain exists only if a nameable anchor can be stated. Where no anchor is nameable, the line records a collapse in
progress rather than a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Evidence frame | researchers, instruments, datasets, peer review | predictions and residuals | measurement, experiment, counter; Fig 2 :29 "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." | yes |
| D2 Public and media frame | editors, journalists, platforms, audiences | attention and agreement, a convention | its proper anchor is the same ground as D1; when it updates on agreement instead it has none and becomes a mirror | yes, but only when ranged against D1's ground |
| D3 Policy and institutional frame | regulators, committees, advisory boards | decisions, conventions, budgets | the ground of the factual question; the convention may not decide the outcome | yes, conditionally |
| D4 Advocacy and stake frame | funders, campaigns, industry, ideological parties | funding and influence | none of its own; an interest in the reading disqualifies | no, it is a stake channel, not a ground-bearing domain |

### Partition or union

D1 through D4 form a **union**, not a partition. They overlap: the same person can occupy D1 and D4, and the same
statement can travel through D2 and D3. D2 and D4 overlap whenever a stake is financed into a public voice. Because it
is a union, one lever cannot clear it: the residue after a single intervention is a specific compound, and Fig 9 :54
requires unbundling before searching again. Fig 9 :56: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It
stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the audience distinguish what is in front of it from what was there before?
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what the claim affords: what would confirm it, what would refuse it?
3. **Objective:** can we say what would count as progress in communicating this particular uncertainty?
4. **Planning:** a route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry the method to the next controversy.

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by
dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has
looked at." The middle link, vocabulary and objective, is where communication campaigns usually break: they have an
abundant vocabulary for balance and almost none for "what would count as a failure of this claim".

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A public disagreement is usually a union, so the first lesson is to split it

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset,
are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

The historic episodes all bundle distinct slots: existence, cause, size, response, and who is trusted. Oreskes and
 Conway's *Merchants of Doubt* (2010, read 2026-11-23) documents the same tactic across tobacco, acid rain, DDT, the
ozone hole and climate: "keep the controversy alive" by continuing to spread doubt long after consensus. The word
"controversy" there is a union word. Communicating it as one dispute is the error.

**Refuter:** show that the specific controversy in question has one residual that trends down under one instrument,
and that every participant is ranging against that same instrument. Then the union finding does not apply.

### F2 - The ground cannot be listed as one of the parties

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame
thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."

Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

"Balancing the science with the sceptics" puts the ground into the room as a participant whose standing is decided by
the other participants. That is the mutual-update collapse arrived at by a different route. The corresponding world
diagnosis is *False balance* (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23): "present an issue as being more balanced between opposing
viewpoints than the evidence supports."

**Refuter:** a ground that genuinely does not change its answer when a frame pushes on it, that both sides separately
range against, and that nobody appointed by vote. Then balance is a convention about meaning, not a corruption.

### F3 - Agreement manufactured by selection is a convention, never corroboration

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Fig 2 :41: "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it
surfaced."

Post-normal science (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23) supplies the world-side statement of the same condition: it applies
when "facts [are] uncertain, values in dispute, stakes high and decisions urgent", and its "extended peer community"
is a space where "perspectives, values, styles of knowing and power differentials are expressed in a context of
inequalities and conflict". That is legitimate only under Fig 2 :31's independence condition. Add voices drawn from
one evidence pool and the room is a mirror chain. See F10 for the re-census.

**Refuter:** two frames with demonstrably low overlap, each separately corrected by the same immovable ground, whose
agreement therefore carries information. This is the legitimate case and is not refused by the finding.

### F4 - What failed historically was the channel, not the ground

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"

Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The 1985 Royal Society report *The public understanding of science* (page read 2026-11-23) "paved the way for an
expansion of science communication". The later *Information deficit model* account (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23)
records that "simply giving more information to people does not necessarily change their views". Those two episodes are
not a contradiction once the quantity is named: more information delivered through the same channel changes the
library, not the contact. Fig 11 :40 governs.

**Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which adding information, with no change in who ranges against what ground,
reliably improves correct inference about the specific uncertainty on a held-out test. Then this finding is narrowed.

### F5 - Declare the interest; an interest in the reading disqualifies

Fig 10 :58-59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed
model runs unchallenged."

*Agnotology* (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23) names the historical form: the study of "deliberate, culturally cultivated
ignorance or doubt, typically to sell a product, influence opinion, or win favour", with the tobacco industry's
manufacture of doubt as the prime example. The lesson is not that interested people may never speak. It is that the
interest must be a declared term in the communication, because an undeclared one is modelled by the audience anyway.

**Refuter:** show an interested party whose stake is absent from the communication but whose evidence is independently
settled by the ground on the exact claim at issue, with no change in inference when the stake is disclosed. Then the
disqualification is too broad.

### F6 - Bring a characterised residual, not the whole source

Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"

Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (essay, read 2026-11-23): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain
description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." A public audience cannot
reconstruct the whole edifice of a field, and should not be asked to. It can check a stated expectation against the
world.

**Refuter:** an audience that, after receiving only the effect description and the refuter, performs no better than one
receiving the full technical source on a ground-visible test. Then this preference is a preference, not a result.

### F7 - Write the falsifier before the claim, and make the sought shape an ordinary term

Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let
it accrue a residual like anything else."

Fig 5 :66: "Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."

Fig 5 :53: "a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"

A practice that cannot say in advance what would count against its own claim is accumulating, not correcting. Fig 5
counts that as a gap the pipeline has no branch for until negation is available. Pre-registering the refuter is the
discipline that supplies it. This is the single most transferable lesson from the episodes: the merchant of doubt wins
by keeping the question open in a way that cannot be closed, not by supplying a better answer.

**Refuter:** a communication that states its falsifier before the claim and is then shown to be unfalsifiable by
construction, or a case where pre-stating the refuter measurably reduces correct inference. Then withdraw.

### F8 - Uncertainty is a residual, not an absence

Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the
residual."

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, read 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the
source", and the metamerism was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument". Past episodes
teach that a confident "we do not know" is often a statement about the instrument, not about the world. The honest
form is a shape and a named limit, not a silence and not a fabricated consensus.

**Refuter:** a case where the absence of a finding is, by the rules of the instrument, a conclusive proof of absence.
Then "never" in Fig 8 :39 is too strong for that domain and the finding must be narrowed.

### F9 - The per-slot rule defeats the headline average

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

In the ozone episode, the existence of the hole, its cause, its trend and the policy response had different grounds and
reached settlement at different times. A single "public understanding score" averages them. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters
for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A headline consensus percentage
is a filter for the budget of attention, not a witness about any one slot.

**Refuter:** a validated instrument for which the per-slot residuals genuinely collapse to one value across all slots
in a named controversy. Then the average is lossless there and the warning does not bite.

### F10 - The extended peer community: a strain, resolved by re-census, not by flipping the verdict

**Strain as first read.** Post-normal science recommends an "extended peer community" including laypeople in quality
assurance. Read casually, this looks like "more voices is better", which strains against Fig 7 :36 (a chain of mirrors)
and Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

**Re-census.** The two lines govern different quantities. Fig 7 governs the *source of the next room's difference*:
low overlap and a residual matched against the last handoff. The extended peer community governs *who may participate
in quality assurance* when facts are uncertain and stakes are high. A lay participant with separate experience is a
low-overlap frame, exactly the resource Fig 10 :80 says can be maintained: "Frames whose independence is renewed by
separate experience can be maintained". An equally spaced set of voices drawn from one media pool is a mirror. No
figure contradicts another after the census is corrected.

**What survives.** Extended peers are admissible under the figures only as low-overlap frames ranged against the same
ground, with the power differential declared. As equal-time balance they are a mirror and their agreement is a
convention. This matches the world source too: the Royal Society's *Science for society* page (read 2026-11-23) says
public engagement is "an essential part of the research lifecycle" while noting that online scientific news is
"increasingly crowded out by misinformation". Contact is the scarce quantity, not the number of seats.

**Refuter:** an extended-peer process whose members are drawn from one evidence pool, whose agreement is then treated
as confirmation of a factual claim. That is the mirror case and the finding stands against it.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"
(the framework's own seat rule, and consistent with Fig 5 :65-66). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following
holds on a ground, not on agreement:

1. A controlled comparison shows that balanced presentation plus additional information yields better correct
   inference about the specific uncertainty than residual-first presentation, across two independent replications with
   different audiences. This would break F2, F3 and F4.
2. The named controversy is shown to have one residual that trends down under one instrument, with all frames ranging
   against that instrument, so that Fig 9's union finding does not bite. This would break F1 and F9.
3. A public-facing institution is shown to have a nameable anchor that both sides separately range against and that
   does not move when either pushes on it, while equal-time presentation is still used. This would break F3.
4. Disclosing an interest is shown to have no effect on the audience's inference about the exact claim, with the
   underlying evidence independently settled on that claim. This would narrow F5.
5. The extended-peer condition is reversed: a high-overlap, single-pool room whose agreement is treated as evidence is
   shown to improve factual inference against a held-out ground. This would break F10.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the split, not the verdict: name the slots of the disagreement before choosing any communication frame
   (F1, Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Never put the ground in the room as a party or a tie-breaker (F2, Fig 2 :46-47).
3. Refuse balance as a verdict. Keep it only as a convention about shared meaning, where Fig 2 :41 permits it (F3).
4. Treat communication failure as a channel failure first. Ask what reading stopped arriving before asking who was
   right (F4, Fig 10 :24).
5. Declare interests in the text of the communication, not in a separate note nobody reads (F5).
6. Send the residual and its refuter, then the links, only on request (F6, Fig 10 :52).
7. Pre-register what would change the claim (F7).
8. Report the shape and the limit, not a fabricated absence or a fabricated consensus (F8).
9. Keep per-slot reporting; do not publish a single understanding score as if it were a verdict (F9).

### Strains left open

- **F10 extended peers:** dissolved by re-census, but the practical test, low overlap between participants, still needs
  an instrument. Named, not hidden.
- **World-world conflict:** *Cultural cognition* (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23) reports that in a nationally
  representative sample "ordinary members of the public do not become more concerned about climate change as their

  overlap; their mutual conflict is recorded rather than smoothed.
- **Round-trip gap:** see `11_audit.md`. The condensed lesson "balance bad, ground good" does not survive the trip back
