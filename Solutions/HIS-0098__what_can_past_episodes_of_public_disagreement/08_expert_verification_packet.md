# Expert verification packet - HIS-0098

**What this is:** a claim in a form a science-communication researcher, risk-communication scholar, or historian of
science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the communication prediction. Not `grounded`.** No audience comparison
is claimed. This is a constraint set and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

Past episodes of public disagreement teach a shape rather than a slogan. Split a controversy into its separate claims,
each with its own ground. Range every frame separately against a ground that does not change when a frame pushes on
it; never put that ground into the argument as a party or a tie-breaker. Treat equal-time balance as a convention about
shared meaning, never as a verdict about the world. Treat a failure of public understanding as a failure of the channel
to the ground before treating it as a failure of the science. Declare interests inside the message. State what would
falsify the claim before stating the claim. Report the remainder as a shape and a limit, not as a manufactured
consensus or a manufactured absence. Same facts bonded symmetrically versus sequenced to a ground and a falsifier are
different communications.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 7

- **Statement:** a ground cannot be made a party to a disagreement or used as a tie-breaker. Agreement among frames
  that share an evidence pool is a convention, not corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :46 "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in
  a vote." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2
  :41 "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is
  variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **What to check:** is the distinction between a convention about meaning and a verdict about the world actually
  drawn in the cited lines, and does it forbid treating balanced voices as evidence?
- **Refuter:** exhibit two frames with demonstrably low overlap, each separately corrected by the same immovable
  ground, whose agreement then carries information. That case is not refused by this sub-claim.

### C2 - practice-facing; checkable against the history of science and communication research

- **Statement:** the transferable failure in these episodes is in the channel, not in the ground, and the residual is
  per claim rather than per debate. More information supplied through the same channel changes the library, not the
  contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :27-28 "the output is not a
  verdict, it is you have lost touch". Fig 9 :4-5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it
  rather than search". Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** do the documented episodes (tobacco, ozone, climate, MMR) actually bundle distinct claims with
  distinct grounds, and is the "information deficit" remedy best read as a channel failure? World corroboration:
  *Information deficit model* (Wikipedia, 2026): "simply giving more information to people does not necessarily change
  their views"; *False balance* (Wikipedia, 2026): "present an issue as being more balanced between opposing viewpoints
  than the evidence supports".
- **Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which adding information with no change in who ranges against what ground
  improves correct inference on held-out items, in two independent replications. Then C2 is narrowed.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** an interest in the reading disqualifies unless declared; a falsifier must be written before the
  claim; and the bond between facts and a falsifier changes the communication even when ingredients are held constant.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is
  disqualifying." Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway
  and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 5 :66 "Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."
  Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and
  decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is the stake rule stated as a rule rather than a recommendation, and does the isomer definition
  actually apply to two communication formats sharing the same atoms?
- **Refuter:** disclosure of the stake makes no difference to inference about the exact claim with the evidence
  independently settled, or two differently bonded formats are indistinguishable on a held-out ground measure. Then
  C3 fails in that domain.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a past controversy yields a copyable lesson. "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." (Fig 4 :22).
- Not that more information never helps. "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it
  improves." (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that laypeople should be excluded. The extended peer community is admissible as low-overlap frames ranged
  against the same ground, and refused as equal-time balance (`01` F10).
- Not that scientists should be the only speakers, nor that a consensus percentage is a verdict.
- Not that any specific communication format, label scheme or journal is figure-derived. These are world bindings,
  provenanced in `04`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an audience measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that
reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the case was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better
instrument". Here:

1. A replicated presentation comparison with held-out items: balanced plus information versus residual-first.
2. A per-slot measurement of the named controversy showing whether the residuals really separate.
3. An anchor test naming what neither side can move, or recording that no such thing exists for the slot.
4. A stake-disclosure test with the underlying evidence settled independently on that claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :40, :41, :46 and Fig 7 :35 (four short passages). Decide whether the convention/verdict distinction is
   really there.
2. Read Fig 10 :24, :52, :59-60. Decide whether the channel and stake claims are stated as rules or as advice.
3. Check C2 against one episode you know well. Was the public failure a channel failure, a genuine scientific
   disagreement, or both in different slots?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish the two bonded formats on a held-out ground, or say why none is
   possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as
   corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
