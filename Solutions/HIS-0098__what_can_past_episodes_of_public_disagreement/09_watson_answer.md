## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what past episodes of public scientific disagreement can teach about communicating
uncertainty now. Option A: treat them as a store of communicable lessons, chiefly that balanced voices plus more
information produce public understanding. Option B: treat them as recorded failures of the channel between a frame and
its ground, and derive a shape: split the union, range frames separately against a ground that cannot move, declare
interests, refuse the false "still open" term, write the refuter before the claim, and report what remains as a shape
and a limit. Option A leads to a convention that can settle a public narrative without consulting anything. Option B
leaves the claim checkable. The residual R is the gap between what a communication leads an audience to treat as
settled and what the available ground settles, indexed per claim and never averaged across a debate.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and
order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: multiple conditions, so a **union compound**. Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard
  question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Symmetry: public presentation is symmetric
  (equal time), while the relation to a ground is asymmetric (frames are corrected by it and cannot correct it).
  Scale: first order inside a discipline (a claim about the world), second order at the public interface (a claim
  about who may speak about the world); the second order composes only over settled first-order terms (Fig 12 :104).
- **D1, evidence frame.** Actors: researchers, instruments, datasets, peer review. Currency: predictions and
  residuals. Anchor: measurement and experiment. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **D2, public and media frame.** Actors: editors, journalists, platforms, audiences. Currency: attention and
  agreement, a convention. Anchor: its proper anchor is D1's ground, but when it updates on agreement it has none and
  becomes a mirror. Domain only while ranged against the ground.
- **D3, policy and institutional frame.** Actors: regulators, committees, advisory boards. Currency: decisions,
  conventions, budgets. Anchor: the ground of the factual question; the convention may not decide the outcome.
- **D4, advocacy and stake frame.** Actors: funders, campaigns, industry, ideological parties. Currency: funding and
  influence. Anchor: none of its own. This is a stake channel, not a ground-bearing domain.
- **Partition or union:** union. D2 and D4 overlap whenever a stake is financed into a public voice; one lever cannot
  clear a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Order by dependency:** perceive the separate slots, build a vocabulary of refutation, state what would count as
  progress, plan the communication, then carry the verified method onward (Fig 3 :19-20). Refuse before mint
  (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Past episodes teach a shape, not a slogan. Split the disagreement into its separate claims; range every frame against
a ground that does not update, never making that ground a party or a tie-breaker; keep equal-time balance as a
convention about meaning only; diagnose a communication failure as a channel failure first; declare interests inside
the message; write the falsifier before the claim; and report the remainder as a shape and a named limit. The
ingredients of a communication do not decide its effect, the bond and order do. D1, D2 and D4 are triangulated; D3 is
single-source and reported as such. This is a `general` structural result with a `specified` practice prediction,
ungrounded and still `candidate`. The five refuters in `01` section 4 are written and unrun.

- **cost if wrong:** a practice built on balance can settle a public narrative without consulting the thing it is
  about, and an undeclared stake runs unchallenged. The cost is bounded because the packet states refuters and makes
  no `grounded` claim. The avoidable extra cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the full frame
  before asking for a verdict. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. Communication practice can be changed without touching the science, withdrawing a primitive, or
  retracting the derivation. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, never agreement. A replicated audience comparison of the two bonded formats on
  held-out items; a nameable non-updating anchor for a slot that currently has none; a per-slot measurement showing
  the union finding does not apply; a disclosure test with the evidence independently settled; or a high-overlap room
  that demonstrably improves factual inference. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
