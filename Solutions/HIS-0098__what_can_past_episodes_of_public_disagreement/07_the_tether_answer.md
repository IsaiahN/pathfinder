# The Tether's answer: what past public disagreement teaches about communicating uncertainty

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the communication prediction. Not `grounded`.** This is a
shape and a route, not a result of an audience experiment. Simulated derivation checked against the figures
(`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The residual it answers

Public disagreement about a scientific claim leaves a gap between what the audience takes the claim to settle and what
the available ground settles. The gap is per claim, not per debate: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not
aggregated." (Fig 1 :19). Past episodes are relevant not as stories to imitate but as recorded failures of a specific
quantity: the channel between a frame and its ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Split before you frame.** Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather
   than search." A controversy is usually existence, cause, magnitude and response bundled together. Each has its own
   ground and reaches settlement at a different time. Communicating the bundle as one dispute is the first error.
2. **The ground is not a party and not a tie-breaker.** Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement
   and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote." Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a
   frame." Frames are corrected by the ground separately; they do not bargain with it (Fig 2 :45).
3. **Equal-time balance is a convention, not a verdict.** Fig 2 :41: "agreement about a shared meaning is the product,
   not a corruption of it"; Fig 2 :40 withholds that permission for the world. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is
   variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Adding voices drawn from one evidence
   pool lengthens a mirror chain (Fig 7 :36).
4. **A communication failure is a channel failure first.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
   The historical question to ask of a failure is not "who won the argument" but "which reading stopped arriving".
   Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch".
5. **Declare the interest in the message itself.** Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest
   in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." The agnotology cases
   show what an undeclared interest does: it is modelled by the audience anyway, and the model runs unchallenged.
6. **Bring the residual and the refuter, not the whole edifice.** Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never
   the source." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". An audience
   can check a stated expectation against the world without first joining the field.
7. **Write what would falsify the claim before the claim.** Fig 5 :66: "Committing a prediction before a run is this,
   already working." Fig 5 :53 names the refusal a false regularity owes: "a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that
   says so". The doubt merchant's move is to keep a slot open after its ground has settled it; the structural answer is
   a negation, not another explanation.
8. **Report a shape and a limit, not a fabricated absence or consensus.** Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is
   never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". "We do not know yet"
   names the instrument's current reach; "both sides make a good point" names a convention.
9. **The bond is part of the message.** Two statements with the same facts, the same uncertainty number and the same
   voices are isomers under Fig 12 :117 when the voices are bonded symmetrically in one and sequenced to a ground and
   a falsifier in the other. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any past controversy has one settled lesson that can be copied. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head
  starts come down." What transfers is the loop, not a recording of a campaign.
- Not that more information never helps. The precise form is Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact
  changes nothing, however much it improves."
- Not that laypeople should be excluded. The extended peer community is admissible as low-overlap frames ranged
  against the same ground (`01` F10); it is refused as equal-time balance.
- Not that scientists should be the only speakers, nor that a consensus percentage is a verdict. Fig 2 :41 permits
  agreement as a convention about meaning only.
- Not that a specific format is derived. Confidence labels, risk-communication protocols and submission venues are
  bindings from world frames, provenanced in `04`, not outputs of the figures.
- Not `grounded`. No audience comparison was run in this solution.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument
that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the metamerism was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a
better instrument". Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Replication test for practice A versus B:** balanced presentation plus information against residual-first
   presentation, on correct inference about the specific uncertainty, with held-out items and two independent
   audiences. This is the ground for findings 2, 3 and 4.
2. **Union test:** per-slot residuals for the named controversy measured separately; if they collapse to one
   instrument and trend down together, the split finding narrows.
3. **Anchor test:** name the thing neither side can move. If no such thing exists for a slot, that slot is not yet a
   domain and should be reported as unreached rather than filled with a balance convention.
4. **Stake disclosure test:** inference about the exact claim with the interest disclosed versus undisclosed, with the
   underlying evidence independently settled on that claim.
5. **Standing refuters** from `01` section 4. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The five refuters are written down and none has been run. The shape terminates at the
limit of what these figures and the read world sources can distinguish. It records the lineage so the stop is
checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical figures README). The world sources agree with parts of the
shape from low-overlap fields, but agreement among sources that share a pool is one witness, not two. Corroboration is
not settlement.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Past public disputes do not supply a slogan for communicating uncertainty; they supply a shape. Split the dispute
> into its separate claims, because each has its own ground and settles at its own time. Range each voice separately
> against that ground, and never put the ground into the argument as a party or a tie-breaker. Equal time is a
> convention about meaning, not evidence about the world. Treat a failure of public understanding as a failure of the
> channel to the ground first. Declare interests inside the message, since an undeclared interest is modelled by the
> audience anyway. Say what would falsify the claim before saying the claim, and report what remains unknown as a
> shape and a limit rather than as a manufactured consensus or a manufactured absence. The same facts bonded by
> balance and by residual-first sequence are different communications, which is why changing the order of a message
> can matter more than adding another fact. What is offered here is that constraint set, five refuters written in
> advance, and the tests that would close it, not a verdict about any particular controversy.

## 7. Cost if wrong, undoable, reversed by

- **cost if wrong:** a communication built on balance can settle a public narrative without consulting anything, and
  an undeclared stake runs unchallenged. The cost is bounded because the packet states its refuters and the shape is
  not claimed as `grounded`. The larger avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending them
  the full frame before asking for a verdict (Fig 10 :42).
- **undoable:** yes. The communication practice can be changed without touching the underlying science or withdrawing
  any primitive. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, never agreement. A replicated audience comparison, a nameable non-updating
  anchor for the slot, or a demonstration that high-overlap rooms improve held-out inference would move the verdict.
  Until one fires, the result stays `candidate`.
