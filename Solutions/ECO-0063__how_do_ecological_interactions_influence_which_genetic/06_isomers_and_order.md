# Isomers, cycles and order: two populations under different interactions

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order rule.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Two populations of the same species, with the same standing alleles, end up with different trait frequencies because their interaction webs differ. The ordinary word "same" is doing two jobs: same atoms and same arrangement. The figures separate them. Three questions are owed: (1) are the two populations isomers under Fig 12's definition, (2) is the feedback a cycle or a chain, and (3) in what order must variation, contact and measurement be checked?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the two populations isomers?

- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Write the two populations as molecules over the shared atom set A = {standing alleles at loci L, relation kinds R}. If population 1 is `L1 → predation → L2` and population 2 is `L1 → competition → L2`, the atom lists are identical and the bond/relation differs. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance (different equilibrium trait frequencies). The difference is invisible in an allele inventory and decisive in the phenotype distribution. This is the strong, checkable form of the answer to the original question: ecological interactions do not change which alleles exist, they change the arrangement in which the same alleles are expressed and retained.

If instead population 2 lacks an allele that population 1 has (it never arrived, or was lost), the two are **not** isomers. They are different molecules over different atom sets, and the difference is an import or a loss, not a rearrangement. The weaker claim still stands: both share a **functional group**, the `state → relation → state` pattern with a different qualifier (predation versus competition), so "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member" applies.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if both populations can be written over the identical standing allele set and identical interaction strengths, yet their trait frequencies diverge reproducibly, the arrangement claim is insufficient and an unenumerated atom or a drift term is owed. Conversely, if the focal loci differ between populations, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. `single-source` on the figure definition (Fig 12), triangulated for the legality of the arrangement by Fig 11 :47 (relations are named as relations, not kinds).

### Q2 — Is the feedback a cycle or a chain?

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The forward edge is interaction → trait frequency. The reverse edge is evolved trait → interaction strength (a changed gape changes which zooplankton are eaten; a changed flower shape changes which pollinator visits). If the reverse edge is present and positive, the loop closes and the object is a cycle. If it is absent, the object is a chain and must be analysed as a chain: effort spent remedying a feedback that does not exist cannot rescue the forward measurement (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."). The opened Post and Palkovacs review supplies the world-side counterpart: they require both a strong phenotype-to-environment effect and a subsequent environment-to-evolution effect for a feedback to exist, and note that the time scales must be congruent.

- **Falsifier:** in a system with a measured forward edge, manipulate the evolved trait and show no change in interaction strength across two independent seasons. Then F6 of `01` is narrowed to that system and the cycle reading is withdrawn there. `triangulated` (Fig 12 :121 plus the opened ecology review).

### Q3 — Which parts are catalysts and which are spent?

- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A predator whose prey are replenished each season is a catalyst in this sense: it applies the same selection pressure across generations because it survives its own use. A predator that exhausts its prey and crashes is a spent part; its selection pressure disappears after one pass. Nothing in an allele inventory records which one a population holds. This is why the same interaction label can have a sustained effect in one lake and a one-off effect in another. The distinction is structural, not a species property, consistent with Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

- **Falsifier:** if a predator that exhausts its prey generates a multi-generation selection gradient indistinguishable from one whose prey replenish, the catalyst distinction does no work here and should be dropped. `single-source` on the figure definition, with the mechanism-side corroboration from the opened chemostat synthesis.

### Q4 — What is the order of dependence?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
- Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

The dependency order is:

1. **Refuse option A first.** If the claim is that interactions generate variation, refuse it before minting anything on top of it (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: a refusing term is owed, and ¬ is not a bond). Fig 6 :21 supplies the refusal.
2. **Standing variation, or an import awaited.** No variation means no selection response, whatever the interaction (the single-clone control).
3. **A vocabulary for the trait and the interactor.** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." This is the middle link that usually breaks.
4. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
5. **Measure per locus across generations, with a non-updating ground.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

Effort at step 5 cannot rescue a break at step 2. Most published effort lands at step 5 because that is where the instruments already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). That is a budget fact, not a verdict.

### Q5 — The two currencies do not add

- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

Describing an allele-interaction arrangement is cheap in bits (a few loci, a named interactor). Settling whether it actually produced the frequency shift is expensive in actions (seasons of sampling, a replicated manipulation, a drift null). A short arrangement over a costly interactor is cheap to state and may take a whole research budget to settle. A long list of loci is expensive to describe and may be refuted by one clean exclusion experiment. Neither figure predicts the other, so both must be read or the choice is made blind. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## CONCLUSION

Two populations under different interactions, over the identical standing allele set, are **isomers** in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different outcome. When the allele sets differ, the weaker **functional-group** reading holds instead, and the difference is an import or a loss. The eco-evolutionary feedback is a **cycle** only when the reverse edge from evolved trait to interaction strength is measured; otherwise it is a chain and should be treated as one. Repeated interactors divide into catalysts and spent parts, a distinction invisible in an allele inventory. The dependency order is refuse, variation, vocabulary, enumerate, measure. Both the bit price and the action price must be read; they do not add.

This framing keeps what is defensible in the residual without importing an unmeasurable universal law: no new primitive, no claim that interactions mint alleles, and no naming of a winning trait. What remains checkable is the arrangement claim and the cycle claim, each with a falsifier above.
