# The Tether's answer: how ecological interactions influence which genetic traits spread

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the identity of winning traits. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Trait frequencies move in directions that track the surrounding community. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The object is the vector of per-locus changes, not a population mean. Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The question is a **union of conditions** (predation, competition, parasitism, pollination, mutation, gene flow, drift), not one dial. Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Interactions are the habitat's relations, and their valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"; Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." No standing list of winning traits survives a change of community.
2. **The habitat is enumerated, never composed.** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52, the DDT-to-eagles example), so second-order interactors can set the pressure on a trait they never touch.
3. **Interactions sort standing heritable variation; they do not mint it.** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :22 "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Mutation, recombination and gene flow supply atoms; selection reweights them. The rotifer single-clone control in the opened literature is the ground-facing counterpart: with one clone, no prey evolution and no feedback.
4. **Selection is the ground's vote, not an expert consensus.** Fig 11 :56 "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
5. **Trait change and interaction change can form a cycle.** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Over the same standing allele set, two populations under different interactions are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance"), or a **functional group** with a different qualifier when their atoms differ (Fig 12 :123 "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member").
6. **Managed cases run the same loop with a seat that can only introduce.** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Isolation substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66), so a transplant inherits actors it did not choose. An introduced allele is an import and needs a source gate (Fig 6 :24).
7. **The framework does not name the winner.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Which allele wins in a named community is a measurement, and the reading alone does not identify its source (selection, drift, gene flow or sampling), so a discriminator is owed.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that ecological interactions generate adaptive variation on demand. That is option A, refused by Fig 6 :21-22 and the chemostat control.
- Not a universal interaction strength, not a list of "good" species, and not a standing list of winning traits (Fig 11 :51).
- Not that a genome-wide mean fitness or mean phenotype is the object of selection (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that natural selection is the whole framework. It is the loop with no maintainer in the seat (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground"). The same loop runs in cultivation and assisted gene flow, with a seat.
- Not that this paper's six physical substrate terms name genes. The method crosses strata with renamed actors (Fig 4 :20, Fig 10 :68); the atoms are not renamed.
- Not grounded. No allele-frequency series, interactor manipulation or discriminator was run by this author.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy" and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

1. **Per-locus time series with a measured interactor.** Track allele frequencies across generations in replicated populations with the interactor manipulated and a drift null. That is the ground reading that would move a specific case from `candidate` to `grounded`. The salmon MHC example in the opened Pelletier et al. review is the model.
2. **Source discrimination.** Show whether the same frequency rise came from selection, drift or gene flow. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
3. **Reverse edge test.** Manipulate the evolved trait and measure whether interaction strength changes. A zero reverse edge turns the cycle into a chain for that system.
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4.** Adaptive rise with zero variation and no import; mean shift with no locus change; same interactor sign under opposite selection regimes; no effect of second-order manipulators; removable interactor without habitat substitution.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The shape stops at the limit of what the opened instruments distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. From the README: "Indiscernibility is not identity." A future discriminator may name a winner; until then the identity is a `gap` and the method is what is offered.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Ecological interactions do not hand a population new genes. They are the habitat's relations, and their effect on a trait depends on the current configuration of the community rather than on the identity of the actor: the same predator can select for camouflage in one prey and for speed in another. That is why there is no standing list of winning traits. What selection does is reweight heritable variation already present in the population, with new variants arriving by mutation, recombination or gene flow; it has to be read per trait, because a population mean can stay flat while loci move in opposite directions. Contact cascades outward, so an interactor two steps away can set the pressure on a trait it never touches, and the evolved trait can in turn change the interaction that selected it, closing a cycle. The figures return that shape and its refuters, not the name of the allele that wins in your system. Naming that requires a discriminator, an allele-frequency time series with the interactor measured, and until one is run the honest status is unreached rather than silence.
