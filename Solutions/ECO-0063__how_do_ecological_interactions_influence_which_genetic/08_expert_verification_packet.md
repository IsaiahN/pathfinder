# Expert verification packet — the Tether on ecological interactions and trait spread

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist, evolutionary geneticist or population biologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the identity of winning traits. Not `grounded`.** No allele-frequency measurement was taken by this author. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Ecological interactions are the habitat's relations, and their valence is relative to the current residual, so there is no standing list of traits that win. They reweight heritable variation that is already segregating or that arrives by mutation, recombination and gene flow; they do not manufacture the variant they favour. The effect must be read per trait slot, because a population mean can stay flat while individual loci move in opposite directions. Contact is enumerated outward, so a second-order interactor can set the selection pressure on a trait it never touches, and an evolved trait can feed back and change the interaction that selected it. In managed cases the same loop runs with a seat that can introduce alleles or actors but cannot subtract contact. The figures return this shape and its refuters, not the identity of the winning allele; that identity needs a discriminator that separates selection, drift and gene flow as sources of a frequency reading.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 1
- **Statement:** interactions are relations with residual-relative valence; the effect is per slot, not a population mean.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"; Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds"; Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** does the framework actually name relations rather than good/bad species, and does it forbid aggregating the residual? These are reading checks against three short passages.
- **Refuter:** the same interactor has the same sign on the same trait in two communities under opposite selection regimes with interaction strength measured in both; or a population mean shifts while every locus frequency at that trait is unchanged. Either fires C1.

### C2 — biology-facing, checkable against a real study system
- **Statement:** selection reweights standing variation and imports; it does not generate adaptive variation on demand. New alleles enter by mutation, recombination or gene flow. Over the identical standing allele set, two populations under different interactions are isomers (Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance").
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"; Fig 6 :22 "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure"; Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** in a system you know, was there segregating variation at the focal loci before the shift, and did any new allele arrive by migration or mutation during the window? Were the two compared populations over the same allele set, or did one lack an allele? If the latter, the functional-group reading (Fig 12 :123 "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member") replaces the isomer claim.
- **Refuter:** a documented adaptive frequency rise at a locus with zero segregating variation and no mutation, immigration, recombination or introgression in the measurement window. That single observation kills the sorting claim for that locus.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation, checkable by reading Figures 11, 12 and 9
- **Statement:** the feedback is a cycle when the reverse edge (evolved trait changes interaction strength) is present, and a chain when it is absent; the habitat is enumerated outward because contact cascades; managed cases introduce but cannot subtract contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction"; Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out"; Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **What to check:** is the reverse edge measured or assumed in the case you know? Is there a second-order interactor that was not enumerated? In a management setting, was the habitat substituted rather than cleaned?
- **Refuter:** a measured forward edge with a zero reverse edge across two independent seasons turns the cycle into a chain; a manipulated second-order interactor with no effect on the focal selection gradient kills the cascade clause for that trait; a reproducible intervention that removes an interactor without substituting contact breaks the asymmetry claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that ecological interactions generate adaptive variants on demand.
- Not a universal interaction strength, a list of beneficial species, or a standing list of winning traits.
- Not that population mean fitness or mean phenotype tracks which traits spread.
- Not a claim that this author can name the allele that wins in any particular community. That is a `gap`, reported as unreached rather than silence.
- Not that natural selection is the whole framework. In the unmaintained case it is the loop with no seat; in a managed case the same loop has an actor that introduces but cannot exclude.
- Not that the six physical substrate terms name genes. The method crosses strata with renamed actors.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. Measure per-locus allele frequencies across generations in replicated populations with the interactor manipulated and a drift null. The salmon MHC time series in the opened Pelletier et al. review is the model.
2. Show whether an identical frequency rise came from selection, drift or gene flow, using an instrument or design that distinguishes the sources. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."
3. Test the reverse edge by manipulating the evolved trait and measuring interaction strength.
4. Run any of the seven falsifiers in `01` section 4. If one fires, withdraw the corresponding claim rather than reinterpreting it.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :47, :50, :52, :61 and Fig 1 :19. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against a study system you know: was there standing variation, did an allele arrive, and were the compared populations over the same atom set?
3. Name one measurement that would distinguish selection from drift or gene flow as the source of a frequency change in that system, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
