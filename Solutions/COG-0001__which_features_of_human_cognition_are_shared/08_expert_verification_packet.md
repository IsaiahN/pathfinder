# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cognitive universals and cultural variation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` for the feature list. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the ground is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Which cognitive features are universal and which vary" is not a two-box sort. The classes overlap: a feature can be present in every known society and still vary in form, as color categories are everywhere while their number and boundaries differ. What stays invariant is a functional group (the kind of machinery), and what varies is the member that fills the slot and the way it is bound. Verdicts must be made per feature and per society, never pooled into a single human-nature score. The framework returns this shape and refuses to name the features; the feature list is `gap` and is owed to comparative measurement, domain by domain, with an anchor that does not move when researchers disagree.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — The classes are a union, not a partition

- **Statement:** "shared" and "varies" are two axes. At least one well-studied feature is present in every society and varies in form.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **What to check:** is the color-term case actually both? Berlin and Kay find a repeatable hierarchy across languages, while individual languages sit at different stages. Does that support overlap rather than a clean sort?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated clean partition, with no feature showing both invariant presence and variable form, defeats C1.

### C2 — The invariant item is a functional group; variation is member or binding

- **Statement:** the shared item is the kind of structure, not a particular instance. Cultural differences are changes of member or of relation among the same parts.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :46, *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*
- **What to check:** take any domain you know (categorisation, grammar, facial expression, counting). Can the cross-cultural facts be stated as one group with different members, or do they require a genuinely new kind of primitive?
- **Refuter:** a variation that introduces a new cognitive primitive rather than a new member or bond of a shared kind defeats C2. Operators :68: *"Seven tests separate the eight named here. Whether there are more is a question no test derived from these can answer."*, and :68 to :69 adds that a relation failing all seven is a claim not yet made

### C3 — Per-slot verdicts, and the ground rather than agreement

- **Statement:** a pooled verdict across features is not admissible, and agreement inside a literature is not the test. The test is an anchor that does not change when frames push on it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 7 :35, *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** does the WEIRD literature (Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan 2010) support per-slot caution without supplying a universal list? Does the Ekman/Russell dispute behave like one contested evidence pool rather than two independent frames (Fig 2 :31, *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*)?
- **Refuter:** pooled verdicts reproducing every per-feature verdict, or an alleged anchor whose answer changes when frames push on it, defeats C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of universal features, and not a list of variable features.
- Not "all cultures are the same underneath" and not "cultures are incommensurable."
- Not derived from the six-term substrate. Fig 13 :74 governs composition inside a system, not the cross-cultural sort; it was tried and re-censused out.
- Not a new "culture" bond or operator.
- Not `grounded`. No cross-cultural measurement was run, and no figure quote is a substitute for one.
- Not a claim that failure to find a universal proves none exists. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not a request for agreement. A refutation is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument per domain, not another argument. The green-reading problem in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Here the sources of a cross-cultural similarity include a shared mechanism, independent invention, diffusion and a shared habitat. A shape cannot discriminate among them; an instrument must.

1. A perceptual-versus-linguistic discriminator for color-boundary differences.
2. A typological corpus independent of the generative frame.
3. A facial-recognition task without posed expressions, forced choice and relative judgment.
4. Samples outside the WEIRD slice, with comparability fixed before the threshold is set.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54 to :55, Fig 12 :117 and :123, Fig 1 :19 and :22, and Fig 2 :29. Six short passages.
2. Name one feature from your own field that is present in every society you know and varies in form. If you cannot, say so. That is a result, not a failure to cooperate.
3. For that feature, say whether the variation needs a new kind of primitive or only a new member of a shared kind.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the possible sources of the observed similarity, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. If C1, C2 or C3 fails, say which and quote the figure or the observation that breaks it.
