## 0. The question stated in effect terms

The question asks us to sort cognitive features into two classes: those present in every known society, and those whose form, rule or frequency changes substantially with culture. Stated as an effect, an answer lets us predict what we will observe when we move a feature from one society to another.

Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching (Formula step, "Direction"): **an answer is a rule that assigns each feature to a class, plus an account of why the classes differ.** Now it can accrue a residual like anything else.

**Residual R.** For each feature slot `s` and society `j`, hold a prediction `Γ(b, a)` about the feature's presence and form, and an observation `o'` from comparative work. Then `R = |Γ(b, a) − o'|` per slot. Three properties matter before any figure line is chosen:

- **Arity:** two-indexed, `(feature, society)`. A one-indexed residual, feature alone, drops the comparison that the question is about.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. Swapping feature and society is a different measurement. Under Fig 12's bond table this is an ordered relation, not a symmetric one.
- **Scale:** cross-societal, from a handful of ethnographies to the large comparative databases. Large scale makes averaging tempting, which is the named failure in Fig 1.

The answer the figures can return is constrained to a shape. The feature names are not in `closure(Γ)` of the figures, so they are owed from the ground, not derivable here.

### Partition or union

This is a **union, not a partition**. The two classes overlap: a feature can be universally present and still vary in form. Color naming is the clean case, every society has color categories, but the number and boundaries of those categories differ (D1). Treating the classes as a partition would force a false choice. Fig 9 :54 governs directly: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Each overlap is a compound with its own loop. D1 through D6 are therefore kept as six separate loops with six separate verdicts. They are never averaged into one grand verdict.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The chain for this question runs:

1. perception: can the feature be detected and discriminated in each society,
2. vocabulary: do we have comparable terms for what the feature affords (emic versus etic),
3. objective: what would count as "shared" rather than "varying",
4. planning: a sampling design that reaches beyond one population,
5. learn and carry: a result that can be checked in the next society.

The break is between 2 and 3. We have instruments for perception and for planning, but comparability of terms and the operational definition of "universal" have no stable instrument. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* A verdict taken below that break is a reading of nothing, not a weak reading.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The verdict is per feature, never averaged

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*

A single "human nature" score, or a single "cultural variation" score, destroys the only signal the question asks for. A feature can be universal in presence and variable in form, so the two-bin table must be filled cell by cell. This is also why the six domains stay separate.

**Refuter:** if per-slot and pooled assignments coincide on every feature tested across all six domains, the slot requirement is idle for this question and F1 is withdrawn as a governing finding.

### F2 — The classes form a union, not a partition

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Color categories, number words and facial expressions all show the same shape: presence is widespread, form is cultural. Asking "is it universal or variable?" forces a false dichotomy. The honest question is "which part is invariant, which part is bound differently, and at what level?" Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

**Refuter:** a demonstrated clean partition, with no feature showing both invariant presence and variable form, would refute the union finding and permit a single sort rule.

### F3 — The universal is a functional group, the variation is the binding

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*

Across D1 to D6 the recurring shape is: the same kind of machinery (categorising, naming, expressing, evaluating, counting, stimulus-response) is present, and cultures differ in which member fills the slot and how it is bonded. Fig 4 :20 triangulates from a low-overlap direction: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The kernel persists across size and cast of frame, the actors vary. Fig 12 :103 adds level relativity: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

**Refuter:** a feature whose cultural variation introduces a new kind rather than a new member or bond of a shared kind would refute F3. That would require a seventh primitive in that domain, which the domain census currently does not show.

### F4 — The ground decides, agreement does not

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

A shared view among cross-cultural psychologists, or a shared view among ethnographers, is one evidence pool talking. The anchor for each domain is the comparative measurement named in section 1. Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

**Refuter:** an alleged anchor whose reported value changes when researchers reinterpret it would fail Fig 2 :29 and be demoted to a frame.

### F5 — Independence is bought, not assumed

Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 8 :50: *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*

The world search was therefore run across low-overlap fields: cognitive psychology, ethology, linguistics, mathematics and cross-national survey work. Two pages quoting the same review count as one witness, recorded as overlap in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Refuter:** if any two sources claimed as independent are found to draw on the same dataset or the same review, the triangulation mark for that domain is withdrawn.

### F6 — The feature list is `gap`, not silence

Fig 9 :32: *"→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

The figures return the shape of the answer. They do not contain the names of cognitive features, and they do not contain the ethnographic record. Any specific list produced here would be an import with no source gate, forbidden by Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The honest report for D1 to D6 content is `gap`.

**Refuter:** a per-slot ground measurement that names a feature and survives the anchor test of F4 would promote that slot from `gap` to `specified`, then to `grounded` only when the ground pays.

### F7 — A dependency break sits at comparability

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The chain breaks at vocabulary and objective: emic/etic comparability and the operational threshold for "shared". Effort spent on more sophisticated statistics (above the break) cannot rescue a term whose meaning did not travel. The world literature names this exact failure in the move from an emic construct to a pseudoetic one.

**Refuter:** demonstration of a working instrument that fixes comparability and threshold across two low-overlap frames would move the break and require a fresh census.

### F8 — A shared feature is a property of agent and habitat

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

A feature that looks universal in one habitat is not thereby a property of the isolated agent. Changing the sample to a different habitat changes the contact, not just the measurement. Triangulated with Fig 4 :20 at a different scale.

**Refuter:** an agent-only change that moves the ground, with habitat held fixed, would refute the capability claim in Fig 11 (the figure itself names this as its standing falsifier at :71 to :72).

### F9 — Filters are not witnesses

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

The comparative databases, the journal impact factors and the size of a literature are filters. They tell us where to spend the search budget. They do not tell us whether a feature is universal. This is the rule that blocks the obvious shortcut: "many studies found X, therefore X is universal."

**Refuter:** a case where a budget filter is used as evidence in a domain verdict, and the verdict has not been re-derived without it, is a defect in this solution and must be corrected.

### F10 — No new "culture bond" may be invented

Operators :68: *"Seven tests separate the eight named here. Whether there are more is a question no test derived from these can answer."*, and :68 to :69 adds that a relation failing all seven is a claim not yet made

It is tempting to posit a special operator that maps a universal mind onto a cultural form. None of the seven bonds does this, and no test derives an eighth. The variation is recorded as a difference of binding among the existing bonds, not as a new operator.

**Refuter:** a derived eighth bond, passed by all the identification tests, would change this finding. Until then the temptation is recorded and refused.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. These are written now, before any per-domain verdict is stated, and they are not tuned to the results above.

The shape claim *"shared features form a functional group whose members and bindings vary, the two classes overlap as a union, and the feature names are unreachable from the figures"* is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Partition result.** A feature is found with invariant presence and invariant form across every known society, and no other feature shows both invariant presence and variable form. That yields a clean partition and defeats F2.
2. **New kind result.** A cultural variation introduces a new kind of cognitive primitive rather than a new member or binding of a shared kind. That defeats F3 and the functional-group reading.
3. **Averaging result.** A pooled verdict across feature slots reproduces every per-slot verdict, so the slot discipline changes nothing. That defeats F1.
4. **Anchor result.** An alleged ground for a domain changes its answer when frames push on it, so no anchor exists for that domain. That domain's verdict must be withdrawn rather than repaired.
5. **Independence result.** Two frames marked as triangulating are shown to draw on one evidence pool. That domain's mark falls to single-source or gap.
6. **Naming result.** A figure line is produced that names a specific cognitive feature and pays for it at the ground. That defeats F6 and the `gap` verdict.

If any fires, the domain verdict is rewritten and the shape claim is reduced accordingly. No result from the world search is allowed to flip a figure verdict directly; a conflict sends the census back to step 1.
