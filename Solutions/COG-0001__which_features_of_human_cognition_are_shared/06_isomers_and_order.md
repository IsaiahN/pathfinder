# Isomers, functional groups and order in the universals question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

## RESIDUAL

The question asks which cognitive features are shared and which vary. Two structures are possible and they are not the same:

1. Same parts, different arrangement (isomer).
2. Same arrangement kind, different member (functional group).

The figures decide between them. If the cross-cultural data show the same atoms bound differently, the universal is the atom set and the variation is the bond. If they show the same bond kind with different fillers, the universal is the functional group and the variation is the member. Both readings are figure-derived; the data decide which applies in a given domain, and that decision is `gap` here.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Which structure does the universal/variation contrast have?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*

**Result.** Both structures appear, at different levels. The recurring cross-cultural pattern is the functional-group form: the group (categorising, naming, expressing, evaluating, counting, stimulus-response) is present, and the member varies by society. Where a single society is compared with another at a finer resolution, the same atoms with different bonds (isomer form) is the better description. Color terms illustrate both: the group "basic color term" is everywhere, and the member set and boundary bonds differ (Fig 12 :123 for the group, Fig 12 :117 for the boundaries).

**Level note.** Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* What is universal at one level can be a compound at another. A feature that looks irreducible in one society's folk psychology is not thereby a primitive.

### Q2 — Is the bond ordered or symmetric?

- Operators :59: *"Symmetry decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*

The feature-society residual is non-symmetric, so it is an ordered relation, not a symmetric one. This matters because under a symmetric bond, reordering the operands does not produce a new term, while under an ordered bond it does. A "universal" claim that ignores which society is being measured has silently treated an ordered relation as symmetric. That is the averaging error of Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Result.** The sort rule is ordered. Verification: swap a society label and re-run the sort; if the assignment changes, the relation was ordered all along.

### Q3 — What order governs the search?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The break sits at vocabulary/objective (comparability and threshold), not at data volume.

Fig 9 :43: *"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."* Applied here: the verdict is whether a feature is in the comparative closure at all; the lookup is which low-overlap frame holds a discriminating instrument; elimination is the per-slot check after arrival.

**Result.** Do not rank domains by "how universal" they look (Fig 1 :19 forbids the aggregate). Rank the *work* by dependency: fix comparability first, then threshold, then sampling.

### Q4 — Analogues across low-overlap fields

| field | analogue | low overlap with D1 to D4? | what it corroborates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethology (animal behaviour) | fixed action patterns, described as *"stereotyped, complex, species-characteristic, released, triggered, and independent of experience"* | yes, separate evidence pool | universals are habitat-relative (Fig 11 :39) |
| Mathematics (ethnomathematics) | counting everywhere, bases and number words radically different | yes, separate evidence pool | functional group with variable member (Fig 12 :123) |
| Linguistic typology | shared constraint kind versus parameter settings | partial, overlaps D2 | same group and member distinction |
| Cross-national survey work | evaluative machinery with different thresholds and objects | partial, overlaps D4 | per-slot, no pool shortcut (Fig 1 :19) |

The first two rows are the genuine independence purchases. The last two are recorded as partial overlap and never counted as second witnesses for the same claim (Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*).

### Q5 — Universals versus variation as a worked union

Worked case, D1 color terms, `specified`:

- Invariant group: a language partitions the color continuum into named basic categories.
- Variable member: which categories are basic, and where the boundaries fall.
- Variable bond: the boundary relations between neighboring categories.
- Union, not partition: the same feature is in both the shared column and the varying column, at different levels. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- What would settle the disputed part: a discriminating instrument for whether the boundary differences are perceptual, linguistic or both. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."*

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE CONCLUSION

The isomer and functional-group reading is refuted if:

1. A domain shows variation that requires a new kind of primitive rather than a new member or bond of an existing group (Operators :68: *"Seven tests separate the eight named here. Whether there are more is a question no test derived from these can answer."*, and :68 to :69 adds that a relation failing all seven is a claim not yet made).
2. A domain's feature-society residual turns out to be symmetric, so swapping society labels changes nothing and the ordered-bond reading is wrong.
3. The union collapses to a partition in every domain, with no feature showing both invariant presence and variable form.

Any of these sends the derivation back to the domain census; it does not produce a softer reading of the same figure line.

## CONCLUSION

The universal/variation contrast is best read as functional group versus member or binding, with isomer as the finer-resolution case. The bond is ordered, so no pooled shortcut is available. The search order is dependency order, with comparability and threshold as the uninstrumented break. Low-overlap analogues from ethology and ethnomathematics corroborate the shape without supplying feature names.
