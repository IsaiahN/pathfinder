## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, then stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Fig 9 :32: *"→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (fetched 2026-11-22): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for this question a shape is the narrowed form of an answer we can state before we can name the features. **"Shared kind with variable members and bindings"** is a shape. **"All societies have X"** is a name, and no figure line supplies it. The shape constrains: it rules out a clean partition, rules out a pooled verdict, and rules out a new "culture" primitive. It does not fill the table of features.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Applied here: the concept of a functional group (Fig 12 :123) is the near vocabulary; the feature list is what we cannot yet describe.
- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* The residual is described as a feature-society comparison, not in framework jargon, before low-overlap fields are searched.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here the question we can answer ends where the empirical question begins. That is the honest stop, not a failure.
- The same essay names the union case directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is Fig 9 :54 in essay form and it is why D1 to D6 are six loops.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things license a name, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* A cross-cultural measurement that survives the anchor test of Fig 2 :29 would name a slot.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to :52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the warning. An instrument reads green but *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Several sources can produce one reading. Applied here: observing a behavior in many societies does not identify its source. It could be a shared mechanism, independent invention, diffusion, or a response to a shared habitat. Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*

The essay continues: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* So the remedy for a disputed universal is a discriminating instrument, not a longer list of societies all showing the same behavior.

## 4. What "shape" means specifically for universals versus variation

Three constraints, all figure-derived, form the shape:

1. **Per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A feature-by-feature verdict, never a species-wide score.
2. **Union.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Presence and form are separate axes.
3. **Functional group, not a new kind.** Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."*

A correct answer in this shape would read: for each domain, name the invariant functional group, name the slots that vary, record which member or bond fills each slot in each society, and state the instrument that would settle the disputed ones. An incorrect answer reads: a flat list of universals and a flat list of variations.

## 5. Prestige, agreement and the simulation limit

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A claim backed by famous names or a large literature gets a hearing; it does not get settled. Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Same essay on the status of this work: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

From *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* A shape that fits every known society is still not proof that the list is complete.
