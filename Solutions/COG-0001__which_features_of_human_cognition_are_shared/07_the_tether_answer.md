# The Tether's answer on universals and cultural variation

**Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` for the feature list. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We want to assign cognitive features to "shared across all known societies" or "varies substantially across cultures." The assignment is a two-indexed residual, `(feature, society)`, and it is non-symmetric. What failed to resolve is which part of a feature survives a change of society and which part does not. The figures can constrain the form of that assignment. They do not contain the feature names.

## 2. The shape

1. **The two classes are a union, not a partition.** A feature can be present in every society and vary in form. Color naming is the case: categories are everywhere, their number and boundaries are not. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* `general`.
2. **The universal is a functional group; the variation is the member or the binding.** Fig 12 :123: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :117: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* `general`.
3. **Verdicts are per feature, never averaged.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global "human nature" score is legal with one live counter-society (Fig 1 :13). `general`.
4. **A shared feature is a property of agent and habitat, not of the isolated agent.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Changing the habitat changes the contact, not merely the measurement. `general`.
5. **The ground settles; agreement does not.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict."* `general`.
6. **Per domain, the shape applies as follows.** `specified`:
   - D1 Categorisation: invariant categorising group, variable category members and boundaries.
   - D2 Language: invariant constraint kind, variable feature specification.
   - D3 Expression: invariant expression machinery, variable form and judgment rule.
   - D4 Social and moral cognition: invariant evaluative machinery, variable thresholds and objects.
   - D5 Numeration: invariant counting and ordering, variable bases and number-word morphologies.
   - D6 Animal continuity: capability relative to agent and habitat, species-typical features not agent-only.
7. **The feature list is `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* No figure line names a cognitive feature, and no import was made with a source gate and a shadow test.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of universals. No feature is named as universal or variable.
- Not a claim that all cultures are the same underneath, nor that they are incommensurable. Both would be verdicts at the wrong level.
- Not a derivation from the six-term substrate. The state/structure composition rule (Fig 13 :74) governs composition inside a system, not the cross-cultural sort. It was tried and re-censused out.
- Not a new "culture" operator. Operators :68: *"Seven tests separate the eight named here. Whether there are more is a question no test derived from these can answer."*, and :68 to :69 adds that a relation failing all seven is a claim not yet made.
- Not grounded. No cross-cultural measurement was run here.
- Not a claim that a failed search proves absence. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 4. What would settle it

The ground, domain by domain, with an anchor that does not move when frames push on it.

- **D1:** an instrument that discriminates perceptual from linguistic sources of a color-boundary difference. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a green reading *"cannot comment on the source."*
- **D2:** a typological corpus with enough independence from the generative frame to test whether shared constraints are discovered or stipulated.
- **D3:** a recognition task that avoids posed expressions, forced choice and relative judgment, the three inflation factors named in the criticism of Ekman's paradigm.
- **D4:** samples outside the WEIRD slice, with comparability fixed before the threshold is set. Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan 2010: *"no obvious a priori grounds for claiming that a particular behavioral phenomenon is universal based on sampling from a single subpopulation."*
- **D5 and D6:** ethnographic and ethological records already supply variation; what is owed is the invariant group statement, checked against a discriminating measure.

Pre-registered refuters are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` section 2. They were written before the per-domain verdicts.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is fully derived inside the figures. The feature list is unreachable from the figures at this budget and with these instruments. That is a verdict, not a silence. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The round trip here is the second-seat census in `11_audit.md`. The gap is recorded there; no feature name is smuggled in to fill it.

*How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* A shape that fits every known society is still not the list.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not produce a list of cognitive universals. They say that the list must be assembled feature by feature, never by pooling across societies, and that "shared" and "varies" are not two boxes but two axes: a feature can be everywhere and still take different forms. What stays invariant is a functional group, the kind of machinery a society has; what varies is which member fills the slot and how it is bound. The same pattern holds for color categories, grammar, facial expression, moral judgment, counting and species-typical behaviour. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so it refuses to say which feature is universal. That part is owed to the ground, domain by domain, with an anchor that does not change when researchers disagree about it. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
