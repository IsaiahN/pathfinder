# Solution COG-0001

**Question:** Which features of human cognition are shared across all known societies, and which vary substantially across cultures?

**Source:** `cognitive science.txt` line 4

**Section:** 1. Universals and cultural variation

**Mode:** `general` for the shape claim, `specified` per domain, `gap` for the feature list. No claim in this folder is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question as an effect

Given a candidate cognitive feature, an answer would tell us what to expect when we move from one society to another. If the feature is shared, we predict its presence regardless of the cultural frame. If it varies, we predict that its form, rule or frequency changes with the frame. The two predictions are not exclusive: a feature can be present everywhere and still take different forms. That is why the split is treated as a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :54).

An answer would look like a rule that assigns each feature to a class, plus an account of why the classes differ. What this solution actually delivers is narrower: the figures fix the **shape** such a rule must have, and they cannot name the features. The feature list is `gap`, not silence.

## Boundary

This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a ground reading. It cannot settle the empirical question it reports on. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` header and `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

Nothing outside this folder was changed. Figure quotes come from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` only, cited as `Fig N :line`. World sources were fetched on 2026-11-22 and are recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name any particular cognitive universal.

The reasons are figure-grounded. Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40). A verifier handed the full interior cannot verify the claim (Fig 10 :42). A room cannot add an anchor (Fig 7 :35).
