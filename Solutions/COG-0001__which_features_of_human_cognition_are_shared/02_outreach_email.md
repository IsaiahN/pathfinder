# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the recipient can see a residual and its refuter without any framework vocabulary. The two-stage offer collapses if stage 1 cannot be checked on its own.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cognitive universals, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Which features of human cognition are present in every known society, and which change substantially with culture? A common way to answer is to sort features into two bins and report a list. The output I want checked is about the *shape* that any such answer must have, not the list.
>
> **The output (`general`, not `grounded`):** the two bins are not a partition, they are a union. A feature can be present in every society and still take different forms. Color naming is the plain case: every society has color categories, but languages differ in how many they have and where the boundaries fall. So "universal or variable?" is the wrong question. The right question is "which part stays invariant, which part is bound differently, and at what level?"
>
> The second part of the output: what stays invariant is a functional group (Fig 12 :123, *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*), and what varies is the member and the binding (Fig 12 :117, *"same atoms, different bond, different substance"*). This holds across categorisation, grammar, expression, social judgment, counting and stimulus-response behaviour. Each slot must be judged on its own (Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"*). A pooled "human nature" score hides the very signal the question asks about.
>
> **What the output does not do:** it does not name any cognitive feature. It returns a shape, not a name. The feature list is `gap`, meaning unreachable from the figures, not silence about the world. I am not claiming that any particular feature is universal, that a named instinct exists in all societies, or that a particular cultural practice is a variation of a fixed module. Those are empirical claims and need a ground, not a derivation.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I ran this:**
> 1. A clean partition: one invariant feature with invariant form, and no feature showing both invariant presence and variable form.
> 2. A cultural variation that introduces a new kind of cognitive primitive rather than a new member or binding of a shared kind.
> 3. Pooled verdicts across features reproducing every per-feature verdict, so the slot discipline changes nothing.
> 4. No stable anchor for any domain, that is, an alleged ground whose answer changes when frames push on it.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, the output is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - What "shape" means, from the essays: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-library-of-babel-locating-the
> - Why a name is not licensed by a reading: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior. |
| No request for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
