## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question: how a learner detects that the environment has changed enough to invalidate what it previously learned. Option A: treat "enough" as one global error threshold and name a standard detector. Option B: treat the residual as a union compound and return the shape a detection procedure must have. What each leads to: A collapses five distinct verdicts into one alarm, averages away a live slot, and uses a criterion that may update with the learner, so drift becomes coherent and invisible. B keeps the slot signal, separates habitat change from channel decay from a false term, pins the anchor, states what would refute the shape, and stops at the limit of the instruments.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A learner's held terms keep missing their predictions after a change. Arity: at least two conditions (held terms and the generating process), with a three-way split underneath (habitat change, channel decay, false term), so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54), not one hard question. Symmetry: anisotropic, indexed by slot, so one live slot can coexist with a near-zero global residual (Fig 1 :13). Scale: signal arrives within one scale, while refusal changes the controller's vocabulary and the habitat/channel split crosses the membrane.
- **D1, the environment / habitat.** Actors: actors, conditions, relations in contact with R. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, which settles and does not update (Fig 11 :73). Union with D2 at the observation; categorically distinct from the ground.
- **D2, the learner's loop and channel.** Actors: Gamma, belief b, weighting w, action a, observation o-prime, slots s. Currency: R per slot; two incommensurable prices in bits and actions (Fig 12 :126). Anchor: the same ground, reached through a channel that can decay (Fig 10 :24). Partition of R into five bins.
- **D3, the checking arrangement.** Actors: held-out readings, second closures, outside frames. Currency: independence, how little evidence is shared (Fig 2 :31). Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35). Separate frame; never pooled with D2.

All three domains have a nameable anchor, so all three are domains. The sought-for threshold is not a domain: it is a preference until bound as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65).

Order by dependency: contact before verdict (Fig 3 :19); describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); unbundle before searching again (Fig 9 :54); refuse before mint (formula step 2); SEARCHED before INWARD and OUTWARD (formula step 7); ground settles last (Fig 5 :70).

Strain test applied: ADWIN averages a time window while Fig 1 :19 forbids aggregating slots. Re-census resolves it: a slot is an object tracked, a window is a run of time for one scalar, so the two govern different objects. The practical warning, that a pooled scalar loses the one-live-slot signal before the detector sees it, is kept. No figure was strained to fit.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a named detector and a threshold do not. Keep the residual per slot, because a global average can be flat while one tracked thing is failing. Read "enough" as a residual that persists and does not trend down on a term already held, which is the shrinking test with the opposite sign. Split the decision into five bins; only a false held term owes a refusal, and the other bins cannot reach it. Pin the anchor, because a criterion that updates with the learner makes drift coherent and invisible. Remember that the habitat changes while the ground does not, and that a decayed channel produces the same reading but a different verdict. Buy independence rather than more copies of one monitor. Bind the threshold before the run. And never report "no alarm" as proof of no drift. The three drift causes are isomers under one residual reading: same reading, different cause, different remedy. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The stop is at the limit of the available instruments, with the lineage recorded.

- **cost if wrong:** two-sided and locatable. A false alarm removes the standing of a still-valid term and pays for a refusal that was not owed; a missed detection keeps acting on a term the ground no longer pays for, and the loss accumulates silently because a pooled metric stays green. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable cost here is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a named detector and a full theory before the shape and its refuter have been checked.
- **undoable:** yes. A refusal adds a term and removes nothing; "NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED" (formula step 3). The refused term stays in the record, so if the habitat returns, new growth can re-establish reach. Keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41) and the correction is computable from derivation rather than stipulated.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot record against a pinned anchor showing that only persistent, non-trending-down residuals lead to correct refusals would move the shape toward `grounded`; a source-discriminating instrument that separates habitat change from channel decay would settle the isomer claim; a global aggregate that detects as well as the per-slot reading, an updating anchor that works as well as a pinned one, a persistent residual reliably removed by re-fit, or a finite proof of no drift would each break the corresponding finding. Prestige or volume of agreement is not such an instrument.
