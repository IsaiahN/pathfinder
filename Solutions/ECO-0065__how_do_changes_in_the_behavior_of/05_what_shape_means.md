## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The local canonical rule states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can measure it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- Shape: a behaviour change is a contact-graph change, and the network reshapes through a cascade enumerated outward from the residual.
- Not shape: a cascade coefficient, a fixed sign for "predator" or "pollinator", a species list, or a management recipe.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* bootstraps vocabulary by proximity: *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the starting vocabulary is the question's own nouns, predator, prey, pollinator, network, and the nearby figure vocabulary is contact, cascade, valence, bond, node, state.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect terms here are interaction turnover, state change, and contact distance from the actor that changed.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is why the outreach email in `02` sends a predicted pattern, not a theory.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."* In this question, the starting nouns (predator, prey, pollinator) are not thrown away; they are re-read as roles once contact and residual have been named.

## 4. Why the source question matters here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."* An instrument that reads an interaction frequency cannot say whether that frequency came from a changed predator, a changed prey, a changed pollinator, or a changed season. The same reading has several possible sources. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*, and in that essay the resolution *"was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: a net change in visitation or predation rate is a projection. Attributing it to the actor whose behaviour was observed is a source named from a reading. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a stronger narrative. This is the same limit the audit records as F2.

## 5. The habitat is not an ecological niche: correction to a natural mapping

This is the most important correction for an ecology question, and it comes from the canonical article itself, in its section "Three mappings offered and declined":

> *"A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes. The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align, which is why the figure says enumerated, never composed rather than borrowing the ecological term outright."*

So the framework does **not** take its "habitat" to be an ecological niche, and this solution does not silently equate them. The word is used in the framework's sense: the field of contact with a particular residual, which changes when the residual changes. A question about "keep the pollinator community viable" and a question about "maximise seed set" generate different habitats over the same species. This is a genuine refusal, not a terminological quibble, and it is why `01` F7 records the mapping as declined.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* repeats the capability claim (*"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*) and contains a spelling variant of Fig 11 :50, *"malefactor"* where the figure says *"malfactor"*. Both forms are recorded in `01` section 6; the figure's spelling governs.

## 6. Why the honest stop is not a refusal to answer

*How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"This process isn't about reaching the truth. It stops when nothing you have registers a difference from it. That's operational, and it's the only version that doesn't require knowing the answer in advance."* The same essay on habitat alteration: *"when altering a habitat by unintentionally introducing a predatory threat into the ecosystem, or disturbing the balance by adding or removing vital resource points, you can inadvertently create major unpredictable consequences downstream."* That is the framework's own ecological instance of F3, and it is a reason to stop at a shape rather than to fabricate a coefficient.

The stop is recorded with the lineage, per the canonical rule: *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A reading that matches a cascade is still a reading, not the source.
