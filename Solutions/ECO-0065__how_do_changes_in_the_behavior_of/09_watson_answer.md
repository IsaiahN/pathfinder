## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says happens when the behaviour of a predator, prey or pollinator changes and an ecological network changes with it. Option A: treat those actor classes as kinds with fixed effects and read the network change off the actor list. Option B: treat them as contact roles whose valence is relative to a named residual, enumerate the habitat outward, and follow the cascade. What each leads to: A gives a first-order story that misses effects several steps out and assigns fixed signs that the figures refuse; B gives a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open coefficient until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: one behaviour change plus one measurement choice, so a union compound, not one lever (Fig 9 :55-58: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either). Symmetry: predation and pollination are directed, the order of operands matters. Scale: one actor's behaviour, the interaction graph, ecosystem state. Anchor: a measured interaction turnover or state change, never agreement among ecologists.
- **Domain 1, habitat / contact frame.** Actors: predators, prey, pollinators, plants, competitors, conditions. Currency: contact, cascade mass, valence relative to R. Anchor: measured interaction frequencies and state outcomes. Question type: enumeration, not composition.
- **Domain 2, structural / substrate frame.** Actors: the six terms, especially network (structure) with space, time and energy (states), plus the bonds. Currency: composition across kinds and isomerism. Anchor: the six-term set as metalaw. This overlaps domain 1 in the network, so it is a compound with its own loop (Fig 12).
- **Domain 3, empirical ecology frame.** Actors: field experiments, censuses, models. Currency: observations and residuals. Anchor: the experiment in its own system. Several distinct systems are used below, so overlap between them is low.
- **Domain 4, verification / management frame.** Actors: reviewers, managers, maintainers. Currency: agreement versus ground. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run the second seat shares the primary's evidence pool, so its independence is zero.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Name R, enumerate direct contacts, enumerate outward until the cascade stops mattering, name the states, compare arrangements, measure the outcome. Perception precedes vocabulary; vocabulary precedes objective. Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a link below it.

**Strain test.** Fig 11 says the habitat is enumerated, never composed; Fig 12 and Fig 13 say a network composes. Re-census resolves this: Fig 11 governs how the list is obtained, Fig 12 and Fig 13 govern how a held structure composes. Different objects, no strain after re-census. The apparent niche/habitat equivalence is explicitly declined in the canonical article, so it is not a strain either.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

Every source below was opened on 2026-11-23 and is recorded with author, venue, year and quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`. World sources corroborate but never overrule the figures.

- **Ecology, plant-pollinator networks.** CaraDonna et al., *Ecology Letters*, 2017. Quote: *"Week-to-week turnover of interactions (1) was high, (2) followed a consistent seasonal progression in all years of study and (3) was dominated by interaction rewiring (the reassembly of interactions among species)."* Prediction: same species reassemble their interactions over a season; a species list alone misses the reshaping. Corroborates C1 and C3.
- **Ecology, mutualist networks.** MacLeod et al., *Ecology*, 2016. Quote: *"All 52 bee species varied in plant partner choice across years"* and *"rewiring and fidelity both exist in mutualist networks, but that once sampling effects have been accounted for, they are less common than has been reported."* Prediction: behaviour change (foraging choice) rewires bonds at constant species, but chance must be separated. Corroborates C3, and adds a caution against over-reading turnover.
- **Ecology, kelp forests.** Sheridan and Randell, *Oecologia*, 2026. Quote: *"changes in sea urchin foraging behavior in response to food availability or predators can cause state shifts from forests to urchin barrens."* Prediction: a behaviour change registers at ecosystem state; the cue that matters is not the one a first-order account expects. Corroborates C2 strongly, in a different system and evidence pool.
- **Ecology, freshwater cascades.** Biagioli et al., *Journal of Animal Ecology*, 2026. Quote: *"climate warming can strengthen trophic cascades, destabilize population dynamics and magnify the ecological impacts of predator loss through complex, temperature-dependent changes in species interactions and demographics."* Prediction: a state (temperature) changes interaction strength and cascade magnitude, matching the requirement to name states. Corroborates the state requirement.
- **Mathematical ecology.** Acharya et al., *Mathematical Biosciences and Engineering*, 2026. Quote: *"trait-mediated indirect effects are frequently observed and can influence population dynamics as strongly, possibly even more strongly, than direct effects"* and *"the combined influence of fragmentation and trait-mediated dispersal can substantially reshape the population dynamics and species coexistence outcomes."* Prediction: behaviour-only effects can dominate density effects and reshape coexistence. Corroborates C2 from a theoretical frame with low overlap.
- **Ecology, marine.** Kressler et al., *Ecological Applications*, 2026. Quote: *"juvenile sharks respond more strongly to the 'seascape of fear' than prey availability"* and *"Tidal cycles emerged as a key abiotic driver."* Prediction: a state (risk landscape) can outrank prey availability in shaping space use. Corroborates the valence-relative and state claims.
- **Network science, low-overlap frame.** Newman, *SIAM Review*, 2003 (arXiv cond-mat/0303516). Quote: *"researchers have in recent years developed a variety of techniques and models to help us understand or predict the behavior of these systems"*, covering *"small-world effect, degree distributions, clustering, network correlations"* and *"dynamical processes taking place on networks."* Prediction: the arrangement of a graph, not just its node count, governs what the network does. Corroborates the bond-carries-information claim from a outside-biology frame.
- **Essays by the framework author (high overlap, weak corroboration).** *The Library of Babel* gives the shape rule; *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument requirement; *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives describe-the-effect-first; *Arrival of the Fittest* gives the habitat/niche refusal. These share the framework's author and evidence pool, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered. Agreement across the six ecology studies is from several distinct systems, but they all belong to the same broad field, so they are corroboration of a shape, not a second derivation of it.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the coefficient does not. Predator, prey and pollinator are contact roles whose valence is relative to the residual; the habitat is enumerated outward from that residual; a behaviour change reshapes the network through a cascade whose consequential contact can be several steps from the actor that moved; the network is a structure needing named states; and same species with different wiring is a different substance. The niche mapping is refused. Nothing here is `grounded`: the numeric coefficient, the sign for a particular actor in a particular system, and the length of the cascade in any given case are a `gap` left to the ground. The global verdict is `specified` and candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, non-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending the whole framework before the check (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*) and by selecting recipients for agreement (Fig 2 :40). Both are avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The structural claims and the coefficient claim are separate. Removing the coefficient claim leaves the shape intact, and a superseding packet can be sent without withdrawing any figure line. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A multi-order contact census in which first-, second- and third-order contacts are all unchanged while the state changes would refute the cascade claim; a valence that survives every residual would refute F1; identical state outcomes from nets with identical species and bonds but different behaviour would remove the isomer mechanism; an account with no named state would remove the substrate requirement. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
