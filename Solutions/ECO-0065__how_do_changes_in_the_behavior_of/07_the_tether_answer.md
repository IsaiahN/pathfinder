# The Tether's answer: how behaviour change reshapes ecological networks

**Mode: `specified` for the cascade direction and the structural claims, `general` for the shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading and not a measurement of any particular system.

## 1. The residual it answers

A behaviour change in a predator, prey or pollinator is observed, and the network changes. The residual is the gap between a first-order account, which reads the change off the actor that moved, and the network-level change that has to be explained. It is a union of a contact change and a measurement choice (Fig 9 :55-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), so it has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **The actors are roles, not kinds.** Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Which effect a predator or pollinator has depends on the outcome being asked about. There is no standing list of good actors (Fig 11 :51).
2. **The habitat is enumerated.** Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed"*; *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* The network cannot be derived from actor labels; contact is read off the world.
3. **The reshaping is a cascade, not a local substitution.** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A behaviour change can register two or three steps from the actor whose behaviour changed. Counting only direct partners is a first-order measurement and misses it.
4. **No contact change, no network change.** Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Capability and effect live in the agent-and-habitat, not in the agent alone (Fig 11 :39).
5. **The network is a structure and needs states.** Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*; Fig 13 :29: *"nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."* A complete account says where the interaction happens, when it happens, and what is spent in it. Network does not compose with network; it composes across kinds (Fig 13 :74).
6. **Same species, different wiring, different substance.** Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* A foraging reassignment at constant species is an isomer change, not a relabelling. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
7. **Management can introduce and cannot exclude.** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A managed response is permanent work plus an enumerated cascade, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a cascade coefficient, a sign for a whole actor class, or a species-level prediction. The framework returns a shape and a route, not a name or a number (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that its "habitat" is an ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping: a niche is an organism's relation to its environment, while this habitat is contact with a residual that moves when the question changes. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5.
- Not a replacement for ecological network analysis, and not a new primitive. Network is already one of the six (Fig 13 :23).
- Not a management recommendation. The asymmetry constrains what a maintainer may do; it does not choose the intervention.
- Not `grounded`. No interaction turnover, cascade length or state change was measured here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and the case was closed *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Cascade test:** after a behaviour change, measure first-, second- and third-order contacts on a named residual. If all are unchanged while the state changes, the cascade claim is refuted.
- **Valence test:** attempt to assign a fixed sign to predator, prey or pollinator across residuals. If one survives, the valence-relative claim is refuted.
- **Bond test:** compare two nets with identical species and bonds but different behaviour. If state outcomes match, the isomer reading loses its mechanism.
- **State test:** try to give a complete, checkable reshaping account with no space, time or energy state named. If one exists, the substrate requirement does not bind here.
- **Instrument test:** use independent frames drawing on different evidence. A pooled review is one witness (Fig 2 :40).

The standing refuters are written in `01` section 3 and repeated in `08`. Any one firing retracts the relevant row of `01` F10.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is derived; the coefficient is not. A source-discriminating instrument at the right scale would name the route, and `THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 gives the order: SEARCHED first (is the coefficient already in the closure and unfound?), then INWARD (extend the interaction instrument from one already returning something), then OUTWARD (find a frame whose closure predicts the shape). The three are kept distinct. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a cascade coefficient for predators, prey or pollinators, and it does not treat them as actor kinds with fixed effects. It says they are contact roles whose valence depends on the outcome being asked about; that the habitat containing them must be enumerated outward from that outcome rather than derived from a species list; and that a behaviour change reshapes the network because it changes contacts, with the consequential change often two or three steps from the actor that moved. The network is a structure, so a complete account has to name where, when and with what energy the interactions occur, and two nets with the same species but different wiring are different substances, not two spellings of one net. What is offered is that shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The niche mapping is explicitly refused: this habitat is contact with a residual, not an organism's relation to its environment. The coefficient stays open until a ground settles it.
