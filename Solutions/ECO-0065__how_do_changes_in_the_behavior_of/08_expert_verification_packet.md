# Expert verification packet: the Tether on behaviour change in ecological networks

**What this is:** a claim in a form a behavioral ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the cascade direction and structural claims, `general` for the shape. Not `grounded`.** No coefficient or state change is claimed as measured here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Predator, prey and pollinator are contact roles whose valence is relative to the residual being asked about, not actor kinds with fixed effects. A behaviour change reshapes an ecological network because it changes contacts, and the consequential contact can be two or three steps from the actor whose behaviour changed; accounting that stops at direct partners is first order and misses it. The network is a structure, so a complete account must name where, when and with what energy the interaction occurs. Two nets with the same species and different wiring are isomers, not spellings of one net. The framework's habitat is contact with a residual, not an ecological niche; that mapping is explicitly declined. What is offered is the shape, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** valence is relative to the residual; predator, prey and pollinator are relations rather than kinds; the habitat is enumerated outward from the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :43-44 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* and *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **Refuter:** assign a valence to one of those actor classes that holds across every residual and cannot be refused; or produce a rule that generates the correct habitat list from actor labels without measuring contact.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against a real system

- **Statement:** a behaviour change reshapes the network through a cascade, with the consequential contact potentially several steps out; direct-partner accounting is first order and will miss it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** in a system you know, name a behaviour change in a predator, prey or pollinator and the residual at stake. Are the first-, second- and third-order contacts on that residual unchanged? If they are, the claim predicts no reshaping. If they change but the state does not, the claim predicts no state effect. The published cases in `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD are starting points, not confirmations of this packet.
- **Refuter:** show all first-, second- and third-order contacts on the chosen residual unchanged after the behaviour change while the network still changes.

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the network is a structure that composes across kinds and needs named states; same species with different wiring is a different substance (an isomer).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 13 :74-75 - *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* and *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."*
- **What to check:** can a complete account of a network reshaping be given without naming a space, time or energy state? Does a foraging reassignment at constant species produce a different measured state outcome, as the isomer claim predicts?
- **Refuter:** give a complete checkable account with no state named, or show that nets with the same species and the same bonds but different behaviour have identical measured state outcomes.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a cascade coefficient, a sign for a whole actor class, or a species-level prediction. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that the framework's habitat is an ecological niche. The canonical article's "Three mappings offered and declined" refuses that mapping because the definitions do not align.
- Not a replacement for ecological network analysis, and no new primitive. Network is already one of the six terms (Fig 13 :23).
- Not a management recommendation. Only the asymmetry is claimed: introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this derivation.
- Not corroborated by a room of agreeing readers. A room does not add an anchor (Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*).

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument that discriminates sources, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A multi-order contact census around a named residual, separating first-, second- and third-order effects.
2. A valence test across at least two different residuals for the same actor species.
3. A paired-network comparison at constant species with different measured wiring and a measured state outcome.
4. Where a maintainer is involved, a record of what was introduced and what cascade was enumerated in advance. Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-54 and Fig 12 :117, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one system you know: name the behaviour change, name the residual, and say how far out the consequential contact sits.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a rewiring from a species-turnover explanation of the same interaction table, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
