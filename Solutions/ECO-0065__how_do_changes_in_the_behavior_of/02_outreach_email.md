**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 3 are fixed in the order below. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable prediction about predator, prey and pollinator effects on networks

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about behaviour change in ecological networks that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** predator, prey and pollinator are contact roles, not actor kinds with fixed effects. Their valence is relative to the residual being asked about, so the same actor can help one outcome and harm another. From that it follows that a behaviour change reshapes a network through a cascade of changed contacts, and that the consequential contact can be two or three steps away from the actor whose behaviour changed. Counting only direct partners is a first-order measurement and will miss that residual. This is a prediction of a **shape and a direction**, not a measured cascade coefficient.
>
> The same account says that a behaviour change which leaves every contact and valence unchanged changes nothing at the network level. The network itself is a structure, so a complete account has to say where the interaction happens, when, and what is spent in it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular study confirms the framework, that the framework is a replacement for ecological network analysis, or that I have measured a cascade. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment; the habitat in this account is defined by contact with a residual that moves when the question changes. The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align, so I am not borrowing the ecological term.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. In a system where a predator, prey or pollinator changes behaviour, all first-, second- and third-order contacts on the chosen residual are unchanged, while the network still changes.
> 2. A valence for one of those actor classes can be assigned that holds across every residual and cannot be refused.
> 3. Two networks with the same species set and the same interaction bonds, but different behaviour, produce the same measured state outcomes.
>
> If any holds, the relevant part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, especially Fig 11 lines 43 to 54 and Fig 12 line 117.

> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

> Thanks,
