## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Bloom et al. is a filter, not a verdict.** The paper shows research productivity declining across industries and *"substantial heterogeneity across firms"*. It supports per-project indexing (Fig 1 :19) and the need to distinguish *underivable* from *unreachable-at-budget*. It does not say which project will pay, and its averages are exactly the pooled readings Fig 1 :22 warns about. Do not present the 10 percent aggregate decline as an answer to BUS-0034.
2. **Cohen and Levinthal and Teece are one witness, not two.** The first names the ability to use external knowledge (the import condition C2); the second names the distribution of returns (the composition and payment condition C3). They share an economics and management evidence pool, so by Fig 2 :31 and :40 their agreement is the pool talking. The Berkeley exaptation page is the independent, low-overlap witness for the mechanism shape.
3. **Fleming's variance result is a qualified corroboration.** New combinations are *"more variable"*, producing both more failure and more breakthrough. That matches the figure's claim that an import adds an atom but does not guarantee settlement. It does not support a naive "distant combinations always win" claim; Fleming explicitly reports that new combinations are *"less useful inventions on average."*

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the three-condition shape were true before giving them any source.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost of being wrong about research funding is high, so independent frames are worth more than a large audience.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which of the three conditions they would instrument first; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the sources, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (with an explicit third stage)

1. **Stage 1:** validate the shape against the ground. The recipient checks the three conditions without holding the framework's interior. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the theory that produced it. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and the RSI essay warns that simulated outputs can be produced without deriving the real thing.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is a `specified` shape with written refuters, not a `grounded` result. Nothing in this folder may be represented as settled by a market measurement.
