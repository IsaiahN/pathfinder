# Expert verification packet - the Tether on research investment and commercial value

**What this is:** a claim in a form a research-management or innovation-economics scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No per-project market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A research investment is not a stock that buys an innovation. Its outcome is a property of investment and habitat. Three conditions must hold for a project slot to yield a major, commercially paid innovation: the project settles against a reachable ground and the channel stays open; its reach is renewed by an import from a closure that differs; and the settled term composes into something the market pays the investor for. They are a union, not a gradient, and are kept separate. Quick loss is channel failure rather than ground decay. A true result that cannot compose is exactly the case of a discovery with little commercial value. The capability that produced earlier successes can change valence when the residual changes, and the framework returns a shape, not the name of the winning project.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus management-facing; checkable by reading Figures 10, 6 and 5

- **Statement:** quick loss is a failure of the channel, not the ground; a major innovation needs an import; and settlement does not imply composition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*; Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*; Fig 5 :70 - *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one."*
- **What to check:** are these three distinct quantities in the figures, and do they map to distinct failure modes in research management (channel decay, closure stagnation, settled-but-uncomposable result)?
- **Refuter:** more search inside a demonstrably fixed closure produces a genuine new atom; or a settled term always composes into a paying product with no further atom and no complementary asset.

### C2 - method-facing; checkable against any R&D dataset

- **Statement:** outcomes must be indexed per project, and internal metrics are not anchors. Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **What to check:** can a published dataset show, prospectively and per named project, which investment will be commercially major, using only internal metrics? If yes, the exclusion of internal metrics as anchors is false. Note that Bloom et al. report *"substantial heterogeneity across firms"*, which supports per-slot indexing, while their aggregate decline is an average and is a filter, not a witness.
- **Refuter:** an internal metric whose per-project prediction is confirmed by the market.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 9, 12 and 11

- **Statement:** the question is a union that must be unbundled; two equally funded investments can be isomers; and the value of a capability is relative to the residual. Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** is the union reading right, or can one lever restore all three conditions with no categorically different residue? Is the isomer reading right, or can an investment's outcome be predicted from its input list alone? The Berkeley exaptation page is a low-overlap instance of the valence rule: *"a feature that performs a function but that was not produced by natural selection for its current use."*
- **Refuter:** a single lever restoring all conditions in all failure types with no residue; or a capability whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a name of a specific project, field, funding level, or input mix. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that more R&D spending is the answer, and not that talent and process are irrelevant. Both are filters or candidate Γ, never verdicts (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a single cause. A blended innovation score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) that destroys the diagnostic.
- Not a derivation of absorptive capacity, appropriability, or recombinant uncertainty from the figures. They are bindings from the management frame, provenanced as such, with zero evidentiary weight until a source gate and shadow test are supplied.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a market measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-project instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and metamerism was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

1. **C1 instrument:** can we tell, prospectively and per project, whether the channel to the ground still carries a live residual rather than pooled readings and saturated metrics (Fig 10 :26)?
2. **C2 instrument:** can we tell whether a new atom entered from a differing closure, rather than more search inside the same one (Fig 6 :21, :23)?
3. **C3 instrument:** can we tell whether a settled term composes into a paying product, and whether the investor is positioned to receive the payment (Fig 5 :69-70; Teece 1986, single-source for the business name)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :24-29, Fig 6 :21-23, Fig 5 :69-70, Fig 9 :57, Fig 12 :117, Fig 11 :49. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a dataset you know: does any internal metric predict per project which investment will be commercially major?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a failed channel from a failed import from a failed composition, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. No theory needs to be read for this check.
