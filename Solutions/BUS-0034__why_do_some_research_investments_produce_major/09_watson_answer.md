## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the variation in outcomes across research investment slots. Option A: the outcome is a stock the investment buys, so more spend, more talent, or more time is the answer. Option B: the outcome is a relation of investment and habitat, and it is a union of three conditions with different instruments. What each leads to: A spends the budget on a filter and ignores the per-project signal (Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :60); B returns a checkable shape, three pre-registered refuters, and an honest stop at the limit of the available instruments.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what an investment's library predicts and what the ground returns, indexed by project slot s and time. Arity: three-place relation (Γ, a, o′), so it carries an ordered bond `->` and a comparison bond `⋛` for "major" and "commercial". Symmetry: not symmetric; the thresholds are relative to alternatives. Scale: project loop, research organization, market and economy. Fig 1 :19 says the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.
- **Domain 1, the project loop.** Actors: researcher, instruments, project Γ. Currency: residual reduction, |φ| cost. Anchor: a settled measurement (Fig 2 :29 names "a measurement, an experiment"). Partition question: which compositions settle.
- **Domain 2, the research portfolio.** Actors: PIs, teams, funders, reviewers, a shared library and something that ranks what they produce. Currency: funding, papers, citations, patents, influence. Anchor: the ground's settlement and the market's payment; internal metrics are not anchors (Fig 1 :26-27). Overlaps D4.
- **Domain 3, the commercial ground.** Actors: customers, adopters, competitors, owners of complementary assets. Currency: willingness to pay, adoption, profit. Anchor: the market (Fig 2 :29), legitimate because the question is constitutively about commercial value (Fig 2 :36).
- **Domain 4, the temporal channel.** Actors: instruments, metrics, percepts. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24). Overlaps D2.
- **Domain 5, the closure and import domain.** Actors: the organization's Γ and external frames whose closures differ. Currency: reach, atoms, |φ| cost. Anchor: settlement, "then check it against the ground, then carry it up" (Fig 5 :32). Overlaps all domains.
- **X1 genius as free-floating property:** no nameable anchor independent of the ground, not a domain yet. **X2 spend level:** measurable but a filter and a pooled average, absorbed as a budget input, not a domain.
- **Union, not partition.** D1/D3, D2/D4 and D5 overlap. Unbundle into C1 settlement and contact, C2 reach renewal, C3 composition and payment. Order by dependency: perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry (Fig 3); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test: an early attempt to apply Fig 13's six physical terms to research inputs fit only by renaming spend as energy and researchers as gradients. Re-census sends Fig 13 to the honest-stop role only (strain S1). One other strain remains named: `⋛` takes two quantities but its operand at the second-order composition is a settled molecule now serving as an atom, which needs an instrument to decide its type.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; no specific investment is named. A research investment yields a major, commercially paid innovation when its channel to a paying ground stays reachable and a term actually settles (C1), its reach is renewed by contact with a closure that differs so a new atom can enter and compose (C2), and the settled term composes into something the market pays the investor for (C3). It yields little commercial value when any of these fails, and the capability that produced past success can change valence when the residual changes. The conditions are a union, kept separate and never averaged. Which particular investment will pay is a `gap`. The management names (absorptive capacity, appropriability, recombinant uncertainty) are bindings with provenance, not derivations and not imports that move the wall. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, but the real cost is external. A funder who accepts the shape without refuting it may spend on a filter (more spend, more people) while the actual failure is a dead channel, a fixed closure, or a settled term that cannot compose. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is reduced by leading with the shape and its four refuters and recording the outside verdict before showing any theory.
- **undoable:** yes. The three conditions are separable, so a wrong mapping can be dropped without discarding the rest. `06_isomers_and_order.md` already records which parts are bindings and which are figure-derived. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive must be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A prospective per-project instrument that discriminates C1 from C2 from C3, with predictions registered in advance and confirmed by the market, would move the shape toward `grounded`. An internal metric predicting per project which investment will pay, more search in a fixed closure producing a new atom, a settled term that always composes and pays, or one lever restoring all conditions with no residue would break the shape. Agreement among reviewers or among management sources would not reverse it (Fig 2 :35, :40).
