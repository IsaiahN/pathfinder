# Are two research investments isomers? Structure, order, and competing explanations

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6). No project-level measurement was used.

## RESIDUAL

Two investments can hold apparently identical ingredients: comparable money, comparable headcount, comparable time, comparable scientific inputs. Yet one yields a major innovation and the other yields little commercial value. Two questions are owed: (1) can that difference be a difference of **arrangement** rather than a difference of ingredients, in Fig 12's sense of isomer, and (2) what is the **order** of the chain from investment to payment, and where can it break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available for the comparison (from `01`, section 1): a project's Γ (knowledge, routines, tacit know-how), a (the funding action), a ground return o′, a slot s, and the bonds held in the operator set, including sequence `→`, production `⇒`, comparison `⋛`, subtraction `-`, and negation `¬`. No new primitive is available without import. The three conditions C1, C2, C3 from `01` are the candidate substructures.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two equally funded investments isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- An input list (budget, headcount, facilities, years) is a flat formula. It records ingredients and not arrangement. Fig 12 :6-17 names that exact failure as "the flat formula": the same written formula can describe melt, freeze and boil because *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not."*
- Under the figures, two investments with the same atom set can therefore be different substances if their bonds differ: one bonds its Γ to a differing closure by import (Fig 6 :23) and settles (Fig 5 :32) before composing (Fig 12 :104); the other composes only inside its own closure (Fig 6 :21). Same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is a real isomer claim, and it is `specified`, not grounded.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two investments with identical atom sets and identical bonds (same closure contacts, same settlement history, same ground) can be shown to yield different outcomes with no further difference anywhere, the isomer reading fails, because outcome would then depend on something the framework does not record. Conversely, if an investment's outcome can be predicted from its input list alone, prospectively and per slot, Fig 12 :5 is refuted for this domain and the flat-formula answer wins.

### Q2 - Functional group: what do all paying investments share?

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :123: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- All three conditions C1, C2, C3 can be present in investments with completely different atoms (different fields, different instruments, different teams). What they share is the functional group: a term settles, an atom enters from a differing closure, and the settled term becomes an atom for a paying composition. The qualifier says which member: which ground, which import, which composition.
- The Berkeley exaptation case is the low-overlap analogue: a trait's present function is not its original function, so the functional group is defined by the relation to the current residual, not by a standing list of good ingredients (Fig 11 :51). This maps to research management as: there is no standing list of good research inputs that survives a change of residual.

### Q3 - What is the order from investment to payment?

- `→` is sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). The loop is ordered: perceive, route, mint, accept, settle, promote, repeat (Formula steps 1 to 8).
- `⋛` is comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87). Both "major" and "commercial value" are comparisons, not ingredients. The Operators table is explicit: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* (Operators :52).
- The dependency order from Fig 3 (quoted in `01` section 1.4) gives the chain: perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to learn and carry. A chain ordered by dependency means effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20).
- **Strain named, not smoothed:** a threshold comparator `⋛` takes two quantities, but the outcome at the end of an investment chain is a settled molecule now serving as an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104). Whether such an atom counts as a quantity at that level is a type question needing an instrument, not an argument. It is recorded here as a strain, the same strain the exemplar recorded for second-order ⋛. It does not change the verdict; it limits the claim.

### Q4 - Round-trip gap R_T between "research input" and "innovation"

- Symbols table: R_T = gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x)), the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **Sent up (x):** the three-condition shape. A term settles, an atom enters from a differing closure, the settled term composes and is paid for.
- **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand used in funding practice, "research input to innovation output", usually measured as spend, publications and patents.
- **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim that a particular investment produced a major commercial innovation.
- **R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints do not survive the trip: (a) the distinction between settlement and composition (Fig 5 :69-70), (b) the requirement of a differing closure for a new atom (Fig 6 :23), and (c) the per-project indexing that the averaged metric drops (Fig 1 :22). What survives cleanly is that a result must be settled before it can be composed over. Treating the shorthand as identity would set R_T to zero and reproduce the dead-reckoning error. R_T was not measured numerically because no per-project ground reading exists here; it is recorded as the enumerated set of dropped constraints.

### Q5 - Competing explanations, and what each would have to give up

| candidate explanation | figure mapping | what it explains | what it cannot explain without an import |
|---|---|---|---|
| More spending | X2, a budget filter (Fig 9 :60) | search budget, reach already in closure | adding an atom; idempotence forbids it (Fig 8 :32) |
| Better people | X1, no anchor independent of ground | capability inside the agent | agent-only improvement that does not change contact changes nothing (Fig 11 :40) |

| Appropriability (Teece) | C3 failure class inside D3 currency | who captures the payment | settlement and composition; those are C1 and C2 |
| Recombinant uncertainty (Fleming) | variance of a genuine import | why novel combinations fail more often and sometimes break through | which combination pays; that is settled at the ground |
| Exaptation (Gould and Vrba, via UC Berkeley) | valence relative to residual (Fig 11 :49-51) | why a past strength becomes a present rigidity, and why current utility is not original function | nothing extra; it is the low-overlap instance of the figure's own rule |

No candidate is promoted to the verdict. Each is a filter or a binding with provenance recorded. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## CONCLUSION

Two equally funded investments can be **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different bonds, different substances. What all commercially major investments share is not a list of ingredients but a **functional group** (settle, import, compose and be paid), with the particular ground, import and composition as qualifiers. The chain is ordered by dependency and carries a comparison at its end, and the usual break is at the middle link, where there is no instrument. The round-trip gap between "research input" and "innovation" is nonzero and is recorded as three dropped constraints, not assumed away. One type strain remains named: ⋛'s operand at the level of a settled molecule serving as an atom needs an instrument, not an argument.
