## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *size* of a research investment but *why the gap between what an investment's library predicts and what the ground returns stays small for some project slots and grows for others*. In effect terms (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`):

- **b** is the funder's belief about what technology or the market will need, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is the investment: which questions, instruments, people, teams, duration, and closure contacts to fund.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the organization's library of knowledge, methods, and routines predicts the investment will yield.
- **o′** is what the ground actually returns: a settled result (nature pays) and/or a payment (the market pays).
- **s** is the project or program. R is indexed per project and by time, never aggregated into "R&D spend" or "average return on R&D" (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "innovation is a stock the investment buys": leads to the prediction that the outcome is a property of the investment considered alone (more spend, more talent, more time).
- Option B, "innovation is a relation that must be maintained and renewed": leads to the prediction that the outcome is a property of investment-and-habitat and that it fails when contact with the ground or the reach to a differing closure fails (Fig 11 :39, Fig 11 :40, Fig 6 :23).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): the conjunction of conditions that must hold for a slot to yield a major, commercially paid innovation, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of another. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The question is a **union** of conditions, not one gradient, so a single lever answer is refused (Fig 9 :54, Fig 9 :55, Fig 9 :57).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of research productivity.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. The Academy of Management directory notice is explicit that its member list may not be compiled for *"unwanted/unsolicited communications, mass mailings or spamming."* Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
