## QUESTION

How much does startup success depend on founder skill versus timing, networks, financing, and luck? Restated in effect terms: why does R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| stay small for one new venture and grow for another, and can that gap be divided into additive, assignable shares? R is indexed per startup and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: the market, named in Fig 2 :29. Option A: report a five-way percentage split. Option B: refuse the split, unbundle the union, and return a shape with refuters. Option A sends an averaged frame-internal filter as a verdict and loses the per-slot signal; Option B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and the instrument that would close it.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: the question asks for the wrong object. Startup success is not a stock divided into five additive shares; it is a per-startup contact relation with at least four separable conditions, channel reachable, reach renewed by contact with a differing closure, room matched to the changing question, and a source discriminator when several sources produce the same reading. Founder skill is Γ, not a free-floating cause; networks and financing are import operators, not percentages; timing is a habitat condition; luck is the re-entering remainder, not a sixth co-equal cause. The market, not frame agreement, is the anchor. Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal split. The entrepreneurship pool corroborates the context dependence and the instrument gap only after re-mapping "founder skill" to Γ; the conflict was returned to the census and did not flip the verdict. Low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not a startup percentage. Which condition binds at a given startup, and the universal split, are declared `gap`, not hidden answers.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and a false sense of measurement. A venture failing C2 would be sold channel instruments, or a venture failing C1 would be told to import more, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs four. A published five-way share would look like an answer while being a frame-internal filter. The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible if the shape and refuters travel with the claim. Fig 2 :42 prices the antidote: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) a prospective per-startup statistic predicting the binding condition with assignable shared fraction, (2) a success verdict that moves with frame opinion while the market does not, (3) unchanged success after a founder-held-constant habitat substitution that removes payment, (4) intersection terms proven zero, (5) reach renewed purely by internal composition across a habitat change, or (6) a source discriminator that assigns all outcomes to named sources with no remainder. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-startup attribution is fully predicted by an averaged five-way decomposition would also reverse it. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
