## What the wider literature bears on this question

Three streams, with overlap declared:

1. **Entrepreneurship and venture studies** (Giones et al.; Gudmundsson and Lechner; Lerner et al.; the Journal of Business Venturing and ETP venue pages). These corroborate context dependence, survival bias and the fact that success-narrative evidence is truncated. They are **one evidence pool**, so they count as one witness (Fig 2 :40). They are filters for the budget and instruments for the discriminator, not the verdict.
2. **Complexity physics and economics** (Pluchino et al.; Frank). Genuinely lower overlap. They corroborate the mechanism shape: a winner reading is a projection, and luck is the part the reading cannot assign. They do not return a universal startup percentage.
3. **Human cognition and decision-making** (Lifchits et al.; the Granovetter network result is adjacent but from sociology). Low overlap. Corroborates that selected success examples move beliefs in opposing directions and that weak ties reach different closures.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The one apparent conflict, Giones et al. reporting context-dependent human capital versus a naive fixed "founder skill" cause, was returned to the census and resolved by re-mapping skill to Γ (section 6 of `01`).

Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
