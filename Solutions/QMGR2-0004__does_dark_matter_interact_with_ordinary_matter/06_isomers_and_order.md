# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the limit.** Simulated derivation checked
against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles two conditions: whether a non-gravitational coupling exists, and whether such
a coupling is within reach of a given instrument. Two questions are owed here: (1) what is the
structure of the channel list, that is, are the enumerated experiments alternative spellings of one
reading or genuinely different terms; and (2) what is the order of the derivation, that is, which
link must be settled before which. A third, analogue question: is the relation between the gravity
channel and a hypothetical second channel an isomer relation under Fig 12, or something else.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the channel list a set of isomers, or a set of different terms?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
  invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
- Compare the three channels as the literature reports them. Xenon recoil searches infer a nuclear
  recoil from prompt scintillation and delayed charge. Collider searches infer missing transverse
  momentum recoiling against a visible object. Haloscopes infer a microwave photon from axion to
  photon conversion in a magnetic field. They do not share an atom set: the operand measured is
  different in each case. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are different
  substances with different substances' anchors.
- The isomer claim becomes real only at a higher level, where all three are written as instances of
  one form: a coupling between a dark sector term and a standard-model term, producing an excess
  above a stated background. Under that shared description they are same atoms (coupling, standard
  model object, background), different bond (recoil, missing momentum, photon conversion),
  different substance. That is the claim worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one channel cannot be written with the same three atoms
  (for example, if axion conversion has no background term that plays the same role), the isomer
  claim fails and the three remain separate substances with no shared form.

### Q2: Is the union a partition or a compound?

- Fig 9 :54-58: a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, and "a case in two
  conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific
  compound."
- Existence and reachability overlap: a non-detection is informative about existence only where
  the instrument had reach. A single lever (more exposure, a stronger coupling limit) clears the
  region where only reachability fails and leaves the existence condition standing.
- So the union is a **compound with its own loop**, not one hard question. It needs its own ground
  per condition: a detection for existence, a published efficiency and background model for reach.
- **Falsifier:** if a single measurement could settle both conditions at once (for example a
  closed-set theorem over all masses and couplings), the union reading is wrong and the problem is
  one question. By Fig 9 :44 there is no sealed room, so this is expected not to fire.

### Q3: What is the order, and which link is likely to break?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the
  one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- The chain here is: (1) perceive the residual (gravitational readings exist); (2) vocabulary for
  what the channels afford (recoil, missing momentum, conversion); (3) say what counts as progress
  (an excess above background at a stated significance); (4) plan the instrument; (5) carry the
  limit to the next problem.
- The middle link, having terms for what the situation affords and being able to say what would
  count as progress, is the fragile one (Fig 3 :24-27). A null result reported without its
  sensitivity floor is a reading taken below the break: it is "a reading of nothing", not a weak
  reading.
- **Falsifier:** if a collaboration publishes a null result with no efficiency curve, no background
  model and no mass range, then step 3 is missing and no verdict about the residual can be read
  from it at all.

### Q4: What is the analogue that makes the inference safe?

- The metamerism case from *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): a spectrometer
  reads green and "cannot comment on the source". One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or
  a scheme not yet discovered can give the same reading. "The full set of ways to produce a reading
  is not known to be closed or determined." It was "proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival
  of a better instrument."
- Applied here: a recoil-like event, a missing-energy event or a cavity photon is a reading. The
  source could be dark matter with a non-gravitational coupling, or a modeled background, or a
  mechanism not yet enumerated. Fig 8 :39 forbids turning the absence of a discriminated source into
  a proof that the source class is empty.
- The neutrino fog supplies the physical instance: solar neutrino coherent scattering is a source
  that produces the same reading as light dark matter scattering. This is corroboration from the
  world, not a figure line. It predicts that more exposure alone will not separate the sources.
- **Falsifier:** a detector that discriminates the two sources in the same channel would refute the
  claim that the fog is an irreducible ambiguity at that instrument and would move the question to
  a new ground.

### Q5: Does anything move the wall?

- Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose
  closure differs."
- Fig 6 :26: "An import adds no atom to the world."
- A positive detection imports a new term into the framework's library. It adds no atom to the
  world; it gives the frame access to a term the world already held. The import needs a source gate
  (provenance) and a shadow test (does it explain a gap), and it is debited against independence.
- The figures themselves do not move the wall here. Only the detector does.

## CONCLUSION

The three experimental channels are not isomers as currently described: they differ in the operand
measured. Under a shared coupling description they become a testable isomer claim with a stated
falsifier. The question is a union compound of existence and reachability, not one hard problem.
The chain is ordered habitat then instrument then depth-versus-absence then verdict, with the
middle link (what counts as progress) the likely break. A null result with no sensitivity floor is
a reading below the break and carries no verdict. The analogue that keeps the inference safe is
metamerism: readings do not determine sources, and the remedy is a discriminating instrument, not
more of the same exposure or a name assigned to the reading.
