**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One claim about dark matter interactions that I would like you to try to break

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does
> not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does dark matter interact with
> ordinary matter through anything besides gravity?" bundles two different questions. One is
> whether a non-gravitational channel exists. The other is whether any channel lies within the
> reach of a given instrument. An upper limit answers the second and not the first. So the honest
> report is: several independent instruments (liquid-xenon recoils, collider missing energy,
> axion haloscopes) have returned no signal to published floors, and that result bounds coupling
> strength over the mass ranges they cover. It does not establish that the only coupling is
> gravitational.
>
> **Why I think that distinction matters:** the figures I work from define the ground as whatever
> settles a question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks: a
> measurement, an experiment, a counter. They also say that some unreachability is depth and some
> is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same. A null result in a detector
> is exactly a case where those two look the same. The neutrino fog in direct detection is a
> concrete version: solar neutrinos produce a signal indistinguishable from light dark matter
> scattering, so more exposure buys less information.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures tell us the answer. They do not. I am not claiming a
> fifth force exists, that any particular particle is the dark matter, that WIMPs are excluded, or
> that axions are excluded. I am not claiming that a null result proves a negative. The figures
> return a shape here, never a name and never a detector outcome.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a provenanced non-gravitational detection in any channel would move "a coupling exists" from
>    candidate to settled and kill the "null is not no" reading as a live verdict;
> 2. a closed-set theorem showing no non-gravitational coupling is possible at any mass would make
>    the negative answer settled (I expect no such theorem, since there is no sealed room, but I
>    have written the refuter down so the expectation is checkable);
> 3. a demonstration that the channels I list are one evidence pool wearing several names would
>    collapse the independence claim.
>
> If any of those holds, I will retract the framing. A one-line "this fails because..." is more
> useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them,
> are the authority.
