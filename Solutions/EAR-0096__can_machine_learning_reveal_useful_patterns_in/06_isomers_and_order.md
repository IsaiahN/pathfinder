### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55-56: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- The two halves have different grounds: capability is settled by a held-out observation paying for a pattern; the guard is settled by the checking arrangement keeping correlation out of the verdict seat.
- ⇒ They are **union conditions**, not two versions of one hard question. A single lever (say, more data) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing: more data can improve the pattern without fixing the guard. This is Fig 9 :56-57, **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- **Falsifier:** if one intervention is shown to improve both halves together, with the same ground and the same checking arrangement, the union claim fails.

### Q2 — What kind of thing is a misleading correlation?

- Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Operators table: `+` is conjunction, *"both hold at once"*, symmetric; `⇒` is production, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, ordered; `→` is sequence, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, ordered. (These are quoted from the local operators table, not from FIGURES_TEXT.md.)
- A correlation between X and Y records a symmetric co-variation, the `+` bond. A causal claim about X and Y asserts `⇒` or `→`. The operands are identical; only the bond differs. Under Fig 12 the two are **different substances**, and the bond is not recoverable from the operands.
- So "misleading correlation" is not a weaker pattern. It is a **correct observation with the wrong bond attached**. That is why adding more observations of the same pair does not fix it: the missing thing is a typed relation, not more of the same atoms. Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** A model that can only report co-variation cannot write the route, intervention, or mechanism.
- **Falsifier:** if a typed production relation were recoverable from the operands (the two measured variables) alone, with no intervention and no outside frame, then the bond would be recoverable and this analysis fails. The Operators table denies it by definition; an empirical demonstration to the contrary would be a genuine conflict and is recorded here as the standing test.

### Q3 — Are the two halves isomers?

- Under Fig 12's definition, the two halves of this question share atoms (the same six-term substrate, the same ground) but differ in the bond applied: one compares a prediction to an observation (`⋛` comparison against the ground), the other refuses a bond (`¬` negation of a causal reading based on co-variation).
- As the question writes them, they are not isomers in the chemical sense: they are not two substances with the same atoms and a different single bond. They are **two loops over a shared habitat**, a union with a compound at the intersection (Fig 9 :57-58: *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*).
- The defensible isomer-like claim is narrower: **a correlation and the corresponding causal claim are isomers over the same two variables**, same atoms, different bond, different behaviour under intervention. That is the claim worth testing, and it carries the Q2 falsifier.
- **Falsifier:** if the two readings have the same behaviour under every intervention, they are not different substances and the isomer claim fails.

### Q4 — What is the order of the chain?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Dependency order for this question:

  2. Name the ground and describe the residual in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28).

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union of two conditions**, not one hard problem, and the chain must be unbundled before search. A misleading correlation is **a correct symmetric bond (`+`) carrying a causal bond's (`⇒`) meaning**, an isomer-like substitution that more data cannot repair. The guard order is dependency order: split, describe, refuse, then accept. Per-slot residual reporting is the round-trip measurement that keeps a global score from hiding a live slot.
