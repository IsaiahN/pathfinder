# Expert verification packet - the Tether on early-warning signals

**What this is:** a claim in a form an earth-system or complex-systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain verdicts, `gap` on a universal numeric reliability rate. Not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can early-warning signals reliably indicate an approaching threshold?" is a union of four questions, not one. The crossing is the ground; the indicator is a channel to it. An admissible reliability claim names a system class, ranges against crossings, reports a false-alarm rate and a miss rate as an ordered pair, and discloses the sampling interval, window, detrending and case-selection rule. Both error directions are channel failures: conditional selection of collapsed systems makes stable systems look warning-positive, and a large shock can cross without preceding slowing. A warning threshold is a filter for attention and sampling budget, not a verdict that a crossing is approaching. Absence of a signal is a report of "not detected at this resolution," not proof of no threshold. No figure line yields a universal numeric reliability rate; that quantity is `gap` and is owed to the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** no single global reliability rate; reliability is measured per system class against crossings, and a warning threshold is a filter, never a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** show that a single aggregate reliability preserves the per-class false-alarm and miss rates to within the decision tolerance, or exhibit a decision context in which the warning signal itself is the questioned outcome and there is no ground to range it against.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against the early-warning literature

- **Statement:** the observation channel produces errors in both directions, and published error rates vary sharply across datasets and indicators. At a fixed 5 percent false-alarm rate, variance-based detection was 25 percent in simulation, 5.0 percent in a chemostat and 5.4 percent in a glaciation record. At a fixed 90 percent detection rate, false alarms were 49 percent, 81 percent and 93 percent. Selecting collapsed systems inflates warning statistics in stable systems.
- **What to check:** are those numbers and the conditional-selection result faithfully reported? Boettiger and Hastings, *J R Soc Interface*, 2012, Tables 1 and 2; Boettiger and Hastings, *Proc R Soc B*, 2012, abstract and Figure 2. Dakos et al., *PNAS*, 2008, "Perspectives" section, on short series and sudden shocks.
- **Refuter:** a replicated, prospectively registered multi-system study showing both error rates at zero with finite data and no conditional selection. That would falsify the channel-decay claim.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 10

- **Statement:** a null early-warning result is "not detected at this resolution" and cannot entail absence of a threshold; improving reliability means improving the channel, along two opposed tracks (INWARD: longer series, replicates, direct perturbation tests; OUTWARD: import a model-based indicator), not minting another generic statistic.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25 - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*; Fig 6 :32 - *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*; Fig 6 :27 - *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*
- **Refuter:** a proof that a null result at a stated resolution entails absence of an approaching threshold, or a single instrument that removes both error directions at once without replication or an imported model.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

## 5. What would settle it

The ground, not more agreement. Three concrete checks:

1. Prospective registration with a declared system class, indicator, window, detrending and accepted false-alarm rate, followed by measurement of both error cells against observed crossings.
2. Replication across genuinely independent systems, with the case-selection rule fixed before the crossings are known.
3. A source-discriminating instrument: direct measurement of the return rate via controlled perturbations, or a likelihood-based model comparison that separates approaching-bifurcation from stable-null systems.
