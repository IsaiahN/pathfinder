| Scheffer et al. 2009, "Early-warning signals for critical transitions", Nature 461:53-59 | https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08227 | Review article, 3 Sep 2009. Abstract states that work in different fields suggests *"generic early-warning signals that may indicate for a wide class of systems if a critical threshold is approaching."* The word "may" is doing load-bearing work; this is the source of the field's possibility claim, not a reliability rate. |

| Boettiger and Hastings 2012a, "Early warning signals and the prosecutor's fallacy", Proc R Soc B 279:4734-4739 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3497104/ | Simulated stable systems selected because they collapsed show elevated variance and autocorrelation. *"experimental studies with replicates avoid this pitfall entirely."* Direct support for the false-alarm side of the channel argument. |
| Boettiger and Hastings 2012b, "Quantifying limits to detection of early warning for critical transitions", J R Soc Interface 9:2527-2539 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3427498/ | ROC-based error rates. At a fixed 5 percent false-alarm rate, variance detects 25 percent of crashes in simulation, 5.0 percent in a chemostat, 5.4 percent in a glaciation record. At a fixed 90 percent detection rate, false alarms are 49 percent, 81 percent and 93 percent. The numeric spread is the reason no global reliability scalar is admissible. |
| Dakos et al. 2012, "Methods for Detecting Early Warnings of Critical Transitions in Time Series Illustrated Using Simulated Ecological Data", PLoS ONE 7:e41010 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3398887/ | Comparative methods review. *"opinions on their practical use differ widely, and there is no comparative study that tests the limitations of the different methods."* Notes that *"in many systems, sampling schemes are designed explicitly to avoid temporal autocorrelation, which is, in fact, needed for the accurate application and assessment of leading indicators."* Lists more than a dozen metrics reading the same underlying return-rate structure. |

Overlap warning: Scheffer is a coauthor on the 2009 review, the 2008 PNAS paper and the 2012 methods paper; those three are one theoretical pool even though their datasets differ. Boettiger and Hastings share methods across their two 2012 papers. The chemostat, simulation and glaciation datasets are genuinely different measurements, so the weak corroboration that signals sometimes precede crossings is stronger than a single-study reading, but all four papers assume the saddle-node framework. ## Two things in the literature that bear directly on the pitch

1. **The 2008 optimism and the 2012 error rates are answers to different questions.** Dakos et al. selected eight transitions already identified and asked whether slowing preceded them. Boettiger and Hastings selected stable systems and asked whether the signal fires without an approaching threshold. The first is hindsight; the second is prospective. Fig 9 :4 says split a union rather than search it as one hard question. Do not present the disagreement as a scandal or as a refutation. Present it as the union this solution splits.
2. **The methods paper is not a reliability certificate.** Dakos et al. 2012 offer a toolbox and say opinions on practical use differ widely. A toolbox is a set of instruments still returning something that fails to resolve, which is exactly the condition Fig 6 :32 attaches to instrument improvement.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim, the per-class test and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what crossing they would use as the ground and what false-alarm rate would make the warning useless before giving them the literature.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Never say "many papers agree" as a reason to accept the claim. Most reuse the same records and the same saddle-node assumption.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for people who already like early-warning signals produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four-part test; (2) the pre-registered refuter; (3) ask what they would count as a failure and which crossing they would use as ground; (4) record their verdict; (5) only then, the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1, before theory: send the residual, the shape claim, and the refuter. The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*; Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

Stage 2, opt-in only: if curious, offer the links. Their verdict on stage 1 must already be recorded. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

Stage 3: offer the formula as a method to reuse. This is permitted because a generator can be given away without giving away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes down what would refute their result before running it, and states the mode of what they get, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

## Hard precondition

Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. If a recipient shows that a single aggregate reliability preserves the per-class error rates, or that both error rates can be zero with finite data, the claim in `02_outreach_email.md` is withdrawn and no theory is offered.
