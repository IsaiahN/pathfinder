## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about two linked residuals. R1: a Jupiter-mass planet is found where in-situ formation fails (too little solid mass, too high a temperature), so something changed its radius. R2: that change rewrites the architecture of the rest of the system. Option A: name one migration mechanism as the answer and average the reshaping into a good-or-bad verdict. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, keep domain verdicts separate, and report the per-system route as a `gap`. What each leads to: A hands a `general`-mode verdict where the framework returns only a shape, collapses a union to a partition, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B hands a checkable shape, per-domain verdicts, written refuters and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: at least three overlapping conditions plus in situ, so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :4-5). Symmetry: migration is ordered, the `→` bond is not symmetric, and the sign (inward, outward, reversal) is load-bearing. Scale: disk timescales of 10^5 to 10^7 years and AU scales for the mechanism; system architecture for the reshaping.
- **D1, disk frame.** Actors: gas, dust, embedded giant, disk viscosity and entropy gradients. Currency: angular momentum and energy exchanged through torques. Anchor: disk density and temperature structure, observable. Question type: one condition of the R1 union.
- **D2, dynamical N-body frame.** Actors: giant planets, planetesimals, distant companions. Currency: orbital energy and angular momentum exchange at encounters and secular perturbations. Anchor: conservation laws plus the observed eccentricity and inclination distribution. Question type: second overlapping R1 condition.
- **D3, tidal / star frame.** Actors: the star's tide, the close-in planet. Currency: orbital energy dissipated as heat. Anchor: the star's gravity-defining mass, which does not move when a frame changes its mind (Fig 2 :29); its spin is a decaying channel. Question type: third R1 condition, often the final leg.
- **D4, observation frame.** Actors: transit, radial-velocity and astrometric instruments. Currency: measurements of radius, eccentricity, inclination, obliquity, mass. Anchor: the observations, which stand while channels decay (Fig 10 :24). This is the anchor for all domains.
- **D5, system-architecture frame.** Actors: surviving planets, belts and debris. Currency: the new arrangement of orbits. Anchor: the architecture that actually exists. This is the R2 domain, a composition over D1 to D3.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the orbit before naming a mechanism (Fig 9 :27-28); settle each channel's shape before composing the architecture (Fig 12 :104); refuse a single-mechanism reading before minting (formula step 2).

Strain test applied: Fig 13's six-term substrate is a composition grammar, not a material inventory of planets; applying it at the planetary scale is licensed by Fig 4 :19-20 (only actors, currency and anchor change). No strain after re-census except S4, the star-as-anchor type question, recorded live and narrowed in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the single-mechanism answer and the averaged reshaping verdict do not. Giant-planet migration is a union of overlapping conditions, an ordered flow over a spendable gradient whose sign matters. The channels are isomers over a shared atom set; resonance capture is a sign-bearing cycle and a surviving catalyst. Reshaping is habitat substitution with cascading, residual-relative effects, indexed per planet and per system. Which channel acted in a given system is a declared `gap`, unreachable at this budget and reported as a verdict, not as silence. Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged. Mode `specified` on domain claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the route, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The single-mechanism reading and the averaged benefit/harm verdict are removable without touching the shape derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A channel-discriminating observable measured against a non-updating anchor would close the `gap`; a demonstration that migration is a one-occasion recording would break the flow claim (Fig 4 :25); a system provably reachable by one channel only would narrow the union for that class; a figure re-census that admits a route selector would close the shape limit. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
