**Subject:** Check one structural claim about giant-planet migration before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know about giant-planet migration. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the per-system route, not `grounded`):** the question "how do giant planets migrate" is a union of overlapping conditions, not one mechanism with one name. The planets that end up close to their stars can get there by disk-driven flow, by planet-planet scattering or secular excitation followed by tidal circularization, or possibly by forming in place. Each channel has a different anchor: a disk structure, an eccentricity distribution, a tide. The sign of the movement is load-bearing, because a giant can move inward, outward, or reverse. What the framework adds is not a new mechanism but a constraint on the shape: migration is an ordered flow over a spendable gradient, alternative routes to the same final orbit are isomers whose difference lives in the record rather than in the final architecture, and a moved planet substitutes a habitat, with cascading effects whose valence depends on which residual you care about.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names which channel acted in a particular system. It does not. The final orbit does not record its own route. The per-system channel is a declared `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget rather than as silence. I am also not claiming a universal migration rate, a ranking of mechanisms by importance, or that an ecology analogue derives the astrophysics. The analogue is an import with a source gate and a shadow test, and I say so.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a figure line that selects a unique channel from the final architecture alone;
> 2. a measurement that reads the route directly in a single system with a non-updating anchor;
> 3. a demonstration that migration is a recording of one occasion rather than a cumulative flow;
> 4. a result that the six-term composition grammar cannot be applied at the planetary-dynamics scale;
> 5. a low-overlap import that changes the planetary verdict rather than only explaining a gap.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
