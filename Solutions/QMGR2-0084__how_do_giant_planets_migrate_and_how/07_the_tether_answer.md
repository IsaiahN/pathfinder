# The Tether's answer: giant-planet migration and the reshaping of systems

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the per-system route. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A Jupiter-mass planet is found where in-situ formation fails: too little solid mass, too high a temperature. The question splits into two sub-residuals, not one. **R1, the mechanism:** what changes the radius (per planet, per system). **R2, the reshaping:** what that change does to the rest of the system. Fig 9 :4-5 says to split rather than search when each rule works on a different subset; Fig 1 :19 says the residual is indexed by object slot, not averaged.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

**D1, disk frame (`specified`, triangulated).** Migration is an ordered flow over a spendable gradient: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." (Fig 13 :76). A giant exchanges angular momentum with a disk whose density and entropy vary with radius. The `→` bond is ordered (Fig 12 :70-72), so inward, outward and reversal are different terms. The Grand Tack's inward-then-outward path (Walsh et al. 2011) makes the sign load-bearing. The disk channel tends to circular, coplanar, often resonant outcomes.

**D2, dynamical frame (`specified`, triangulated).** Planet-planet scattering and secular or Kozai excitation plus tidal friction form a second, overlapping condition. Resonance capture is a cycle whose sign decides whether migration closes, stalls or reverses (Fig 12 :121) and a catalyst that survives its use (Fig 12 :119). This channel can produce eccentric, misaligned or retrograde giants, often with a distant companion. The channels are a union (Fig 9 :55), not a partition.

**D3, tidal / star frame (`specified`, single-source on figure side with world corroboration).** The star's tide dissipates orbital energy as heat and circularizes a high-eccentricity orbit. The star's gravity is the non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :29); its spin is a decaying channel (Fig 10 :24). This leg returns a shape, not a name for the earlier history.

**D4, observation frame (anchor for all three).** Measurements of radius, eccentricity, inclination, obliquity and companions are the ground. "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24). Agreement among models sharing simulation codes is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40), not two witnesses.

**R2, system-architecture frame (`specified`, triangulated).** A moved planet substitutes a habitat: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." (Fig 11 :66). Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52): truncation of the planetesimal disk gives a small Mars, belt excitation and repopulation give a mixed-composition asteroid belt, resonance capture can remove an early generation of super-Earths, and scattered icy bodies can deliver water. Valence is relative to the residual, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." (Fig 11 :51): the same Jupiter is a benefactor to a water-delivery residual and a malfactor to a planet-survival residual.

**The `gap` (`general`, confirmed).** No figure line, and no source opened this session, selects which channel acted in a particular system from the final architecture. Fig 13 :128-129: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." This is `unreachable` at this budget, not a silence (Fig 13 :134), and not proof that no discriminator could exist (Fig 8 :39).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a new migration mechanism. No atom is added; the channels are bindings onto held terms (Fig 6 :21).
- Not a unique channel for any given system. The union is not collapsed (Fig 9 :55).
- Not a universal migration rate, ranking or percentage. Fig 9 :32 returns a kind of thing, never the number.
- Not that all migration is destructive or all migration is beneficial. Valence is per residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that an ecology analogue derives the astrophysics. It is an adopted import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles the shape claim here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): a reading that "cannot comment on the source" needs a spectrometer, not a debate. Concretely:

1. An observable uniquely produced by one channel (for example a channel-specific obliquity plus companion signature) measured against a non-updating anchor.
2. A system provably reachable by only one channel, with that channel measured, which would narrow the union for that class.
3. A demonstration that migration is a cumulative flow rather than a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25).
4. A figure re-census that admits a route selector inside the six-term set, which would close the `gap`.
5. Standing refuters from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: derive a seventh term, apply the substrate out of domain, or show a settled molecule cannot become an atom for the next architecture.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not name a single way that giant planets migrate. They say the question is a union of overlapping conditions, not one mechanism: a giant can flow inward or outward with the disk, be scattered or excited into an eccentric orbit that tides later circularize, or perhaps form in place. What the channels share is a shape, an ordered flow over a spendable gradient, and what distinguishes them is the bond, so two systems with the same final hot Jupiter can be isomers with different histories. The final orbit does not record which route produced it, so the framework leaves the per-system channel open and owes a discriminator. What it does settle in shape is the reshaping: a moved giant substitutes a habitat, triggers second and third order cascades, and its effects are beneficial or harmful only relative to the residual you care about. The small mass of Mars, the mixed asteroid belt and the absence of close super-Earths in our own system are the record of such turns. The result is `specified`, ungrounded, and carries its refuters in advance.
