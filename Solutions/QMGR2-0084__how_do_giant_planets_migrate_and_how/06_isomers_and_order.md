## RESIDUAL

The primary audit establishes that "how do giant planets migrate" is a union of channels and that the final orbit does not record its route. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are the channels **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what **order** governs the reshaping, given that a giant's movement changes the starting point of the next turn?

## DERIVATION

### Q1  - Are the migration channels isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Over a shared atom set `{star, giant, angular momentum, disk or companion, time}` the disk-driven and the scattering-driven hot Jupiter share every ingredient. They differ in the bond: a smooth `→` flow over a radial gradient versus a scattering encounter followed by a tidal `→` chain with a secular cycle in between. The behaviour differs decisively: the first tends to low eccentricity, low obliquity and resonant chains; the second can be eccentric, misaligned or retrograde and often retains a distant companion.
- Wikipedia "Hot Jupiter" (opened 2026-11-23) records both channels and says "approximately 50% of hot Jupiters have distant Jupiter-mass or larger companions, which can leave the hot Jupiter with an orbit inclined relative to the star's rotation." This is world corroboration of the behavioural difference, not the figure verdict.
- **Verdict:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 12 :117), triangulated with the world population fact. The two channels are isomers over the shared atom set. If written with disjoint atoms (disk-only versus companion-only) they are instead two members of a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."), namely `giant → structure → new orbit`.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one channel can be shown to require an atom the other does not, and the shared-atom writing is impossible, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q2  - Is resonance a cycle, a catalyst, or both?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Resonance capture is a genuine cycle: migration brings orbits into commensurability, the resonance changes the torque, and the changed torque alters the migration, which can halt or reverse it. The Grand Tack is the sign-bearing case: the captured Saturn changes the net torque from inward to outward (Walsh et al. 2011, opened 2026-11-23).
- The resonance is also catalyst-like: the resonant relation survives its own use and continues to organize the system after the gas disk is gone (the Laplace resonance among Io, Europa and Ganymede; Wikipedia "Orbital resonance", opened 2026-11-23).
- **Verdict:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side for each structure; the world sources corroborate the mechanism without deciding the classification.
- **Falsifier:** if a resonance is shown to be consumed by the migration that creates it, with no surviving organizing relation, the catalyst reading fails and only the cycle reading stands.

### Q3  - What order governs the reshaping?

- Fig 13 :88-89: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."
- A giant does not move through a fixed backdrop. Its movement truncates a planetesimal disk, excites a belt, scatters embryos, captures resonances and may deliver volatiles. The next event composes against the architecture the last one left. This is why the Mars problem, the asteroid-belt structure and the absent super-Earths are not separate questions but successive turns (Wikipedia "Grand tack hypothesis", opened 2026-11-23).
- Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades." Scoping the habitat to direct contact misses the third-order effect: a moved giant changes the feeding zone of a terrestrial embryo, which changes the planet that forms, which changes the water delivered later.
- Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Moving a planet inward does not remove the outer disk; it substitutes a new set of actors, and "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" (Fig 11 :68).
- **Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 13 :88-89, Fig 11 :52 and :66-69 with independently anchored world cases (Solar System architecture, exoplanet terrestrial-planet simulations).
- **Falsifier:** if a giant's migration can be shown to leave every other orbital parameter statistically unchanged, the cascade and substitution claims fail.

### Q4  - Why the route is a lineage property, not an artefact property

- Fig 13 :128: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 13 :129: "the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."
- Two isomeric histories end in the same term. The difference is in the record: which torque acted, in what order, with which companion. The practical consequence is that a claim about route must carry its provenance, and an import from a population study must be marked as such (Fig 8 :50-52).
- **Verdict:** `general`, triangulated with the source-discrimination essay (OPENED), which states that "behavior is a projection with lossy information" and that only a better instrument resolves the source.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstrated observable that is uniquely produced by one channel and by no other would make the route recoverable from the artefact and would falsify this claim.

### Q5  - Two currencies, and why a single "migration strength" number is wrong

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- The description cost of a channel is the torque law and the initial conditions; the action cost is the observation needed to settle which channel acted in this system (obliquity measurement, companion search, disk imaging). A short description can be expensive to settle, and a long simulation can be cheap to refute with one obliquity measurement. Summing them into one "strength" score loses the distinction and is the named error.
- **Verdict:** `general`, triangulated with Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## CONCLUSION

Over a shared atom set the migration channels are **isomers**: same ingredients, different bond, decisively different orbital behaviour. Resonance capture is both a **cycle** whose sign decides whether migration stops or reverses and a **catalyst** that survives its own use. The reshaping is **ordered**: each turn composes against the architecture the previous turn left, so habitat substitution and cascades produce a lineage, not a clockwork return to a fixed backdrop. The per-system route is a property of that lineage, not of the final artefact, which is why it is reported as a `gap`. Five falsifiers are stated above, all written before the conclusion of this derivation.
