## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

Before the run I bind the sought-for shape so it can accrue a residual like any other term (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction"). An answer here would be: (1) a named set of channels by which a giant's orbital radius changes, (2) a stated reason each channel applies where it does, and (3) an enumerated account of what the movement does to the rest of the system. Each part is indexed per planet and per system, not averaged ("The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :19). What the figures can return is the **shape** of that answer: migration is a directional flow over a gradient, the mechanisms form a union of overlapping conditions, alternative routes to the same final architecture are isomers whose difference lives in the record, and a moved planet substitutes a habitat with cascading, valence-relative effects. The figures cannot name which channel acted in a particular system. That is a declared `gap`, reported as unreachable rather than silence ("Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" Fig 13 :134).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by likelihood of agreeing with the derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. A room does not upgrade the label: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." (Fig 7 :35).
2. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40).
3. Showing the framework before the verdict spends the reader's independence: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42); "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." (Fig 10 :52).

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
