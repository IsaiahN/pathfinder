# Cycle, catalyst, threshold: structure and order in a slow-variable shift

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim in `01` §0 says a slow stock accumulates, a loop sign or spent difference governs the flip, and a network propagates it. Two structural questions are owed: (1) is the slow stock a cycle or a catalyst under Fig 12's definitions, and (2) what bond structure and order does the route from accumulation to system-wide flip have, so that the claim is a route and not just an inventory of ingredients?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the slow stock a cycle, a catalyst, or an isomer of either?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

A stock that accumulates by feeding its own output back is a **cycle**: the output re-enters as input. A stock that is consumed and regenerated per use is a **catalyst**. These two have the same atom set (the stock, the flow, the state) but different bonds: the cycle's output re-enters (a returning `→`), the catalyst's part survives its own use (a bond that preserves the operand). Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in an inventory that lists only "stress" and "resource" and decisive in behaviour, because only one of them runs away.

The practical consequence: "accumulated stress" and "resource depletion" are not interchangeable instances of one slow-variable kind. Stress typically has the cycle bond (more stress produces more conditions for stress); depletion often has the subtraction bond (Fig 12 :82, "A with B removed or absent") with a slower regeneration term. They are isomers, not synonyms. Collapsing them into one "slow variable" commits the flat-formula error Fig 12 names: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not."

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a given stock's output does not re-enter as input, and the stock is not regenerated by its use, then it is neither cycle nor catalyst and the isomer reading fails for that system. If two stocks differ only in label and share both bond and threshold, they are not isomers and the distinction is cosmetic.

### Q2 - What is the order of the route?

- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

The route has an order, and a list of ingredients cannot carry it. Written as bonds:

```
(slow stock) --→ (local threshold crossing) --⇒ (contrasting regime) --→ (cascade through network)
```

- `→` sequence: the crossing needs the accumulation to have happened. "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72).
- `⇒` production: the contrasting regime did not exist before the crossing. "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77).
- The cascade is a further sequence over settled local flips. Each local flip, once settled against the ground, becomes an atom for the next neighbour, which is Fig 12 :104 applied at the network level.

The **goal** of an intervention is a comparison `⋛`: keep the stock below a threshold. The **progress** of recovery is a subtraction `−`: how much of the accumulated stock has been removed. A vocabulary with only "stress" and "resource" can name the state and cannot name either the goal or the progress. This is the middle-link break of Fig 3 :26: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress" has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious test, so it attracts the least attention.

- **Falsifier:** if the local flip and the system-wide flip are demonstrably simultaneous with no inter-slot dependency, the `→` order is wrong and the route collapses to one event. That is refuter 3 in `01` §5.

### Q3 - Is the threshold a well-typed comparison?

- Operators table :48-51: "comparison / a threshold relation between two quantities / no / is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities."

`⋛` compares two quantities. At first order the operands are the slow stock and a critical value, both quantities, so the bond is well typed. At the network level the operands are settled local flips now serving as atoms; whether those count as quantities at that level is the same open type question the exemplar met for quantum gravity. It needs an instrument, not an argument. World corroboration: Lenton et al. 2008 combine the controlling parameters into "a single control ρ" with a critical value ρcrit, and note that several important phase changes have model-dependent reversibility. That is a working, not a proof, of the comparison typing.

- **Falsifier:** show that the transition is governed by a symmetric conjunction of conditions (all must hold, none larger than another) with no threshold relation between two quantities. Then `⋛` is the wrong bond and the shape must be restated.

### Q4 - Hysteresis as two cycles with different basins

The world literature reports hysteresis: the return path differs from the forward path. Under Fig 12 :121, a cycle closes only for one sign of the loop. Hysteresis is therefore two cycle readings, one for each direction, with a different loop sign in the intervening region. This is a composition of the cycle structure, not a new primitive. Li et al. 2019 make the operational version explicit: a "breaking time" and a later "point of no return," with a salvage window between them. The window is a sequence segment `→` inserted into the route, priced in actions rather than bits (Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds.").

- **Falsifier:** a system that returns along exactly the forward path with no distinct basin and no salvage window has no hysteresis; the two-cycle reading is wrong there.

## CONCLUSION

The slow stock is a **cycle** in the runaway case and a **catalyst** in the regenerating case; the two are isomers under Fig 12 :117, same atoms, different bond, decisive difference in behaviour. The route from accumulation to system-wide flip is an ordered chain of sequence, production and cascade, with a comparison as the goal and a subtraction as the progress measure. The threshold comparison is well typed at first order and strained at the network level, where the operands are settled flips serving as atoms. Hysteresis is two cycles with different loop signs plus a salvage window, not a new member of the substrate. What the figures provide is the bond grammar and the order; the names of the specific transitions remain imports with provenance.
