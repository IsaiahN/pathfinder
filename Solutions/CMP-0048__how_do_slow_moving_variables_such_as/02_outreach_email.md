**Subject:** Check one output on slow variables before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know about regime shifts. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the mechanism; not `grounded`):** a slow variable is a stock whose change is small per turn but whose remainder is carried into the next turn. Each pass therefore begins from where the last one ended, so the stock drifts even while every individual step looks negligible. In the framework's words, "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place." The flip comes when the loop that holds the current regime changes sign or spends its stored difference; the framework says "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes." The system-wide extent is a cascade: "And contact cascades... the residual that mattered was three steps out." And before the flip, an observer inside the system cannot tell a robust regime from one about to fail: "from inside the frame the two look the same."
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures derive a saddle-node bifurcation, that critical slowing down is a universal early-warning signal, or that a global resilience score measures the approach. The mathematical name is an import from your literature, not a figure result. The framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Published tests of critical slowing down conflict: some find it before seizures, some find none in 105 seizures. I record that conflict rather than smoothing it; it does not change the shape claim, which is about the structure, not about which statistic works.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this email):**
> 1. a slow stock that changes with no remainder carried into the next turn;
> 2. an abrupt flip with no loop-sign change, no stored difference spent, and no threshold crossed;
> 3. a system-wide flip with no network path, that is, independent simultaneous crossings in every slot;
> 4. a measurement in which the aggregate residual tracks the flip as well as or better than per-slot residuals, consistently before the flip;
> 5. an instrument inside the frame that reliably separates depth from a genuine approach before the shift, without using the shift to calibrate it.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** is each characterization faithful to the systems you know? Does the per-slot claim survive? Which of the five would you try first? Please answer before reading the source material below so your verdict is not shaped by it.
>
