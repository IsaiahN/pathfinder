# Outreach draft  -  check the shape claim before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the exclusion condition C2 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F5 is understood. The whole claim turns on the difference between "entanglement observed" and "classical channels excluded". If that distinction is lost in the email, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the gravity entanglement witness, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the two-mass entanglement witness is a filter, not a verdict. A spin correlation above threshold, with every classical channel between the masses excluded, licenses one shape: the gravitational mediator has at least one nonclassical degree of freedom, so it can carry quantum information. It does **not** license a name: no particular quantum gravity theory, no graviton, no Planck-scale claim. The experiment is mesoscopic, and the inference is conditional on the exclusion argument. Without that exclusion, the same readout can arise under classical Newtonian time evolution, so the witness is a union of two conditions, not one measurement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a classical local channel, electromagnetic, Casimir or otherwise, reproduces the same spin correlations in the same geometry while the gravitational contribution is absent;
> 2. a positive result is reported while a second-order habitat actor remains in contact and unexplained, so the exclusion condition is not met;
> 3. the reported signal is a global average with no per-slot decomposition, so the live slot was never isolated;
> 4. the readout was tuned to the predicted answer, so the anchor moved with the frame.
>
> I wrote these before writing the conclusion. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this tests Planck-scale spacetime, that it delivers a theory of quantum gravity, that it settles the classical-versus-quantum question with a null result, or that a list of people who already agree would corroborate anything. I am also not claiming that the figure framework names the mediator. It returns a shape, not a name, and I have recorded where I earlier overstated that.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Bose et al., *A Spin Entanglement Witness for Quantum Gravity*, PRL 119, 240401 (2017): https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06050
> - Marletto and Vedral, *Gravitationally-induced entanglement between two massive particles is sufficient evidence of quantum effects in gravity*, PRL 119, 240402 (2017): https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06036
> - Huggett, Linnemann and Schneider, *Quantum Gravity in a Laboratory?* (2022), the review that calls the witness claim equivocal: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09013
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, send the three public paper links and the local figure text. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust, belief or endorsement. The first question is always "what would count as a failure here?"
