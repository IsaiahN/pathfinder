## 1. The residual it answers

There is no existing observation that separates "the gravitational interaction can transmit quantum information" from "it cannot" at a scale where both frames predict the same macroscopic world. The observation needed is a two-slot spin correlation with a written exclusion of classical channels. It is a **union compound**, not one hard measurement: "entanglement observed" is `C1 (correlation above threshold)` AND `C2 (classical channels excluded)` (Fig 9 :54). A lever that improves coherence clears C1 and leaves C2 binding.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it, not a debate. Here the corresponding checks are:

1. **Threshold test:** does the two-spin correlation cross the witness threshold with the intended geometry? (C1)
2. **Exclusion test:** with all enumerated classical channels suppressed or independently bounded, does the correlation survive? (C2, the current bottleneck). Fig 11 :52-53 is the standing warning: the residual that matters is often three steps out.
3. **Symmetry test:** is there an exchange asymmetry between the masses? If yes, a non-gravitational actor has entered. If no, the test is inconclusive, not confirmatory.
4. **Discriminator test:** can any instrument distinguish the quantum-mediator history from the classical-time-evolution history of Marchese et al. (2025)? If not, the isomer claim in `06` is withdrawn.
5. **Standing figure falsifiers:** derive one of the six from the other five; show that a witness threshold is a name-bearing verdict rather than a filter (reversing Fig 9 :60); show the spin readout was tuned to the prediction so the anchor moved.
