## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Are the two histories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**
- The quantum-mediator history and the classical-channel history share ingredients (two masses, an interaction, a correlation readout). They differ in the bond: a nonclassical channel can mediate entanglement; a local classical channel cannot, absent the classical-time loophole Marchese et al. identify.
- If the exclusion condition C2 (see `01` F5) is satisfied, the classical history is refused and only the quantum-bond molecule remains. If C2 is not satisfied, the two are **isomers by observation**: same formula-level readout, different substance, and the difference is invisible to the correlation alone.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a controlled experiment shows the two histories yield identical behaviour under every available discriminator, including the exchange-asymmetry test in Q2, and no instrument can be built to separate them, then they are not isomers but one substance under two names. The isomer claim fails and the honest report is Fig 13's *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* plus a recorded lineage.

Status: **single-source for the figure definition** (Fig 12), with the physics-facing check supplied by Marchese et al. and Huggett et al. Not triangulated, because both world sources address the same inference and share its literature.

### Q2  -  Symmetry as a discriminator

- Fig 12 :23: **"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term."** Operators table :15: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*
- The Newtonian gravitational interaction between two equal masses is symmetric under exchange. The entanglement witness (concurrence, spin-spin correlation) is likewise swap-invariant.
- Therefore: an observed **asymmetry** between the two masses in the same geometry is a signature that some non-gravitational actor has entered the habitat. This is not a philosophical preference. It is a test the instrument can run.
- Bose et al. (2017) put the masses in symmetric adjacent interferometers and place them *"far apart enough to keep Casimir-Polder forces at bay"*, which is the practical version of this move.
- Caveat recorded: asymmetry is sufficient to flag a non-gravitational actor, but absence of asymmetry is not sufficient to certify gravity. More than one actor can be symmetric. The test is a filter, not a verdict (Fig 9 :60).

### Q3  -  What order does the inference run in?

The order is fixed by dependency, not by rhetorical convenience:

1. **Describe the residual** (Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*). The residual is a two-slot correlation with an exclusion requirement.
2. **Refuse before mint** (formula step 2: REFUTED *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*). The term held is "any correlation above threshold witnesses quantum gravity". Marchese et al. give a reason to refuse it. The refusal is ¬, a unary operator, not one of the seven bonds (Operators table :70-78).
3. **Settle the first-order observation** before the second-order interpretation. Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** The witness readout settles at the instrument level; the claim about the mediator composes over it.
4. **Name last, if at all** (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). A theory name requires a provenanced import with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 8).

Reversing this order is the actual failure mode in public discussion: the theory name is announced first, then the experiment is described as testing it. Fig 9 :43: *"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."*

**Falsifier:** exhibit a case in which naming the theory before describing the residual improves the discrimination. Under Fig 9 this cannot happen; if it did, the dependency order of the figures would need re-census.

### Q4  -  Structure analogues the question actually needs

- **Functional group** (Fig 12 :123): **"a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** All tabletop gravity tests share `quantum state (state) → mediator structure → measured state (state)`. The qualifier (which mediator, which noise actors) decides the member. The two-mass entanglement witness and GQuEST interferometry are different members of one group, not the same experiment.
- **Catalyst** (Fig 12 :119): **"a part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** The exclusion protocol is catalytic: a shielding or geometry argument, once settled, is reused across runs without being spent. Recording which exclusions are still fresh versus inherited from a different apparatus is part of the provenance obligation.
- **Cycle** (Fig 12 :121): **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Instrument improvement is a cycle: the residual produced by one interferometer is the input to the next (Fig 6 :32-33: an instrument *"is improved from a worse instrument already returning something"*). The 2026 rotor result is a turn of this cycle, not a separate event.

### Q5  -  Two currencies, and why the bottleneck is not cheap to state

Fig 12 :125-127: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."*

The exclusion condition C2 is **cheap in bits** (a paragraph: exclude EM, Casimir, thermal, tidal, back-action) and **expensive in actions** (years of apparatus work, as the 2017 to 2026 arc shows). A paper can assert the exclusion in one sentence and still owe a decade of measurement. Fig 12 :127-128: *"A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."* This is precisely the current situation. Treating the written exclusion as if it settled the physical question is the error the two-currency rule names.

## CONCLUSION

The two candidate histories are **isomers by observation** until C2 is measured: same ingredients and same readout, different bond and lineage. The gravitational bond is exchange-symmetric, which provides a real but one-sided discriminator. The inference must run describe, refuse, settle, name, in that order, and ¬ (refusal) is required because the seven bonds alone can only accumulate claims. Exclusion is cheap to write and expensive to establish, so the bottleneck is a budget fact, not a gap in prose. The isomer claim has a stated falsifier: if no instrument can separate the histories, they collapse into one substance and the claim is withdrawn in favour of a recorded lineage.
