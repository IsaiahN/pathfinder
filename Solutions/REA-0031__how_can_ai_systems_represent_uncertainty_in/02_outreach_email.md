**Subject:** Check one claim about calibration before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a system's residual is indexed per object slot, not averaged across slots. So a global calibration score near zero is a legal state even when one slot is still live. If a confidence is to stay calibrated on a task the system has not met, three things must hold together: the residual must be visible per slot; the thing that settles calibration must be an anchor that does not move when the graded system pushes on it; and what transfers to the new task must be a method, not a recorded score. A vocabulary that cannot refuse a held confidence can only accumulate, so a unary refusal operator is also required. This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement, and it does not name a winning algorithm.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that temperature scaling, conformal prediction, Bayesian inference, or any named method is the figure-derived answer. Those are bindings from particular frames with their own assumptions and grounds. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record of provenance separates them. Until that record is shown they carry zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a live slot with positive residual that stays invisible in the calibration report after the system is explicitly given per-slot measurement;
> 2. an anchor whose reading changes when the graded frame pushes on it, yet calibration is still claimed to be settled by it;
> 3. reach renewed by pure internal composition with no new atom, no import, and no differing closure;
> 4. a recorded confidence from task A that transfers to unfamiliar task B unchanged, with no method carried and no new contact;
> 5. a system that removes the standing of a held confidence without any unary negation, by addition alone.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the background sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture, the thirteen figures and formula: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks, Guo et al., ICML 2017: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html
> - A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification, Angelopoulos and Bates: https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the architecture link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
