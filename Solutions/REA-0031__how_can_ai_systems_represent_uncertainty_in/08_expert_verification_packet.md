# Expert verification packet — the Tether on uncertainty calibration

**What this is:** a claim in a form a calibration researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A confidence that stays calibrated across unfamiliar tasks must satisfy three conditions together: the residual is read per slot and never averaged into the only report; the thing that settles calibration is an anchor the graded frame cannot move, not a metric the model produces about itself; and what crosses to a new task is a method from a differing closure, with a source gate, a shadow test, and a unary refusal operator so a held confidence can be refused. A saturated confidence metric is a channel alarm, not a verdict. An unfamiliar task is a new closure, so more search inside the old closure cannot supply a missing atom. The framework names no winning algorithm; that slot stays open until a ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus empirical; checkable by reading Figure 1 and by inspecting a calibration report

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed per slot; a global average near zero can coexist with one live slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :13 — *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."*
- **What to check:** does a reported expected calibration error conceal a subgroup with positive residual? Is support evaluated per slot before any method is chosen?
- **Refuter:** a live slot with positive residual that remains visible in the report after the system is explicitly given per-slot measurement; or a demonstration that the aggregate preserves per-slot support.

### C2 — the anchor and the channel; checkable by a calibration researcher against their own ground

- **Statement:** calibration is a relation to something the graded frame cannot move; a saturated or pooled metric is a loss of contact, not a result. A confidence is an ordered comparison, not a self-grounding magnitude.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 10 :24 — *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; :26 — *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; :27-28 — *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."* Fig 13 :20 — *"ordering, so more and less can be said."*
- **What to check:** can the frame change the reading by pushing on it? Does the confidence distribution collapse to one value on the unfamiliar task while outcomes still vary? Is a self-produced calibration metric being used to certify the model that produces it?
- **Refuter:** an anchor whose reading changes under push yet still settles calibration; or an instrument that stays discriminating while the reported calibration drifts, with the drift attributable to the ground rather than the channel.

### C3 — transfer across the membrane; checkable by comparing a known task with a genuinely new closure

- **Statement:** only methods cross to a new task; recordings and benchmark scores do not. Import from a differing closure is the only operator that moves the wall, and a refusal operator is required.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 — *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 11 :70 — *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 8 :32 — *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 12 :140 — *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*
- **What to check:** does calibration transfer unchanged when the habitat is substituted, or only when the method is carried and re-grounded? Is there any unary refusal in the representation at all? Was search inside the old closure tried before import?
- **Refuter:** reach renewed by pure internal composition with no new atom and no differing frame; a recorded confidence that transfers unchanged to a new habitat with no method carried; or a system that removes the standing of a held confidence by addition alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no final method, no last calibration algorithm.
- Not a claim that temperature scaling, conformal prediction, Bayesian inference or any named method is figure-derived. These are bindings from other frames with their own assumptions and grounds.
- Not a claim that a benchmark score is the ground. Instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :37).
- Not a claim that averaging is forbidden. A global near-zero average is a legal state; it can hide a live slot.
- Not a claim that the question is one hard problem. It is a union of contact failure and reach failure (Fig 9 :55-57).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that this packet is independent corroboration. It shares its evidence pool with the rest of the solution.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"* — the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does a live positive residual stay invisible after per-slot measurement is supplied?
2. Can the graded frame move the anchor's reading?
3. Does the confidence distribution saturate or pool on the unfamiliar task, and is the drift in the channel rather than the ground?
4. Is the governing term in closure(Γ) and unfound, or absent (more search versus import)?
5. Can the system refuse a held confidence without a unary negation operator?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :29, Fig 4 :22, Fig 10 :24 and :26, Fig 8 :32, Fig 11 :70 and Fig 12 :140. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against their own calibration reports and their own ground.

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
