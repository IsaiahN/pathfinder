### Q2 — Is the bond ordered or symmetric?

- Three symmetric bonds: `+` conjunction, `∥` disjunction, `≡` identity (Operators table :11-28). Four ordered: `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison (:29-52).
- A calibration relation is ordered: prediction ranges against the observation, and swapping them changes the term (Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).
- It is not `≡`: a confidence and an outcome are not two names for one referent. It is not `+`: both holding at once is not the claim. It is `⋛` over quantities at the level where the comparison is made.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that reported confidence and realised frequency are related by a symmetric bond, or that swapping them leaves the calibration claim unchanged. If so, the comparison reading fails.

### Q4 — Are the three conditions a union or a partition?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- C1 (visible residual) can hold while C2 (anchor) fails: a system can see its per-slot error yet grade itself with its own metric. C2 can hold while C3 (transfer) fails: an immovable benchmark anchor says nothing about a new closure. So the conditions overlap and the question is a union. A single lever, for example more calibration data, clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersection standing.

**Falsifier:** a single intervention that restores all three conditions with no categorically different residue, in a case where two conditions demonstrably failed. That would collapse the union reading.

### Q5 — Are human metacognition and machine calibration isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Both frames hold a comparison between a confidence and an outcome, an anchor that settles it, and a refusal option (the animal decline-the-test response; machine abstention). Written over that shared atom set `{comparison, anchor, residual, refusal}`, they are isomers under Fig 12: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance (one settled by experiment and survival, one by benchmark and deployment).
- As the question is normally written, however, they share almost nothing: machine calibration names a scalar and a dataset; human metacognition names a confidence rating and a task. On that narrow writing they are a **functional group**, not isomers: both are `confidence ⋛ outcome`, with different qualifiers.
- The stronger claim, over the full shared atom set, is the one worth testing. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either frame can settle calibration without a refusal operand (a vocabulary that can only accumulate), or without an anchor it cannot move, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

## CONCLUSION

A confidence carries an ordered comparison, not a bare Spectrum atom. The conditions form a union, and the procedural order is SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD. Human metacognition and machine calibration are a functional group as usually written and a real isomer claim over the full shared atom set, with a stated falsifier. The round-trip gap is nonzero and is the honest measure of what the shorthand cannot hold. This rescues what is defensible in the calibration question without naming a method or claiming a ground reading.
