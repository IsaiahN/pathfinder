## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the size of a confidence number but whether a second-order term (a stated uncertainty) tracks the ground per slot when the slot moves into a task the system has not met. In effect terms: the belief b mixes private history and inherited prior by w; the system acts a and predicts Γ(b,a); the observation o′ returns; R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is measured per slot s (Symbols table :18-20, :36-43; THE_FORMULA step 1). "Calibrated" means the stated uncertainty ranges against a reading that the frame cannot write, on an anchor that does not move when the frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29, Fig 2 :45). "Unfamiliar task" means the slot lands in a closure whose atoms Γ does not hold, so no amount of search inside Γ adds what is missing (Fig 8 :32).

- Option A, "uncertainty is one confidence scalar calibrated by averaging residuals over a benchmark": leads to a live slot vanishing into an average (Fig 1 :22), a frame scoring itself with a quantity it produces (Fig 1 :27), and a synthetic solve that proves wiring and never capability (Fig 11 :70).
- Option B, "uncertainty is a per-slot residual held against an immovable anchor, with a refusal operator and reach renewed by import from a differing closure": leads to calibration as a relation rather than a property of a number, and to transfer by methods rather than recordings (Fig 4 :22, Fig 4 :25).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (THE_FORMULA, "Direction"): a shape with three conditions that must hold together, (C1) per-slot residual with a live-mass support test, (C2) an anchor whose reading is not authored by the frame being graded, and (C3) a method that crosses the membrane to a differing closure with a source gate, a shadow test, and negation so a held confidence can be refused. Plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of C1 from a failure of C2 from a failure of C3. Until that shape is bound it is a preference, predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it (Fig 5 :62-66).

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged, with the choice of a specific calibration method declared a `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the AI question, and no claim that the framework names which calibration algorithm wins.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
