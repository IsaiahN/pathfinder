# Expert verification packet — the Tether on when evidence is sufficient

**What this is:** a claim in a form a judgment-and-decision-making researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No measured threshold is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Sufficiency to act is an ordered comparison, not a certainty bar. The residual that would remain if one acted now is compared against the cost of one more observation. The residual is tracked per live case, because a global average hides the live case. Two costs are priced in different units, the cost of describing what remains unexplained and the cost of the actions spent settling it, so they cannot be added into one scalar without hiding which is binding. Waiting pays only while independent search can still find something in the current reach, or while a genuinely different frame can be imported. Once the residual persists and stops trending down, the choice is to act, to refuse the term, or to change frame. The rightness of the act is settled by its paid outcome, not by agreement. The framework gives no number, drift rate, boundary height or cognitive mechanism.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Framework-internal and comparison-facing; checkable by reading the operators and bond tables

- **Statement:** "enough evidence" is a `⋛` comparison of two quantities, not a fixed certainty criterion. The act decision and the mint decision are different operators.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Fig 12 :89 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."; operators table :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"; Fig 5 :42 "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."
- **Refuter:** show that a single scalar rule predicts both when to act and when a new belief is warranted, survives across tasks, and has no separable comparison and bargain; or show that the framework's own `⋛` must be a stored certainty criterion rather than an ordered comparison.

### C2 — Per-slot and two-currency; checkable by a JDM researcher against experimental designs

- **Statement:** sufficiency is decided on a per-case residual, never on a global average, and the description cost and the settling cost are held apart.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."; Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."; Fig 12 :127 "They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."
- **What to check:** does a global confidence instruction hide a live case that a per-case instruction reveals? Can a design separate the cost of specifying the hypothesis from the cost of collecting observations, so that one binds while the other is held fixed?
- **Refuter:** evidence that sufficiency is decided on an aggregated global measure with no lost live signal; or a rule that trades the two costs additively at every level and predicts both decisions correctly.

### C3 — Waiting bound and stop signal; checkable against search and foraging paradigms

- **Statement:** waiting has a payoff only while search at the same reach can still find something or a different frame can be imported. The operational stop signal is a residual that persists and does not trend down.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."; Fig 6 :50 "Fails by spending budget on a region already swept. Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent."; Fig 13 :117-118 "So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."; Fig 5 :58 "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at, which is the same shrinking test the selector already runs, read with the opposite sign."
- **Refuter:** a fixed-sample procedure at matched error rates that uses no more observations than a sequential boundary rule across several decision domains; or stop thresholds invariant to a manipulated opportunity cost of time where the normative optimum clearly shifts.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numeric threshold, drift rate, boundary height, likelihood ratio or aspiration level. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that people actually use this rule. The descriptive mechanism is empirical and belongs to JDM research.
- Not that the framework is Bayesian, or a restatement of satisficing, Wald's SPRT, drift diffusion, or the marginal value theorem. Each is an independent witness to the shape, with its own mechanism.
- Not that the two costs can be converted into one utility number. Fig 12 :127 forbids that at the level of composition.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment in this folder pays for the claim.
- Not a final decision theory. The canonical README's "This is not a Theory of Everything" section applies: this is a generator of a shape.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating experiment, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: one reading "cannot comment on the source", and "the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists". Here:

1. Vary the opportunity cost of time and measure whether the stop threshold moves in the normally predicted direction.
2. Separate description cost from observation cost and see which one binds when the other is held fixed.
3. Contrast a reachable-at-budget search with an unreachable one, and test whether importing a different frame changes the stopping point.
4. Contrast global-confidence and per-case instructions and test whether one hides a live case the other reveals.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :87, :89, :125, :127 and Fig 1 :19, :22. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a design you know: could a global confidence score hide a live case in your paradigm?
3. Name one instrument that would separate description cost from settling cost, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
