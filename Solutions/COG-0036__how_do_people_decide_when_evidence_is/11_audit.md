## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down" and Fig 4 :30 requires that it be measured: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. 1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 12 :87 and :125-127, Fig 5 :42 and :58, Fig 8 :32, Fig 6 :50.
2. **Abstraction (`T_A`):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: sufficiency is a per-slot comparison; two currencies held apart; waiting bounded by reach; flat residual is the stop signal; ground settles.
3. **Concretisation (`T_E`):** a concrete laboratory claim, for example "participants stopped when posterior confidence reached 0.95 and the reward rate was maximised by a single scalar cost function."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **two-currency constraint**. A single scalar cost function that adds description and observation costs is exactly what Fig 12 :127 forbids at the level of composition.
- the **per-slot requirement**. A global posterior confidence of 0.95 can aggregate a live case away, against Fig 1 :22.
- the **act versus mint distinction**. A confidence bar describes a threshold state, not an ordered comparison of two quantities, and it does not separate the act from accepting a new belief.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: sufficiency is a comparison whose stop signal is a residual that stops trending down. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the metalaws would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
