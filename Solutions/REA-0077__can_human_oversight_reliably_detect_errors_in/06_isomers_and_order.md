## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a maker (system), a checker (person), a ground (task correctness), and an arrangement (interface, rubric, time, case selection, other frames). Bonds held: comparison (⋛) between checker and ground, sequence (→) between links, negation (¬) as refusal. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Union or partition?

- The two competence regimes (system better than human / system worse than human) are not disjoint inputs to two separate questions. They share the same checker, the same ground question and the same arrangement. So they **overlap** and the split is a union, not a partition.
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Consequence: a lever such as "give the reviewer more time" clears the region where only attention is missing. It leaves the intersection standing, where the reviewer both lacks time *and* lacks a ground. That intersection is the superhuman-task case in the question. It is "a specific compound" (Fig 9 :57-58), not a harder case of either condition.
- **Falsifier written before the run:** if the two regimes can be handled by independent instruments with no shared checker, no shared ground and no shared arrangement, the union reading fails and the partition reading stands. None of the sources read shows such independence.

### Q2. Dependency order, not importance order

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Applied:

1. **Ground legitimacy** (Fig 2 :36-38). Nothing downstream can be graded until the anchor is named. If human judgment is the anchor for a correctness question, the chain is already in the mutual-update collapse.
2. **Perception and vocabulary** (Fig 3 :6-7, :25-27). The checker needs terms for what the output affords. A link with no instrument is not a working link.
3. **Independence** (Fig 2 :31-32, Fig 8 :48-49). The checker's evidence must differ from the maker's, or agreement is the pool talking.
4. **Per-slot residual** (Fig 1 :19, :22). Aggregation before this point destroys the signal.
5. **Refusal** (Fig 5 :53, Operators table :58). A rating-only record cannot carry the correction.
6. **Authority and channel** (Fig 10 :24, :42). The refusal must cross back, and the channel must be kept reachable.
7. **Reliability claim** last. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

A reading taken at step 7 without steps 1 to 6 is "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :22-23), not a weak reading.

### Q3. Are the two regimes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The question's two regimes do **not** meet the definition. The system's superhuman tasks and subhuman tasks are not two substances built from the same atoms under different bonds; they are two positions of the same maker relative to the same checker. Writing them as "two systems" would be the flat-formula error Fig 12 names: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not" (Fig 12 :14).

The correct analogy is Fig 12 :123's **functional group**: "a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The functional group here is `maker, checker, ground, arrangement`; the qualifier is which competence regime is being examined. Same structure, different slot values, not different substances.

- **Falsifier:** if the superhuman regime requires an atom the subhuman regime does not (for example, a ground that exists in one case and is absent in the other), the functional-group reading fails and the two cases become genuinely different compounds with separate loops. This is checkable: name the ground in each regime and compare.

### Q4. Analogues as the question needs

Low overlap with the oversight literature is deliberate: these analogues are corroboration of shape, not witnesses for the verdict.

| analogue | field | shape it instantiates | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two navigators taking bearings on one fixed landmark | human cognition, tacit knowledge | frames range against a ground separately; agreement is not the verdict | *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "Two navigators don't find their position by agreeing with each other. Each takes a bearing on the same fixed landmark" |
| A court ruling on evidence neither side can move | institutions | an anchor that is not a party to the disagreement | *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move." |
| Independent replication in science | epistemology | separate experience renews independence | *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "independent replication is separate experience plus something real to check against" |
| Debate with a human judge | machine learning | the checker compares frames rather than producing the answer | Irving, Christiano and Amodei, https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00899 |
| Searchers distributed at the same reach | search theory | more searchers help only if independent | Fig 6 :49-50, Fig 7 :12-13 |
| Mice monitoring their own timing errors | animal behaviour | error monitoring is a mechanism with a ground, not a global self-report | Öztel and Balcı, Sci Rep 2024, PMID 39375395 |

Each analogue is the same functional group at a different scale. None supplies a reliability coefficient. That is the point: the mapping returns a kind of thing, never the answer (Fig 9 :32).

### Q5. Order across scales, and the round trip

Fig 4 :30 requires the cross-scale transform to be bracketed: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

1. **Sent up:** the person-level claim, "this reviewer caught the error."
2. **Abstraction:** a method claim, "this arrangement detects errors of class C against ground G with checker independence I."
3. **Concretisation back down:** a new deployment, a new reviewer, a new task distribution.

R_T is nonzero by construction: the method drops the particular occasion, and the new occasion adds cases the abstraction did not hold. The gap is not noise to be eliminated (Fig 4 :31): "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold." A deployment that treats the person-level result as the arrangement-level result is dead reckoning.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a **union with an internal dependency order**, not a partition and not a pair of isomers. The two competence regimes share the functional group `maker, checker, ground, arrangement`; they differ in which slot is stressed. Reliability must be evaluated at step 7 of the chain, after the ground, vocabulary, independence, per-slot and refusal links have each been measured. A single aggregate measurement collapses the union and reads at a step whose inputs have not arrived.
