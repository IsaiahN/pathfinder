# Isomers, order and the J-curve analogue

**Mode: `specified` on the economic analogue, `general` on the figure rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks two things at once: what produces a genuine slowdown, and how to distinguish it from a measurement problem. Two further derivations are owed. (1) Are the "genuine" and "measurement" readings of the same observed rate isomers under Fig 12, or merely two descriptions of one thing? (2) What order do the parts come in, and does the sequence bond in the J-curve story carry the figure's pricing?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in this analogy: an observed rate, a prior trend, sector slots, an instrument, a technique set, an imported general-purpose technology, and complementary intangible investment. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is introduced; the six substrate terms are not reused as economic atoms (see the limit below).

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are the two explanations isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Both readings share the same ingredients: a falling measured rate, a prior trend, a set of sector slots, and a set of possible sources. They differ in the bond between the reading and its source. In the genuine reading the rate is bonded to a change in the ground by a threshold comparison (⋛) with productive capability. In the measurement reading the rate is bonded to a change in the channel by a comparison with the instrument's own history. Same atoms, different bond, different substance: the policy response differs categorically (change the conditions of production versus change the instrument).
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** The aggregate statistic records the ingredients and not the arrangement, so nothing downstream of the headline number can tell the two apart. That is exactly the diagnostic problem the question asks about.
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The weaker claim is that both readings are members of one functional group (a rate compared with a trend), with different qualifiers (ground-shift versus channel-shift).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the aggregate rate alone can discriminate the two explanations, with no additional bond and no additional instrument, the isomer claim fails and the two readings collapse into one description. Equivalently, if both explanations require exactly the same instrument and return the same policy action, they are not different substances.

**Status: single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the economics check still unmeasured.**

### Q2. What order do the parts come in?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces." The correct order is perception (can the instrument resolve a fall?), then vocabulary (what would count as a genuine fall?), then objective (what is the policy trying to change?), then planning (which lever). Running the causal story before the measurement check spends effort at a step whose input has not arrived.
- Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing." A slowdown explanation built on an unresolved reading is such a reading.
- Formula step 2 sorts the residual before minting: REFUTED owed a refusal first, not an explanation. Applied here, "the rate fell" is not automatically a premise for a causal story; the channel possibility has to be refused by an instrument before the genuine explanation is owed.

**Falsifier:** if a policy lever can be evaluated without first resolving whether the fall is a channel event, the dependency order claim fails for that case. (It may still hold in general.)

### Q3. Does the J-curve story use a figure bond?

- Fig 12 :70-72 defines sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened", with the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- The J-curve account (Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson 2021, opened 2026-11-23) is a sequence, not a conjunction: investment in intangible complements comes first; measured productivity is understated in the GPT's early years; the benefits are harvested later and productivity is overstated. Swapping the order destroys the account. Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence." The J-curve paper has a route, which is why it is a stronger candidate than a flat list of hypotheses.
- Fig 12 :107-110 adds the boundary condition: "Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish." Once an intangible adjustment is accepted as a statistic, the cost of establishing it is not re-paid inside every use, but across a boundary that drops the record, "everything is" re-earned. An agency that does not carry the adjustment methodology forward must re-derive it each time.

**Falsifier:** if the J-curve pattern can be written as a symmetric conjunction (investment and measured dip as co-present states with no ordering), the claim that it uses the sequence bond fails. That would also make it a weaker explanatory object.

**Status: triangulated** for the sequence reading (Fig 12 definition plus the paper's abstract) but the economics remains a world source, so the verdict on the historical case is not a figure verdict.

### Q4. What is the limit of this analogue?

- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 governs the six physical substrate terms (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network). An economy does not have those as atoms. The migration here is of **rules**, not of terms: composition inside a closure, import moves the wall, settled molecules become atoms, residual per slot, ground settles.
- Fig 4 :27-29: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point. Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible." Carrying the physical figures up to an economic shape and back down to a policy claim loses the units and the instruments. The round-trip gap R_T is nonzero and is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.
- So the analogue licenses the shape, not a derivation of economic growth from the substrate. Claiming more would be the naming error the README forbids.

**Falsifier for the analogue itself:** if a figure rule can be shown to depend on the specific physical six (for example, if cross-kind composition fails whenever the atoms are not space/time/energy and spectrum/gradient/network), the migration is invalid and all economic uses of these figures in this folder must be withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The genuine and measurement readings are isomers over a shared ingredient set: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the headline rate and decisive in the response. Their correct order is dependency order (instrument before explanation), and the J-curve account is a real sequence bond, not a flat list. The limit is explicit: the rules migrate, the physical atoms do not, and the round trip is lossy. The isomer and sequence claims are checkable structural claims with stated falsifiers; none is a ground reading.
