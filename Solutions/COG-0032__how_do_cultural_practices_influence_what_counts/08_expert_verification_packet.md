# Expert verification packet: cultural practices and convincing explanations

**What this is:** a claim in a form a philosopher of explanation, a cultural psychologist, or a cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No community was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Convincing explanation" runs two questions with different anchors, and the compound must be split before either is studied. One question asks whether the explanation gets the world right; its anchor is whatever settles the question independently of what any frame thinks. The other asks whether it is a good answer for a person in a community; its anchor is human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people. Cultural practices change the price of an explanation (the cost of stating it against the confusion it removes) and the closure (what can be composed as a candidate at all), and they legitimately settle the convention of legibility. They do not change the ground for a verdict about the world: agreement in a shared evidence pool has zero verdict weight. Compellingness is a property of explanation and habitat, not of the explanation alone. Which practice, in which community, moves which standard is an open gap with a stated instrument, not a hidden answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the split itself, checkable by reading Figures 2 and 9

- **Statement:** there are two anchors, not one; agreement is the product for the convention question and disqualifying for the verdict question; the compound is a union.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **What to check:** is the two-anchor reading forced by the figure lines, or is one anchor sufficient for both senses? Is the compound genuinely a union (overlapping conditions with a binding intersection) rather than a partition?
- **Refuter:** a re-census showing one anchor governs both senses, or a community measurement in which legibility and world-verdict covary perfectly under a single standard.

### C2: the mechanism, checkable against Figures 5 and 6 and against the reader's own field

- **Statement:** cultural practices change what is convincing by changing the cost of stating an explanation and the closure that fixes which candidates can be composed. Neither changes the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
- **What to check:** is the MDL bargain a faithful account of why familiarity and vocabulary make an explanation feel economical? In your field, does a shared vocabulary make some explanations one move and others a page of scaffolding? Does cross-field contact ever add a candidate that internal search could not reach?
- **Refuter:** an explanation strictly cheaper to state and leaving strictly less unexplained is judged no more convincing in every community sampled; or a candidate term is added to a frame's reach with no contact with a differing closure.

### C3: the habitat claim and the open gap, checkable by reading Figure 11 and naming an instrument

- **Statement:** compellingness is a property of explanation-and-habitat, not of the explanation alone; there is no standing list of intrinsically convincing styles; naming which practice moves which standard is currently unreachable, not silent.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** does an explanation's persuasive force change when the surrounding residual changes? Can you name an instrument that would separate vocabulary cost from convention from ground contact in a judged explanation? If you can, that is the missing thing and the gap closes.
- **Refuter:** an explanation retains identical compellingness across a documented change of habitat and residual; or a standing list of explanation styles is shown to be invariant across residuals.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that culture is irrelevant to reasoning. Cost and closure are real mechanisms.
- Not that a community's agreement can settle a world-directed question. That is the coherent-drift collapse (Fig 2 :34).
- Not that a convention may decide an outcome. "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" (Fig 10 :47).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.
- Not a ranking of cultures, not a universal explanation style, not a claim that different traditions are isomers over a shared atom set.
- Not that the specific practice moving a specific standard has been identified. It has not.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument that discriminates the three confounded sources of a "convincing" judgment: vocabulary cost, community convention, and contact with the ground. The analogue in *The Hard Problem is About Source* (verified) is the spectrometer: a reading that cannot distinguish its sources "demonstrates its own inadequacy", and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Here that means a cross-community design in which cost is varied while convention and ground contact are held, and vice versa. No such result is claimed here.
