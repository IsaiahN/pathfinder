# The Tether's answer: cultural practices and convincing explanations

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading. No community was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same explanation can be judged convincing in one community and unconvincing in another. That variation does not shrink with effort because two different rules are working on two different subsets of the question. Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones." "Split it rather than search."

## 2. The shape

1. **"Convincing" names two questions with two different anchors.** One asks whether the explanation gets the world right; its anchor is whatever settles the question and does not move when a frame changes its mind (Fig 2 :29). The other asks whether it is a good answer for a person; its anchor is human judgment, because that question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."). This is a **union**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55). An answer that averages the two into "culture matters somewhat" has thrown away the signal (Fig 1 :19).
2. **Cultural practices change the price of an explanation.** An explanation is a term bought against a residual: it is worth having when stating it plus what it leaves unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes (Fig 5 :43). A community's shared vocabulary makes some explanations cheap to unpack and others expensive. That is why an explanation feels convincing in proportion to how little work it costs the reader. It is a mechanism, not a verdict change.
3. **Cultural practices supply the closure.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." What can be composed as a candidate explanation, and what counts as novel, is relative to the atoms a community already holds (Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."). Contact between cultures moves the wall; more search inside one culture does not (Fig 8 :32).
4. **For the convention question, agreement is the product.** Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." A community's own standard of legibility is the legitimate authority for "is this a good answer for a person here". It remains bounded: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47), and a declared meaning is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :18).
5. **For the verdict question, agreement has zero weight.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". If a community's consensus is allowed to decide the world question, the anchor becomes another frame and the group drifts together coherently, which is why nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). Agreement can give an explanation a hearing; it cannot settle it.
6. **Compellingness is not a property of the explanation alone.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." A term's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), so there is no standing list of intrinsically convincing explanations. This dissolves "which explanation style is best" the way Fig 11 dissolves "what is intelligence".
7. **The framework does not name which practice moves which standard.** It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). That specific question needs a source-discriminating instrument that is not held, so it is recorded as a `gap` (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,").

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that culture is irrelevant to reasoning. It changes cost and closure, and those are real mechanisms.
- Not that any community's agreement can settle a world-directed question. That is the coherent-drift collapse.
- Not that there is one universal explanatory style, or that styles form an isomer series over a shared atom set. They generally do not share a Γ (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any stratum settles `left(R,phi)` for a community in this derivation.
- Not a ranking of cultures by rationality. No anchor governs such a ranking that does not itself update on frames.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would reverse it

The instrument needed is one that separates three things currently confounded in any observed judgment of convincingness: vocabulary cost, convention, and ground contact. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (verified) gives the pattern: a reading that cannot discriminate its sources demonstrates its own inadequacy, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the analogue.

The four pre-registered falsifiers are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3. In short: a measured world outcome that moves with community opinion; a strictly cheaper explanation judged no more convincing in every community; a community that denies agreement is the product for its own legibility standards; or a re-census reducing the compound to a partition. Any one reverses the verdict at the named point. The record keeps the refutation and lets the reach fall because something was added, not because anything was struck out.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The missing thing is an instrument, not more argument. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip and its gap `R_T` to be measured rather than assumed zero; `11_audit.md` enumerates the three constraints the shorthand drops. The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it. This derivation cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that culture is irrelevant to reasoning, nor that truth is whatever a community finds convincing. It says the phrase "convincing explanation" runs two questions together: does the explanation get the world right, and is it a good answer for a person here. Those have different anchors. Cultural practices legitimately set the second, by supplying the shared vocabulary that makes an explanation cheap to unpack and the convention that makes it legible; on that question, agreement is the product. On the first question, agreement in a shared evidence pool has zero weight, because a community's consensus can give an explanation a hearing but cannot move the ground. There is no standing list of intrinsically convincing styles: compellingness lives in explanation and habitat together. The framework returns that two-condition shape, four falsifiers written in advance, and the description of an instrument that would close the open question of which practice moves which standard. It does not pretend to have read that instrument.
