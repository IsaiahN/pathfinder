## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The framework returns the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. From *The Library of Babel* (verified): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For COG-0032 the shape is not "culture matters" and not "reasoning is universal". Both are fillers that fit any dataset. The shape is: **two questions with two anchors, one governing legibility and one governing truth; cultural practices change the price and the reach of an explanation but not the ground.** It constrains any answer (an answer must say which sense it is about and name its anchor) without filling in which practice, in which community, does what.

## 2. How the shape is obtained, and why the bundle must be split first

- *Library of Babel* (verified): the searcher uses the vocabulary already held that is *closest* to the target, then recurses on recurring overlapping terms. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- The same essay warns that a failure to find an answer may mean "your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one". That is exactly F1 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`: the work here is splitting before searching, not searching harder.
- Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" "rather than the cause". So the residual is described in effect terms first; only then is a literature or a culture chosen.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (verified): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is the outward-test discipline applied to outreach.

## 3. What licenses a name, and what remains a gap

Only two things license naming a particular mechanism: the ground settling a term, or a provenanced import with a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Here neither is available for the specific question "which practice moves which standard". *The Hard Problem is About Source* (verified) explains why: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Several cultural histories can produce the same observed judgment of convincingness. The reading does not determine the source. "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists" (verified), and it was a spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the metamerism case.

So the correct form is a `gap` with an instrument description, not a name. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 4. The decisive essay for this question: context decides meaning, not truth

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (verified) is the closest thing in the eight essays to a direct treatment of this exact question. Its claims:

- "Context, subtext and pretext decide what a message means to you. None of them decides whether it is true."
- "Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."
- Culture is "a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge... what they share isn't a set of beliefs but the backdrop they read each other against."
- Two frames that only check against each other drift together, "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
- The safeguards match the figure rules: independent replication is separate experience plus something real to check against; courts rule on evidence "which neither side can move".

This essay is framework-author material and shares the figures' evidence pool, so it corroborates at the canonical level only. It does not count as an independent world witness. What it does is make the figure-derived split explicit in human terms: the reader's history changes what an explanation *means* and how cheap it is to unpack, and that is a different question from whether it is true.

## 5. Why the split is not a compromise

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (verified): "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." Applied here, a community's consensus can give an explanation a hearing (a budget to be tried) but cannot settle whether it gets the world right. That is not a middle position between relativism and universalism. It is a refusal to let one quantity do two jobs.

The same essay adds the verification limit: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." This derivation, including this file, is such an output. Its verdict is `candidate` until an outside frame checks it against a ground.

## 7. Consequence for the public answer

The framework returns a shape: the two-anchor union, the cost mechanism, the closure mechanism, and the open gap on which practice does what. It does **not** return a ranking of cultures, a list of intrinsically convincing explanation styles, or a verdict on any particular community's standards. Any answer that supplies those is supplying the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such (Fig 8 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world.").
