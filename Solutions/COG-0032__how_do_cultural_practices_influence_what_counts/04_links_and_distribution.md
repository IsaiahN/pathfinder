## Wider literature that bears on this question

The world sources separate into three evidence pools, not nine. Two SEP entries are one philosophy-of-explanation pool. Nisbett and the cross-cultural psychology entry are one cultural-psychology pool. The MDL entry is an information-theory pool. Agreement between pools is weak corroboration; agreement within a pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").

1. **The pragmatic turn in philosophy of explanation.** van Fraassen's answer-question account makes explanatory success relative to a contrast class and a relevance relation fixed by context. That is a strong independent fit for the convention half of the shape. But Kitcher and Salmon's "astral influence" objection is equally important here: if the relevance relation is unconstrained, anything explains anything. That is the world-facing reason a community's standard cannot be allowed to settle a verdict. The shape navigates between the two by splitting the question.
2. **Explanatory depth and the "eye of the beholder."** Sober's line is the cleanest world-side statement of the two-axis problem: one axis of explanatory assessment is beholder-relative, the other is supposed to be objective. Fig 11 :39 ("capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone") makes the same structural move for explanations. Strevens's "framework" idea, where what is black-boxed is presupposed rather than explained, is a close analogue of a community's Γ: different communities framework different things.
3. **Cultural variation in cognition.** Nisbett's *Geography of Thought* and the cross-cultural psychology literature establish that reasoning and attribution vary with cultural practices and that the emic/etic distinction matters. The pseudoetic failure, exporting a Western emic as if it were a universal etic, is the exact failure Fig 9 :27 warns about: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
4. **Simplicity as a price.** MDL makes the cost term precise: a regularity lets you compress data, and the hypothesis with the shortest total code is preferred as "the best explanation of data D". This is the independent, non-framework source for Fig 5 :43's bargain, and it explains why a community's vocabulary (its code) changes which explanation feels economical.

No source conflicts with the figure verdict. The one apparent conflict, van Fraassen's context-relativity against Fig 2 :40's refusal of agreement as a verdict, was returned to the census and resolved by the union split in F1/F7; it did not flip the verdict.
