## 0. The claim under audit, and the sought-for shape bound as a term

The question is not one question. It is a compound in which the word "convincing" carries two senses that are settled by two different anchors.

- **Sense V (verdict).** "Convincing" means the explanation gets the world right. Its anchor is whatever settles the question and does not move when a frame thinks otherwise: Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Sense C (convention).** "Convincing" means the explanation is a good answer *for a person* in a community. Its anchor is human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people: Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."

**The shape claim, written down before the run and treated as an ordinary term:** cultural practices change the cost of stating an explanation, the vocabulary available to compose one, and the convention of what counts as a legible answer for a person; they do not change the ground that settles a verdict about the world. The two halves form a union with different anchors and must be unbundled before either is searched (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").

If this shape is wrong, the honest alternatives are: (a) one anchor governs both senses (a partition, not a union); or (b) cultural practices do move the ground for world-directed questions. Both are testable and both have refuters in section 3. Writing the shape down now makes it refusable by the same test as everything else, which is the discipline `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" demands of a frame that cannot check the direction of its own question.

## 1. Residual and domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

**Residual in effect terms.** For the same event E and the same candidate explanation phi, the judgment "phi is convincing" varies systematically with the cultural practices of the judging community. The residual is the gap between that variation and the question of what fixes phi's adequacy independently of the judging community. It is not a single number.

- **Arity:** at least three-place (phi, E, judging community C), indexed per community. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Averaging across communities is how the live signal disappears.
- **Symmetry:** "convincing" is a threshold relation, a comparison of phi against a standard, not a symmetric join of two operands. It has an ordered, contrastive structure: convincing *to whom, about what, rather than what alternative*.
- **Scale:** individual judgment, community convention, cross-community comparison. The membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").

One line per candidate domain, with actors / currency / anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Explanation-as-term | atoms in Γ, bonds, candidate term phi | cost of phi plus leftover versus prior residual | settlement against the ground | admitted (Fig 5) |
| D2 Community convention | members of a community, shared vocabulary, standards of legibility | agreement about shared meaning | human judgment, because the question is about people | admitted (Fig 2 :36-37, :41) |
| D3 Verdict about the world | competing frames, instruments | contact, residual R | measurement / outcome, which cannot be moved by a frame | admitted (Fig 2 :29, :33) |
| D4 Cross-community contact | frames with differing closures | imports, head starts, surplus | the ground, never agreement | admitted (Fig 6, Fig 8) |
| X1 "One universal standard of convincingness" | none nameable that governs both senses | none | splits into D2 and D3 anchors | union, absorbed (Fig 9 :55) |
| X2 "Cultural practice determines truth" | the community | agreement | no independent anchor; the anchor would update on frames | refused (Fig 2 :33) |

**Partition or union.** Union. D2 and D3 are two conditions with different anchors, not two harder cases of one condition (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). D1 supplies the mechanism, D4 the cross-community route.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** What an explanation is (D1) must be settled before what a community supplies to it (D4), which must be understood before asking which convention is legible (D2) and which verdict is true (D3). Refuse the false fusion before minting anything (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second.").

## 2. Findings F1 to F8

### F1: the question is a union, not one hard question (decisive for the shape)

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones." "Split it rather than search."

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The dispute about convincing explanations does not shrink with effort because two rules keep working on different subsets. For the person-question (is this a good answer for a person here), the community's own standard works and is the point of the question (Fig 2 :41). For the world-question (does the explanation get the world right), the community's standard is disqualifying (Fig 2 :40). One lever clears only the region where one condition holds; the intersection stays standing. **Verdict: union, unbundle before searching.** Mode `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for this question.

### F2: cultural practices change the cost term of the explanation bargain (mechanism, not verdict)

Fig 5 :26: "does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?" Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." Fig 5 :44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."

An explanation is a term phi bought against a residual R. What makes it worth having is a bargain: cost(phi) + left(R,phi) must be less than cost(R). Cultural practices bear directly on the first term. They supply the vocabulary, the familiar atoms and the bond set that make one explanation cheap to state and another expensive. The same arrangement read through a community's existing library can be a single familiar move or a page of scaffolding. This is why explanations feel convincing in proportion to how little they cost to unpack, and why a compressed explanation that works for an expert fails for a beginner.

This is a **mechanism**, not a verdict change. It alters the price, not the ground. Fig 12 :107 (via the tacit knowledge essay) records that settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish, so the cheapness of an explanation is a property of the reader's library, not of the explanation's truth. **Verdict: cultural practices change what is convincing by changing cost. Triangulated** across Fig 5 and Fig 12 (low overlap: mint bargain versus composition price). Mode `specified`.

### F3: cultural practices supply the closure of a community (reach)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

A community's practices determine which atoms and bonds are in its Γ, and therefore what can even be formulated as a candidate explanation without an import. What is unthinkable in one frame is a one-step composition in another. This is why contact between cultures can expand what counts as an available explanation, and why more search inside one culture does not: Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."

The tacit knowledge essay states the human version directly: "Culture is a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge... what they share isn't a set of beliefs but the backdrop they read each other against." **Verdict: practices set the closure, so they set what can be offered as an explanation. Triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 13 (closure law versus closed-set/open-arrangements law). Mode `specified`.

### F4: the convention half has a nameable anchor and agreement is its product

Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence."

For sense C, the anchor is legitimate because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36). Agreement within a community about what reads as a good explanation is not a defect there. It is the thing being produced. This is the part of "convincing" that cultural practices legitimately settle, and refusing to admit it is its own error (denying that conventions are real). Note the constraint that still binds: a convention becomes invisible when it is declared rather than checked (Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"), and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47). **Verdict: for the convention question, cultural practices are the legitimate authority, bounded by the requirement that the convention remain checkable. Triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 10 (anchor valence versus convention rule). Mode `specified`.

### F5: the verdict half cannot be settled by cultural agreement

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

If a community's agreement is allowed to decide whether phi gets the world right, the anchor has become another frame, the system enters the coherent-drift collapse, and nobody notices because the drift is smooth. Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." The tacit knowledge essay puts the human case precisely: "Context, subtext and pretext decide what a message means to you. None of them decides whether it is true. Only the ground does that." And: "Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."

So the answer to "do cultural practices influence what counts as a convincing explanation" is yes for sense C and no for sense V, and treating the two as one question is the failure. **Verdict: cultural agreement has zero verdict weight. Triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 10 and the tacit knowledge essay, with the caveat that the essay shares the framework author and evidence pool, so it corroborates at the canonical level only. Mode `specified`.

### F6: the compellingness of an explanation is a property of explanation and habitat, not of the explanation alone

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :49: "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

An explanation does not carry its own compellingness as a property. A term that is a benefactor to one community's residual is inert or a malfactor to another's. There is no standing list of intrinsically convincing explanations. This dissolves the tempting question "which explanation style is most convincing everywhere" in the same way Fig 11 dissolves "what is intelligence": the substrate question is the wrong question, the contact question survives. **Verdict: convincingness lives in explanation-and-habitat. Triangulated** across Fig 11 and Fig 5 (valence relative to residual versus cost relative to library). Mode `general` for the structural claim.

### F7: what the framework cannot name, and one honest strain

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

The figures license the two-condition shape, the cost mechanism, and the closure mechanism. They do **not** name which specific practice, in which community, moves which standard. That would require a source-discriminating instrument (a cross-cultural measurement that separates vocabulary cost from convention from ground contact), which is not held. Recording that absence is a verdict, not a silence.

**Strain, named and returned to census.** An apparent conflict: Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment the anchor for "a good answer for a person," while Fig 2 :40 makes agreement disqualifying for a verdict. Re-census resolves it without softening either line: the two sentences govern different question types (convention versus world-verdict), exactly the union F1 found. Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence" is the rule that makes both readings consistent at once. **No strain survives re-census.** The second seat in `11_audit.md` checks this independently.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor test fails.** A community's standard for a world-directed question is shown to move the ground, that is, a measured outcome changes because the judging community changed its mind. This breaks Fig 2 :33 and :40 and makes the verdict half a convention.
2. **Cost test fails.** In a controlled cross-community comparison, a candidate explanation that is strictly cheaper to state and leaves strictly less residual is judged no more convincing in every community sampled. This breaks the F2 mechanism (Fig 5 :43-44).
3. **Convention test fails.** Agreement about shared meaning is shown to be disqualifying for the convention question, that is, a community's own members reject the claim that agreement about what counts as a good answer for a person is the product. This breaks Fig 2 :41.
4. **Union test fails.** A genuine re-census shows one anchor governs both senses, turning the compound into a partition. This breaks F1 (Fig 9 :55, :57).
5. **Frame escape.** A search inside one closed cultural frame is shown to add an atom without contact with a differing closure. This breaks Fig 8 :32.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or repaired at the named point, and the correction is recorded here rather than silently overwritten. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the split, not with an answer (F1). Ask "which of the two senses of convincing do you mean" before any cross-cultural evidence is discussed.
2. State the mode on every claim (section 2.8). The cost and closure claims are `specified`, not measurements.
3. Describe the residual in effect terms before picking a culture or a field (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."). Otherwise any culture chosen will seem to fit.
4. Keep world agreement off the verdict axis (F5). A cross-cultural consensus is a convention fact, never a world fact.
5. Treat conventions as checkable, not declared (Fig 10 :18, :47). Make the standard explicit so it can be violated.
6. Offer the generator, not the recording: the two-condition shape and its refuters, then the figures (Fig 4 :22).
7. Record which practice in which community moves which standard as a `gap`, not a guess (F7). Say what instrument would close it.
