## QUESTION

How do cultural practices influence what counts as a convincing explanation? Restated as an effect: for a fixed event E and candidate explanation phi, why does the judgment "phi is convincing" vary with the judging community, and what can that variation legitimately change? Option A: treat "convincing" as one question and answer that culture either does or does not affect it. Option B: split the question into a world-verdict sense and a person-convention sense, give each its own anchor, and locate the cultural mechanism in the price and reach of an explanation. A sends a category error to the ground and spends the independence of the people asked to check it. B sends a checkable two-condition shape, four refuters, and a named instrument gap. Anchor: Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** At least three-place (phi, E, judging community C), indexed per community, never averaged (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). Symmetry: "convincing" is a threshold comparison of phi against a standard and an alternative, not a symmetric join. Scale: individual judgment, community convention, cross-community contact; the membrane is lossy (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
- **D1 Explanation-as-term.** Actors: atoms in gamma, bonds, candidate term phi. Currency: cost of phi plus leftover versus prior residual. Anchor: settlement against the ground. Admitted.
- **D2 Community convention.** Actors: community members, shared vocabulary, standards of legibility. Currency: agreement about shared meaning. Anchor: human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people. Admitted.
- **D3 Verdict about the world.** Actors: competing frames, instruments. Currency: contact, residual R. Anchor: measurement or outcome, which cannot be moved by a frame. Admitted.
- **D4 Cross-community contact.** Actors: frames with differing closures. Currency: imports, head starts, surplus. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Admitted.
- **X1 One universal standard of convincingness.** No anchor governs both senses that does not update on frames. Not a domain yet; absorbed as a union.
- **X2 Cultural practice determines truth.** No independent anchor; the anchor would update on frames. Refused.

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." D2 and D3 are two conditions, not two harder cases of one (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). D1 supplies the mechanism, D4 the cross-community route. **Order by dependency:** compose the candidate (D4/D1), then judge legibility (D2), then range against the ground (D3); refuse the false fusion before minting (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second.").

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: "convincing explanation" is a union of a world-verdict question and a person-convention question with different anchors, and the compound must be split before either is studied. Cultural practices change the price of an explanation (Fig 5 :43) and the closure that fixes what can be composed (Fig 6 :23), and they legitimately settle the convention of legibility (Fig 2 :41), bounded so that a convention never decides an outcome (Fig 10 :47). They do not change the ground for a verdict (Fig 2 :33, :40). Compellingness is a property of explanation-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39). Which practice moves which standard is a `gap`, not a hidden answer. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **Cost if wrong:** a bounded and locatable misdirection. A real convention disagreement would be sent to an instrument that only measures truth, or a genuine world dispute would be treated as a difference in taste. The worse cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the full framework before the split, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its four refuters.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was struck out (Fig 6 :42-43). The split, the four refuters and the lineage are recorded, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, never agreement. Specifically: (1) a measured world outcome that moves with community opinion; (2) an explanation strictly cheaper to state and leaving strictly less unexplained judged no more convincing in every community sampled; (3) a community denying that agreement about shared meaning is the product for its own legibility standards; (4) a re-census reducing the union to a partition; or (5) an instrument that separates cost from convention from ground contact and closes the named gap. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
