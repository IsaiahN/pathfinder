# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 3 are intact in the version you are sending. If the split is removed, the offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Two standards are hiding inside "convincing explanation". Could you check the split?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The shape (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The phrase "convincing explanation" runs two questions together. One asks whether the explanation gets the world right. The other asks whether it is a good answer for a person in a community. These have different anchors. For the first, the anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change when a frame changes its mind. For the second, the anchor is human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people. Agreement is the product for the second question and is disqualifying for the first. So a single answer such as "culture changes what is convincing" or "reasoning is universal" is a category error. The compound has to be unbundled before either half is studied.
>
> What cultural practices do change is the price of an explanation and the vocabulary available to build one. An explanation is worth having when stating it plus what it leaves unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes. A community's shared history makes some explanations cheap to unpack and others expensive. That is the mechanism. It changes the cost, not the ground.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that a community's agreement can settle a world-directed question. If it could, the anchor would have become another frame and the whole group would drift together without noticing. Not that there is one universal explanatory style that wins everywhere. The compellingness of an explanation is a property of explanation and habitat, not of the explanation alone. And not that I can name which practice in which community moves which standard. I cannot; that needs an instrument I do not have, and I am recording it as an open gap rather than filling it with a guess.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written down before this message:**
> 1. A measured world-directed outcome changes because the judging community changed its mind, so the anchor moved.
> 2. In a controlled comparison, an explanation that is strictly cheaper to state and leaves strictly less unexplained is judged no more convincing in every community sampled.
> 3. A community's own members deny that agreement about what counts as a good answer for a person is the product of that community's shared meaning.
> 4. A re-census shows one anchor governs both questions, so this is a partition and not a union.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
