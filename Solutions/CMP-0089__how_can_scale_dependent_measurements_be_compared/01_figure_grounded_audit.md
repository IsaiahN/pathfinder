## 0. The claim under audit

> A system looks organized at one scale and disordered at another. How can the two measurements be compared?

Restated in effect terms. Two readings of one system disagree: one returns ORDER, the other DISORDER. A decision (a model choice, an intervention, a scientific claim) depends on what to do with the disagreement. The options are:

- **A:** declare one scale correct and the other an artifact (a verdict issued from inside one frame);
- **B:** average the two into one scalar (an order parameter, pooled across scales);
- **C:** refuse to compare (silence);
- **D:** measure the round-trip gap between the two scale descriptions, keep each reading indexed to its own scale, and range each against an anchor that does not update.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction").** A comparison operation that (i) does not aggregate, (ii) does not issue a verdict about which scale is real, (iii) returns a quantity with the same standing as `R_T`, and (iv) names an anchor external to both readings. This binding lets the refuter below fire.

## 1. Figure census

R by arity, symmetry, scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and dependency order (Fig 3).

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Two readings of one system. Arity: two, but nested rather than independent (one scale is a coarse-graining of the other), so the relation is a *transform relation*, not a triangulation relation. Symmetry: broken. Going up and coming down are different operations (Fig 4 :22), and the comparison bond is ordered (Fig 12 :87, *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"*). Scale: R lives *across* scales, on the membrane, not inside either.
- **Domain 1, measurement and composition.** Actors: the six terms (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures), the seven bonds and negation, plus the instrument reading at a scale. Currency: composition in `closure(Γ)` and settlement at the ground. Anchor: the figures as metalaws for legality of composition; the ground for a reading. Type: partition (which compositions are legal).
- **Domain 2, the cross-scale transform.** Actors: one machine at two sizes, kernel, fine description `x`, coarse description `T_A(x)`, returned description `T_E(T_A(x))`. Currency: methods up, head starts down. Anchor: the round-trip gap `R_T`. Type: this is the domain that governs the comparison directly.
- **Domain 3, the ground / verification frame.** Actors: experiment, dataset, instrument, external checker. Currency: residuals and settlement. Anchor: the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29-30). Failure mode: pooled readings and saturated metrics (Fig 10 :25-26).
- **Partition or union.** The two conditions (ORDER at scale A, DISORDER at scale B) co-occur on one system, so this is a **union compound** in Fig 9's sense: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :54-58). It needs its own loop, the round trip. It is not two independent witnesses (see F2).
- **Dependency order (Fig 3).** 1 perceive (can each scale resolve anything?), 2 vocabulary (are there terms for ordering, for the chaos residue, and for the transform?), 3 objective (what counts as a comparison: `R_T`, not a verdict), 4 planning (design the round trip), 5 learn and carry (record the lineage). The chain typically breaks at 2 to 3, the link with the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :24-27).

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The comparison is the round-trip gap, not a verdict (decisive, `general`)

Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*
Fig 4 :28-29: *"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."*
Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

Symbols table :77-78: *"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."* `THE_FORMULA.md` step 6 writes the same quantity: *"unbracketed the transform is dead reckoning: R_T = gap( x , (T_E ∘ T_A)(x) )"*.

So the difference between the organized reading and the disordered reading is not a contradiction to be settled by choosing a scale. It is the expected signature of a lossy transform. The comparison operation is `R_T`: carry the description up, bring it back down, measure what did not survive. Option A (pick a scale) is dead reckoning. Option B (average) is F3 below.

**Refuter for F1.** A named system with a named pair of scales where the transform is information-preserving and invertible, so `R_T = 0` and the two readings match exactly after the round trip. That would break Fig 4 :28-29 for that case. World corroboration: the renormalization group source states *"Since the RG transformations in such systems are lossy (i.e. the number of variables decreases), there need not be an inverse for a given RG transformation."* (physics, low overlap with the framework).

### F2 - The two readings are not two independent witnesses (`general`)

My first reading was that the fine and coarse measurements are two frames, so their agreement or disagreement could be triangulated. **This strain sent me back to the census.**

Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."*

The two scale readings draw on the same system and the same ground. They are one frame at two resolutions, not two instruments pointed at the same thing from different evidence pools. Treating them as two witnesses would reproduce Fig 2's "common cause" collapse: *"they never talk, and still agree"* (Fig 2 :19-21).

Fig 2 :47 is decisive about anchors: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* So the fine-scale reading cannot be used as the anchor for the coarse-scale reading, or vice versa. The anchor is a third thing: a measurement, experiment, counter, or outcome that neither reading can move.

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

**Refuter for F2.** Show that the two scale readings actually draw on disjoint evidence and disjoint instruments, in which case Fig 2 triangulation applies and they would be two witnesses rather than one frame at two sizes. Until then, agreement between them is the pool talking, not corroboration.

### F3 - Do not average: aggregation throws away the only signal (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*

A single order parameter computed across scales is exactly the aggregation Fig 1 forbids. A global measure near zero is compatible with a live residual at one scale. The comparison must keep the readings indexed to their scale, the way `R` is indexed to a slot.

World corroboration, two independent fields: Openshaw (geography) wrote that *"the areal units (zonal objects) used in many geographical studies are arbitrary, modifiable, and subject to the whims and fancies of whoever is doing, or did, the aggregating"*; and Simpson's paradox is defined as a case where *"a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined."* Both are cases where combining readings destroys the group-level signal. Neither source draws on the framework.

**Refuter for F3.** A demonstrated case where pooling across scales preserves the live residual and the group-level comparison, so Fig 1 :22 fails for that measurement.

### F4 - Order and disorder are not two readings of one quantity (`general` on structure, `specified` on the mapping)

Fig 13 :35: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network."*
Fig 13 :38-39: *"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."*
Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"* (spectrum, a structure).
Fig 13 :32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*
Fig 13 :124-125: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*

Order is the structure called ordering (spectrum). Disorder (chaos) is a remainder a composition leaves behind, and the detail a crossing discards reappears as noise. They are different kinds of thing: a structure and a derivative residue. They are not two values of one scalar that a single comparison could rank. Fig 12 :117 then gives the weaker, checkable structural claim: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If the two scale readings concern the same atoms arranged differently, they stand as isomers, and a single scalar comparison cannot capture the difference. That mapping is `specified`, not a figure-derived necessity.

World corroboration: scale invariance at a critical point is described as *"a feature of objects or laws that do not change if scales of length, energy, or other variables, are multiplied by a common factor"*, and the renormalization group source notes the ordered/disordered fixed points of the Ising model. A scale-invariant quantity is a candidate anchor, but finding one is a ground result, not a figure verdict.

**Refuter for F4.** A single invariant quantity that predicts both readings at both scales with no residue and no loss, so the two readings are one quantity after all. Or a demonstration that the two readings concern different atom sets, which kills the isomer claim.

### F5 - Keep the anchor reachable: pooling is channel decay (`general`)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*

A pooled or saturated measurement has not produced a new verdict about the system; it has lost contact with the ground. The comparison must therefore inspect the channel as well as the readings. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

World corroboration from philosophy of statistics: Sprenger and Weinberger (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) write that *"the judgment that one should partition the population in one case but not the other cannot be based on the probabilities alone, but requires the additional information supplied by the causal model."* The readings alone do not decide the comparison; something outside them does.

**Refuter for F5.** A case where a pooled reading changes contact with the ground in a way that improves, rather than loses, the comparison, so Fig 10 :24 fails for that channel.

### F6 - The object that crosses the membrane is the description, not the recorded value (`general`, strain resolved by re-census)

**Strain named.** Fig 4 :22 says *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A raw measurement value is a recording, not a method. My first reading (carry the coarse number up and compare it with the fine number) strains this line.

**Re-census.** What crosses the membrane is the *rule that produced the reading*, the description or generator, not the number it returned. Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* So `R_T` is a gap between descriptions: *"Measures what the coarser description cannot hold"* (Symbols table :78). The proper comparison transports the measurement procedure and asks what the coarser procedure cannot retain. This is why the answer is a shape and a route, not a single number to be looked up.

**Refuter for F6.** A case where a recorded value, with no accompanying method, is sufficient to reconstruct the fine-scale description and the gap, so Fig 4 :23 is wrong about what may cross.

## 2b. Two additions after the canonical usage check

### F8 - "Just use a scale-invariant order parameter" is REFUSED as stated

A scale-invariant quantity would be an attractive single comparison, and the world sources confirm such quantities exist. But the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* (`FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, section "How to apply them"). Naming *the* invariant for a given system is a ground result (a measurement that the quantity survives the round trip with `R_T = 0`), not something the figures decide. Offering it as a figure-derived answer would be the same error the exemplar recorded in QMGR-0037: turning a visible choice into an invisible one.

**Replacement wording that survives:** *"A comparison is licensed if a quantity survives the round trip with a measured gap; which quantity does so is settled by an instrument, not by the framework."*

### F9 - This is a union compound, so it must be unbundled before search

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

So the question "how can they be compared" is not one question. It separates into (i) can each scale resolve a residual at all (perception), (ii) do we have terms for ordering and for the chaos residue (vocabulary), (iii) what counts as a comparison (objective), and (iv) what is the gap (planning). A single lever, for example rescaling the data, clears only the first and leaves the rest standing.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any contact: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map, failure genera, as cited in the exemplar). These were written before the world search.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A named system has an information-preserving, invertible scale transform with measured `R_T = 0` (breaks Fig 4 :28-30).
2. Averaging across scales preserves the live residual that each scale carries (breaks Fig 1 :22).
3. The fine and coarse readings are shown to draw on disjoint instruments and disjoint evidence, so they are genuinely two frames and triangulation applies (breaks F2).
4. A single invariant quantity predicts both readings with no residue, so order and disorder are one quantity (breaks Fig 13 :35 and :38-39).
5. A pooled reading is shown to improve contact with the ground relative to the separate readings (breaks Fig 10 :24).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Replace the question's implied choice of a correct scale with the round-trip gap `R_T` (F1). Report the gap, not a position.
2. Keep both readings indexed to their scale; never publish a single pooled order parameter (F3).
3. Name a third anchor that neither reading can move, and check the channel for pooling and saturation (F2, F5).
4. Say that order and disorder are different kinds of thing, and offer the isomer reading only at `specified` mode with its falsifier (F4).
5. Forward the generator (the measurement procedure and the refuter), not the recording (Fig 4 :22-25).
6. Unbundle the question before searching for a theory (F9). Describe the residual in effect terms first (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 6. Round trip and honest stop

`R_T` here is qualitative, not numeric, because no ground reading was taken. Sent up: the verbatim figure set (Fig 4 :27-30, Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 2 :29-32, Fig 13 :20/:32/:124-125). Abstraction: "compare scales by the round-trip gap, keep readings separate, range against a third anchor." Concretisation: a researcher's actual practice of pooling or picking one scale.

Three constraints survive the trip: no aggregation, no verdict from inside one frame, external anchor. One constraint is dropped in ordinary practice: the measurement of the gap itself, which is why dead reckoning is the default failure. That dropped constraint is the honest report of what this solution gives up. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set `R_T` to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

**Honest stop.** The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish. There is no measurement of `R_T` for a named system in this folder, so nothing here is `grounded`. *"A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* (Fig 8 :39): not finding a scale-invariant quantity in these sources is not proof that none exists.
