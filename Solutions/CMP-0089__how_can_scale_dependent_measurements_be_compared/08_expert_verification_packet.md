# Expert verification packet - comparing scale-dependent measurements

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scaling or measurement researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No gap has been measured for a named system. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Order at one scale and disorder at another are not two readings needing a winner, and not two numbers to average. Coarse graining is lossy and not reversible, so the comparison is the round trip: carry the measurement procedure up, bring it back down, and report the gap between what was sent and what came back. Both readings stay indexed to their scale, because a global average near zero is compatible with one live residual at a single scale. They are one system at two sizes, not two independent witnesses, so the anchor must be a third thing neither reading can move. Order is a structure (ordering); disorder is a residue a composition leaves behind. Where both readings use the same atom set they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, with the difference invisible in any single formula. The framework does not name which scale-invariant quantity, if any, has a zero gap. That is a measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 4 and Figure 1

- **Statement:** a cross-scale comparison is a round-trip gap, not a verdict and not an aggregate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27 - *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Symbols table :75-78 defines `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`.
- **Refuter:** a named system with an information-preserving, invertible scale transform and measured `R_T = 0`, or a case where pooling across scales preserves the live residual each scale carries.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable by a scaling or measurement researcher

- **Statement:** the two readings are one system at two sizes, not two independent instruments, and the anchor must be external to both.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31-32 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :47 - *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.
- **What to check:** do the fine and coarse readings draw on disjoint instruments and disjoint evidence in your system? If they do, Fig 2 triangulation applies and this sub-claim is wrong. If they are two resolutions of one dataset, the sub-claim stands and you must name a third anchor.
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated pair of scale readings with genuinely independent instruments and evidence pools, plus a measurement showing the anchor moves when one reading is pushed.

### C3 - framework-internal with a world-facing test

- **Statement:** order and disorder are different kinds of thing (a structure and a composition residue), and where both readings use the same atoms they are isomers rather than two values of one scalar.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :124-125 - *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** enumerate the atoms each reading actually uses. If they share the set and differ in arrangement, the isomer claim holds. If one reading needs an atom the other does not contain, the weaker functional-group reading holds instead (Fig 12 :124).
- **Refuter:** a single invariant quantity that predicts both readings at both scales with no residue, or a demonstration that the atom sets differ.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that one scale is correct and the other an artifact.
- Not a single order parameter, entropy measure or universal invariant that settles the comparison.
- Not that chaos is really order at a finer resolution, or the reverse.
- Not that the two readings are independent witnesses; their agreement is one evidence pool.
- Not that the framework can name the invariant that survives the round trip. Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that the isomer mapping is `grounded`. It is `specified` and carries the atom-enumeration falsifier.
- Not a derivation of one scale from the other, and no new primitive.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument that measures `R_T` for a named system at a named pair of scales, ranges each reading against an anchor neither can move, and enumerates the atoms in each description. The essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the precedent: a reading that cannot discriminate sources demonstrates its own inadequacy, and the question was closed by a spectrometer, not an argument.

1. Does an invariant quantity survive the round trip for your system, and what is the measured gap?
2. Are the two readings independent instruments or two resolutions of one dataset?
3. Do they share an atom set and differ only in arrangement?
4. Does the finer reading stay a description, or is it being used as the ground for the coarser one?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :27-32 and Fig 1 :19, :22, four short passages.
2. Name your two scales and say whether the transform between them is invertible. If it is, C1 is refuted.
3. Say whether the two readings use disjoint instruments. If they do, C2 is refuted.
4. Enumerate the atoms each reading uses. If the sets differ, the isomer claim narrows to the functional-group claim.
5. Name one instrument that would measure the gap, or say why none is possible.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
