## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Renormalization group, block spins.** Coarse graining is *"a way to define the components of the theory at large distances as aggregates of components at shorter distances."* The source states that *"Since the RG transformations in such systems are lossy (i.e. the number of variables decreases), there need not be an inverse for a given RG transformation."* At the Ising fixed points the system appears ordered at one limit and disordered at another, and at the critical point changing the scale does not change the physics. This is the closest independent witness to Fig 4 :27-30. It corroborates, it does not decide.
2. **Modifiable areal unit problem.** Openshaw's line and the scale and zoning effects show that the result depends on the aggregation unit, and that correlation can increase as the unit grows. This is the geography-side witness to Fig 1 :22. It is a different evidence pool from the framework.
3. **Simpson's paradox and the SEP entry.** The reversal on combining groups, plus the SEP's point that the right partition cannot be read off the data alone, is the statistics-side witness to Fig 1 :22 and Fig 2 :45. Again, corroboration only.
