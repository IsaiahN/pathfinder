**Subject:** What would you count as a failure of this cross-scale comparison?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** suppose one measurement of a system returns ORDER and another, at a different scale, returns DISORDER. The claim is that these are not two readings that need a winner, and not two numbers to average. They are two descriptions related by a transform that loses information going up. The comparison is the round trip: carry the measurement procedure to the coarser scale, bring it back, and measure the gap between what you sent and what came back. That gap is the comparison. It is not an error to eliminate and it is not a verdict about which scale is real.
>
> The framework's own wording is that the transform "loses information, and that is the point", and that you should "take the round trip and measure the gap". A global number near zero is compatible with one live residual at a single scale, so the readings stay indexed to their scale. Both readings must range against a third anchor that neither can move. A finer reading cannot be the anchor for a coarser one; that would put a party on the ground.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one scale is correct and the other is an artifact; that there is a single order parameter that captures both; that chaos is really order at a finer resolution; or that I know which scale-invariant quantity, if any, would survive the round trip. Finding such a quantity is a measurement, not something the framework can name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a named system where the scale transform is information-preserving and invertible, so the round-trip gap is zero and the two readings match exactly;
> 2. a case where averaging across scales preserves the live residual that each scale carries;
> 3. a demonstration that the two readings use disjoint instruments and disjoint evidence, so they are genuinely two witnesses rather than one system at two sizes;
> 4. a single invariant quantity that predicts both readings with no residue, so order and disorder are one quantity after all.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the comparison is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
