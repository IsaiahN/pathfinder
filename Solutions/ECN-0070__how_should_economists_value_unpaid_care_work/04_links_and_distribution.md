## Four things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The methods are isomers, not versions of one number.** The same time-use atoms, joined by a different wage bond, produce 15 to 26 per cent of GDP under replacement cost and 44 to 69 per cent under opportunity cost (Jones et al. 2026, reporting van de Ven et al. 2018). This is Fig 12's *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Cite it when explaining why "the value" is the wrong object.
2. **The instrument is blind to the axis that matters.** Replacement cost reproduces feminized low pay; opportunity cost varies by caregiver socioeconomic status; sex-disaggregated mean wages lower the estimated value precisely because women do more care at lower wages. This is Fig 2's *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
3. **The convention is visible in the 2025 SNA revision.** Satellite accounts are encouraged *"without altering the core production boundary"* (OECD 2025). That is the honest form of a convention: it makes the work visible without pretending a convention was a measurement.
4. **What the survey cannot see is residual.** ONS records only active care and explicitly says passive care is valuable too. The care ethics literature adds that the relational and dependency dimension is not captured by an hour count. Neither is a reason to give up; both are reasons to name the blind axis next to the estimate.
