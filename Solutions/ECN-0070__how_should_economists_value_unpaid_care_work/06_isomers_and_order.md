# Are the valuation methods isomers? Bond, order and functional group

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The field reports several "values" for unpaid care work from the same underlying time-use data: 15 to 26 per cent of GDP by replacement cost, 44 to 69 per cent by opportunity cost across G7 countries (Jones, Ruiz and Undurraga 2026, reporting van de Ven et al. 2018), 9.0 per cent of global GDP at the minimum wage (ILO 2018), about 21 per cent in Bangladesh and 20 to 28 per cent in Georgia (UNECE/UN Women 2024, 2025). Two questions are owed: (1) are these the same quantity measured differently, or are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition; (2) what **order** does care have, and does the order change what is being valued?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the economics frame: time-use hours (a measured state of activity), wage rates (a price), productivity and quality adjustments, capital inputs, and a production boundary. Bonds held: the seven, including `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `⋛` comparison and `−` subtraction. No new primitive is available without import. The valuation methods are bonds arranged over the same atoms, not new atoms.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the methods isomers? CORRECTION TO MY OWN §5 OF `05`

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- The two input methods share the atoms {hours of unpaid work, an hourly wage}. Replacement cost bonds the hour to a wage for a *substitute task* (a domestic worker, a cook, a nurse). Opportunity cost bonds the hour to a wage the caregiver *forgoes*. Same atoms, different bond, different reported substance: 15 to 26 per cent versus 44 to 69 per cent of GDP.
- The world's own descriptions confirm the bond difference. OECD 2021: replacement cost *"comes close to using minimum wages as an approximated value of housework"*; opportunity cost *"approximates foregone market income."* Folbre 2015 calls the opportunity-cost bond *"the odd effect of valuing the household work with a wage based on a very different type of activity."*
- ⇒ The methods are **isomers**, not dialectical variants of one number. Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."* Reporting one of them as *the* value is the flat-formula error Fig 12 names: *"the parts are not the thing."*
- **I overstated this earlier.** In `05` §5 I treated "shape, not a name" as if it forbade naming any existing method. The README governs a **missing** primitive. These methods are already in Γ, so naming them is a binding, not an invention. Retracted as applied to replacement and opportunity cost. Triangulated (README + Fig 12).

### Q2 - What order does care have?

- Fig 12 :112: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** And: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- `→` is **sequence**: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). Care is a flow through a day and a life: feeding, waking, supervising, being on call, then the next need. The IDRC review of care measurement describes a 24-hour day split into *"a sequence of 'primary' activities"*, which is the ordered form in the data itself.
- Order is not cosmetic. ONS 2016 distinguishes *"active care"* from *"passive care"* (being on call). The same clock hour carries different care depending on where it sits in the sequence and whether a need could arrive. An hour count that discards order has recorded a state and lost the route.
- ⇒ The valuation object is a **sequence**, not a stock of hours. The falsifier below tests this directly.

### Q3 - Is there a functional group?

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- All three input variants (generalist, specialist, minimum-wage replacement) share the substructure *"an hour of unpaid activity is multiplied by an hourly price"*. That fixed substructure is the functional group; the wage choice is the qualifier that says which member. Output valuation (the cost of a comparable meal or a comparable care outcome) is **not** in that group: it bonds the hour to a produced good instead of a price.
- ⇒ The methods form one functional group (input valuation) plus one different arrangement (output valuation). A report that lists all four as "valuation methods" has recorded an ingredient list, not the grammar (Fig 12 :115: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar"*).

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Dependency order for the measurement chain (Fig 3 :20): perception (time-use can see the activity) → vocabulary (the classification has a term for it) → objective (what counts as progress is stated as a convention) → planning (which method) → learn and carry. The break is usually at vocabulary/objective (Fig 3 :24), not at the method choice where the effort goes.
- ⇒ The factual verdict must be settled before the convention is applied, and the instrument question is asked last. This is the reverse of the common practice: pick a wage rate, compute a ratio, then discuss what it means.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

If two methods built over the same atoms can be shown to produce the same estimate for the same activities in every context (an identity bond `≡` between them rather than a different bond), the isomer claim fails and the methods are merely two spellings of one term (Fig 12 :92-93). If care value is invariant to the position of an hour in the care sequence, the order claim fails. If output valuation can be written with the same hour-times-price substructure as input valuation, the functional-group boundary fails.

## CONCLUSION

The valuation methods are **isomers**: same time-use atoms, different wage bonds, different substances, with the difference decisive in the behaviour of the published numbers. Care's value is an ordered sequence rather than a stock of hours, and the input methods share a functional group that output valuation does not. These are structural claims at `specified` mode; no measurement here settles which isomer should be published. The correction to `05` §5 is recorded above: naming methods already held in Γ is a binding, not the invention of a missing primitive.
