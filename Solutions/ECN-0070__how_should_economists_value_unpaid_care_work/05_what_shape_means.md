# What "shape" means - canonical reading for ECN-0070

Sources fetched directly on 2026-11-23: the canonical figures README (local copy), `THE_FORMULA.md`, and three of the eight essays (*The Library of Babel*, *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, *The Hard Problem is About Source*). The other five essays are the framework's canonical reading list and are listed with URLs in `10_source_ledger.csv`, but no verbatim quote from them is used here, so nothing is attributed that was not read this session.

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; and Fig 13 :135 notes it *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- **A shape:** keep R indexed by household and activity; separate the convention from the verdict; name each instrument's blind axes; measure the round-trip gap on transfer.
- **A name, withheld:** "use replacement cost", "use opportunity cost", "add 26 per cent to GDP", "women's natural duty to care". Each is a binding from a frame, not a figure-derived answer.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *"closest to"* the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* In this question the effect is "a gap between counted and performed activity that persists under valuation effort", not the cause "patriarchy" or "the SNA boundary". Naming the cause before describing the effect is the inversion Fig 9 warns against.
- *Library of Babel* on steering: the books returned must be *"tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search."* That is the anchor discipline of Fig 2.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"* - the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, a time-use measurement or a recipient outcome that pays for the valuation rule.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A wage rate imported from labour economics into national accounting is exactly such an import; without provenance it carries zero evidentiary weight and its agreement with the desired number is one pool talking.

Fig 2 :29 is the anchor test: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A market wage fails it on the axis that matters here, because it moves with the gender pay gap it is meant to correct.

## 4. Why an average ratio is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* One reading, many possible sources. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

A wage-based valuation reads a price and names a worth. Several sources can give the same price: genuine productivity, occupational segregation, a gender pay gap, a local minimum wage. The report of the ratio does not discriminate among them. That is the metamerism error, and what is owed is a better instrument (time-use plus outcome plus an axis audit), not a single number.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* The accounts cannot validate their own boundary.

## 5. Q2 - ANSWERED: what licenses a particular wage rate?

**Nothing in the framework.** The framework returns a shape. A particular wage rate or valuation method is licensed only by the ground settling a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the rate is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."*

**Does the field commit the error the README forbids?** Yes. It names the source of a reading (market wage = worth of care) without an instrument that discriminates among sources. The correct form stays: the gap stays per slot, the convention stays declared, and the name stays open.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3/§4 (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1)

I initially overstated the "shape, not a name" rule as a ban on naming any method already in the field's vocabulary. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Replacement cost, opportunity cost and time-use surveys are **already in the economics library**. Naming one is a **binding** from the measurement frame onto a held term, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains wrong in the field's practice is calling that binding a derivation or a ground settlement, and reading the source of a price off the price itself. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* Gathering prestigious names to endorse a percentage is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried, and let the ground (time-use measurement, care outcomes) settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the economics claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Why this matters for ECN-0070 specifically

The question is phrased "how should economists value", which sounds like a request for a convention. Fig 2 :39-41 says correlation has valence: agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict. The honest reading is that the question is a union. Part of it (the unit of account, the boundary) is a convention where agreement is the product. Part of it (what households actually produce, who does it, what the instruments cannot see) is a verdict about the world where agreement from a shared wage table is worthless. A shape tells you which part you are answering. A single number lets you forget.
