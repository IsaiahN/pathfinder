# Union, order and analogue: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order rule.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6, §7).

## RESIDUAL

"Generalize" is being used as if it were one relation between a theory and a population. It is not. It is an ordered crossing of a membrane plus a set of overlapping habitat conditions. This file derives three things the main audit needs: (Q1) why the four conditions form a union and not a partition, (Q2) what order the crossing has, (Q3) whether two settings under the same theory are isomers or members of a functional group, and (Q4) what the reaction norm analogue does and does not license. Each has a falsifier.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

The four conditions in `01` §3 overlap. A setting can reproduce the habitat conditions (C2) yet still be read through schooling-bound terms (C3 fails). Two frames can be independent (C4) yet both carry a recording rather than a kernel (C1 fails). A study can satisfy C1 and C3 and still fail C2 because the goal-required caretaker psychology was absent. These are not four populations that can be separated into four boxes; they are four bindings that co-occur in different combinations.

⇒ The question is a **union**, verdicts are per condition and per setting, and there is no single generalization coefficient. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

- **Falsifier:** if a single intervention were shown to clear all four conditions at once across every setting, with no intersection left standing, the union reading fails and a single-lever partition would be the right census. Written before the run.

### Q2 - What order does the crossing have?

- Fig 12 :71-72: "→ sequence: A then B; B needs A to have happened" and the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- Fig 4 :22-24: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
- Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

The order is not arbitrary and not symmetric:

```
describe R in effect terms
  → decide kernel versus recording (what may cross)
    → enumerate the target habitat and reproduce the goal-required conditions
      → cast terms as effects
        → check frame independence
          → read the per-setting residual against the ground
```

Swap the second and third steps and the analysis asks whether a theory generalizes before deciding whether anything re-applicable exists to generalize. That is the Fig 3 middle break: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

- **Falsifier:** if a target-setting measurement is shown to settle the kernel/recording question directly, without first enumerating the habitat, the dependency order is wrong and should be reversed. Written before the run.

### Q3 - Are two settings under one theory isomers, or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

Two settings in which the same theory pays are **not isomers** in the strict sense if their habitat atoms differ: the atom set is not the same, so Fig 12 :117 does not apply. They are members of a **functional group**, defined by the shared arrangement (kernel transferred into a reproduced habitat, effect terms, independent ground). The group says what kind (a generalization that pays), the qualifier says which member (this setting).

The stronger isomer claim is available under a fixed atom condition: if both settings are described over the same atom set (settings, customs, caretaker psychology, local work, play, storytelling), differing only in the bond each puts between the kernel and the child's competence, then they are isomers, "same atoms, different bond, different substance." The difference would be invisible in a pooled theory score and decisive in the setting's behavior. That is the checkable form worth sending to a cross-cultural researcher.

- **Falsifier:** if either setting requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Written before the run. This is the same fixed-atom condition recorded as live strain S1 in `01` §7.

### Q4 - What the reaction-norm analogue licenses, and what it does not

The Reaction norm entry (opened 2026-11-22): "In ecology and genetics, a reaction norm, also called a norm of reaction, describes the pattern of phenotypic expression of a single genotype across a range of environments." This is a genuinely low-overlap corroborator. It supports the mechanism shape: the same starting structure yields different outcomes across environments, so an outcome cannot be attributed to the starting structure alone. Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

What it does **not** license: it is not a figure line, so it cannot decide the verdict. It also does not license a biological determinism reading of children, nor a claim that a developmental "genotype" exists that can be held constant across cultures. The analogue governs the *shape* of the dependence (outcome is a function of habitat), not the identity of the terms. Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The record here says the reaction norm is an import from biology, used for corroboration only.

An engineering analogue named by the framework itself: Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A standardized school assessment is a substituted habitat. Passing it proves the wiring the assessment was built to test, not the child's capability in the habitat the theory claims to describe.

- **Falsifier:** if a target-setting outcome were shown to be invariant across all habitats that differ in the enumerated conditions, the reaction-norm analogue would be refuted as applied here. Written before the run.

## CONCLUSION

The generalization question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Its crossing is ordered: describe the effect, decide what may cross, reproduce the habitat, cast terms as effects, check independence, then read the ground per setting. Two settings where the same theory pays are members of a functional group; they are isomers only under a fixed-atom condition, which is named as a live strain rather than assumed. The reaction norm and the test-harness analogue corroborate the habitat dependence but do not decide the verdict and are recorded as imports. Four falsifiers were written before the conclusions (Q1 to Q4).
