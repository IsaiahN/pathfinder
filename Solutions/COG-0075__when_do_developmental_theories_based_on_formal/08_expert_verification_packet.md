# Expert verification packet: the Tether on schooling-based developmental theories

**What this is:** a claim in a form a developmental scientist or cross-cultural methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No target-setting measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Does a developmental theory based on formal schooling generalize to children raised in other settings?" is a union of overlapping conditions, not one question with a yes or no answer. Generalization holds where (1) what crosses is a re-applicable method and its binding arrangement, not a recording of a classroom occasion; (2) the target habitat reproduces the settings, customs and caretaker psychology the theory's goal required; (3) the terms describe the effect the child produces, not the schooling instrument that read it; and (4) the observation in that setting is an independent ground, read per setting and never averaged into a universality rate. The framework does not name which theory or which culture wins. It offers the shape, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close each condition.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and method-facing; checkable by reading Figure 4 and by inspecting any named theory

- **Statement:** a theory crosses a habitat boundary as a method or as a recording; only the method can generalize.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :23 - "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." Fig 4 :25 - "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- **What to check:** take one named schooling-based theory and ask what, precisely, would be carried into a non-schooling setting. If the answer is a norm or an age table, it is a recording. If it is a rule about how participation in routine activity produces competence, it is a candidate kernel.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a cross-habitat prediction that is a re-applicable method with its arrangement intact, with none of the source habitat's goal-required conditions reproduced. If that exists, C1 is false.

### C2 - habitat-facing; checkable by a cross-cultural developmental researcher

- **Statement:** the target habitat is a substitution, not a removal; the goal-required conditions must be reproduced and the inherited actors were not chosen.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :67 - "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68 - "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails." Fig 4 :34-35 - "The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
- **What to check:** enumerate settings, child-rearing customs and caretaker psychology in the target setting, then list what the named theory's predicted competence required in school. Which requirements are present, which absent, and which were never recorded? A habitat census is the instrument, not a new argument.
- **Refuter:** a target-setting measurement showing the theory's prediction pays with the goal-required conditions demonstrably absent. If that holds, C2 is false.

### C3 - methods-facing; checkable by a cross-cultural methodologist

- **Statement:** terms must be cast as effects; measurement equivalence is a filter for the budget and not a witness for the verdict; the residual is per setting.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :30-31 - "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Fig 6 :30 - "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :60 - "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** restate the predicted competence as what these children do under these routine circumstances. Ask whether an invariance test is being used to decide the verdict rather than to bound the comparison. Ask whether a pooled result hides a live slot. Note the live field disagreement: the Measurement invariance entry records that Welzel and colleagues criticize excessive reliance on invariance criteria, while proponents reply that nomological validation hinges on comparability. That dispute is an instrument question, not the verdict.
- **Refuter:** a schooling-bound term shown to pay against a different setting's ground with no effect-term description, or a global average shown to track every per-setting residual with no live slot hidden. If either holds, C3 is false.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal developmental theory, a culture-free milestone table, or a claim that children in one setting are behind those in another.
- Not that measurement equivalence settles the question. It is a filter (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a group average tracks the per-residual signal (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- Not that the answer is symmetric: the source-to-target crossing differs from the reverse (Operators table :33).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at a target setting is reported here.
- Not that the framework names which theory or culture wins (Fig 9 :32).
- Simulated derivation, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument in a target setting, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; "it was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here:

1. A habitat census naming which goal-required conditions are reproduced and which are absent.
2. An effect-term restatement of the prediction, checked in that setting by someone who did not use the schooling evidence pool.
3. A per-setting residual reading with the live slots named.
4. A refusal recorded for each slot where the prediction does not pay, rather than a new moderator added.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :22-25 and Fig 11 :67-69 and Fig 9 :30-31, :60 (six short passages).
2. Name one schooling-based developmental theory and say whether what it carries across settings is a method or a recording.
3. Name one target setting and list which of that theory's goal-required conditions are reproduced there.
4. Say what you would count as a failure of C1, C2 or C3, or name one instrument that would close a condition.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record the verdict before reading any framework material.
