## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, when a developmental theory built from children in formal schooling generalizes to children raised in other settings. Option A: treat generalization as one relation and report a single universality verdict. Option B: treat it as a union of overlapping conditions and return the shape each answer must have. What each leads to: A averages the per-setting signal away (Fig 1 :22), promotes an instrument to a witness (Fig 9 :60) and names a theory or culture the framework cannot name (Fig 9 :32); B keeps verdicts per domain, states refuters in advance and stops at the instrument limit with a recorded lineage.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A schooled-derived prediction Γ(b,a) misses the observation o' for a child in another setting. Arity: two habitats at minimum, source and target, each target a separate slot. Symmetry: ordered, not symmetric; Operators :33 ("swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."). Scale: abstraction from one habitat (T_A) and concretization into another (T_E), with nonzero round-trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :30). Fig 9 :55 names it a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition.
- **Domain 1, the schooling-based theory frame.** Actors: schooled children, assessments, curriculum, researchers. Currency: milestone ages and norm-referenced scores. Anchor: measurement inside that habitat for claims about that habitat; for the generalization question it is a frame, not the ground, because Fig 2 :30 says "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Question type: what crosses the membrane, method or recording.
- **Domain 2, the target habitat.** Actors: child, caregivers, siblings, local work, play, storytelling, subsistence. Currency: the actual trajectory and locally valued competence. Anchor: observation in that setting, nameable and non-updating. Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Question type: substitution of habitat, with two silent failure modes (Fig 11 :68-69).
- **Domain 3, the cross-cultural methods frame.** Actors: instruments, equivalence tests, coding schemes. Currency: invariance claims and residuals. Anchor: the ground in each setting, never agreement between two instruments reading one pool. Question type: INWARD instrument question (Formula step 7), a filter for the budget.
- **Domain 4, the verification and outreach frame.** Actors: expert readers in developmental science and cross-cultural methods. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on the same files, so independence is zero (Fig 2 :40).
- **Partition or union.** Union. C1 through C4 overlap; one lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56). Verdicts per domain, never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R in effect terms before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); decide what crosses (Fig 4 :22) before enumerating the habitat (Fig 11 :67); cast terms as effects (Fig 9 :30-31); check independence (Fig 2 :31); then read the per-setting residual (Fig 1 :19).

Strain test applied: Fig 4 governs the scale membrane (method versus recording); Fig 6 governs the closure wall (import versus mint). Re-census assigns each to its own object, so no contradiction. The fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim in `06` Q3 remains a live strain and is named, not smoothed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union of four conditions, not one relation with a yes or no answer. A schooling-based developmental theory generalizes where (1) a re-applicable kernel crosses, not a recording; (2) the target habitat reproduces what the theory's goal required; (3) terms travel as effect descriptions; and (4) the target observation is an independent ground read per setting. Measurement equivalence is a filter, a failed prediction owes a refusal for that slot, and the source-discriminating instrument is the ground. Which theory, which culture and which age is a declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not silence. The whole is `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the shape, ungrounded, candidate. This is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** a wrongly generalized recording is imposed on children whose habitat never supplied the goal-required conditions, and the failure is invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :68). The second cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a refuter (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). Both are avoidable by leading with the separator and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A failed condition is withdrawable without discarding the separator; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state the withdrawals. No primitive has to be abandoned, and a refusal adds a term rather than deleting one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A habitat census plus an effect-term measurement in the target setting showing the prediction pays with the goal-required conditions absent would break C2; a demonstration that a global average tracks every per-setting residual would break C3; two frames sharing one evidence pool shown to corroborate independently would break C4; a measurement in which a schooled term pays without an effect description would break the effect-term condition. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
