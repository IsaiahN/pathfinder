## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks "when do they generalize." Stated as an effect rather than a theory:

> A theory T is abstracted from the observed development of children in formal schooling. T is then used as a prediction Γ(b,a) for a child in a different setting. Observation o' in that setting misses. The question is: under what conditions does the transform from the schooling habitat to the other habitat preserve what the theory's goal required?

Per Symbols table :38-40, R "is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read." The unperceived part here is the set of habitat conditions that schooling silently supplied and that the theory did not record.

**Arity.** Two habitats at minimum: source (schooling) and target (other setting). This is not one population sampled twice. It is a transfer across a boundary, and each target is a separate slot.

**Symmetry.** Ordered, not symmetric. Fig 12 :72 writes sequence as "A then B; B needs A to have happened" and the Operators table :31-33 gives the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." A theory from schooling applied to a herding community is not the same move as a theory from a herding community applied to schooling: the carrier, the currency and the unchosen actors differ. The symmetry question is decided by Fig 4 :19-20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

**Scale.** The theory is an abstraction from many children in one habitat (T_A) and a concretization into another (T_E). Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round-trip gap R_T is the honest measurement of what cross-cultural generalization gives up.

**Sought-for shape, bound before the run (Formula, "Direction").** Generalization is contact-preserving transfer of a re-applicable kernel into a reproduced habitat, checked per setting against the ground. This is an ordinary term now, so it can accrue a residual and be refused. It is not a list of cultures and not a universal milestone table.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

- **D1, the schooling-based theory frame.** Actors: schooled children, age-graded assessments, curriculum, researchers. Currency: milestone ages, norm-referenced scores, stage assignments. Anchor: the measurement inside the schooling habitat, which settles claims *about that habitat*. For the generalization question this is a frame, not the ground, because Fig 2 :30 says "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." A school norm moves when the school population changes, so it cannot anchor a claim about children outside school.
- **D2, the target habitat.** Actors: the child, caregivers, siblings, local work, play, storytelling, subsistence demands. Currency: the child's actual trajectory and locally valued competence. Anchor: observation in that setting, which does not change because a theory predicts otherwise. Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." This is a domain: the anchor is nameable.
- **D3, the cross-cultural methods frame.** Actors: instruments, equivalence tests, coding schemes, cross-cultural researchers. Currency: invariance claims and residuals. Anchor: the ground in each setting, never agreement between two instruments that read the same pool. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Measurement equivalence is a real instrument question, so this is a domain, but its verdict is not the verdict of D2.
- **D4, the verification and outreach frame.** Actors: expert readers in developmental science and cross-cultural methods. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run every seat reads the same files, so its independence is zero (Fig 2 :31-32; Fig 2 :40). Recorded as a domain because its anchor is nameable, flagged as non-independent.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Four conditions overlap: (a) what crosses the membrane, kernel or recording; (b) whether the habitat the theory's goal required is reproduced; (c) whether the terms travel as effect descriptions or stay schooling-bound; (d) whether the target observation is an independent frame. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." One lever clears a region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, so verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." The order here is:

1. Describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
2. Decide what crosses the membrane, method or recording (Fig 4 :22-25), because that decides whether there is anything to transfer.
3. Enumerate the target habitat and check what the theory's goal required is reproduced (Fig 11 :66-69), because a theory with no reproduced goal has no ground to pay against.
4. Check the terms travel as effect descriptions (Fig 9 :30-31; Fig 6 :30).
5. Check independence between frames (Fig 2 :31; Fig 8 :50-52).
6. Only then ask whether a prediction pays, per setting (Fig 1 :19).

The middle link, step 3, is the one with no instrument in most cross-cultural work. Fig 3 :25-27: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." This is where the audit stops when no target measurement exists.

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

### F1 - What crosses the membrane decides the whole answer (decisive)

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Fig 4 :22-24:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
> **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**

Fig 4 :25-26:

> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

A developmental theory built from formal schooling can carry one of two things. If it carries the re-applicable kernel (the invariant rule by which a child's participation in routine activity produces the observed competence), then the actors, currency and anchor are re-bound in the target setting and the theory can generalize. If it carries a recording of one occasion (the age at which schooled children passed a particular test), then replaying that recording in another setting produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one.

This is the first necessary condition. It is `specified`, not `grounded`: no measurement here tells us which actual theory carries a kernel.

**Verdict status: triangulated.** Two low-overlap figure routes: the membrane rule (Fig 4 :19-26) and the recursion rule (Fig 12 :103-104, "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." and "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."). World corroboration: Rogoff 2003 and Super and Harkness 1986 both describe the schooled "universal child" as an abstraction that drops the setting. The figure verdict does not depend on them.

### F2 - Isolation is substitution of habitat, not removal of it

Fig 11 :66-69:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

Taking a schooling-based theory to children raised in other settings is a substitution of habitat. The theory's goal (say, the age at which a child can take responsibility for another, or perform a class of inference) was achieved inside a habitat that supplied specific settings, customs and caretaker psychology. The target habitat supplies different ones. If the conditions the goal required are not reproduced, the theory's prediction has no ground to pay against, and the failure is silent until the goal fails. The child's "capability" is not an agent-only property (Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves.").

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A researcher can add an instrument or a setting to the study; they cannot subtract the habitat, because contact "does not consult you." So the honest statement is not "the theory fails in that culture" but "the theory's required conditions were not reproduced in that setting, and the residue is unmeasured."

**Verdict status: triangulated.** Fig 11 plus Fig 4 :34-35 ("The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."). World corroboration: Super and Harkness's developmental niche names exactly three reproduced subsystems (settings, customs, caretaker psychology), and the reaction norm shows the same starting genotype yielding different phenotype across environments. The three are low overlap (framework; cultural-developmental methods; biology), so this is the strongest corroborated condition in the folder.

### F3 - The residual is per setting, never a global generalization rate

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :28:

> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

"The theory generalizes to children in other settings" is an aggregate over slots. A near-zero global residual with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"). In practice this means a multi-site study can report that a developmental milestone holds "across cultures" while one setting carries all the failure mass. The per-setting verdict is the unit. A universality claim is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

**Verdict status: triangulated.** Fig 1 plus the WEIRD review (Henrich et al. 2010), which reports that 96% of psychological samples came from countries holding 12% of the world's population and that "members of WEIRD societies, including young children, are among the least representative populations one could find for generalizing about humans." The measurement invariance debate independently shows that a group-averaged statistic can hide exactly the between-group variance a comparison needs.

### F4 - Terms must be cast as effects, not as schooling readings

Fig 9 :30-31:

> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

Fig 6 :30:

> **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

A milestone named as a test score, a grade, or a classroom task is sharpened into terms only the schooling frame can read. It cannot travel, not because the target children lack the competence, but because the description cannot be instantiated there. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22) gives the instrument form: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information" and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." A test score is a reading, not a source; several habitat paths can produce the same score.

Rogoff 2003 supplies the exact inversion this condition forbids: "many researchers make conclusions from work done in a single group in overly general terms, claiming that 'the child does such-and-so' rather than 'these children did such-and-so.'" The effect-term reading is "these children, in these routine circumstances, did such-and-so."

**Verdict status: triangulated.** Fig 9 and Fig 6 are two figure routes; the essay and Rogoff are world corroboration, not the source of the verdict.

### F5 - Independence, not agreement across the same pool

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> **"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."**

Fig 7 :12-13:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

A theory tested on more schooled children and then on more schooled children is one long room: motion, no progress. Generalization evidence requires a frame that does not draw on the schooling evidence pool. Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." If the cross-cultural study uses the same instrument, the same coding scheme and the same research assistants trained in the same program, it is one frame wearing two names, and its agreement is "the pool talking" (Fig 2 :20-21).

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is high: a wrongly generalized theory is imposed on children whose habitat did not supply the conditions the theory assumed.

**Verdict status: triangulated.** Fig 2 and Fig 8 plus Fig 7, three figure routes with different objects (anchor independence, union surplus, room chain). Henrich et al. corroborate at the world level that population variation is real; they do not decide the figure verdict.

### F6 - The anchor for the cross-setting question is the target observation

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :45:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26-28 names the channel failures: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" and "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." The schooling frame's own norms (mean score by age, stage onset) are instruments for questions inside that frame. They become a false anchor for the generalization question if they are treated as the standard the target children must match. The question is constitutively about the target child's development, so Fig 2 :36 applies directly: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." The ground is what happens in that setting.

**Verdict status: single-source on the figure side** (Fig 2 and Fig 10 are both framework primaries, so I do not call this triangulated across independent witnesses). World corroboration from the developmental niche literature is consistent but shares the same broad evidence pool as cross-cultural developmental psychology.

### F7 - Measurement equivalence is a filter, not a witness

The cross-cultural methods literature contains a live disagreement. The Measurement invariance entry (opened 2026-11-22) defines the property: "measurement invariance or measurement equivalence is a statistical property of measurement that indicates that the same construct is being measured across some specified groups." It also reports a conflict: Welzel and colleagues "criticize the excessive reliance on invariance tests as criteria for the validity of cultural and psychological constructs" and found that "the invariance criteria favor constructs with low between-group variance, while constructs with high between-group variance fail these tests." Proponents reply that nomological validation "hinges on the assumption of comparability."

By the conflict rule this goes back to the census, it does not flip the verdict. Re-census: the two sides are arguing about an **instrument** (INWARD in Formula step 7), whereas the figure verdict is about the **verdict** (does the theory's prediction pay in the target setting). Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 10 :26-28 warns that "saturated metrics, pooled readings" lose touch with the ground while the ground itself does not decay. An invariance test is a filter on whether a comparison is admissible; it is not a witness that a developmental theory generalizes.

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." So the remedy is a better instrument in the target setting, not a verdict declared by a statistic.

**Verdict status: triangulated** on the figure separation (Fig 9 :60 plus Fig 10 :24-28 plus Formula step 7). The world disagreement is recorded as a conflict, not used as a witness.

### F9 - A failed regularity owes a refusal, not a better story

Fig 5 amendment, :50-56 (the fifth bin):

> **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

Operators table :58:

> **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

Formula step 2: "REFUTED - a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation." If a schooling-based theory's prediction fails in a target habitat, the owed move is to refuse its standing for that slot, not to append a cultural moderator that leaves contact unchanged (Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."). A vocabulary without negation can only accumulate: it can correct and cannot carry the correction.

Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else." That is why the shape was bound in §0 before this finding.

**Verdict status: triangulated** (Fig 5 amendment, Operators table, Formula step 2).

### F10 - The kernel/recording distinction is not a ban on generalization

Correction to my own first pass, kept visible. I first wrote this as "schooling-based theories do not generalize." That is an overstatement. Fig 4 :22-24 permits a method or a head start to cross, and Fig 4 :34-35 says the landing habitat remakes the seed. The claim is conditional: generalization holds when what crosses is the kernel and the goal-required conditions are reproduced. When those fail, the theory does not generalize and should be refused for that slot, not declared universally false. Section 7 records this as F10.

The same correction applies to the word "universal." Fig 13 :134-135 says the framework "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Naming a specific culture or a specific milestone would be a binding from the developmental-science frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Fig 10 / Seat Map discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Kernel condition.** A cross-habitat prediction is demonstrated to be a re-applicable method whose binding arrangement survives intact into the target habitat with none of the source habitat's goal-required conditions reproduced (breaks F1 and F2).
2. **Per-slot condition.** A global average across settings is shown to track every per-setting residual with no live slot hidden and no failure mass concentrated (breaks F3).
3. **Effect-term condition.** A term sharpened into schooling-only readings is shown to travel to another setting and pay against that setting's ground without an effect-term description (breaks F4).
4. **Independence condition.** Two frames drawing on one schooling evidence pool are shown to provide independent corroboration of a cross-setting claim (breaks F5 and Fig 2 :40).
5. **Anchor condition.** A target-setting measurement shows the theory's prediction pays with the goal-required habitat conditions demonstrably absent (breaks F2 and F6).
6. **Gap condition.** A figure line is found that names which developmental theories or which cultures generalize, making F8's declared gap unnecessary (breaks F8).

If any fires, withdraw the corresponding condition and keep the record visible. Until a target-setting measurement is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying, so these are written down before the verdict, not fitted to it.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the mode on every claim. An unmeasured condition is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict (`THE_FORMULA.md` :107).
2. Lead with the four-condition separator and its refuter, not with a list of cultures (Fig 9 :32, Fig 4 :22).
3. Keep verdicts per setting. Do not report a universality rate as if it were a witness (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :60).
4. Rebind actors, currency and anchor explicitly when moving a theory into a new habitat, and enumerate what the goal required before comparing predictions (Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 11 :67).
5. Where a prediction fails, refuse it for that slot rather than appending a moderator (Fig 5 :53, Operators :58).
6. Use public institutional, opt-in venues in developmental science and cross-cultural methods; ask what would refute the shape, not whether the reader agrees (`03_public_contact_points.csv`, Fig 10 :52).

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the generator: the conditions, the refuter and the lineage, not the conclusion.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **What is the residual?** Per-slot miss when a schooled theory predicts a child in another setting. Fig 1 :19, Symbols :38-40. Not an average.
2. **Where is the break?** Between vocabulary/objective and the habitat enumeration that would say what the goal required. Fig 3 :25-27. A link with no instrument is not a working link.
3. **SEARCHED?** More schooled samples are a search inside one closure. Fig 8 :32: no search adds an atom. This is cheapest and assumed by the other two.
4. **INWARD?** Sharpen the target-setting instrument and the effect description. Fig 6 :32; Fig 9 :30-31. The measurement-invariance dispute lives here.
5. **OUTWARD?** Import the target habitat's own frame (caregiver psychology, local work and play, subsistence demands) with a source gate and shadow test. Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52. Debited against independence.
6. **Refuse or mint?** If a held regularity is false in that slot, refuse it for that slot (¬), do not mint a moderator. Fig 5 :53, Operators :58.
7. **Stop?** When R stops arriving. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Closure" :89: the loop stops at perfect prediction or a closed channel, and from inside those look the same. Record the lineage and the R_T. No `grounded` label.

## 7. Strains, gaps and the round trip

- **Live strain S1, operand type of the kernel.** Fig 4 :23 says "a method that can be reapplied" crosses; Fig 12 :104 says a settled molecule becomes an atom. Whether a developmental theory is a method or a settled molecule depends on how its arrangement was recorded, which is an empirical question about the particular theory, not a figure question. Named, not hidden; it is the fixed-atom condition on the F1 claim.
- **Live strain S2, invariance as filter versus witness.** Recorded in F7; dissolved by re-census into two quantities (instrument admissibility versus ground payment) and kept visible because the field itself disagrees.
