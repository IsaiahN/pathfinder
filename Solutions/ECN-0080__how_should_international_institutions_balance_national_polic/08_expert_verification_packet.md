# Expert verification packet: the Tether on autonomy versus rules

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist or institutional scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain findings, not `grounded`.** No outcome series was read. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Balance" is the wrong shape for this question. There are three residuals, one for trade, one for finance, one for investment, and averaging them into one openness score throws away the signal that says where to look. National governments and international institutions are two frames, not anchors; both must range against outcomes neither can move. Rules are useful conventions while they remain coordination devices that can be violated and checked. A rule that decides a national outcome without an external check has stopped being a convention and become a frame pretending to be the ground. The reading must be owned by someone who is not measured by it, and what crosses between national and international scales should be a reusable method, not a recording of one country's success. Preserving policy space is instrumentally valuable because low overlap is what gives the union a surplus. Three goals at once, perfect autonomy, perfect integration and full domestic control, cannot be composed; which one to release is decided at the ground, not named by the framework.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual is per object slot and must not be aggregated into one balance scalar.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." And Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **What to check:** are trade, finance and investment actually overlapping conditions (a capital control changes trade; a tariff changes investment location)? If yes, a single lever will leave intersections standing.
- **Refuter:** show a single lever that makes all three slot residuals trend down together, with no intersection term. If it fires, the per-slot requirement is withdrawn.

### C2: framework-internal and institutional, checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** national and international actors are frames, not anchors; rules are conventions until they decide an outcome; the reading must not be owned by a measured frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :86 "Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."
- **What to check:** name one current rule where the institution's own agreement is used as the evidence that the rule worked. That is the failure class this claim predicts. Also: name one case where independent statistics changed a dispute outcome.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a rule that determinately settles a national outcome, has no external check, and still functions as a convention with no truth value; or a frame that owns the reading and is nevertheless corrected by the ground on that same reading. Either firing breaks the claim.

### C3: framework-internal with an outside analogue, checkable by reading Figures 8 and 6

- **Statement:** low overlap between the national and international frames is what gives their union a surplus, and three-at-once is unreachable from one frame's closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- **What to check:** Rodrik's trilemma (*"democracy, national sovereignty and global economic integration are mutually incompatible: we can combine any two of the three, but never have all three simultaneously and in full."*) and the monetary trilemma (*"you can't have it all: A country must pick two out of three."*) are outside analogues, not authorities for the figures. Do they predict the same barrier at low overlap?
- **Refuter:** show two frames with near-total evidence overlap whose union produces a novel, ground-settled term; or show all three goals jointly attainable at full strength. Either firing sends the claim back to the census.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a policy prescription. No tariff rate, capital control, treaty clause or institutional design is named by the figures.
- Not that more rules or more autonomy is always better. Both are frames; the ground decides.
- Not that existing institutions are illegitimate. Agreement about shared meaning is efficient (Fig 2 :41); the objection begins when a convention decides an outcome without a check.
- Not that agreement among independent experts settles anything. Agreement is a convention or a shared evidence pool, never an anchor (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No outcome series, dispute record or crisis count was read.
- Not that the framework names which trilemma corner to release. It returns a shape, not a clause (Fig 9 :32).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement:

1. A source-discriminating instrument per slot that distinguishes a rule coordinating meaning from a rule deciding the outcome.
2. An instrument for the middle dependency link: what counts as progress in this slot, measured rather than assumed.
3. A stability or crisis outcome that pays for one corner of the finance trilemma.
