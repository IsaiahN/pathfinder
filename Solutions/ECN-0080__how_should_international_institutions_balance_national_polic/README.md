# Solution ECN-0080

**Question:** How should international institutions balance national policy autonomy with rules for trade, finance, and investment?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 105

**Section:** 8. Trade, globalization, and industrial policy

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain findings, nothing `grounded`. No measurement was taken; this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual in effect terms.** Each of three object slots (trade, finance, investment) has a national frame with its own ground and an international rule frame with a claimed ground. The residual is the gap between what each returns when the other acts. "Balance" is a threshold comparison between autonomy and rules, measured per slot, never one global average. An answer would look like: a rule that can be violated and checked, a named anchor that does not update, and an explicit statement of which frame may own the reading. That shape is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refuted (Formula, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure reasons, quoted verbatim:

1. **An anchor is not a party to the negotiation.** Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." A list selected for likely agreement is a negotiation, not a triangulation.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly that pool.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: public opt-in institutional channels avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM style failures that unsolicited bulk personal-email campaigns attract. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
