# Union, comparison and dependency: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

"Balance national policy autonomy with rules for trade, finance, and investment" is borrowed vocabulary. It could mean a single scalar trade-off, a partition of three independent problems, or a union of overlapping conditions. Three questions are owed: (1) which structure does the question have, (2) which bond does "balance" name, and (3) in what order must the parts be settled?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Actors: national frames (D1), international rule frames (D2), observer frame (D3). Currency: policy instruments, rules, measurement. Anchor: outcome data per slot; agreement is never an anchor. Full census in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Trade, finance and investment are **not** a partition. A capital control (finance) changes trade flows; a tariff (trade) changes investment location. They overlap, so the question is a **union compound** with its own loop, not three independent questions in a list.
- The consequence is structural: a single lever clears where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." (Fig 9 :57)
- *Library of Babel*, in effect terms: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the three slot residuals are empirically separable under one lever with no intersection term, the union claim fails and the question becomes a list. Marked `single-source` for the figure definition, corroborated at low overlap by Rodrik's trilemma (three conditions, any two, never all three).

### Q2: Which bond is "balance"?

- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison." Operators table :51: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."*
- "Balance" is therefore ⋛ (comparison), not `+` (conjunction) and not a weighted sum. Under `+` the two sides would both have to hold at once, which is not what is being asked. Under a sum the two currencies would add, and Fig 12 :125 says: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- **Type caveat, live:** ⋛ is defined over two quantities. Autonomy and rule are frames, not quantities. The comparison is legal only after each has been reduced to a measured quantity at the same level (a per-slot residual). This is a type obligation, not a metaphor. Named, not hidden.
- Two readings are refused:
  1. **Balance as conjunction.** That would require autonomy and rule to both hold unconditionally; it is the three-at-once barrier F10.
  2. **Balance as sequence.** Fig 12 :73: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Whether a country first liberalizes then regulates, or the reverse, is a different substance. The figures do not license an order at the level of the two sides.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a rule set in which autonomy and rule are both quantities at one level with a threshold relation that the ground settles. If no such reduction exists, the comparison is a shape only, not a measurement.

### Q3: Are the three slots isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As the question writes them, trade, finance and investment share only the *form* "national frame ⋛ rule frame." They are members of the same **functional group** (a comparison over a per-slot residual), with different content in the slots.
- The stronger claim: if all three are written over the full shared atom set, differing only in which residual the comparison ranges against, they are **isomers** in Fig 12's sense. The aggregated "openness" formula cannot tell them apart, and that is exactly why the aggregate is unsafe.
- **Falsifier:** if one slot can be settled without an atom the other two require, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim remains.

### Q4: What order do the parts come in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- The chain for this question is:
  1. **Perception:** is there a fact here? (Fig 9 :4) Which slot has a live residual?
  2. **Vocabulary:** what would count as progress in this slot? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26-27: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments... A link with no instrument is not a link that works."
  3. **Objective:** name the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
  4. **Planning:** design the rule, keeping it violable (Fig 10 :19).
  5. **Learn and carry:** settle against the ground, then carry the *method* upward (Fig 4 :22).
- "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can." (Fig 3 :22) Imposing a rule before the objective link has an instrument is effort above the break.
- **Falsifier:** show that a rule imposed at step 4 can be settled at the ground even when step 2 has no instrument. That would make the dependency chain a list of advice rather than a constraint.

### Q5: What do the outside analogues add?

World sources are corroboration only. Each was opened on 2026-11-23.

- **Political economy, Rodrik (2007, quoted in the Wikipedia entry on the political trilemma):** *"democracy, national sovereignty and global economic integration are mutually incompatible: we can combine any two of the three, but never have all three simultaneously and in full."* Predicts a barrier in the union, corroborates F10. Low overlap with the figures.
- **International finance, the impossible trinity (Fleming 1962, Mundell 1960-63; Krugman 1999 quoted there):** *"you can't have it all: A country must pick two out of three."* Predicts a per-slot barrier in finance, corroborates F10. Low overlap.
- **Political science / law, subsidiarity (Wikipedia, OED definition quoted there):** *"the principle that a central authority should have a subsidiary function, performing only those tasks which cannot be performed at a more local level."* Predicts a scale membrane with a direction, corroborates Fig 4 :22.
- **Cybernetics, Ashby's law of requisite variety (Wikipedia, quoting Ashby 1956):** *"only variety can destroy variety."* Predicts that a regulator needs at least as many distinguishable states as the phenomenon, corroborates Fig 8 :45-49 and F1's anti-averaging rule. Low overlap.
- **Reliability engineering, common mode failure (Wikipedia):** *"Failures in multiple parts of a system may be caused by a single fault"* and *"Strategies for the avoidance of common mode failures include keeping redundant components physically isolated."* Predicts that harmonized frames fail together, corroborates Fig 2 :18-21 and F7. Low overlap.

None of these decides a figure verdict. If any conflicted with a figure, the census would be reopened; no conflict was found.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union** of three overlapping slots, not a partition and not one scalar. "Balance" is a **comparison** (⋛) over per-slot quantities, not a sum and not a conjunction, with a live type obligation. The parts are a **functional group** and, under the full shared atom set, a real **isomer** claim whose aggregate formula is unsafe. The order is **dependency**, perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to carrying, and the middle link (what counts as progress in this slot) has no instrument yet. The falsifiers above are written before the run.
