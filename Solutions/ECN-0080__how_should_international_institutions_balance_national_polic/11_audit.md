## 1. Independent domain census (written from the residual line first, before reading the primary census)

I started only from the question as an effect statement: for each of three fields, a national actor wants to act on its own ground and an international actor holds a rule that constrains that action; the residual is the gap between the predicted outcome and the returned outcome when the other acts.

- **R (arity, symmetry, scale).** Arity: two sides (autonomy, rule) times three slots. Symmetry: the relation is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join; Fig 12 :112 says "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison." Scale: one frame national, a population of frames international, with a lossy membrane (Fig 4 :27). This is a **union compound**, because the slots overlap; a capital control changes trade, a tariff changes investment location.
- **Domain 1, national frame.** Actors: governments, regulators, voters, domestic firms. Currency: instruments, welfare, legitimacy. Anchor: national outcome data, elections. Nameable. Stake: interest in outcome unavoidable; interest in reading disqualifying.
- **Domain 2, international rule frame.** Actors: treaty bodies and secretariats. Currency: rules, commitments, compliance, rulings. Anchor: outcome data, not the institution's agreement. The hazard is self-anchoring.
- **Domain 3, observer frame.** Actors: statisticians, courts, researchers, markets. Currency: measurement, residual, provenance. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulation its independence is zero.
- **Partition or union.** Domains: union. Slots: partition for measurement. Both readings are needed and they are different objects.
- **Order by dependency.** Establish that there is a fact; describe R per slot before choosing a frame; name an anchor that cannot move; separate conventions from verdicts; design rules last. The middle link, what counts as progress in this slot, has no instrument.

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. ## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual), Fig 10 :47 (convention vs verdict), Fig 8 :47-49 (independence and union surplus) and Fig 6 :23 (barrier).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: three slots, both sides range against a ground, rules are conventions until they decide outcomes, preserve policy space, three-at-once unreachable.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete institutional policy: a specific tariff, capital control or treaty clause.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **operand type of ⋛**: the shorthand compares autonomy and rule as if they were quantities at one level; the figures define ⋛ over two quantities, and neither side is measured here;

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the three slots must not be averaged and a rule that decides an outcome without a check has left its seat. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
