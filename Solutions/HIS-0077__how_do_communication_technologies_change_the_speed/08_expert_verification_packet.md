# Expert verification packet - the Tether on communication technologies and competing claims

**What this is:** a claim in a form a communication researcher, historian of science or information
 theorist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows
what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No measurement is
claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A communication technology changes the channel, not the ground. Speed and reach are channel
quantities: how fast a signal moves and how many receivers it reaches. The ground, which settles which
claim holds, does not move with them. Credibility is a frame-internal quantity produced by the same
community that would use it to decide, so it cannot witness the claim it credits; more transmission
inside a shared closure compounds agreement without adding evidence. The one way a technology bears
on a settled claim is by changing contact, by delivering the claim to frames whose evidence differs or
by making independent checking cheaper. Speed, reach and credibility form a union, not a partition, and
must be unbundled before any of them is searched.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading two short passages
- **Statement:** the channel and the ground are different quantities. A change in transmission medium
  does not alter the ground's answer to a settled question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 2 :29 -
  *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any
  frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** are these two lines present verbatim in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`,
  and do they say what the claim says they say? Then: can you name any case where a medium-only change
  flipped a settled measurement?
- **Refuter:** a medium-only change that alters the ground's answer to an already-settled question. If
  it holds, the channel/ground distinction fails and the claim is withdrawn.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading four short passages
- **Statement:** credibility accrued is a frame-internal quantity disqualified as a verdict, and more
  transmission in a shared closure compounds agreement, not evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :26 - *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*; Fig 1 :27 - *"They
  are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*; Fig 7 :12-13 -
  *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's
  agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** does Fig 1 disqualify credibility as a self-score rather than as a social fact?
  Does the echo-chamber and Woozle-effect literature (sources in `09_watson_answer.md`) describe the
  same mechanism at low overlap, and does its disagreement about effect size matter to the figure
  verdict? It should not: the figures govern admissibility, not prevalence.
- **Refuter:** credibility in a closed pool that predicts ground settlement above chance after
  independence and ground contact are controlled for; or retransmissions that improve verdict
  accuracy with independence held fixed.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with one open type question
- **Statement:** the only ground-bearing effect is a change of contact. Speed, reach and credibility
  are a union requiring unbundling; speed and reach are isomers of one transmission event.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing,
  however much it improves."* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on
  contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 9 :55-56 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union
  of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only
  one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still
  binds."* Fig 13 :76 - *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which
  state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- **What to check:** is "contact" a nameable anchor here (Fig 2 :29-30), so Domain 3 is a real domain
  and not a metaphor? Does the historical print-press record in the world sources show a contact
  change (more correspondents, shared methods, results that could be tested) rather than a credibility
  change? That is the discriminating observation.
- **Refuter:** reach that rises while the independence distribution is unchanged and a settled verdict
  moves anyway; or one mechanism that moves speed, reach and credibility with no residual in any
  intersection.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of any communication technology as good or bad. No name is derived, only a shape.
- Not that widespread belief makes a claim true, and not that credibility is socially worthless.
- Not that faster transmission is inherently harmful. A contact change can help; the verdict is
  conditional on independence.
- Not that the three effects can be averaged. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not
  aggregated."*
- Not a derivation of any historical outcome. The print-press and misinformation sources are imports
  with provenance, not figures results.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument in this run.
- Not a verdict from a human anchor. The figures are the ground for the design question; the question
  of which technology did what in history is left to the instruments named in section 5.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Channel-only test:** change the medium, hold the claim and receiving frames fixed. Does the
   ground's answer to a settled question move?
2. **Credibility test:** regress settlement on credibility after controlling for independence and
   ground contact. Does credibility still predict?
3. **Repetition test:** increase retransmissions with independence fixed. Does accuracy improve?
4. **Contact test:** measure the independence distribution of receivers before and after. Does the
   technology change contact, and does that change track any shift in settlement?
5. **Union test:** find one mechanism that moves all three effects with no residual.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Open `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and read Fig 10 :24, Fig 1 :26-27, Fig 7 :12-13 and :35, Fig 11 :40,
   Fig 6 :23, Fig 9 :55-56 and Fig 13 :76. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 line by line. Are the quotes verbatim and do they license the claims?
3. Check C3 against your own field. In the case you know best, did the technology change contact or
   only throughput? Name the observation that would distinguish them.
4. Name one instrument that would run the contact test, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
