## 0. The residual, stated as an effect

The question is not "did the printing press, the telegraph, radio, television, the internet or social
media help?" That is a verdict without a ground. The residual is the observed delta when a
communication technology enters a field of competing claims:

> claims travel faster (speed), reach more actors and more places (reach), and the credibility
> assigned to competing claims changes (credibility).

State it as a question about quantities and anchors:

- Which quantity does each effect change, the channel or the ground?
- What is the anchor that settles it?
- What observation would reverse the claim?

The sought-for answer is bound now as an ordinary term (formula, "Direction"), before any figure is
chosen: *an adequate answer assigns each of the three effects to a named quantity, names the anchor
that settles it, and states a falsifier per assignment.* A figure that only fits by ignoring one of
the three effects has been chosen by preference, not by derivation, and must be re-censused.

## 2. Domain census (derive first, then quote)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three effects, not one. Speed and reach are properties of transmission; credibility is a
  property of a frame's relation to a claim. Fig 9 :5 gives the test: *"First: is there a fact here?
  Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different
  subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- **Symmetry:** speed and reach are symmetric under a change of message content (a faster channel moves
  true and false claims alike); credibility is not symmetric, it is a directed relation between a
  claim and a frame. Fig 12 :89 gives the bond test: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both
  being present? Yes."* Comparison, not conjunction, governs credibility.
- **Scale:** first order inside one frame (a claim arrives and is credited); second order across frames
  (many frames credit the same claim); third order at the ground (the claim is settled or not). Fig 13
  :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

- **Domain 1, the transmission channel.** Actors: emitter, signal, medium, receiver. Currency:
  throughput and coverage. Anchor: channel fidelity, a measurable engineering quantity (bit rate,
  error rate, capacity). This is a genuine domain: the anchor does not change its answer because a
  claim is popular.
- **Domain 2, the credit frame.** Actors: competing claims and the frames that rank them. Currency:
  credibility accrued and agreement. Anchor: the ground, which does not update on agreement. This is
  a genuine domain: measurement is nameable. Its own currency is disqualified as its own verdict
  (Fig 1 :27).
- **Domain 3, the habitat of receivers.** Actors: audiences, institutions, gatekeepers, peer frames.
  Currency: attention, trust, and the independence between frames. Anchor: the ground for truth;
  human judgment for the separate question "is this a good answer for a person" (Fig 2 :36-37). This
  is a genuine domain: contact is nameable and can be enumerated (Fig 11 :43-44).

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** The three effects overlap: a change in reach can change which frames are in
contact, which can change credibility, which can change the apparent support for a claim. Fig 9
:55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate
ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections
standing, because the second condition still binds."* A lever that increases speed clears the speed
region and leaves the credibility intersection standing. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Perception: does a signal arrive and is it distinguished from the previous one (Fig 3 :4-5)?
2. Vocabulary: do receiving frames have terms for the claim (Fig 3 :6-7)?
3. Objective: what would count as settlement, in the frame's own terms (Fig 3 :9-10)?
4. Settlement at the ground, before any second-order comparison of claims.
5. Learn and carry: only methods cross up, never recordings (Fig 4 :22-25).

A reading taken at step 5 (credibility) when step 4 (settlement) has not run is a reading of nothing
(Fig 3 :22-23). This is why reach cannot be substituted for settlement.

### Strain test applied

Fig 2 :31-32 (*"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"*)
and Fig 7 :13 (*"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*) appear to strain against the everyday
claim that a widely believed claim is better supported. Re-census resolves it: the figures govern
the verdict question (what the ground settles), while "widely believed" governs a social-credibility
question. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census. The strain is recorded in section 6
rather than erased.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The channel and the ground are different quantities (decisive)

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

A communication technology is a channel instrument. It changes how fast a signal moves and how many
receivers it reaches. The ground (a measurement, a replication, an instrument reading) does not move
when the channel changes. Therefore "faster and wider" is not evidence that a competing claim became
more true. Verdict for Domain 1: **triangulated** across Fig 10 and Fig 2 with low overlap (one
governs channel decay, the other anchor legitimacy). Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show a case where changing only the transmission medium changes the ground's answer to
an already-settled question (for example, a measurement whose outcome flips because it is reported
by radio rather than by post). If that holds, the channel/ground split fails and this solution is
withdrawn.

### F2 - Credibility accrued is a frame-internal quantity, disqualified as a verdict

Fig 1 :26-27:

> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 1 :23 and :27 together make the point: *"The ground enters as the only metric"* and a frame
cannot score itself with what it produces. Credibility is produced by the same frames that would use
it to decide. It is therefore not admissible as a witness for the claim it credits. This is the
single most important figure line for this question, and it is why the reach-credit story fails: a
technology that generates credibility is generating a frame-internal number, not a ground reading.
Verdict for Domain 2: **triangulated** across Fig 1 and Fig 2 :40. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a frame whose credibility score predicts ground settlement above chance after
controlling for independence and ground contact, and show that removing the score degrades the
verdict. Then credibility would be carrying information the ground does not, and Fig 1 :27 would
have misidentified the quantity.

### F3 - Speed and reach compound agreement, not evidence

Fig 7 :12-13:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

A message repeated through a network of frames that share a closure is a room chain. More hops give
more agreement and no more evidence. This is exactly the structural content of the echo-chamber and
Woozle-effect findings opened this session: repetition inside a closed system manufactures a feeling
of truth, and frequent citation of an unsupported claim makes non-facts look established. The world
sources corroborate but do not decide; the figures decide. Verdict for Domain 2: **triangulated**
(Fig 7 plus Fig 2 :40). Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** a controlled experiment in which increasing the number of retransmissions of an
unchanged claim, with receiving frames' independence held fixed, improves the accuracy of the
resulting verdict at the ground. That would make agreement a witness, contrary to Fig 7 :35.

### F4 - The disqualifying correlation is the default effect of reach

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :42:

> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Reach tends to transmit a claim to receivers who already share the sender's frame: same language,
same institutions, same feed. The increased credibility that follows is the pool talking, not two
frames confirming each other. Fig 8 :48 says the same thing from the union side: *"How much two
frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether
their union has any surplus."* Verdict for Domain 3: **triangulated** (Fig 2 plus Fig 8, low
overlap). Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** show a technology that raises reach and raises the measured independence of the
receiving frames at the same time, and that the resulting agreement survives a ground test. That is
the one case where increased reach is not a disqualifying correlation, and it is the case F6 licenses.

### F5 - The only ground-bearing route is a change of contact

Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :50:

> **"Fails by spending budget on a region already swept. Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent."**

This is the positive half of the answer. A communication technology *can* matter to which claim is
settled, but only through the habitat: by delivering a claim to a frame whose closure differs (so
that disagreement becomes triangulable, Fig 2 :35), by making independent search cheaper, or by
carrying a provenanced import (Fig 6 :14, *"the only operator that moves the wall"*). The 17th-century
printing press is the historical instance recorded in the opened source: scholars could *"circulate
them with printing to more people, communicate faster with each other, and reach a wider geographical
range"* and printing *"created a community of astronomers working on common problems with common
methods and reaching agreed solutions."* That is a contact change, not a credibility change. Verdict
for Domain 3: **single-source** for the figure derivation (Fig 11), corroborated at low overlap by
the history of science source. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** show a technology that increases speed and reach while leaving the independence
distribution of receiving frames unchanged, and that still moves a ground-settled verdict. Then
contact is not the only route and Fig 11 :40 is too strong.

### F6 - The three effects are a union; unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :54-56:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Speed, reach and credibility are not three labels for one effect. A lever on speed (compression,
bandwidth) does not clear the credibility intersection. A lever on credibility (verification badges)
does not clear the reach intersection. The compound has its own loop and its own ground. Verdict:
**single-source** for the figure rule, with the world sources silent on the union claim. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** measure one technology change that moves all three effects through one mechanism, with
no residual in any intersection. Then the union reading is unnecessary and the split should be
collapsed.

### F7 - Provenance separates corroboration from amplified adoption

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

When two outlets repeat the same claim, the contents look like agreement. Whether it is corroboration
or one source wearing two names depends on the record. This is also what the opened *Woozle effect*
source documents: repeated citation gives a claim *"undeserved credibility"* and a five-sentence
letter became an *"extensive study"* and a *"landmark study"* by accretion. Verdict: **triangulated**
(Fig 8 plus Fig 2 :40). Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** erase all provenance in a case where two frames independently derived the same claim and
show that a checker can still tell convergence from adoption. Then the record is not the discriminator.

### F8 - Prestige and credibility share a failure mode

The opened essay *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* states:

> **"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."**
> **"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."**

It also names the exact illusion this question is about: *"the prestige loop looks like agreement, and
agreement feels like truth."* A claim credited because it is everywhere is being funded by reach and
then mistaken for settled. Fig 2 :42 prices the repair. This finding is **single-source** at the
figure level (Fig 2 :42) and corroborated at high overlap by the framework author's essay; it is a
world claim, not a figure verdict. Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show a case where high reach, with no ground contact, reliably settles a competing claim,
and where removing reach leaves it unsettled. Then prestige is settling an action, contrary to the essay.

### F9 - Compared claims are second-order, and cannot be merged by reach

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Two competing claims, each settled or unsettled at first order, can be compared only after each is
settled. A technology that increases the number of comparisons does not settle the operands. Fig 13
:113-115: *"The array is unbounded and the reach is not. More compositions never add a member, and no
chain reaches outside what the six and their bonds already span."* Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 12).
Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** show a settled comparison between two claims neither of which has been independently
settled, and where the comparison itself changes the standing of both at the ground.

### F10 - What survives, with its mode attached

| claim | mode | figure / source |
|---|---|---|
| A technology changes the channel (speed, reach), not the ground | `general` | Fig 10 :24; Fig 2 :29 |
| Credibility accrued is frame-internal and cannot witness truth | `general` | Fig 1 :26-27; Fig 2 :40 |
| More transmission in a shared closure compounds agreement, not evidence | `general` | Fig 7 :12-13, :35 |
| Reach matters at the ground only through contact with independent frames | `specified` | Fig 11 :40; Fig 6 :23, :50 |
| The three effects are a union and must be unbundled | `general` | Fig 9 :54-56 |
| The comparison of competing claims is second-order | `specified` | Fig 12 :104 |

Every row carries its own falsifier from F1-F9. None is `grounded`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the framework's own discipline, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The
shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A medium-only change alters the ground's answer to an already-settled question (breaks F1).
2. Credibility in a closed pool predicts settlement above chance after controlling for independence
   and ground contact (breaks F2).
3. Increasing retransmissions of an unchanged claim, with independence fixed, improves ground
   accuracy (breaks F3).
4. Reach rises, independence is unchanged, and a ground-settled verdict moves (breaks F5).
5. One mechanism moves speed, reach and credibility with no residual in any intersection (breaks F6).
6. A settled comparison is achieved between two unsettled claims (breaks F9).

If any fires, the corresponding domain verdict is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, no part
of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the channel/ground distinction (F1) and the disqualification of credibility (F2). They
   are the strongest figure-derived points and they are checkable by reading two short passages.
2. Keep the three domain verdicts separate. Report speed and reach as channel results, credibility as
   a frame-internal signal, and contact as the only ground-bearing route. Never average them into
   "technology makes claims more trustworthy."
3. Do not offer a verdict on any historical technology from this package. The figures license the
   shape of the answer, not a ranking of printing, radio or social media. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind
   of thing, never the answer itself."*
4. Ask for a refutation against the six pre-registered items, not for agreement. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a
   characterised residual, never the source."*
5. Use the public institutional submission and contact points in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
   Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
