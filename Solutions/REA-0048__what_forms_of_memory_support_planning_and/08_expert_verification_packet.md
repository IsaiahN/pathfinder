# Expert verification packet: What forms of memory support planning and learning across long periods?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the role predictions. Not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The figures do not return a list of named memory systems. They constrain the shape such a list must have. Four role constraints follow: the record and the reach are two different memory quantities, one monotone and one fallible; what crosses a scale boundary across long periods is a reapplicable method or a head start, not a recording replayed from one occasion; planning memory must carry typed bonds such as sequence, comparison and subtraction, not just contents; and long horizons bind on the maintenance of the channel to the ground, because the ground does not decay while the channel does. The named forms from cognitive science are bindings onto these roles, imported from a world frame with provenance recorded, and they carry no figure-derived authority. Duration is a scale on a held axis, not a seventh memory primitive. Six refuters were written before the run. The claim is a shape and a set of checks, not a result.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The record and the reach are two quantities (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 6)

- **Statement:** a memory answer must say which of the two it describes. Storage grows monotonically; availability is derived and can fall. A bigger store is not more memory available now.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :42, "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." and Fig 6 :43, "So the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not." Corroborated in a different statement by `THE_FORMULA.md`, "What is recorded only grows; what is reachable is derived and can fall."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a functioning system in which the store and the currently usable set are the same monotone quantity, so nothing ever becomes unavailable without being deleted, and the system still plans and learns.

### C2. Across long periods the carrier is a method or head start, not a replayed recording (world-facing, checkable against skill acquisition and agent-memory work)

- **Statement:** a memory form whose only mode of reuse is replay supports repetition, not novel planning. What crosses a scale boundary is a compressed, reapplicable method or a head start that makes the next search cheaper.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :23, "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." Fig 4 :25-26, "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."
- **What to check:** the procedural-memory literature holds that "with practice, procedural knowledge develops, which operates largely outside of working memory" (Wikipedia, Procedural memory, 2026); the generative agents architecture "store[s] a complete record of the agent's experiences using natural language, synthesize[s] those memories over time into higher-level reflections, and retrieve[s] them dynamically to plan behavior" (Park et al., 2023). Does the reuse in each case compress into a method, or is it verbatim replay? The Bischof-Kohler criticism that future behaviour may be "cued by their current motivational state" (Wikipedia, Episodic-like memory, 2026) is the replay rival already named.
- **Status:** `specified`; single-source for the figure rule, with two low-overlap world corroborations. **Refuter:** show a system that reliably produces a novel successful plan from verbatim replayed occasion-specific recordings, with no compression into a reapplicable method and no head start reducing the next search.

### C3. Planning memory carries bonds, and the long horizon binds on the channel (checkable by reading Figures 12 and 10)

- **Statement:** a vocabulary without typed relations can name a state but not a route to one; and over a long horizon the failure is loss of contact, not exhaustion of storage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112, "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :25-26, "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate genuine planning from unconnected atoms with no sequence, comparison or subtraction structure; or exhibit a long-horizon memory system whose ground channel does not degrade without active maintenance, or where growth of the store guarantees continued contact.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a derivation of the textbook list of episodic, semantic, procedural, working and prospective memory. Those names are world imports, one per role, with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not that any one named form is the method form or the route form. That binding is left open.
- Not that "long-term memory" is a new primitive; time is already a state (Fig 13 :11-12, "time / the ordered succession of differences").
- Not that an episode can never contribute to planning; recombination under bonds is a composition, distinct from replay-only reuse. See the retraction in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment, lesion, ablation or measurement was performed here.
