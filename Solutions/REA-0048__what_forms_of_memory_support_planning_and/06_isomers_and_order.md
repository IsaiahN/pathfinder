## RESIDUAL

The question asks which forms of memory are required for planning and for learning across long periods. The residual, in effect terms, is a decision between two options:

> **Option A:** one memory faculty, where "more memory" means a bigger or longer store.
> **Option B:** non-interchangeable forms with distinct roles, each with its own failure mode.

Option A collapses the record into the reach and the method into the recording, and cannot explain why a stored episode does not by itself produce a novel plan. Option B commits to an ordered set of roles with separate refuters. This file does not re-decide that residual. It supplies the three things the main audit needs: a structure test for deciding whether two named forms are genuinely different or the same thing under two names (isomers), a dependency order among the roles, and analogues that make the figure lines checkable.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale** (Fig 9 :21-22, "R, described / arity · symmetry · scale"):

- **Arity:** two capacities named, planning and learning, over a time axis. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." This is a union compound and must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again").
- **Symmetry:** the roles are not interchangeable. A recording cannot substitute for a method (Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."), and a state cannot substitute for a structure (Fig 13 :29).
- **Scale:** within one run versus across scales and agents (Fig 4 membrane), and across many loop cycles, which is what "across long periods" adds. Time is already a state in the substrate (Fig 13 :11-12: "time / the ordered succession of differences"), so duration is a scale on an axis already held, not a new kind.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are the named memory forms isomers, or the same form under two names?

Fig 12 :117:

> "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

An isomer claim in this question is a claim that two named forms hold the same ingredients, for example content about a past occasion and a retrieval route, but differ in the bond. Episodic memory and semantic memory are the live case: the Memory & Cognition call for papers notes that "numerous findings have put in doubt the clear-cut nature of this distinction" (journal page, fetched 2026-11-23). Under Fig 12 the way to decide is not to count names but to identify the bond that differs.

- If the two forms differ only in the retention interval (minutes versus years), they are not isomers. Duration is a scale, not a bond (Fig 13 :43-44). That is the strain dissolved in the main audit's F8 re-census.
- If they differ in the typed relation they carry, for example one binds content to a dated occasion under a sequence bond and the other holds content with the occasion dropped, they are genuine isomers under Fig 12 :117. The difference is invisible in a flat ingredient list and decisive in whether the form can be reused to plan.

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." So the structure test is: name the atoms held, name the bond, then check whether swapping the bond yields a different substance. This is a `general` structural test, not a claim about any particular taxonomy.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two named memory forms with identical atoms and identical bonds whose behavioural consequences are nevertheless decisive and different. If that is possible, bond identity does not decide form identity and Fig 12 does not govern this question.

### Q2. What is the order of the four roles?

The roles are not a bag of ingredients. They are ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20) and by what each supplies to the next:

1. **Record and reach first.** Fig 6 :43: "So the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not." Nothing downstream can use a store that is confused with its own availability.
2. **Route vocabulary second, at the planning link.** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The bonds required are sequence, comparison and subtraction (Fig 12 :112).
3. **Method and head start third, at the learn-and-carry link.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." This is what crosses a scale boundary and what makes the next search cheaper (Fig 4 :24: "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once.").
4. **Channel maintenance as the standing condition, not a fifth form.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Without this, the first three roles drift together and the drift is coherent (Fig 2 :34: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices.").

The order predicts that store-size interventions fail below a broken objective or planning link, and that a method which crosses up must first have been settled against the ground (Fig 5 :72: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows").

### Q3. What analogues make this checkable?

Three analogues are used, each with a declared source and a limit:

- **Consolidation as a membrane crossing.** Memory consolidation is "a process in the brain that stabilizes newly learned information, allowing the memory to be stored long-term", with systems consolidation over "weeks to years" (Wikipedia, Memory consolidation, 2026). This is a world analogue of Fig 4's upward transform, which is lossy by design (Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."). It is an import with provenance, not a figure-derived name.
- **Reconsolidation as channel maintenance.** Recalled memories "can become unstable again after retrieval and may need to be updated before being re-stabilized" (same source). This is an analogue of Fig 10 :24, and it is why a long horizon is a maintenance question rather than a storage question.
- **Reflective compression as method formation.** The generative agents architecture "store[s] a complete record of the agent's experiences using natural language, synthesize[s] those memories over time into higher-level reflections, and retrieve[s] them dynamically to plan behavior" (Park et al., 2023, arXiv:2304.03442). Its ablation of observation, planning and reflection is a low-overlap world check on the dependency order. The rival explanation is the Bischof-Kohler criticism that future-oriented behaviour may be "cued by their current motivational state" (Wikipedia, Episodic-like memory, 2026), which is exactly the replay-only case Fig 4 :25-26 says produces repetition rather than a novel plan.

All three analogues are secondary. None draws on the figures, so none is a witness for the figure verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The support structure is an ordered set of roles, not a list of interchangeable forms. Two named forms are different only if a bond differs (Fig 12 :117); a difference of duration alone is a scale, not a new kind (Fig 13 :43-44). The roles order as record and reach, bond-bearing route, method and head start, with channel maintenance as a standing condition. The falsifier above was written before the run. The named cognitive-science forms remain bindings with provenance, never figure-derived names (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
