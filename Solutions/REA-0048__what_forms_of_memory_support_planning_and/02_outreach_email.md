**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide whether the memory taxonomy is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape claim about memory and planning that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual in effect terms:** which forms of memory are *required*, not merely useful, for planning and for learning across long periods? The two obvious answers are (A) one memory faculty, where more and longer storage is what matters, and (B) several non-interchangeable forms with distinct jobs. What is at stake: A cannot explain why a stored episode does not by itself produce a novel plan; B commits to a structure with separate refuters.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the predictions, not `grounded`):** the framework constrains the shape such a list must have, and it does not name the forms. Four role constraints follow:
>
> 1. **Record and reach are different quantities.** What is recorded only grows; what is reachable is derived and can fall. A bigger store is not the same thing as more memory being available now.
> 2. **Across long periods, what carries forward is a method or a head start, not a recording of one occasion.** A recording replayed produces a repeat of a past success, not a new one. Reapplicable, compressed methods and head starts that make the next search cheaper are what cross a scale boundary.
> 3. **Planning memory must carry relations, not just contents.** A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence, a goal is a comparison, progress is a subtraction.
> 4. **Long horizons bind on the channel, not the store.** The ground does not decay; the channel does. A memory system that is not re-checked against its ground drifts, and the drift is coherent, so it looks like it is working.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this derives the textbook list of episodic, semantic, procedural, working and prospective memory. Those names come from your field, not from the figures. They are bindings onto the roles above, arrived at as imports with provenance, and they carry zero evidentiary weight until that provenance is shown. I am also not claiming that "long-term memory" is a new primitive; duration is a scale on an axis the framework already holds.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a working system with one monotone memory quantity and no separate fallible reach;
> 2. a novel successful plan produced from verbatim replayed recordings, with no method compression and no head start;
> 3. genuine planning from unconnected atoms with no sequence, comparison or subtraction structure;
> 4. a long-horizon system whose ground channel needs no maintenance, or whose growing store guarantees continued contact.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the relevant role is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a memory problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the essays warn that outputs can be simulated rather than derived. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## Two-stage offer in one line

A stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
