# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this offer assumes the shape claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is intact. If any refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 fires, stop and record the retraction before sending anything.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the institutional recipe.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Subject:** Check one output about government information before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm working on a figure-grounded derivation about how governments acquire reliable information about citizens, firms and local conditions. I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework treats "reliable information" as a three-part effect, not a property of a report. (1) The residual, the gap between what a government's model predicts and what it observes, must stay indexed per citizen, per firm and per locality. A national average that looks settled while one locality is live is a legal state, not a success. (2) The reference the report ranges against must not change its answer because of what the government wants to hear. (3) The channel to that reference must stay reachable; the ground does not decay, the channel does. A fourth condition, a verifier that cannot be the producer of the claim, is required where the cost of being wrong is high. These four conditions overlap; they are a union, not a list of independent options.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular census design, register, sample size, disclosure rule or agency chart follows from this. It does not. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that a national average is useless, or that intuition in public administration is wrong. Those are bindings from other frames and need their own provenance and their own ground.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a pooled national series that retains the per-locality signal aggregation is said to destroy, measured by an instrument independent of the series;
> 2. a metric used to decide an outcome that is demonstrated to have remained a non-updating anchor under repeated policy changes, with a moving ground and a discriminator;
> 3. two frames drawing on the same evidence whose agreement still settles a question about the world, with a discriminator showing it is not the shared pool talking;
> 4. a checker that produced the claim and still verifies it soundly under a protocol where the ground could move independently of both;
> 5. a demonstration that the four conditions form a partition with no overlapping intersections, so one lever clears the whole residual.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I'd value from you:** tell me what you would count as a failure, and which of the five refuters is easiest to run with the data you already have. I am interested in the discriminator, not the verdict.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Recipe withheld under the gap | Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". |
| No "true answer", no "trust", no "bombshell" | Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not ask for endorsement. |
| Effect terms, not borrowed jargon | Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask each user for their own ground and refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check what it is looking for; the author's warning that solutions "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." |

## Two-stage offer, stricter than a courtesy

1. **Stage 1 (this email).** The shape claim and its five refuters. The recipient records what they would count as a failure *before* seeing any theory.
2. **Stage 2 (opt-in only).** If they are curious, the three links. Once a recipient has absorbed the whole framework, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40); their stage-1 verdict is therefore recorded first and their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3 (method reuse).** The formula is a generator, so giving it away gives away no answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must write a refuter for their own problem and state the mode of what they get.

One-to-one only. No bulk send. No request for trust. No ranking of recipients by likely agreement: that would manufacture exactly the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
