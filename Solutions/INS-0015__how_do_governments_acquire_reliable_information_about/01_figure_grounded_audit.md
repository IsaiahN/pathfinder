## 0. The residual, in effect terms

A government must act on a world it cannot see directly. The loop is the one in the Symbols table: `b` is the belief, `a` the action, `Γ(b,a)` the prediction, `o'` the observation, `R` the residual. The Symbols table defines `R` as "The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it." It is always a slice, "what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."

For this question the slots are citizens, firms and localities. The residual is not one national number. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

So "reliable information" is not a property of a report. It is a three-part effect:

1. **Slot fidelity.** `R` stays visible at the citizen, firm and locality slot. A global figure near zero with one live local slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state").
2. **An unmoved anchor.** The reference the report ranges against does not change its answer because of what the government wants to hear. Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :30: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
3. **A reachable channel.** The reading reaches the decision maker without the channel decaying into "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" (Fig 10 :25–26). Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** and Fig 10 :28: **"you have lost touch"**.

Anything that delivers all three is a candidate answer. Anything that delivers a national average, a self-confirming report, or a stale reading has already failed the residual definition before any institutional theory is invoked.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

I describe `R` by arity, symmetry and scale, then give one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet. I then say partition or union and order by dependency (Fig 3).

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three (citizens, firms, local conditions), and each is itself a set of slots. Fig 9 :4 opens with "First: is there a fact here?" and :5 distinguishes "several well-formed ones" from "one hard question". The three are not one measurement problem with three labels.
- **Symmetry:** the relation between a government report and the ground is not symmetric. The ground may correct the report; the report may not correct the ground (Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."). A reading that updates on the report has changed kind and become a frame (Fig 2 :34).
- **Scale:** first order at the slot (one enumerator, one household, one price), second order at the aggregated series (a national CPI, a poverty rate). Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom at the next level, which is why aggregation is legal at all; it is not thereby a substitute for the slot reading.

### Domains

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Measurement and perception** | citizens, firms, local conditions, enumerators, instruments, registers, price collectors | observations `o'`, per-slot residual `R_s` | the ground: a measurement, a counter, a market, an outcome | yes, Fig 2 :29 |
| **D2 The government's reporting frame** | ministries, agencies, statistical offices, field offices, ministers | reports, agreed figures, published series | should be the same ground, but is at risk of becoming mutual update or a common pool | yes, but contestable, Fig 2 :13–21 |
| **D3 Outside frames** | households reporting expectations, firms, markets, peers, press, independent indices | self-reports, behavioural traces, transactions, alternative series | partly the ground; these are imports with provenance, not free standing truth | yes, with source gate, Fig 8 :52 |
| **D4 Verification and the seat** | auditors, courts, comptrollers, parliamentary committees, researchers | residuals surfaced, conventions authored, rulings | the ground, never agreement; must be outside the slice graded | yes, Fig 10 :86 |

**Partition or union.** This is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Reliable information needs slot fidelity *and* an unmoved anchor *and* independent frames *and* a refusal seat. A single lever (more surveys, more auditors, more transparency) clears a region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are therefore kept per domain and never averaged into one "information quality" score.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** The order is: perception (is anything returning a reading at all?) → vocabulary (are there terms for what a condition affords: "what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked", Fig 3 :7) → objective (what counts as progress, in the system's own terms, Fig 3 :10) → planning → learn and carry. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Effort spent above a break arrives at a step whose input never arrives.

## 3. Findings, one loop per domain

### F1: The national average is the wrong object (D1, decisive for the framing)

Fig 1 :19, :22 and :28 (quoted in §0) are the governing lines. Fig 2 :13–15 describes the failure when frames update on one another: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence." A national figure that looks settled while a locality's slot is live is precisely the "legal state" of Fig 1 :13. The world source on survey error agrees from a different field: the US Census Bureau's *Measures of Nonsampling Error* (2020) says "systematic errors tend to accumulate over the entire sample" and "bias caused by systematic errors cannot be reduced by increasing the sample size." This is the same arithmetic as Fig 1 :22 read from the measurement side. **Mode `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 1 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 (common cause) and the survey-statistics source (low overlap). **Refuter:** show a pooled national series that retains the per-slot signal that aggregation is claimed to destroy, or a measurement in which a global residual near zero with a live local slot is impossible.

### F2: An anchor that updates is not an anchor (D2, decisive for the reporting frame)

Fig 2 :29–30 (quoted in §0), Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update.", Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :43: **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**.

This is the centre of the institutional answer. A statistical office whose published number is adjusted to make a policy look successful has stopped measuring and started authoring a convention. The OECD *Review of the Statistical System and Official Statistics of Argentina* (2019) reports the concrete case: "methods and data were distorted in order to produce figures which downplayed problems of inflation and poverty, and reduced debt service obligations... Argentina's official statistics steadily lost credibility." The IMF issued a Statement of Concern in 2012 and a Declaration of Censure in 2013. Goodhart's original statement, "any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes" (Goodhart 2013, quoting his 1975 observation), says the same thing from monetary economics. The Lucas critique adds a second mechanism: "any change in policy will systematically alter the structure of econometric models" (Lucas 1976, as quoted in the secondary record), so a relationship used for policy is not invariant under the policy it is used for. **Mode `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 2, Fig 10, the OECD case (public administration), and Goodhart/Lucas (economics, partly one pool). **Refuter:** produce a documented case where a metric used to decide an outcome remained a non-updating anchor over repeated policy changes, with an instrument that would detect the change. Without the instrument, "it did not move" is a reading from inside the frame.

### F3: Independence is a quantity, not a courtesy (D3)

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :32: "is what makes agreement mean something." Fig 2 :40 and :41 give the two valences of correlation: "disqualifying for a verdict" and "efficient for a convention." Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."** Fig 8 :49: "completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

The engineering corroboration is unusually clean. Stanford AA203 sensor-fusion notes (2021) say "Using measurements from multiple sensors (potentially different types of sensors) is an effective technique for reducing the uncertainty in downstream perception and estimation tasks" and name "sensor diversity" and "redundancy of individual sensors" as the two mechanisms. The same notes warn that fusion assumes conditional independence; where sources are correlated, the fused estimate is not more certain, only more confidently wrong. That is Fig 2 :44 verbatim in another discipline: "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Kish's design effect (Zipf and Valliant 2026) gives the formula: clustering increases the design effect and decreases the effective sample size, so a larger number of correlated observations buys less information, not more. **Mode `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 2, Fig 8, survey statistics (Kish) and engineering (sensor fusion). **Refuter:** demonstrate two frames drawing on the same evidence whose agreement still settles a question about the world, with a discriminator that shows the agreement is not the pool talking.

### F4: The channel decays while the ground does not (D2 and D1)

Fig 10 :24 (quoted in §0), Fig 10 :25–26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings", Fig 10 :27–28 **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**, and Fig 10 :29: "→ keep the anchor reachable". A statistical system is a channel. Its failure mode is not that the world changes; it is that the reading stops updating while everyone continues to treat the series as a verdict. The OECD Argentina review records that after 2016 "Publication of key data series was suspended for several months, with new and more reliable series then progressively coming on stream," and that data from 2007 to 2015 should be "used with caution." Cavallo, Cruces and Perez-Truglia (reported by UCLA Anderson Review, 2018) show the channel from the citizen side: "household inflation expectations aligned neatly with the alternative, unofficial inflation indices, which clocked in approximately 10 percentage points higher than the official data." Households kept a channel to a ground the official series had lost. **Mode `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 10, the OECD case and the expectations study (different instruments, low overlap). **Refuter:** show a series whose channel decayed but whose readings continued to track an independent ground at every slot, measured by an instrument not derived from the series itself.

### F5: A refusal seat is required, and it must not be a party (D4)

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Fig 10 :43: **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."** Fig 10 :86: **"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."** Fig 2 :46: **"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."** Fig 2 :47: **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."**

This rules out the common institutional fix in which the body that produced the figure also certifies it. An audit office that cannot refuse the figure, or a ministry that publishes both the number and the verdict on the number, is a party in the ground's seat. Fig 10 :59: **"The condition is that nothing about which value the ground returns today serves the occupant: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."** The seat authors conventions (shared meanings and definitions) but not verdicts: Fig 10 :46 "It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam." and Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". **Mode `specified`, with the seat's impossibility clause `general`. Single-source on the framework side (Fig 10), corroborated by the institutional cases and by the zero-knowledge analogy Fig 10 :84 records. Refuter:** show a checker that produced the claim and still verified it soundly under a protocol where the ground could move independently of both.

### F6: The habitat is enumerated, not composed (cross-cutting)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Applied here: you do not design an information system from a list of "good sources" (census, register, survey, big data). You enumerate everything in contact with the slot's residual: the household, the enumerator, the questionnaire wording, the price collector, the shop, the administrative form, the platform, the incentive, the minister who wants a number, the rival index, the court. Then you go outward until the cascade stops mattering. Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades." and the DDT example at Fig 11 :52–54 show why second-order effects matter: an instruction to record employment benefits as work is three steps from the published unemployment rate. The OECD review records exactly such a departure: "the person interviewed would be recorded as having worked for pay in the survey reference week whenever he/she received an employment benefit (irrespectively of how they answered the question on having worked in the week)." **Mode `general` structural, `specified` per slot. Triangulated** across Fig 11 and the OECD case. **Refuter:** show a standing list of sources that keeps its valence across a change of residual (for example across a shift from measuring prices to measuring inclusion), with the residual re-measured at each slot.

### F7: The chain probably breaks at vocabulary and objective, not at perception (cross-cutting)

Fig 3 :25: **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests."** Fig 3 :26 says the link between them, "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27 (quoted in §1): "A link with no instrument is not a link that works." Fig 3 :29: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

For government information the perception link is well instrumented (sample frames, response rates, price collection). The vocabulary link (does the system have a term for what a local condition affords?) and the objective link (what would count as progress for this measurement?) are not. A system can collect more data and still be unable to say what it is for. Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation"**. The World Bank Statistical Performance Indicators (2024) make the same point from development practice: "Statistics have value only if they are used. So the first pillar is data use." and their Data Sources pillar says the system must "draw on sources inside and outside the government." **Mode `specified`. Triangulated** across Fig 3, Fig 12 and the SPI framework (public administration, lower overlap). **Refuter:** show a system whose vocabulary and objective links are measured as reliably as its perception link, with the instrument used and the round trip recorded.

### F8: Outside data are imports, with a source gate and a shadow test (D3)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 6 :24: **"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."** Fig 8 :51: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**. Fig 8 :52: "and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

A household survey, a transaction trace and a satellite serially do not become reliable by being added to a dashboard. Each is an import from a frame with a different closure. It needs a provenance record (who collected it, under what incentive, with what wording) and a shadow test (does it explain a gap the government's own frame could not?). The World Bank SPI "Data Sources" pillar is the institutional version: sources go "beyond the typical censuses and surveys to include administrative and geospatial data as well as data generated by private firms and citizens." **Mode `specified`. Single-source on the figure side for the gate itself (Fig 6/8), corroborated by SPI practice. Refuter:** show an imported series that moved the wall (not merely added a column) with no provenance record and no gap it explained, and a ground reading confirming the wall moved.

### F9: No figure returns a recipe (cross-cutting, declared gap)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 9 :42: **"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** No figure line selects a census interval, a sample size, a privacy rule, a degree of decentralisation, or which of four information sources gets what weight. These are not hidden answers; they are a `gap` of the **shape-not-a-name** kind, and the honest report is the shape plus the instrument that would settle the name. This is a verdict, not an omission. It is `unreachable at this budget`, not shown to be underivable in principle (Fig 8 :40–43). **Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that names a concrete institution or numerical allocation for this residual, quoted verbatim with its line number, and show it governs without strain.

## 4. Strains and re-census (kept visible)

**S1, apparent Fig 2 versus Fig 11 conflict.** Fig 2 says an anchor must not update; Fig 11 says valence is relative to the residual ("An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries", Fig 11 :49) and that "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51). Does that make the anchor relative too? Re-census: no. Fig 11 governs **actors in a habitat**, whose usefulness flips when the residual changes. Fig 2 governs **the reference used to settle a question**, which is legitimate precisely when "the question is constitutively about it" (Fig 2 :36). Different objects. A price collector is an actor whose valence can flip; the transaction prices they record are an anchor for the price question. Strain dissolved by re-census, matching the canonical rule that a reading which makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

**S2, "human judgment is the right anchor" (Fig 2 :37–38) applied to local conditions.** Fig 2 :37 says "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct". A government's information question about local conditions is partly about people, so which is it? Re-census: the question "what are local conditions" is constitutively about measurable facts (counts, prices, employment, coverage), so a measurement, counter or market is the legitimate anchor for that sub-question (Fig 2 :36). The separate question "is this a good outcome for people" is constitutively about people and has human judgment as its anchor. Two sub-questions, two anchors, not one conflict. Kept visible because a careless reading would turn the anchor into a popularity poll.

**S3, Fig 12 two currencies (bits and actions).** Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** A richer measurement system costs bits (description, coding, classification) and actions (enumerating, auditing, fieldwork). Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** A proposal that lists only the data fields undercounts the action cost, and one that lists only the fieldwork undercounts the description cost. This is a live design constraint, not a defect; it is named and not smoothed into one "cost" figure.

**S4, the "shape, not a name" correction.** My first pass treated the rule as forbidding the naming of any concrete instrument. That overstates it. The canonical README's phrase is "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)", which governs a primitive **not held** in the library. Naming an available binding (a household survey, an administrative register) is provenance, not the invention of a seventh primitive. The correction is preserved in `05_what_shape_means.md` §5b and `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1, and it narrows F9 rather than removing it: the gap is the numerical recipe and the institutional layout, not the mention of real instruments.

After re-census: one live design constraint (S3), three dissolved strains (S1, S2, S4), none hidden.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Slot refuter.** A pooled national series is shown to retain the per-slot residual signal that Fig 1 :22 says aggregation destroys, measured by an instrument independent of the series.
2. **Anchor refuter.** A metric used to decide an outcome is demonstrated, with a moving ground and a discriminator, to have remained a non-updating anchor over repeated policy changes (breaks F2, Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47).
3. **Independence refuter.** Two frames drawing on the same evidence are shown to settle a question about the world, with a discriminator showing the agreement is not the shared pool talking (breaks F3, Fig 2 :40).
4. **Seat refuter.** A checker that produced the claim is shown to verify it as soundly as one that did not, under a protocol where the ground could move independently of both (breaks F5, Fig 10 :42).
5. **Union refuter.** The four conditions are shown to compose as a partition with no overlapping intersections, so one lever clears the whole residual (breaks Fig 9 :54–58).
6. **Instrument refuter.** A figure line is produced that names a concrete institution, ratio or sample size for this residual, quoted verbatim and governing without strain (breaks the declared gap F9).

If any fires, the corresponding shape claim is withdrawn and the retraction is recorded here, not silently erased. Until one is run, nothing in this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **State the residual per slot.** Replace "improve information quality" with "reduce `R` at this citizen, firm and locality slot" (F1).
2. **Protect the anchor, not the number.** An independent statistical function with a declared stake, a convention-authoring role, and a refusal seat is the figure-grounded requirement (F2, F5). The OECD Argentina case is the cost of not doing it.
3. **Buy independence deliberately.** Where the cost of being wrong is high, multiple frames with different evidence are a purchase, and purity is a quantity (F3, Fig 2 :42). Engineer correlations rather than assuming them; see the design-effect arithmetic.
4. **Keep the channel reachable.** Treat stale percepts and pooled readings as a loss of touch, not as a data-cleaning problem (F4, Fig 10 :24–29).
5. **Enumerate the habitat per residual.** Do not deploy a standing list of good sources (F6).
6. **Instrument the middle links.** Measure vocabulary and objective before buying more perception (F7, Fig 3 :27).
7. **Send a generator, not a recording.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Send the shape and the refuter, then the links, then the method.

## 8. Closing verdict of this audit

The figures return a **shape** for reliable government information, not an institution. The shape has four conditions in a union: residual indexed per slot; a reference that does not update on the report; independent frames whose agreement is triangulation rather than a shared pool; and a verification seat outside the slice it grades, able to refuse, with its unavoidable stake declared. The channel to the ground must be maintained, because the ground does not decay while the channel does. The middle links of Fig 3's chain (vocabulary and objective) are the ones most likely to be broken and least likely to be instrumented. No figure line returns a census design, sample size, agency chart or weighting scheme; those are a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, and the instrument that would close them is owed. Everything here is `specified` or `general` and simulated. Nothing is `grounded`.
