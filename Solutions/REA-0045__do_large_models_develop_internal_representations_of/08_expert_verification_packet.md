# Expert verification packet: the Tether on internal representations and reliable reasoning

**What this is:** a claim in a form an interpretability researcher or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question bundles a state claim (a model has internal states "of" objects, people, events) and a capability claim (those states support reliable reasoning). The state claim has no anchor of its own: probe accuracy and compression are frame-internal quantities, scored by the frame that produces them. The capability claim has a figure-derived shape: capability is a property of model-and-habitat, the error signal is indexed per object slot rather than averaged into one benchmark number, and an internal change that does not change contact changes nothing. An internal feature can name a state, but a route to a grounded goal needs ordered bonds the feature does not contain. A null interpretability result is not proof of absence. The internal name stays open until a ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the capability part of the question is decided by contact, per slot, against a ground, not by internal structure considered in isolation. The state part is a `gap`, not a "no".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Refuter:** hold reliable reasoning constant while removing contact with environment, tools and per-slot feedback. If capability survives, Fig 11 :40 is false here. Or show that a frame-internal quantity (probe accuracy, compression achieved) legitimately settles the question, which breaks Fig 1 :27.

### C2 - interpretability-facing; checkable against the probe literature

- **Statement:** a probe reading is an INWARD instrument, not a verdict. It can be non-selective (measuring the probe's own capacity) and a null result cannot prove absence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :34 "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- **What to check:** does a cited representation claim use control tasks (Hewitt and Liang 2019, "popular probes on ELMo representations are not selective"), and does it connect the decoded property to per-slot correctness on novel cases under an independent ground?
- **Refuter:** a sealed, complete search of the internal state space with a proven selector, after which absence would be provable. Fig 9 :44 says no frame is sealed; producing one would break the figure.

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** the question conflates an identity bond (a feature *is* the object representation) with a production bond (the feature *supports* reasoning). A state does not compose a route without priced, ordered bonds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Operators table: ≡ is "two names, one referent"; ⇒ is "A produces B; B did not exist before".
- **What to check:** apply the bond tests. Substituting the world object for the activation does not preserve meaning, so ≡ fails. The feature is present before the behaviour, which does not rule out ⇒, but presence alone does not establish it.
- **Refuter:** show that the identity and production readings are the same term under the six bond tests, or that feature presence alone predicts per-slot correctness on novel slots under an independent ground. Then the bond carries no extra information and the isomer claim fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a yes or no answer to the question as written. The literal C1 claim is a `gap`.
- Not that large models lack internal structure. Internal structure is well supported; its standing as a grounded representation of world entities is the open part.
- Not that a specific feature is or is not the representation of a specific object. That binding is unproven.
- Not that interpretability is useless. It is an INWARD instrument for sharpening.
- Not that a null result proves absence.
- Not that reasoning is impossible in models.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument and a ground, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does a selective probe or causal intervention remain necessary for per-slot correctness on novel slots with an independent anchor?
2. Does reliable reasoning survive the removal of contact with environment, tools and per-slot feedback?
3. Can an instrument distinguish among the several internal configurations that yield the same decode, so that the source rather than the reading is named?
