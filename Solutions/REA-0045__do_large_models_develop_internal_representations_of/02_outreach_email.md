**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 are stated plainly in your own words. The output must survive stage 1 or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** A shape claim about internal representations and reliable reasoning, with its refuter

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question and would like your verdict on one output before I show you any theory. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to trust the framework. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** do large models develop internal representations of objects, people, and events that support reliable reasoning? It joins two different claims. One is a state claim about model-internal geometry. The other is a capability claim about getting the right answer across cases, at the ground. They need separate checks and separate anchors.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the shape that survives a figure-grounded derivation is this. Capability is a property of model-and-habitat, never of the model alone, so reliable reasoning is decided by contact, not by internal structure considered in isolation. The relevant error signal is indexed per object slot, not averaged into one benchmark number. "Representation of objects, people, and events" names a cause inside the model, and the rule is to describe the effect rather than the cause until a ground settles the name. Finding no feature in activation space does not prove absence, and a probe trained on the same activations it is meant to explain is one frame, not two.
>
> **What would refute this shape claim, written before I sent this:**
> 1. a selective probe or intervention shown to be causally necessary for per-slot correctness on novel slots, with an anchor independent of the model's own outputs;
> 2. reliable reasoning measured to be unchanged when contact with the environment, tools, and per-slot feedback is removed;
> 3. a frame-internal quantity, such as probe accuracy or compression achieved, admitted as the thing that settles the question.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the internal representation, that a specific feature is the representation of a specific object, that a null interpretability result proves absence, or that any of this is `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a measurement. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay with the verbatim figure set: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - The canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
