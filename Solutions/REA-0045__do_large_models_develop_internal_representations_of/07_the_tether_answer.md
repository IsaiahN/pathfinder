# The Tether's answer on internal representations and reliable reasoning

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question joins two conditions. C1 asks whether a large model has internal states that are "of" objects, people and events. C2 asks whether those states support reliable reasoning. C1 is a state claim about model-internal geometry; C2 is a capability claim about ground contact. Fig 9 :54-58 makes this a union compound, not one hard question. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it is a specific compound with its own loop.

## 2. The shape

1. **Capability is a property of model-and-habitat, never of the model alone.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." So reliable reasoning is decided by contact, not by internal structure considered in isolation. The direct-field corroboration is ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), which reports that interacting with an external Wikipedia API overcomes hallucination and error propagation in chain-of-thought reasoning.
2. **The residual is indexed per object slot, not aggregated.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single benchmark number is the wrong unit for "objects, people, and events." The ground reading is per slot.
3. **"Representation" is a borrowed cause-word until grounded.** Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." The effect is per-slot correctness; the cause is a name. Calling an internal state a representation of a world object is an unproven binding from the ML frame, not a derivation.
4. **A state is not a route.** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." An internal feature names a state; reliable reasoning needs a plan (sequence), a goal (comparison) and progress (subtraction). The bond between them carries the claim and is not recoverable from the feature alone.
5. **Failed search is not absence.** Fig 6 :27: "some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." A probe that cannot decode an object, or decodes it non-selectively, does not settle whether the model has a ground-settled correspondence to it.
6. **The ground settles the name.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The anchor here is a per-slot outcome an environment or measurement that refuses, not the model's own benchmark output and not agreement among probes sharing activations (Fig 2 :40).

## 3. Verdicts per domain, kept separate

- **D1, model-internal structure: `gap` for C1 as literally posed.** No nameable anchor; the currency (probe accuracy, compression) is frame-internal and disqualified by Fig 1 :27. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). This is not a "no"; it is a refusal to score the frame with its own output.
- **D2, behaviour and ground contact: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 2 + Fig 1).** Reliable reasoning is a contact property of model-and-habitat, measured per slot against an unmovable ground.
- **D3, verification and instruments: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 8 + Fig 10).** Interpretability instruments sharpen within our own terms; they cannot by themselves ground a representation claim, and a null result is not absence.
- **D4, habitat and corpus: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 6 :23).** The corpus is compressed output, not the generator; whether an internal "of" relation forms depends on contact with the world that produced the traces.

These verdicts are never averaged into one yes or no.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that large models lack internal structure. The linear representation hypothesis and circuit work support the existence of internal structure; that is not the question.
- Not that a specific feature is or is not the representation of a specific object. That binding is unproven and held open.
- Not that interpretability is useless. It is an INWARD instrument for sharpening, kept separate from OUTWARD contact frames (Fig 6 :31).
- Not that a null interpretability result proves absence.
- Not that reasoning is impossible in models. The claim is about what would have to be observed to call it reliable and grounded.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles the per-slot causal claim in this simulated derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **A grounded internal link.** A selective probe or causal intervention, with control tasks, demonstrated necessary for per-slot correctness on novel slots, against an anchor independent of the model's own outputs. This would close C1 to C2.
2. **A contact test.** Reliable reasoning measured with and without contact with environment, tools and per-slot feedback. If it is invariant, Fig 11 :40 is false here.
3. **A discriminating instrument for source.** Following *The Hard Problem is About Source*: several internal configurations can yield the same decode, so what is owed is a spectrometer-like instrument that distinguishes them, not a name.
4. **Reformulate the falsifier so it can fire.** "The model has representations" is unfalsifiable as written. "Feature F is necessary for per-slot correctness on novel slots under an independent ground" is falsifiable.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures return a shape and a route; they do not return a yes or no to the question as written. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). The eight essays are a human's careful guess, and "another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six" (*They're Scanning Books*). The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether large models have internal representations that support reliable reasoning, because the question bundles two different claims. The state claim about internal geometry has no anchor of its own: probe accuracy and compression are scored by the same frame that produces them. The capability claim is answerable in shape: reliability is a property of model and habitat, measured per object slot against an unmovable ground, and an internal change that does not change contact changes nothing. A feature can name a state, but a route to a goal needs bonds the feature does not contain. So the framework offers the shape any grounded answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The internal name stays open until the ground pays for it.
