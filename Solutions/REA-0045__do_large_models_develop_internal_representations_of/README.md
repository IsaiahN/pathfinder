## What an answer would look like

Restated as an effect: the question joins two conditions. C1 is a state claim about model-internal geometry (are there internal states that are "of" objects, people, events?). C2 is a capability claim about ground contact (do those states cause reliable reasoning?). An answer must say what would be observed for each condition to hold or fail, must name the anchor that settles each, and must not let a frame-internal quantity score the frame that produces it. Bound as an ordinary term: the sought-for shape is "a per-slot ground claim about model-and-habitat, with the internal-state name held as an unproven binding." Writing it down lets it accrue a residual like anything else (formula step, Direction).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
2. **A shared evidence pool is one witness.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." This applies to this derivation itself: its second seat is simulated and shares the pool.
3. **Do not hand the frame's interior to a verifier.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Stage 1 of the offer carries the residual and its refuter, never the theory.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid both the structural failure and the legal one.
