# Supporting derivation: union, comparison and cascade order

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a total order over five interaction types by strength of shaping resilience. Three supporting questions are owed before the main verdict can be stated:

1. Is the question a partition (one type per case, so types can be compared as separate candidates) or a union (types co-occur, so the case is a compound)?
2. What is the structure of a comparison between two interaction types, and what currency makes it well formed?
3. What is the order of contact by which an effect reaches the residual?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this question: the five named interaction types as *relations*, the species acting in them, the conditions of the habitat, a perturbation and a resilience metric. Bonds held: ⋛ comparison (ordered), → sequence (ordered), + conjunction (symmetric), − subtraction, ¬ negation. No standing strength order is available without an import (Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*).

- **D1 relations and habitat:** currency is contact; anchor is the ground (what survives and recovers).
- **D2 metric:** currency is residual per slot; anchor is a declared measurement.
- **D3 cascade:** currency is contact cascades; anchor is the ground several steps out.
- **D4 verification:** currency is agreement and residual; anchor is the ground, never agreement.

Dependency order: anchor and metric before enumeration; enumeration before cascade; all three before the comparison.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

A community is not partitioned into predation-only, competition-only and mutualism-only cases. The same plant can be pollinated, eaten and competed with at once, which the world literature calls a multilayer network (Domínguez-García and Kéfi 2024). Under Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

The unbundling is by residual and context, not by interaction name. Therefore the "five candidates" are not five mutually exclusive hypotheses. They are five layers of one compound, and a single lever applied to one layer clears the region where only that condition holds while leaving the intersections standing. The ranking question is therefore **not a well-formed partition**.

*Falsifier written before the run:* show communities in which exactly one interaction type is present and the question can be answered on each type separately with the same metric. If all five can be isolated without changing the residual, the partition reading is restored and F5 fails.

### Q2 - What is the comparison, and in what currency?

Fig 12 :86: **"a threshold relation between two quantities."**
Fig 12 :89: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."**

"Most strongly" is ⋛, an ordered comparison. Two requirements follow:

1. **Two operands.** The comparison is binary. A five-way ranking is four such comparisons composed, and the composition must be transitive to yield a total order. Nothing in the figures guarantees transitivity across ecological contexts; in fact Fig 9 :55-58 predicts the opposite (compounds that are categorically different from their parts).
2. **One currency.** The operands must be quantities of the same kind. Attack rates, competition coefficients, per-capita mutualistic benefits and facilitation intensities are different quantities. A common currency (for example, marginal change in recovery rate per unit change in an interaction strength) must be imported and provenanced (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*). The world literature confirms that this import is contested: Suweis et al. 2014 argue that the stabilising effect of mixing interaction types arises from *"the peculiar rescaling of the interaction strengths they imposed."* That conflict is a reason to declare the currency, not a reason to pick a winner.

Strain, named: the figure's comparison line governs two commensurable quantities; the question supplies heterogeneous relations. This is the F8 strain and it remains live.

*Falsifier:* produce a single ground-paying currency for all five types under which the pairwise comparisons are transitive. If produced, the strain is cleared and the ranking becomes well formed.

### Q3 - What is the order of contact?

Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The order is → (sequence): first-order contacts, then second-order, then third-order, stopping when the cascade stops mattering. This is not a ranking of interaction types; it is an ordering of *reach from the residual*. The DDT case is the figure's own instance: the dominant residual was carried by a path several steps out, not by a named pairwise interaction at first order.

The world literature independently reports the same structure. Pringle 2016 writes that *"indirect interactions ... are ubiquitous in ecological communities and can rival direct interactions in their strength."* Domínguez-García and Kéfi 2024 report that plant importance in the whole community can be *"emergent"*, resembling neither single-layer ranking. Both corroborate the sequence, neither supplies a champion.

### Q4 - Are the five types isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Treating predation, competition, mutualism, parasitism and facilitation as five *atoms* is a category error: under Fig 11 :50 they are relations, which are bonds with signs and contexts, not atoms. So the isomer question does not apply to the five names as written. It applies to two *communities* built over the same species set with different arrangements of relations: those are genuine isomers under Fig 12 :117, same species atoms, different bond arrangement, different resilience behaviour. This is the useful re-cast of the original question.

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"** licenses treating an empirically characterised community as an atom at the next level, once the ground settles it. Nothing here is settled, so that promotion is not performed.

*Falsifier:* exhibit two communities over the same species set with identical relation arrangements and different resilience under the same perturbation. That would break the isomer reading and return the difference to unenumerated conditions.

## CONCLUSION

1. The question is a **union**, not a partition. Split by residual and context (Q1).
2. "Most strongly" is a ⋛ comparison requiring one currency and transitive composition; neither is supplied, and the currency import is contested in the literature (Q2). Live strain, named.
3. The operative order is **cascade reach from the residual**, first to third order and outward until it stops mattering, not a rank of interaction names (Q3).
4. The five names are not isomers; two communities over the same species set with different relation arrangements are (Q4).
5. Standing falsifiers: isolate all five types without changing R; produce a transitive one-currency comparison; show a direct interaction that dominates all indirect paths; break the community-isomer reading with identical arrangements and different outcomes.
