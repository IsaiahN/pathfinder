## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census before comparing with the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: the surface form is five candidates against one outcome, but each candidate is a signed relation (predation and parasitism deliver benefits to one party and costs to another; competition and facilitation differ by sign and by whether the partner is needed). The true arity is therefore (relation, sign, residual, context). Symmetry: a superlative is an ordered comparison, not a symmetric co-presence; it needs one axis along which "more" is defined. Scale: a pairwise effect, a community-wide network effect, and a cross-habitat cascade are three different scales, and a superlative silently mixes them.
- **Domain i, the sign and relation layer.** Actors: the five relations and the species in them. Currency: signed effect on a recipient. Anchor: none by itself; sign alone settles nothing about resilience. This is where the primary's F1 lives.
- **Domain ii, the outcome definition layer.** Actors: state, perturbation, recovery trajectory, metric, slots. Currency: residual per slot. Anchor: a measurement that does not update on frames. Without this domain, "most strongly" has no object.
- **Domain iii, the propagation layer.** Actors: indirect paths, third parties, cross-habitat fluxes. Currency: contact reach outward. Anchor: the ground at several steps out. Union with domain i, because a direct-only scope misses the DDT case.
- **Domain iv, the witness layer.** Actors: external checker, residual, refuter. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run its independence is zero.

Order by dependency: define and anchor the outcome (ii) before enumerating relations (i); enumerate before propagation (iii); all three before the witness (iv). Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

Question type: union, not partition. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

My four domains map one-to-one onto the primary's D1 to D4, but I reached them from a different starting point: I began with the sign structure of the five relations and the superlative's need for one axis (Fig 12 :86), whereas the primary began with the habitat's valence rule (Fig 11 :49-51).
