# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the ranking

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are stated plainly and checked. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would count as a failure for this claim about species interactions and resilience?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as asked:** which of predation, competition, mutualism, parasitism and facilitation most strongly shape ecosystem resilience?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is not well posed as a ranking, and here is the concrete reason. An interaction's effect is relative to a specific resilience failure, not a standing property of the interaction. Fig 11 :50 says *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds,"* and Fig 11 :49 says *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Resilience also has to be read per slot: Fig 1 :22 says *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Finally, the effect that matters can be indirect: Fig 11 :52 gives the DDT case, where *"the residual that mattered was three steps out."* So a single champion across all failures, slots and habitats is not a fact the figures supply. The figure-derived shape is a procedure: name the failure and its metric, enumerate every interaction and indirect contact in cascade order, then identify which alteration most reduces the residual.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that competition always wins, or mutualism, or predation. I am not claiming that any of the five has a fixed rank. World literature on context dependence and multi-interaction network robustness supports the *mechanism*, but it is secondary here and it does not decide the verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a standing total order of the five types that is invariant across residuals and habitats;
> 2. an anchor measurement of resilience that does not move when the observing frame changes, under which a stable one-currency order appears across two independent perturbations;
> 3. a case where one direct named pairwise interaction carries strictly more of the residual than every second- or third-order contact path;
> 4. an interaction-only improvement that moves the ground while leaving contact unchanged.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the materials. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Local verbatim figure text used for every quote: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Wider literature that bears on the mechanism: [Domínguez-García & Kéfi 2024, PLOS Computational Biology](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1011770), [Pringle 2016, PLOS Biology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5061325/), [Mougi & Kondoh 2012, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22822151).
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
