## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Context dependence kills the fixed-rank premise.** Pringle 2016 and Chamberlain et al. 2014, from plant-microbe systems and a 247-article meta-analysis respectively, independently report that interaction sign and magnitude depend on context. This is the world-side counterpart of Fig 11 :49-51. Low overlap between the two fields, so this is corroboration, not one pool counted twice.
2. **Interaction types jointly determine robustness, not one at a time.** Domínguez-García and Kéfi 2024 found that the robustness of a two-layer network is a combination of the robustness of its two layers and that keystone status can be *"emergent"* at the whole-community level. This matches Fig 9's union reading and Fig 11's cascade warning.
3. **Diversity of types, not dominance of one type, is the stability mechanism proposed.** Mougi and Kondoh 2012 report that a moderate mixture of antagonistic and mutualistic interactions stabilises dynamics. Note the conflict in the literature: Suweis et al. 2014 (Oikos, abstract read via search integration) argues that this result *"arise[s] as an artifact of the peculiar rescaling of the interaction strengths they imposed."* I record the conflict rather than resolving it, and send the question back to the domain census, which already says that a currency must be declared before comparison (F8). The conflict corroborates the figure verdict that the ranking is underdetermined, not any winner.
4. **The anchor problem is live in ecology.** Zhang et al. 2022 state that no approach existed to compare distances to tipping points across mutualistic systems before their dimension-reduction method. This corroborates D2: without a common metric, "most strongly" cannot be settled.

None of these sources is cited as deciding the verdict. Figures decide; world sources corroborate or conflict, and a conflict returns us to the census.
