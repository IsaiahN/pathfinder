# Expert verification packet: species interactions and ecosystem resilience

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or network scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No ecological measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a refusal, not a winner.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which species interaction most strongly shapes ecosystem resilience?" is not well posed as a standing ranking. The five named items are relations whose sign and magnitude are relative to a specific residual, not kinds carrying a fixed strength. The habitat is enumerated rather than ranked, the residual must be read per species or patch rather than as one global average, and the dominant effect can lie in a second- or third-order contact cascade. The figure-derived shape is a procedure: name the resilience failure and its metric, enumerate all interactions and indirect contacts outward, and identify which alteration most reduces the residual. A comparison across types additionally needs one declared currency, which neither the question nor the figures supply. This is offered as a checkable shape with refuters written in advance, not as a conclusion about which interaction wins.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus ecology-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and your own field knowledge

- **Statement:** the candidates are relations relative to a residual, not fixed kinds, so no standing strength order follows from them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Also Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* And Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is the claim faithful to how interaction outcomes behave in your system? The world-side counterpart is Pringle 2016 (*"It is common to speak instead of the mutualism-parasitism continuum"*) and Chamberlain et al. 2014, whose meta-analysis of 247 articles found *"mutualism was most likely to change sign across contexts."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a standing total order of the five types by resilience effect that is invariant across residuals and habitats.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the ranking question is a bundled union, the residual is per slot, and the dominant path can be indirect; therefore a single global champion is not a well-formed fact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :52-53 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** does the world literature show multi-layer co-occurrence and emergent whole-community importance? Domínguez-García and Kéfi 2024 report that plant importance in the whole community can be *"emergent"*, resembling neither single-layer ranking.
- **Refuter:** show a case where one direct named pairwise interaction carries strictly more of the residual than every second- or third-order contact path, under the same anchor.

### C3 - the live strain; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** "most strongly" is a ⋛ comparison between two quantities, so a five-way ranking needs one commensurable currency and a transitive composition; neither is supplied, and the currency import is contested.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :86 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Fig 12 :89 - *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- **What to check:** can you name one ground-paying currency in which attack rates, competition coefficients, mutualistic benefits and facilitation intensities are commensurable, and under which the four pairwise comparisons are transitive? Note the conflict in the literature: Suweis et al. 2014 argue that the stabilising effect of mixing interaction types in Mougi and Kondoh 2012 *"arise[s] as an artifact of the peculiar rescaling of the interaction strengths they imposed."*
- **Refuter:** produce that currency and show the order is stable across two independent perturbation types. Until then this strain is recorded, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any one of the five interaction types is the strongest shaper.
- Not that the five types are unimportant. They are the relations the enumeration must include.
- Not that resilience is unmeasurable. An anchor is required, and candidate instruments exist.
- Not that all ecological contexts differ. A context-invariant order is refuter 1 and would withdraw the claim.
- Not a derivation from Fig 13's physical six-term set. That reading was retracted (R1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- Not `grounded`. No ecological measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The four pre-registered refuters are in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4 and `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. In short: an invariant standing order; an anchor with a stable one-currency order; a direct interaction dominating all indirect paths; or an interaction-only improvement that moves the ground. Candidate instruments already in the literature are scaled recovery rates and tipping-point distances (Zhang et al. 2022), multilayer robustness (Domínguez-García and Kéfi 2024), and context-resolved interaction outcomes (Pringle 2016; Chamberlain et al. 2014).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-52, Fig 1 :19 and :22, and Fig 12 :86 and :89. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own study system: have you seen the same partner species change sign with context?
3. Check C2: in a network you know, does the loss that matters sit at first order or several steps out?
4. Check C3: name a currency that would make the five types comparable, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
