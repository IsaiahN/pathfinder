## 0. The claim under audit and the residual in effect terms

> Which species interactions (predation, competition, mutualism, parasitism, or facilitation) most strongly shape ecosystem resilience?

Stated as an effect, the question asks: **if we declare one interaction type the strongest shaper of resilience, what follows?** The practical consequence is that monitoring, restoration and intervention budgets are allocated to that type. So an answer is not a label; it is a ranking that will be acted on. The residual R is the observed mismatch between a predicted recovery of an ecosystem after a perturbation and the recovery actually measured, together with the unstated demand for a total order over five interaction types.

Two things must be separated before any figure line is chosen:

- **The object of the question.** A ranking of *kinds*, each carrying a fixed strength of shaping.
- **What the figures say the object is.** Interactions are *relations* whose valence depends on a residual, and the habitat is *enumerated*, not ranked (Section 2).

The residual is therefore **not** "which of five things is best." It is "what would have to be true for a stable ranking of five relations to be meaningful across residuals and habitats?" That is the question the figures can address.

An answer would look like: a total order over {predation, competition, mutualism, parasitism, facilitation} under one declared currency of resilience, stable across perturbations and habitats, with a refuter written in advance. Binding that shape as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction") lets it accrue a residual like anything else, rather than remaining an invisible preference.

## 2. Domain census before choosing a figure line

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** the question presents five candidate kinds and asks for a total order. Under Fig 11 :50 the candidates are not kinds but relations, so the true arity is a triple (relation, residual, context), not five scalars. The comparison is over triples, not over names.
- **Symmetry:** "most strongly shapes" is a ⋛ comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :86), tested by *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* (Fig 12 :89). It is an ordered bond, and it requires a single currency in both operands. The five interactions are measured in different currencies (attack rate, competition coefficient, per-capita benefit, transmission, facilitation intensity), so the comparison is not well formed until a currency is declared. This is a strain, recorded in F8, not a hidden assumption.
- **Scale:** pairwise direct interaction is first order; Fig 11 :52 reports a residual that mattered *"three steps out."* The ranking question silently conflates first-order and cascade-order effects.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The habitat and its relations** | species, predation, competition, mutualism, parasitism, facilitation, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain) | contact, enumerated (Fig 11 :43-44) | the ground: what survives and recovers | **union.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* |
| **D2 The resilience measurement** | ecosystem state, perturbation, recovery trajectory, metric | residual per slot (Fig 1 :19) | a measurement that does not move when a frame changes (Fig 2 :29) | partition boundary: this domain decides what is being measured, not which interaction wins |
| **D3 The cascade layer** | indirect paths, third species, cross-habitat fluxes, biomagnification | contact cascades (Fig 11 :52) | the ground several steps out | union: it overlaps D1 and cannot be separated from it by direct-contact scoping |
| **D4 Verification and contact** | expert readers, the residual, the refuter | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35) | separate seat; in this simulated run it shares the primary's evidence pool, so its independence is zero |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. D2 has one only when a metric is declared (recovery rate, distance to a tipping point, area under an extinction curve). D1 and D3 have the ground as anchor. D4 has the ground in principle but not in this simulated run.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

*"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"* (Fig 3 :20). The order is: anchor R and state its metric (D2), enumerate the habitat and its relations (D1), then enumerate cascades outward (D3), then ask an outside seat to check (D4). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A ranking attempted before D2 is a reading below the break.

**Partition or union:** union, not partition. The five interaction types co-occur in the same community; the question is a bundled union and must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). This is the structural reason a single ranking is not a well-formed fact.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The candidates are relations, not kinds (decisive)

Fig 11 :50:

> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :51:

> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

The question asks which *kind* most strongly shapes resilience. The figure says the named items are relations whose sign and magnitude are relative to R. A total order over kinds is therefore not a property the world supplies; it is a property the questioner would have to import. No figure line supplies it. Verdict: `gap` for the ranking as asked.

*Refuter:* exhibit a standing order of the five types by resilience effect that is invariant across residuals and habitats, measured at the ground. If one exists, F1 fails.

### F2 - The habitat is enumerated, never composed

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :47 gives the kind of list the figure means: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency."* It is an enumeration of what to look for, not a strength ordering. Fig 11 :48 adds: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* The figure's own operative instruction is to enumerate, not to rank. No line ranks predation above competition or mutualism above facilitation.

*Refuter:* show a figure line that assigns a standing strength order to ecological relations. None was found after a full census of Figures 1 to 13 and both tables.

### F3 - Resilience must be indexed per slot, not averaged

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The phrase ecosystem resilience used as one global number is exactly the average the figure warns against. A single species or patch can carry the live residual while the global metric reads near zero (Fig 1 :13). The superlative therefore hides the question of which slot failed and what failed there. The ranking cannot be well posed until the slot is named.

*Refuter:* provide a measurement in which one interaction type dominates every slot simultaneously, under a global metric that does not hide any live slot.

### F4 - The anchor test

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

For "most strongly" to be a verdict rather than a preference, an anchor is required: a recovery rate, a distance to a tipping point, an area under an extinction curve, or an equivalent measurement that does not move when an ecologist changes their mind. Without one, the ranking is a weighted average of influential frames, and agreement about it is disqualified as evidence (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

*Refuter:* name an anchor that (a) does not update on the frames, (b) is constitutively about resilience, and (c) yields the same total order under two independent perturbations. Until that is done, no ranking is triangulated.

### F5 - This is a union compound, not one hard fact

Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The five interactions overlap in the same community, and each can dominate under a different subset of conditions. That is the signature of a union, not of one scalar fact with five candidates. Splitting the question is the figure's instruction, not a concession. The correct split is by residual and context: for each specified failure (for example, failure of pollinator recovery after drought), ask which relations carry the residual.

*Refuter:* show that disagreements over the ranking shrink with effort toward a single order across all contexts. Under the figure, a disagreement that does not shrink with effort is not one hard question.

### F6 - Contact cascades can carry the residual

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

Even after an anchor is named and the habitat enumerated to first order, the dominant shaper may be a second- or third-order pathway that is not any of the five named pairwise types. The DDT case is the figure's own example: the residual that mattered was three steps out. So the ranking question is unstable not only across residuals (F1) but across the order of contact (F6).

*Refuter:* exhibit a case where a direct, named pairwise interaction carries strictly more of the residual than every second- and third-order path, under the same anchor.

### F7 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, not interaction-alone

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Resilience is a property of the ecosystem together with its habitat, not of a single interaction isolated from the habitat. This is the positive structural claim the figure makes in place of the ranking: what makes a system capable is the configuration of contact, which is enumerated, not the possession of one favoured relation.

*Refuter:* an agent-only or interaction-only improvement that demonstrably moves the ground. The figure names this as the refutation of its capability claim.

### F8 - Comparison needs one currency (strain, named)

Fig 12 :86: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."*
Fig 12 :89: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*

"Most strongly" invokes the comparison bond ⋛, which compares two quantities. The five interactions are not two quantities and are not measured in one currency. To make the ranking well formed one must first choose a common currency (for example, marginal change in recovery rate per unit change in interaction strength), which is itself a modelling commitment and an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*). It is not supplied by the question or by the figures. I record this as a **strain**, not a smooth fit: the comparison line governs a different quantity (two commensurable operands) than the question supplies.

*Refuter:* provide a single, declared, ground-paying currency in which all five interaction types are commensurable and the order is stable. Absent that, F8 stands.

### F9 - What survives: a shape, not a ranking

The residue worth keeping, with mode attached:

| claim | mode | figure |
|---|---|---|
| Interactions are relations whose valence is relative to a residual, not fixed kinds | `general` (structural) | Fig 11 :49-51 |
| The habitat is enumerated, not composed or ranked | `general` (structural) | Fig 11 :43-44 |
| Resilience must be read per slot, not as one average | `general` (structural) | Fig 1 :19, :22 |
| The ranking as asked is a bundled union and must be split | `specified` (falsifiable) | Fig 9 :54-57 |
| For a declared residual, enumerate contacts outward through cascades and identify which alteration most reduces R | `specified` (procedure) | Fig 11 :44, :52 |
| A single global champion across all residuals and habitats does not exist under these figures | `specified`, ungrounded | F1, F2, F5, F6 |

This is a **shape of the answer** (a procedure and a refusal), not the answer itself. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing discipline. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds at the ground:

1. A standing total order of the five interaction types by resilience effect is invariant across residuals and habitats, i.e. one type dominates in every context. (Refutes F1, F2, F5.)
2. An anchor measurement exists that does not move when the observing frame changes, under which a total order in one currency is stable across at least two independent perturbation types. (Refutes F3, F4, F8.)
3. A case is shown where a direct named pairwise interaction carries strictly more of the residual than every second- or third-order contact path. (Refutes F6.)
4. resilience is shown to be a property of an isolated interaction in isolation from its habitat that moves the ground. (Refutes F7.)

If any fires, the `gap` verdict is withdrawn and the ranking is re-opened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the ranking as asked. State the residual in effect terms and name the slot and metric first (F3, F4).
2. Re-cast the question as: for this specified resilience failure, enumerate all interactions and indirect contacts, then identify which alteration most reduces R (F2, F6, F9).
3. Split the union by context and residual before searching (F5). A single champion is a bundled artefact.
4. Lead any expert contact with the residual and the four refuters above, never with the framework (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
5. Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*). Tier or impact estimates may decide what gets studied first; they cannot decide which interaction wins.

## 7. Updated verdict

The ranking as asked is a `gap`: no figure line governs a standing order among the five interaction types, and three independent figure constraints (relational valence, per-slot residual, cascade reach) make such an order unstable. The figure-derived shape is positive: for a specified resilience failure, enumerate the habitat and its contacts outward, name the slot and metric, and identify which alteration of which relation most reduces R. World literature corroborates the mechanism (context dependence, multi-interaction network robustness, diversity of interaction types) but does not and cannot supply the ranking, because it is drawn from the same evidence pool as the question's presupposition. Strains after re-census: one, the comparison currency in F8, named and sent to D2. Nothing here is `grounded`.
