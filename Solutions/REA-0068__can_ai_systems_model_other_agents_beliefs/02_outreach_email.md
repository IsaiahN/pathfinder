# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide whether the distinction matters

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no source-discriminating measurement for real agents, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

**Subject:** Check one claim about prediction versus understanding in AI before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about AI systems that model other agents. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** predicting another agent's behaviour and attributing the belief or intention behind it are two different quantities with two different anchors. Prediction is about behaviour, so held-out behaviour can settle it. Attribution is about a latent that is not the behaviour, and held-out behaviour is only an instrument for it. A model can therefore get the behaviour right and the belief wrong, with the same outward reading in both cases. Two different latent states can produce identical behaviour, so the behaviour alone cannot say which source was present.
>
> The claim is that these are two conditions, not two degrees of one problem. A system that fails prediction needs a different instrument from a system that fails source discrimination. The further claim is that agreement between models, or a high score on a theory-of-mind benchmark, does not separate the two, because models drawing on the same corpus and the same benchmark are one witness wearing several names.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the agent's actual belief from its behaviour, that any particular model does or does not understand, that this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of theory of mind. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name. For synthetic agents whose latent states are recorded as ground truth, the gap is closable in principle. For real agents I do not hold a source-discriminating instrument, and I say so.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a controlled experiment on real agents in which behaviour is held fixed, the latent is varied, ground truth is recorded independently, and a model's attribution tracks the latent rather than the behaviour;
> 2. a transform between two frames that runs without a pose, or with a pose supplied from ground, and is checked against the agent's ground rather than the model's output;
> 3. one behavioural accuracy score that predicts, prospectively and out of distribution, which named agent-slot will be misattributed;
> 4. a verification procedure in which a checker that can reconstruct the attribution still verifies it against ground and catches a wrong one;
> 5. a case where a system's latent-tracking capability changes while its contact and habitat are held fixed.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |
| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Anchor matched to the question | *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* (Fig 2 :36). Behaviour settles prediction, not attribution |
| Two conditions kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a blended ToM score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Pre-registered refuter | *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| No name for the agent's belief | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32); the source essay says the answer is *"unreached until a better instrument exists"* |
| Benchmarks labelled as filters | *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* (Fig 9 :60) |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Once the recipient has the framework, their later agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40) |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-23) |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) |
| Ask the user for their own refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; make the sought-for shape an ordinary term |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A Track B style reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
