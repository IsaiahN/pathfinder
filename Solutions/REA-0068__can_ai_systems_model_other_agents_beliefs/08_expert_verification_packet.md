# Expert verification packet - the Tether on modelling other agents' beliefs

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cognitive scientist, comparative psychologist or NLP researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No source-discriminating measurement is claimed for real agents. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

**Boundary of the check.** The first three sections are the whole stage-1 ask. Provenance, links and the method offer come later and only if a verdict on stage 1 is recorded first.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Predicting another agent's behaviour and attributing the belief or intention behind it are two different quantities with two different anchors. Prediction is constitutively about behaviour, so held-out behaviour can settle it. Attribution is constitutively about a latent that is not the behaviour, so behaviour is only an instrument for it, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. Two different latent states can produce identical behaviour, so the same reading is consistent with several sources, and a model may get the action right while being wrong about the belief. The model stays inside its own closure; reaching the other frame's latent needs either contact with a differing closure or an instrument that discriminates sources. Agreement between models on a shared benchmark is a budget filter, not a witness. For synthetic agents whose latent states are recorded as ground truth, the gap is closable in principle. For real agents it is declared, with a source-discriminating experiment written down as the way to close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 1 and 12

- **Statement:** the anchor must match what the question is constitutively about; behaviour underdetermines the latent source.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :38 - *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
  - Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
  - Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Symbols table, entry `b`: the belief is *"Not the observation."*
- **Refuter:** a behaviour reading from which the latent source is uniquely recoverable under a declared code, demonstrated on two latents that produce the same behaviour; or a controlled real-agent experiment in which behaviour is held fixed, the latent is varied with independent ground truth, and a model's attribution tracks the latent above chance. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one field-facing check

- **Statement:** access to another frame's latent is an import or instrument problem, not more internal search; there is no known pose-free frame transform.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 6 :40 - *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."*
  - Symbols table, `F`: *"The map between allocentric and egocentric at one scale. Distinct from T_A and T_E, which run between scales. Cannot be run without a pose."* Frame entry: *"there is no known transform between two of them."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Rabinowitz et al. 2018 write that their ToMnet builds models *"from observations of their behaviour alone."* Pang et al. 2025 write that *"successfully solving isolated ToM tasks is insufficient evidence to indicate the presence of ToM."* Sclar et al. 2023 report *"spurious patterns in existing theory of mind benchmarks."* Do these three sentences not already separate a behaviour-built model from a source-validated one, and is the remedy in each case not an instrument or a differing closure rather than more of the same composition? A "no" to both questions is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that a model added an atom corresponding to the agent's latent purely by internal composition with no ground-truth latent entering the loop; or a pose-free frame transform checked against the agent's ground. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - Verification and competing explanations

- **Statement:** benchmarks and agreement are filters, not witnesses; the verifier must be outside the attribution; "understanding" is an afterwards label on a lineage that met its ground.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 7 :13 - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
  - Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
  - Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43 - *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."*
  - Fig 11 :36 - *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."*
- **What a researcher can check:** Sap et al. report that *"even ChatGPT and GPT-4 do not display emergent Theory of Mind"* and give 60 percent accuracy on ToMi mental-state questions. Is that a measurement of C1 on a particular room, and does it leave C2 untouched? A documented case where that score prospectively predicts which agent-slot will be misattributed would refute the reading.
- **Refuter:** one accuracy score that predicts out-of-distribution attribution errors per named agent; or a verification procedure in which a checker that can reconstruct the attribution still catches a wrong one against ground; or a change in latent-tracking capability with contact held fixed.

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about mind, and no final or unique theory claim.

## 5. What would settle it

A controlled latent-manipulation study that separates C1 and C2 per agent-slot. Concretely:

1. **Behaviour test:** is the model's predicted behaviour against held-out o′ near zero per slot? That settles C1 only. Fig 1 :19, :22.
2. **Source-discriminator test:** with behaviour held fixed, vary the latent and record ground truth independently. Does the model's attribution follow the latent? That settles C2. This is the instrument the source essay says is owed.
3. **Provenance test:** is there a record of where the latent came from, so convergent derivation and adopted import can be told apart? Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*
4. **Outside-checker test:** can a checker that did not build the attribution verify it against ground? Fig 10 :42.
5. **Out-of-distribution test:** do results survive radically different patterns, not just novel particulars, as Pang et al. request?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :36, :38, Fig 12 :5, :117, Fig 6 :21, :23, Fig 7 :13, Fig 10 :42 and Fig 11 :36. Nine short passages, about nine minutes.
2. Check C1 against your own field: name one case where two different latent states give the same behaviour reading and the reading alone does not determine the source. About four minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would vary a latent while holding behaviour fixed with ground truth recorded, or say why none exists for your species or system. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
