# The Tether's answer on modelling other agents' beliefs

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading. No source-discriminating measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would explain how the gap between what a model predicts an agent will do and what the agent actually does can be near zero while the model is wrong about the belief or intention that produced the behaviour. R is indexed per agent and per mental-state slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about the difference between two quantities, not about the sophistication of any model.

The union must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is two conditions, not one hard question:

- **C1:** behavioural prediction is good, meaning R against observed behaviour o′ is small per slot.
- **C2:** source attribution is right, meaning the model's latent tracks the agent's latent under controlled variation with ground truth recorded.

## 2. The shape

1. **The two halves have different anchors.** Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Prediction is constitutively about behaviour, so held-out behaviour settles it. Attribution is constitutively about a latent that the Symbols table says is *"Not the observation."* Behaviour is only an instrument for it, and *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :38). Using the prediction anchor for the attribution question is the specific error.
2. **The source is not in the reading.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Two latent states can be isomers under Fig 12 :117: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance"*, invisible in the behaviour and decisive later. So the same behavioural reading is consistent with several sources.
3. **The model stays in its own closure.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Access to the other frame's latent is an import or an instrument problem, not a matter of more internal search. Fig 6 :40: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."*
4. **There is no known frame transform without a pose.** The Symbols table defines `F` as *"The map between allocentric and egocentric at one scale"* and says it *"Cannot be run without a pose"*; the frame entry says *"there is no known transform between two of them."* This is a structural limit of the held figure set, not a proof of impossibility. A pose-bearing transform is a live refuter.
5. **Agreement is not evidence.** Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; :36: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A benchmark score is a filter. Pang et al. 2025 put the same demand in the field's own words: *"successfully solving isolated ToM tasks is insufficient evidence to indicate the presence of ToM."*
6. **A model cannot verify its own understanding.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."* The checker must be outside the attribution it grades.
7. **"Understanding" is an afterwards label.** Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The answerable question is what contact and what instrument make latent tracking possible, not whether a model deserves the label.
8. **The honest stop is a declared gap for real agents.** No anchor independent of a source-discriminating instrument is nameable for C2 on real agents. For synthetic agents with recorded latent ground truth the gap is closable in principle: the experimenter's latent is an import from a closure that differs. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No controlled latent manipulation has paid for this shape.
- Not a name. It does not say which model does or does not understand, which architecture is right, or which mental-state label is correct. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not an average. It makes no claim from a benchmark leaderboard. Such numbers are filters for budget, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a claim that behavioural prediction is worthless. It is well posed and useful; it is simply a different quantity (Fig 2 :36).
- Not a claim that understanding is impossible. The real-agent gap is at the instrument boundary; the synthetic case shows the instrument can exist.
- Not corroboration by agreement. Rabinowitz, Sap, Sclar and Pang share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :31, :40). Bender and Koller and Schwob et al. corroborate the mechanism shape from different fields.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the reading is the model's behavioural accuracy; the candidate sources are a true latent belief, a false belief, a learned policy, a transient percept, or simple pattern matching.

The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. a controlled real-agent experiment holding behaviour fixed while varying the latent with independent ground truth, where the model's attribution tracks the latent and not the behaviour;
2. a frame transform that runs without a pose, or with a pose from ground, and is checked against the agent's ground;
3. one accuracy score that prospectively predicts which named agent-slot will be misattributed out of distribution;
4. a checker that can reconstruct the attribution yet still catches a wrong one against ground;
5. a change in latent-tracking capability with contact and habitat held fixed.

A study that records C1 and C2 separately per agent, manipulates the latent, and reports which condition failed before the attribution error would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded` for that habitat.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops where the instruments stop and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is a source discriminator, characterized in effect terms and owed. Naming a belief from a reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The eight points in section 2 are a shape; the ninth thing, the agent's actual belief, is not held for real agents and is not pretended. The simulated status of this derivation is itself part of the stop: it cannot settle what it reports on.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say which AI system understands and which does not. It says that predicting another agent's behaviour and attributing the belief behind it are two different quantities. Prediction is about behaviour, so held-out behaviour can settle it. Attribution is about a latent that is not the behaviour, so behaviour is only an instrument for it, and several different latents can produce the same reading. A model can therefore get the action right and the belief wrong with the same outward score. It stays inside its own closure, the map into another frame needs a pose that is not held, and agreement between models drawing on the same corpus is one witness, not many. For synthetic agents whose latent states are recorded, the gap is closable in principle; for real agents it is declared, with the source-discriminating experiment written down as the way to close it. What is offered is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the honest stop. The agent's actual belief stays open until a better instrument exists.
