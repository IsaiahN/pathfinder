## The residual, in effect terms

A model is compressed. Something is kept and something is thrown away. The question asks for the condition under which what is kept is the structure that matters, and the condition under which the loss falls precisely on the rare case that the ground would have paid for. Option A treats this as one dial with a safe setting. Option B treats it as a union of conditions with different anchors, and asks which condition holds at which slot.

## What survives the figures

Compression is a lossy cross-scale transform, and the honest report of what it gave up is the measured round trip gap R_T, not the compression ratio (Fig 4 :27-32). `Compression achieved` is explicitly a frame-internal quantity with no standing as a verdict (Fig 1 :25-27). The residual is indexed per object slot, so a global error near zero with one live rare slot is a legal state, and aggregating across slots is how that signal disappears (Fig 1 :13, :19, :22). Structure lives in the bonds, not in the ingredient list, and a flat formula can lose the arrangement with nothing signalling the loss (Fig 12 :5, :14-16, :46). The channel decays while the ground does not: dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings sever contact without changed accuracy (Fig 10 :24-28). Preservation and erasure are therefore two conditions with different anchors, not opposite ends of one number (Fig 9 :54-57). No figure line returns a safe compression ratio. That is a declared `gap`, not silence.
