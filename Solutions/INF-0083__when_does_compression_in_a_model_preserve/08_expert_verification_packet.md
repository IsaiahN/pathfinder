# Expert verification packet - when does compression preserve structure and when does it erase rare cases?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a model-compression researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical ratio. Not `grounded`.** No compressed model was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Compression is a lossy cross-scale transform, so the honest report of what it gave up is the measured round trip gap R_T at named slots, not the compression ratio. The ratio is frame-internal and cannot score the compression that produced it. Compression preserves useful structure when a reapplicable method crosses, the typed bonds survive, load-bearing terms still carry an established derivation, and the leftover is paid at each slot. It erases rare cases when the residual is aggregated, readings are pooled or transients dropped, a metric saturates, and the compressed model scores itself with its own ratio. Preservation and erasure are two conditions with different anchors, not ends of one dial. The framework returns that separator, five refuters written in advance, and the per-slot instrument that would close the question. It does not return a safe ratio.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 4 and 9

- **Statement:** the compression ratio cannot decide preservation; the measured round trip gap at named slots can.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :25 "coverage, and compression achieved" and Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a compression metric that is ground-anchored, does not move when the model's own scoring changes, and has R_T exactly zero at every slot of interest. If so, the ratio can carry the verdict and C1 fails.

### C2 - framework-internal and representation-facing; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** what survives is the typed bond arrangement, not the retained atom or parameter list. Two compressions over the same retained set are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. Collapsing spellings under a symmetric bond is legitimate; flattening an ordered bond is erasure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :93 "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed."
- **What to check:** are the retained components mere ingredients, or is there a typed arrangement that a behaviour test at a slot could distinguish? Over a fixed retained set, do two schemes differ only in bond?
- **Refuter:** drop all bond information, keep only the ingredient multiset, and show behaviour unchanged at every slot. Or show an ordered bond collapsed into a symmetric one with zero leftover at every slot. If either holds, the bond is not load-bearing and C2 fails.

### C3 - residual-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 10

- **Statement:** a rare case is a live per-slot residual; aggregation and pooling erase it without moving the ground. Unchanged aggregate accuracy is compatible with erasure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" and Fig 10 :25 "stale percepts, dropped transients," Fig 10 :26 "saturated metrics, pooled readings".
- **What to check:** does the reported compression result pool over slots? Is a minority or fine-grained subpopulation reported separately? Bellam and Kim 2025 report minimal aggregate accuracy impact with exacerbated disparate impact on minority groups, which is the shape this sub-claim predicts.
- **Refuter:** produce a ground reading for the rare slot that is identical before and after a compression that pooled it, and show that the per-slot residual did not carry positive mass. If so, the channel did not decay there and C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a safe compression ratio, layer count, or rarity threshold. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not that compression is bad. Pruning and quantization can leave a trainable generator (Frankle and Carbin 2019) or an aggregate-equivalent model (Han et al. 2016). The claim limits what those results certify.
- Not that a fixed list of rare examples exists. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not a derivation of any empirical effect size from the figures. World sources corroborate shape only.
- Not grounded. No compressed model was measured, and the second seat shares the primary's evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot ground reading, not agreement. Owed instruments: (1) R_T measured at named slots; (2) per-slot live mass before and after; (3) a bond discriminator that distinguishes two isomers by behaviour at a slot; (4) a derivation test after any primitive is removed; (5) the five refuters in `01` section 4.
