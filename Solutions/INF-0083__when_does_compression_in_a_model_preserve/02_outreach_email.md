**Subject:** A test for when compression keeps a rare case, before you decide about the framework

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about model compression. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** compression is a lossy transform between scales. The framework's own rule is that the ground enters as the only metric, and that `compression achieved` is a frame-internal quantity a frame cannot score itself with. So the compression ratio cannot be the verdict. What can be checked is a separator:
>
> 1. Compression preserves structure when what crosses is a reapplicable method rather than a recording of one occasion, when the typed bonds between components survive rather than just the ingredient list, when load-bearing terms still carry an established derivation, and when the round trip gap R_T is measured at named slots.
> 2. Compression erases rare cases when the residual is aggregated across slots, when readings are pooled or transients dropped, when the metric saturates, and when the compressed model scores itself with the ratio it produced. A global error near zero with one live rare slot is a legal state, not an inert one.
> 3. Preservation and erasure are two conditions in a union with different anchors, not ends of one dial. A rare case that is also structurally decisive is a compound with its own loop.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a measurement, and I am not claiming a universal safe ratio. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the number.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this email):**
> 1. a compression whose per-slot residual and round trip gap are measured, whose method rather than recording crosses, and which still erases a rare case the ground pays for;
> 2. a compression metric that is ground-anchored and does not move when the model's own scoring changes;
> 3. a bond that can be dropped with no behaviour change at any slot;
> 4. one of the six held terms removed with no established law losing its derivation;
> 5. a single scalar that prices description and settlement without loss and predicts both the aggregate and the per-slot verdict.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule and the shape limit: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the loop is in the formula link. Run it on a compression you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

> Thanks,
> [name]
