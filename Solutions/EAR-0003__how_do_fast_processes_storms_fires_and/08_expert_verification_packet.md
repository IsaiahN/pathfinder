# Expert verification packet - the Tether on fast and slow Earth processes

**What this is:** a shape claim in a form an Earth-system researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the coupling coefficient. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Fast and slow Earth processes are different terms of one gradient over time, not different kinds of process. Their coupling is three ordered relations rather than one strength: fast to slow is production, slow to fast is a sequence and head start, and a crossing is a threshold comparison. Whether the pair stabilizes or amplifies is the sign of a cycle, not a property carried by either process. The transform between scales is lossy, so the round-trip gap between a fast-only and a slow-only description must be measured. The claim is indexed per place and process, returns a shape, and refuses to name a coefficient, a critical timescale ratio or a winner.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13, 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** fast and slow form a gradient over time; the cross-scale relations are typed and ordered; storm, fire and season are a functional group, becoming isomers only under a shared-atom writing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands." Operators table :37 "A produces B; B did not exist before." Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Refuter:** show that the coupling is symmetric under operand swap, that the three fast processes fall outside a shared functional group without any common atom, or that a temporal gradient is not a distinct term from a spatial one.

### C2 - Earth-system facing; checkable against observations

- **Statement:** the slow state is the baseline the fast event acts into, and a cumulative forcing is compared against a threshold in that state. This predicts that the *same* fast forcing gives different slow increments under different baselines, and that the response changes character near a threshold rather than scaling linearly throughout.
- **What to check:** is the response state-dependent in the observational record? Does a threshold comparison hold at any resolution, or is the response linear at every observed scale? Does the slow increment re-enter as input to the next fast response?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Operators table :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities." Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **External corroboration, secondary only:** Spiegl et al. 2026 report that an identical solar downturn produces a stronger response under a warmer background state; Börner and Dijkstra 2026 report that system and forcing timescales are often not cleanly separated, so crossing a threshold need not coincide with observing the event. These support the shape, not the coefficient. They share one Earth-system modelling evidence pool and count as one witness.
- **Refuter:** a documented case in which the same fast forcing gives the same slow increment under different baselines, or a response linear at every observed resolution, or a coupled pair with no definable loop sign that is nonetheless stably classed.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with an instrument owed

- **Statement:** the residual must be registered per place and process, and the round-trip gap between the two descriptions must be measured, not assumed zero.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** does a global coupling scalar predict the place-specific residuals without losing a live slot? Is there an instrument that shows the two descriptions round-trip exactly?
- **Refuter:** an instrument demonstrating an exact round trip, or a global scalar that preserves every live slot signal.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a coupling coefficient, critical timescale ratio, threshold value or named winner. These are a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not a global interaction strength. The residual is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19).
- Not "fast equals disturbance, slow equals stable background". Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not a theory of everything; no final room; the union stays a compound with its own loop.
- Not `grounded`. No part is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not an assertion that the framework names the coefficient. It returns a shape, not a name.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument or a long record, not another argument. Specifically:

1. A source-discriminating measurement showing which bond is live under a given baseline (the metamerism case in `05` §4: the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved green).
2. A threshold test: does the response change character at a measurable forcing level, or stay linear at every resolution?
3. A loop-sign measurement: does the slow increment feed back with a definable sign?
4. A round-trip measurement of `R_T` between the fast-only and slow-only descriptions.
5. A standing-list test against a changed residual.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :76, Fig 12 :46, Operators :30, :37, :48, Fig 1 :19 and Fig 4 :27, :30. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own data: is the same fast forcing giving different slow increments under different baselines?

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
