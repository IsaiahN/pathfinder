## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1 Fast-event frame (`specified`, triangulated).** A storm, fire or season is a per-slot residual at the event scale. Its anchor is the instrument reading. Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fast and slow are different terms of one temporal gradient, not different kinds of thing.

**D2 Slow-state frame (`specified`, triangulated).** The soil horizon, ice sheet or ocean basin is an accumulated remainder. Its anchor is the long record, which does not move because a model thinks so: Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 13 :30-32 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." What looks like amplification is a remainder re-entering an amplifying chain, not a seventh primitive.

**D3 Coupling (the question itself; `specified` on structure, `gap` on coefficient).** Three ordered bonds, not one strength:

1. Fast to slow is **production**: Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands." Operators table :37 "A produces B; B did not exist before."
2. Slow to fast is **sequence** and a head start: Operators table :30 "A then B; B needs A to have happened." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
3. A crossing is **comparison**: Operators table :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities."

The loop sign decides whether the pair stabilizes or amplifies: Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Valence is not carried by either process: Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Model consensus drawn from one forcing and calibration pool is one witness, not many (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.").

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument or long record, not a further argument. The questions a checker should put to the world:

1. **Discriminating instrument:** does the same fast forcing produce different slow increments under different baseline states? That would name which bond is actually live and measure the head start. The metamerism analogue in `05` §4: a reading cannot comment on the source; the spectrometer resolved it.
2. **Threshold test:** does a cumulative forcing cross a comparison threshold in the slow state, or is the response linear at every observed resolution? A negative result retires the ⋛ branch (Operators :48).
3. **Loop-sign test:** does the slow increment re-enter as input to the next fast response with a definable sign? An undefined sign with a stable classification breaks Fig 12 :121.
4. **Round trip:** measure `R_T` between the fast-only and slow-only descriptions. A zero gap breaks Fig 4 :27.
5. **Standing-list test:** does "fast equals disturbance, slow equals stable background" survive a change of residual? A positive result breaks Fig 11 :51.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Fast processes and slow processes are not different kinds of Earth process; they are different terms of one gradient over time. Their interaction is not a single strength but three ordered relations: a storm, fire or season produces an increment in the slow state that did not exist before; the slow state is the head start the next event acts into; and a crossing is a comparison against a threshold. Whether the pair stabilizes or amplifies is the sign of the loop, not a standing property of either. The transform between the two scales loses information, so the gap between a fast-only and a slow-only description must be measured. The framework returns this shape, indexed per place and process, and refuses to name a coefficient, a critical timescale or a winner. What it offers is the shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The coefficient stays open until the ground settles it.
