**Subject:** Check one cross-scale claim before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** storms, fires and seasonal cycles are not a different *kind* of process from soil formation, ice-sheet change and ocean circulation. They are different terms of one gradient over time. What connects them is not a single interaction strength but three ordered relations: a fast event *produces* an increment in the slow state that did not exist before; the slow state is a *head start*, the baseline the next fast event acts into; and a crossing is a *comparison* between a cumulative forcing and a threshold. Whether the pair stabilizes or amplifies is the sign of the loop, not a property carried by either process.
>
> **What the derivation does not claim:** it does not give a coupling coefficient, a timescale ratio at which fast forcing becomes a slow-state driver, or which process wins in a given basin. It returns those as a shape, not a name. The transform between the two scales loses information, so the gap between a fast-only description carried up and a slow-only description brought back down has to be measured rather than assumed zero. The answer is indexed per place and process; a global average would discard the only signal that says where to look.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message was sent:**
> 1. a two-scale coupling with no information loss in either direction;
> 2. a fast/slow coupling that is symmetric under operand swap, with no production or sequence structure;
> 3. a coupled pair whose loop sign cannot be defined, yet is stably classed as stabilizing or amplifying;
> 4. a single global scalar that predicts the per-place residuals without losing a live slot;
> 5. a standing list in which "fast equals disturbance, slow equals stable background" survives a change of residual.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Earth System Dynamics journal and interactive public review: https://esd.copernicus.org/
> - EGU General Assembly and its sessions across biogeosciences, climate, cryosphere, hydrology and ocean sciences: https://www.egu.eu/meetings/general-assembly/
> - AGU publications and annual meeting: https://www.agu.org/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. This framing's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and this note is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
