## Five findings in the wider literature that bear on the claim

1. **Aggregation loses the live signal.** Zoller et al. 2026 (*Earth System Dynamics* 17, 1719 to 1764) write that "any type of aggregation comes at the cost of information loss" and that indirect impacts are spatially heterogeneous. This is the same warning as Fig 1 :22, from a completely different instrument, a global vegetation model.
2. **Timescales are not cleanly separated.** Börner and Dijkstra 2026 (*Earth System Dynamics* 17, 1671 to 1686) write that "the system and forcing timescales are not cleanly separated", so a tipping event can be delayed after crossing a threshold, avoided under temporary overshoot, or occur without crossing one. This is why the shape claim refuses a single timescale ratio (F9).
3. **A loop has two numbers.** Levermann 2026 (EGUsphere preprint) reports that feedback strength and feedback timescale suffice to locate tipping points, and gives rules for combining loops. This matches the cycle bond of Fig 12 :121 and the two currencies of Fig 12 :125.
4. **The slow state conditions the fast response.** Spiegl et al. 2026 (*Earth System Dynamics* 17, 1703 to 1718) show that an identical solar downturn produces a stronger response under a warmer background state, and that doubling forcing amplitude does not make the dynamical response more distinct. This is the head-start relation of F3 and F4.
5. **The measured ground does not move with model fit.** Cox et al. 2026 (EGUsphere preprint) report a calibration deficit: newer Earth system models reproduce the current climate better but the historical warming record less well. This is Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" in a present-day instrument.

Overlap is declared. Sources 2, 3, 4 and 5 come from one broad Earth-system modelling pool and count as one witness for the field claim, not four. The ecology source (biomagnification) and the control-theory and cybernetics quotes are the genuinely low-overlap corroborators. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending the full derivation before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the paper.
- **Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."** A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source" and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors; once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict is recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must still write a refuter and state a mode.
- **Hard precondition:** no venue in this field accepts a simulated, AI-generated interpretation as ordinary text. ESD's AI policy forbids generative AI for text or interpretations and requires declaration. So the material suitable for stage 1 is the *refuter and the question*, not a submitted paper. If a ground-checked revision is made by a human author with real measurements, the declaration and the data availability statement are part of the submission.
