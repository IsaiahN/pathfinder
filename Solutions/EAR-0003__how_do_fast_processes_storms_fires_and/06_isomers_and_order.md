## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond holds in which direction?

- Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands." Operators table :30 "A then B; B needs A to have happened." Operators table :37 "A produces B; B did not exist before." Operators table :48 "a threshold relation between two quantities."
- Fast to slow is **production (⇒)**: a storm, fire or season produces an increment in soil loss, carbon storage or mixed-layer state that was not there before. The production test is "is there a B before A fires?" No.
- Slow to fast is **sequence (→)** with a head start: the accumulated state is a precondition for the fast event's effect. The sequence test is "swap them. Does it become a different thing?" Yes (Fig 12 :72).
- A crossing is **comparison (⋛)**: a cumulative forcing against a threshold in the slow state. The framework supplies the relation, not the number.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if an observed coupling is symmetric under operand swap, or shows no threshold structure at any resolution, Q1 fails.

### Q2 - Are storms, fires and seasons isomers over a shared atom set?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Written as the question writes them, storm, fire and season have different atom lists (wind, combustion, insolation; Fig 11 :46), so by Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are members of a **functional group**: fast forcings over a temporal gradient, with different qualifiers.
- The stronger form: write each over the full shared atom set of the receiving state, `state → temporal gradient → state`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair. Then they **are** isomers, same atoms arranged differently, decisive in behaviour (a fire's soil effect differs from a storm's even with identical magnitude).
- Fig 12 :23 "this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term and nothing consults the bond to find that out." Under a symmetric bond the spellings collapse; under an ordered bond they are different terms. Here the bonds are ordered, so the spellings do not collapse; this is why the functional group has three members rather than one.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one of the three can be written without an atom the others require, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3 - What is the order of the whole composition?

- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom" (the figure's line continues "for the same reason Figure 4 says there is no top").
- Both scales are settled by instruments: the fast event is measured, the slow state is measured by a long record. Each can therefore become an atom for the next level. Their cross-scale composition is **second order**, legal because both first-order molecules settled (Fig 12 :106 "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part.").
- The ordered chain is: `fast event ⇒ slow increment → new baseline ⋛ threshold → changed fast response`. The output re-enters as input, which is Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **Falsifier:** if the slow baseline does not re-enter as input to the fast response, the cycle is a chain and cannot close; the loop-sign claim fails and only the one-way production remains.

### Q4 - Which parts are catalysts, and which are spent?

- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- A soil biota community or an ice-albedo feedback can survive its own use and alter later couplings; a single storm's sediment pulse is spent. The framework warns that a notation naming only ingredients cannot tell them apart (Fig 12 :14-16 "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not" and "nothing signals that anything was lost").
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that shows a putative catalyst is spent after one use, with no regeneration, collapses the catalyst reading to a one-occasion recording (Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion.").

### Q5 - The two currencies, and why they do not add

- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- A higher-resolution coupled model buys bits; the long record needed to settle a slow-state response buys actions. Fig 12 :127 "a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."
- Spiegl et al. 2026, opened this session: doubling the solar forcing amplitude raised the initial stratospheric signal while "the dynamical response does not become more distinct". More description did not buy settlement.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that adding descriptive resolution settles the slow-state question at the ground with no additional observation.

## CONCLUSION

The cross-scale interaction is not one bond and not one strength. It is an ordered chain of production, sequence and comparison, closed into a cycle whose sign decides whether the pair stabilizes or amplifies. Storm, fire and season are a functional group, and become isomers only under the stronger shared-atom writing. Fast and slow are different terms of one temporal gradient, and the coupling is second order because both sides settled first. Catalysts and spent parts must be distinguished by instrument, not by ingredient list. Each claim carries its own falsifier above. Nothing here returns a coefficient, a threshold value or a winner; those remain the declared gap of `01` F9.
