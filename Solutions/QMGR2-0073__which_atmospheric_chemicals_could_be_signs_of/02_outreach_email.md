**Subject:** Check one output about exoplanet biosignature gases before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which atmospheric chemicals are signs of life?" cannot be answered by sorting molecules into two piles. A spectral feature reports what is in an atmosphere, not how it got there. Biology and abiotic chemistry can terminate in the same gas, so the reading is symmetric under a change of source. What survives is a shape: a candidate biosignature is a detected feature bonded to a planetary context that excludes the known abiotic routes for that feature under that star and that planet, plus at least one witness from a different kind of measurement. Two gases in one spectrum are one witness, not two. O2 plus methane in thermodynamic disequilibrium, with liquid water and the stellar UV field checked, is the strongest version of that shape I know of, and it is still a candidate, not a detection of life.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named gas is *the* biosignature; that O2 plus methane is sufficient; that a non-detection means no life; or that this is grounded. On a remote world the source ground is unreachable, so the honest output is a graded confidence with the excluded false-positive mechanisms recorded.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a molecule with exactly one physically possible production route, and that route requires life;
> 2. a remote measurement whose output changes with the biological-origin hypothesis rather than only with composition;
> 3. two gases from a single retrieval shown to be statistically independent evidence about source, or one gas with no context crossing a stated confidence threshold;
> 4. an existing instrument that decides biological origin from composition alone.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Exoplanet Biosignatures: A Review of Remotely Detectable Signs of Life (Schwieterman et al., Astrobiology 2018, open access): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6016574
> - Abiotic formation of O2 and O3 in high-CO2 terrestrial atmospheres (Segura et al., A&A 2007): https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1557
> - Abiotic Ozone and Oxygen in Atmospheres Similar to Prebiotic Earth (Harman et al., ApJ 2014): https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0004-637X/792/2/90/meta
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link I can send on request. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
