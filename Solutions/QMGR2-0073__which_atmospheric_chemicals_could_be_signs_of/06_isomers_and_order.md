# Isomers, functional groups, and order in the biosignature question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The candidate-gas literature presents a list of molecules (O2, O3, CH4, N2O, CH3Cl, DMS, DMDS, CH3SH, haze products). Two questions are owed: (1) are these distinct *things* in the figure's sense, or repeated spellings of one thing; and (2) what is the **order** of the derivation from a metabolism to a remotely detected feature? The second matters because a feature several steps downstream is a lossy reading of a reading.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the molecular species in the retrieval library plus the context actors (stellar UV, liquid water, crust redox state, co-gases). Bonds held: the seven, in particular `⇒` production and `⋛` comparison, plus the unary negation ¬. No new primitive is needed. The source of a molecule is not in the molecule, so the source is the missing atom in this closure; it can only arrive as a provenanced import from the context frame (Fig 6 :23).

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are O2 and O3 two biosignatures or one?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* O2 and O3 are not isomers in the strict chemical sense (different atom counts), but they are the same element under different bonding and with different source histories. O3 is produced photochemically from O2. Des Marais et al. 2002 gave highest priority to *"Detection of O2 or its photolytic product O3"*. Schwieterman et al. 2018 describe the chain as O2 generated by photosynthesis and *"then photochemically processed into O3 in the stratosphere"*.

So O3 is not a second independent witness. It is a **downstream spelling of O2**. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Recording O2 and O3 as two entries in a candidate list overcharges the vocabulary by counting one provenance twice. Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* The step from O2 to O3 is a lossy transform; the round trip back to "there was biology" must be measured, not assumed.

**Result:** O2 and O3 are one slot with two observable forms. Counting them as two witnesses is the flat-formula error. Triangulated (Fig 12 + Fig 4 + Des Marais 2002), `specified`.

### Q2. Are the sulfur gases isomers, and why does it matter?

Schwieterman et al. 2018 list DMS, DMDS and CH3SH as candidate sulfur biosignature gases, with C2H6 as a detectable photochemical by-product. Dimethyl sulfide ((CH3)2S) and ethanethiol (CH3CH2SH) share the formula C2H6S but differ in connectivity, a textbook structural isomer pair. Fig 12 :97-98 names the chemical precedent: *"Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."*

**Result:** two gases can share an ingredient set and differ in bonding, spectrum, lifetime and biological source. A retrieval that reports only "C2H6S-like absorption" has recorded the formula and dropped the bond. This is a genuine isomer claim, `specified`, single-source for the figure definition with the chemistry as world corroboration.

### Q3. What is the functional group here?

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The functional group shared by the reduced gases (CH4, N2O, DMS, CH3SH) is **a reduced species in an oxidising atmosphere that must be replenished**. The group says what kind of signature; the qualifier (CH4 versus DMS, modern Earth versus anoxic early Earth) says which member. Hitchcock and Lovelock 1967 based their argument on exactly this: *"evidence of a large chemical free energy gradient between surface matter and the atmosphere in contact with it is evidence of life"*, with the proviso *"provided the extent of the disequilibrium is significantly greater than abiological processes would permit."*

**Result:** the honest level of generality is the functional group, not the individual gas. This is why a candidate list can be useful as a budget filter (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*) and cannot be the verdict.

### Q4. What order does the derivation have?

Using the bond table:

1. `metabolism ⇒ gas` (production: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*). CH4 from methanogenesis; O2 from oxygenic photosynthesis.
2. `gas → spectral feature` (sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*). Retrieval goes the other way, from feature to gas, which is the round trip of Fig 4.
3. `oxidising inventory ⋛ reducing inventory` (comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). This is the thermodynamic-disequilibrium claim, and ⋛ is the right glyph because the question is *which is larger relative to equilibrium*, not merely whether both are present.
4. `context ⊢ exclusion of abiotic route ¬` (negation, not a bond: *"this does not hold"*). A false-positive mechanism is ruled out. Fig 12 amendment: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* A candidate list without ¬ can only grow; it cannot carry the correction.

Order matters. Step 4 must run before the conclusion, not after. Schwieterman et al. 2018 note that *"Each potential false positive mechanism that is ruled out by observations increases the robustness of a biogenic interpretation"* (reported in the NExSS O2 workshop material, read via search extract). That is the negation step doing work.

### Q5. Is the union a molecule or a pile?

Fig 12 :99: *"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."* The conjunction `feature + excluded context + independent witness` is a molecule; the candidate list is an ingredient pile. Under a symmetric bond, *"k operands have k! spellings and one term"* (Fig 12 :92), so ordering the three parts on the page does not create three additional claims. Under the ordered bonds in the chain above, order does create different claims, and the derivation must record it.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate gases are not independent entries. O2 and O3 are one slot observed at two depths; the sulfur gases exhibit genuine isomer structure; the reduced gases share a functional group; the disequilibrium is a comparison, not a conjunction; and the exclusion of abiotic pathways is a negation that must precede the verdict. The defensible object is a **bonded three-part shape**, not a list, and the bond is the part that current candidate lists drop.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two entries in the candidate list can be shown to have independent provenance, independent spectral discriminators, and independent abiotic backgrounds, then counting them separately is correct and the "one slot" reading of Q1 and Q3 fails. If a gas is found with no abiotic route at all, the negation step becomes unnecessary and the shape collapses to a name.
