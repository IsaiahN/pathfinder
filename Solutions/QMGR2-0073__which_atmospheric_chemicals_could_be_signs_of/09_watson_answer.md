## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a proposed split of atmospheric gases into signs of life versus gases that arise without life. Option A: answer with a ranked list of gases and treat the molecule as carrying its own provenance. Option B: refuse the molecule-only question as a union compound, and return the shape a candidate must have plus an honest `unreachable` verdict on the source. What each leads to: A delivers a `general`-mode verdict from a composition reading, invites false positives, and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B delivers a checkable conjunction, four written refuters, and a graded confidence with the lineage recorded.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** One molecular feature on one planet under at least two competing source hypotheses. Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), not a list of independent gases. Symmetry: the reading is symmetric under source substitution, so the source is not in the contents (Fig 8 :50 "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence"). Scale: molecular line, planetary context, temporal replenishment.
- **Domain 1, spectroscopic observation.** Actors: telescope, spectrograph, retrieval model, line list. Currency: spectral depth and inferred mixing ratio. Anchor: the measured spectrum, which is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Question type: perception and vocabulary for the gas, never for the source.
- **Domain 2, abiotic chemistry and geochemistry.** Actors: CO2 and H2O photolysis, volcanism, impacts, serpentinization, crust redox state, stellar UV. Currency: reaction fluxes and sinks. Anchor: laboratory rates and photochemical models validated on Solar System spectra. Question type: can X be made here without life?
- **Domain 3, biology.** Actors: oxygenic photosynthesis, methanogenesis, microbial sulfur and methylated-gas metabolism. Currency: metabolic fluxes. Anchor: terrestrial organisms and cultures. Question type: can X be made by life under some conditions? Never: by life *here*.
- **Domain 4, context and verification.** Actors: water, host-star UV, co-gases, time variability, outside checkers. Currency: constraints that exclude abiotic pathways. Anchor: the actual biota on that planet, unreachable remotely at this budget. Question type: was X made by life *here*?
- **Domain 5, outside analogue (medicine).** Actors: screening test, confirmatory test, gold standard. Currency: likelihood ratios and pre-test probability. Anchor: biopsy or autopsy. Question type: does a positive reading settle a cause by itself?
- **Order by dependency.** Detect the feature, then enumerate the competing routes, then state what would count as excluding one, then assemble independent witnesses, then attempt ground settlement. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The chain breaks between the second and third links for remote exoplanets.

Strain test applied: the Fig 11 habitat concept is a cross-level application to a planetary atmosphere, and the Fig 8 provenance structure is an analogue when applied to a molecule's source. Both are named in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7 and are not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The molecule-only question is refused as written. No atmospheric chemical is a sign of life by itself, because a molecular reading does not determine its source and every standard candidate can arise abiotically under some characterised context. The figure-licensed answer is a shape: a detected feature bonded to a context that excludes the known abiotic routes under that star and that planet, plus a witness from a closure that differs from the one that produced the reading. O2 with CH4 in thermodynamic disequilibrium, together with liquid water, a checked stellar UV field and the named false-positive mechanisms excluded, is the strongest current instance of that shape, and it is `specified`, not `grounded`. The same gas changes valence with the habitat, the source verdict on a remote planet is `unreachable`, and the honest output is a graded confidence with a lineage rather than a name. One failure class was found in the world literature itself: Harman et al. 2014 show that O3 plus CH4 is not a strong biosignature without context, which corroborates rather than flips the figure verdict.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the shape is sent with its four refuters. The real cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by handing them a candidate list and a conclusion before they have described the residual. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter. If the shape itself is wrong, the most likely failure is over-conservatism: a real biosphere that does not produce any of the enumerated gases, a "false negative" category the literature already names.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no gas is banned. The conjunction can be narrowed as new abiotic pathways are characterised, and each exclusion stays ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41 "so keep every cut ranked and reversible"). A published claim can be superseded by a corrected confidence statement.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A remote instrument whose output changes with the biological-origin hypothesis would move domain 4 from `unreachable` to `grounded`; a gas with an empty abiotic closure would restore a molecule-alone biosignature; a demonstration that two gases from one retrieval are statistically independent evidence about source would restore the two-gas shortcut; a derivation that excludes one of the six substrate terms would break the shape itself. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
