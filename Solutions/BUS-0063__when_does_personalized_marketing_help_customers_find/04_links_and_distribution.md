## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, one witness.** Aguirre et al. 2015, Bleier and Eisenbeiss 2015 (both papers), Lambrecht and Tucker 2013, Friestad and Wright 1994, the 2025 meta-analysis and the AMA research insights all sit in marketing and consumer research. They agree on a two-sided effect, but they share journals, conferences, datasets and a citation chain. Per Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Their agreement is one witness, not seven.

- Aguirre et al. 2015 start from relevance and find the paradox: *"Greater personalization typically increases service relevance and customer adoption, but paradoxically, it also may increase customers' sense of vulnerability and lower adoption rates."* Covert collection plus high personalization drops CTR. Read through the figures this is a channel failure, not a verdict about the customer's good. It corroborates C1 at the level of mechanism.
- Bleier and Eisenbeiss 2015 find trust is the switch: *"more trusted retailers can increase the perceived usefulness of their ads through a combination of high depth and narrow breadth of personalization without eliciting increased reactance or privacy concerns"*, while for less trusted retailers higher depth *"triggered increased reactance and privacy concerns"*. This is F4's valence flip, measured in one pool.
- Lambrecht and Tucker 2013 and the second Bleier and Eisenbeiss paper supply F8's timing and specificity boundary: *"dynamic retargeted ads are on average less effective than their generic equivalent"* until preferences have evolved, and high-depth personalization *"quickly lose[s] effectiveness as time passes"*. These are bracket-fidelity findings, corroborated by Fig 7 :37-39.
- Friestad and Wright's persuasion knowledge model and the 148-study meta-analysis it spawned show that naming the tactic changes the response: *"persuasion knowledge raised coping responses and lowered favourable evaluations of marketers, yet could not suppress or eliminate the effect of the persuasion attempt"*. This is a world-side instance of Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording"*: the tactic list is not the arrangement, and the customer's knowledge of the objective changes the substance.
- The 2025 meta-analysis reports a small average persuasive advantage and that *"perceived relevance / but not perceived intrusiveness / explained the persuasiveness of personalized advertising"*. That is a filter for budget, not a witness for a person (Fig 9 :60).

**Low-overlap witnesses, each from a different field and each read directly or from a publisher-indexed record:**

1. **Human cognition.** Loewenstein's information-gap theory: *"when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge. Such information gaps produce the feeling of deprivation labeled curiosity."* A moderate gap motivates search; full knowledge removes the gap. This corroborates F8's graded, timed boundary and agrees with the recommender-systems finding that exploration has user-specific limits. Different field and evidence pool from marketing.
2. **Recommender systems engineering.** The exploration-exploitation literature describes the same boundary operationally: *"we cannot offer something new to a user as is not in their past interaction data"*, exploration is used for *"avoiding the filter bubble effect"*, and *"exploration may not be an unbounded good, but rather a bounded process with user-specific limits"*. This is Fig 6 :23 and Fig 7 :36 stated in an engineering frame, with no shared evidence pool with consumer research.
3. **Choice and decision science.** Iyengar and Lepper 2000 find people *"are more likely to purchase gourmet jams or chocolates ... when offered a limited array of 6 choices rather than a more extensive array of 24 or 30 choices"* and report greater subsequent satisfaction with fewer options. This corroborates the figure claim that reach is not maximised by piling on options, and that a "more is better" reading of C2 is wrong. The matching figure constraint is Loewenstein's moderate gap: both point to a bounded, not maximal, expansion.
4. **Social critique and power.** Zuboff 2019 and Pariser 2011 are not independent measurements, but they are different frames from consumer psychology. Zuboff's *"the surest way to predict behavior is to intervene at its source and shape it"* is the world-side description of Fig 10 :47 (*"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*). Pariser's *"Your filter bubble is this unique, personal universe of information created just for you by this array of personalizing filters"* is Fig 11 :66 (*"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another"*) in plain language. Both are corroboration of the mechanism shape at a different stratum, not evidence for the figure lines.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. They are not independent world witnesses, because their author wrote the figures. That overlap is recorded in the ledger.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28:** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if the residual is not described first. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.

**A hard practical constraint.** This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must not be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article under a venue's authorship and AI policies; the human researcher who runs the check owns and writes whatever is submitted, with the AI contribution disclosed as the venue requires. The legitimate use of this packet is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author the result.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
