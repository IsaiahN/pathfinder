# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no person-level welfare measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check two conditions about personalization before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about personalized marketing that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** personalization helps a customer find useful products only when two conditions hold at once.
>
> 1. The customer's own considered judgment, taken when they can genuinely decline, stays as the thing that settles whether the product was useful. The firm's click-through, conversion and lifetime-value metrics stay as instruments for allocating budget and never become the thing being optimized for the customer.
> 2. The recommendation brings the customer into contact with something outside their own past behaviour, so their options move outward instead of being a replay of what they already clicked.
>
> These are two different failure modes, not two degrees of one problem. A system that fails 2 but keeps 1 gives the customer a comfortable mirror: nothing harmful is decided about them, but no new product is found. A system that fails 1 is manipulation even if it shows something novel, because someone else's metric is deciding what the customer gets. The full filter-bubble case fails both. A single "relevance score" cannot see the difference.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular tactic is always manipulative, that covert data collection by itself is manipulation, that personalization never works, that I can name which recommendation will help a named customer, or that this is settled by evidence. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a regime where the engagement metric demonstrably decides what is shown, yet customers' ex post informed judgments and welfare improve and their options expand;
> 2. a regime with no new outside input, pure replay of past clicks, that still produces durable ex post judged-useful discoveries;
> 3. a technique whose measured effect stays the same across a documented change in what the customer needs and how much they trust the firm;
> 4. an averaged campaign metric that predicts, prospectively and per named customer, who will be manipulated;
> 5. a recommendation with the same depth and timing that pays equally for all customers and at all stages of search, with no decay;
> 6. a showing that "the customer's considered judgment" moves whenever the firm pushes and no substitute anchor can be named.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |
| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Two conditions kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a blended score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| No name for the manipulative tactic | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32) |
| Metric barred from the checking seat | *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47); *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person"* (Fig 2 :37) |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Once the recipient has the framework, their later agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40) |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-22) |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) |
| Ask the user for their own refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; make the sought-for shape an ordinary term |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A Track B style reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
