## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether personalization is good or bad in general. It is the **boundary condition**: which regime keeps the customer's own considered judgment as the thing that settles whether a product was useful, and which regime substitutes the firm's engagement proxy for that judgment and therefore becomes manipulation.

- **b** is the firm's belief about what this customer wants, formed from collected data, the customer's history and inherited priors.
- **a** is the personalization action: which product, ad, price, framing or ranking is served to this customer-slot.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library predicts the customer will do (click, convert, repeat, lifetime value).
- **o′** is what the customer actually experiences: whether the product was useful to them, judged under conditions where they could refuse.
- **s** is the customer. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is indexed per customer, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22).

There are two frames in contact here, each with its own loop. The firm ranges its model against market response. The customer ranges their own belief about what would help them against what actually happens after the recommendation. Personalization is a firm action that enters the customer's habitat. Help and manipulation are classifications of what that action does to the customer's loop and which anchor sits in the checking seat.

- Option A, "personalization helps when it is accurate": leads to the prediction that better prediction of clicks is sufficient. This conflates the firm's metric with the customer's welfare and fails the anchor test (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Option B, "help and manipulation are two conditions of a relation": leads to the prediction that the customer's welfare judgment must stay the anchor **and** the recommendation must move the customer's reach outward through contact with a closure that differs from their own past behaviour. Failure of the first condition is manipulation; failure of the second is a mirror that is unhelpful without necessarily being manipulative (Fig 9 :57).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **pair of conditions whose conjunction keeps the customer's welfare anchor reachable and the customer's reach moving outward, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one from a failure of the other**. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only account of personalized marketing.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
