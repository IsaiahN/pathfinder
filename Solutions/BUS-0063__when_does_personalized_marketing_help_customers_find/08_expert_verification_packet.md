## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Personalized marketing helps a customer find useful products only when two conditions hold at once. First, the customer's own considered judgment, taken under conditions where they can decline, remains the thing that settles whether the product was useful; click-through, conversion and lifetime value stay as instruments for budget, not as the objective being served to the customer. Second, the recommendation brings the customer into contact with something outside their own past behaviour, so their options move outward rather than being a replay of past clicks. These are two failure modes with different instruments, not two degrees of one problem. A system that fails the second condition but keeps the first gives a comfortable mirror: unhelpful, but not necessarily manipulative. A system that fails the first condition is manipulation even if it shows something novel, because a proxy has taken the checking seat. The same data and the same model bonded to a different objective are a different substance, so a list of tactics cannot settle a named case by inspection. The claim is a shape any candidate answer must have; it does not name which tactic manipulates which person.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 10 and 1

- **Statement:** because this question is constitutively about what is good for a person, the person's considered judgment is the anchor; an engagement proxy that starts deciding outcomes has stopped being a proxy; a firm cannot score itself with a metric it also produces.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
  - Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* The same passage notes that judgment *"is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
  - Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
  - Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **Refuter:** a regime in which CTR or CLV demonstrably decides what is shown, yet customers' ex post informed judgments and welfare improve and their options expand, with no hidden proxy substituting for their judgment; or a demonstration that considered judgment moves whenever the firm pushes with no substitute anchor nameable. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus two low-overlap checks

- **Statement:** help requires an import from a closure that differs from the customer's own past behaviour; pure replay composes inside a closed set and cannot add the undiscovered product; a filter bubble is a substituted habitat, not an absence of information.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
  - Fig 8 :45 - *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
  - Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** recommender-systems work states the same boundary operationally: *"we cannot offer something new to a user as is not in their past interaction data"*, and treats exploration as *"avoiding the filter bubble effect"* at a cost to short-term revenue. Does that not name C2's import, and is a system that never explores anything outside logged behaviour not a closed set by construction? A "no" to both is a real refutation of the mapping. The second check is Pariser's *"Your filter bubble is this unique, personal universe of information created just for you by this array of personalizing filters"*, which is a plain-language statement of habitat substitution.
- **Refuter:** a regime with no new atom entering from a differing closure, pure replay of past behaviour, that still produces durable ex post judged-useful discoveries; or a demonstration that a firm renewed a customer's reach purely by internal composition with no new input. Either breaks idempotence and C2 with it.

### C3 - Structure and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the union must be unbundled before a lever is chosen; two systems with identical data and model but different objective bonds are isomers, so a model card alone cannot predict the customer outcome; the valence flip follows from valence being relative to the residual; the objective link in a real pipeline usually has no instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What a researcher can check:** Friestad and Wright 1994 and the meta-analysis of their model report that *"persuasion knowledge raised coping responses and lowered favourable evaluations of marketers, yet could not suppress or eliminate the effect of the persuasion attempt"*. Does that not show that the objective bond, once visible, changes the substance while the ingredients stay fixed? And does the applied literature's focus on relevance, intrusiveness and CTR not confirm that the person-level objective link is uninstrumented?
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure cells and leaves no categorically different residue; or two systems with identical atoms and identical objective bonds whose customer outcomes diverge with no difference in habitat or imported atoms, which would move the explanation out of composition and into habitat alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about marketing, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage lift of personalization over generic ads.
- Not that personalization is harmful, or that covert data collection alone is manipulation. Covert collection corrupts the channel (Fig 10 :26); the manipulation condition also requires proxy-in-seat and narrowed reach.
- Not that engagement metrics are irrelevant. They are instruments for budget and channel health, barred from the checking seat only.
- Not that any specific tactic is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the marketing literature independently corroborates this. Aguirre, Bleier and Eisenbeiss, Lambrecht and Tucker, Friestad and Wright and the meta-analysis share one evidence pool. The cognition, recommender-systems and social-critique sources corroborate the mechanism at different strata, not the per-customer fact.
- Not that this packet, produced with an LLM, is submittable or publishable as authored work. See the AI-authorship note in section 6.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-customer study that measures C1 and C2 separately, records the objective bond as well as the model ingredients, and reports which condition failed before the harmful outcome. Concretely:

1. **Anchor test:** is the person's considered judgment on the product, taken under a real option to decline, the thing that settles the case, or did a CTR/CLV objective decide what they saw? Indicators: a convention deciding an outcome (Fig 10 :47), stale percepts and pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
2. **Import test:** did any option from a closure that differs from the customer's logged behaviour enter their consideration before the discovery? If not, C2 is refuted (Fig 6 :23).
3. **Isomer test:** do two systems with identical data and architecture but different objective bonds produce different customer outcomes? If not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands (Fig 12 :117, :124).
4. **Timing and fidelity test:** does the recommendation's value decay with time since last visit and depend on the customer's current construal, as the applied literature and the information-gap account predict (Fig 7 :37-39)?
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a useful discovery from a habit, a price response and vulnerability-driven compliance when all produce the same purchase reading? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :36-38, Fig 10 :47, Fig 6 :21 and :23, Fig 11 :66, Fig 12 :117, Fig 9 :57 and Fig 3 :27. Eight short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of recommenders: is there a documented case of durable ex post judged-useful discovery with no input from outside logged behaviour, no exploration and no differing closure? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a useful discovery from a habit and from vulnerability-driven compliance using observable customer data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
