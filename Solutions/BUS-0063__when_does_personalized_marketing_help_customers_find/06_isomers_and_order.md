## Q1 - Are two systems with the same model isomers?

> Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
> Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
> Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**
> Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Two systems holding identical atoms (past clicks, demographics, catalog, ranking function) can differ in the **bond**: in one, the objective bond connects the model to the customer's considered welfare; in the other, it connects the model to CTR or CLV. The bond is a **comparison** (`⋛`, a threshold relation between two quantities) serving as the goal, and it is exactly the half that a system description which lists only ingredients fails to record. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the "model card" and decisive in what the customer experiences.

If the two systems share only a pattern, for example `customer → model → item`, while differing in which atoms occupy the slots and what the objective compares, the weaker claim holds: they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A model-card comparison that lists data sources, features and architecture cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group, because the bond is not in the list. An audit that records which quantity the ranking function optimises can. This is the figure-grounded version of the intuition that "it is not what data you have, it is what you optimise for", but it is stronger: the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two systems with identical atoms and identical objective bonds nevertheless diverge in customer outcome with no difference in habitat or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be D2, which moves the explanation out of composition.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the marketing application. The world corroboration is Friestad and Wright's persuasion knowledge finding that *"persuasion knowledge raised coping responses and lowered favourable evaluations of marketers, yet could not suppress or eliminate the effect of the persuasion attempt"*: the same technical attempt changes substance once the customer knows the objective. That is one marketing pool, not an independent frame.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
> Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Order for a firm serving a personalized recommendation:

1. **Perception.** Can it read the customer's response at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests: impression, click, dwell.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ_f have terms for what this situation affords this customer, and can it say what would count as progress for them? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :25-26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere for the person? Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.
4. **Planning.** Which item to serve, under incomplete information about the customer's current construal.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the person's considered judgment, carry it to the next occasion.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain. C1 (anchor maintained) is mostly a failure of links 2 and 3: the objective is never stated in the person's terms, so a proxy silently occupies the seat. C2 (reach renewed) is mostly a failure of link 5: the system names what worked as a click and never imports an atom from a differing closure. That mapping is why one lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The click-facing link (1) has abundant instrumentation; the carry link (5) has almost none that is person-facing. Budget piles up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 3.

**Falsifier:** a pipeline whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working against ex post person-level judgment, and which fails only at serving the right item. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F6 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personalization paradox** (Aguirre et al. 2015: covert collection, vulnerability, trust cues) | the channel between firm and person; covert collection corrupts it | close to C1 and to Fig 10 :24-26 | Does not say which objective sits in the checking seat; measures CTR, not the person's considered good |
| **Trust and depth/breadth** (Bleier and Eisenbeiss 2015) | the valence switch across trust states | Fig 11 :49-51, same technique different valence | One marketing pool; the trust state is an instrument, not the anchor |
| **Overpersonalization and specificity** (Bleier and Eisenbeiss 2015; Lambrecht and Tucker 2013) | bracket fidelity across handoffs | Fig 7 :37-39 | Gives a timing curve, not the condition that separates help from manipulation |
| **Persuasion knowledge** (Friestad and Wright 1994) | the customer's own knowledge of the objective as a coping mechanism | Fig 12 :5, the unrecorded bond | Coping lowers favourable evaluation but does not eliminate the effect, so knowledge alone is not a ground |
| **Filter bubble** (Pariser 2011) | substitution of the customer's habitat | Fig 11 :66, Fig 7 :32 | Describes the narrowed closure; does not by itself identify proxy-in-seat, so it is the C2 failure cell, not the whole answer |
| **Instrumentarian power** (Zuboff 2019) | a social-scale account of behaviour shaping | Fig 10 :47, a convention deciding an outcome | A different stratum and a critique, not a per-customer measurement |
| **Information gap and choice overload** (Loewenstein 1994; Iyengar and Lepper 2000) | a bounded optimum in novelty and option count | Fig 7 :37-39, Fig 6 :23 | Cognitive mechanism, not the business verdict; refuses "more options is always better" |
| **Explore-exploit in recommenders** (Yang et al. 2023; exploration saturation 2026) | the operational form of C2 with user-specific limits | Fig 6 :23, Fig 11 :61 | Optimises long-run engagement in most formulations; can still leave the wrong objective in the seat |
| **Luck / undisclosed objective** | an unexamined bond that happens to pay | Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* | Cannot be excluded from inside a model card. Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the cognition, engineering and critique fields corroborate mechanism, not the per-customer verdict.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the personalization-paradox emphasis on covert collection, as channel corruption and C1;
- the trust emphasis, as the valence flip of Fig 11 :49-51;
- the overpersonalization and specificity emphasis, as bracket fidelity (Fig 7 :37-39);
- the filter-bubble emphasis, as substituted habitat and failed import (Fig 11 :66, Fig 6 :23);
- the exploration-emphasis, as the operator that renews reach with a user-specific bound (Fig 6 :23 and the moderate-gap cognition sources);
- the persuasion-knowledge emphasis, as the bond becoming visible to the person (Fig 12 :5).

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific tactic that will be manipulative for a named customer. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two systems with the same atoms but different objective bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two systems sharing only a `customer → model → item` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the vocabulary/objective link and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard applied explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
