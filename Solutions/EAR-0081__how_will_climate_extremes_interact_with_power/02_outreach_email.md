# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Not for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated on the same page. The output must be checkable or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about climate extremes and five infrastructure systems

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on the rules, `gap` on the forecast):**
>
> 1. Power, transport, water, communications and health are five object slots, not one risk object. Their residuals must be reported separately. Aggregating them into a single risk score is how a live signal disappears into an average.
> 2. The question is a union compound, not a list. Hazard, exposure, vulnerability and interdependency overlap, so unbundle before searching and then recombine at the couplings.
> 3. The thing to enumerate first is the field of contact: power to water pumping, telecoms to grid control, road access to repair crews, backup fuel. The loss that matters is often three steps out, not at the asset that was hit.
> 4. The bonds are ordered and include feedback loops, so a one-way chain of dominoes is the wrong picture.
> 5. The anchor is realized service outcome, not agreement among models. A model agreement that has never been ranged against outage data has no verdict weight.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving you a probability, a date or a damage estimate for any sector. The figures I use contain no climate physics and no fragility curves. That part is a genuine gap and I am reporting it as one. A search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence, and a gap is not an answer I am hiding.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before running anything:
> 1. One aggregated risk index predicts per-sector outages on held-out events with no loss of per-slot information.
> 2. First-order contact enumeration alone predicts all second- and third-order service losses on held-out events.
> 3. A symmetric, order-free coupling representation predicts cascade direction and timing as well as an ordered graph.
> 4. Model agreement with no event ground selects the adaptation that actually minimized realized loss.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line failure is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - IPCC AR6 WGII Summary for Policymakers, section B.5 on compound and cascading risks: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/summary-for-policymakers
> - Verschuur et al. 2024, cross-sector climate risk to infrastructure review, PLOS Climate: https://journals.plos.org/climate/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pclm.0000331
> - Bondank and Chester 2020, interdependency failures as a complex process, Frontiers in Water: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/water/articles/10.3389/frwa.2020.00021/full
> - Cha et al. 2025, Storm Eowyn cross-sector cascade, npj Natural Hazards: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44304-025-00161-9
> - Pescaroli and Alexander 2016, vulnerability paths of cascading disasters, Natural Hazards: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-016-2186-3
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is public in the figures repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
