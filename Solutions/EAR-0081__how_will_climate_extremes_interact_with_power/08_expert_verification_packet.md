# Expert verification packet: the Tether on climate extremes and infrastructure

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate-risk or infrastructure researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the forecast. Not `grounded`.** No outage probability, timing or damage estimate is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Power, transport, water, communications and health are five object slots whose service residuals must be reported separately, not averaged into one infrastructure risk score. The question is a union compound of hazard, exposure, vulnerability and interdependency, so it must be unbundled before search and recombined at the couplings. The field of contact must be enumerated outward, because the service loss that matters is often several steps from the asset that was hit. Cascade bonds are ordered and include feedback loops, so a domino chain is the wrong picture. The anchor is realized service outcome, not agreement among models. The empirical forecast itself is a `gap`: the figures return a checkable shape, not a number. Four refuters were written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** residuals are per slot; aggregation destroys the signal; the question is a union compound.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** a single scalar risk index predicts per-sector outages on held-out events with no loss of per-slot information; or hazard and interdependency are shown to be additive, so a hazard-by-hazard sum reproduces observed multi-sector loss with no interaction term.

### C2 - World-facing; checkable by a climate-risk or infrastructure researcher against their own literature

- **Statement:** the contact field must be enumerated beyond direct contact, and the cascade is cyclic and ordered, not a symmetric domino chain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **What to check:** in the 2003 Northeast blackout and Storm Éowyn, did the largest service losses occur at the directly damaged asset or several steps out? Is the power to telecoms to power-recovery path a genuine feedback loop? Does the literature treat co-location as symmetric and cascade as ordered?
- **Refuter:** first-order contact enumeration alone predicts all second- and third-order service losses on held-out events; or a symmetric, order-free coupling representation predicts cascade direction and timing as well as an ordered cyclic graph.

### C3 - Framework-internal with a world consequence; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** the anchor is realized impact, never model agreement; the assessment frame has no ground of its own and cannot decide a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
- **Refuter:** a validated model whose agreement with other models, with no event ground, selects the adaptation that minimized realized service loss.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- No probability, date, magnitude or first-failing sector for any climate extreme.
- No aggregated infrastructure risk score, and no ranking of adaptation options by agreement.
- Not that the figures are climate science or engineering. They supply no fragility curve, hazard distribution or repair-time law.
- Not that model agreement is corroboration, and not that a large reviewer list is a witness.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an outage measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that cannot discriminate sources demonstrates its own inadequacy. Here:

1. Per-slot outage and recovery records by hazard and asset, instead of aggregate damage totals.
2. Event forensics tracing trigger to third-order loss, including road access and backup fuel.
3. Night-time lights and vessel-tracking proxies where utility outage data are confidential.
4. The four pre-registered refuters run on held-out events.

If a figure re-census found a line governing a specific outage probability, the `gap` would close. Until then it stands.
