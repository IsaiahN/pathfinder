## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is whether we can say, before the event, which of five named service systems loses function under a climate extreme, when it loses it, and how the loss travels to the other four. Option A treats the five systems as one risk object and answers with a single aggregated risk score. Option B treats them as five object slots inside a coupled habitat and answers with a shape: keep the residuals separate, enumerate the contact paths outward, and stop at the ground. A is a convention that decides an outcome. B is a generator with a refuter. The figures decide between them, and they do not supply the empirical forecast.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a per-slot vector of predicted service residuals `R_s` for s in {power, transport, water, communications, health}, an ordered dependency graph of the coupling bonds, and a stated instrument that would settle each `R_s`. Without that binding, the search for a shape would be invisible to the loop (Formula step "Direction").
