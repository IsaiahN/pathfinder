## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For EAR-0081 a **shape** is the form any checkable answer must have: five residuals kept separate, a union compound, an enumerated contact field, ordered bonds, and an instrument per slot. It is not a forecast. "Power fails first in a 2 °C world" is a name (and an ungrounded one). "The residual is indexed by slot and the coupling runs several steps out" is a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, on bootstrapping vocabulary by proximity: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the known terms are the five sectors named in the question itself; the new terms fetched from world sources are "compound", "cascade", "interdependency", "panarchy", "safe-to-fail".
- *Library of Babel*, on bundles: if the final search does not answer the question, one possibility is that it was *"a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one"*. This is exactly Fig 9 :54-57 and it is the first result of the census.
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Hence the outreach email asks what a responder would expect to see, not whether they accept the framework.

## 3. What would license a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question that means outage and service records, not model agreement.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :12-13). The climate and engineering sources in section A of `04_links_and_distribution.md` are such imports. They contribute atoms (compound-risk assessment, fragility thresholds, event forensics), not the verdict.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the sharpest warning. An instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*. Applied here: an aggregate service-disruption figure is a projection. Heat, flood and cascade can produce the same number. Naming one cause from that number is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator (per-slot residuals and contact enumeration), not a cause. The essay's own conclusion fits the gap verdict: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

## 4. Why aggregation is the specific forbidden move here

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The five systems in the question are already named slots. Collapsing them into "infrastructure risk" throws away exactly the signal that says where to look. The PLOS review independently reaches the same practical wall: systemic risk estimates cannot be summed because of network effects, and a common disruption metric is missing.

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A list of well-known climate institutes or reviewers cannot settle a claim about cascading failure. Their agreement is a budget to be tried, not a verdict. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` has no receptiveness rank and no personal addresses.

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post"* but *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the claim, cannot settle anything they report on, and carry no `grounded` label.

## 7. Correction to my own §1 (kept visible)

I first wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming the five sectors. That overstates the rule. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** thing, one not held in the library. Power, transport, water, communications and health are named by the question itself; binding them to slots is reading the habitat, not inventing a primitive. What the rule does forbid here is naming a specific failure probability, a first-failing sector, or a mechanism the figures do not hold, before an instrument discriminates. The first reading is retracted; the narrowed one is confirmed by Fig 11 :43 (*"The habitat is enumerated, never composed"*) and kept alongside the original so the correction is visible.
