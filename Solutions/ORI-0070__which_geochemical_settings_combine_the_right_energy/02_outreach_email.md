**Subject:** Four constraints on a prebiotic setting, and what would refute them

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about geochemical settings and prebiotic chemistry. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which geochemical settings combine the right energy, concentration, stability, and chemical ingredients?" is a union compound, not four checkboxes. Read in effect terms, any candidate setting must satisfy four different constraints at once:
>
> 1. **Energy** must be a spendable difference. Energy on its own is conserved, finite, and directionless; a gradient over a state is what composes a flow. A setting with heat but no measured spendable gradient does not meet this condition.
> 2. **Concentration** is a gradient bonded to space, that is, proximity and containment that hold reactants. It is a different term from a gradient over time.
> 3. **Stability** is an ordered succession that closes: the product of one pass re-enters as the input of the next. A single dry-down is a chain and cannot close by construction.
> 4. **Ingredients** are a bonded arrangement, not an element list. The relation carries information the operands do not.
>
> These are constraints on the *shape* of an answer, not a claim that any one named setting satisfies them. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Hydrothermal vents, hot springs, ice interfaces, and tidal pools are bindings from the world frame onto that shape. Which binding is right is settled by a measurement, an experiment, or an ancient rock, not by agreement among researchers.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the one setting, that vents beat hot springs, that a wet-dry cycle is sufficient, or that the four conditions have been measured together anywhere. Agreement from people who share the same evidence pool is one witness, not two.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. A measured setting that satisfies all four conditions simultaneously and across all reaction stages (which would defeat the union reading at the world level).
> 2. A directionless energy term, with no spendable difference, driving a sustained reaction network.
> 3. One physical variable shown to be both a spatial gradient and a temporal succession with no change of bonded state.
> 4. A single dry-down that re-enters its own output across geological time without a cycle.
>
> I wrote these down before contacting you. If any holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local extract in this repository)
> - Lu, Li and Liang, "The role of minerals in prebiotic evolution", Fundamental Research 2026: https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC13644521
> - Simonis et al., "Abiotic synthesis of RNase-resistant polymers via thermodynamically controlled wet-dry cycling", Scientific Reports 2026: https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC13518880
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the figures are public. Run the four constraints on a setting you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time,
> [name]
