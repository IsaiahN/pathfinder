## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical statement is in the figures README: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and stops at the limit of what the instruments can distinguish. Fig 13 :133: *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question"*. The shape is what remains when the search has been narrowed enough to describe but not enough to name.

For this question the shape is four constraints held at once:

- a spendable difference, not energy in the abstract (Fig 13 :14: *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*; Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent"*);
- a spatial gradient or containment that holds reactants (Fig 13 :10: *"near, far, between, contained"*);
- an ordered succession that re-enters its own output (Fig 13 :12: *"the ordered succession of differences"*; Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*);
- a bonded arrangement of reacting entities, not an element list (Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).

A ranked list of named settings is not a shape. It is a name with a score attached.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the target, then narrow recursively. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: when contact is not possible, send an effect description. *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Fig 6 :30 carries the same rule: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* distinguishes adding a term from adding an axis: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."*
- *How Intelligence Actually Works* states what the narrowing is for: *"The mapping is not a means to intelligence; the mapping is the intelligence."* A shape is a mapping narrowed to the point where a ground can refuse it.

The pre-registered refuter in `01` is this discipline applied to the question. Writing down what would refute the shape turns a preference the loop cannot see into an ordinary term that can accrue a residual.

## 3. What licenses a name

Nothing inside the shape licenses a name. Two things outside it do:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. A measured gradient, an experiment or an ancient rock can bind a name to the shape.
2. **A provenanced import whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :26: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).

*They're Scanning Books* warns that even declared primitives are a choice: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* Naming a setting is the same kind of choice, and it must be recorded as the searcher's contribution until the ground pays.

## 4. Why a ranked list is the specific forbidden move

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and cannot say whether the source was one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK or a scheme not yet found. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. The green reading does not determine the source. It was a spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

A setting that "ticks four boxes" is the same projection error. Several quite different configurations can produce the same checkbox pattern, and the pattern cannot say which one actually ran the chemistry. What is owed is a discriminating measurement.

The second failure is correlation. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* says *"You may all agree and be wrong"* and *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* *How a Society of Agents Would Work* is blunter: *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different."* The seat map states the rule directly: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A shortlist assembled from researchers who share the same evidence pool is a convention with the appearance of a witness. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 5. Correction to my own earlier reading

An earlier draft of this package treated "shape, not a name" as a ban on ever naming a setting. That overstates it. The README phrase governs a **missing** thing, something not held in the library. Hydrothermal vents, hot springs, ice interfaces and tidal pools are already in the world frame. Naming one is a binding from that frame onto the four-constraint shape, not the invention of a new primitive.

The correction does not soften the verdict. Two other points survive unchanged:

- The four constraints are a **union compound**, so no name is licensed until each live slot is settled and the cross-stage handoff is accounted for (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).
- The binding is ungrounded until a ground pays. Calling a name derived because it fits the shape is the projection error of section 4.

Both the retracted overstatement and the corrected form are kept here so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Honest standing

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* is explicit about output like this: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"*. It also calls such output *"simulacra"*.

This document is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a ground reading. It cannot settle which geochemical setting, if any, satisfies all four constraints at once. Its only defensible output is the shape, the written refuter and the record of where the name slot remains open.
