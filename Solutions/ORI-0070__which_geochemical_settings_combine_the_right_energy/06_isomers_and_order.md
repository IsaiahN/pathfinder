# Isomers and order: supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** Nothing here is `grounded`. This file supports `01` and `07` by saying exactly how the four constraints are structured, in what order they may be settled, and which analogues are licensed.

## 1. The structure is a union compound

The question reads like four checkboxes. Fig 9 :55 says that reading is wrong: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Applied here, the four wanted properties overlap inside any one setting and remain separately binding. A case that satisfies only energy is not a harder case of the answer. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. So the residual stays indexed by slot:

| slot | state or structure | spent as | failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 energy | energy bonded to a gradient | a flow | conserved, finite, directionless alone |
| S2 concentration | gradient bonded to space | proximity and containment | dilution, loss of products |
| S3 stability | ordering bonded to time | a cycle that re-enters its output | a one-off chain, no closure |
| S4 ingredients | network with typed bonds | a recorded arrangement | a flat element list |

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single setting score would throw away the only signal that says where to look. The cross-setting handoff is a second-order union compound: the product of one stage is the input to another, and it is composed only after each first-order stage is settled.

**Structural claim.** The answer shape is the union of four non-interchangeable slots plus a rule for handing material between stages. It is not a partition of one question and not a ranking of names.

## 2. The order is by dependency, not by importance

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order this derivation actually used:

1. **State the residual in effect terms.** What is being decided is whether chemistry can keep going rather than stall. This comes before any figure line is chosen.
2. **Write the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** THE_FORMULA, Direction: a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Binding the shape makes it refusable.
3. **Refuse before mint.** A name that lacks a spendable gradient fails support, not novelty. No name is minted in this folder.
4. **Settle S1 before S2, S2 before the compound.** Energy without a spendable difference cannot drive concentration; concentration without retention cannot feed a cycle.
5. **Use filters only for the budget.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
6. **Ask for the refuter before showing theory.** Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

Reversing this order is the specific error of starting with a shortlist. A list of settings is a recording of where previous searches stopped, not a generator of the answer. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*

## 3. Isomers: same atoms, different bond, different term

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

This is the right analogue for vents versus hot springs versus ice interfaces. They share much of the same atomic inventory (water, rock, redox pairs, monomers, mineral surfaces). What differs is the bonding:

- a vent bonds a sustained redox gradient to a flow-through state, with long persistence but poor retention of products;
- a hot spring bonds a gradient to space through wet-dry containment, with strong local concentration but a different persistence problem;
- an ice interface bonds concentration to a freeze-thaw succession, a different temporal operand again.

Fig 13 :76 settles why these cannot be merged: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term. Here the operands are the same and the bonds are not, so the terms are isomers, not spellings.

**What the Operators table does not license.** It defines its threshold symbol as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. It compares two quantities. It is not an operator that fuses four settings into one name. An earlier draft tried to use it that way and the reading was refused in `01`.

## 4. Two licensed analogues

- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: *"A part that survives its own use."* This is why mineral surfaces matter in the world frame. Lu, Li and Liang 2026 report minerals that catalyze reactions and stabilize intermediates. That is corroboration from a world source, not a derivation of a name.
- **Recursion across levels.** Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So a stage that has been settled against a ground can be an operand at the next stage without adding a primitive.

Both analogues are used in `07` only to explain shape and order. Neither is used to smuggle in a setting name.

## 5. Falsifier, written before the conclusion

This supporting derivation is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single physical variable is shown to be both a spatial gradient and an ordered temporal succession with no change of bonded state (breaks the slot distinction in section 1 and the Fig 13 :76 reading).
2. A measured setting satisfies all four slots simultaneously across all reaction stages (breaks the union conclusion at the world frame; the figure shape itself would survive, but its application here would not).
3. An ordering is found that makes the four slots independent of dependency, so that S3 could be settled before S1 with no loss (breaks Fig 3 :20).
4. A bonded arrangement is shown to make no difference to behaviour in a prebiotic network (breaks Fig 12 :117 and the isomer reading).
5. The threshold relation in the Operators table is shown to be a fusion operator over four operands (breaks the refusal in section 3).

Until one of these is run, sections 1 to 4 are `specified`, ungrounded candidates. A reported failure is more useful than a reported success, and any failure will be kept visible in `11_audit.md` rather than silently repaired.
