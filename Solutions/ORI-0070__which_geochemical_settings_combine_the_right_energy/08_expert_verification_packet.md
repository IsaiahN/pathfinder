## 0. The claim under check

The question *which geochemical settings combine the right energy, concentration, stability, and chemical ingredients?* is a **union compound of four non-interchangeable constraints**, not four checkboxes that a named setting can tick. Any candidate setting must simultaneously supply:

1. a **spendable difference** (a gradient bonded to a state), not energy in the abstract;
2. a **spatial gradient or containment** that holds reactants;
3. a **temporal succession that closes**, where the product of one pass re-enters as the input of the next;
4. a **bonded reacting network**, not an element list.

The figures return this shape. They do not return the name of a setting. Vents, hot springs, ice interfaces and tidal pools are bindings from the world frame onto the shape, and which binding is right is a question for a ground.

This claim is `specified`, not `grounded`. Nothing here has been measured in a chamber, a rock or a field site.

## 1. Sub-claim A: energy is a spendable difference, and concentration is a different bonded term

**Verbatim lines.** Fig 13 :14 "conserved, finite, directionless alone". Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent". Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fig 13 :10 "near, far, between, contained".

**What it predicts.** A setting with heat, light or redox potential but no measured spendable gradient does not satisfy slot 1. A setting that concentrates reactants spatially but does not hold them does not satisfy slot 2. The two failures are different and are not traded against each other.

**Refuter.** Show a directionless energy term, with no spendable difference, driving a sustained reaction network; or show one physical variable that is both a spatial gradient and an ordered temporal succession with no change of bonded state.

**Checkable in twenty minutes?** Yes, by reading the four lines above in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and asking whether any reading of the two failures merges them. No measurement needed for the figure check.

## 2. Sub-claim B: stability is a closing cycle, and ingredients are a bonded arrangement

**Verbatim lines.** Fig 13 :12 "the ordered succession of differences". Fig 12 :121 "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :119 "A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."

**What it predicts.** Persistence is not duration. Wet-dry, heating-cooling and freeze-thaw are candidate closures because the product of one pass is the input of the next. A single dehydration is a chain and cannot close by construction. A catalyst is the part that survives its own use, which is why mineral surfaces enter the world literature. Listing CHNOPS, minerals or monomer concentrations records atoms and omits the relation.

**Refuter.** Show a one-off event that re-enters its own output across geological time without a cycle; or show a bonded arrangement that makes no difference to behaviour in a prebiotic network.

**Checkable in twenty minutes?** Yes, the five lines are short and the refuters are stated before any world evidence is shown. The world check is two abstracts: Simonis et al. 2026 for the chamber, Lu et al. 2026 for mineral surfaces. Text: *"thermodynamic control within an environmental simulation chamber can drive the formation of complex, enzyme-resistant biopolymers"* and *"By adsorbing and concentrating reactants, catalyzing key reactions, stabilizing intermediates, and providing protective microenvironments"*.

## 3. Sub-claim C: the four constraints form a union, and the name is not derived

**Verbatim lines.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133 "A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

**What it predicts.** A four-checkbox ranking is a projection, not a verdict. Several different settings can produce the same checkbox pattern, and the pattern cannot say which one ran the chemistry. The settings are enumerated from the world and their valence is relative to the residual. The figures cannot rank them.

**Refuter.** Produce a measured setting that satisfies all four slots simultaneously and across all reaction stages; or derive the name of a specific setting from the six-term set alone, with no world-frame binding and no measurement.

**Checkable in twenty minutes?** The figure half is. The world half is not: the closest world statement, Lu, Li and Liang 2026, says *"prebiotic evolution probably proceeded through complementary reactions distributed among multiple geological settings"*. That is corroboration from one witness, not a proof, and it is labelled as such.

## 4. Non-claims, stated before the check

- No claim that vents beat hot springs, or that ice interfaces or tidal pools are excluded.
- No claim that any named setting satisfies the four slots.
- No claim that wet-dry cycling is sufficient, or that a single closure was measured against the other three.
- No claim that the four constraints are a partition, or that a checkbox count is a verdict.
- No claim that agreement among readers corroborates the claim, especially where they share the origins-of-life evidence pool. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- No claim that the absence of a single setting in the literature proves none exists. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
