- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

- Order derived:

  2. **Vocabulary:** terms for what structure affords (averages, variance, lensing convergence, selection). Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked."

  4. **Plan and carry:** choose instruments and record what worked with provenance.
- **Strain check:** most published effort lands above the break, because there the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30: "Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist"). Reading the question from "dark energy" downward is a reading of nothing at the description link. No strain after re-census: Fig 3 governs the order of links; Fig 9 governs the union shape. ### Q3 - Are the explanations isomers of one another?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The "atoms" of the rival explanations are the same: a dimming, a redshift dependence, and structure along the line of sight. The **arrangement** differs:
  - backreaction: structure changes the effective global expansion (the dimming is dynamical);
  - lensing/selection: structure changes the observed flux or the selection function (the dimming is observational);
  - inference/modeling: the pipeline maps the same data to a different parameter (the dimming is a model artifact).
- By Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers of the explanation**: same observational atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the Hubble-diagram formula and decisive in what would settle them. As the question currently bundles them they share a **functional group** (a structure-related residual), with the qualifier deciding which member.
- This is a structural analogue, `general`, not a physics derivation of any model. The metamerism case in *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23) is the same shape: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The instrument reads green and "it cannot comment on the source."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two of the three explanations can be written with the same arrangement over the same atoms (so that they are the same substance under a different name), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. If a source-discriminating measurement separates them, the isomer reading is confirmed at the ground and the `gap` on magnitude closes.

### Q4 - What does the union compose to?

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of three overlapping conditions, worked in dependency order (describe, vocabulary, objective, instruments), and its rival explanations are isomers of one observational reading: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The isomer claim is `general` structural, with a stated falsifier. The union and order claims are `specified`. Neither supplies the magnitude, which remains `gap` at the figure level (Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,").
