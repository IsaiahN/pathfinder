# Outreach draft: check the test design before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No request for trust, agreement or endorsement.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker can see the residual, the seal and the refuter without any framework interior. If the stage-1 object cannot be checked on its own, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about validation that you can refute in five minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in model validation: what would count as evidence that a model's predictions carry over to new conditions, rather than that it fits the data it was trained on? I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to read a theory first. If the claim below fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` shape, `specified` prediction, not `grounded`):** a transport test needs three things. (1) An anchor the model cannot update, meaning the test conditions are sealed before model selection and never used for tuning. (2) Test conditions that draw on a different evidence pool from training, not a random split of the same historical data. (3) The residual reported per condition, including the worst case, rather than a single average. The reason is structural: a fit score is produced by the frame being scored, so it cannot settle a question about the world. A holdout drawn from the training distribution substitutes the very habitat the claim is about.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular model generalizes; that cross-validation is useless; that a passed suite proves transport. A finite set of new conditions leaves the model a candidate, not a validated generalizer.
>
> **What would refute the claim. I wrote these down before writing the rest.**
> 1. Showing that a score the model produces itself can settle a transport question with no range against an independent observation.
> 2. Showing that train and test samples from one evidence pool carry as much information about deployment behaviour as samples from conditions whose closures differ.
> 3. Showing that a substituted test habitat demonstrates capability rather than wiring.
> 4. Showing that averaging residuals across conditions preserves every live per-condition failure.
> 5. Showing that a finite suite of unseen conditions proves absence of failure.
>
> If any holds, I retract the corresponding point. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source material. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I can also send the formula as a method to reuse, with the request that you write your own refuter and state your own mode before running it.

| Offer the formula only at stage 3 | Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| Never pool later agreement | Fig 2 :40; a reply is a conversation, not a witness. |
| State the stake | The author of this package benefits if the shape survives. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." The stake is declared here rather than left unnamed. |

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the three-part shape claim and the five refuters. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the checker is curious, send the source links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. Their later agreement is never added to the evidence for stage 1.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method. Ask each user to state a ground and a refuter before they run it. A reported failure is more useful than a reported success.
