**Question:** How can researchers test whether a model's predictions generalize to new conditions rather than merely fit historical data?

**Source:** `Questions/complexity science.txt` line 47

**Section:** 4. Prediction, uncertainty, and causal inference

**Field for public contact points:** model validation research

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape of a generalization test, `specified` on the falsifiable predictions about same-pool splits and per-condition reporting. Nothing here is `grounded`: no measurement was taken and no model was deployed.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Boundary and standing.** This is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a ground reading. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, which was opened 2026-11-22: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." ## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation that Fig 2 :40 names. The ask in `02_outreach_email.md` is for a refutation, not an endorsement.
