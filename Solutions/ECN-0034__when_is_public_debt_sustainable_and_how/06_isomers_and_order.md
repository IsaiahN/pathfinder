# Isomers and order: sovereigns over a fixed atom set

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two sovereigns can share a headline debt ratio and differ radically in serviceability. What is the structural relation between them, and in what order must the measurement chain be read? Two questions are owed:

1. Are two sovereigns with the same debt ratio but different structure **isomers** under Fig 12's definition?
2. What is the dependency order of the sustainability measurement chain, and where does it break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms already held in the economics frame: debt stock, GDP, interest rate r, growth rate g, primary balance, maturity, currency of issue, creditor class, contingent liabilities, monetary regime, fiscal institutions. Bonds held: the seven, including ⋛ comparison, → sequence, and − subtraction. No new primitive is needed. Negation ¬ is available for refusal (Operators table :58).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are two sovereigns with the same ratio isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fix the atom set to `{debt stock, GDP, r, g, primary balance, maturity, currency, creditor class}`. Two sovereigns A and B share every atom value except the **bond** between currency of issue and the revenue base: A issues in its own currency and bonds it to domestic tax revenue; B issues in foreign currency and bonds it to foreign exchange earnings.
- The ingredient list (the headline ratio) is identical. The behaviour under a terms-of-trade shock is not. By Fig 12's definition these are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- This is not an analogy. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." The ratio records the ingredients and not the bond, which is exactly the flat-formula failure Fig 12 :5 names: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two sovereigns with identical atom values and identical bond structure (same maturity, currency, creditor class and regime) show systematically different servicing outcomes across repeated episodes, the isomer claim fails and a hidden atom is owed. That would be an import, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), not a re-description.
- **Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 and the world source on foreign-currency versus domestic-currency debt (Wikipedia, Debt-to-GDP ratio, 2026), which is low overlap with the framework.

### Q2 — Is a sustainability label a functional group or an isomer?

- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The pattern `service capacity ⋛ service obligation` is a functional group. It says what kind of thing a sustainability judgement is (a comparison). The qualifier (which sovereign, which slots) says which member.
- Two sovereigns that differ only in the qualifier are not isomers of each other in the strong sense; they are two members of the same functional group. They become isomers only when the **bond** differs while the atoms are held fixed (Q1).
- **Falsifier:** if the comparison pattern can be missing entirely from a sustainability judgement and the judgement still settles at the ground, the functional-group claim fails.

### Q3 — What is the dependency order of the measurement chain?

Per Fig 3:

| link | content for this question | status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | debt stock, maturity, currency, rates, primary balance | usually reached |
| 2 vocabulary | terms for what the fiscal situation affords: rollover, solvency, space, forced adjustment | often assumed, rarely measured |
| 3 objective | what counts as progress: servicing without a disruptive adjustment | the usual break |
| 4 planning | the fiscal path and contingency funding | cannot be read if 3 is broken |
| 5 learn and carry | name what worked, verify, carry to the next episode | blocked by 4 |

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

**Consequence:** adding more debt data above the break (link 1) cannot rescue a missing definition of what counts as sustainable (link 3). This is the structural reason why "more measurement" is not the answer to "how should sustainability be measured." The objective link must be instrumented first.

### Q4 — What does the two-currency price say about the choice of measure?

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

- A highly disaggregated per-slot measure is expensive in bits (many descriptions) and may be cheap in actions (one data extract settles it).
- A compact aggregate ratio is cheap in bits and can be expensive in actions (a hidden maturity wall takes a whole crisis to settle).
- Neither figure predicts the other, so both readings are taken or the choice is made blind. Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- **Mode:** `general` on the two-currency rule, `specified` on this application.

### Q5 — Comparison bond type at second order (live strain)

- Fig 12 :87 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities." The Operators table :51 gives the test: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- First order: r against g is a clean comparison of two quantities. Debt service against revenue is another.
- Second order: the operands become whole service-capacity and service-obligation compositions, each settled and serving as an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it.").
- Whether these compositions count as "quantities" at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. I record this as a **live strain**, not a correction. It is named in `01` section 7, `08` and `09`, and its refuter is in `01` section 4.

## CONCLUSION

Two sovereigns with the same headline ratio but different currency or maturity bonds are **isomers** over a fixed atom set: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the ratio and decisive in behaviour. The general pattern `service capacity ⋛ service obligation` is a **functional group**, with each sovereign as a member. The measurement chain breaks at the objective link, so more data cannot substitute for a stated and instrumented definition of what counts as sustainable. The comparison bond is clean at first order and strained at second order, and that strain is recorded rather than hidden.
