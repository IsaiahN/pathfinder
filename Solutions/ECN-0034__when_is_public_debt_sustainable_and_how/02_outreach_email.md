**Subject:** One checkable claim about how debt sustainability is measured

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a set of figures, not a measurement of any sovereign.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "public debt is sustainable" is not a stock level and not a single threshold. It is a per-slot comparison between service capacity and service obligation, judged against a ground that does not move when a model moves. Four conditions survive the figures:
>
> 1. The anchor is realized servicing (payment, rollover, restructuring, forced adjustment), not a rating or a ratio that changes because a rater changed a model.
> 2. The residual is measured per slot: maturity, currency of issue, creditor class, contingent liabilities. A headline ratio is a pooled reading that hides a live rollover signal.
> 3. The measure is a threshold relation between two quantities: interest rate against growth rate, service against revenue, adjustment required against adjustment available.
> 4. Sustainability is a property of the sovereign in its habitat (monetary regime, fiscal institutions, demographics, creditor base), not of the debt number alone.
>
> What the figures return is this shape, not a number. I am explicitly not claiming a universal safe debt ratio, that r below g is sufficient, or that the inter-temporal budget constraint is the ground. Any such number is a filter for attention, not a witness for the verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a single aggregate ratio shown to preserve every live slot residual;
> 2. a chosen anchor shown to move when a frame pushes on it and still counted as the anchor;
> 3. sustainability shown to be a symmetric conjunction of stock levels rather than a threshold comparison;
> 4. two sovereigns with identical ratio, maturity, currency and creditor composition shown to have systematically identical servicing outcomes across different monetary regimes;
> 5. a universal threshold derived from the framework itself instead of imported from the empirical literature.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
