# Expert verification packet: the Tether on public debt sustainability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public-finance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No sovereign measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Public debt is sustainable" is not a stock level and not a single threshold. It is a per-slot, ground-anchored comparison between service capacity and service obligation, reported over a named habitat. Four conditions survive the figures: the anchor is realized servicing, which does not move when a model moves; the residual is indexed per slot (maturity, currency, creditor, contingent liabilities), because an average hides a live rollover signal; the measure is a threshold relation between two quantities (r against g, service against revenue, adjustment required against adjustment available); and the property belongs to the sovereign in its habitat, not to the debt number. The framework returns this shape, not a number. Universal thresholds are filters for attention, not witnesses for the verdict. Five refuters were written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the residual must be indexed per slot and the anchor must not update on the frames it corrects. A headline ratio is a pooled reading, and a rating is an instrument, not a ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients," and "saturated metrics, pooled readings."
- **What to check:** is there a sovereign episode where the headline ratio was unchanged and one maturity or currency slot still failed to roll over? If yes, the averaging failure is real. If such a case cannot exist in principle, C1 fails.
- **Refuter:** show that a single aggregate preserves every live slot residual, or show the chosen anchor moves when a frame pushes on it and is still counted as the anchor. Either breaks Fig 1 :19/:22 or Fig 2 :29-30.

### C2 — economics-facing; checkable against the fiscal sustainability literature

- **Statement:** the measure is a threshold comparison, not a stock level; there is no consensus operational definition, and the inter-temporal budget constraint is a candidate instrument, not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Operators table :51: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- **What to check:** Wikipedia, Fiscal sustainability (2026) states "There is no consensus among economists on a precise operational definition for fiscal sustainability" and that "Many economists have voiced grave concerns over using inter-temporal budget constraint as a de facto definition." Wikipedia, Debt-to-GDP ratio (2026) states "there is no universally agreed 'safe' or 'dangerous' debt-to-GDP threshold." Do these claims survive a check against the primary sources they cite? Does the fiscal gap or S2 indicator behave as a comparison rather than a level?
- **Refuter:** show sustainability is a symmetric conjunction of stock levels, or show a universal threshold is derived from the framework itself rather than imported. Either breaks Fig 12 :87 or Fig 9 :60.

### C3 — habitat and instrument; checkable against a named sovereign

- **Statement:** sustainability is a property of the sovereign in its habitat, and the missing thing is a discriminator among the sources of one rollover-success reading.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."
- **What to check:** take one sovereign. Enumerate maturity, currency of issue, creditor base, central-bank regime, fiscal institutions and contingent liabilities. Does a single rollover-success reading discriminate among low ratio, r < g, own-currency issuance, captive creditors and a backstop? If not, name the instrument that would.
- **Refuter:** show two sovereigns with identical ratio, maturity, currency and creditor composition have systematically identical servicing outcomes across different monetary regimes (breaks Fig 11 :39-40), or demonstrate a discriminator that names the source from one reading (closes the shape-not-a-name limit).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a sustainable debt ratio, a safe threshold or an operational definition.
- Not that r < g is sufficient; it is one comparison in one slot.
- Not that the inter-temporal budget constraint is the ground.
- Not that a market or a rating agency is the anchor (Fig 2 :29-30).
- Not a verdict in the MMT versus mainstream macroeconomics dispute.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a sovereign measurement.
- Not that the framework names which slots settle a case. The slot list is a visible choice (Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual.").

## 5. What would settle it

A ground-visible event plus a discriminator, not further argument.

1. A rollover failure in one slot while the headline ratio is unchanged (settles the per-slot claim at the ground).
2. An instrument that distinguishes low ratio, r < g, own-currency issuance, captive creditor base and central-bank backstop from one reading (names the currently missing source).
3. A cross-regime comparison of atom-identical sovereigns (settles the habitat claim).
4. Any one of the five refuters in `01` section 4 firing (withdraws or narrows the shape).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :29, Fig 9 :60, Fig 11 :39-40 and :44, Fig 12 :87. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the sources cited: is there really no consensus definition, and is the inter-temporal budget constraint really contested?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish the sources of a rollover-success reading, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
