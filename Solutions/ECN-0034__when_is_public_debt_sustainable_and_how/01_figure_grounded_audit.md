## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks two things that are often bundled: (1) when is a debt path sustainable, and (2) how should sustainability be measured. Stated as an effect:

- **R (the gap).** The distance between what a fiscal model predicts (the debt path stabilizes, financing needs are met, announced policies are implemented) and what the ground returns (rollover succeeds or fails, spreads move, restructuring happens, inflation erodes the real value, a disruptive adjustment is forced).
- **Arity.** Multiple slots, not one scalar: maturity buckets, currency of denomination, creditor class, contingent liabilities, the primary balance path, and the monetary regime. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. The core relations are comparisons: interest rate r against growth rate g, debt service against revenue, adjustment required against adjustment available. Fig 12 :87 defines the comparison bond ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities."
- **Scale.** First order inside each slot (one maturity wall, one currency exposure, one creditor base), second order at the sovereign level. The aggregate debt-to-GDP ratio is a second-order composition, not the ground.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"). The shape this audit tests is: *sustainability is a per-slot, ground-anchored comparison between service capacity and service obligation, reported as a shape until a ground settles a name; any single threshold is a filter for attention, not a witness for the verdict.* Writing that down lets it accrue a residual and be refused. The pre-registered refuter in section 4 is that refusal.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale is stated above. One line per candidate domain, with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union | order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Sovereign fiscal mechanics | treasury, tax authority, debt manager, bondholders | cash flows: revenue, interest, principal, rollover | realized servicing, a market or counter that does not move because a model moved | union of liquidity, solvency, institutional capacity and political feasibility | first order |
| D2 Measurement and forecasting frame | DSA models, rating agencies, fiscal councils, debt/GDP, r-g, fiscal gap, S2 | indicators and projections | should be the ground; often an internal criterion such as the inter-temporal budget constraint | partition boundary question: which indicator is admissible evidence | second order, consumes D1 data |
| D3 Verification and outreach frame | expert readers, journals, public fiscal institutions | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | partition: which frame can refute | third order, consumes D1 and D2 |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain.** A proposed D4, "market sentiment as an independent anchor," is refused at census: a market that updates on what frames think is a frame, not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30, and Fig 2 :34 on coherent drift). It is folded into D1 as an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure.

**Union, not partition.** D1 is a union of overlapping conditions. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever (cut the debt ratio) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** The chain runs: perception (debt stock, maturity, currency, rates) then vocabulary (terms for what the fiscal situation affords: rollover, solvency, space) then objective (what counts as sustainable) then planning (the fiscal path) then learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is where this question usually breaks. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." More data above the break cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

Strain test applied as each finding is written. If a line only fits with a strain, it governs another quantity and the census is redone. Strains are named in section 7, not smoothed over.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 — The ground for "sustainable" is realized servicing, not agreement among raters (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when" and Fig 2 :30: "you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

A debt-to-GDP ratio, a credit rating, and a DSA rating are instruments. They become anchors only if they stop moving when a model moves. They generally do not. Fig 2 :37-38 warns that an instrument "can be wrong on axes they do not measure." The realized servicing outcome (did the sovereign pay, on time, in the promised currency, without a forced adjustment) is the ground. This is **triangulated** across Fig 1 and Fig 2, two figures with low overlap (one governs metric selection, the other governs correction).

**Mode:** `general` on the structure of the anchor, `specified` when applied to a named sovereign.

### F2 — An aggregate ratio hides the live rollover slot (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A sovereign can sit at a comfortable headline debt ratio and still carry a live short-term foreign-currency maturity wall. The average is precisely where that signal disappears. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :25-26 lists the channel failures: "stale percepts, dropped transients," and "saturated metrics, pooled readings." A single ratio is a pooled reading.

**Mode:** `specified`. Refuter: show that a single aggregate preserves every live slot residual. Triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 10.

### F3 — The measure is a comparison, not a stock level (triangulated)

Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities." The Operators table :51 says a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one.

The standard debt dynamics identity puts the comparison at the centre: the change in the debt ratio depends on (r - g) applied to the existing ratio plus the primary balance. World source (Wikipedia, Debt-to-GDP ratio, 2026) gives this identity explicitly. The fiscal sustainability literature adds the fiscal gap and the infinite-horizon tax gap (S2), both of which are adjustments required against adjustments projected. These are ⋛ relations: "a threshold relation between two quantities," not a conjunction of ingredients.

A vocabulary holding only the stock level can name what a sovereign has and cannot say how far it is from serviceability. Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." **Triangulated** across Fig 12, the Operators table, and the world literature (low overlap: a formal bond definition versus an empirical fiscal-gap measure).

**Mode:** `general` on the bond type, `specified` on the r-g and fiscal-gap reading.

### F4 — No universal threshold exists; any number is a filter, not a witness (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A 60 percent Maastricht reference value or a 90 percent growth-at-risk finding is a filter: it decides where scarce attention goes. It cannot be the witness that settles a particular sovereign, because the ground is realized servicing. World source (Wikipedia, Debt-to-GDP ratio, 2026): "Economists and international institutions caution that there is no universally agreed 'safe' or 'dangerous' debt-to-GDP threshold; the sustainability of public debt depends on factors such as growth prospects, interest rates, and fiscal institutions." World source (Wikipedia, Fiscal sustainability, 2026): "There is no consensus among economists on a precise operational definition for fiscal sustainability, rather different studies use their own, often similar, definitions."

**Triangulated** across Fig 9 (framework) and two world sources with different scopes. The world sources are one economics evidence pool, not two independent witnesses, but they agree with the figure verdict rather than driving it.

**Mode:** `specified` with world corroboration.

### F5 — Question 1 and Question 2 are a union with different anchors (triangulated)

Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :4-5: disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working on a different subset, "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

"When is it sustainable" asks about the sovereign-in-habitat and settles at realized servicing. "How should it be measured" asks about the indicator and settles at an instrument that discriminates the slots. They are not one question with two halves; each has its own loop and its own ground. **Mode:** `general` on the split, `specified` per domain. Triangulated across Fig 9 and the domain census.

### F6 — Sustainability is a property of the sovereign-in-habitat, not of the debt number (triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." For this residual the enumerated habitat includes the maturity profile, currency of issue, creditor base, central-bank regime, fiscal institutions, demographic path and contingent liabilities. Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

This is why a government that issues its own currency is a **different habitat**, not a point on the same debt-ratio scale. World source (Wikipedia, Fiscal sustainability, 2026) records that "Many economists have voiced grave concerns over using inter-temporal budget constraint as a de facto definition or criterion for fiscal sustainability" and that under plausible assumptions it is "in fact not the correct criterion for sustainability." A criterion that ignores the habitat is the wrong instrument on that axis. **Triangulated** across Fig 11 and the world literature.

**Mode:** `specified`.

### F7 — The framework returns a shape, not a threshold (single-source on the figure side, world corroborated)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The canonical figures README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."

The shape returned here is the four-condition separator in section 5: non-updating ground, per-slot indexing, comparison structure, and habitat enumeration. The framework does not return 60 percent, 90 percent, or any operational definition. A specific threshold is a **binding from the empirical frame**, provenanced as such, not a derivation and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."). Marked **single-source** on the figure side (Fig 9 plus the README are the same framework pool) with world corroboration from F4.

**Mode:** `specified`.

### F8 — Unreachable versus underivable; the proto-instrument condition (triangulated)

Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

For a sovereign with no market signal and no reliable data, there is no instrument to sharpen. There is no edge to extend. This is distinct from a threshold that is already in the closure and was not found. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The honest output for the universal threshold is therefore `gap` / unreachable-at-this-budget, stated as a verdict and not as a tentative silence. **Triangulated** across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 13.

**Mode:** `general` on the distinction, `specified` on this application.

### F9 — Expert agreement cannot corroborate the shape (triangulated)

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

A panel of fiscal economists who read the same DSA templates is one evidence pool. A rating consensus built on the same models is one pool. What is useful is a low-overlap frame that ranges against realized servicing separately. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." **Triangulated** across Fig 2, Fig 7 and Fig 10.

**Mode:** `general`.

### F10 — "Sustainable" must be a refusal-capable term (single-source on the operator, world corroborated)

Fig 5 :41: "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely." Formula step 3 gates a term by SUPPORT, REACHABILITY and NOVELTY, and step 5 adds settlement: the ground settles it, a gate is not the ground.

The Operators table :58 says "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A sustainability label that cannot be refused (that can only be re-rated upward or downward without ever being withdrawn) is an accumulating term, not a checkable one. The pre-registered refuter in section 4 supplies the negation. Marked **single-source** on the figure/operator side, with world corroboration from the Herndon, Ash and Pollin episode recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md`: a widely cited threshold finding was corrected by a graduate student finding a spreadsheet error, which is a refusal of a held term, not a new term.

**Mode:** `specified`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Per-slot refuter.** A single aggregate debt ratio is shown to preserve every live slot residual, so Fig 1 :19 and :22 do not govern this case.
2. **Anchor refuter.** The chosen ground is shown to move when a frame pushes on it (a rating that changes because the rater changed its model, not because servicing changed) and is still treated as the anchor. This breaks Fig 2 :29-30.
3. **Comparison refuter.** Sustainability is shown to be a symmetric conjunction of stock levels rather than a threshold relation between quantities. This breaks Fig 12 :87 and the Operators table.
4. **Habitat refuter.** Two sovereigns with identical debt ratios, maturity, currency and creditor composition are shown to have systematically identical servicing outcomes across regimes with different monetary and institutional contact. This breaks Fig 11 :39-40.
5. **Filter refuter.** A universal threshold is derived from the framework itself rather than imported from the empirical frame. This breaks Fig 9 :60 and the import rule in Fig 6 :23.
6. **Instrument refuter.** A discriminator is demonstrated that names the slot from a single reading where several sources produce that reading, so the shape-not-a-name limit does not govern here.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a threshold (Fig 10 :52, Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Report sustainability per slot, and keep the aggregate ratio explicitly labelled as a second-order summary, not the ground (Fig 1 :22).
3. Express the measure as comparisons: r against g, service against revenue, adjustment required against adjustment available (Fig 12 :87).
4. Enumerate the habitat for each sovereign before applying any rule (Fig 11 :44, :48).
5. Treat every threshold as a filter for the budget. Use witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
6. Hand the outside frame a characterised residual and ask what would refute it. Do not hand them the framework first (Fig 10 :42, :52).
7. State the mode on every substantive claim. A shape is `specified`, never `grounded`, until a ground pays for it.

## 7. Strains, re-census and honest gaps

- **S1, stock versus comparison.** First pass read debt/GDP as the quantity. Re-census: the ratio is a state summary; sustainability is the comparison over it (Fig 12 :87). Strain dissolved.
- **S2, threshold versus verdict.** A threshold is useful for triage, which is a budget use (Fig 9 :60). Promoted into a verdict it becomes a filter handing down an answer. Kept as a live design constraint, not a contradiction.
- **S3, DSA ratings as anchor.** Re-census puts ratings in D2 as instruments. They become anchors only if they stop updating when a model changes. Kept visible.
- **Live strain.** The comparison bond ⋛ is defined over "two quantities" (Fig 12 :87). At second order the operands can be whole service-capacity and service-obligation compositions. Whether these count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`, not hidden.
- **Declared gap.** No figure line returns a universal sustainable debt ratio or an operational threshold. This is the shape-not-a-name gap, reported as unreachable-at-this-budget (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39), not as a silence and not as an invitation to invent a number.

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives. Sustainability is a per-slot, ground-anchored comparison over a named habitat, reported as a shape until a ground settles a name. A single ratio is a filter, not a witness; a rating is an instrument, not an anchor; agreement among similarly equipped frames is one pool, not corroboration. What does not survive is any universal threshold, any claim that r < g alone closes the question, and any treatment of the inter-temporal budget constraint as the ground. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. After re-census: one live type strain (the operand type of ⋛ at second order), one declared gap (the universal threshold), both recorded rather than smoothed.
