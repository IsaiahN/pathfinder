# Outreach draft: validate the shape claim first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are understood well enough to defend without the theory. If the stage-1 claim does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about replication fidelity before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific, checkable claim about early RNA replication. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** "Copy accurately enough to persist but inaccurately enough to evolve" is not one optimisation problem with a best error rate. It is two conditions that share a field: the information that made the molecule a replicator must survive each handoff, and the next generation must differ from the last so that the habitat can vote between variants. In the figures' terms, progress per handoff is a product of the bracket's fidelity and the ground's pressure on the room. Zero either factor and the chain runs in place. Too faithful, and a copy is an identity bond: one more mirror in a chain of mirrors, motion without progress. Too loose, and the copying channel loses contact with what worked, the remainder compounds, and the population disperses into random sequences. The answer's shape is a **band with two failure edges**, not a number. Copy errors are per-site residuals, not a single global average.
>
> **What I explicitly do not claim.** I do not claim the framework derives a critical mutation rate. That number comes from quasispecies theory, a different frame, and I would present it as a provenanced import with a source gate, not as a figure result. I also do not claim the sharp threshold is universal: it depends on the fitness landscape, and where lethal genotypes exist the sharp threshold is absent even though high mutation pressure still destroys localisation. I do not claim a laboratory ribozyme demonstration is the same as a sustained evolutionary system; a synthetic harness proves wiring, not capability. And this is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before this run:
> 1. A self-replicating system that stays perfectly faithful across generations and still accumulates adaptive heritable variation with no external novelty.
> 2. A system with positive per-generation progress when bracket fidelity is zero or when the habitat applies no differential pressure.
> 3. A re-census showing that the handoff line I rely on governs a different quantity than molecular copying.
> 4. A measured system that keeps information and evolvability where the threshold relation predicts dispersal, in a landscape where that relation's stated preconditions hold.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local copy in this package): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. The governing lines are Fig 7 :26 and :37-39.
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to check the method:** the eight-step loop is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Shape claim and refuter first, theory later | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors before recording a verdict. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A recipient selected for likely agreement is a mirror. |
| Two failure edges, not one optimum | Fig 9 :54-58, union rather than partition; Fig 7 :37-39, zero either factor stops the chain. |
| State the mode on the claim | Formula mode rule: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* An unmeasured band is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict. |
| Numeric threshold withheld and labelled an import | Fig 8 :50-52, convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. |
| Landscape conditionality stated, not hidden | Wilke 2005; the retraction and confirmation log in `01` keeps qualifications visible. |
| No request for trust or prestige | Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| One-to-one, opt-in venues | Fig 2 :40, agreement from a shared evidence pool is disqualifying for a verdict. No list, no bulk send. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| Ask for their own falsifier | Formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; write the sought-for shape down as an ordinary term. |

**Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence. The verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
