# The Tether's answer: how could an early RNA replicator copy itself accurately enough to persist but inaccurately enough to evolve?

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is usually posed as a search for a single best error rate. In effect terms it is a two-sided failure. Too accurate: each copy is a mirror of its template, the lineage persists, but no heritable difference appears for the habitat to vote on, so the lineage cannot adapt when conditions change. Too inaccurate: the information that made the molecule a replicator disperses into noise, the copying channel loses contact with what worked, and the population fails to localise about any sequence. The residual is the band between those edges, a union compound with its own loop and ground (Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*).

## 2. The shape

1. **Progress per generation is a product, not a sum.** Fig 7 :26: **"Progress per handoff: R = h² × S"**. Fig 7 :37-39: **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"** **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"**, **"the ground's pressure on the room"**. Accuracy is one factor; selection pressure is the other. Raising one cannot rescue the other at zero.
2. **Persistence requires that the bracket survive the handoff.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."** The copy carries what will grow one, not the grown thing. If the channel loses contact with the sequence that worked, the lineage has nothing to carry.
3. **Evolution requires that the next room differ from the last.** Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 7 :32: **"→ one long room: motion, no progress"**. This is why perfect copying is a failure mode, not an ideal: an identity bond produces no new term.
4. **A heritable remainder is the raw material, not just noise.** Fig 13 :31-32: **"because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Copy errors are per-site. A global average hides the slots that matter.
5. **The ground votes between variants; agreement between copies does not.** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 11 :56: **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."** There is no designer in the prebiotic case. Selection ran.
6. **The two edges are isomers of one copying relation.** Same atoms (template, copy, residual, habitat), different bond: identity ≡ at the stasis edge, noisy production ⇒ at the dispersal edge (Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*). Replication is a cycle whose output re-enters as input; the sign of the loop decides whether it closes (Fig 12 :121).
7. **The answer is a band, not a number.** Fig 9 :32: **"→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. The critical mutation rate of quasispecies theory is a provenanced import with a source gate (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), and its sharp form is landscape-conditional (Wilke 2005). It does not come out of the figures.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a critical mutation rate. No μ_c value is derived or endorsed as a figure result. When the number is used, it is an import and must be labelled as such.
- Not a universal sharp threshold. Where lethal genotypes exist, the sharp threshold is absent even though mutation pressure still destroys localisation by other routes. The qualification is in `01` F9.
- Not a single "right" fidelity. Any answer that gives one optimum without naming the two failure edges has not answered the residual.
- Not a maintained system. No seat distills actors or introduces novelty. Fig 11 :36: *"Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*
- Not a laboratory demonstration of life. Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this derivation settles where the band lies for any real system.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A serial-transfer experiment with measured per-site fidelity and a habitat that applies differential pressure. The questions an instrument must answer:

- Does the bracket survive the handoff for a given per-site error distribution (which slots carry positive error mass, not just the mean)?
- Does the population localise about a master sequence, or disperse, at that distribution?
- Does the next generation differ from the last in a heritable way that changes what the ground keeps?
- Is the landscape of the system one in which a sharp threshold is expected at all, or one with lethal genotypes where delocalisation comes by another route?

A source-discriminating instrument is also needed. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. Here a measured error rate does not by itself say whether misincorporation, slippage, modification or selective loss produced it.

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3: a perfect-copy system that still evolves without external novelty; positive progress with either factor at zero; a re-census showing the handoff line governs another quantity; a measured system that keeps information and evolvability where the threshold predicts dispersal under the threshold's stated preconditions.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The derivation stops at the shape, the two failure edges, the per-site indexing rule and the no-maintainer condition. It does not stop at a number because no ground reading exists here. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable: residual, census, figure lines, refuter, qualifications. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (figures README). Agreement among these simulated seats is one evidence pool, not a witness (Fig 2 :40).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> An early RNA replicator does not need one "right" error rate. It needs two conditions at once: the information that made it a replicator must survive each handoff, and the next generation must differ from the last so the habitat can vote between variants. The figures put it as a product: progress per handoff equals bracket fidelity times the ground's pressure, and zero either factor stops the chain in place. Too faithful, and a copy is an identity bond, one more mirror in a chain with motion but no progress. Too loose, and the channel loses contact with what worked, the remainder compounds, and the population disperses. Errors are per-site residuals, not a global average. What the framework returns is the shape of the answer, a band with two named failure edges, not a critical mutation rate. That number comes from another frame, must be provenanced as an import, and is conditional on the fitness landscape. What would settle it is a serial-transfer experiment with measured per-site fidelity and real differential pressure, not further argument. This is a simulated derivation, not a measurement, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
