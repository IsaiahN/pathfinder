# The Tether's answer on adaptation measures and new vulnerabilities

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts and the separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal standing list, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which measures reduce risk in the long term and which create new vulnerabilities. Stated as an effect, it asks for an intervention that lowers observed loss at a named slot against a reference that does not change its answer because the implementing frame reports success, without raising loss at another slot or a later time. It is a **union compound** of at least three conditions, not a two-list partition (Fig 9 :54 to :58). Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Each condition has its own anchor and its own loop.

## 2. The separator (the answer)

1. **A measure has no fixed sign.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." So "reduces risk" and "creates vulnerability" are the same measure at two different residuals or slots. The two lists collapse.
2. **Risk is indexed per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A falling global average is compatible with a growing residual in one district, one ecosystem, or the next generation.
3. **A reduction counts only if the anchor holds still.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". A measure evaluated against its own reported success is a mirror, and "A mirror cannot correct you" (Fig 2 :35).
4. **New vulnerabilities are cascades.** Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Risk transfer is moving a residual to another slot, not eliminating it.
5. **Durability is permanent work, not a terminal state.** Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room". Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change."
6. **The two currencies stay apart.** Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A cost benefit ratio decides what gets tried first; it does not decide whether a measure is durable.
7. **Three verdicts, not two lists.** For each (measure, slot, hazard, horizon): `durable reduction` if all eight conditions in `01` section 4 hold; `new vulnerability` if a specific positive residual is named at another slot or later time; `indeterminate pending an instrument` if no instrument can yet discriminate.
8. **The literal list is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." No figure line names a measure or returns a numerical net risk for a named place. That is a verdict, not an omission.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a standing list of good and bad measures. No figure line returns one (Fig 11 :51).
- Not a universal percentage, discount rate, or net-benefit threshold (Fig 9 :32, Fig 12 :125).
- Not that any existing measure is unsafe by kind. Valence is relative to a residual (Fig 11 :49 to :51), and the risk-compensation literature itself says the offset is "usually small in comparison to the fundamental benefits of safety interventions".
- Not that "shifted" is the same as "unchanged". A transferred residual is a new positive residual at another slot (Fig 11 :66 to :69).
- Not that adaptation can be finished. No last room (Fig 7 :6).
- Not `grounded`. No adaptation outcome was measured in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the equivalents are:

- **Slot discriminator:** an instrument that separates a fall in loss at the treated slot from a transfer of loss to a neighbouring or downstream slot. Without it, "reduced risk" and "shifted risk" read the same (Fig 1 :22).
- **Anchor test:** an evaluation whose reference does not move when the implementing frame's reported success changes. If the reference moves, the channel has decayed while the ground did not (Fig 10 :24 to :28).
- **Cascade instrument:** a measurement at the second and third order depth where the residual actually sits, not at first order only (Fig 11 :52 to :54).
- **Threshold test:** whether a given "new vulnerability" is a crossing of a threshold (`⋛`, Fig 12 :86 to :87) or a growth of a magnitude. This is the live type question.
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 5: invariant measure sign, a metric whose decline entails every slot declined, a self-updating anchor that still predicts, a fully enumerated cascade with no off-slot residual, a finite set of measures after which `R` stops arriving.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The climate adaptation page says "The need for adaptation varies from place to place" and that "Adaptation measures vary by region and community". The figures agree at a deeper level: the habitat is enumerated, never composed, and its valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :43, :49). No single separator can return a per-place verdict without a per-place measurement. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (figures README). Prestige or agreement is not an instrument (Fig 2 :40, Fig 10 :59).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank adaptation measures and does not claim that any measure is permanently safe. It says that a measure has no fixed sign: the same intervention can lower loss at one place and raise it downstream or later, because an actor's valence is relative to the residual. So the question is a union of overlapping conditions, not two lists. What survives is a separator: name the slot, describe the hazard before choosing a measure, require an anchor that does not update on the project's own success reports, enumerate the habitat out to the cascade where the residual actually sits, keep benefit and burden in separate columns, allow the room to change when the hazard changes, and measure the gap between modelled and observed benefit. It returns three verdicts, durable reduction, new vulnerability, or indeterminate pending an instrument, with a failure condition written in advance. It refuses to name the winning measure because it returns a shape, not a name. The list stays open until a ground reading settles it.
