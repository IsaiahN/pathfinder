## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 to :135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (opened this session): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is the eight-condition separator. "Build a seawall here" is a name. "No standing list survives a change of residual; enumerate the habitat and test the anchor" is a shape.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (opened this session): use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30 to :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried from exemplar, not re-opened): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping (opened this session): "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place".

Applied here: the question's own words "reduce risk" and "new vulnerabilities" are borrowed from a field. They are converted to effects, a loss residual per slot and a positive residual at another slot or time, before any measure or figure line is selected.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself.

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The figures README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to :52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session) gives the discriminator requirement: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." For adaptation, a reported fall in average loss does not discriminate among "risk genuinely reduced", "risk shifted to another place", and "risk deferred to a later decade". The spectrometer, not an argument, is what is owed.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried from exemplar, not re-opened): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." So the six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list, and naming a measure onto it is a binding, not a discovery.

## 4. Why the two-list framing is the named error

Fig 11 :49 to :51: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries... No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The question asks for a list of measures with fixed signs. The figures say signs are relations.

The evolutionary biology source (opened this session) states the same shape in a different field: an adaptation may be "reasonable at the time" and later "become less and less suitable and more of a problem or hindrance in its own right, as time goes on". The climate adaptation source (opened this session) quotes the IPCC: maladaptation includes "increased or shifted vulnerability to climate change, more inequitable outcomes, or diminished welfare, now or in the future". These are low-overlap corroborations of the mechanism, not measurements of any measure.

## 5. Correction to my own §3 and §4, kept visible

I first wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any adaptation measure at all. **That overstates the rule and is retracted here.** The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". The subject is a primitive not held in the library. Levees, mangroves, seawalls, irrigation and early warning systems are already-available bindings from the climate frame. Naming one is a binding onto a held atom or a provenanced import, not the invention of a seventh primitive. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom" applies: such a binding does not move the wall.

What remains wrong is claiming the binding is figure-derived, or that a list of bindings is what the framework returns. The correct form is the separator plus a provenance record for any named measure. Both the overstatement and its retraction are preserved so neither is quietly lost. The same correction appears in `01` section 7 and `06` Q1.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened this session): "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A list of famous adaptation authorities who endorse a measure is the named collapse, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). The legitimate ask gives the separator a budget to be tried and lets an outcome settle it.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay (opened this session): "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of any adaptation measure and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on.
