### Q2 : Are a seawall and a mangrove belt first-order molecules?

- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- A first-order molecule here is `state → structure → state`: a hazard state, an intercepting structure, an exposed state.
- Concrete defence: `storm surge → barrier → settlement`. Nature-based defence: `storm surge → vegetation and sediment → settlement`.
- Both are legal cross-kind compositions. Both are first order.
- Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." So which structure mediates which state pair is part of the term.
- Physics-facing caveat, not a figure line: a mangrove belt also acts over time (accretion, storm recovery) and a seawall acts over sediment transport, so each first-order molecule may have a second term attached. Mark `specified`, unmeasured. Triangulated for legality; single-source for the physical description.

### Q3 : Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As usually written, `concrete barrier + settlement` and `vegetation + sediment + settlement` do **not** share the same atom set. They are two members of the same functional group, "introduce an interceptor between hazard and slot", with different members.
- The stronger version: hold the atom set fixed as `{barrier, vegetation, sediment, flow, exposure, time}` and vary only which structure mediates which state pair and with which bond. Then the two are isomers in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour at the ground. This is the claim worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either measure requires an atom the other does not, even after the atom set is fixed, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. A case in point is a pure early-warning system, which introduces information rather than a physical interceptor; it does not belong to the same isomer set.

### Q4 : What is the order of a durable reduction?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The dependency chain for a measure is:

1. **Perceive the slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." No measure can be judged before the slot is named.
2. **Name what the situation affords.** Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". This is the vocabulary link.
3. **Say what counts as progress.** Fig 3 :9 to :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". This is the objective link, and it is the one most often missing an instrument (Fig 3 :27).
4. **Choose a route under incomplete information.** Only now select a measure. Fig 9 :27 to :28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
5. **Introduce, then enumerate the cascade.** Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
6. **Refuse where a short-term term denies a long-term residual.** Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Formula step 2 puts refusal before mint.
7. **Re-open the room when the question moves.** Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

A measure judged out of this order produces a verdict that cannot be used by the step it was meant to serve. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

### Q5 : Is the union second order?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**.
- A first-order measure that has been settled against the ground at its slot can become an atom at the next level, alongside the neighbouring slot and the future state.
- The comparison between two such settled measures, `⋛`, is a second-order composition. It is a threshold relation, not a new primitive. Fig 12 :86 to :87: "comparison / a threshold relation between two quantities".
- Type caveat, still open: at second order the operands are molecules now serving as atoms. Whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Triangulated for the recursion; strained-for-now on the operand type. Named, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, grey and green interception are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. Without the fixed set they are a functional group: same kind, different member. The dependency order is perceive, afford, objective, route, introduce and enumerate, refuse, re-open. It is illegitimate to skip to "which measure" before the first three links, because the objective link without an instrument is the break where most adaptation monitoring stops. The second-order comparison of two settled measures needs no new primitive, but its operand type remains a live question.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the field's ordinary practice without asserting that the figures name a measure. `this seawall is durable` remains a provenanced binding and an unproven claim; `these two measures are first-order isomers over a shared atom set, judged in dependency order with a refuter` is a checkable structural claim.
