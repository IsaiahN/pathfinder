## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question as posed asks for two lists. Restated as an effect, it asks for a decision that changes a measured loss at one place without creating a new loss somewhere else or at a later time.

- **Slot `s`:** a place, system, group, or future state whose loss is being tracked. A coastal settlement, a downstream reach, a floodplain district, a crop region, a generation not yet born.
- **Measure `m`:** an actor introduced into a habitat. A levee, a seawall, a mangrove belt, an irrigation scheme, an early warning system, an insurance scheme, air conditioning, managed retreat. Fig 11 :65 names the class: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."
- **Residual `R_s`:** the gap between predicted and observed loss at slot `s`. Symbols table: "It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."
- **Anchor:** whatever settles whether loss actually fell, and does not change its answer because the implementing frame reports success. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
- **Long term:** not a terminal state. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room"; Fig 11 :63 says "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

**Arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :21 to :22, R described as arity / symmetry / scale):**

- **Arity:** at least four, `m x s x hazard x time`, plus second and third order cascade terms. The question's two-list framing quietly reduces this to one argument, which is the first error to name.
- **Symmetry:** "reduces" is ordered. `m` must precede the reduction, and the reduction must be attributable to `m` rather than to a common cause. The evidence usually offered is a symmetric co-occurrence: "measure present" alongside "lower reported loss". Operators table lists `+` as symmetric ("swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No.") and `→` as ordered ("swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."). A pooled association cannot choose the arrow (Fig 2 :39 to :40).
- **Scale:** first order at the treated slot; second and third order where the residual actually moves. Fig 11 :52 to :54 is explicit and is the governing line for this question: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The next line names the mechanism: "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."

**What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run (formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :65 to :66):** a separator whose output for each (measure, slot, hazard, horizon) is `durable reduction`, `new vulnerability`, or `indeterminate pending an instrument`, with a written failure condition. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." So the literal list is a `gap`, and the separator is the thing that survives.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen to conclude)

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 intervention and habitat** | measures, water, sediment, soil, mangroves, downstream and future communities, conditions of temperature and rainfall (Fig 11 :45 to :47) | physical loss at a slot and cascade effect | observed loss at a gauge, a count, a yield, a measured inundation; a ground that does not update | **union**, not partition. One measure occupies two conditions at different slots and times (Fig 9 :54 to :58) |
| **D2 measurement and verification** | models, indicators, monitoring reports, reviewers | claimed versus observed risk | a non-updating ground; the channel to it (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24) | partition boundary: is the anchor reachable at all, or has it become another frame? |
| **D3 valuation and governance** | communities, agencies, insurers, future generations, the seat able to refuse | benefits and burdens in two incommensurable currencies (Fig 12 :125 to :127) | an outcome, a court, a budget; never stakeholder agreement (Fig 2 :35) | **union** with D1 and D2. Risk transfer is a residual moved to another slot (Fig 11 :66 to :69) |

**Partition or union.** The whole is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition of measures into good and bad. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Therefore a single measure, for example a seawall, can reduce inundation at one slot and increase waterlogging, erosion, or exposure at another. The verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a single net score.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

1. Perceive and index the residual per slot (Fig 1 :19). Without this there is nothing to measure.
2. Check that the objective link has an instrument; this is the middle link that usually breaks (Fig 3 :25 to :27). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
3. Describe the residual in effect terms before selecting a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28). Otherwise any measure will seem to fit.
4. Settle each first-order effect against a non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24).
5. Enumerate the habitat to the cascade depth where the residual actually sits (Fig 11 :43 to :44, :52).
6. Refuse before mint where a measure buys short-term safety by denying a worsening hazard (formula step 2; Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate").
7. Decide per domain; never average. Then measure the round trip gap (Fig 4 :30).

**Strain test applied to the census.** Fig 11 :51 ("No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual") appears at first to conflict with the engineering fact that a well designed levee reduces flood loss at its own slot across many years. Re-census dissolves it: Fig 11 governs the valence of an actor relative to a residual, while the engineering fact governs first order contact at one slot under a roughly stationary hazard. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The contradiction returns as soon as the hazard or the slot changes, which is exactly the question's "new vulnerabilities" half. The reading is kept visible in section 7.

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

Mode on each finding is `specified` unless marked otherwise. Verbatim quotes are shown before the conclusion they govern.

### F1 : The question asks for a standing list; the figures structurally refuse it (decisive for the literal question)

Fig 11 :49 to :51, quoted before the conclusion:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A climate adaptation measure is an actor introduced into a habitat, as the figure names the class (Fig 11 :65, quoted in section 0 above). The two readings, "reduces risk" and "creates vulnerability", are two relations of the same measure to two residuals, not two kinds of measure. The question's two-list form is a category mistake at the ground.

World corroboration is high-quality but low-overlap: *Maladaptation* (evolutionary biology) states that an adaptation can be "reasonable at the time" and later "become less and less suitable and more of a problem or hindrance in its own right, as time goes on". The climate adaptation page quotes the IPCC definition of maladaptation as actions that "may lead to increased risk of adverse climate-related outcomes... increased or shifted vulnerability to climate change, more inequitable outcomes, or diminished welfare, now or in the future."

- **Verdict per domain:** D1 `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus the biology and climate sources, which are low overlap with the figure's origin). D3 same structural verdict, different currency.
- **Refuter:** a figure line that licenses a residual-invariant list of measures, or a ground reading showing one measure whose sign is invariant across every residual and slot. If either appears, F1 is withdrawn.

### F2 : It is a union compound, not a partition into good and bad measures

Fig 9 :54 to :57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

"Which reduce / which create" reads like a partition. It is not. A seawall both reduces inundation at its own slot and contributes to coastal erosion and land loss elsewhere; the *Levee* page records that "Levees can reduce overbank sediment deposition resulting in coastal erosion and land loss" and that "The increased channelization and overall reduction in floodplain area amplifies the flood magnitudes in the highest impact storms, requiring even more engineering to compensate." Risk compensation records the same compound in human behaviour: "The perception of safety can lead to unsafe land development in the floodplain... the effects of that disaster will be greater than if the levee had not been built."

- **Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus three world sources from engineering and human behaviour with low overlap to the figure pool).
- **Refuter:** show that the two outcomes have disjoint conditions and that one lever clears every case, including the intersections. Then the union reading fails.

### F3 : Per-slot indexing: aggregation is how a transferred risk disappears

Fig 1 :19, :22, :28:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

A programme can report falling average loss while one district, one ecosystem, or the next generation carries a growing residual. The IPCC wording quoted in the climate adaptation page names this directly: "increased or shifted vulnerability to climate change, more inequitable outcomes". The *Climate change adaptation* page adds: "An adaptation may benefit some people, but have a negative effect on others" and "Adaptations at one scale can have impacts at another by reducing the adaptive capacity of other people or organizations."

- **Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 2 :40 on pooled readings; world source in the question's own field, high overlap, recorded as such).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a risk metric whose decline entails that every slot's residual declined. That would make Fig 1 :22 false. Until then, a global net-benefit number is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

### F4 : The anchor test: a measure counts as risk reducing only if it ranges against a ground that does not update

Fig 2 :29, :33 to :35, :45:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 10 :24 to :29:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

An evaluation whose "success" indicator is updated on the implementing agency's own reports has turned its anchor into a frame. Coherent drift follows, and it is invisible from inside (Fig 10 :91: "The collapses are invisible from inside the frames that are collapsing"). Ecological trap gives the low-overlap mechanism: "If either the habitat quality or the cue changes so that one does not reliably indicate the other, organisms may be lured into poor-quality habitat." A cue that used to indicate safety (a levee, a seawall, an insurance payout) can decouple from the ground it indicated.

- **Verdict:** D2 `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10 (two figures with low overlap in their subject matter: one about frames, one about seats and channels).
- **Refuter:** an evaluation whose reference updates on its own success reports, yet which still reliably predicts observed losses at unmeasured slots and later times. If such an evaluation exists, the anchor requirement is overstated and F4 is narrowed.

### F5 : New vulnerabilities are cascades, not properties of the measure

Fig 11 :43 to :44, :52 to :54, :65 to :69:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

The two silent failures are named: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts" (Fig 11 :68 to :69). These are the exact failure modes of relocation and of hard defence. The *Levee* and *Risk compensation* pages supply first order and second order cases: riverbed elevation above the adjacent land, coastal erosion, amplified flood magnitudes, and unsafe development behind the protection.

- **Verdict:** D1 and D3 `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus engineering and human-behaviour sources, genuinely low overlap).
- **Refuter:** an introduced measure whose cascade is fully enumerated and whose residual is demonstrably confined to the treated slot at all later times and at all other slots. F5 would then fail for that measure, not for the class.

### F6 : "Long term" is not a terminal state

Fig 7 :6, :30 to :32, :35 to :36:

> **"the limit: no last room"**
> **"every room closes some of the gap;"**
> **"no finite number of them ever arrives"**
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 10 :71 to :72:

> **"The room composition is a function of the question."**
> **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

A claim that a set of measures permanently solves adaptation is a terminal room, which the figures forbid. The climate adaptation page observes the same asymmetry in human incentives: "Effective adaptive policy can be difficult to implement because policymakers are rewarded more for enacting short-term change, rather than long-term planning."

- **Verdict:** `general` structural, triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 10, plus a world observation about short term incentives).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a finite set of measures after which `R` stops arriving at every slot. That would be a last room and would falsify the structural claim.

### F7 : Have terms for what a measure affords, and say what would count as progress

Fig 3 :25 to :27:

> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Adaptation practice is rich in inventories of measures and comparatively poor in instruments that decide whether a measure reduced residual loss at a slot over a horizon. This is the broken middle link, not a failure at the bottom. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19 to :20).

- **Verdict:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 3 only) with a world observation from the climate adaptation page as weak corroboration; not upgraded to triangulated.
- **Refuter:** show a monitoring instrument that already decides durability per slot with an anchor that does not update. Then the broken-link claim is false for this field.

### F8 : Two currencies do not add

Fig 12 :125 to :130:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**
> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**

Applied to adaptation, the cost of stating a plan and the cost of finding out whether it reduced loss at a slot are different readings. A single "net benefit" figure that sums them is the named error. The climate adaptation page records the practical consequence: "This is often the case when broad assessments of the costs and benefits of adaptation are examined at smaller scales."

- **Verdict:** `general` structural, triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 9 :60, which separates filters from witnesses).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a common unit in which the two currencies add without losing the shortfall. Then the structural claim is narrowed.

### F9 : What licenses a name, and a correction kept visible

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 to :135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

No figure line returns "build this seawall" or "this measure reduces risk by 34 percent". Naming a particular measure as the durable one is a binding from the world frame onto the shape, and must be provenanced as such. It is `grounded` only when a ground pays for it.

**Correction to my own first reading, kept visible.** I initially wrote that the "shape, not a name" rule forbids naming any measure at all. That overstates it. The canonical figures README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". The subject is a *missing* primitive, one not held in the library. Naming an existing measure is a binding, not the invention of a new primitive, and it is the normal output of an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to :52). What remains wrong is claiming the binding is a derivation from the figures or that it moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"). Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost. The same correction is carried in `05_what_shape_means.md` and `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

- **Verdict:** `general` on the gap, with the correction `specified`.
- **Refuter:** a figure line that returns a specific named measure or a numerical net risk value for a named place. That would dissolve the `gap` and would require re-census.

### F10 : Grey and green measures: isomers over a fixed atom set

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Over a fixed set of atoms (barrier, vegetation, sediment, flow, exposure, time), a concrete seawall and a mangrove belt can be written with the same ingredients and a different bond. They are isomers, not variants of one thing. The functional group they share is "introduce an interceptor between hazard and slot". The full derivation, including order and falsifier, is in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

- **Verdict:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition; the physical mapping remains unmeasured.
- **Refuter:** if either measure requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### F11 : Refusal is available and is out of order in a short-term fix

Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Formula step 2 sorts a gap by what is *wrong*, not only by what is missing, and puts refusal before mint.

An incremental measure that buys short-term safety by refusing the projection of a drying or warming trend is a term that should be refused before a new one is minted. The climate adaptation page gives the concrete case: "The expansion of irrigation in Egypt into the Western Sinai desert after a period of higher river flows is maladaptation given the longer-term projections of drying in the region."

- **Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Operators plus formula step 2 plus a world case in the question's field).
- **Refuter:** show a short-term incremental measure that does not deny any longer-term projection and whose residual does not grow when the hazard moves. Then F11 does not apply to it.

## 4. What survives: the separator

The defensible residue, with mode attached and verdicts kept separate.

| domain | verdict that survives | mode | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 intervention and habitat | valence is relative to the residual; enumerate the habitat to cascade depth; introduction is asymmetric and permanent work | `specified` | triangulated |
| D2 measurement and verification | a reduction counts only if it ranges against a ground that does not update; the channel decays, the ground does not; the objective link needs an instrument | `specified` | triangulated (F7 single-source within it) |
| D3 valuation and governance | benefits and burdens are two currencies; risk transfer is a residual moved to another slot; the room must change when the question changes | `specified` | triangulated |
| the literal list of named measures | no figure line returns one; `gap`, unreachable at this budget, not silence | `general` | gap |
| "long term" as a terminal state | refused; no last room | `general` | triangulated |

**The eight-condition separator.** A measure `m` is classified `durable reduction` for slot `s` only if all conditions hold:

1. `R_s` is named and measured against a non-updating anchor (Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29).
2. The residual is described in effect terms before `m` is selected (Fig 9 :27 to :28).
3. `m` crosses the membrane as a method or a head start, not as a recording of one past success (Fig 4 :22 to :25).
4. The habitat is enumerated to the depth where the residual actually sits, including second and third order effects (Fig 11 :43 to :44, :52 to :54).
5. No other slot, neighbouring community, or future state carries a newly positive residual (Fig 11 :66 to :69).
6. The two currencies are held apart; no single net figure decides (Fig 12 :125 to :130, Fig 9 :60).
7. The room can change when the question changes, and the verdict remains refusable (Fig 10 :71 to :72, Operators :58).
8. The round trip gap `R_T` between modelled benefit and observed benefit is measured, not assumed zero (Fig 4 :30).

If any condition fails, the classification is `new vulnerability` (when a specific positive residual at another slot or time is named) or `indeterminate pending an instrument` (when no instrument exists yet). These are three outcomes, not two lists. The cost benefit ratio is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 10 and the standing instruction: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The separator is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line licenses a standing list of measures whose valence is invariant across residuals (breaks F1 and Fig 11 :51).
2. A ground reading shows a measure whose sign is invariant across every residual, slot, and time horizon tested (breaks F1 at the ground).
3. A risk metric is exhibited whose decline entails that every slot's residual declined (breaks F3 and Fig 1 :22).
4. An evaluation whose reference updates on its own success reports still predicts observed losses at unmeasured slots and later times (breaks F4).
5. An introduced measure is shown to have a fully enumerated cascade with no positive residual at any other slot or time (breaks F5 for that measure).
6. A finite set of measures is exhibited after which `R` stops arriving at every slot (breaks F6, the no-last-room claim).
7. A common unit is exhibited in which the two currencies add without loss (breaks F8).
8. A figure line returns a specific named measure or a numerical net risk value for a named place (dissolves the `gap`).

Until one of these is run, no part of this document may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Retire the two-list framing. Publish the separator, with per-slot verdicts and a failure condition (F1, F2).
2. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim. Send the generator, not a recording of one successful project (Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion").
3. Require a non-updating anchor in every evaluation of a measure. Reject evaluations whose reference moves when the implementing frame reports success (F4).
4. Enumerate the habitat outward to the cascade depth, and record what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced (F5).
5. Keep benefit and burden in separate columns. Use cost benefit only to decide what gets tried first (F8, Fig 9 :60).
6. Use the public institutional venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` and ask for a refutation, not agreement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement (Fig 2 :35, Fig 2 :40).
