## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity, symmetry, scale).** The residual is observed loss at a named place and hazard against a reference that does not move when the implementing frame reports success. Arity: measure, slot, hazard, time, plus cascade terms. Symmetry: "reduces" is ordered, while the evidence offered is usually a symmetric co-occurrence, so the arrow is not chosen by the evidence. Scale: first order at the treated slot, second and third order where the residual actually moves. Fig 11 :52 gives the canonical case: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **Domain 1, intervention and habitat.** Actors: measures, water, sediment, vegetation, downstream and future communities. Currency: loss at a slot and cascade effect. Anchor: a gauge, count, yield or inundation that does not update. Question type: union. One measure occupies two conditions at different slots and times.
- **Domain 2, measurement and verification.** Actors: models, indicators, reports, reviewers. Currency: claimed versus observed loss. Anchor: the ground and the channel to it. Question type: partition boundary, is the anchor reachable at all.
- **Domain 3, valuation and governance.** Actors: communities, agencies, insurers, future generations, a seat able to refuse. Currency: benefits and burdens in two currencies. Anchor: an outcome, a court or a budget, never agreement. Question type: union with the other two; risk transfer is a moved residual.

Order by dependency: perceive the slot, name what the situation affords, state what counts as progress, then choose a measure (Fig 9 :27 to :28), then introduce and enumerate, refuse before mint (Operators :58), and re-open the room when the question moves (Fig 10 :72).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. I derived D1 from Fig 11 :52 and D2 from Fig 10 :24, whereas the primary derived D1 from Fig 11 :49 to :51 and D2 from Fig 2 :29. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries. That is weak structural agreement inside one evidence pool, not corroboration.
