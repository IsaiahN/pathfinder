# Expert verification packet : the Tether on adaptation measures and new vulnerabilities

**What this is:** a claim in a form an adaptation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal list, not `grounded`.** No adaptation outcome is claimed to have been measured.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A climate adaptation measure has no fixed sign. Its valence is relative to the residual and the slot, so "reduces risk" and "creates new vulnerabilities" are two relations of one measure, not two kinds of measure. The question is therefore a union of overlapping conditions and must be answered per slot, not by a ranked list. What the figures license is a separator: an eight-condition test that returns `durable reduction`, `new vulnerability`, or `indeterminate pending an instrument` for each (measure, place, hazard, horizon). The conditions are per-slot indexing; describe the residual before choosing a measure; a method rather than a replayed recording; enumerate the habitat to cascade depth; no positive residual at another slot or later time; benefits and burdens in separate columns; a room that changes when the question changes; and a measured round-trip gap between modelled and observed benefit. No figure line names a measure or returns a percentage, so the literal list is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal plus field-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 1

- **Statement:** there is no standing list of measures with invariant valence. Sign is relative to the residual and indexed per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** does the field's own vocabulary already encode this? The IPCC maladaptation definition quoted on the *Climate change adaptation* page includes "increased or shifted vulnerability to climate change, more inequitable outcomes, or diminished welfare, now or in the future". The *Maladaptation* page says an adaptation can be "reasonable at the time" and later become "more of a problem or hindrance in its own right, as time goes on".
- **Refuter:** exhibit a measure whose sign is invariant across every residual, slot and horizon tested, or a risk metric whose decline entails that every slot declined. Either breaks C1.

### C2 : verification-facing; checkable by anyone who has evaluated an adaptation project

- **Statement:** a reduction counts only if it ranges against a ground that does not update on the implementing frame's own success reports, and the monitoring channel can decay while the ground does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26: "saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
- **What to check:** in a project you know, what was the anchor, and did it move when the project's own reporting changed? Is the success metric a reading of the ground or a reading of the project's description of the ground? The ecological trap source gives the low-overlap mechanism: "If either the habitat quality or the cue changes so that one does not reliably indicate the other, organisms may be lured into poor-quality habitat."
- **Refuter:** an evaluation whose reference updates on its own success reports, yet which still predicts observed loss at unmeasured slots and later times. If such an evaluation exists, the anchor requirement is overstated and C2 is narrowed.

### C3 : habitat-facing; checkable by a flood, coastal, or ecosystem practitioner

- **Statement:** new vulnerabilities are second and third order cascade effects of an introduction, not a property the measure carries. Isolation substitutes a habitat; you inherit actors you did not choose.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"; :69, "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".
- **What to check:** the *Levee* page records that "Levees can reduce overbank sediment deposition resulting in coastal erosion and land loss" and that "The increased channelization and overall reduction in floodplain area amplifies the flood magnitudes in the highest impact storms, requiring even more engineering to compensate." The *Risk compensation* page records the human side: "The perception of safety can lead to unsafe land development in the floodplain... the effects of that disaster will be greater than if the levee had not been built." Are these first or second order effects in your case, and was the habitat enumerated to that depth before the measure was chosen?
- **Refuter:** a measure with a fully enumerated cascade whose residual is confined to the treated slot at all later times and at all other slots. C3 fails for that measure, not for the class.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of good and bad measures. No figure line returns one.
- Not a universal percentage, net-benefit threshold, or discount rate.
- Not that any existing measure is unsafe by kind. The *Risk compensation* source says its effect is "usually small in comparison to the fundamental benefits of safety interventions"; that magnitude caveat stands.
- Not that adaptation can be finished. Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room".
- Not that a transferred risk is unchanged risk. It is a new positive residual at another slot.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation, not a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The five failure conditions in `01` section 5 are pre-registered. In addition:

1. A slot discriminator that separates a fall in loss at the treated slot from a transfer elsewhere.
2. An anchor that does not move when the implementing frame reports success.
3. A cascade instrument reading at second and third order depth.
4. A threshold test distinguishing a `⋛` crossing from a magnitude change (the live type question).
5. A source-discriminating instrument in the sense of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that can "comment on the source" rather than only on the reading.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49 to :51, :52, :61, :66 to :69 and Fig 1 :19 and :22. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one project you know: name the anchor and say whether it updated on the project's own reports.
3. Name one slot where a measure you know reduced loss and one where it raised loss, and say which order the effect was.
4. Say which of the five failure conditions you would try first, and why.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
