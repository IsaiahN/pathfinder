## QUESTION

Option A: return two lists of measures with fixed signs. ## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The question's residual is observed loss at a named place and hazard against a reference that does not change its answer because the implementing frame reports success. Arity: at least four indices, measure, slot, hazard and time, plus second and third order cascade terms. Symmetry: "reduces" is ordered (`→`), while the evidence usually offered is a symmetric co-occurrence (`+`); a pooled association cannot choose the arrow. Scale: first order at the treated slot, second and third order where the residual actually moves. Fig 11 :52 names the governing case: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **Domain 1, intervention and habitat.** Actors: measures, water, sediment, soil, vegetation, downstream and future communities, temperature and rainfall (Fig 11 :45 to :47). Currency: physical loss at a slot and cascade effect. Anchor: an observed gauge, count, yield or inundation that does not update. Question type: union, not partition. One measure occupies two conditions at different slots and times.
- **Domain 2, measurement and verification.** Actors: models, indicators, monitoring reports, reviewers. Currency: claimed versus observed risk. Anchor: a non-updating ground; the channel to it. Question type: partition boundary, is the anchor reachable or has it become another frame.
- **Domain 3, valuation and governance.** Actors: communities, agencies, insurers, future generations, a seat able to refuse. Currency: benefits and burdens in two incommensurable currencies. Anchor: an outcome, a court or a budget, never stakeholder agreement. Question type: union with D1 and D2; risk transfer is a residual moved to another slot.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perceive the slot, name what the situation affords, state what counts as progress, only then choose a route, then introduce and enumerate the cascade, refuse before mint, and re-open the room when the question changes. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :51 ("No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual") seems to conflict with the engineering fact that a well designed levee reduces loss at its own slot for years. Re-census dissolves it. Fig 11 governs valence relative to a residual; the engineering fact governs first order contact under a quasi-stationary hazard. Different objects. The strain returns as soon as the hazard or slot changes, which is the second half of the question. Kept visible in `01` section 7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures return a separator, not a list. A measure has no fixed sign, so "reduce risk" and "create new vulnerability" are the same actor at two residuals or slots, and the question is a union of overlapping conditions rather than a partition into good and bad measures. A measure is called durable only when the residual is indexed per slot, the hazard is described before the measure is chosen, what crossed is a method rather than a recording, the habitat is enumerated out to the cascade, no other slot or later time carries a positive residual, the two currencies stay apart, the room can change when the question changes, and the round trip gap between modelled and observed benefit is measured. Otherwise it is a new vulnerability or indeterminate pending an instrument. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The literal list is a `gap` and a verdict, not silence. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded but serious. A false durable label on long lived infrastructure locks in exposure for decades, and the most damaging measures are precisely the least reversible. Fig 9 :40 says "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up" and :41 says "so keep every cut ranked and reversible". Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is reduced by keeping revision possible and by buying a low overlap check before the decision, not after it.
- **undoable:** partly. A method or a head start, for example an early warning system or a reversible operating rule, can be withdrawn or revised. A recording of one occasion, for example a fixed barrier or a settlement sited behind it, cannot be subtracted from the habitat (Fig 11 :61). A wrong cut that has been made physical is costly to reverse, so the cut should be kept ranked and reversible before it is built.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that separates reduced loss from shifted loss; an anchor that does not move when the implementing frame reports success; a measurement at second and third order cascade depth; a threshold test distinguishing a `⋛` crossing from a magnitude change; a figure re-census that admits a standing list; or any of the five pre-registered failure conditions in `01` section 5. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
