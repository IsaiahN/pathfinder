## What the wider literature contributes

These are corroboration, not verdicts. They cluster in one broad field, philosophy and foundations of physics, so their agreement is a single evidence pool, not independent triangulation. A search for sources in human cognition, animal behaviour, engineering, and business through the available search integration returned no results on 2026-11-23; that absence is recorded rather than hidden. The verdict still comes from the figures.

1. **Separability and common causes.** Bub reports Einstein's conditions that each separated component have its own properties and that acting locally not alter a distant one, then notes that Bell-type correlations cannot be reduced to distributions over common causes. This corroborates F3: separateness is defeated by a shared source, not rescued by distance.
2. **Local causality.** Myrvold and Genovese give Bell's PLC-1: "The direct causes (and effects) of events are near by, and even the indirect causes (and effects) are no further away than permitted by the velocity of light." They also record Bell's remark that uncorrelated sets "could have common causes in the overlap of their backward light cones". This corroborates the D1 and D2 split and the common-cause reading.
3. **Decoherence and level-relative separateness.** Bacciagaluppi writes that decoherence ensures the "dynamical independence of each other" of branches, prefers states tied to position, and warns that if everything interacts with everything else, "everything is generically entangled with everything else". This corroborates F4 while keeping it `specified`: the classical regime is a level-specific fact, not a fundamental one.
4. **Relational localization and emergence.** Huggett and Hoefer report that GTR "postulates no fixed 'prior' or 'background' spacetime structure" and that under Mach's principle "in the absence of matter, space itself should not be able to exist". Weinstein and Rickles report that "general relativity discards the fixed kinematical structure of spacetime, so that localization is rendered relational" and that low-energy spacetime may be emergent. These corroborate F2 against a fundamental smooth background. They also supply the strain named in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6b: a relational reading is a binding onto the six-term set, not a new primitive.
5. **Entanglement and connectivity of regions.** Van Raamsdonk: "Disentangling the degrees of freedom associated with two regions of spacetime results in these regions pulling apart and pinching off from each other". This is the closest world-source match to F3, but it is a physics binding, so it cannot name the framework's independence term. It corroborates the shape only.
