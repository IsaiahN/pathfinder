## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are adjacency and independence the same relation?

- Fig 13 :10 gives the entire spatial vocabulary: "near, far, between, contained". That is adjacency, and it is a state.
- Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." That is a relation between evidence pools, not between positions.
- Fig 13 :36: "adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction." The figure separates adjacency from the other relations it holds, but it does not equate adjacency with independence. Fig 2 defines independence in a different vocabulary entirely.
- Therefore: two predicates, two domains. "Distant" is D1; "separate" is D2. The question is a union of two conditions, not a harder version of one (Fig 9 :57).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a verbatim figure line equated near/far with evidence overlap, or made independence a state in the six-term set, the split fails and this file is withdrawn. No such line exists in `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.

Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 13 :10, :36 and Fig 2 :31, low overlap between the substrate figure and the ground figure).

### Q2: What order do the conditions compose in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces". A reading of "separate" before "region" is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).
- Order by dependency: (1) the space state supplies near/far and a region; (2) two such regions are compared for evidence overlap; (3) a physics binding names what, in the world, makes the pools not overlap. Step 3 consumes step 2, which consumes step 1.
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Naming decoherence before describing the residual is the inversion that makes any physical mechanism look like the answer.

Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 3 and Fig 9 are independent figures).

### Q3: Are the two regions first-order molecules, and is their comparison second-order?

- A first-order molecule is state → structure → state, one crossing, legal under Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." A region therefore cannot be a bare stretch of space; it needs a structure in the slot if it composes at all.
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". Two settled regions can serve as atoms for a comparison at the next level. The comparison is second-order, not a new primitive.
- **Falsifier:** if a region cannot be written as state → structure → state at first order, or if a settled region cannot serve as an atom, the second-order claim fails.

Verdict: `single-source` for the level rule (Fig 12 and the level-relativity clause of Fig 13 are one framework). `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q4: Are two regions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 13 on the gradient: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." So two regions built from the same six atoms but with different internal bindings are different substances.
- Over the full shared atom set {Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}, two regions that differ only in which structure mediates which state pair are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition. This is a checkable structural claim.
- Weaker form: if the regions are written with different atom sets, they share only a **functional group**, Fig 12 :124: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Both readings are legal; the stronger one is the one worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either region can be written without an atom the other requires (for example a region with no Space), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

Verdict: `single-source` for the figure definition; the physics-facing check is unmeasured.

### Q5: What analogue governs the entangled-to-separate transition?

- **Membrane (Fig 4 :21):** "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." The transition from entangled regions to separate ones is a crossing, not a contradiction.
- **Lossy transform (Fig 4 :30):** "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round-trip gap R_T between the entangled description and the separated description must be measured, not assumed zero. `11_audit.md` enumerates it qualitatively; no ground reading was taken.
- **Network (Fig 13 :24):** "that entities relate, and influence travels". A shared source that couples two regions is a network connection in the vocabulary, not a spatial nearness. This is the structural reason a common cause defeats separateness at any distance (F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`). It is the cleanest analogue in the six-term set for the physics of entanglement.

Verdict: `triangulated` for the membrane and network readings (Fig 4 and Fig 13 have different subjects and low overlap).

## CONCLUSION

"Distant" and "separate" are distinct predicates in distinct domains. A region is first-order over the six-term set; the comparison of two settled regions is second-order and needs no new primitive. Over the full shared atom set the regions are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier; over different atom sets the weaker functional-group claim stands. The entangled-to-separate transition is a membrane crossing with a measured round-trip gap, and a shared source is a network connection, not a nearness. None of this names the physical operation that makes two evidence pools not overlap. That remains the open slot.
