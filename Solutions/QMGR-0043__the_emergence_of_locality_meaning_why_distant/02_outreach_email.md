**Subject:** Check one output about locality before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on one specific output and would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question:** why can distant regions of spacetime be treated as separate? The output does not answer that with a mechanism. It says what any answer must look like.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's minimum set contains a state called space whose whole content is "near, far, between, contained". Its removal test is decisive: without space there is "no spatial symmetry for Noether, and no region to make isolated mean anything". So a region, and the very idea of an isolated thing, are owed to the space state. They are not primitives the theory starts with. Second, "separate" is not distance. The framework defines independence as "how little they draw on the same evidence", and its common-cause panel shows two frames that never talk and still agree because they share one evidence pool. Two regions coupled to a shared source are, in this vocabulary, one frame wearing two names however far apart they look. Third, the smooth continuum was considered as a seventh member and removed, so it is a composition rather than a floor.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. derive adjacency or "region" from the other five terms without the space state;
> 2. exhibit two frames whose evidence pools fully overlap being legitimately treated as separate for this residual;
> 3. measure a case where mere distance settles separateness even with a shared common cause present;
> 4. show that a seventh primitive is needed to hold smooth locality.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that spacelike separation, commuting algebras, or decoherence *are* the framework's independence. Those are bindings from the physics frame onto terms the framework already holds. The framework returns a shape, not a name: it can say two pools must not overlap, not which physical operation makes them not overlap. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them. Until provenance is shown, such bindings carry zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
