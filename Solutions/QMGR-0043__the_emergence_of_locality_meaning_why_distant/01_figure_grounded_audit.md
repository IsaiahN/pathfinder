## 0. The claim under audit, stated in effect terms

Physics does something that works: it takes one connected spacetime and partitions it into regions, then assigns each region its own state space, its own local dynamics, and, after decoherence, its own definite properties. The question asks why this is legitimate. It is not asking whether the partition is useful, but what in the ground licenses "separate."

R, the residual, is the gap between a single connected substrate and the observed fact that regions can be treated as independent systems. In effect terms: something failed to resolve, namely the standing of the decomposition itself. Arity: two conditions (connectivity of the substrate and independence of the regions), which Fig 9 calls a union compound, not one hard problem. Symmetry: "separate" is a relation between two regions, and the figures' independence is about evidence overlap (Fig 2 :31), not geometric distance alone. Scale: the decomposition holds at some levels and fails at others, so it is level-relative (Fig 13 :104).

Bound term (formula "Direction"): an answer would look like a rule stating (i) what supplies a region at all, (ii) what makes two regions independent, and (iii) what physical operation, if any, realizes that independence. That shape is written down here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual. No answer is filled in where the instruments cannot distinguish.

## 3. Findings

### F1: "region" and "isolated" are owed to the space state, not primitive (decisive for D1)

Fig 13 :10: "near, far, between, contained". This is the entire content the minimum set gives for the state space.

Fig 13 :62: "no spatial symmetry for Noether, and no region to make isolated mean anything". This is the removal test. Remove space and "region" and "isolated" both stop having a referent. So locality is not a free-standing feature of the world; it is what the space state supplies, and a theory that tries to derive adjacency from the other five terms owes a derivation it cannot give (Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it.").

Fig 13 :36: "adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction." Adjacency is its own question, answered by space and not by time or spectrum. That is why "distant" is a spatial predicate and not a temporal or ordering one.

Triangulation: three separate lines of Fig 13 (:10, :36, :62), low overlap between them (the content line, the distinction line, the removal test). `triangulated`. Mode `general` for the structural claim that a region presupposes the space state.

### F2: locality is not a primitive; the continuum was already refused (D1)

Fig 13 :43: "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this". Fig 13 :44: "A generating set must not contain what its own members produce." A smooth background is a spectrum without gaps or smoothness over space and time, so it is a composition, not a member of the floor.

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

Consequence: "spacetime" in the question is already a derivative term. Treating it as the floor on which locality rests reverses the dependency. The floor is space, a state, and a structure sits between space and time if they compose at all. This matches the exemplar's finding on the same figure and is the strongest figure-grounded point here.

`triangulated` (Fig 13 :43-44 and :74). Mode `general`.

### F3: "separate" is independence, not distance (decisive for D2)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :18: "one evidence pool". Fig 2 :20: "so the agreement is the pool talking,". The collapse panel named "common cause (hidden shared source)" (Fig 2 :6-7) shows two frames that never talk and still agree, because the pool speaks through both.

Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." It decides both whether agreement means anything and whether a union has surplus. Two regions linked by a shared source are, in the figure's terms, one frame wearing two names, regardless of how far apart they look.

So the figures' answer to "why can distant regions be treated as separate" is not "because they are far apart." Distance is supplied by D1. Separateness requires non-overlapping evidence, that is, no shared source. A common cause defeats it at any distance. This is a `specified` prediction of the shape, not a measurement.

1. A derivation of adjacency or "region" from the other five terms without the space state (breaks Fig 13 :62 removal test and :41 indivisibility).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no claim here is `grounded`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
