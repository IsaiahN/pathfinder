## What this folder is

A derive-first, figure-grounded treatment of one residual: physics routinely partitions one connected spacetime into regions that can be treated as separate systems, and the question asks what licenses that partition. The figures are primary and decide. World sources (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entries and one arXiv essay) are secondary corroboration only. This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading**, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## Question restated as an effect

What is being decided is whether separateness of distant regions is a primitive fact, a derivative composition, or a level-relative approximation. Option A treats "region" and "separate" as given features of the world. Option B treats them as what the space state and the independence relation compose, with the slot for the physical realization left open. A leads to a false primitive and an unlicensed import. B leads to a checkable shape, written refuters, and an honest stop at the limit of the instruments. The answer is bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else (formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not add an anchor. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make its agreement worthless.
2. Correlation disqualifies a verdict. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". The simulated second seat in `11_audit.md` shares this folder's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.
3. Asking for trust before a check spends independence. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
