# Outreach draft — validate the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape below survives the recipient's own check. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about misleading sources before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a model that reproduces a source's pattern faithfully has not thereby identified whether that source is misleading. The two are different quantities. Fluency, likelihood and citation counts are scores the model or its community produces for itself, and they do not register a gap against anything the source did not author. Under the framework I am using, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* (Fig 1 :27) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35).
>
> What would be needed instead is a shape with three parts: (1) an anchor that does not move when the model or the source moves, *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter"* (Fig 2 :29); (2) a second frame whose closure differs from the source's, because *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* (Fig 6 :23); and (3) a term that can refuse, since *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* (Operators table :58). The source's residual must then be indexed per source, not averaged into one score (Fig 1 :19).
>
> The mechanism I would expect to see is this: among all bound, predicting terms, the one to refuse is *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* (Fig 5 :59). I wrote that down before writing this email. The hard part, and the part I want your help with, is that the thing being looked for is often not a bound term at all: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."* (Fig 5 :62). So the remedy is a discipline: write down what a misleading source would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular deployed model does this. That is a `gap` in this work, not a finding. I am also not claiming that a blacklist of unreliable sources transfers, or that more retrieved documents help. The opposite is reported in the literature I read. And I am not claiming the framework is the only account of source reliability.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. A model flags a source as misleading above chance when that source is its only evidence and no ground feedback or second frame is available.
> 2. Detection is achieved with one closure, no import and no instrument.
> 3. A source's misleading-ness is derived from its own pattern by composition alone.
> 4. An accumulation-only vocabulary removes a source's standing to be composed over without any refusal operator.
> 5. A scaling result adds reliable per-source refusal without changing contact with any non-updating ground.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures are the generator. Run the shape on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
