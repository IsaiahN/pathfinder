# Isomers of data advantage, and the order of the four conditions

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Two firms can hold the same data atoms and still diverge in market outcomes, or hold different data and converge. The question asks what carries the difference. Two things are owed: (1) are two data-advantage configurations **isomers** under Fig 12, and (2) in what **order** must the four conditions of F1 to F4 be tested if a lever is to clear a condition rather than wasted on one that already works?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held at the business level (not the six substrate primitives): data assets, models, capital, talent, routines, customer relationships. Bonds held: the seven plus negation, in particular sequence → and comparison ⋛. ## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are two firms with the same data atoms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- If firm A and firm B hold the same data atoms but arrange them differently in the habitat (different method, different customer loop, different complement of routines), then by Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different market substance. The difference is invisible if you list only their datasets and decisive in their outcomes.
- If they hold different data but the same arrangement (the same `data → method → market` sequence with different fillings), they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :123): the group says what kind of system, the qualifier says which dataset.
- ⇒ The "same data means same advantage" intuition and the "different data means different advantage" intuition are both wrong in general. What decides is the bond, that is the arrangement, which is F3.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), triangulated with convergent evolution (biology) in `09`. Corroborated from the business pool by Barney's imitability criterion and by dynamic capabilities.

### Q2: What order are the four conditions in?

Using Fig 3's dependency order rather than importance:

1. **Anchor first (F4).** Before asking about data, fix what settles the question. Fig 2 :29: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* If the "advantage" cannot be read at the market, there is no residual to explain.
2. **Overlap and closure second (F5, F1).** Measure how much the two frames draw on the same evidence (Fig 8 :48). If they overlap almost completely, they are *"one frame wearing two names"* (Fig 8 :49) and there is no advantage to explain. If they are disjoint, ask whether the atom is derivable or must be imported (Fig 8 :41: *"Underivable: go outside."*).
3. **Substrate third (F3).** Ask whether the arrangement crosses the record boundary. Fig 12 :109: *"Which boundary matters is the one the record does not cross."*
4. **Capability last (F2).** Only then ask whether the data changes contact, because capability is *"a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* (Fig 11 :39).

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Buying more data (step 1 in most strategy decks) is effort spent above the break when the broken link is the substrate or the anchor.

### Q3: Which bond carries the durability claim?

- ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 / Operators table). Durability is a threshold over time: does the gap still pay after t? Using ⋛ here is a comparison of two readings of the same slot at two times, which the bond permits. It is **not** a new "moat operator". Operators table :69: *"missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet."*
- → is sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :71 to :73 as carried in the Operators table). The habitat chain in Q2 is a sequence, so the order is part of the term; swapping two steps makes a different term, not a spelling of the same one.
- **Strain, named:** the market is the anchor (F4), yet the durability comparison ranges two observations of the anchor against the anchor itself. This is the ground ranging against the ground, not two frames. Fig 2 :46: *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."* Resolution: the two readings are two observations, not two frames, and the anchor does not update between them. If it does update, F4 is broken and the moat claim is void. Kept as a named strain rather than smoothed.

### Q4: Analogues, as this question needs them

- **Chemistry / isomerism** (Fig 12 :97-98): silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties. That is the Q1 case. Status: figure-cited.
- **Evolutionary biology / convergent evolution** (Wikipedia record, fetched 2026-11-22): *"Convergent evolution creates analogous structures that have similar form or function but were not present in the last common ancestor of those groups."* Rivals reach the same capability without importing from the incumbent. Corroborates Fig 8 :50 and F5. Low overlap with the business pool.
- **Economics / non-rivalry** (Wikipedia record, fetched): a rival good's consumption *"prevents simultaneous consumption by other consumers."* Data does not fit that definition, so one firm's use does not deplete another's. Corroborates Fig 6 :25 and F1. Inference marked `general` and attributed to this derivation, not to the source.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion)

The isomer-and-order derivation is refuted if either:

1. Two firms with identical data atoms and demonstrably different arrangements still show no outcome difference attributable to the arrangement, while a third with the same data and same arrangement does show one. That kills the isomer claim and leaves data as the differentiator.
2. A lever applied at step 3 (substrate) clears the gap while steps 1 and 2 are demonstrably broken (no market reading, or frames sharing one evidence pool). That kills the dependency order and returns the conditions to a list rather than a chain.

## CONCLUSION

Two configurations with the same data but different arrangements are isomers under Fig 12, and the durable part is the bond rather than the atom. The four conditions are a dependency chain, not a checklist: anchor, overlap and closure, substrate, capability. ⋛ legitimately names the over-time comparison, provided the anchor does not update between readings. One strain (ground ranging against ground) is named and resolved by treating the two readings as observations, not frames. No new primitive and no new operator is minted.
