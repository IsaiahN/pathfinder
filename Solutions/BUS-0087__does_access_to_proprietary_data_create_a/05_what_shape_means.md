## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and the surrounding lines say it *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (fetched): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name its instance. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- *Shape:* a durable advantage must sit outside competitors' closure or be costly to import; must be agent-and-habitat rather than data in isolation; must have an arrangement that does not cross the record boundary cheaply; and must keep paying at the market ground.
- *Name (withheld):* "firm X's dataset Y yields a five-year moat." That is a binding to a case and a duration, not something the framework returns.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* (fetched) names the same discipline: *"the replicator is a tool that provides the shape of something that you described."*

## 4. Why a named dataset from an outcome reading is the forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (fetched): *"However, the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* And: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*

Applied here: a market outcome reading cannot say which source produced it. Two firms with the same revenue trajectory may have gotten there by exclusive data, by a better habitat, or by an unrecorded method. Naming "the data" as the source from the outcome alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator: an entry study, a codification experiment, a cross-firm data-portability test. That is why the shape in `07` lists ground tests and why the refuter in `01` §5 is written first.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (fetched): *"The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* This derivation cannot certify that data is or is not the moat. It can only say what any answer must look like.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a proprietary dataset? ANSWERED with a correction

**First reading (kept visible).** I initially read the rule as forbidding any mention of a specific dataset. That overstates it.

**Correction.** The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. A dataset is not a missing primitive; it is an atom inside a firm's Γ, acquired from the business frame. Naming one is a **binding** from that frame onto an atom, not the invention of a new member of the six. What remains forbidden is claiming that the framework derives which dataset wins, or that the binding moves the wall (Fig 6: *"IMPORT adds an atom"*, and Fig 13 :112: *"More compositions never add a member"*).

**Why the correction matters for this question.** The strategic mistake the figures diagnose is not naming a dataset; it is treating an outcome reading as a source reading (section 4). The shape rule and the metamerism rule are different constraints, and both are now recorded rather than one silently replacing the other.
