# Outreach draft: check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the residual, the four domain verdicts and the refuter are intact. If the stage-1 claim does not survive a reader's check, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about data moats, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about competitive advantage and data that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A firm has a performance gap at the market that persists while rivals try to close it. The question is what holds that gap open. "Proprietary data" is one candidate held asset. The output below is a `specified` shape, not a measurement and not a `grounded` result.
>
> **The shape claim.** The question is not one yes or no. It is a union of four conditions, each with its own verdict:
>
> 1. **Derivation versus acquisition.** A rival cannot compose its way to a data atom its frame does not hold. That part of "lasting" is true by construction. But the barrier is to derivation, not to acquisition: the atom can be imported through licensing, leakage, a broker, or a partner. "Competitors have not found it" is not proof that they cannot.
> 2. **Data alone is a head start, not a capability.** Capability is a property of the firm and its habitat, not of the firm in isolation. A rival handed the data but not the surrounding system gets a test harness, which proves wiring and never capability. The durable part is distributed across the habitat.
> 3. **Durability is a substrate property.** Where the method that turns data into an outcome is recorded and portable, a rival gets it as a lookup and the establishing cost was paid once, so the gap erodes. Where it is tacit and unrecorded across the boundary, every rival pays again, so the gap persists. Cost in bits and cost in actions are different currencies.
> 4. **The market settles it.** Data volume is a frame-internal score. Only the market, changing what it pays over time, decides whether any advantage is lasting. If all firms draw on the same evidence pool, they are one frame with several names and nobody has the advantage.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named dataset guarantees a moat of any length; that data cannot be copied; that this framework is a theory of everything; or that this is settled by experiment. It returns a shape, not a name, and the shape stays open until a ground test closes it.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written before this message:**
> 1. a rival reaches the same market outcome from public atoms alone, with no import and no copy of the habitat, and the gap closes;
> 2. supplying the data alone, with the habitat fixed, produces and sustains the gap;
> 3. the gap persists after the method is fully published and adopted, or erodes while it stays tacit;
> 4. the gap grows in internal metrics after the market stops paying;
> 5. a persistent difference is measured between firms drawing on the same evidence pool.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The formula, eight steps and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
