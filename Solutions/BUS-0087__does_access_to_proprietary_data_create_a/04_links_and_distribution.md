## Wider literature that bears on this question

The four domain verdicts are figure-derived. The following literature is secondary corroboration from evidence pools genuinely different from the framework's, so it is weighted accordingly.

1. **Business strategy, resource-based view.** Barney 1991, via the Wikipedia record: sustainability depends on imitability and substitutability, and a resource must be valuable, rare, imperfectly imitable and non-substitutable. Predicts that possession of data is not itself durability. Corroborates F1 and F3.
2. **Business strategy, dynamic capabilities.** Teece, Pisano and Shuen 1997, via the Wikipedia record: capability is adapting, integrating and reconfiguring skills and resources to a changing environment. Predicts that the durable object is the reconfiguring system, not the dataset. Corroborates F2.
3. **Business strategy, absorptive capacity.** Cohen and Levinthal 1990, via the Wikipedia record: the ability to recognize, assimilate and apply external knowledge, with path dependence once investment stops. Predicts that import is neither automatic nor free. Corroborates F1 and F3.
4. **Human cognition and knowledge.** Polanyi 1966, via the Wikipedia record: *"we can know more than we can tell."* Predicts that part of the arrangement cannot cross a record boundary. Corroborates F3.
5. **Evolutionary biology.** Convergent evolution: analogous structures arise independently without a common ancestor. Predicts that rivals can reach the same capability without importing from the incumbent. Corroborates Fig 8 :50 and F5. Low overlap with business sources.
6. **Economics.** Rivalry: a rival good's consumption prevents simultaneous consumption by others. Data does not fit that definition, so one firm's use does not deplete another's. Corroborates Fig 6 :25 *"An import adds no atom to the world"* and the acquisition half of F1.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. Where two business sources agree (for example RBV and dynamic capabilities), they are checked for overlap: both sit in the strategy canon and partly share an evidence pool, so their agreement is one witness at most. The biology and economics sources are genuinely low overlap.
