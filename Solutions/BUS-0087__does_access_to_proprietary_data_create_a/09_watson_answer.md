## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that access to proprietary data creates a lasting competitive advantage, versus the claim that competitors can reproduce the same capabilities. Option A: data access is a durable anchor. Option B: the capability is reproducible and the advantage is a head start with an expiry. What each leads to: A licenses hoarding and a frame-internal data-volume score; B licenses a search for the habitat and arrangement that actually carry the gap. The figures refuse both as single verdicts and return a four-condition shape instead.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A persistent performance gap at the market slot "firm outcomes". Arity: three overlapping conditions (import barrier, capability substrate, record boundary) plus the ground that settles them, so a **union compound**, Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Symmetry: firm and rival range against the same market, so symmetric in grounding, asymmetric in holdings. Scale: first order inside a firm's market loop, second order at competitor entry and convergence.
- **D1 incumbent frame.** Actors: the firm, Γ_inc (models, data, routines). Currency: predictive accuracy paid in market outcomes. Anchor: the market. Question type: can a rival derive the held atom?
- **D2 competitor frame.** Actors: rival firms, Γ_comp. Currency: the same market outcomes. Anchor: the same market. Question type: import, convergence, or neither?
- **D3 habitat / market frame.** Actors: customers, brokers, suppliers, regulators, talent, data as a condition. Currency: contact. Anchor: the market. Question type: is capability in the agent alone or in agent-and-habitat?
- **D4 record-substrate frame.** Actors: the arrangement (method, code, know-how) and its copies. Currency: bits to describe, actions to settle. Anchor: does it still pay at the ground? Question type: does the arrangement cross the boundary as a lookup?
- **Partition or union.** Union. D1 and D2 share the ground, D3 contains them, D4 cuts across all three. Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Anchor first, overlap and closure second, substrate third, capability last. Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

## WORLD (secondary, sources read 2026-11-22)

World sources are corroboration only. Field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, and corroborates|conflicts are given for each. The framework's own essays are high overlap with the figures and are listed in `05` and `10`; the records below are the genuinely lower-overlap ones.

- **Business / strategic management.** Jay Barney, 1991, *Journal of Management*, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource-based_view (opened 2026-11-22): *"The sustainability of any competitive advantage depends on the extent to which resources can be imitated or substituted."* Also VRIN: *"valuable, rare, imperfectly imitable and not substitutable."* Predicts that possession of data is not durability and that imitability is the test. **corroborates** D1/D2 and D4. Overlap: low with the framework pool, medium with the other business sources here.
- **Business / capabilities.** Richard Makadok, 2001, *Strategic Management Journal*, via the RBV record (opened 2026-11-22): capabilities are *"an organizationally embedded non-transferable firm-specific resource whose purpose is to improve the productivity of the other resources possessed by the firm."* Predicts that the durable object is embedded and non-transferable. **corroborates** D3 and D4. Overlap: medium with Barney, low with the figures.
- **Business / dynamic capabilities.** David Teece, Gary Pisano and Amy Shuen, 1997, *Strategic Management Journal*, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_capabilities (opened 2026-11-22): *"adapting, integrating, and reconfiguring internal and external organizational skills, resources, and functional competences to match the requirements of a changing environment."* The same record cites Nelson and Winter 1982 routines evolving by variation, selection and retention. Predicts capability as a system in contact with a changing environment, not a static asset. **corroborates** D3 and the anchor verdict. Overlap: low with the figures, medium with other strategy sources.
- **Business / absorptive capacity.** Wesley Cohen and Daniel Levinthal, 1990, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorptive_capacity (opened 2026-11-22): *"ability to recognize the value of new information, assimilate it, and apply it to commercial ends."* Also: once investment stops, a firm *"may never assimilate and exploit new information in that field."* Predicts import is neither automatic nor free, and is path dependent. **corroborates** D1/D2 and D4. Overlap: low with the figures.
- **Human cognition / tacit knowledge.** Michael Polanyi, 1966, *The Tacit Dimension*, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacit_knowledge (opened 2026-11-22): *"we can know more than we can tell."* Also tacit knowledge is *"difficult to extract or articulate"*. Predicts that part of an arrangement cannot cross a record boundary. **corroborates** D4. Overlap: low with business and with the figures.
- **Animal behaviour / evolutionary biology.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_evolution (opened 2026-11-22): *"Convergent evolution creates analogous structures that have similar form or function but were not present in the last common ancestor of those groups."* Predicts rivals reach the same function independently, without import from the incumbent. **corroborates** Fig 8 :50 and the overlap verdict. Overlap: very low.
- **Economics / rivalry.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivalry_(economics) (opened 2026-11-22): *"a good is said to be rivalrous or a rival if its consumption by one consumer prevents simultaneous consumption by other consumers."* Data does not fit that definition. Predicts one firm's use does not deplete another's, so the atom is available to import. Inference marked `general` and attributed to this derivation, not to the source. **corroborates** Fig 6 :25 and the acquisition half of D1/D2. Overlap: very low.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered. Agreement among the three business sources counts as one witness at most because they partly share a strategy canon; the biology and economics sources are independent pools.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union compound and has no single yes or no. Data is a real barrier to derivation and only a conditional barrier to acquisition. The durable object is the firm-and-habitat system and the unrecorded arrangement, not the dataset. Codification and publication erode the gap; tacitness preserves it. The market, not an internal data metric, decides, and firms drawing on one evidence pool are one frame with several names. This is a `specified`, ungrounded shape. It closes against the figures in four domains; no `gap` is claimed.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the shape is published with its refuters, because an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is a firm hoarding data while the broken link is the arrangement or the anchor, which Fig 3 :19 calls effort spent above the break. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. The shape adds conditions and withdraws no primitive. A later market test can narrow any one verdict without re-opening the others, and a sent packet can be superseded.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. The import test, the data-only transfer test, the codification test, the overlap test and the per-slot market test in `07` §5 would each fire a named refuter. A persistent gap surviving full codification plus independent convergence would reverse the substrate and overlap verdicts; a gap growing while the market stops paying would reverse the anchor verdict. Until one fires, the verdict stays `candidate`.
