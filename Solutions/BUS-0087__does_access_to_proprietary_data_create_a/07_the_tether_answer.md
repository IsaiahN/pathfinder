# The Tether's answer on proprietary data and lasting advantage

**Mode: `specified` on the four domain verdicts and the shape, `general` on the rule that the market settles any particular case. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05` §6), not a ground reading. No experiment, entry study or market test was run for this package.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm holds a performance gap at the market while rivals try to close it. The question asks what holds it open. "Access to proprietary data" is one candidate held asset. The residual is a **union compound** of four overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). It has its own loop and its own ground for each condition.

## 2. The four verdicts, kept separate and never averaged

1. **Derivation barrier, not acquisition barrier.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :32: *"no search, however long, adds an atom."* A rival cannot compose its way to a data atom it does not hold, so the "lasting" claim is true against derivation by construction. But Fig 6 :23: *"The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Licensing, leakage, brokers and partners are imports, and Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* So the barrier to acquisition is conditional, not logical. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* **Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.**

2. **Data alone is a head start, not a capability.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* A rival handed the dataset without the surrounding system gets wiring, not capability. The incumbent's advantage is likewise distributed across the habitat, not parked in one file. **Verdict: `specified`, single-source in the figures, corroborated by Teece, Pisano and Shuen (1997) and Cohen and Levinthal (1990).**

3. **Durability is a substrate property of the arrangement.** Fig 12 :108: *"re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once"*; :109: *"Which boundary matters is the one the record does not cross."* Codified and portable methods cross the boundary as lookups, so gaps erode. Tacit, embedded methods do not, and *"the result carries forward and the deriving does not"*, so each rival pays again and gaps persist. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Bits and actions must be read apart. **Verdict: `specified`, single-source in the figures, corroborated by Polanyi (1966) and by routine-based strategy literature.**

4. **The market is the anchor.** Fig 2 :29 names *"a market"* as an anchor, and :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Data volume is a frame-internal score. Only the market, changing what it pays over time, settles durability. And if all firms draw on the same evidence pool, Fig 8 :49: *"completely are one frame wearing two names"*, so nobody holds the advantage. **Verdict: `general`, triangulated.**

## 3. The shape, stated once

A lasting data advantage has the following shape: the data atom is costly for a rival to import (not merely unfound); the advantage lives in an agent-and-habitat system rather than in the dataset alone; the enabling arrangement does not cross the record boundary as a cheap lookup; and the gap keeps paying at the market while frames remain genuinely independent. Remove any one condition and the word "lasting" changes meaning. The framework returns this shape, not a name, a dataset, or a duration.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that data is copy-proof. Search within a closure cannot add an atom, but import can move the bound (Fig 6 :23).
- Not that competitors' current failure proves their future failure (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that a firm with more data wins. Market outcome is the only metric (Fig 1 :23).
- Not that agreement among strategy experts settles anything (Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict."*).
- Not `grounded`. No source-discriminating instrument, entry study or codification experiment was run for this package.
- Not a unified theory of the firm, and no seventh primitive. Fig 13 :112: *"More compositions never add a member."*

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

1. **Import test.** Can a rival acquire the same atom through a broker, licence, partner or leakage within the relevant cost and time budget? If yes, the acquisition barrier is weak (F1).
2. **Data-only transfer test.** Supply the data to otherwise fixed firms and measure whether the gap appears and persists. If yes, the agent-and-habitat claim is broken (F2).
3. **Codification test.** Publish and transfer the method; measure whether the gap closes. If it closes, the arrangement was the moat and it was recorded (F3). If it persists while tacit, re-census F3.
4. **Overlap test.** Measure how much evidence the firms actually share. High overlap means no data advantage exists between them (F5, Fig 8 :48-49).
5. **Market test.** Does the gap keep paying at the market over time, per slot rather than in aggregate (Fig 1 :19, :22)? If internal metrics rise while the market stops paying, the anchor is broken (F4).

## 6. The honest stop

The shape closes against the figures in four domains, so no `gap` is claimed. But closing against the figures is not closing against the world. The six-term substrate is a visible guess about what composes, not a measurement of which business assets are scarce. The answer stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing import from derivation, data from habitat, and recorded from tacit arrangements. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that proprietary data is or is not a moat. It says the question is four questions. Data is a real barrier to a rival deriving what its frame does not hold, but only a conditional barrier to acquiring it, and a rival's failure to find it is not proof that it cannot. The durable part is not the dataset: capability belongs to the firm and its habitat, and what turns data into an outcome is an arrangement whose portability decides whether rivals pay the cost once as a lookup or pay it again from scratch. The market, not a data-volume dashboard, is what settles whether any of this keeps paying, and firms drawing on the same evidence pool are one frame with several names. What the framework offers is the shape any lasting advantage must have, the refuters written in advance, and the ground tests that would close it. The case stays open until those tests are run.
