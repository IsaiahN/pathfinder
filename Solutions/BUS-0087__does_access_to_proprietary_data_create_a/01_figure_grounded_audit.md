## 0. The residual under audit

> Does access to proprietary data create a lasting competitive advantage, or can competitors reproduce the same capabilities?

Stated as an effect, the question asks what holds a live performance gap at the object slot "firm outcomes" open against the market over time.

- **Option A (lasting).** The gap does not trend down because a held asset, the data, is an anchor competitors cannot range against.
- **Option B (reproducible).** The gap trends to zero because competitors can reach the same capability by import or convergence.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *durable-advantage shape* is the set of conditions that must hold for a gap at a slot to keep paying at the ground while other frames try to close it. Binding that shape makes it refusable. A bare "yes, data is a moat" is a `general`-mode verdict with no ground reading, the named output-layer defect.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** three overlapping conditions, not one question. (i) import barrier on the data atom, (ii) substrate of the capability (data alone versus agent-and-habitat), (iii) record boundary of the enabling arrangement. This is a **union**, not a list. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Symmetry:** firm and competitor range against the *same* market anchor, so the relation is symmetric in grounding even when asymmetric in holdings. The "lasting versus reproducible" comparison is a threshold over time, not a symmetric join of two equal operands.
- **Scale:** first order is a firm's use of data inside its own market loop; second order is competitor entry and convergence across time. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 incumbent frame | firm, its library Γ_inc (models, data, routines) | predictive accuracy paid in market outcomes | the market |
| D2 competitor frame | rival firms, Γ_comp, lacking the data atom | the same market outcomes | the same market |
| D3 habitat / market frame | customers, suppliers, data brokers, regulators, talent, data as a condition | contact | the market as ground |
| D4 record-substrate frame | the arrangement (method, code, know-how) and its copies | bits to describe, actions to settle | does it still pay at the ground |

All four have a nameable anchor (the market for D1 to D3, the ground test "does it still pay" for D4), so all four survive the anchor test. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :29 also gives the license: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The market is named as an anchor in the figure itself. That is the strongest single figure-grounded point in this question.

**Partition or union.** Union. D1 and D2 overlap on the shared ground, D3 contains both, D4 cuts across all three. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fix the ground first (D3), then the closure relation between D1 and D2, then the record substrate (D4), then the capability claim (D3 again), then the verdict. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Hoarding more data cannot rescue a broken arrangement.

## 3. Findings F1 to F6

### F1: Data is a derivation barrier, not an acquisition barrier (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

A competitor whose Γ_comp lacks the data atom cannot compose its way to it. That part of "lasting" is true by construction: no search inside the competitor's closure adds the atom. But the barrier is to **derivation**, not to **acquisition**. The bound moves on contact with a differing frame (licensing, leakage, scraping, a data broker, a joint venture), and an import "adds no atom to the world", so the atom is already available to be carried. A claim of the form "competitors could never reproduce this" is not proved by their current failure: absence of a find is not proof of absence.

The Formula step 7 distinction matters here. Fig 8 :41: *"Underivable: go outside."* Fig 8 :42: *"already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."* If the dataset is genuinely secret, the competitor is **underivable** and needs an import. If it is public and merely unfound, the competitor is **unreachable-at-this-budget** and needs more search, not an import. These issue different instructions and are routinely confused in strategy talk about "proprietary" data.

**Triangulation:** low overlap between the closure mechanism (Fig 6) and the limit of search (Fig 8), so the verdict is triangulated within the figures. Both are framework-internal; world corroboration is recorded separately in `09`.

**Refuter:** a competitor reaches the same market outcome from public and common atoms alone, with no import of the incumbent's data and no copy of its habitat, and the gap closes; or the data atom is shown derivable from atoms already in the competitor's closure.

### F2: Capability is agent-and-habitat, so data alone is a head start (`specified`, single-source in figures)

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat’s actors, which you did not choose."*
Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*
Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*

"Access to data" changes an input, not necessarily contact with the market. The capability is the whole system: data plus model plus method plus the conditions and relations around it. A rival handed a copy of the dataset but not the habitat gets a substituted habitat, which proves wiring and never capability. Conversely, the incumbent's advantage is not located in the dataset alone; it is distributed across actors and relations that are harder to keep entirely out of reach.

Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* A data-moat argument that enumerates only the dataset is first-order habitat scoping, the same error the figure names for DDT reaching the eagles.

**Status:** single-source within the figures (Fig 11). It is corroborated from a genuinely different evidence pool by Teece, Pisano and Shuen (1997) and by Cohen and Levinthal (1990), recorded in `09` and `10`.

**Refuter:** a controlled test in which supplying the data alone to a firm with an otherwise fixed habitat produces and then sustains the market gap. That would show the agent-plus-habitat claim is wrong for this residual.

### F3: Durability is a substrate property of the arrangement (`specified`, single-source in figures)

Fig 12 :107: *"Whether that cost must be paid again depends on the substrate"*
Fig 12 :108: *"re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once"*
Fig 12 :108: *"the result carries forward and the deriving does not"*
Fig 12 :109: *"Which boundary matters is the one the record does not cross."*
Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
Fig 12 :44: *"the arrangement is priced or it is free"*

This is the sharpest figure bearing on "lasting versus reproducible". Where the arrangement is recorded in a form that crosses the boundary to competitors, a rival gets it as a lookup and the establishing cost was spent once: publishing the method, shipping the model, hiring the trained team. Where it is not recorded across the boundary, the deriving does not carry forward, every lineage pays again, and the gap persists.

Crucially, bits and actions are different units and do not add (Fig 12 :125-126). A firm that optimizes the bits (codify, publish, standardize tooling to attract talent) can hand the arrangement across the boundary while believing it is only reducing internal description cost. The price of the arrangement is the choice it records, so a free arrangement carries nothing.

**Re-census / strain resolved here.** I first tried to apply these lines to the raw dataset itself. That strains: Fig 12 speaks of settled *molecules*, that is arrangements, not of bare operand atoms. Raw data is an operand. Re-census assigns :107-110 to D4, the method and know-how that turn data into an outcome, and leaves the data atom to Fig 6 :21-25 and Fig 4 :22-25. After re-census no strain remains. The first reading is kept visible in the retraction log below.

**Refuter:** the gap persists after the enabling method is fully codified, published and adopted by competitors; or it erodes while the method remains tacit and unrecorded across the boundary. Either result breaks the substrate prediction.

### F4: The market, not the data holding, is the anchor (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :29: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Fig 1 :25 names *"coverage, and compression achieved"* and :26 names *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* as frame-internal quantities that do not count. A claim of the form "we hold more proprietary data than any rival" is the same genus: a quantity the frame produces and then scores itself with. It is not a verdict. Only the market, changing its payments over time, settles whether an advantage is lasting. Fig 2 :37-38 puts it plainly: *"instruments can be wrong on axes they"* and :38 finishes *"do not measure."* In this domain that is why internal data-volume dashboards cannot substitute for market outcomes.

**Triangulation:** low overlap between the metric rule (Fig 1) and the anchor rule (Fig 2), so `general` and triangulated.

**Refuter:** a gap that persists and grows in internal metrics after the market has stopped paying for the offering. If the "anchor" moves when pushed by the frame, it was not an anchor (Fig 2 :30: *"it is not an anchor."*).

### F5: Convergence dissolves a moat without anyone copying the incumbent (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*
Fig 8 :49: *"completely are one frame wearing two names."*
Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Two failure modes hide inside "proprietary". If every firm buys from the same brokers and trains on the same public corpora, the frames overlap almost completely and are one frame with several names, so no one has a data advantage. If instead rivals reach the same capability from independent evidence pools (their own telemetry, their own customers, independent experiments), they hold the atom by convergence at maximum independence. No import from the incumbent occurred, yet the moat is gone. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* The first thing to measure is overlap, not exclusivity.

**Refuter:** a persistent, measured market difference between two frames that demonstrably draw on the same evidence pool. That would show convergence/overlap does not dissolve the advantage and breaks this finding.

### F6: The question is a union compound; one yes/no is the wrong output shape (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :5: *"Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The four conditions (import barrier, substrate, record boundary, ground) are separate loops. A single lever that clears the region where one condition holds leaves the intersections standing, because the other conditions still bind. Per-slot indexing also matters: a data advantage can be live in one customer segment and absent in the aggregate. A global market statistic can hide the only slot that matters.

**Refuter:** show that the four conditions form a genuine partition (mutually exclusive, so one lever clears all of them), or that the residual shrinks monotonically with effort and is therefore one well-formed question after all.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The durable-advantage shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A competitor reaches the same market outcome from public and common atoms alone, with no import of the incumbent's data and no copy of its habitat, and the gap closes. (Breaks F1 and F2.)
2. Supplying the data alone, with the habitat otherwise fixed, produces and then sustains the market gap. (Breaks F2.)
3. The gap persists after the enabling method is fully codified, published and adopted by competitors, or erodes while the method stays tacit. (Breaks F3.)
4. The gap grows in internal metrics after the market has stopped paying. (Breaks F4.)
5. A persistent market difference is measured between frames drawing on the same evidence pool. (Breaks F5.)
6. The four conditions are shown to be a partition rather than a union, or the residual shrinks with effort as one question. (Breaks F6.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not answer yes or no. Publish the four domain verdicts separately, each with its mode and refuter (F6).
2. Lead with F1: data is a derivation moat and only a conditional acquisition moat. The "could never reproduce" claim fails Fig 8 :39.
3. Put F2 and F3 at the center: the durable part is the habitat and the unrecorded arrangement, not the dataset. This is where world literature corroborates from a different evidence pool.
4. State the ground test (F4). Ask what would refute the shape, not whether the reader agrees.
5. Measure overlap before exclusivity (F5). Shared data pools mean no one has the advantage.
6. Send the generator (the census, the refuter, the links) and not a recording. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
