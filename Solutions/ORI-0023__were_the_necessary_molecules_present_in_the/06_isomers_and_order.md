## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition, and what does the search order become?

- Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Applying the four conditions: supply (D1 and D3), co-location (D2 habitat), coincidence (time channel), concentration (instrument). They overlap. Supply can hold without co-location (molecules delivered to different pools); co-location can hold without coincidence (same pool at different times); both can hold without concentration (dilute ocean). The intersection is a specific compound, not a harder supply problem.
- Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Method: four verdicts before any search.
- **Falsifier:** if one instrument could settle all four at once (for example, a measurement that simultaneously identified the locale, dated the co-occurrence and measured concentration), the union split would be an unnecessary decomposition. I do not have such an instrument, and Fig 9 :44 says an unsealed frame cannot be searched to the end: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*
- Status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 2 :29 distinct anchors plus Fig 13 :35 distinct primitives).

### Q2: Can adjacency and duration be one condition?

- Fig 13 :35: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity."*
- Fig 13 :36: *"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction"*
- Fig 13 :36-37: *"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."*
- So "same place" and "same time" are separate frames with separate instruments. Spatial association in a thin section is not contemporaneity; contemporaneity is not spatial association. If the field treated them as one fact, one would be redundant, which Fig 13 forbids.
- Fig 13 :75: *"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."* Co-presence is not a primitive state-state bond; a structure (a process, cycle, gradient or surface) must mediate.
- **Falsifier:** a figure re-census in which adjacency and duration are one reading would break Fig 13 :36 and dissolve this derivation. A measurement of direct space-time co-presence with no mediating structure would break Fig 13 :75.
- Status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 13 :35-36 and the world separation of supply from local concentration).

### Q3: Are one-pot and flow accounts isomers?

- The Miller-Urey, Butlerow formose and Oró syntheses are "one pot" accounts: ingredients mixed, products formed (described in the Patel paper's comparison section).
- Patel et al. (2015) report that their own network *"would be difficult"* in one pot because the Kiliani-Fischer-type homologation needs periodic delivery of HCN and H2S and other reagents need sequential delivery. They move to a flow scheme in which *"streams with different reaction histories merge"* and the products *"would all end up mixed in pools."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Both accounts operate over overlapping molecular sets (sugars, amino acid precursors, nucleotide precursors, lipid precursors). They differ in arrangement: simultaneous mixture in one vessel versus sequential delivery and spatial convergence. That is a different bond, hence a different substance with different predictions about where and when co-location occurs.
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The weaker reading: both accounts share the functional group "a local concentration of feedstocks exposed to an energy source", differing in which member fills it. If the isomer claim fails, this is what stands.
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Mineral surfaces and wet-dry cycling are catalysts in this sense. The one-pot directory does not record which parts are catalytic, which is Fig 12 :5's missing half again.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one account can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, if the flow scheme needs a feedstock the one-pot scheme never produces), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If both share the full atom set and differ only in arrangement, the isomer claim holds.
- Status: `specified`, single-source on the figure definition (Fig 12 :117) with the chemistry as a corroborating but same-question source. Not `grounded`: no Hadean locale discriminates the two.

### Q4: What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Each supply stream, once settled against its own ground (a laboratory yield, a meteorite assay), becomes an atom for the co-location question at the next level.
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Order: enumerate habitat (Fig 11 :43) -> bind a vocabulary for co-presence at concentration -> state the locale-and-window objective -> search (SEARCHED, then INWARD and OUTWARD as opposed instruments) -> carry methods (Fig 4 :22).
- Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Moving the question from a Hadean pool to a laboratory flask substitutes a habitat. What you failed to reproduce (the time depth, the mineral surface, the UV flux) is invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*). This is the standing caveat on every laboratory co-production result.
- Status: `general` on the order, `specified` on the substitution caveat.

## CONCLUSION

The bundle is a union, not a partition, and must be unbundled into four verdicts before search. Adjacency and duration are distinct substrate readings, and co-presence is not a primitive because a structure must sit between space and time. The one-pot and flow accounts are isomers over an overlapping molecular set, with a functional-group fallback and a stated falsifier. The order is habitat first, vocabulary second, objective third, search fourth. Nothing here returns a date, a locale or a molecule list. Those are names, and where the framework returns a shape they are owed an import with provenance and a shadow test.
