**Subject:** Check one shape claim about the Standard Model parameters before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the physics, `gap` on the numbers):**
>
> The Standard Model is a closure. Once you fix its gauge symmetry and field content, you still have to hand it roughly 19 numbers (masses, couplings, mixing angles, the Higgs sector parameters, the QCD vacuum angle). Nothing in the Lagrangian produces those numbers. They enter from measurement. So for each number there is no prediction gap to close: the measured value is inserted before the prediction is made. The live residual is the absence of a derivation, and it has not shrunk after decades of work inside the theory.
>
> The shape claim is this: **the parameter values are atoms imported from the ground, not molecules composed inside the theory.** More calculation inside the present closure cannot derive them, because a closure cannot add an atom. Explaining them requires a frame whose closure actually contains them as derived terms and which contacts the same experimental ground. The framework can describe the shape of that frame. It does not name one, and I am not claiming a specific theory here.
>
> I also want to be precise about what the question is not. "Roughly 19" is a counting convention: reputable sources say 18, 19, or 28 depending on what is counted. The numbers themselves are measured with high precision. I am not disputing any measurement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a Standard Model parameter derived inside the theory from the symmetry and the other parameters, with no measured number inserted;
> 2. a candidate frame that predicts a parameter value or a relation among values, stated before measurement and confirmed at the ground;
> 3. a single mechanism that explains all the slots with equal residual reduction, so that working slot by slot is unnecessary.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. I wrote down a fourth too: if a measurement distinguished two counting conventions and made one false rather than merely different, my point about convention would fail. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, verbatim text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the formula on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
