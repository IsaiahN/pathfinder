## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether the measured numbers are correct. They are measured to high precision and the theory predicts observables accurately once they are inserted. What is being decided is whether the numbers are **derived terms** of the theory or **imported atoms**.

Write the Standard Model as a library Γ. A prediction is Γ(b,a), the library applied to a belief (the parameters as currently fixed) and an action (a calculated process). The ground returns o′, a measured mass, coupling, or mixing angle. For each parameter slot s the theory's R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is driven to zero **by construction**, because o′ is put into Γ before the prediction is made. The number was not produced by the theory; it was fed to it. The live residual is therefore not a prediction error. It is the persistent, flat absence of a derivation on those slots (Fig 5 :58-60, a residual that *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"*).

- Option A, "the values are derived within the Standard Model": predicts that enough calculation inside the existing closure will produce them. This is falsified on inspection: they are inputs to the Lagrangian, not outputs of it.
- Option B, "the values are imports from the ground, and their origin requires a frame with a different closure": predicts that more searching inside the Standard Model cannot produce them, and that a source-discriminating frame outside it would be needed. This is the Tether verdict.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): the *shape* of the missing frame, namely a closure in which the parameter values appear as derived terms and which contacts the same ground. Bound as a term, that shape can accrue a residual and be refused. Unbound, it is only a preference and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B: `general` on the closure structure, `specified` on the physics application, `gap` on the individual numbers. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the physics question, no naming of a specific beyond-Standard-Model theory, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of the parameters.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
