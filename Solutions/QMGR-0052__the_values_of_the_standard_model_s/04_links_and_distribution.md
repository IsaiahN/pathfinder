## What in the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The residual is stated in the physics literature, not invented here.** Donoghue 2007 and Cahn 1996 both say the parameters are arbitrary inputs and that explaining their origin is the open problem. Wikipedia's reference table and Challenges section reproduce the same point. These sources establish that "not predicted by the theory" is the field's own description of the situation.

2. **The count is a convention.** Cahn says eighteen, Wikipedia lists nineteen, Donoghue counts twenty-eight. Wikipedia itself notes that explaining neutrino masses *"will require an additional 7 or 8 constants"*. This corroborates the convention finding (F5) and warns against treating "19" as a ground fact.

3. **A pattern over the values is not a derivation.** Donoghue reports that quark and lepton masses are *"consistent with being a random distribution"* in log m under a scale-invariant weight. Love 2023 documents the well-known human tendency to see patterns where none exist. Together, these are a caution, not a solution: the figures' rule is *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* (Fig 9 :27-28). A statistical regularity is a shape to be explained, not the explanation.

4. **Two philosophical reactions are recorded, neither decided.** SEP 2026 lists accepting fine-tuning as a primitive given and expecting future theories to avoid it. Donoghue 2007 explores the multiverse as a third. Under the figures these are candidate imports; without a source gate, a shadow test, and ground settlement they carry zero verdict weight. Note especially Donoghue's own limit: *"Without a unique ground state, we cannot use the prediction of the parameters as a proof of string theory."*

5. **Cost is a real, measurable quantity in model selection.** Roos 2017 states the MDL bargain: prefer the model with the shortest total description, counting model complexity. This is a world-source corroboration of the figures' own acceptance test (Fig 5 :45 Rissanen 1978; Fig 12 :100-102), from a field with no stake in this framework. It supports saying that a theory needing many externally fixed numbers has a longer description than one that derives them; it does **not** select a particular theory.
