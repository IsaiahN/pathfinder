## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* Gathering more agreeable responders manufactures influence, not evidence.
2. **Correlation has valence.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* The question is exactly about how influence makes agreement worthless as a verdict, so selecting recipients for receptiveness would re-enact the failure under study.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends the only thing a checker contributes.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 "must not hold" the interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk outreach to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. Public opt-in venues avoid both.
