# Expert verification packet - status, authority, and whose knowledge is copied or trusted

**What this is:** a claim in a form a social epistemologist, social psychologist, or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separations, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a copy or trust decision is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7). It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The effect of status and authority on whose knowledge is copied or trusted is a union of overlapping selections (copy a model, trust a testimony, credit a name), and its valence flips with the product. For a shared meaning, correlation is efficient and a prestigious source is a cheap coordinator. For a verdict about the world, agreement is disqualifying when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool. The failure mechanism is a room with no non-updating anchor: frames update on each other, converge on whoever had the most influence, and cannot see the drift because it is coherent. Prestige can fund a trial of a claim but cannot settle it, because standing is a stake that survives the outcome on which it was earned. A trust decision counts as a verdict only with independent readings, an anchor that does not update on the readers, and a checker able to refuse the claim. The framework returns none of a rank order of experts, a credibility weight, or a universal deference fraction.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal and observationally checkable: the no-anchor influence mechanism

- **Statement:** in a room with no reachable anchor, the group's convergence is a weighted average of influence, and the drift is invisible from inside because it is coherent.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :13-15, normalized across the line break: *"no anchor; each updates on the others they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*. Also Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* And Seat Map :283: *"A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence."*
- **What to check:** is there a dissociation paradigm in your field in which content and influence are varied separately? The claim predicts selection tracks influence when no anchor is reachable and tracks content when one is.
- **Refuter:** show a no-anchor group converging reliably on the least influential member across domains, or tracking content rather than influence under dissociation.

### C2 - The valence split: convention versus verdict

- **Statement:** the same influence is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 2 :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Seat Map :163 *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*
- **What to check:** in a shared-meaning task, does deference to a prestigious source produce a coordination benefit? In a world-claim task with a later ground measurement, does deference with a shared evidence pool match independent verdicts? The claim predicts yes to the first and no to the second.
- **Refuter:** a convention with deference and no measured coordination benefit, or a correlated shared-pool verdict matching a triangulated verdict on a later measurement.

### C3 - Framework-internal: prestige is a budget, not a settlement, and standing survives

- **Statement:** status may allocate a trial but cannot settle it; standing is a stake that outlives the outcome that earned it, so it must be firewalled from settlement rather than removed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :61 *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* And the opened essay *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*
- **What to check:** in your institution or dataset, does allowing prestige to settle outcomes compound error across rounds? Does a reputation update exactly with the current outcome, or does it outlive it?
- **Refuter:** a system where prestige-based settlement shows no compounding error, or a reputation that tracks the current measurement without lag.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that authority or status is always a bias. For conventions, deference is efficient and is the product.
- Not that a prestigious source is never right, or that any named person is untrustworthy.
- Not a rank order of experts, a credibility weight, or a deference fraction. No figure line returns one; that is a declared `gap`.
- Not that influence can be removed. Stakes are structural and are declared, not eliminated (Fig 10 :59).
- Not that a standing committee of reputable people solves the problem. Fig 10 :72: *"When the question changes, the room should change."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Dissociation test:** vary status and content independently at the copy or trust decision, with and without a reachable anchor.
2. **Convention/verdict test:** measure coordination benefit under deference in a shared-meaning task and accuracy under deference in a world-claim task against the same later ground.
3. **Independence test:** independent readings versus a shared evidence pool on the same later measurement.
4. **Firewall test:** prestige allocates trials but never settles them; check whether error stops compounding.
5. **Standing test:** does reputation update with the current outcome or outlive it?
6. Any of the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 firing.

3. Name one instrument in your field that discriminates a source from a credibility reading, or say why none exists.
