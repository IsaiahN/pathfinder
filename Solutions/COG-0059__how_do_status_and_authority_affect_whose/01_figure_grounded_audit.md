## 0. The question as a residual, stated in effect terms

Source question, `cognitive science.txt` line 72: "How do status and authority affect whose knowledge is copied or trusted?"

Restated as an effect, not a topic: there is a selection event at which a copier or truster takes up one model's knowledge rather than another's. The residual is the gap between what that selection delivers and what the ground pays for. In formula terms, per slot: `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`. The slot is the individual copy or trust decision, not the population rate (Fig 1 :19, :22). Status (prestige, rank, reputation, visibility) and authority (legitimate position, uniform, title) are candidate inputs to that selection. Nothing in the question says whether those inputs help or harm the alignment.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction").** Before the run, write down what an answer would look like so it can accrue a residual like anything else. An answer is acceptable only if it:

1. separates the cases where influence coordinates a shared meaning (cheap and useful) from the cases where influence decides a verdict about the world (disqualifying);
2. names, for each domain, the anchor that would have to be reachable for a copy or trust decision to count as a verdict;
3. states what would refute the separation;
4. returns a shape and a route, not a rank ordering of named individuals and not a universal deference coefficient.

If an answer cannot do these four things, it is a list of biases, not a derivation. The pre-registered refuter is in section 4 and was written before the conclusion.

## 2. Domain census before choosing any figure line

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity: three or more overlapping conditions, not one.** At minimum (a) copying a behavior because the model is high status, (b) trusting a testimony because the source holds authority, (c) crediting and re-copying a name because credit accumulated. These are not levels of one variable. A single lever, for example "defer to credentials," clears the region where only authority binds and leaves the intersections where authority and popularity and reputation all bind. Fig 9 :54-58: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Verdict: **union**, so verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Symmetry: "affect" is symmetric while "whose" is directed.** Status feeds trust and trust feeds status; the arrows run both ways (the prestige loop). A symmetric correlation cannot choose the arrow (Operators on `+` versus `→`/`⇒`). Only a ground that does not update on the reputation can break the circle. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **Scale: individual, group, institution, recursive.** A child copying an adult, a nurse obeying a doctor, a chimpanzee watching a dominant, a reviewer reading a famous name, and a funding committee allocating budget are the same loop at different strata with different actors, currencies, and anchors. Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."* Levels are not copies.

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor. Where the anchor cannot be named yet, the item is a phenomenon awaiting a domain, not a domain.

- **D1, social learning and copying.** Actors: model and copier (adult and child, trained demonstrator and naïve observer, high-ranking and low-ranking chimpanzee). Currency: behavioral fidelity and adoption frequency. Anchor: whether the copied behavior pays against the habitat when practiced, a nameable outcome (food obtained, task solved). Without an outcome test, the copy is a convention or a mirror, not a verdict.
- **D2, testimony and authority.** Actors: authority figure and evaluator (doctor and nurse, experimenter and subject, expert and public). Currency: credibility assigned, obedience given. Anchor: an outcome that does not update on the authority's standing (patient outcome, board score, measurement). If the anchor is the source's own reputation, the arrangement is collapse 1, and Fig 2 :15 applies: *"is whoever had the most influence"*.
- **D3, verification and social epistemology.** Actors: frames and checkers (reviewers, jury members, crowd estimators, peer review). Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence is the quantity that makes agreement mean something (Fig 2 :31). This is the only domain where "trust" becomes a verdict rather than a deference.
- **D4, credit and status dynamics.** Actors: high-prestige and low-prestige contributors, funders, citers. Currency: credit, visibility, funding, attention. Anchor: the accumulated record of actions, which is nameable but structurally liable to decay because standing survives the outcome it was earned on (Fig 10 :61). This domain is a stake feeding back into the other three, not an independent verdict domain.
- **Awaiting a domain:** "cultural fit," "charisma," and "intuition about expertise" have no nameable anchor as stated. They are labels applied after a selection, so they are not admitted as domains. They are recorded as gaps where relevant, not smuggled in as causes.

### 2.3 Partition or union

Union (Fig 9 :54-58). Copy, trust, and credit overlap. One lever leaves intersections standing. The shared condition across all four domains is independence from an updating reference, so the separator is structural rather than topical.

### 2.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Establish the verdict rule and the anchor (D3), because the other domains assume an answer to "what would settle this copy or trust decision."
2. Refuse the route where influence is treated as a ground before minting any account of why copying occurs (Formula step 2: REFUTED owes a refusal, not an explanation).
3. Describe the residual in effect terms before picking a field (Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).
4. Settle the first-order copy or trust decision before composing the second-order credit dynamic (Fig 12 :104).
5. Split the union into per-domain verdicts before searching (Fig 9 :4-5).

## 3. Findings, each with a verbatim figure line and a refuter

### F1 - The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not one effect (structural, decisive for framing)

Fig 9 :54-55: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*

**Derivation.** Asking one number for "the effect of status and authority" aggregates four different selections. A prestige-biased copy in D1, an obedience in D2, an independent testimony verdict in D3, and a citation credit in D4 do not share a currency. A single coefficient is a filter for the budget, not a witness for a verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

**Refuter.** If a single mechanism (for example a general confidence heuristic) is shown to generate all four selections with one parameter that predicts each decision at the slot level, the union collapses to a partition and this finding fails. Until such a demonstration, per-domain verdicts stand.

### F2 - The core mechanism: with no anchor, a group converges on whoever had the most influence (triangulated, governing)

Fig 2 :13-15, normalized across the line break: *"no anchor; each updates on the others they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*

Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Seat Map :283: *"A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence."*

**Derivation.** This is the figure's direct answer to the mechanism part of the question. Status is not a separate contaminant added to a clean copying process. In a room with no reachable non-updating anchor, the selection rule *is* influence, and the output is a weighted average of who is believed. Because the drift is coherent, participants experience it as agreement, so it is invisible from inside. The world evidence corroborates the shape from three low-overlap directions. Sherif's autokinetic study is the laboratory version: subjects in a dark room converged on a common estimate and kept the group judgment when tested alone, because *"the movement of the light is ambiguous"* and *"the participants were relying on each other to define reality"* (Social proof, Wikipedia). The authority-bias literature reports obedience dropping from 65% to 20% when the experimenter's lab coat was replaced by plain clothes (Authority bias, Wikipedia), which is the uniform marker of legitimacy rather than content. The imitation literature reports that *"children tend to imitate older, competitive, and trustworthy individuals"* (Imitation, Wikipedia), and the animal literature reports that chimpanzees *"tended to imitate the behaviors of the older, higher ranking chimpanzee as opposed to the younger, lower ranking individual"* (Animal culture, Wikipedia). Four different pools, one shape.

**Refuter.** Show a case where a group with no reachable anchor converges on the *least* influential member, reliably and across domains, or show that the convergence tracks content rather than influence when content and influence are dissociated. Either would break the weighted-average-of-influence claim.

**Status.** `triangulated`: Fig 2, Fig 7, and the seat map on the figure side (low overlap between the mirror figure, the room chain, and the seat rule), with three independent world fields on the corroboration side.

### F3 - Correlation has valence: the same influence is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict (triangulated, the separator)

Fig 2 :39-41: *"Correlation has valence."* *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*

Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*

**Derivation.** This is the missing half of any honest answer. Copying the high-status member is not always a failure. Where the product is a shared meaning (a norm, a word, a greeting, a technical convention), correlation *is* the product, and a prestigious source is a cheap coordination device. It becomes a defect at the moment the convention decides an outcome about the world, because then the agreement stops being a convention and starts being a verdict with no anchor. The test is not "did influence operate" but "what is the product, and is there a non-updating anchor." This is why the question as posed cannot be answered with a single valence.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a shared-meaning convention where deference to a high-status source produced no coordination benefit and the group would have been better off with independent selection, with the outcome measured; or exhibit a world-claim verdict where correlated agreement with a shared pool was as reliable as triangulated agreement.

**Status.** `triangulated`: Fig 2, the seat map, and Fig 10 :47 on the figure side. Single-source on the world side for the convention half, since the world sources document the disqualifying half more strongly.

### F4 - Prestige is not action: status funds a trial, it cannot settle one, and standing survives the outcome (triangulated)

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*

Fig 10 :48: *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"*

Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened 2026-11-22): *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* And the mechanism of the failure: *"The prestige loop confirms itself. The high-prestige person is believed because they are high-prestige. The action is assumed to work because the person is believed. The ground is never consulted. The error compounds."*

**Derivation.** Status has a legitimate function: it allocates a budget for trying a claim. The illegitimate function is verdict substitution, where the reputation of the source is read as the result of the action. Fig 10 :61 explains why this is structural rather than personal: standing is a stake that outlives the outcome on which it was earned, so even a checker with no interest in today's result carries yesterday's interest into today's reading. The world evidence matches. The Matthew effect literature reports that *"eminent scientists will often get more credit than a comparatively unknown researcher, even if their work is similar"* and that *"credit will usually be given to researchers who are already famous"* (Matthew effect, Wikipedia), with an experimental cultural market showing that *"the group that saw which songs were the most popular and were downloaded the most were then biased to choose those songs as well."* In social media, a user with a million followers *"is perceived as more trustworthy and reputable than a similar user with a thousand followers"* (Social proof, Wikipedia). These are the prestige loop at the institutional and platform scales.

**Refuter.** Show a system where prestige is allowed to settle outcomes and the error does not compound over repeated trials, with an outcome record showing no drift. Or show that standing does not survive the outcome that earned it (a reputation that updates exactly with the current measurement and no faster). Either would falsify the structural claim.

**Status.** `triangulated`: Fig 10 and the opened essay on the primary side (different objects, figure law versus design rationale), with three low-overlap world fields (sociology of science, platform studies, social psychology).

### F5 - A trust verdict needs independence and a non-updating anchor, not more agreement (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29 (partial): *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

**Derivation.** "Whose knowledge is trusted" becomes a verdict only under three conditions: independent readings (so agreement is not one pool talking), an anchor that does not update on what the frames think, and a checker that cannot reconstruct the claim from its own picture. The world sources give the mathematics and the crowd evidence. Condorcet's jury theorem assumes *"each voter has an independent probability p of voting for the correct decision"* and shows that adding voters approaches certainty only when `p > 1/2` (Condorcet's jury theorem, Wikipedia) (the independence assumption is the load-bearing one). Wisdom of the crowd is defined as *"the collective opinion of a diverse and independent group of individuals (rather than that of a single expert) yields the best judgement,"* and the same page warns that *"Social influence can cause the average of the crowd's answers to be inaccurate"* and that *"the crowd needs to be independent but also diversified."* Authority bias is exactly the removal of that independence: *"the tendency to attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of an authority figure (unrelated to its content)"* (Authority bias, Wikipedia).

**Refuter.** Show a trust arrangement where many frames drawing on one evidence pool, with no independent anchor, reliably matched a later ground measurement over repeated trials. Or show that a verifier who could reconstruct the claim verified it as well as one who could not.

**Status.** `triangulated`: Fig 2 and Fig 7 (low overlap), with mathematics, crowd psychology, and social psychology as three world fields, though the latter two share a social-science pool and count as fewer than two full witnesses.

### F6 - The channel decays: credibility markers saturate and the question moves while the room stays fixed (single-source on the figure side, corroborated)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

Fig 10 :72: *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."*

The imitation synthesis adds a population-level version: *"an over-saturation of imitation and imitating individuals leads humans to collectively copy inefficient strategies and evolutionarily maladaptive behaviors, thereby reducing flexibility to new environmental contexts that require adaptation"* (Imitation, Wikipedia).

**Derivation.** The cues people use to decide whom to trust (uniform, title, follower count, prior fame, the same respected reviewer) are channels to a ground, not the ground. Fig 10 :24 says channels decay: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* (Fig 10 :25-26). A saturated credibility marker is a channel failure that looks like a working one. And because the question moves (a new domain, a new instrument, a new failure), a fixed panel of authorities becomes a fixed room, which Fig 10 :72 names as the failure rather than the correlation. This is why "whose knowledge" cannot be answered with a standing list. Seat Map :217: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

**Refuter.** Exhibit a standing panel of authorities whose credibility markers neither saturate nor go stale across a documented change of question, with outcomes still tracking the ground.

**Status.** `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 10 and the seat guide are the same framework pool); the world corroboration is from a different field (animal and human social learning) but is mechanism-shape only.

### F7 - No figure line returns a rank order of people or a deference coefficient (gap, recorded as a verdict)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 13 :134 (the framework's own limit on naming): the framework returns what a missing thing would look like, a shape and not a name. Canonical figures README: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."*

**Derivation.** A genuine re-census finds no figure line that selects a specific expert, a credibility weight, or a fraction of deference. The figures fix the conditions under which a copy or trust decision is a verdict (independence, anchor, refusal seat, method not recording), and they fix which situations are a collapse. They do not name the person. This is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not a silence and not unreachable in principle: a ground measurement in a specific domain would settle a specific version of the question. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

**Refuter.** Produce a figure line that prescribes a credibility weight or ranks informants by status. The absence of such a line after re-census is the only basis for this gap, so it would be closed by a line I have not found.

**Status.** `gap`.

### F8 - Re-census of an apparent contradiction: human judgment as anchor versus an outcome as anchor (strain dissolved)

Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*

Fig 2 :37-38 (partial): *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct"*

**Derivation.** A first pass reads these lines as saying that a question about trust must be anchored in human judgment, which seems to license exactly the appeal to reputation the other findings refuse. Re-census dissolves it. There are two different questions with two different objects. (1) The empirical social-science question "whose knowledge do people actually copy and trust" is constitutively about people, so observed human behavior is the right ground; an instrument that measures something else (for example citation counts as a proxy for trust) can be wrong on the axis of actual behavior. (2) The epistemic question "is the copied knowledge true" is constitutively about an outcome, so human judgment is only an instrument and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. The first is a measurement of selection; the second is a verdict on content. Mixing them is the error. No strain remains after re-census, matching the canonical rule that a reading making two figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

**Refuter.** Show that for the epistemic question, a population's judgment about a source's trustworthiness tracks the truth of the source's claims as well as an outcome measurement, across domains. That would collapse the distinction.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union fails:** a single mechanism with one parameter predicts copy, trust, and credit selections at the slot level across all four domains.
2. **Influence mechanism fails:** a group with no reachable anchor reliably converges on the least influential member, across domains, or converges on content rather than influence under dissociation.
3. **Valence fails:** a shared-meaning convention with deference shows no coordination benefit, or a world-claim verdict backed by correlated agreement with a shared evidence pool matches triangulated verdicts against a later ground measurement.
4. **Prestige/action fails:** a system in which prestige settles outcomes shows no compounding error over repeated trials, or standing updates exactly with the current outcome.
5. **Independence fails:** many frames sharing one evidence pool with no independent anchor match a ground measurement as well as independent frames.
6. **Channel fails:** a fixed panel of authorities keeps outcomes tracking a moved ground with no channel decay.
7. **Gap fails:** a figure line is produced that ranks informants or prescribes a deference weight.

If any fires, the derivation is withdrawn or narrowed at that point. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator, not the list of biases: ask first what the product is (a convention or a verdict), then which anchor is reachable, then whether the readings are independent. F1, F3, F5.
2. Treat status as a budget allocator and a declared stake, never as a verdict input. Refuse the substitution explicitly. F4.
3. Keep verdicts per domain and never average them into a single "authority effect." F1.
4. Build rooms that differ and match the surfaced residual; rotate checking frames because independence is spent by use. Fig 7 :36 and Fig 10 :72.
5. Route methods, not recordings: send the derivation and the refuter, not a conclusion dressed as a finding. Fig 4 :22-25.
6. Accept the gap: no figure line names a person or a weight. Provide the instrument that would settle a specific case instead. F7.
