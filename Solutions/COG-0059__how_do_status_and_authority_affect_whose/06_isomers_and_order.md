# Isomers, union structure, and order in the status/authority question

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer and order claims, `general` on the structural split, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7). No measurement was used.

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how status and authority affect whose knowledge is copied or trusted. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are the two named selections, copy and trust, the same term bonded differently, or different terms; and (2) in what order must the separator be applied for the verdict to be sound?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (the one this file needs)

Atoms available: a model or source, a method or claim, a copier or evaluator, an outcome. Bonds available: the seven, including `+` (symmetric), `→` (sequence), `⇒` (ordered), and `¬` (refusal). No new primitive is available without import. "Status" and "authority" are not atoms; they are bindings of the model atom to a property of the social frame (rank, title, visibility, legitimacy). They are different bindings, so they are candidate isomers over a shared atom set.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are "copy because high status" and "copy because it paid" isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124 (functional group): *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Over the atom set `{model, method, copier, outcome}`, the two adoptions are:

1. **Prestige-bonded adoption:** copier ← model, where the binding reason is the model's rank or title. The outcome atom is not on the live path.
2. **Ground-bonded adoption:** copier ← (method tested against outcome), where the binding reason is that the method paid.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance: (1) is a convention that feels like truth, (2) is a verdict that can be refused. In the formula they look alike, because both are "copier adopts method from model." Only the record (provenance) and the behaviour under a changed outcome separate them. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

The three named inputs are isomers too, over a wider set. *"Copy the high-status model"*, *"obey the legitimate authority"*, and *"cite the famous name"* share the atoms but differ in currency (fidelity, obedience, credit) and in anchor (habitat payoff, patient outcome, accumulated record). They are three members of a functional group whose kind is "influence-weighted selection" (Fig 12 :124).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if prestige-bonded and ground-bonded adoptions can be separated by the observable behavior of the copier alone, with no record and no outcome test (for example, if they produce distinguishable copying errors), they are not isomers and the isomorphism claim fails. If a single atom is sufficient to generate all three selections, the functional-group claim fails and the union in `01` F1 collapses.

### Q2 - The union is not a partition

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

Consequences for this question:

- A lever that only reduces deference to titles clears the authority-only region. It leaves the region where a title and a large following and an accumulated reputation all bind. That region is *"categorically different"* (Fig 9 :57), not a harder case of the authority-only region.
- A lever that only adds more reviewers does not clear the shared-pool region. Seat Map :283: adding people *"raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor"*.
- The shared condition is independence from an updating reference, so the one lever that reaches the intersections is the anchor plus independence condition, not a bias-reduction campaign.

### Q3 - Order: refuse before mint, describe before looking, settle before composing

1. **Refuse before mint (Formula step 2).** The route "status is a ground for trust" is REFUTED, not NOVEL. What is owed is a refusal: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Explanation of the prestige loop comes after the refusal, not instead of it.
2. **Describe before looking (Fig 9 :27-28).** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Characterize the residual as a selection with or without an anchor before choosing social psychology, animal behaviour, or epistemology as the frame.
3. **Settle the first-order molecule before the second-order composition (Fig 12 :104).** A specific copy or trust decision must be settled against an outcome before credit can be composed over it. Composing credit over an unsettled adoption is dead reckoning.
4. **Bracket the round trip (Fig 4 :30).** *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Any condensation of "status affects trust" into a single coefficient loses the per-slot indexing, the union, the independence condition, and the refusal. The gap is qualitative and enumerated in `11_audit.md`.
5. **Rotate the checking room (Fig 10 :72).** *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."* Independence is spent by checking, so the answer includes a refresh rule, not a standing roster.

Each analogue corroborates the shape only. None settles the human question, and several come from pools that overlap each other.

## CONCLUSION

The two named adoptions are **isomers over a shared atom set** (same atoms, different bond, different substance), with a falsifier stated above. The four domains form a **union**, not a partition, so verdicts stay separate and one lever does not clear the intersections. The order of application is: refuse the status-as-ground route, describe the residual in effect terms, settle first-order decisions before composing credit, bracket the round trip, and rotate the checking room. The governing figure for the mechanism is Fig 2's no-anchor collapse, and the governing figure for the separator is Fig 2's correlation-valence rule.
