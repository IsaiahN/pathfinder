## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the effect of status and authority on whose knowledge is copied or trusted. Option A: treat it as one effect with one valence and answer with a list of biases and a deference coefficient. Option B: treat it as a union of overlapping selections whose valence flips with the product, and return the separator plus the conditions under which a copy or trust decision becomes a verdict. What each leads to: A produces a coefficient that cannot be checked at the decision level and re-enacts the very influence-driven agreement it purports to measure; B produces per-domain verdicts, a refusal of status-as-ground, five written refuters, and a declared gap where no figure line names a person or a weight.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Per-copy and per-trust decision: the gap between what the selection delivers and what the ground pays for (Fig 1 :19, :22). Arity: three or more overlapping conditions, so a union compound, not one hard question. Symmetry: "affect" is symmetric while "whose" is directed; the arrows between status and trust run both ways, so only a non-updating ground breaks the circle. Scale: individual, group, institution, recursive across strata with different actors, currency and ground.
- **Domain 1, social learning and copying.** Actors: model and copier. Currency: behavioral fidelity and adoption frequency. Anchor: whether the copied behavior pays against the habitat when practiced, a nameable outcome. Without an outcome test the copy is a convention or a mirror, not a verdict.
- **Domain 2, testimony and authority.** Actors: authority figure and evaluator. Currency: credibility assigned and obedience given. Anchor: an outcome that does not update on the source's standing. If the anchor is the source's reputation, the arrangement is the no-anchor collapse.
- **Domain 3, verification and social epistemology.** Actors: frames and checkers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence is the load-bearing quantity. This is the only domain in which trust becomes a verdict.
- **Domain 4, credit and status dynamics.** Actors: high and low prestige contributors, funders, citers. Currency: credit, visibility, funding. Anchor: the accumulated record of actions, nameable but liable to decay because standing survives the outcome that earned it. This is a stake feeding back into the other three, not an independent verdict domain.
- **Awaiting a domain:** charisma, cultural fit, and intuition about expertise have no nameable anchor as stated. They are labels applied after a selection and are not admitted as domains.
- **Partition or union:** union. One lever clears the authority-only region and leaves the intersections where authority, popularity and reputation all bind.
- **Order by dependency:** refuse status-as-ground before minting an account (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle each first-order decision before composing credit over it (Fig 12 :104); bracket the round trip (Fig 4 :30); rotate the checking room (Fig 10 :72).

Strain test: the apparent conflict between human judgment as anchor and an outcome as anchor is two questions with two objects, the empirical selection question and the epistemic truth question. Re-census dissolves it (see `01` F8). No strain remains from that pair.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only; they never overrule the figures. Overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Sociology of science.** Wikipedia contributors, "Matthew effect" (Wikipedia, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"eminent scientists will often get more credit than a comparatively unknown researcher, even if their work is similar"*. Predicts credit accumulation and reputation substitution; corroborates D4 and the prestige loop. Low overlap with the other pools, though it shares the general social-science tradition.
- **Human cognition, social psychology.** Wikipedia contributors, "Authority bias" (Wikipedia, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"the tendency to attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of an authority figure (unrelated to its content)"*. Predicts credibility assigned from position rather than content; corroborates D2. Overlap: high with social proof and imitation.
- **Social psychology.** Wikipedia contributors, "Social proof" (Wikipedia, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"people copy the actions of others in choosing how to behave in a given situation"*. Predicts Sherif-style convergence and rapid convergence on a single choice with little information; corroborates D1 and the no-anchor mechanism. Overlap: high with authority bias.
- **Human development and animal behaviour.** Wikipedia contributors, "Imitation" (Wikipedia, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"children tend to imitate older, competitive, and trustworthy individuals"*. Predicts status-biased model selection and overimitation of irrelevant steps; corroborates D1. Overlap: moderate with social proof; the overimitation studies are cited by more than one page.
- **Animal behaviour.** Wikipedia contributors, "Animal culture" (Wikipedia, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"chimpanzees tended to imitate the behaviors of the older, higher ranking chimpanzee as opposed to the younger, lower ranking individual"*. Predicts status-biased copying without testimony; corroborates D1 at low overlap with the human social-psychology pool.
- **Mathematics of aggregation.** Wikipedia contributors, "Wisdom of the crowd" (Wikipedia, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"the collective opinion of a diverse and independent group of individuals (rather than that of a single expert) yields the best judgement"*. Predicts that independence and diversity, not volume or prestige, produce a verdict; corroborates D3. Low overlap.
- **Mathematics / social choice.** Wikipedia contributors, "Condorcet's jury theorem" (Wikipedia, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"each voter has an independent probability p of voting for the correct decision"*. Predicts that independence is the load-bearing precondition and that adding dependent voters adds nothing; corroborates D3. Genuinely low overlap.
- **Framework essay, opened directly.** Isaiah N, "The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap" (Adventures in Machine Learning, 2026; accessed 2026-11-22): *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action."* Predicts the budget/settlement separation; corroborates D4 and the separator. Overlap: maximal with the figures (same author), so no independent witness weight.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The effect of status and authority on whose knowledge is copied or trusted is a union whose valence flips with the product, not one effect with one sign. For a shared meaning, correlation is efficient and deference is a cheap coordinator. For a verdict about the world, correlation with a shared evidence pool is disqualifying, and the mechanism is the no-anchor room in which the group converges on whoever had the most influence and cannot see its own drift. Prestige allocates the budget to try a claim but cannot settle it, because standing is a stake that outlives the outcome that earned it. A copy or trust decision counts as a verdict only with independent readings, an anchor that does not update on the readers, and a checker free to refuse. No figure line returns a person, a weight, or a deference coefficient, and that gap is a verdict. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims, five refuters and a provenance record before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a list of biases and a conclusion instead of a separator and a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the claim and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The separations do not rely on a named person or a coefficient, so no binding to a particular field must be withdrawn if one fails. A fired refuter narrows the union at that domain; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state the narrowing conditions. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A dissociation experiment showing selection tracks content when an anchor is reachable and influence when it is not would confirm the separator; one showing the opposite would reverse it. A convention with deference and no coordination benefit, a shared-pool verdict matching a triangulated verdict on a later measurement, a no-anchor group converging on the least influential member, a prestige-settled system with no compounding error, or a fixed room tracking a moved ground would each reverse the relevant domain. A figure re-census producing a line that ranks informants or prescribes a weight would close the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
