# The Tether's answer: status, authority, and whose knowledge is copied or trusted

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separations, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No experiment, survey, or observation was carried out here.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the mechanism by which status and authority select whose knowledge is copied or trusted. The residual is per copy or trust decision, not a population average (Fig 1 :19, :22): the gap between what the selection delivers and what the ground pays for. The question bundles at least three overlapping selections (copy a model, trust a testimony, credit a name), so it is a union compound with its own loop, not one question with a single coefficient (Fig 9 :54-58). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The effect has no single valence; the product decides it.** Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Where the product is a shared meaning, a convention, agreement is the product and a prestigious source is a cheap coordinator. Where the product is a verdict about the world, agreement is worthless if the agreeing parties share an evidence pool. *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 2 :43). So the honest answer to "how do status and authority affect whose knowledge is copied or trusted" is: they coordinate cheaply in one case and disqualify the result in the other, and the two must not be pooled.
2. **The mechanism, where it fails, is no-anchor convergence on influence.** Fig 2 :13-15: with no anchor, each frame updates on the others, *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."* Because the drift is coherent, nobody notices. Seat Map :283: agreement in such a room is *"a weighted average of influence,"* and adding people *"raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* Status here is not a contaminant added to a clean process; it is the selection rule itself.
3. **Prestige funds a trial, it cannot settle one.** Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action."* So status has a legitimate function, allocating attention and budget, and one illegitimate function, substituting the record of past success for the test of present success. The substitution compounds because the prestige loop *"looks like agreement, and agreement feels like truth."*
4. **A trust verdict needs independence, a non-updating anchor, and a refusal seat.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The mathematics agrees: Condorcet requires independent voters, and crowd aggregation removes random error but not a shared bias. Deference to authority removes exactly the independence that makes agreement mean something.
5. **Channels decay and rooms go stale.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Uniforms, titles, follower counts, and familiar reviewer names are channels to a ground, not grounds. They saturate, and a fixed room fails when the question moves (Fig 10 :72). Hence no standing list of trustworthy people survives a change of residual.
6. **What the framework refuses to return:** a rank order of experts, a credibility weight, or a universal deference fraction. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). That is a declared gap, not a silence.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that authority or status is always a bias. For conventions, deference is efficient and is the product.
- Not that a prestigious source is never right. Prestige can buy the trial that lets the ground settle the claim.
- Not that any named person or institution is untrustworthy. The derivation is about the arrangement, not individuals.
- Not that all agreement is corrupt. Agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it.
- Not that influence can be removed. Stakes are structural; declare what cannot be removed (Fig 10 :59).
- Not that a standing committee of good people solves this. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles any of the four verdicts.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Dissociation test:** vary status and content independently at the copy or trust decision. If selection tracks content when the anchor is reachable and tracks influence when it is not, the separator pays.
2. **Convention/verdict test:** measure the coordination benefit of deference in a shared-meaning task and the accuracy of deference in a world-claim task against the same later ground. The valence flip must appear.
3. **Independence test:** compare independent readings against readings from a shared pool on the same later measurement. Independent frames must win on verdicts and lose nothing on conventions.
4. **Firewall test:** allow prestige to allocate trials but not to settle them, and check whether error stops compounding across rounds.
5. **Standing test:** check whether a reputation updates exactly with the current outcome or outlives it. The claim predicts it outlives it.
6. **Standing refuters:** the five in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the claim.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure returns the conditions of a sound copy or trust arrangement, not a specific person or weight. A ground measurement in a particular domain would settle that domain's version of the question; no such measurement was taken here. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence," and a declared `gap` is a verdict, not silence. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not say that status and authority are simply bad for knowledge. They say the effect flips with what the selection produces. When people are coordinating on a shared meaning, deference to a prestigious source is cheap and efficient, and agreement is the product. When people are deciding whether a claim about the world is true, the same agreement is worthless if the readers draw on one evidence pool, and the mechanism is stark: in a room with no anchor that does not move when the room changes its mind, the group converges on whoever had the most influence, and because the drift is coherent nobody notices. Prestige can pay for a claim to be tried, but it cannot settle the claim, because standing is a stake that outlives the result that earned it. Trust becomes a verdict only when the readings are independent, the anchor does not update on what anyone thinks, and a checker who cannot reconstruct the claim is free to refuse it. What the framework will not give you is a list of people to trust or a number for how much to defer. That is a shape and a route, with refuters written in advance, and it stays open until a measurement settles a specific case.
