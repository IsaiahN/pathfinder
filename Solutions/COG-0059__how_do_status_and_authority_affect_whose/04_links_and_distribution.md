## The wider literature and what it bears on

The seven encyclopedia articles are secondary syntheses, not primary studies. I opened and read the syntheses; I did not open the primary studies they cite. That reduces their weight and is declared in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

- **Low overlap, genuinely different fields:** animal culture (chimpanzee prestige bias), Condorcet jury theorem (independence as a formal precondition), Matthew effect (credit accumulation in science). These three are the strongest world corroborators because their evidence pools do not overlap the social-psychology pages.
- **One broad social-science pool:** authority bias, social proof, and imitation share the experimental social-psychology and developmental tradition and overlap in the studies they cite (Milgram, Sherif, Cialdini, Bandura, Lyons). They are one witness with several faces, not several witnesses.
- **No world source conflicts with the figure verdict.** The conflict rule was not triggered. Agreement between these sources and the figures is expected where they describe the same prestige loop, so it is corroboration of shape only, not a second derivation.

Two things in the fetched material bear directly on the claim:

1. **Sherif's autokinetic study** is the laboratory version of Fig 2 collapse 1. Subjects who had given different estimates in private converged on a group estimate and kept it when tested alone, because the stimulus was ambiguous and *"the participants were relying on each other to define reality."* That is a room with no anchor, not a verdict.
2. **The independence precondition** appears independently in the crowd and jury mathematics: averaging removes random error but not a shared bias, and the jury theorem requires independent votes. That is Fig 2 :31 and :40 in another notation.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22-25**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the derivation and the refuter first. Sending the conclusion first makes the recipient a believer, not a checker.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them the links.
- **Seat Map :163**: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for people already inclined to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Later agreement from a recipient who has seen the theory is not a witness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the three local source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only then the formula as a reusable method with a user-written refuter.
