# The Tether's answer: how reliably can species counts alone predict ecosystem functions?

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the empirical reading, not `grounded`.**
This is a synthesized shape and a route, not a measured reliability coefficient. It is a simulated
derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures and against world sources
that were actually opened on 2026-11-23. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*:
*"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the
formula yourself"*), it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

The observer holds one integer, the species count `S`, and predicts a vector of ecosystem functions
`F`: production, nutrient retention, decomposition, pollination, stability, and so on, at a site in
a habitat. The residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` is per function, per site, per context. "How reliably"
asks for the consistency with which the leftover `left(R, φ)` stays small when `φ` is the rule "use
`S` alone".

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem:

> Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
> Fig 9 :57 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
> Fig 9 :59 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*

It bundles identity at fixed count, trait and abundance arrangement, and habitat context. One lever
on `S` clears only the region where the other conditions do not bind.

## 2. The specified answer (the shape)

**1. A single reliability number is the wrong shape of answer.** The residual is indexed, not
aggregated:

> Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
> Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
> Fig 1 :13 *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*

Counting a community is itself aggregation across object slots. Ten species here and ten there are
one term under `S = 10`. Symbols table :43: *"One object, region or entity being tracked. R is
measured per slot, never as a single global number."* The honest report is a matrix of functions by
habitats, with per-slot residuals and the worst live slot shown.

**2. A count is a symmetric, many-to-one projection, so discriminating information is lost before
prediction.**

> Fig 12 :23 *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"*
> Fig 12 :92 *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"*
> Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Every ordering and every identity substitution of a set of `k` species gives the same integer. The
world analogue is metamerism: *"In colorimetry, metamerism is a perceived matching of colors with
different (nonmatching) spectral power distributions."* A count reads ten and cannot say which ten,
just as a colourimeter reading green *"cannot comment on the source"* (essay *The Hard Problem is
About Source*).

**3. What decides function is the arrangement, which the count does not record.**

> Fig 12 :117 *"same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
> Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Communities sharing a regional species pool are isomers in the figure's sense: same atoms, different
arrangement, different function. Gagic et al. (2015) found trait indices gave *"greater explanatory
power than species richness or abundance"* and that responses were due to *"identity and dominance
patterns of the trait composition rather than the number or abundance of species per se."* Hong et
al. (2022) found effects *"mainly driven by interspecific complementarity"*. Both are claims about
bonds, not counts.

**4. Habitat is load-bearing and cannot be substituted by a count.**

> Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
> Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
> Fig 11 :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
> Fig 11 :47 *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*
> Fig 11 :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

Carrick and Forsythe (2020) found that in recovering ecosystems the composition-function relation was
*"weak and saturating (directly accounting for only 2% of the variation)"*, and Hong et al. (2022)
found biodiversity effects *"often not to the same degree"* across environments. A count is not a
habitat. Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52), so a function three steps out can be the one that matters.

**5. More counting cannot close the gap, because the loss is upstream of search.**

> Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
> Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

The remedy is an import from a frame whose closure differs, with a provenance gate and a shadow test.
Trait ecology and habitat ecology are such frames, and Fig 8 :29 requires *"low overlap: B does not
draw on A's evidence"* for their surplus to exist. Note that this is underivable in principle, not
merely unreached at this budget: Fig 8 :40 *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."*

**6. The missing discriminator is characterisable as a shape, not named by the framework.**

> Fig 13 :133 *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*
> Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

The shape is: a predictor indexed by function slot, cross-kind, embedded in an enumerated habitat,
settled per function against measured data. That is not a nomination of one magic variable, and not a
single coefficient.

**7. Counts are a budget filter, never a verdict.**

> Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
> Fig 7 :13 *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
> Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

`S` is cheap and is legitimately used to triage sites for a full functional and habitat survey. It is
not a reason to let it decide the function, and a room of users of the same metric cannot repair it.

## 3. Mode and non-claims

Mode is declared on every substantive part: `general` for 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7; `general` plus
`specified` for 3. Nothing here is `grounded`. Explicit **non-claims**:

- **Not** that species richness has no predictive value. The literature read finds a general
  positive biodiversity effect. The surviving claim is insufficiency in general, not zero effect
  (this retracts the stronger wording in the first draft of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).
- **Not** that traits beat counts in every system, on every function. That is what the specific
  studies found on their taxa, functions and contexts, so it stays `specified`.
- **Not** a single numerical reliability for "counts alone" across all functions. That would be the
  aggregation failure Fig 1 forbids.
- **Not** a claim that the framework names a best predictor. It returns a shape; names need
  provenance and a shadow test (narrowed as in `05_what_shape_means.md` §5).
- **Not** a claim that more data, more samples, or a larger model of `S` will settle
  identity-dependent functions.
- **Not** a ground reading. No per-slot reliability coefficient is reported, because no function,
  site set, habitat, model and error metric were measured together in this session.

## 4. What would settle it: falsifiers written before the conclusion

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is withdrawn if any of
the following fires, all of which are also in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 and
`08_expert_verification_packet.md`:

1. **Symmetry test.** For the target functions, every permutation and identity substitution at
   fixed count leaves the function unchanged, so the discarded information is irrelevant.
2. **Per-slot test.** A count-only predictor keeps per-slot `R` small across functions, sites and
   habitats, with no live slot and no context dependence, on held-out data.
3. **No-surplus test.** Adding trait, identity, abundance and habitat variables yields no
   out-of-sample improvement for any function in the set, so the union has no surplus.
4. **Sufficiency test.** Species count is a sufficient statistic for the function vector:
   conditioning on full composition adds nothing. Wikipedia (*Sufficient statistic*): *"A
   sufficient statistic for a model parameter contains all of the information that the dataset
   provides about that parameter."*

Ground requirement for any coefficient: name the function, the site set, the habitat conditions, the
model, and the error metric. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not
change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Agreement from a shared BEF literature pool is
one witness, not three. Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are
navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The round trip from full
composition to `S` and back has a nonzero gap that is reported qualitatively, not measured here.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached is not the same as unreachable, and here the loss is underivable from the count frame.**

> Fig 13 :134 *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

The stop is not "researchers have not yet found the right way to use counts." It is that a count has
already thrown away the discriminating information, so for identity-dependent functions no rule
inside the count frame can recover it. The framework returns the shape of what a sufficient
predictor would have to look like and refuses to nominate one universally sufficient variable. The
round-trip gap `R_T` (Symbols table :75) is not measured without a ground reading, so the numeric
part of "how reliably" is honestly left open. This document is the simulated derivation it says it
is; it cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give one reliability number for predicting ecosystem functions from species
> counts, and it does not claim counts are useless. It says that the question is a union of
> overlapping conditions, and that a count is a symmetric many-to-one projection: swapping species
> at fixed count leaves the integer unchanged but can change production, nutrient retention or
> decomposition substantially. The residual is per function, per site and per habitat, so a global
> accuracy can be near zero while one function slot still carries a live, unreduced error. The
> information that decides function is the arrangement of identities, traits, abundances and
> relations, read in an enumerated habitat, which a count does not hold. The opened studies find
> trait identity, dominance and complementarity explain more than richness on their data, and that
> context changes the coefficient. What the framework returns is a shape, not a name: a
> slot-indexed, cross-kind, habitat-embedded predictor settled per function against measured data.
> Counts are a cheap filter for triage, never a witness for the function. Four refuters were written
> before this conclusion; an expert who runs one and it fires should send the refutation, not an
> endorsement. No coefficient is claimed here, because this is a simulated derivation, not a ground
> reading.
