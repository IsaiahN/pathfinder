## 2. Three findings in the wider literature that bear on the claim

1. **Traits beat counts in the studies read.** Gagic et al. (2015) analysed eight datasets across five animal groups and seven ecosystem functions. Trait-based indices consistently outperformed richness or abundance. This is a `specified` result on those datasets, not a universal law, but it is direct corroboration of the structural claim that the arrangement carries information the count loses.
2. **Context is not noise.** Carrick and Forsythe (2020) found that sites within the same intact ecosystem shared only about 48% similarity in species composition while being about 69% similar in functioning, and that in recovering ecosystems composition accounted for only 2% of variation in function. Hong et al. (2022) found biodiversity effects were often different under different global-change drivers. A count-only predictor that ignores habitat is not missing a small correction; it is missing a condition.
3. **Richness is still useful.** Hong et al. and the broader BEF literature find a generally positive biodiversity effect, often larger under stress. The honest position is bounded sufficiency, not "counts are useless." This is why the audit retracts the stronger wording and keeps the weaker, checkable one.

None of these sources conflicts with the figure verdict. The three ecology papers draw on overlapping BEF literature, so their mutual agreement is one evidence pool, not three witnesses. The mathematics and colour-science sources are independent fields and carry more independent weight for the structural claim.

## 5. What is deliberately not sent

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: a room does not upgrade the label (Fig 2 :35, Fig 7 :13); correlation disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40); and asking for trust before checking spends the only independence the outside reader contributes (Fig 10 :42). Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, which is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
