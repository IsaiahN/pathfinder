# The Tether's answer on patents and innovation

**Mode: `specified` on the four per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal net effect. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No patent, firm, instrument or measurement was evaluated by this agent.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a patent encourages innovation by rewarding invention or impedes it by restricting competition and follow-on research. In effect terms, a declared exclusion right is introduced into a habitat of searchers whose closures differ, and we are asked what happens to contact with a non-updating ground. The two branches of the question are two quantities with two anchors: an action budget for the holder, and the reach of followers across closures. Fig 9 :4-5 splits the disagreement into several well-formed questions rather than one hard one with two opposite answers.

## 2. The shape, per domain, never averaged

1. **The either/or is refused.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A patent is a benefactor to the inventor's reward residual and a malfactor to the follower's access residual. There is no standing answer "patents are good" or "patents are bad." Verdict: `general` structural, triangulated.

2. **A grant is a budget, not contact.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." The reward branch gives the holder a budget to try. The market or clinical outcome still settles. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 10 and Fig 11.

3. **Exclusion matters at the crossing.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." A patent does not move the wall; it prices or blocks the crossing. The restriction branch fires when follow-on work needs an atom from a differing closure and the license channel does not keep the atom reachable. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 12 :121.

4. **Disclosure is the bond.** Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Open disclosure carries a head start; restrictive disclosure carries a recording of one occasion. Under the same atom set these are different substances. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 12.

5. **Counts are filters.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Patent counts and R&D spend are predicates the system also produces. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 5 and Fig 9.

6. **The net is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." No figure line returns a single global effect, and the two currencies cannot be added. The `gap` is a verdict, not silence. Verdict: `gap`, shape-not-a-name and unreachable-at-this-budget kind.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that patents always help and not that they always hurt. Both are refused at D1.
- Not that a patent count, a citation count or an R&D total is innovation. All are frame-internal filters.
- Not a universal percentage, not an optimal patent term, not a policy recommendation for any country. No instrument here returns those.
- Not a derivation of economics from the six-term substrate. Reward and exclusion are bindings from the economics frame onto held terms, not new primitives and not imports that move a wall.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing was settled by measurement in this run.
- Not a decision about the live strain in `01` F9: legal exclusion in a habitat where contact is a fact. The boundary is where enforcement operates and needs an instrument.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The discriminator would have to read settled new terms per slot, under contrasting disclosure arrangements, against an anchor that does not update on the holder's own report. Concretely:

- Does the follow-on distribution differ when the same claimed atom is disclosed openly versus restrictively, with selection into patenting controlled? Sampat and Williams's quasi-experimental designs are a partial instance; they are not a global answer.
- Does the reward change contact with the ground, or only the action budget? A design that separates funding from adoption would decide D1.
- Does a license channel keep the atom reachable across a differing closure, so the cycle of Fig 12 :121 closes? That is the instrument owed at the F9 boundary.
- Standing refuters from `01` section 4: a figure line licensing a single net effect; identical per-slot effects under a non-updating anchor; a reward shown to change contact; no disclosure effect with access restricted; a partition rather than a union; an instrument returning a stable global net.

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The answer stops where the instruments stop and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The center stays open.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "patents help" or "patents hurt." It says the question is a union of two overlapping conditions with different anchors. A grant puts quasi-rents in the holder's budget, and a budget is not contact with the ground; the market or the clinical outcome still settles whether the invention worked. The claimed atom also has to travel to followers whose closure differs from the holder's, and the exclusion right prices or blocks that crossing. Whether it blocks depends on disclosure: the same atoms under open versus restrictive disclosure are, in the framework's terms, isomers, different substances invisible in a patent count. Counts and R&D spend are budget filters, not witnesses, and the two currencies do not add to a global net. What the framework returns is a per-slot verdict with a disclosure arrangement named, a refuter written in advance, and an honest `gap` where the question wants a number.
