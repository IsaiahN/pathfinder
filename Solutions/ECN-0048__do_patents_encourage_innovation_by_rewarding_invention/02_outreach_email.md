**Subject:** One checkable separator on "do patents help or hurt innovation?"

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with a position on
> patents. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do patents encourage innovation by
> rewarding invention or impede it by restricting follow-on research?" is a union of at least
> two overlapping conditions with different anchors, not one dial with a plus and a minus.
> Written in effect terms:
>
> 1. A grant declares a meaning and puts quasi-rents in the holder's budget. It does not by
>    itself change contact with the ground. The market or the clinical outcome settles. A reward
>    changes the action budget, not the anchor.
> 2. The claimed atom is composed into follow-on terms by people whose closure differs from the
>    holder's. The exclusion right prices or blocks that crossing. Whether it blocks depends on
>    whether the license channel keeps the atom reachable.
> 3. Disclosure is the bond, not a detail. The same atoms under open versus restrictive
>    disclosure are different arrangements, and the figures treat different arrangements as
>    different substances.
>
> So the honest answer is a per-slot verdict under a stated disclosure arrangement. It is not a
> global percentage. No figure line returns one, and I do not have an instrument that does.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that patents always help, that patents always hurt, that a patent
> count measures innovation, or that my derivation settles the empirical literature. Patent
> counts and R&D spend are budget filters. They are quantities the system also produces, so they
> cannot score the system. This is a shape and a separator, not a result.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. a figure line that licenses one net effect over all slots, collapsing the union;
> 2. a ground reading with a non-updating anchor showing the same direction and magnitude in
>    every technology domain, so the patent carries the valence rather than the residual;
> 3. a demonstration that a reward changes contact with the ground and not just the budget;
> 4. a demonstration that restricting access never reduces composition across a differing
>    closure while disclosure is held constant.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the separator. A one-line
> "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage,
> no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the
> authority.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The WATSON answer in this folder: `09_watson_answer.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem
> you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result
> *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its

## Two-stage offer, explicit

**Stage 1 (this email):** the separator, the per-slot shape, and the refuter. Record the reader's verdict before any framework material is shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local links and the WATSON answer. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. By Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." By Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

**Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it. Fig 4 :22 permits methods to cross. It does not permit replaying this conclusion as an answer.

**No stage 4:** there is no ask for endorsement, no list of believers, and no mass send.
