## 3. Wider literature that bears on the question

The four substantive economics papers are one broad evidence pool in the sense that they are all economics-of-innovation work, but they are low overlap in design and in field: 19th-century world-fair exhibits (Moser), gene sequences and clinical trials (Sampat and Williams), development and regional patent systems (Hall), and cross-industry R&D productivity (Bloom et al.). They are counted as distinct witnesses for mechanism shape, with overlap declared in the ledger. The anticommons entry (law and ecology) and the overjustification entry (human cognition) are genuinely low-overlap corroborators. The two essays share the framework author's evidence pool completely with the figures, so their agreement carries no independent weight.

None of these sources settles the question. Moser and Hall report per-slot or per-stage effects; Sampat and Williams report a conditional effect driven by disclosure; Bloom et al. report a declining productivity channel that a larger reward budget does not repair. All four fit the union shape derived from the figures before they were opened, which is corroboration of shape, not a decision on the welfare question.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22 — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending a finished verdict on patents before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the separator and the refuter; offer the links after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42 — "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A reader who has absorbed the full framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 — "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40 — "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** Selecting recipients because they are already on one side of the patent debate manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
- **Fig 11 :49 — "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Do not address the recipient as a patent skeptic or a patent advocate. Address the residual.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which slot they would test first; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 5. The two-stage offer and its figure-grounding

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 — "Bring a characterised residual, never the source" — and Fig 2 :45 — "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict is recorded first and their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 again. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** The formula's Direction section says a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds." Each user writes down what would refute their result before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 separator survives. It currently survives internal checking but carries one live strain (legal exclusion versus habitat contact, `01` F9). Declare that strain in any message you send. Do not present it as settled.
