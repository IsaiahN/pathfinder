## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that patents encourage innovation by rewarding invention, or impede it by restricting competition and follow-on research. Option A: assert the reward branch, patents help. Option B: assert the exclusion branch, patents hurt. Option C: refuse the either/or, split the residual into per-slot domains with different anchors, and report a `gap` where a global net is demanded. What each leads to: A and B each deliver a standing valence that Fig 11 refuses, and each selects evidence from only one of two overlapping conditions. C delivers a checkable separator, refuters written before the run, a bond-level test, and an honest stop. The figures decide C.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the effect of a declared exclusion right on the production of settled new terms. Arity: two conditions with overlap, so a union compound (Fig 9 :4-5), not one dial with opposite poles. Symmetry: reward is a grant to one actor, restriction is an ordered exclusion of others; they are not symmetric operands and do not subtract (Fig 12 :125, :130). Scale: first order inside the holder's closure (a funded search), second order at the union of holder and follower closures (cumulative follow-on work, Fig 12 :104).
- **D1 Reward and budget.** Actors: inventor, patent office, market. Currency: quasi-rents, action budget. Anchor: the market or adoption outcome that pays (Fig 2 :29). Question type: does funding change contact or only the action budget.
- **D2 Exclusion and follow-on.** Actors: holder, followers in differing closures, licensees. Currency: access to a claimed atom, licensing terms. Anchor: subsequent settled terms that would not exist but for access. Question type: union, because the two closures overlap at the claimed atom.
- **D3 Measurement and verdict.** Actors: economists, patent and citation counters. Currency: patent counts, R&D spend, effect sizes. Anchor: ground-level outcomes, never agreement between frames using the same proxies. Question type: union of overlapping proxy conditions.
- **D4 Disclosure and the bond.** Actors: holder, readers of the published application, followers. Currency: a published method as a head start, or a withheld one. Anchor: whether the follower can compose from the disclosure without a license. Question type: isomer over a fixed atom set (Fig 12 :117).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Split the union before searching (Fig 9 :54); state the bond before comparing arrangements (Fig 12 :5); settle a holder's first-order term before it becomes an atom for follow-on work (Fig 12 :104); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: Fig 10 :47 (a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one) was first applied directly to a patent right and strained. Re-census moved that claim to D1, where Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :49 govern without strain. One live strain remains: Fig 11 :61's habitat rule (exclusion is not available to a seat) versus legal exclusion in a human habitat. It is declared in `01` F9 and not used as a verdict.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. Their agreement with the figure shape is reported, not used to decide the verdict. Fields were chosen for low overlap with the framework.

- **Economic history / industrial organization.** Petra Moser, "How Do Patent Laws Influence Innovation? Evidence from Nineteenth-Century World Fairs", NBER Working Paper 9909, 2003 (published AER 2005). Quote: "I find no evidence that patent laws increased levels of innovative activity but strong evidence that patent systems influenced the distribution of innovative activity across industries." Predicts a direction change rather than a level change, so per-slot valence. Corroborates D1 and the union verdict. Overlap: low field, distinct design (world-fair exhibits include unpatented innovations).
- **Economics of science / genomics.** Bhaven Sampat and Heidi Williams, "How Do Patents Affect Follow-On Innovation? Evidence from the Human Genome", American Economic Review 109(1), 2019, author manuscript opened in full. Quote: "both of our quasi-experimental approaches suggest that on average gene patents have had no quantitatively important effect on follow-on innovation." Also documents selection in the line "patented genes appear more valuable" (the source continues with paired dashes around "prior to being patented", omitted here to keep this file dash free). Predicts that reward and exclusion do not add to a compulsory effect, and that disclosure arrangement governs the contrast. Corroborates D2, D3 and D4. Overlap: low field and low design overlap; the only caveat is that the restrictive-disclosure contrast (Celera) is discussed inside the same paper.
- **Law and economics / ecology.** Michael Heller and Rebecca Eisenberg, "Can Patents Deter Innovation? The Anticommons in Biomedical Research", Science 280, 1998, read through the public encyclopedia entry because the Science page returned 403. Quote from the entry: "The 'tragedy of the anticommons' occurs when a resource has many owners, all of whom have the ability to exclude others from using it, leading to the under-utilization of that resource." Predicts the block case in D2. Corroborates D2's exclusion branch, with the entry's own note that surveys of scientists dispute an anticommons effect. Overlap: low field; tertiary source, reduced weight, declared.
- **Human cognition.** Wikipedia, "Overjustification effect", opened 2026-11-23. Quote: "providing expected external incentive, such as money or prizes, for an already intrinsically rewarding activity can reduce a person's intrinsic motivation to perform that activity." Predicts that a reward can change what the activity is for, and that the effect is not uniform. Corroborates D1's claim that the reward-to-output arrow is not mechanical. Overlap: genuinely low field; tertiary source, used for mechanism shape only, and the entry records contested ecological validity.
- **Development economics.** Bronwyn H. Hall, "Patents, Innovation, and Development", NBER Working Paper 27203, 2020. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The either/or is refused. The residual is a union of overlapping conditions: a grant gives the holder an action budget but does not change contact with a ground that does not update on the holder's own report; the claimed atom must cross into a differing closure for follow-on work, and the exclusion right prices or blocks that crossing; the disclosure bond decides whether the crossing carries a head start or only a recording of one occasion. Patent counts and R&D spend are budget filters, not witnesses. The two currencies do not add, so no global net is returned; that is a declared `gap`, not silence. Verdicts are kept separate per domain: D1 `specified` triangulated, D2 `specified` triangulated, D3 `specified` plus `gap` triangulated, D4 `specified` triangulated. The whole result is `specified` on the structure, `general` on the valence rule, `gap` on the net, ungrounded, candidate. One live strain is the legal-exclusion versus habitat-contact boundary, which needs an instrument.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a verdict on patents instead of a separator. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second cost is policy: if the separator is wrong, a reader might use it to dismiss genuine per-slot evidence, so the four refuters travel with it.
- **undoable:** yes. The separator can be withdrawn without discarding the world sources or the figure census. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already carry the refuters and the live strain. No primitive has to be given up; only the union claim and the bond claim fall if their refuters fire.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that reads settled new terms per slot under contrasting disclosure arrangements, with selection controlled and an anchor that does not update, could return a stable net and collapse the `gap`. A figure line licensing a single net effect would collapse the union. A demonstration that a reward changes contact rather than only the budget would break D1. Evidence that restricting access never reduces composition across a differing closure with disclosure held constant would break D2 and D4. A partition result would break F1. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
