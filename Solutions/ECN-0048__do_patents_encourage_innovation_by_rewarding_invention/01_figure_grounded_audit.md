## 0. The residual, stated before any conclusion (Direction)

The question arrives as a either/or: reward versus restriction. That framing already smuggles in a single quantity, "innovation", on which two opposite effects are supposed to be added. The first discipline is to bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

**Sought-for shape, written down before the run:** an answer to this question would be a *per-slot verdict* of the following form. For technology slot *s*, under disclosure arrangement *d*, with an anchor *A* that does not update on the patent holder's own report, the exclusion right either (i) leaves the channel by which followers in a differing closure reach the atom and return settled terms to the ground reachable, or (ii) severs that channel. The verdict is indexed per slot; it is not a global percentage. It states the arrangement and the anchor. It carries a refuter.

That shape is a constraint, not a filler. It does not say whether patents help or hurt. It says what any answer must look like before it can count. The formula's Direction rule is explicit: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape it seeks is not a term it holds. Binding the shape makes it refusable.

**Effect statement of R:** a declared exclusion right is introduced into a habitat of searchers with differing closures. What changes is (a) the action budget of the holder and (b) the reach of followers across closures. These are two quantities with two anchors. The word "innovation" in the question is a label for their unverified sum.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two conditions, reward and exclusion, with overlap. The same patent both grants a reward to the holder and restricts followers. This is a union compound, not a partition and not one hard question with two opposite answers.
- **Symmetry:** "reward" is a grant directed at one actor; "restriction" is an ordered exclusion of other actors. The two are not symmetric operands that can be subtracted to give a net. Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- **Scale:** first order inside one inventor's closure (a single search funded or not funded); second order at the union of the holder's closure and the followers' closures (cumulative follow-on work). Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". The level decides what counts as an atom.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet. All four below have one.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable, non-updating) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Reward and budget** | inventor, patent office, market, investors | money, quasi-rents, action budget | the market or adoption outcome that pays or does not (Fig 2 :29 "a market, a patient outcome") | partition boundary: does funding change contact or only the action budget? |
| **D2 Exclusion and follow-on** | holder, followers in other closures, licensees | access to a claimed atom, licensing terms, composition across closures | subsequent settled terms, that is publications, trials, products that would not exist but for access | union: holder closure and follower closure overlap at the claimed atom |
| **D3 Measurement and verdict** | economists, patent counters, citation counters | patent counts, R&D spend, citations, effect sizes | ground-level outcomes, never agreement between frames using the same proxies | union of overlapping proxy conditions; one lever leaves intersections standing |
| **D4 Disclosure and the bond** | holder, readers of the published application, followers | a published method (a head start), or a withheld one | the follower's ability to compose from the disclosure without a license | isomer question over a fixed atom set: same atoms, different bond |

### 1.3 Partition or union

Union, four ways:

1. Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The reward story works well in settings where imitation is cheap and the invention would not be made without appropriation; the exclusion story works well where follow-on work requires the claimed atom. Different subsets.
2. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The same patent occupies both conditions, so a single lever (abolish patents, or strengthen patents) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
3. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A patent that both funds a search and blocks a follower is not "more of the same" patent. It is a specific compound with its own loop.
4. Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." D1 and D2 are two frames whose agreement about "innovation" is worthless if both are read off the same patent count (Fig 2 :40).

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

1. **Perception and vocabulary first:** name the per-slot residual (D3). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." If the instrument reads only patent counts, the link between "reward" and "innovation" has no instrument and has never been looked at.
2. **Objective second:** state what would count as progress in each domain (an adoption outcome for D1, a subsequent settled term for D2, a discriminating instrument for D3, a license-free composition for D4).
3. **Disclosure arrangement third (D4):** whether the patent publishes a usable method determines whether D2's crossing is reachable at all.
4. **Settlement of first-order terms fourth:** a holder's invention must be settled against the ground before it can serve as an atom for follow-on work (Fig 12 :104). Only then does the second-order comparison (reward versus exclusion) have operands.

Strain test applied before quoting: Fig 12 :125 (two currencies) was initially tried on "reward versus competition" as if money and market power were the two currencies. That was a strain. Re-census assigned Fig 12 :125 to D1 versus D2 (action budget versus reach of composition), where the units genuinely differ. No strain after re-census. The one live strain is recorded in section 7.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 — The question is a union compound, not a two-option choice (governing)

Fig 9 :4-5, quoted in section 1.3. Fig 9 :54-57. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A patent is not a good actor or a bad actor. It is a benefactor to the inventor's reward residual and a malfactor to the follower's access residual. Moser's world result (opened this session) is the same shape: *"I find no evidence that patent laws increased levels of innovative activity but strong evidence that patent systems influenced the distribution of innovative activity across industries."* The question "help or hurt" asks for a standing valence where the figures require a per-slot relation. Verdict: **refuse the either/or as written; split into D1 to D4.** Triangulated across Fig 9 (union), Fig 11 (valence), and the formula's Direction rule (bind the shape). Low overlap between the union rule and the valence rule, so the triangulation is not one claim restated.

**Refuter:** a figure line that licenses a single net effect over all slots, or a ground reading showing identical direction and magnitude across all technology domains with a non-updating anchor. Neither is in hand.

### F2 — A reward is a budget, not contact with the ground (D1)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

A patent grant declares a meaning: this inventor may exclude others. It puts quasi-rents in the holder's budget. By Fig 11 :40, an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing however much it improves. Nothing in the figures says funding a search is the same as the search paying against a ground. The market or the clinical outcome settles; the grant does not. Bloom, Jones, Van Reenen and Webb (opened this session) report *"research effort is rising substantially while research productivity is declining sharply"*, which is exactly the shape of more budget with no corresponding change in contact.

Verdict D1: **`specified`, triangulated** across Fig 2 (anchor), Fig 10 (convention), Fig 11 (contact), with low overlap between the three. The claim "patents encourage innovation by rewarding invention" is a claim about budget. It is not a verdict about settlement until an anchor pays.

**Refuter:** show a reward changing contact with a non-updating ground rather than only the action budget, or produce a figure line that makes funding sufficient for settlement. The overjustification literature (opened this session) points the other way: *"providing expected external incentive, such as money or prizes, for an already intrinsically rewarding activity can reduce a person's intrinsic motivation to perform that activity."* That is not a patent result, but it is a low-overlap cognition corroborator that the reward-to-output arrow is not mechanical.

### F3 — Exclusion prices or blocks the crossing between closures (D2)

Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall" (import). Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Follow-on innovation is the cycle of Fig 12 :121: the output of one settled term re-enters as input to the next. That requires the atom to travel to a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23) and the surplus to be realised where the closures differ (Fig 8 :45). A patent does not move the wall (Fig 6 :21, only import moves the wall); it puts a price or a prohibition on the crossing. The question is whether the channel stays reachable. Heller and Eisenberg's anticommons (read via the public Wikipedia article, the Science original returned 403 to automated fetch and is recorded as such in the ledger) states the block case: *"The 'tragedy of the anticommons' occurs when a resource has many owners, all of whom have the ability to exclude others from using it, leading to the under-utilization of that resource."*

Verdict D2: **`specified`, triangulated** across Fig 6 (reachability), Fig 8 (surplus at differing closures), Fig 12 (cycle), Fig 11 :61 (introduction available, exclusion not). The "restricts follow-on research" branch is real but conditional: it fires when the follow-on term must be composed in a different closure and the license channel does not keep the atom reachable.

**Refuter:** show that restricting access never reduces composition across a differing closure while disclosure is held constant, or that the follow-on term can be composed entirely inside the holder's closure. Sampat and Williams (full text opened) is the strongest existing test and it does **not** fire that refuter in the open-disclosure case; it does report a large decline for a restrictive-disclosure comparison (Celera, on the order of 30 percent). That asymmetry is carried into D4, not averaged away.

### F4 — The bond is the variable, not the atom (D4)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

A published patent application can carry a method, which is a head start that makes the next search cheaper (Fig 4 :22). A withheld or restrictively disclosed result carries a recording of one occasion, which cannot generate a new search (Fig 4 :25). The atom set is the same (gene sequence, claim, inventor); the bond differs (open disclosure versus restricted). Under Fig 12 :117 those are isomers or, if the atom sets differ, members of a functional group. This is why Sampat and Williams's result is structurally legible: holding disclosure constant, gene patents had *"no quantitatively important effect on follow-on innovation"*, while the more restrictively disclosed Celera data was associated with large declines. The figures predicted the shape of that contrast before the world source was opened; the world source then corroborated it.

Verdict D4: **`specified`, triangulated** across Fig 4 (membrane direction) and Fig 12 (bond carries information), two figures with low conceptual overlap. The falsifier is in `06_isomers_and_order.md`: if two arrangements with identical atoms and identical disclosure behave the same, the isomer claim fails.

### F5 — Patent counts and R&D spend are frame-internal (D3)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 1 from the figure text: "predicates minted, and credibility accrued. They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 5 :48: "Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A patent count is a predicate minted. R&D spend is a budget figure. Both are quantities the patent system and the firms within it also produce, so neither can score the system. The line "patents increased innovation by X percent" computed from counts is a filter handing us a verdict. Moser's 19th-century world-fair data mattered precisely because it included innovations that were *not* patented, which is what a census of patents cannot contain.

Verdict D3: **`specified` on the proxy charge, `general` on the structural claim, triangulated** across Fig 1 (per slot, frame-internal), Fig 5 (output is not success), Fig 9 :60 (filters versus witnesses). World corroboration: Sampat and Williams document *"novel evidence of selection into patenting: patented genes appear more valuable—prior to being patented—than non-patented genes"*, which is the selection problem a naive count cannot see.

**Refuter:** an instrument that reads settled terms directly, per slot, with a non-updating anchor, and that is not produced by the system it grades.

### F6 — No figure returns the global net effect; that is a `gap`, not silence (D3)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 13 from the figure text: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The question as posed wants one number for all technologies, all regimes and all disclosure arrangements. No figure line returns it. Fig 9 :32 says the framework returns a kind of thing. Fig 13 :115 says the arrangements over a fixed set do not run out, so a global net across all of them is not a single measurement. Hall's development survey (opened this session) says *"The conclusion suggests that patents may be relatively unimportant in development, even for middle income countries"*, which is a per-slot result, not a universal one.

Verdict: **`gap`** on the universal net effect, of the shape-not-a-name and unreachable-at-this-budget kind, reported as a verdict. This is not an invitation to invent a number.

**Refuter:** a figure line that names the net, or a world measurement with a non-updating anchor and a complete per-slot census. Until one fires, the honest form is a per-slot separator plus the refuter.

### F7 — The rank of "reward" and "restriction" is a sequence, not an equivalence

Operators table: `→` is sequence, "A then B; B needs A to have happened". The question treats reward and restriction as opposite ends of one scale. They are ordered differently: the grant precedes the holder's search; the exclusion acts on later followers. Fig 12 from the figure text: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Treating them as a symmetric comparison would require a bond the figures do not provide for two quantities in different currencies (F1). This is a notation finding, not a new empirical claim. It is kept visible rather than smoothed over.

### F8 — Two currencies, one error (C1 to D2 bridge)

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." The reward side is priced in money and market power; the follow-on side is priced in access and settlement actions. A "net innovation effect" converts one into the other and sums. Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." Verdict: keep two ledgers. Triangulated with Fig 1 :19 (per slot) and Fig 9 :55 (union).

### F9 — The live strain: exclusion in the habitat

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Read literally, this seems to say a patent cannot exclude. **That reading is strained and is not used.** Fig 11 :61 governs a seat acting on a habitat: contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the seat, so a seat cannot remove a habitat. A patent is a legal convention inside the human habitat, not a seat's power over the habitat itself. A patent holder *can* exclude legally; what the figure forbids is supposing that legal exclusion removes contact. Ideas still leak, which is why the disclosure arrangement (D4) matters. Recorded as a live tension, not resolved away: the legal convention and the habitat rule describe different objects, but the boundary between them is exactly where patent enforcement operates and needs an instrument. See section 7 and `11_audit.md`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in section 0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line is found that licenses one net effect of a convention on settled new terms across all slots, so the union collapses into a partition.
2. Independent ground readings show the same direction and magnitude of the patent effect in every technology domain, measured against an anchor that does not update on the holder's own report, so valence is carried by the patent rather than relative to the residual.
3. It is shown that a reward changes contact with the ground and not merely the action budget (breaks D1).
4. It is shown that restricting access never reduces composition across a differing closure while disclosure is held constant (breaks D2 and D4).
5. It is shown that the two conditions are a partition, so one lever clears every region and the intersections are empty (breaks F1).
6. An instrument reads settled new terms per slot without being produced by the patent system, and returns a stable global net (breaks F6's `gap`).

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn and the per-slot verdicts are rebuilt. Until one is run, nothing is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Refuse the either/or at the first sentence. State the four domains and their anchors before any literature is cited (F1).
2. Say the mode on each claim. "Patents fund a search" is `specified`, not a verdict on innovation (F2).
3. Drop the global percentage. Report per-slot verdicts with the disclosure arrangement named (F4, F6).
4. Treat patent counts and R&D spend as budget filters. Use adoption, follow-on settled terms and source-discriminating instruments as witnesses (F5).
5. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the separator and the refuter, not a conclusion that a patent regime is good or bad.
