# Expert verification packet — the Tether on patents and innovation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist, IP scholar or innovation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural separator, `gap` on a global net. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do patents encourage innovation by rewarding invention or impede it by restricting follow-on research?" is a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, not one dial. A grant declares a meaning and funds an action budget; it does not by itself change contact with a ground that does not update on the holder's own report. The claimed atom must also reach followers in differing closures, and the exclusion right prices or blocks that crossing. Disclosure is the bond: the same atoms under open versus restrictive disclosure behave as different substances. Patent counts and R&D spend are budget filters, not witnesses, and the two currencies do not add. The honest output is a per-slot verdict under a stated disclosure arrangement, with refuters written in advance and a declared `gap` where the question wants a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — the union separator (framework-internal and economics-facing)

- **Statement:** the two branches are two conditions with different anchors, and their overlap makes a single net effect a category error rather than an unmeasured number.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 — "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Also Fig 9 :55 — "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 11 :49 — "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** is the reward mechanism and the follow-on restriction mechanism genuinely active in different subsets, and does the same patent occupy both? Moser's world-fair finding of a direction change with no level change is the empirical instance offered; check whether that finding is read correctly.
- **Refuter:** a figure line licensing one net effect over all slots; a ground reading showing identical direction and magnitude in every domain with a non-updating anchor; or a demonstration that the conditions form a partition with empty intersections.

### C2 — reward is a budget, not contact

- **Statement:** a patent grant changes the holder's action budget. It does not settle anything at the ground, which remains the market or clinical outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 11 :40 — "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 10 :49 — "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- **What to check:** can the reader name a case where a patent reward changed contact rather than only funding the search? If so the claim is wrong. Bloom, Jones, Van Reenen and Webb's finding that "research effort is rising substantially while research productivity is declining sharply" is offered as corroboration of the shape, not as a decision.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that a reward changes contact with a non-updating ground and not merely the action budget.

### C3 — disclosure is the bond (the isomer sub-claim)

- **Statement:** over a fixed set of atoms (claimed sequence, grant, holder, follower), open and restrictive disclosure are different bonds and therefore different substances. This is the one sub-claim with a direct world test.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 — "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117 — "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 4 :22 — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **What to check:** Sampat and Williams (American Economic Review 2019, full text opened) find that with disclosure effectively open, gene patents had "no quantitatively important effect on follow-on innovation", while the more restrictively disclosed Celera data was associated with declines on the order of 30 percent. Is the disclosure distinction the right reading of that contrast, or is it a different mechanism (market structure, field differences, selection)?
- **Refuter:** identical follow-on distributions under identical atoms and disclosure, once selection is controlled; or evidence that the Celera contrast is driven by an ingredient the gene-patent sample does not share.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that patents always help or always hurt. Both are refused.
- Not a global net effect, an optimal patent term, or a policy recommendation.
- Not that patent counts, citations or R&D spend are innovation. Those are filters.
- Not a derivation of economics from the six-term substrate; reward and exclusion are bindings, not new primitives.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this agent.
- Not a resolution of the live strain in `01` F9: legal exclusion versus habitat contact. It is declared, not hidden.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument:

1. A per-slot reading of follow-on settled terms under contrasting disclosure arrangements, with selection into patenting controlled and an anchor that does not update on the holder's own report.
2. A design separating the funding effect from the adoption effect, to decide whether a reward changes contact or only the budget.
3. A test of whether a license channel restores the cycle (Fig 12 :121) when the claimed atom must cross into a differing closure. This is the instrument owed at the F9 boundary.
4. The six standing refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, any one of which withdraws the separator.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 9 :55, Fig 11 :49, Fig 2 :29, Fig 12 :5 and Fig 12 :117. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a case you know: did the reward change contact, or only the budget?
3. Check C3 against Sampat and Williams's disclosure contrast. Is disclosure the mechanism, or is something else doing the work?
4. Name one instrument that would separate the two currencies per slot, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
