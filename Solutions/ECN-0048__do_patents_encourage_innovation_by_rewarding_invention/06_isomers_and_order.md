# Union, currencies and isomers: the supporting derivation for ECN-0048

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the bond distinction, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether one lever (the patent) has one effect (on innovation). Three questions are owed: (1) is the split a partition or a union; (2) can the two effects be added into a net; (3) are the two arrangements (open versus restrictive disclosure) isomers over a fixed atom set, or merely different ingredients?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the economics frame: a claimed atom, a grant (declared exclusion), a reward budget, a follower's closure, a disclosed method, a follow-on settled term, a ground that does not update. Bonds held: `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, `−` subtraction, `¬` refusal. No new primitive is available without import, and the import would need a source gate and shadow test.

Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."): the union split must be made before any effect is searched; the two currencies must be separated before any net is attempted; the bond must be identified before the atom set is compared.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- A patent that funds a search (condition R) and blocks a follower (condition E) sits in the intersection. A single lever "abolish patents" clears R-only and E-only but leaves the intersection, because both conditions still bind in different domains.
- **World check:** Moser's world-fair result is a direction change rather than a level change, and Sampat and Williams's is a conditional effect. Both fit the union. Neither was used to choose the split; the split was chosen from Fig 9 first.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a ground reading shows the two conditions never co-occur, the split is a partition and one lever could clear everything. The union claim would fail.

Verdict: **union**, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 11 :49/:51 (valence relative to the residual), with low overlap between the two figures.

### Q2 — Can reward and restriction be added?

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- The reward currency is money and market power; it prices the description of an invention and funds the action. The exclusion currency is access and reach across closures; it prices whether a follow-on action can be taken at all. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall.
- Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global net aggregates both currencies and all slots.
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that converts one currency into the other with a declared rate and a non-updating anchor. None is held. Until one is, the net is a `gap`.

Verdict: **two ledgers, no net**, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 1. `specified` that the currencies differ; `gap` on the numerical net.

### Q3 — Are open and restrictive disclosure isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- The two arrangements hold the same atoms: claimed sequence, patent grant, holder, follower. They differ in the bond from the disclosure to the follower. Open disclosure carries a method, which is a head start that makes the next search cheaper. Restrictive disclosure carries a recording of one occasion, which cannot generate a new search.
- Under Fig 12 :117 that is an isomer claim: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in a patent count and decisive in follow-on behaviour.
- **World check:** Sampat and Williams, full text opened, report that with disclosure held effectively open *"on average gene patents have had no quantitatively important effect on follow-on innovation"*, while the more restrictively disclosed Celera data was associated with declines on the order of 30 percent. That is the isomer shape: same domain, different bond, different outcome. The world source corroborates; it does not decide.
- **Falsifier, written before the comparison:** if two arrangements with identical atoms and identical disclosure return the same follow-on distribution, the isomer claim fails and the difference must be looked for in the ingredients instead.

Verdict: **`specified` isomer claim**, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 4, two figures with low conceptual overlap. Single-source for the figure definition of isomer if Fig 4 is set aside; with Fig 4 it is triangulated.

### Q4 — What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"
- A holder's invention must first settle against the ground (be adopted, be shown to work) before it can serve as an atom for a follower's composition. Only then does the comparison between reward and exclusion have operands at the second order.
- Operators table: `→` is "A then B; B needs A to have happened". The grant comes before the holder's search; the exclusion acts on later followers. The relation is a sequence, not a symmetric comparison. Calling the two "opposite effects" is a notation error, recorded in `01` F7.
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Follow-on innovation is a cycle. The exclusion right changes the sign of the loop: with a reachable license channel, the cycle closes; without one, it becomes a chain that cannot close. That is a structural claim about the bond, not a claim about the welfare of patent systems.
- **Strain named, not hidden:** Fig 12 :121 says a chain cannot close by construction, but a license can re-enter the output as input and restore the cycle. Whether a particular license channel does so is an instrument question, not a figure question. It is the same live boundary as `01` F9.

Verdict: **`general` on the sequence and cycle structure, `specified` on the license-channel condition, with one live strain.**

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of overlapping conditions with two currencies, not a two-option choice. Open and restrictive disclosure are isomers over a common atom set: same claimed sequence and grant, different bond to the follower, different follow-on substance. The reward claim is a sequence with an action budget, not a settlement at the ground. The net effect across slots is a `gap` because the currencies do not add and no figure returns the aggregate.

This framing keeps what is checkable, the per-slot separator and the isomer test, without inventing a global number or a universal verdict on patents. The patent count remains a frame-internal reading whose source is undecided. The falsifiers above are written down so the whole structure can be refused.
