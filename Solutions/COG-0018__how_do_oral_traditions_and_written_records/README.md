## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not which medium is "better" for memory. In effect terms: a rememberer's library Γ predicts what happened and in what order, the world returns o' (what actually happened and when), and R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is measured per remembered slot, not aggregated across a culture (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question asks what changes in that loop when the carrier is a spoken tradition versus a written record.

- Option A, "the medium changes memory content directly": writing gives more or better memory, orality gives less or different memory. This predicts a main effect of medium on the content of what is recalled.
- Option B, "the medium changes what crosses the membrane": the carrier changes which terms are available for binding, what crosses between people and generations (a re-applicable method or a one-occasion recording), how stable the channel is, and whether a non-updating ground stays reachable. The reconstruction loop itself is the same. This predicts an effect on the *form*, *provenance*, and *ground-contact* of memory, and only an indirect effect on content.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* that names, per remembered slot, the carrier, the crossing (method versus recording), the anchor, and the round-trip gap the carrier makes measurable. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. No figure line returns a numerical "writing improves memory by X" or names one medium the winner; that is a declared `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the cognitive-science question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of memory.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict. This matters twice over here: an oral tradition is itself a chain of agreeing rooms (Fig 7 :12-13), so using agreement between traditions as corroboration repeats the very collapse under study.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Two tellers of the same tradition share a pool; they are one witness wearing two names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
