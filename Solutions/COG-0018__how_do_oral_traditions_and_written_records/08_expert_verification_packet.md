# Expert verification packet - the Tether on oral tradition and written memory

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of oral tradition or memory can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the empirical winner. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The carrier of a memory is a membrane, not a content dispenser. What it changes, per remembered slot, is the bond that crosses (a re-applicable method or a one-occasion recording), the stability of the channel, whether a non-updating ground stays reachable, and whether a round trip can be taken and its gap measured. Oral traditions tend to carry methods but to lose ground contact as agreement compounds along a chain; written records tend to carry recordings, to stabilize provenance, and to make the gap measurable while adding the failure mode of closure. These tendencies are kept separate by domain and are never averaged into a rank order of media. Which medium yields more accurate memory in a given society is left open as a `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and literature-facing; checkable by reading Figure 4 and the orality literature

- **Statement:** the governing variable is the crossing (method or recording), not the physical carrier. A written manual is a method; a verbatim recited poem is a recording.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22-23 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* Fig 4 :25 - *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
- **What to check:** does the method/recording distinction survive the counterexamples (written manual, verbatim recitation)? In the literature, Lord's finding reported by Ong: learning to read "disables the oral poet" by substituting "the concept of a text as controlling the narrative" for "the remembrance of songs sung" (Ong, *Orality and Literacy*, pp. 58-59). Does that support the distinction or dissolve it?
- **Refuter:** with the physical carrier held constant, varying method versus recording makes no difference to what is reconstructed after one transmission; or the carrier alone makes a difference when crossing is held constant. Either breaks C1.

### C2 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 7 and 2

- **Statement:** repetition inside a chain of tellers compounds agreement, not evidence; progress requires a differing room selected against a ground that does not update.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :12-13 - *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **What to check:** does this hold for the tradition you know? Bartlett's serial reproduction of *The War of the Ghosts* (1932) shortened and conventionalized the story across retellings; Legare's 2019 review reports that cultural traditions form "causal chains" in which "each link of such a chain is an interpretation of its predecessor." Are there traditions where agreement tracks an independent ground record instead?
- **Refuter:** an oral chain whose agreement tracks an independent ground better than a single teller's account, with the shared evidence pool controlled. That breaks C2 (Fig 7 :13).

### C3 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 4, 10 and 12

- **Statement:** a fixed written record makes a round trip and a measurable gap possible and carries provenance without truth; organizing the past is a bond question, so chronological and causal accounts of the same episodes are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 10 :65 - *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that"*; Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** can a written recension actually be ranged against an oral performance as two arrangements of the same atom set (the isomer claim), or do they always differ in atoms, leaving only a shared functional group (Fig 12 :124)? Can R_T be measured at all in the oral case?
- **Refuter:** the round-trip gap is equal for oral and written cases at the same precision (breaks the measurement claim); or a written record's truth changes because of its provenance (breaks Fig 10 :65); or a memory practice uses a relation failing all seven bond tests (Fig 12 :90, a new claim rather than a missing operator).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that writing improves memory, or that orality is a deficient form of it.
- Not that the carrier determines cognitive content; the reconstruction loop is shared (Fig 1 :23).
- Not that a fixed record is true, or that agreement between traditions is corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the framework names a winner between media; that is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not `grounded`. No experiment, dataset or measurement was used in this derivation.
- Not an import that adds a primitive. Naming a binding onto the held operator set is provenance (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Method/recording test:** hold the carrier constant, vary the crossing, measure per-slot R after one transmission.
2. **Ground-tracking chain:** follow an oral chain against an independent record of what happened.
3. **Round-trip measurement:** measure R_T for oral retellings and written recensions.
4. **Bond test:** look for an organizing relation that fails all seven bond tests.
5. **Provenance test:** check whether a record's truth changes with its dating or attribution.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :22-25, Fig 7 :12-13 and :35, Fig 10 :65, Fig 12 :117 - four short passages.
2. Check C1 against one tradition you know: is the memorable unit a method or a recording? Does the counterexamples list change the verdict?
3. Check C2: find one chain where agreement tracked the ground, or say why none is available.
4. Name one instrument that would measure R_T for an oral retelling, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
