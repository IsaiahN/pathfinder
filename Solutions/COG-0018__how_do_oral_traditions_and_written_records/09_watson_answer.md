## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about how oral tradition and written record influence what people remember and how they organize the past. Option A: the medium changes memory content directly, so one carrier is better. Option B: the carrier changes what crosses the membrane (method or one-occasion recording), the stability of the channel, whether a non-updating ground stays reachable, and whether a round trip makes its gap measurable; the reconstruction loop is shared. What each leads to: A predicts a main effect of medium on recalled content and invites a rank order of media; B predicts effects on form, provenance and ground contact, with content effects only where the crossing reaches a slot, and returns a per-slot shape plus refuters rather than a winner.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two carrier conditions acting on one outcome (memory content plus temporal organization). Symmetry: the carriers are not interchangeable; the transform has directed loss (Fig 4 :27) and literacy alters the oral method asymmetrically. Scale: one rememberer, one telling, a line of tellers, an institution. Fig 9 names it a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57).
- **Domain 1, individual reconstruction.** Actors: one rememberer, their library, the remembered slot, the world. Currency: the per-slot residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|. Anchor: what actually happened, which does not move because a culture changes its mind (Fig 2 :29). Question type: the shared base loop.
- **Domain 2, the carrier as membrane.** Actors: teller or scribe, audience or reader, the message. Currency: what crosses, a re-applicable method or a one-occasion recording (Fig 4 :22-25). Anchor: the ground plus the stability of the physical carrier. Question type: which bond crosses, not which substrate carries.
- **Domain 3, the transmission chain.** Actors: successive tellers or copyists, each a room. Currency: variation, agreement and selection. Anchor: the ground, consulted separately by each room or the chain becomes a mirror (Fig 7 :35-36). Question type: which episodes survive, and in what order.
- **Domain 4, the record and provenance seat.** Actors: records, rulings about what happened, the seat that maintains them. Currency: provenance carried across the seam (Fig 10 :62). Anchor: the ground; provenance shows legitimacy and never truth (Fig 10 :65). Question type: what the fixed record makes checkable.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54-57). The four domains overlap on the same remembered episode; one lever leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** D1 perception and vocabulary before D2 crossing; D2 before D3 chain; D4 rides on D3 but keeps the ground reachable. A link with no instrument is not a working link (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test: the naive mapping "oral = method, written = recording" fits Fig 4 only with a strain, because a written manual is a method and a verbatim recitation is a recording. Re-census assigns the governing quantity to the crossing, not the medium (F7 of `01`). No strain remains after re-census.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only. Overlap is declared rather than hidden.

- **Orality and literacy / humanities.** Walter J. Ong, *Orality and Literacy: The Technologizing of the Word* (Routledge, 1982/2002; archive.org full text, opened 2026-11-22): "writing destroys memory. Those who use writing will become forgetful, relying on an external resource"; "Print encourages a sense of closure, a sense that what is found in a text has been finalized, has reached a state of completion." Predicts externalization plus closure; corroborates the membrane and provenance verdicts. Overlap: distinct field, one witness.
- **Oral formulaic composition / humanities.** Albert Lord's finding as reported by Ong (psychodynamics excerpt, opened 2026-11-22): learning to read "disables the oral poet" by introducing "the concept of a text as controlling the narrative" and interfering with "the remembrance of songs sung." Predicts that orality carries a method that literacy can break; corroborates F1 and F7. Overlap: same conduit as Ong, so counted with Ong as one pool.
- **Cognitive psychology of memory.** Frederic C. Bartlett, *Remembering* (1932), via search-indexed abstracts (opened via results, direct Pubmed cookie gate recorded): "effort after meaning"; in serial reproduction "participants' written narratives about the legend were considerably shorter than the original and became more abbreviated with repeated reproductions." Predicts reconstructive convergence; corroborates the chain verdict. Reduced weight because direct fetch was blocked.
- **Sociology of memory.** Maurice Halbwachs, *On Collective Memory* (University of Chicago Press, 1992; publisher page and Diggit wiki opened): "human memory can only function within a collective context"; "the individual remembers by placing himself in the perspective of the group." Predicts socially framed recall; corroborates D3 and D4.
- **Memory studies.** Jan and Aleida Assmann via Diggit Magazine wiki (opened 2026-11-22): cultural memory is a "system of values, artifacts, institutions, and practices that retain the past for the present and the future"; communicative memory is oral and tied to living memory (80 to 100 years), while cultural memory is "institutionalised, performed, and mediated." Predicts a change of retention regime with the carrier; corroborates the round-trip and closure verdicts. Overlap with Halbwachs: one memory-studies pool.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The carrier is a membrane and a channel, not a content dispenser. Per remembered slot it changes the crossing (method or one-occasion recording), the stability of the channel, whether a non-updating ground stays reachable, and whether a round trip makes a gap measurable. Oral traditions tend to carry re-applicable methods but to lose ground contact as agreement compounds along a chain; written records tend to carry recordings, to stabilize provenance, and to permit a measured gap while adding closure as a failure mode. Organizing the past is a bond question: chronology is sequence, causation is production, and a refused date is a negation; over a fixed atom set the arrangements are isomers. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The empirical winner between media is a `gap`, unreached at this budget. The whole result is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording (a fixed summary) instead of a generator (the shape plus its refuter), which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The naive medium mapping and the "writing is better" filter are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a superseding packet can replace a sent one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled method/recording test, an oral chain whose agreement tracks an independent record, an equal R_T for oral and written cases, a record whose truth changes with provenance, or a memory relation failing all seven bond tests would each break the corresponding verdict. A figure re-census that reinstates the physical medium as the governing quantity at fixed crossing would reverse the F7 re-census. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
