## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question is usually heard as "does writing make people remember more, or differently, than orality?" That is a content question and it hides the mechanism. Stated in effect terms, a rememberer holds a library Γ. Before a retelling or a reading, the library predicts both *what* happened and *in what order*: a belief b, an act of recall a, and a prediction Γ(b,a). The world (or a checkable record of the world) returns o'. The residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is indexed per remembered slot s (Fig 1 :11, Fig 1 :19). Two things vary across oral and literate transmission that are not the content itself:

1. **What crosses the boundary between one mind and the next.** A spoken performance can be either a re-applicable way of composing a memory (a method) or a verbatim replay of one prior performance (a recording). A written text can be either a manual (a method) or an archive entry (a recording).
2. **Whether a non-updating ground remains reachable.** In an oral chain, the only "record" is the next teller; agreement among tellers can drift coherently with no anchor (Fig 2 :13-15). Writing externalizes a record so the drift can, in principle, be measured (Fig 4 :30).

So the residual is: *what does the carrier change in the loop that turns a prediction about the past into a checkable memory of the past?*

- **Option A** (medium acts on content directly): predicts a main effect of medium on what is recalled, independent of what crosses and whether the ground is reachable.
- **Option B** (medium acts on the crossing and the anchor): predicts effects on the *form*, *provenance*, and *ground-contact* of memory, with content effects only where the change in crossing or anchor reaches a slot.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a per-slot shape with four fields, (carrier, crossing as method or recording, anchor, round-trip gap). This binds the sought-for shape so it can accrue a residual like any other term. It is done here, before the findings.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

**R (the residual).** Arity: two carrier conditions (oral, written) acting on one outcome (memory content plus temporal organization). It is not a single dial. Symmetry: the two carriers are not interchangeable. Writing is a transform with directed loss (Fig 4 :27), and literacy alters the oral method asymmetrically (Ong/Lord, section 9). Scale: one rememberer (individual), one telling (transmission), a line of tellers (chain), an institution (canon or archive).

**Domain 1, individual reconstruction.** Actors: one rememberer, their library Γ, the remembered slot s, the world. Currency: the per-slot residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|. Anchor: what actually happened, which does not move because a rememberer or a culture changes its mind (Fig 2 :29). This is the base loop; it does not differ between oral and literate people.

**Domain 2, the carrier as membrane between scales.** Actors: teller or scribe, audience or reader, the message. Currency: what crosses the boundary, a re-applicable method/head start versus a one-occasion recording (Fig 4 :22-25). Anchor: the ground, plus the stability of the physical carrier. This is where "oral tradition" and "written record" actually enter the model.

**Domain 3, the transmission chain.** Actors: successive tellers or copyists, each a room in a chain (Fig 7). Currency: variation, agreement, and selection. Anchor: the ground, which must be consulted separately by each room or the chain collapses into a mirror (Fig 7 :35-36). This is the domain where "organize the past" is decided: which episodes survive, and in what order.

**Domain 4, the record and provenance seat.** Actors: records, rulings about what happened, and the seat that maintains them. Currency: provenance carried across the seam (Fig 10 :62). Anchor: the ground; provenance shows legitimacy and never truth (Fig 10 :65). This seat spans the chain and is where writing changes what can be checked.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54-55). The two halves of the question, what people remember and how they organize the past, are overlapping conditions: the same episode is a slot in D1, a message in D2, a link in D3, and an entry in D4. A single lever (say, inventing writing) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). Each domain needs its own loop and its own verdict.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** D1 perception and vocabulary must be reached before a message can be bound as a term (Fig 3 :4-8, the perception and vocabulary links). D2 (what crosses) precedes D3 (the chain is made of repeated crossings). D4 (provenance) rides on D3 but is the seat that can keep the ground reachable. The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (Fig 3 :19). A reading taken below a broken link is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

**Strain test applied during the census.** The tempting mapping "oral = method, writing = recording" fits Fig 4 only with a strain: a written manual is a method, and a verbatim recited poem is a recording. The line does not fit the medium; it fits what crosses. That strain sends us back to the census (D2 is defined by *what crosses*, not by the physical carrier), and it is recorded openly in F7 rather than smoothed over.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - The carrier is a membrane, not a content source (central finding)

Fig 4 :22-23, :25:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

Fig 4 :19-20 fixes what changes across the boundary: *"the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* In this question the kernel (the reconstruction loop of D1) is identical for a teller and a reader. What changes is the currency that crosses in D2 and the anchor that stays reachable in D4.

**Verdict (D2): `specified`, triangulated (Fig 4 plus Fig 1 :23 ground as only metric).** A written record is, by default, a recording of one occasion carried across a membrane. An oral tradition that is formulaic and re-performed is, by default, a method or head start that makes the next composition cheaper (Ong/Lord, section 9). Neither is intrinsically the other; the test is re-applicability, not substrate. **Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which the same content is delivered as a verbatim script versus a generative oral formula, with the ground held fixed, showing no difference in what is reconstructed after one generation; that would collapse the method/recording distinction for this residual.

### F2 - The reconstruction loop is per slot and the ground is the only metric

Fig 1 :19, :22, :23:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

A claim like "literate cultures remember more" aggregates across slots and is exactly the average that loses the signal. The honest question is per remembered slot: for *this* episode, *this* date, *this* causal link, does the carrier change R?

**Verdict (D1): `general` on the structure, `specified` that the loop is shared, triangulated (Fig 1 plus Fig 2 :29 anchor definition).** Both carriers feed the same loop. **Refuter:** evidence that oral and literate recall use non-overlapping cognitive mechanisms at the slot level that cannot be ranged against one ground; that would split D1 into two domains and require a new census.

### F3 - Repetition compounds agreement, not evidence

Fig 7 :12-13, :35-36:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :12-13)
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** (Fig 7 :36)

Fig 2 :13-15 names the collapse: with no anchor, "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence." A teller who repeats a predecessor without ranging the account against anything outside the chain is a mirror, not a second witness. Bartlett's serial reproduction of *The War of the Ghosts* is the laboratory version: the story becomes shorter and more conventional with each reproduction (section 9). Ong's "A copy of a copy of a copy. You can only do that so many times and retain fidelity" is the same law in the oral case.

**Verdict (D3): `specified`, triangulated (Fig 7 plus Fig 2 plus Bartlett 1932 and Legare 2019, two low-overlap fields).** Repetition within a closure increases coherence and does not increase contact with the past. Progress requires a next room that differs and is selected against the surfaced residual. **Refuter:** a documented oral chain in which tellers' agreement tracks an independent ground record better than a single teller's account, with the shared evidence pool controlled; that would show agreement carrying evidence here and would break Fig 7 :13 for this case.

### F4 - Writing fixes a recording so the round trip and its gap can be measured

Fig 4 :27, :30:

> **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

A written record is a stable surface. Because it persists, a later reader can carry a remembered account up (abstract it), bring it back down (apply it to a specific case), and compare. The Symbols table defines the round trip as R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). In an oral chain the "record" is the next performance, so the gap between what was told and what is now recalled is not available as an object; the chain is navigating by dead reckoning. Assmann's distinction between communicative memory (oral, tied to living memory, roughly 80 to 100 years) and cultural memory (institutionalised, mediated through texts and rituals) is the world-facing correlate (section 9). Ong: "Print encourages a sense of closure, a sense that what is found in a text has been finalized, has reached a state of completion." Closure is the failure mode of treating the fixed recording as the ground.

**Verdict (D4): `specified`, triangulated (Fig 4 plus Assmann and Ong, low overlap between them).** Writing's contribution is not more memory; it is a checkable object and a measurable R_T, with a new failure mode (closure) attached. **Refuter:** a demonstration that the round-trip gap between oral retellings can be measured to the same precision as between written recensions; that would remove the asymmetry this finding asserts.

### F5 - Provenance, not truth, is what the record carries

Fig 10 :62, :65, :47:

> **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."**
> **"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that"**
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 8 :51 states the companion: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* A written chronicle fixes who said what and when; that is provenance. It does not make the chronicle true. Halbwachs gives the social side: "It is in society that people normally acquire their memories. It is also in society that they recall, recognize, and localize their memories." The content of a group memory is a social framework, and a clean written record of a group agreement is still a record of an agreement.

**Verdict (D4): `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 8 plus Halbwachs, different fields).** Writing stabilizes provenance; it can also promote a convention into a verdict when the fixed text is treated as self-certifying. **Refuter:** a case where the provenance of a written record changes its truth value rather than only its perceived legitimacy (a record becomes true *because* it is dated and attributed); that would break Fig 10 :65.

### F6 - The ground does not decay; the carrier-channel does

Fig 10 :24-26:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :25-26)

An oral tradition lives in a channel made of living tellers. When the line of transmission breaks, the channel decays while what actually happened does not. A written record is a more durable channel, but it is still a channel: it can be lost, biased by what was selected for writing (Assmann's selection and framing), or saturated with pooled readings that sever contact. Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* A community's memory is a property of rememberers plus their carrier-habitat, not of literacy or orality in abstraction.

**Verdict (D3/D4): `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 11 plus Assmann).** **Refuter:** a case where an oral channel preserves ground contact across a break in the line of tellers without any external record; that would falsify the channel-decay claim for this case.

### F7 - Strain and re-census: "oral = method, writing = recording" is the wrong partition

This is the honest strain of the derivation. Fig 4 :22-25 seems at first to license a clean mapping: oral tradition is the re-applicable method (head start), written record is the one-occasion recording. Three facts resist it:

1. A written manual or grammar is a method that makes the next search cheaper.
2. A verbatim recited poem is a replay of one occasion; Ong reports Lord's finding that "Learning to read and write disables the oral poet" because it introduces "the concept of a text as controlling the narrative," interfering with "the remembrance of songs sung." Here *writing* destroys the oral *method*.
3. A written archive entry can be re-performed as a method by a community of readers; a memorized formula can be frozen into one canonical version.

**Re-census.** The governing variable is not the physical carrier but the bond that crosses: method or recording (Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands"*; the bond carries the information the operands do not, Fig 12 :5). Medium is the carrier; the crossing is the quantity. This dissolves the strain exactly as the canonical rule requires: *"a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain, not found a flaw."* The strain is kept visible here rather than erased.

**Verdict: strain resolved by re-census; the finding is stated in terms of crossings, not media.** **Refuter:** a demonstration that the carrier, with crossing held constant, changes R per slot; that would reinstate the medium as the governing quantity and require a fresh census.

### F8 - "Organize the past" is a bond question, not an ingredient question

Fig 12 :5, :94, :104:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
> **"The arrangement is the substance."**
> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** Remembered episodes are atoms; the past is a molecule. Chronology is the sequence bond → (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :30); causation is production ⇒ (*"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :36-37); comparison of two eras is ⋛; a refused date or a rejected causal claim is a negation ¬ (Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*).

Bartlett's "effort after meaning" is the cognitive version: recall fits the strange into the familiar, and the schema becomes "not merely something that works the organism, but something with which the organism can work" (p. 206, quoted in section 9). Writing supplies a stable surface on which bonds can be externalized and inspected; orality carries them in formulaic patterns that are re-composed each telling (Ong's "additive," "aggregative," "redundant" characteristics of orally based thought). Neither determines *which* bonds; the figure returns a shape (the bond types available), not a name for the one arrangement a given culture uses.

**Verdict (D1/D3): `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 13 plus Bartlett and Ong, different fields).** **Refuter:** a culture that organizes remembered episodes with a relation failing all seven bond tests (a genuinely new bond); that would be a claim not yet made, not a missing bond (Fig 12 :90: *"A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."*).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* These are written before the conclusion in section 6.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Crossing collapse.** A controlled comparison with carrier held constant but crossing (method versus recording) varied shows no difference in per-slot R after one transmission. This breaks F1/F4.
2. **Agreement carries evidence.** An oral chain is documented in which tellers' agreement tracks an independent ground record better than a single teller's account, with shared evidence controlled. This breaks F3 (Fig 7 :13).
3. **Provenance becomes truth.** A written record's truth value changes because of its dating or attribution, not because of what happened. This breaks F5 (Fig 10 :65).
4. **Zero round-trip gap.** R_T between oral retellings is measured equal to R_T between written recensions at the same precision. This breaks F4 (Fig 4 :30).
5. **Carrier acts at fixed crossing.** With crossing held constant, the physical carrier alone changes per-slot R. This breaks the F7 re-census and reinstates the medium as the governing variable.
6. **A new bond.** A culture organizes remembered episodes with a relation failing all seven bond tests. This breaks F8 and is recorded as a new claim, not a missing operator (Fig 12 :90).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not ask which medium is better. Ask, per remembered slot, what crosses the membrane, whether the ground stays reachable, and what the round-trip gap is (F1, F4).
2. Keep the four domains separate and never average them into a "writing helps by X" effect (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
3. State the mode on every substantive sentence (F2, F9).
4. Put the derivation before the mapping, with dates and provenance, so a reader can tell convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :51).
5. Use the public submission and contact points in `03_public_contact_points.csv` and ask for a refutation rather than belief (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
6. Treat the physical medium as a carrier and the crossing as the variable (F7). This is the one place a naive reading of the figures goes wrong.

## 7. Conclusion

The carrier is not a content dispenser. It is a membrane and a channel. What it changes, per remembered slot, is the bond that crosses (a re-applicable method or a one-occasion recording), the stability of the channel, whether a non-updating ground remains reachable, and whether a round trip can be taken and its gap measured. Oral tradition tends to carry methods and to lose ground contact as agreement compounds along a chain; written records tend to carry recordings, to stabilize provenance, and to make the gap measurable while adding the failure mode of closure. These are `specified`, triangulated tendencies, not a rank order of media. Which yields more accurate memory in a given society is a `gap`: no figure line and no instrument run here returns it.

The strongest single figure-grounded point for outreach is the chain law: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13). It applies equally to a line of tellers and to a list of agreeable expert readers, which is why the packet asks for a refutation and not an endorsement.
