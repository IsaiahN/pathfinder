**Subject:** Check one output about oral tradition and written memory before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework draws a hard line between a method that can be reapplied and a recording of one occasion. Fig 4 :22-23: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down... Never the recording of a particular success."* Applied to memory, an oral tradition that is re-composed each telling carries a method; a written archive entry carries a recording. But the physical medium is not the variable: a written manual is a method, and a verbatim recited poem is a recording. What crosses the membrane is the variable.
>
> Two further consequences follow, and both are checkable. First, repetition inside a chain of tellers increases agreement, not evidence. Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Second, a fixed written record lets you take a round trip and measure the gap between an abstraction and its re-application; an oral chain usually cannot, so it navigates by dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that writing is better than orality, that literacy causes a fixed cognitive change in every case, or that this framework settles the empirical question of which medium yields more accurate memory. That last question is a `gap`: a shape and a refuter are returned, not a winner. *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. With the physical carrier held constant, varying method versus recording makes no difference to what is reconstructed after one transmission.
> 2. An oral chain is found whose agreement tracks an independent ground record better than a single teller's account, with shared evidence controlled.
> 3. The round-trip gap between oral retellings is measured equal to that between written recensions at the same precision.
> 4. A culture organizes remembered episodes with a relation that fails all seven of the framework's bond tests.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Figures README (usage rule): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it; the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
