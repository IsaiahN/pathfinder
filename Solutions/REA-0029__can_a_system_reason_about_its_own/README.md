## The question restated as an effect

Two claims are bundled in the question and must be separated before either is answered.

1. **Representation.** "Reason about its own assumptions" means the system has terms that refer to the terms it composes over. What each option leads to: if the assumptions are bound terms, they are enumerable and can accrue a residual; if the load-bearing assumption is an unbound preference (the direction of the system's own question), no internal check can see it (Fig 5 :61-63).
2. **Detection.** "Detect when those assumptions no longer apply" means the residual of a held term does not trend down against something that does not move when the system changes its mind. What each option leads to: with a pinned anchor the term can be refused and the reach falls safely; without one, the system grades its own output with a quantity it also produces, and the drift is coherent and invisible (Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :33-34).

**What an answer would look like:** a per-domain verdict, not one yes/no. A "yes" for bound terms, a "yes only against an external ground" for detection, a "no" for whole-set self-certification, and a named `gap` for the missing instrument that would make an unbound direction refusable. This sought-for shape is written down here and bound as an ordinary term before the run, per Formula step "Direction". That is the pre-registration, not a conclusion.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, even where a public page displayed one. Venues only.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that a system can reason about its assumptions.
- No bulk-send plan, and no claim that agreement from a pool of likeminded readers would corroborate the verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- No claim that this derivation settles the question. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (N, 2026): *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*
