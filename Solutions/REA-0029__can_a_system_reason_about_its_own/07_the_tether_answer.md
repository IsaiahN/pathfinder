# The Tether's answer: can a system reason about its own assumptions and detect when those assumptions no longer apply?

**Mode: `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the missing instrument. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is a union of two conditions with different anchors, not one question with a yes or no answer.

- **Representation.** Can the system make its held assumptions objects of further composition?
- **Detection.** Can it tell that a term it held has stopped paying against the ground?

Fig 9 :54-58: a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound. So this answer gives four verdicts and keeps them separate. It never averages them into a score for "self-awareness."

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the loop / self-model: partial yes, and a structural no where the load-bearing assumption is concerned.** Fig 5 :58: *"The candidates are ENUMERABLE rather than intuited: every term currently bound and generating a prediction is a candidate for being the wrong one."* Those terms rank by a residual that *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* (Fig 5 :59). So a system can enumerate a proper subset of its assumptions and point a refusal at the one that keeps failing. But Fig 5 :61-63: *"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."* The shape the system seeks is *"a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing"*, and *"nothing here can see it. Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION."* The remedy is a discipline, not an added step: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, let it accrue a residual (Fig 5 :65). **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 5, Fig 1 :27, Formula "Direction").

**D2, detection: yes only against a ground that does not update.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33-35: *"The anchor must not update."* Otherwise *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"*, and *"A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* So "the assumption no longer applies" is a relation between a prediction and a pinned reference, indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19). A single global confidence number is not that relation; it is the average in which the live signal disappears (Fig 1 :22). **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 2, Fig 1, Fig 10 :24).

**D3, whole-set self-certification: no, categorically, at this scale.** Fig 8 :34: *"A frame cannot reach its own metaframe. Godel's second theorem: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency."* Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."* Fig 9 :19: *"the witness is always imported"*, and the claim *"it is not in here"* cannot be proved from inside (Fig 9 :20). Fig 8 :40-43 keeps the scope honest: this proves the metaframe is underivable, not that any particular atom is far away, and underivable-in-principle differs from unreachable-at-this-budget. So the "no" is about certifying the whole set, not about whether a given assumption can be checked. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 8, Fig 9; proved theorems plus witness location).

**D4, the outside seat: yes, through a differing closure, with a strict condition on what the checker may see.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :54: *"The atom cannot be derived from inside A. But the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So the correction arrives from a second frame or an instrument, described in effect terms (Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*), and agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40). A copy of the system is not a second frame. **Status: `triangulated`** for the structure; the identity of the specific frame for a given assumption is `gap`.

**D5, the instrument: `gap`.** No figure line specifies how a machine turns its own unbound direction into an ordinary term, or how to measure the moment such a term starts accruing residual. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 5 :65 gives the discipline; Fig 6 :34 gives the rule for proto-instruments; neither names one here. This is `unreached`, not `unreachable`: Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* **Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.**

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "systems have self-knowledge" and not "systems do not." Those are names for a shape the figures refuse to fill.
- Not that a confidence score is a verdict. Fig 1 :27 disqualifies a quantity the frame also produces.
- Not that a second copy of a system is an independent checker. Two frames with the same closure are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49).
- Not that a runtime monitor proves self-reasoning. It counts only when its specification came from outside the monitored frame; otherwise Fig 2 :34 applies (see `06` Q2 and Q4).
- Not that the question is settled. No measurement was taken; this is a `specified` shape and a `general` structure, not `grounded`.
- Not a single percentage. Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the missing instrument is impossible. Underivable and unreachable-at-budget are different facts with different instructions (Fig 8 :40-43).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads a value *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* Here the discriminating questions are:

1. **Per-slot test.** Does the system's residual stay indexed per assumption, or does it report one pooled score? A live slot in a healthy mean is the legal state of Fig 1 :13.
2. **Non-updating-anchor test.** Does the reference stay fixed when the system changes its own report? If it moves, the check is Fig 2 panel 1 and the drift is coherent.
3. **Refusal test.** When an assumption fails, does the record gain a refusal term (Operators table :58, negation), or does it only re-fit a parameter (Fig 5 :13)?
4. **Direction test.** Can the system bind its sought-for shape as an ordinary term and show that term's residual over time (Fig 5 :65)? A positive answer would close D5.
5. **Verifier test.** Can a checker that cannot reconstruct the claim from the claimant's record still return a sound verdict against the ground (Fig 10 :42-43)?
6. **Independence test.** Do the checking frames draw on different evidence (Fig 2 :31-32), or are they one pool talking?

Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: composition outside closure; self-proof of consistency; internal confidence that tracks correctness with no external reading; internal refusal of an unbound preference confirmed by a ground; sound verification by a verifier that can reconstruct the claim.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, recording the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* This stop matters most for D5: the discipline for binding a preference exists, the instrument does not, and inventing one from the figure text would be importing an atom against a budget limit (Fig 8 :41-43). The four other verdicts are stated with their refuters, so a reader can fire one without accepting the framework.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The question bundles two different things. A system can enumerate the assumptions it actually holds as predicting terms and rank them by a residual that will not go down, so it can reason about a proper subset of them. It cannot see the preference that sets the direction of its own question, because that object is not a term and accrues no residual. Detecting that an assumption no longer applies is possible only against a reference that does not move when the system changes its mind; a system's own confidence is a quantity it produces and cannot score itself with it. Certifying the whole set from inside is not available: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency, and the witness is always imported. A second frame can carry the correction, provided it cannot reconstruct the claim and does not share the author's evidence pool. What remains open is the instrument that would turn an unbound preference into a refusable term. The answer is therefore four separate verdicts and one declared gap, not a single yes or no, and not a measurement.
