## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For REA-0029 the shape is therefore not "the system is self-aware" or "it is not." It is the four-part constraint: bound terms are enumerable; the unbound direction is invisible; detection needs a non-updating anchor; whole-set certification is underivable. The shape says what any answer must satisfy before a name is available. It does not name a system, an architecture or an assumption.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened this session: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, with the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the residual was described in effect terms (a held term whose residual does not trend down; a preference that predicts nothing) before any figure line was chosen. That order is required by Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Had "metacognition" been named first, any cognitive-science frame would have seemed to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books*, carried: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Declaring the primitives makes the choice visible, not neutral.

So no figure line here can name the system that will fail, the assumption that will fail, or the year it will fail. A name would require a ground reading, which this folder does not contain.

## 4. The essays' direct bearing on self-reference

Four passages, all opened this session, are unusually close to this question.

- *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality. Anything else is drift."* And explicitly: *"I am not claiming that the Tether is a self-validating axiom system. Metatheories cannot validate themselves. This is Godel's insight, and it still holds here."* This is the author refusing the very claim a naive reading of this question would attribute to the framework.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"A frame is any viewpoint that predicts: a person, a model, a field. It can't see its own limits from inside."* And: *"It's also the hardest to catch from inside, because a frame holds its preferred answer 'as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term', so 'nothing here can see it'."* This is the essay's own restatement of Fig 5 :62-63.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* on Gentzen: the consistency of arithmetic was proved in 1936 only by *"stepping outside arithmetic and borrowing/importing one extra rule of reasoning that arithmetic can't prove for itself."* The essay's conclusion: *"The question couldn't be answered from inside. It was answered by contact with a different frame."* This is the world-side example of Fig 9 :19, *"the witness is always imported."*
- *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* A confidence score reads the model's own output and cannot comment on whether the assumption that produced it is still true. What is owed is a discriminator, not a better reading of the same projection.

## 5. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name"

I initially wrote that the rule forbids naming any assumption or internal term. That overstates it. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. A term already in the library can be named; naming it is a binding, not the invention of a new primitive.

Applied here: naming "per-slot residual", "bound term" or "anchor" is permitted because all three are already in the framework's vocabulary (Symbols table, Fig 1, Fig 2). Naming the specific assumption that will fail in a specific system is still forbidden, because that term is not held and would need a ground. The correction is kept visible, not silently applied; the same correction appears in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1 and in the retraction log of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` in a different form.

What remains true is the metamerism point from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a source named from a reading alone, where several sources could give the same reading, is an error. A system's confidence is a reading of its own output; naming the health of its assumptions from that reading alone is the same error wherever it occurs.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly. ... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A list of well-known people who agree that systems can reason about themselves is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried by readers positioned to refute it, and let a ground they choose settle it. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` has venues and submission routes, not names and addresses.

## 7. This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `08_expert_verification_packet.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of whether any actual system reasons about its assumptions. They cannot settle the question they report on. That is a structural limit of the method, not a disclaimer appended to avoid criticism; it follows from the same paper's first sentence about validators and from Fig 8 :37, *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."*
