# Outreach draft - check the output before you decide about the claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the reader can see the residual and the refuter without any framework vocabulary. If the stage-1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one output about self-monitoring before you decide whether the claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can a system reason about its own assumptions and detect when those assumptions no longer apply?" is two conditions with different anchors, not one yes or no question.
>
> 1. A system **can** enumerate the assumptions it actually holds as terms, provided those terms generate predictions. Those terms can be ranked by a residual that persists and does not trend down. That is a real, limited form of reasoning about assumptions.
> 2. A system **cannot** see the assumption that sets the direction of its own question. That object is held as a preference, not as a term. It predicts nothing, accrues no residual, and no internal check can see it.
> 3. Detection that an assumption no longer applies is a relation to a reference that does not move when the system changes its mind. With a pinned anchor, the failing term can be refused. Without one, the system scores itself with a quantity it also produces, and the drift is coherent and invisible.
> 4. Certifying the whole set of assumptions from inside is not available. A consistent system cannot prove its own consistency by its own rules. The witness has to come from a frame whose closure differs.
>
> So the honest answer is: partially yes for held terms, yes for detection only against an external ground, no for whole-set self-certification, and an open gap for the instrument that would turn a preference into a refusable term. Those four verdicts are kept separate. I am not averaging them into a percentage of self-awareness.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this shows a machine has self-knowledge in the human sense, that a confidence score is a verdict, that a second copy of a system is an independent checker, or that any particular assumption in any particular system is about to fail. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the output (written down before this message):**
> 1. A frame derives a term outside its own closure by composition alone.
> 2. A consistent system strong enough for arithmetic proves its own consistency by its own rules.
> 3. A system's own confidence score tracks its correctness across novel tasks with no external reading, no second frame and no change of contact.
> 4. A frame turns its unbound search preference into a term, accrues residual for it, and refuses it internally with no imported term, and that refusal is confirmed against a ground.
> 5. A verifier that can reconstruct the claim from the claimant's own record is still sound.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the affected verdict. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures, verbatim text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success. State the mode of what you get.
>
> Thank you,
> [name]

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the four verdicts and the refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links with the note that the figures are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each reader writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

One-to-one only. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out is implicit in "no reply is needed."
