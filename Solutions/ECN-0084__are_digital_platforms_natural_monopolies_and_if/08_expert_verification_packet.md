# Expert verification packet - the Tether on platform monopoly and utility regulation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a platform economist, competition lawyer or economic historian can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separators and the regulatory-form verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal "should". Not `grounded`.** No market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of four overlapping conditions, not a conditional chain: supply-side cost shape, demand-side coordination shape, habitat and channel shape, and policy form. "Natural monopoly" is a name for a threshold cost-shape, and the "natural" asserts a non-updating anchor that the demand co-determination of platform markets does not supply. Network effects are demand-side utility, not supplier cost, so tipping cannot prove subadditivity. A concentration reading is a lossy projection that cannot comment on its source. "Regulated like a public utility" splits over the same instrument set into an access convention that keeps a market ground reachable and an outcome-fixing rule that stops being a convention and is liable to capture. Verdicts are per market slice and never averaged. The literal "should" is a `gap` with a refuter, not an opinion.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Economics-facing; checkable by reading the figure line and the standard definitions

- **Statement:** "Natural monopoly" is a threshold relation between quantities, not a substance, and the figures return that shape rather than the economics predicate. Network effects and scale economies are different currencies; a concentration reading cannot be used as the cost measurement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 9 :30-31 - *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- **What to check:** does the standard definition of natural monopoly match a subadditivity threshold, and does the standard definition of a network effect place it on the demand side rather than in the supplier's cost function? Both definitions are quoted with sources in `01` F2 and F3.
- **Refuter:** a subadditivity measurement for a named platform slice, under a demand fixed independently of user expectations, that survives variation in multihoming and switching costs; **or** a demonstration that network effects enter the cost accounting in the same units, collapsing the two currencies.

### C2 - Framework-internal and source-discrimination; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the source essay

- **Statement:** an anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. Because platform demand and the relevant market boundary move with expectations and multihoming, the "natural" in natural monopoly does not supply a non-updating anchor. Concentration is a projection with a lossy source, so several causes yield the same reading.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 - *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."
- **What to check:** in a market you know, does the cost comparison change when expectations or multihoming conditions change, with technology held fixed? Can you name an instrument that distinguishes cost subadditivity from cumulative advantage and from coordination, or must you infer the cause from the same concentration reading?
- **Refuter:** an instrument that uniquely attributes a platform's concentration reading to cost subadditivity rather than to path dependence, coordination, or privilege; **or** a figure re-census in which a non-updating cost anchor is found for a named slice and survives the movement test.

### C3 - Policy-facing; checkable by reading Figure 10 and the regulatory record

- **Statement:** public-utility-style measures divide into two substances over the same instruments. A convention authors shared meaning and keeps the ground reachable. A rule that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention and is liable to capture. A single lever on one side of a two-sided market moves the other side.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :46 - *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does."* Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 10 :43-44 - *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking. Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom. It transmits verdicts and issues none."* Fig 9 :55-56 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- **What to check:** take one measure you know (access mandate, interoperability duty, price cap, entry licence). Does it change the meaning the parties share, or does it produce the outcome? Did the regulator remain able to refuse, and was the rule captured over the period? Did a price change on one side move the other?
- **Refuter:** a price or entry rule that keeps a non-updating ground reachable, leaves the checker able to refuse, and is demonstrably uncaptured over a stated period; **or** a case where regulating one side of a two-sided market leaves the other side's price and the multihoming condition unchanged, both measured before and after.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all platforms are natural monopolies, and not that none are. No measurement was taken.
- Not that network effects are a supply-side cost phenomenon.
- Not that concentration has one cause, nor that it has none.
- Not that all regulation is harmful, and not that public utility regulation is the correct analogue.
- Not a welfare verdict, not a recommendation to regulate or deregulate in the abstract. The "should" is a declared `gap` (Fig 13 :134 - *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment or market measurement.
- Not a claim that the framework names the economics predicate. "Natural monopoly" is a binding from the economics frame onto a threshold relation the loop can describe; the figures return the shape (see the correction in `05` section 5).

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument and a record, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does the cost comparison for a named platform slice change when expectations and multihoming change, with technology held fixed?
2. Can an instrument assign the concentration reading to one source rather than several?
3. Does a given regulatory measure keep a non-updating ground reachable and remain a convention, or does it produce the outcome it judges? Was it captured?
4. Did pricing on one side of the market move the other side?
