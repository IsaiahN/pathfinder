## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the bundled claim that digital platforms are natural monopolies and therefore should be regulated like public utilities. Option A: accept the predicate, the category and the policy inference, and answer yes or no for the whole category. ## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union. Order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R (the residual).** The bundle presented as one conditional chain. Arity: four conditions, so a union compound (Fig 9 :55-56), not a binary question with an arrow. Symmetry: the cost predicate is an ordered threshold, the coordination predicate is multiple-valued and self-fulfilling, the policy predicate is a form distinction. Scale: cost and coordination are first order at one market slice; policy is second order, composed over settled slice verdicts. Fig 9 :4-5 says split before searching. Fig 1 :19 says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Domain 1, cost structure.** Actors: incumbent, entrants, cost function, demand. Currency: long-run average cost and subadditivity. Anchor: a cost measurement under a demand fixed independently of expectations. Question type: threshold. Nameable anchor, so a domain.
- **Domain 2, demand coordination.** Actors: users on each side, expectations, multihoming and switching decisions. Currency: utility as a function of compatible users, not supplier cost. Anchor: observed adoption and tipping under stated multihoming conditions. Question type: multiple equilibria with a signed cycle.
- **Domain 3, habitat and channel.** Actors: adjacent platforms, innovators, data and interoperability conditions, regulator. Currency: contact and reach. Anchor: the market outcome that pays, which does not change because a frame thinks it should. Question type: union with the other domains.
- **Domain 4, policy form.** Actors: regulator, platform, business users, end users. Currency: rules and the outcomes they decide. Anchor: the ground the rule keeps reachable, never agreement. Question type: partition first (convention versus outcome-fixing verdict), union inside the convention class.
- **Partition or union.** R1 to R4 are a union with unassignable overlap; the same concentration reading is produced by several sources. The regulatory class is a partition with two members. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).
- **Order by dependency.** R1 and R2 are ranged in parallel, R3 consumes both, R4 consumes all three. This is a dependency graph with a merge, not a chain. The question's "if so" is a dependency marker, not a sufficient inference.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question's packaging fails; the shape survives. "Natural monopoly" is a threshold cost-shape, and the "natural" asserts a non-updating anchor that demand co-determination does not supply (Domain 1, `specified` on the moving-anchor finding, `gap` on any slice verdict). Network effects and scale economies are two non-additive currencies, so tipping does not prove a cost advantage, and concentration is a lossy reading whose source needs an instrument (Domain 2, `specified`). A platform verdict is platform-and-habitat, with no standing list of regulated firms (Domain 3, `specified`). "Regulated like a public utility" splits into an access convention that keeps the ground reachable and an outcome-fixing rule that stops being a convention and invites capture; a single lever on one side of a two-sided market leaves the other side bound (Domain 4, `specified` on form). The literal "should" is a declared `gap`, not an averaged opinion and not silence (Fig 13 :134). Nothing here is `grounded`. The strongest single figure-grounded point is the union finding: four conditions, per-slice verdicts, kept separate and never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the form is honoured. The packet states mode, writes refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a label instead of an effect, or by showing the framework before the stage-1 verdict (Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*). Fig 2 :42 - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Regulatory error is costly, so that cost is worth avoiding.
- **undoable:** yes. The rejected packaging is removable without touching the four domain derivations. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no term has to be withdrawn from the record. What cannot be undone is a checker's independence once it has been spent.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A subadditivity measurement under expectations-independent demand that survives multihoming variation would establish a cost anchor for a named slice; a source-discriminating instrument would attribute a concentration reading to one cause; a price or entry rule shown to keep a non-updating ground reachable, leave the checker able to refuse and remain uncaptured over a stated period would clear the outcome-fixing form; a figure re-census returning a universal allocation rule would break the per-slot finding. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
