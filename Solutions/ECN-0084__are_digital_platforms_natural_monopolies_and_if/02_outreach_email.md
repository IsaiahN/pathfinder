# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01` section 4 is written down in the version you actually send, and until the claim is stated in effect terms rather than as a label. Stage 1 collapses on contact if the recipient is handed a theory instead of a checkable output.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about platform monopoly before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "are digital platforms natural monopolies, and if so should they be regulated like public utilities" is not one conditional chain. It is a union of at least four overlapping conditions: a supply-side cost shape, a demand-side coordination shape, a habitat and channel shape, and a policy-form shape. The dependency order is real, but a positive finding on costs does not carry through to the policy question without passing through the coordination and habitat findings. Verdicts have to be given per named market slice and never averaged into a verdict for "platforms" as a category.
>
> The specific claim that I would like checked is this: the usual inference from a platform tipping to that platform being a natural monopoly adds two different currencies. Subadditivity of a cost function is a supply-side, threshold quantity. Network effects are demand-side utility as a function of the number of compatible users. The standard economics definitions say so in almost those words. A concentration reading alone cannot comment on which source produced it, because cost subadditivity, expectation-driven tipping, cumulative advantage and historical exclusion can all produce the same reading. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources, not a name.
>
> On the policy half: "regulated like a public utility" splits into two forms over the same instruments. One is a convention, for example interoperability, portability, access and non-self-preferencing, that keeps a market ground reachable and remains a rule about shared meaning. The other is a rule that fixes prices or entry and thereby decides the outcome. The second has stopped being a convention. Regulatory capture is what that failure looks like in the published record. A price lever on one side of a two-sided market also moves the other side, so it does not clear the compound.
>
> The literal "should" is a `gap` in this derivation, not an averaged opinion and not silence. The framework returns the shape any candidate answer must have; it does not return a welfare verdict. I am flagging that rather than filling it.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. a subadditivity measurement for a named platform slice, under a demand fixed independently of expectations, that survives variation in multihoming and switching costs;
> 2. a demonstration that network effects enter the supplier's cost accounting in the same units as subadditivity, so the two conditions are one quantity;
> 3. an instrument that uniquely attributes a platform's concentration reading to cost subadditivity rather than to path dependence, coordination, or privilege;
> 4. a rule that fixes price or entry, keeps a non-updating market ground reachable, leaves the checker able to refuse, and is demonstrably uncaptured over a stated period.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is moved or withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures and formula repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

> - The source-discrimination essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a market you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Legal and practical note

One-to-one only. No bulk send, no automation, no purchased list. The venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are institutional contact forms, consultation portals and public submission routes. Where a public body publishes a general enquiry address, the email in this draft is sent directly to that published institutional channel, not to a named individual, and an opt-out line is included. Nothing in this folder is sent by this agent.
