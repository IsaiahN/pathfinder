# Are the two regulatory forms isomers? Structure, order and the sign of the loop

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles a cost predicate and a policy form under one "if so". Two supporting derivations are owed:

1. If a regulation is a composition over held instruments, what makes "access and interoperability rules" and "price and entry rules" different things rather than different amounts of the same thing?
2. What is the order of the union, and does the positive feedback of network effects close a cycle or merely a chain?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for the policy domain: the instruments named in the opened DMA source (data portability, interoperability, access for business users, non-self-preferencing, unbundling, limits on data combination, and price or entry controls). Bonds held: the seven typed bonds and negation. No new atom is available without import from the regulatory frame. The two forms are compositions over the same instrument set, so the difference must be in the bond.

For the coordination domain: atoms are users on each side, adoption decisions, expectations, multihoming and switching costs. The relevant bond is a cycle with a sign, not a sequence.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two regulatory forms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Form C (access, portability, interoperability, non-self-preferencing, access to business users) composes the instruments under a rule that authorises shared meaning and leaves the ground to decide outcomes. It is a convention in the sense of Fig 10 :45-46: *"It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."* and *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*
- Form V (price control, rate-of-return control, mandated entry or service obligations that decide who wins) composes the same instruments under a rule that decides outcomes. Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
- The ingredient lists overlap heavily; the behaviour diverges. That is Fig 12's isomer condition, not a difference of degree.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a given instrument can be shown to have the same effect on the market ground under both forms, with the same bond type in each, then the two are the same substance under two names and the isomer claim fails. This is an empirical question about the instrument, not a definitional one.

### Q2 - Which bond does each form use?

- Fig 12 :46 - *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- Form C uses comparison and sequence in the service of a convention: a rule says that two sides may interoperate (a typed relation between parties), and a comparison fixes when access is due. It does not produce a new market outcome; it removes a block. The closest bond is the ordered sequence (→) plus threshold comparison (⋛), with the ground, not the rule, deciding the result.
- Form V uses production (⇒) in the sense of Fig 12 :77 - *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*: the rule brings about the price or the entrant. Fig 10 :43-44 requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking - *"Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom. It transmits verdicts and issues none."* A rule that produces the outcome the regulator is meant to judge has put the seat inside the frame.
- This is the structural reason for the regulatory-capture finding in F6 of `01`: Stigler's "regulation is acquired by the industry and is designed and operated primarily for its benefit" is what a producing rule looks like when the regulated party can reach the producer.
- **Type caveat, kept open:** whether a given real measure is convention or production is not decidable from the statute's words. It needs the observed bond, which is a question for an instrument and a record. Named, not hidden.

### Q3 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :20 - *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 9 :55-56 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- The four residuals are ordered but not linear. R1 (cost) and R2 (coordination) can be ranged in parallel against different anchors. R3 (habitat) consumes both. R4 (policy form) consumes all three. This is a dependency order with a merge, not a four-step chain.
- The question's "and if so" treats R1 as sufficient for R4. Fig 3 :19 - *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* If R2 and R3 are unsettled, the policy step is above a break.
- **Falsifier:** a figure re-census in which cost subadditivity alone predicts the regulatory form with coordination and habitat held at zero would linearise the order and break this finding.

### Q4 - Does the network-effect loop close, and with what sign?

- Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- The network-effect source describes adoption feeding adoption: positive feedback, critical mass, bandwagon, tipping. That is a cycle with a positive sign. Under Fig 12's rule it can close, and a chain representation of the platform's growth cannot close by construction.
- The same source gives the conditions under which it does **not** close: utility from network effects must exceed utility from differentiation, multihoming and switching costs must be high, and shocks can reintroduce competition. The competitive-exclusion source gives the ecological form: exclusion holds "only if the ecological factors are constant", and the paradox of the plankton shows many species coexisting on few resources. These are the negative-sign or open-loop cases.
- A positive cycle that closes is still not a cost anchor. It produces the concentration reading; it does not name the source of that reading (F4 of `01`).
- **Falsifier:** a case where adoption feeds adoption, all three tipping conditions hold, and the market nonetheless does not concentrate, with no shock in the observation window. That would break the cycle claim here.

### Q5 - Where is the functional group?

- Fig 12 :123 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- All public-utility-style measures share a functional group: a rule that authorises a relation between a platform and its users or business users, intended to keep a service reachable. The group says "access rule". The qualifier decides whether the rule stays a convention or becomes an outcome-fixing verdict. That is why the phrase "regulated like a public utility" is misleading: it names the group and hides the qualifier.
- The competitive-exclusion source supplies the matching ecological analogue: a shared resource plus a slight advantage yields dominance under constant factors, but "raising the dimensionality of the system" through heterogeneity, trade-offs and lag can prevent exclusion. The group is competition for a resource; the qualifier is the dimensionality of the habitat.

## CONCLUSION

The two regulatory forms are **isomers over the held instrument set**: same atoms, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117). Form C is a convention that keeps the ground reachable; Form V is a production rule that decides the outcome and has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :45-47). The union order is a dependency graph with a merge, not the question's conditional chain. The platform growth mechanism is a positive cycle that can close under three stated conditions and stay open otherwise, and its closing does not name the source of the concentration reading.

This framing preserves what is defensible in the public-utility analogy, the shared functional group of access rules, without the part that fails: treating a cost label as sufficient for a policy form. "Natural monopoly implies utility regulation" remains a refusal. "Access rules over a shared atom set divide into two bonded substances with different failure modes" is a checkable structural claim with a stated falsifier.
