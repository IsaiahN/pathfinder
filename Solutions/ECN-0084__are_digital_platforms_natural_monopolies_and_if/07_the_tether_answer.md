# The Tether's answer on platform monopoly and utility regulation

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators and the regulatory-form verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal normative "should" and on any universal answer for the category. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

"Are digital platforms natural monopolies, and if so should they be regulated like public utilities?" is a bundle of four overlapping conditions presented as one conditional chain. Fig 9 :4-5 - *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 13 :136 - *"It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."*

The four conditions are: a supply-side cost shape, a demand-side coordination shape, a habitat and channel shape, and a policy-form shape. They form a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :55-56). The dependency order of Fig 3 :20 - *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* - makes the policy question posterior to the others without making a cost finding sufficient for it.

## 2. The shape, per domain

### Domain 1, cost structure (per named market slice)

1. **"Natural monopoly" is a threshold, not a substance.** Fig 12 :87 - *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* The effect is subadditivity: one supplier's long-run cost over the whole market below the sum over any partition of it. The economics source read this session gives the definition in those terms and attributes the formal version to Baumol (1977).
2. **The label is a name borrowed from the economics frame.** Fig 9 :30-31 - *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* The framework returns the shape (a threshold under a stated demand), not the predicate.
3. **The word "natural" asserts an anchor stability the measurement does not supply.** Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 - *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* For platforms, the demand over which average cost is taken is co-determined by expectations and multihoming. The network-effect source: "Consumer expectations are key in determining which outcomes will result." Where the measured quantity moves with what frames expect, it is not the non-updating anchor the word "natural" claims.
4. **Verdict:** `specified` that the label does not supply its own anchor, `gap` on whether a given slice actually has a stable subadditive core. Not a "no". Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

### Domain 2, demand coordination (per named market slice)

1. **Network effects and scale economies are two currencies.** Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* The network-effect source: "Network effects are the demand side counterpart of economies of scale, as they function by increasing a customer's willingness to pay due rather than decreasing the supplier's average cost." Inferring a cost property from a tipping observation adds quantities in different units (Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*).
2. **The growth mechanism is a positive cycle that can close.** Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Adoption feeds adoption. The cycle closes only when utility from effects exceeds differentiation, multihoming costs are high and switching costs are high. The competitive-exclusion source gives the constant-factors caveat and the plankton counterexample.
3. **Concentration is a lossy projection.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Cost subadditivity, expectation tipping, preferential attachment ("those who are already wealthy receive more than those who are not") and historical exclusion all yield the same reading. Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error.
4. **Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across the figure set and three low-overlap fields. The source attribution is `gap` until an instrument discriminates.

### Domain 3, habitat and channel

1. **A platform verdict is platform-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39-40 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Multihoming, interoperability, data portability, innovation shocks and envelopment are conditions of contact, not decorations on a cost curve.
2. **No standing list.** Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* There is no standing list of platforms to which a regulation applies independently of the slice and the residual. The DMA's per-service designation is the world's implicit acknowledgement of this.
3. **Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 plus DMA and two-sided-market sources).

### Domain 4, policy form

1. **"Regulated like a public utility" splits into two bonded substances over the same instrument set.** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
   - **Convention form:** interoperability, portability, access, non-self-preferencing. Fig 10 :45-46 - *"It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."* and *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."* Agreement about the shared meaning is the product (Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*).
   - **Outcome-fixing form:** price or entry rules that decide who wins or what is charged. Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* Fig 10 :43-44 - *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking. Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom. It transmits verdicts and issues none."*
2. **One lever leaves the intersection standing.** Fig 9 :55-56 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* The two-sided-market source names the world form: the waterbed effect, where pricing on one side has repercussions on the other.
3. **Capture is the predicted failure of the outcome-fixing form.** Regulatory capture source, Stigler (1971): "as a rule, regulation is acquired by the industry and is designed and operated primarily for its benefit"; "a captured regulator is often worse than no regulation, because it wields the authority of government."
4. **Market power is not the thing the policy question is about.** The DMA source: "reaching a 'dominant position' is by no mean considered illegal, nor is the idea of a 'winner takes most' scenario. However, practices that lock in dominant positions or that impose unfair conditions, terms to third parties might be treated as unlawful." The conduct is the target, not the label.
5. **The literal "should" is a declared `gap`.** The figures return the form a rule must take to keep the ground reachable and remain a convention. They do not return a welfare verdict. Fig 13 :138-139 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."* Fig 13 :134 - *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* This is unreached at this budget, not underivable in principle; a welfare instrument with a non-updating anchor would close it.
6. **Verdict:** `specified` on the form split and the single-lever finding, `gap` on the literal "should". Triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 10, the DMA source and the regulatory-capture source (different evidence pools).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all digital platforms are natural monopolies, and not that none are. No slice measurement was taken.
- Not that network effects are a cost advantage. They are a demand-side quantity in a different currency.
- Not that concentration has one source. Four sources are in the opened literature and none was discriminated.
- Not that all regulation is bad, and not that public utility regulation is the right analogue. The convention form and the outcome-fixing form are different substances.
- Not a welfare verdict, not a recommendation to regulate or deregulate in the abstract. The "should" is a `gap` with a stated refuter.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any platform slice pays for any claim here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator and a record, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Cost discriminator:** a subadditivity measurement for a named slice under a demand fixed independently of user expectations, with multihoming and switching costs varied. Refuter 1.
- **Source discriminator:** an instrument that assigns the concentration reading uniquely to one of cost subadditivity, cumulative advantage, coordination or privilege. Refuter 3.
- **Convention test:** does a given rule keep a non-updating market ground reachable and leave the checker able to refuse, or does it produce the outcome it is meant to judge? A record of the bond over time, including capture. Refuter 4.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** collapse of the two currencies into one accounting; a uniform slice result; a figure re-census returning a universal allocation rule.

## 5. The honest stop

The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Metatheories cannot validate themselves." The shape is a candidate generator for the form of an answer, not the answer. Fig 2 :42 - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Regulatory error is costly, so the independent check is worth buying before the framework is shown to anyone.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes" or "no" to the question as posed, because the question is a bundle: cost structure, demand coordination, habitat and policy form are four overlapping conditions, not one conditional chain. It says that "natural monopoly" is a threshold cost-shape, not a substance, and that the word "natural" asserts a stability the platform's demand-side coordination does not supply; that network effects and scale economies are two currencies that cannot be added, so tipping does not prove a cost advantage; that a concentration reading cannot name its own source, because cost subadditivity, cumulative advantage, coordination and exclusion all produce it; and that "regulated like a public utility" splits into two different substances, an access convention that keeps a market ground reachable and an outcome-fixing rule that stops being a convention and invites capture. Verdicts are given per market slice and never averaged. The literal "should" is a declared gap, with a refuter written in advance. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the instruments that would close it, and the falsifiers. The ground decides.
