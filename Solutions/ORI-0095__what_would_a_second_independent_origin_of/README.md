# Solution ORI-0095

**Question:** What would a second, independent origin of life in our Solar System reveal about how common life is?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 130

**Section:** 10. Life beyond Earth and broader implications

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the derived constraints and the lower-bound claim, `gap` on the numeric frequency value itself. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive claim carries its mode in the file where it appears.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Boundary.** Work was confined to this folder. No file outside it was modified. Sources were the canonical figure text at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, the figures README, `THE_FORMULA.md`, the eight essays (those actually opened are named in `10_source_ledger.csv`), and the public web pages listed and opened for `04_links_and_distribution.md`. This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. By the framework's own rule it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## Restatement as an effect

Decide what follows, for an estimate of how common life is, from observing a second origin that is *independent* of Earth life. Option A: read the observation as "life is common" and publish the number. Option B: treat independence as the governing condition, refuse the singleton-origin hypothesis, cut the estimate down to a lower bound and a shape, and state what instrument would have to pay before a frequency can be named. A leads to a `general`-mode verdict smuggled out as a measurement and to the double counting of one event; B leads to a checkable shape, a pre-registered refuter, and an honest `gap` on the value.

## What would count as an answer, bound as an ordinary term

Before the run, the sought-for shape is written down so it can accrue a residual like anything else: an answer has (i) a count of independent origins, (ii) an enumerated denominator of opportunities, (iii) a refused singleton hypothesis, and (iv) a lineage-discriminating instrument that says why the count is two rather than one. Any candidate that yields a numeric frequency without (i) to (iv) fails the bound term.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the result.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for agreement manufactures the correlation that would make its agreement worthless. Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
