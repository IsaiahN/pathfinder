# Outreach draft: check the independence claim before deciding what it means

**Use:** one-to-one. Adapt as a cover note for a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked against the verbatim figure text and the refuter in section 4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is still standing.

---

**Subject:** Would you count a second origin as a second data point?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question and would like your verdict on one claim before I show you any theory. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking for trust. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms:** what would a second, independent origin of life in our Solar System reveal about how common life is?
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** finding life on a second world does *not* by itself raise the estimated frequency of life. It raises it only if the second biosphere is independent of Earth life. If the two share an ancestry, they are one event read twice. The figure I am checking puts it this way: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* What an independent second origin would actually deliver is smaller and sharper than "life is common": it refuses the hypothesis that Earth life is the only origin, and it sets a lower bound on the fraction of suitable worlds that develop life. It does not return a number.
>
> **What is deliberately not claimed:** that life is common; that the frequency is near one; that biochemistry resembling Earth's proves either independence or common ancestry; that current non-detection on Mars or the icy moons shows life is rare. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and a count without an enumerated denominator is not a rate.
>
> **The refuter, written before this run:** if a lineage test returns the same genetic code, the same handedness and the same core molecular machinery beyond what convergence plausibly produces, then the two detections are one event, the count stays at one, and the frequency inference fails. If no instrument at any resolution could separate independent origin from common source for the pair, the honest verdict is "out of reach at this budget", not "life is common".
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the three public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Drake equation, section on the fraction of planets that develop life: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_equation
> - Kipping 2020, objective Bayesian analysis of life's early start: https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09008
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure helps more than a reported success.
