# Expert verification packet: a second independent origin and the frequency of life

**What this is:** a claim in a form an astrobiologist, origin-of-life chemist or statistician can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the derived constraints, `gap` on the frequency value. Not `grounded`.** No second biosphere has been measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result. The whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Finding life on a second world does not by itself raise the estimated frequency of life. It does so only if the second biosphere is independent of Earth life. If the two share an ancestry, they are one event read twice. A genuine second origin refuses the singleton hypothesis, that Earth life is the only origin, and bounds the fraction of suitable worlds that develop life from below. It does not return a number, because a frequency also needs an enumerated denominator and a lineage-discriminating instrument. Independence governs; the value is a `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** a detection and an independent origin are different quantities. Two biospheres sharing an ancestry are one frame with two names, not two witnesses.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31-32, *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Also Fig 2 :39-40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
- **Refuter:** a lineage test establishing a separate origin gives independence; one establishing shared ancestry leaves the count at one and the frequency inference fails.

### C2 : world-facing; checkable against the origin-of-life and astrobiology literature

- **Statement:** the independence condition is already explicit in the field. The Drake equation page states that independent life on Mars, Europa, Enceladus or Titan would move the count from zero to one degree of freedom and push the life fraction toward one, while adding that uncertainty remains because of small sample size and *"the chance they are not really independent"*. The Panspermia page states that two-planet biosignatures may instead indicate panspermia.
- **What to check:** is that a fair reading of the sources, opened 2026-11-23? Does any current mission return a lineage verdict, or only a detection?
- **Refuter:** a published result showing that two detections alone decide independence, with no lineage record, would break C2. A source-discriminating instrument that pays would discharge the `unreachable-at-this-budget` part and strengthen it.

### C3 : framework-internal derivation, with a mathematics cross-check

- **Statement:** the count must be per opportunity, not aggregated, and a lower bound is not a frequency. Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 11 :43, *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The Rule of succession page confirms the mathematical dependence on observed trials and warns that the total-ignorance form is *"not valid when s=0, or s=n"*.
- **Refuter:** enumerate the opportunities completely and show the denominator is known, and show a ground measurement returning the rate directly. Then the `gap` closes and C3 is withdrawn.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that life is common; no frequency value is asserted.
- Not that two detections are two independent origins.
- Not that similar biochemistry decides shared ancestry or convergence. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not that current non-detection means rarity. Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.
- Not a theory of everything, and no claim that this framework is the only one that could answer the question. Fig 6 :27: depth and genuine absence look the same from inside.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Lineage test:** does the second biosphere differ in genetic code, handedness and core molecular machinery beyond plausible convergence?
2. **Habitat enumeration:** how many opportunities were actually tried, and were they enumerated from the world rather than composed from a suitability theory?
3. **Third-world test:** with two independent origins, does a third suitable world remain sterile?
4. **Standing refuters from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4:** shared ancestry confirmed; no discriminator in principle; one opportunity only.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :31-32 and Fig 8 :50-52. Two short passages.
2. Check C2 against the Drake equation and Panspermia pages. Are the quotes fair?
3. Name one instrument that could return a lineage verdict for a candidate biosphere, or say why none is possible. That decides whether the current link is `unreachable-at-this-budget`.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
