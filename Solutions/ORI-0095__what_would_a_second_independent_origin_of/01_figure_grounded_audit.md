## 0. The residual stated in effect terms

The question asks for a frequency: how common life is. Today the count of origins we can name is one (Earth). A frequency is a count of **independent** origins against a count of **opportunities**. The residual R is the gap between a one-instance existence claim and a rate statement. Three things are missing before the gap closes: a second count that is genuinely independent of the first, an enumerated denominator, and an instrument that can tell independence from shared ancestry.

The sought-for shape is bound now, before the run, so it can accrue a residual: an answer must contain (i) a count of independent origins, (ii) an enumerated denominator of opportunities, (iii) a refused singleton hypothesis, and (iv) a lineage-discriminating instrument explaining why the count is two rather than one. A bare number fails this term.

**Mode:** `general` for the binding, because it states what any answer must contain, not what the value is.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two conditions bundled into one question. Condition 1, a second biosphere is detected. Condition 2, it is independent of Earth life. By Fig 9, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Finding life clears condition 1 and leaves the intersection standing.
- **Symmetry:** the two conditions are not interchangeable. Shared ancestry is a common-cause collapse (Fig 2 panels on common cause), so "detected" does not imply "independent". The relation is ordered: detect, then discriminate lineage, then infer.
- **Scale:** the move is from one object (the Earth biosphere) to a population of opportunities (worlds in the Solar System and beyond). That is a membrane crossing (Fig 4), so a method, not a recording, must cross, and the round trip has a gap.

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Arrival, prebiotic chemistry | molecules, replicators, protocells, gradients, vents | chemical transitions, energy flow | yes: lab experiment and the geological record | partition, which step is being counted |
| D2 Inference, frequency estimation | observed origins n, opportunities N, rate fl | degrees of freedom, counts, posterior mass | yes: the actual count of independent origins | union compound, then narrowing |
| D3 Detection and lineage discrimination | missions, spectrometers, sequencers, biosignatures, genetic code | observations that separate shared source from convergence | yes: a measurement that pays | middle link, currently without an instrument |

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor. D1 pays in experiments (the transition from non-life to life has not been observed experimentally, per the Abiogenesis source). D2 pays in a count. D3 pays in a source-discriminating observation. If no such observation exists at any resolution, D3 does not become a domain with a different answer; it becomes `unreachable-at-this-budget`, which is a verdict, not a silence.

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. The question bundles "found life" with "independent of Earth life". A single lever (a detection) clears the first condition and leaves the compound. Fig 9 :55 names the residue: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Detect a biosphere (perception).
2. Have terms for what the detection affords: shared source versus convergent origin (vocabulary, the middle link).
3. Say what counts as progress here: a discriminated count, not a detection (objective).
4. Plan the instrument (planning).
5. Only then infer a frequency.

Effort spent at step 4 before step 2 arrives at a step that cannot use it. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 3. Findings

### F1 : Independence, not detection, is the governing quantity (D3)

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :31-32: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
Fig 2 :39-40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Two biospheres that share an ancestry are one event read twice. The second detection does not add a degree of freedom; it adds a second spelling of the same count. The Drake equation source (opened 2026-11-23) states the same condition in world terms: *"While this would raise the number of degrees of freedom from zero to one, there would remain a great deal of uncertainty on any estimate due to the small sample size, and the chance they are not really independent."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (independence and correlation), Fig 7 (agreement compounds), and Fig 8 (convergent versus adopted), with low overlap between the three figure arguments. The world source corroborates but is secondary.

**Refuter:** a lineage-discriminating measurement that establishes a separate origin (different genetic code, different handedness, different core molecular machinery beyond what convergence plausibly produces) gives independence; a measurement establishing shared ancestry leaves n = 1 and the frequency inference fails.

### F2 : The same reading cannot name its source (D3)

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

A second biosphere that looks biochemically like Earth's can be an independent convergence (same atoms, different arrangement history) or a common-source dispersion (panspermia). The reading "life detected" does not decide between them. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, opened 2026-11-23) names the mechanism: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not an argument from resemblance. The Panspermia source adds the operational warning: *"if biosignatures could be detected on two (or more) adjacent planets, that would provide evidence that panspermia is a potentially required mechanism for abiogenesis"*, not evidence of two independent origins.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: `triangulated`** across Fig 8 and Fig 12 for the record-dependence, with the essay and the Panspermia source as secondary corroboration.

**Refuter:** produce an instrument that discriminates source at the required resolution; if no such instrument could exist at any resolution, the verdict for this pair is `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not "life is common".

### F3 : Two related detections are not a second witness (D2)

Fig 7 :12-13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."*
Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

If the two biospheres are handed along one ancestry, each new detection is a room like the last. Adding worlds raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor. This is the same arithmetic as F1 read from the chain side.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 7 plus Fig 2), one evidence pool across the framework but two distinct figures.

**Refuter:** show that the two observations draw on no shared evidence source; if they do, the "two witnesses" reading is withdrawn.

### F4 : What the observation yields is a shape and a refusal, not a frequency (D2)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

A genuine second origin refuses one term we hold: "Earth life is the only origin", a singleton hypothesis. Fig 5 :53 names that kind of residual: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."* The refusal is a minted term, not an average. What survives is: at least two independent origins occurred, so the singleton branch is refused, and fl is bounded away from zero. The frequency value itself is a `gap` at the figure level. The figures return the shape any answer must have and stop; the essays say the same thing from the search side: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

**Mode:** `general` for the shape claim, `gap` for the number. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 plus the figures README, opened locally; the essay is secondary corroboration).

**Refuter:** a figure re-census that admits a numeric frequency inside closure(Γ) from two counts alone, or a ground measurement that returns the frequency directly, would clear the `gap`.

### F5 : Per-slot counting and the denominator (D2)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*
Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*

A count of origins is meaningless without the count of opportunities that were tried. Averaging over worlds discards exactly the signal the question asks for. The opportunities (Mars, Europa, Enceladus, Titan, early Venus) must be enumerated from the world, not composed from a theory of what "suitable" means. The Rule of succession source (mathematics, opened 2026-11-23) makes the same dependence formal: the estimate is a function of observed successes and trials, and its own page warns that it is *"not valid when s=0, or s=n"* under the total-ignorance prior. With one observed origin and one known opportunity the denominator is not enumerated.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 1 and Fig 11 are low-overlap figure arguments). The mathematics source is secondary and is declared as a different field.

**Refuter:** enumerate the opportunities and show the count is complete; if the habitat cannot be enumerated, the lower bound stands and the rate remains unidentifiable.

### F6 : Arrival is not survival, and a second origin is a second tree (D1)

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*
Fig 11 :56: *"nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*
Fig 10 :70: *"The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."*

Before a biosphere exists there is no Darwinian ground to evolve against; that is arrival. Once it exists, selection runs; that is survival. A second independent origin bears on the arrival step, not on the evolution of intelligence. Kipping (2020, opened 2026-11-23) predicts the same split: *"if we re-ran Earth's clock, one should statistically favor life to frequently re-emerge, but intelligence may not be as inevitable."* The Great Filter source adds: *"finding multicellular life on Mars (provided it evolved independently) would be bad news"*, because it would move the filter, not place it.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: `triangulated`** for the arrival/survival split (Fig 11 plus Fig 10), with two world sources as secondary corroboration. This verdict belongs to D1 only; it is not averaged into D2.

**Refuter:** show the second biosphere descends from the same last universal common ancestor, in which case it is a branch of the one tree and says nothing new about arrival.

### F7 : Not finding a second origin is not evidence of rarity (cross-domain)

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

Earth remains the only known instance. The Abiogenesis source says *"The transition from non-life to life has not been observed experimentally."* The Drake source records that searches for a shadow biosphere found none. Neither result licenses "life is rare"; both are non-detections inside an unsealed closure.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 8 plus Fig 13).

**Refuter:** none available inside the framework; this is a limit, not a finding that could be overturned by more of the same search. A new instrument could change what registers.

### F8 : The middle link currently has no instrument (D3, ordering)

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The chain currently stops at "detected" for Mars, Europa and Enceladus; the step "independent or shared" has no instrument that has paid. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Better telescopes aimed at detection cannot rescue the lineage link.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status: `single-source`** for the ordering claim (Fig 3 alone). This is recorded as single-source rather than dressed up as triangulated.

**Refuter:** name and run an instrument that returns a lineage verdict; the link then has an instrument and F8 is discharged.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the discipline that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, these were written here before any verdict was drawn. If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn.

1. **Shared ancestry.** A lineage test returns the same genetic code, the same handedness and the same core molecular machinery beyond what convergence plausibly produces. Then the second detection is one event read twice, n stays 1, and the frequency inference fails.
2. **No discriminator in principle.** A survey of instruments establishes that no measurement at any resolution can separate independent origin from common source for the relevant pair. Then the verdict is `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not "life is common" and not `gap`.
3. **One opportunity.** A ground measurement shows abiogenesis requires a condition realized exactly once among the enumerated opportunities. Then the lower-bound inference fails even with two detections, because the denominator collapses to one.
4. **Third-world failure.** Two independent origins are confirmed and a third suitable world with the same conditions remains sterile across the relevant window. Then the "fl close to 1" reading is refused.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and the refuter, not with "life is common". The independence condition is the thing a reader can check.
2. Enumerate the habitat before estimating anything (Fig 11 :43). A count without a denominator is not a frequency.
3. Treat the lineage instrument as the dependency break (Fig 3 :27). Detection alone cannot carry the inference.
4. Keep the three domain verdicts separate. Do not trade D1's arrival result for D2's number.
5. Stop at the shape and record the `gap`. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
