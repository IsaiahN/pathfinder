## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Union or partition?

Fig 9 :55: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

- Let A be "a second biosphere exists" and B be "it is independent of Earth life".
- A detection establishes A. It does not establish B.
- Therefore the residual is a **union compound** with its own loop, not one hard question and not a list of two separable questions.
- A single lever (better detection) clears A and leaves A and B standing together. This is Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Falsifier:** if a single instrument returns both "life present" and "lineage independent or not" in one measurement, the union unbundles into a partition and this finding is withdrawn.

### Q3 : What does "same biochemistry" mean?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two biospheres using the same set of molecules are **not** thereby the same event:

- If they are independent, they are Fig 12's isomers at the level of histories: same atoms, different arrangement route, different substance in the sense that matters for the frequency question.
- If they share a source, they are one substance with two spellings. `≡` identity applies: *"two names, one referent"* (Fig 12 :66).
- The ingredient list cannot decide between these. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier:** show that the two biospheres' arrangement histories are recoverable from their ingredient lists alone. Then the identity/convergence distinction collapses and this finding is withdrawn.

### Q5 : The order of refusal before mint
