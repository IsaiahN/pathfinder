## RESIDUAL

The question asks for "the correct account of information" far from equilibrium. Before a single account can be named we need to decide what makes two accounts the *same* account, what makes them different, what order they compose in, and which currency prices them. Three questions are owed: (1) are Shannon-style and thermodynamic-style accounts isomers under Fig 12, or merely members of a functional group; (2) what is the dependency order of the four bundled conditions; (3) can bits and kT ln 2 be added?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the accounts isomers, or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Write a Shannon account (source, channel, receiver; probability model over messages) and a thermodynamic account (energy, gradient, flow; probabilities over microstates). Their atom sets are not the same: one uses codes and probability models, the other uses energy and gradient. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers** as literally written. They are members of a **functional group**: both are "a measure over a distribution", with the qualifier saying which distribution.
- The stronger version: over the shared atom set `{spectrum, gradient, network, space, time, energy}`, where both accounts are written as cross-kind compositions of the six and differ only in which structure mediates which state pair, they **are** isomers (same atoms, different bond, different substance). That is the checkable structural claim, and it is unmeasured here.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either account can be written without an atom the other requires (e.g. a thermodynamic information measure with no Gradient, or a Shannon measure with no Spectrum/ordering), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q2 - What is the order of the four conditions?

- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces".
- Dependency order established in `01` §2: D1 (physical: gradient composing with energy into a flow) -> D2 (quantitative: the measure) -> D3 (frame-relative residual) -> D4 (bond-carried structure) -> D5 (verification).
- Fig 3 :25-27 "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress... attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." For this question, D3 (the frame) is the middle link: SEP *Information* records the long philosophical dispute over whether information is a property, a relation or both, with no shared instrument that settles it. A reading taken below that break is a reading of nothing.
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that settles the frame-question before the physical-gradient question, such that D3's output no longer depends on D1, dissolves this order and the chain must be re-censused.

### Q3 - Can bits and actions be added?

- Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them". Fig 12 :128 "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind." Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- The Shannon-style measure prices the description (bits). The Landauer-style cost prices settling a bit in kT ln 2 (actions/energy). They answer different questions: how long is the code versus what does it cost to reset the memory. Maroney's "which entropy?" and Wikipedia's "no a priori relationship between logical reversibility... and thermodynamic reversibility" corroborate at low overlap.
- **Verdict:** held apart. Not averaged, not summed, not a single "correct account". **triangulated** (Fig 12 + Maroney + Wikipedia).
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that one unit prices both without residue, with the conversion factor specified and checked at the ground.

### Q4 - Order of the union (the bond between the conditions)

- Fig 8 :54 "The atom cannot be derived from inside A. But the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable." Fig 9 :54 "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- The four conditions are first-order accounts of one six-term set. Their comparison is second-order and is licensed only after each first-order account is settled against its own ground. "Correct account" is a second-order demand made before the first-order settlements exist. That is the ordering error.
- **Falsifier:** a first-order account that is settled on all four conditions simultaneously, so the second-order comparison has nothing left to do.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate accounts are **not isomers as literally written**; they are members of a **functional group** (one measure-over-a-distribution shape with different qualifiers). The stronger isomer claim requires rewriting both over the full shared six-term atom set and is checkable with the falsifier above. The dependency order is physical -> quantitative -> frame-relative -> structural -> verification, with the frame as the unmeasured middle link. Bits and actions are two currencies that do not add. This rescues what is defensible in "there is one correct account" without naming one: the accounts share a shape, and their union is a comparison that must wait on first-order settlements.
