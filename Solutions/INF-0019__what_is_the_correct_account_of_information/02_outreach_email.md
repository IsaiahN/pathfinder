# Outreach draft - check one output before deciding whether the account is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send before the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §8 survives the reader's own ground check. The output must survive contact or the two-stage offer collapses.

**Subject:** Check one output about information far from equilibrium before you decide whether the account is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "the correct account of information in systems far from equilibrium" is not one question. It bundles at least four conditions: what information physically is when a system is held away from equilibrium, how it is quantified, what frame it is relative to, and how it is carried by arrangement. Each keeps working on its own subset. Figure 9 says disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." So I return a shape with four constraints, not a definition.
>
> The four constraints: (1) information is a residual measured per slot against a ground, not a global scalar; the figures say "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (2) Far from equilibrium is a gradient that can be spent, and "Energy and a gradient compose a flow"; the detail a crossing discards "reappears above it as noise." (3) Bits and thermodynamic cost are different currencies, "in different units", so a system holding one accounting will not notice the shortfall. (4) "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording", so the arrangement carries what an inventory misses.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that Shannon entropy, thermodynamic entropy, mutual information or algorithmic complexity is *the* residual; that the figures derive Landauer's bound; that there is one unified theory of information; or that anything here is settled by experiment. The figures cite Shannon, Kolmogorov and Rissanen as borrowed machinery with the source named, not as results of the substrate. The framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one measure shown against a ground to settle all four conditions with no residual left on any slot;
> 2. an account defined with no frame, ground or slot that still identifies which slot is live;
> 3. a far-from-equilibrium system with no spendable gradient, or a crossing whose discarded detail never returns as noise;
> 4. a single unit that prices description and settlement without residue;
> 5. two arrangements over the same atoms with different bonds shown identical on every ground test.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in the project, citation form `Fig N :line`).
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Information Processing and Thermodynamic Entropy" (Maroney): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information-entropy/
> - Wolpert, "Stochastic thermodynamics of computation", arXiv:1905.05669: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05669
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage offer, adapted

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the four constraints and the refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing anything else.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the reader is curious, offer the figure text, the canonical README and the two verified world links. State that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, also opt-in.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode and ground.
