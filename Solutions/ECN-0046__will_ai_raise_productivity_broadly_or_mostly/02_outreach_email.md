# Outreach draft: check the shape of the measurement first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim S and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0 and section 3 are stated in your own words. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One check on how AI productivity effects should be measured

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I am working on:** will AI raise productivity broadly, or mostly benefit a narrow set of firms and workers?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the answer to that question is not a single number. It is a distribution over slots (firms, workers, tasks) with a breadth threshold written down before anyone looks at the data. Two things have to be kept apart: how many tasks the technology can improve, and how many firms and workers actually come into contact with it and its complements. A better model with unchanged contact does not, on this account, change measured productivity. The shape says where to look and what would count as an answer. It does not say which answer is true.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming AI will be broad, and I am not claiming it will be narrow. I am not offering a magnitude, a date, or a number of jobs. Benchmark scores are not the verdict here; measured output per hour is. I am not asking you to trust a framework or to endorse a conclusion.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. Firm, worker and task decomposition adds no information over the mean for this question.
> 2. One condition accounts for all the dispersion, so the capability/diffusion split collapses.
> 3. A capability improvement that changes no measured contact changes measured productivity.
> 4. A frame-internal benchmark score settles the productivity question without a ground reading.
> 5. The verdict flips across reasonable breadth thresholds and that dependence is not reported.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The question I would value your verdict on:** what would you count as a failure of that shape, and what breadth threshold would you have written down before seeing the data? Please answer that before looking at anything I have written.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop and its mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - A well-identified study on the distribution question: https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local file. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Distribution discipline

One recipient at a time. No list sorted by likely agreement, no bulk send, no personal emails harvested from private posts. Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A room selected for receptiveness is a convention, not corroboration. Record the stage-1 verdict before the stage-2 offer is made, and keep the two records separate.
