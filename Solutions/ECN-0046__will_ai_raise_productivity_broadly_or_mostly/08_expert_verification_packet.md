# Expert verification packet: the Tether on broad versus narrow AI productivity effects

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist or productivity researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No empirical result is claimed. This is a shape and a measurement route, and this run is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The answer to "broadly or narrowly" is not a single productivity number. It is a distribution over firm, worker and task slots with a breadth threshold written down before measurement. Capability breadth and diffusion breadth are two conditions of a union, not one binary, and they need separate instruments. A capability improvement that does not change contact with complements and workflow does not, on this account, change measured productivity. Firm-level and worker-level breadth are different terms, so both options in the question can be true at once. The framework does not predict which answer the data will give; it states the form any checkable answer must have and the refuters that would kill it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The unit of the answer is the slot, not the aggregate

- **Statement:** any answer that reports only a mean cannot distinguish broad from narrow.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :13 **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**
- **What to check:** does the study or dataset actually resolve firm, worker or task slots, and does the breadth statistic depend on the distribution rather than being a deterministic function of the mean?
- **Refuter:** slot decomposition adds no information over the mean for this question. If that is demonstrated, C1 fails.

### C2 - The binary is a union of two conditions, and contact is load-bearing

- **Statement:** capability breadth and diffusion breadth are separate conditions; a capability improvement with unchanged contact leaves measured productivity unchanged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 11 :39 **"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40 **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
- **What to check:** in the rollout evidence you know, is the heterogeneity driven by task capability, by adoption and complements, or by both? Is there any case where a model improvement moved output with no observed change in workflow, data or management?
- **Refuter:** one condition accounts for all dispersion, or a contact-free capability change moves output. Either refutes C2.

### C3 - "Broad" is a pre-registered comparison, and the two scales are different terms

- **Statement:** the verdict depends on a threshold that must be fixed before the data are seen; firm-level and worker-level breadth are different terms, not two spellings of one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."** Fig 13 :76 **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."** The Operators table defines ⋛ as **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**.
- **What to check:** is a breadth cut stated, and is the result robust across reasonable cuts? Are firm-level and worker-level results reported separately, or blended into one headline?
- **Refuter:** the verdict is invariant to every reasonable cut, and one mechanism bounds both scale-specific statistics to the same value. Then C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that AI will be broad, and not that it will be narrow. No magnitude, date or job count.
- Not that benchmarks measure productivity. Benchmarks are frame-internal to the capability frame (Fig 1 :27).
- Not a rejection of aggregate statistics. They are the projection through which the ground is reported; their revisions are the round-trip gap.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this run.
- Not a second witness. This packet shares one evidence pool with its own audit; its internal agreement is consistency, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A slot-resolving instrument with a pre-registered breadth cut and a measured contact variable. Three things to look for:

1. **Distribution:** firm, worker and task level output effects with dispersion, not just a mean. The NBER study provides the template: *"issues resolved per hour"* with gains concentrated among novices.
2. **Contact:** randomized or staggered rollout with complementary investment measured, so the Fig 11 link is observed rather than assumed.
3. **Revision:** the official aggregate reported with its revision band. The BLS Q3 2026 release revises Q2 2026 from 1.4 to 2.1 percent, which is the round-trip gap made visible.
