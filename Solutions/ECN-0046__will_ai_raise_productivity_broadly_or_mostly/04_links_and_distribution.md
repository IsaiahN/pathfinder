## What the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The distribution is the finding.** The NBER study and its Digest summary both report large heterogeneity: gains for novice and low-skilled workers, little or none for the most experienced. That is a per-slot result in the language of Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* It does not by itself answer whether *firms* are affected broadly or narrowly; it shows what the right unit of measurement looks like.
2. **The aggregate and the distribution are different objects.** The BLS release supplies an aggregate that is revised; the firm and worker studies supply dispersion. Both are needed. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
3. **Simulations are good at direction and poor at distribution.** CEPR DP22205 is the cleanest independent corroboration of the framework's own warning that a simulated derivation cannot settle its own claim. It predicts exactly the failure mode this solution guards against: getting the direction right while misreporting subgroup differences.
4. **A gap in the search, stated honestly.** Attempts were made to open a well-known macro-level critique of AI's aggregate payoff and a major science-journal experiment; those pages returned 403 or 404 and were not cited. Their absence is recorded, not filled with a summary.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described the measurement sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a stage-1 verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what breadth threshold they would have written before showing them ours.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send list chosen for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) the question, what would count as a failure and what threshold they would have set; (3) record their stage-1 verdict; (4) only then, the three links, with the note that the figure text, not the summary, is the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual, the bound shape S and the five pre-registered refuters. Ask for a failure or a threshold. No theory, no links, no request for agreement.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their stage-1 verdict is already on record, so later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

**Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits this: sending a generator does not send an answer. Each user still needs a ground and a refuter of their own, because `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

## Hard precondition

Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is a shape and a refuter, not an empirical verdict, and it is explicitly labelled `specified` and simulated. If a recipient reads it as a claim that AI will or will not raise productivity broadly, the message has failed and should be withdrawn.
