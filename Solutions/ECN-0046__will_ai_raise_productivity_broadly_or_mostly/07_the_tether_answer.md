# The Tether's answer on broad versus narrow AI productivity gains

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No empirical result is asserted. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An instrument reports aggregate productivity growth. The question asks whether the change is spread across many firms and workers or concentrated in a few. The aggregate cannot answer this because the operation that produces it discards the distribution. Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** The question bundles two conditions (capability breadth and diffusion breadth), so it is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape

1. **The answer is a distribution, not a number.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Firm, worker and task slots must be measured separately, then aggregated. A flat aggregate is compatible with a few large winners and many flat slots; Fig 1 :13 calls this **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**.
2. **"Broad" is a threshold, not a group mean.** Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."** The cut must be written down before the data are seen, or the verdict will be chosen by the cut.
3. **Capability and diffusion are different conditions.** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** A better model clears only the capability condition. The diffusion condition (contact with complements, data and workflow) still binds.
4. **Capability alone does not move productivity.** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :39: **"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Broad versus narrow is primarily a question about the size and shape of the contact surface.
5. **Firm-level and worker-level breadth are different terms.** Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."** Both options in the question can be true at once at different slots. There is no forced choice between "broadly" and "mostly narrow".

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that AI will be broad, and not that it will be narrow. Both are verdicts owed from the ground.
- Not a magnitude, a date, a wage effect, or a job count.
- Not that benchmark scores measure productivity. They are frame-internal to the capability frame (Fig 1 :27).
- Not that the aggregate is useless. It is the projection through which the ground is reported, but its revisions are R_T (Fig 10 :24, Fig 4 :30).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An instrument that resolves slots and withstands the anchor test of Fig 2 :29-30. Concretely:

- **Distribution instruments:** firm-level and worker-level panels with output per hour by task, plus the pre-registered breadth cut. The NBER study opened for this run shows the form such an instrument takes: *"issues resolved per hour"* with heterogeneity across skill levels.
- **Contact instruments:** randomized or staggered rollout with measured complementary investment (data, process redesign, training). This is the Fig 11 contact variable. A capability-only measure does not observe it.
- **Revision bracket:** the official aggregate, reported with its revision band rather than as a point. The BLS release for Q3 2026 prints both the new estimate and the revised prior quarter.
- **Threshold robustness:** report the verdict across all pre-registered cuts. If it flips, the dependence is the finding.

**Standing refuters** (from `01` section 3): slot decomposition adds nothing; the union collapses to a partition; a capability change with no contact change moves output; a benchmark settles productivity without a ground reading; the verdict flips across cuts unreported.

## 5. The honest stop

**Underived by the figures, reachable with world instruments.** There is no figure line that yields the distribution itself, and more searching of the figures will not add one (Fig 8 :32). But instruments already return something at this resolution, so this is not an unreachable question. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. The essays add: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* This is not that output.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether AI will raise productivity broadly or narrowly; it says what an answer to that question would have to look like. The effect must be measured as a distribution over firms, workers and tasks, not as a single average, because averaging is precisely what hides the signal the question asks about. "Broad" is a threshold written down before the data are seen, and the threshold is a comparison, not a group mean. The question is really two conditions bundled together: how many tasks the technology can improve, and how many firms and workers actually come into contact with it and its complements. A better model with unchanged contact does not change measured productivity, so the diffusion link is where to look. Firm-level and worker-level breadth are different terms, so both halves of the question can be true at once. What is offered is the shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The empirical answer is left to the ground.
