# Breadth as a threshold, the union order, and two different slots

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question is written as a binary: broad or narrow. Two things are owed before it can be measured. (1) What kind of bond is "broad"? (2) In what order do the two conditions, capability and diffusion, depend on each other? A third question follows: are firm-level breadth and worker-level breadth the same term under a different name, or different terms?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: a slot (firm, worker or task), a measured effect per slot, a distribution over slots, a threshold, a capability, and a contact. Bonds held: the seven, including ⋛ comparison and → sequence. The ground is measured output per hour; the habitat is the set of firms, workers and complements in contact with the technology. No new primitive is introduced.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What kind of bond is "broad"?

Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

The Operators table defines ⋛ as **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** and adds *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.

"Broad" is therefore a ⋛ comparison, not a + conjunction of two group means. It compares a quantity of the distribution (for example, the fraction of slots above a minimum effect) against a threshold written down in advance. Without that cut, the word has no truth value and cannot fire as a refuter. This is the bound term S in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

**Falsifier for Q1:** show that "broad" is a bare conjunction of two aggregate means with no threshold, and that the verdict never depends on a cut. If so, the comparison analysis is idle and should be withdrawn.

### Q2 - What is the order of the union?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Dependency order: capability must exist at a task before contact can deliver it, and contact must occur before a slot's measured effect can be attributed. So the chain is capability → contact → measured per-slot effect → breadth comparison → verdict. A policy that only improves capability works on a link that already works in this story and cannot rescue a missing contact link. This is why adoption and complements are not an afterthought; they are the middle link Fig 3 :26-27 says attracts the least attention.

**Falsifier for Q2:** a measured case in which contact and complements are demonstrably irrelevant, so the chain collapses to capability → effect. The union would then be a partition and the ordering claim would be refuted.

### Q3 - Are firm-level breadth and worker-level breadth the same term?

Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

The figure's rule generalizes: the bond to the slot is part of the term. A breadth statistic over firms and one over workers share the same comparison operator but bond to different slots, so they are different terms, not two spellings of one. Finding diffuse gains over skill levels within a firm does not license a claim about dispersion across firms. Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Here the atoms are the same (a distribution and a threshold); the bond is to a different slot, so the substance differs. The two readings can move in opposite directions without contradiction. Adding them into one headline is the flat-formula error of Fig 12.

**Falsifier for Q3:** a single mechanism that bounds the two breadth statistics to the same value at both scales, so the slot distinction carries no information.

### Q4 - What does this measurement cost?

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Stating the shape is cheap: name the slots, the effect and the threshold. Settling it is expensive: a firm-level panel or a randomized rollout is a whole budget. A frame holding one accounting will propose breadth definitions it cannot afford to test, and the budget, not the elegance, decides how much of the space is reached. This is why the verification packet asks for a 20-minute desk check of the *shape*, not of the empirical result.

## CONCLUSION

The binary is a ⋛ threshold over a per-slot distribution, not a comparison of two aggregates. Its dependency order is capability → contact → per-slot effect → threshold → verdict, so the diffusion link is the one most likely to be missing and least likely to be instrumented. Firm-level and worker-level breadth are different terms bonded to different slots, so both options in the question can be true at once without contradiction. The description is cheap and the settlement is expensive; the two costs do not add.
