# Dual use, valence and order: supporting derivation for INS-0012

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit names two conditions, C1 (a maintained case-level channel to a ground) and C2 (a checking seat that cannot author what it checks), and claims the tools themselves change valence when the residual changes. Three questions are owed: (1) are service capacity and repressive capacity **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or the same molecule under a different bond; (2) what is the dependency order among the competing explanations; (3) what would a name, as opposed to a shape, require.

No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"*). Actors in D4: the checking seat, defined by what it cannot see.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are service capacity and repression isomers, or the same molecule under a different bond?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 11 :49-51: the same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds.

Consider a linked citizen record used by a benefits agency and the same linked record used by an enforcement unit.

- **Same atoms:** the record, the legal authority to hold it, the staff who read it, the data link.
- **Different bond:** in the first case the record is bonded to a benefit decision through a service objective; in the second it is bonded to a targeting decision through an enforcement objective. The operator joining the record to the outcome differs, and which one holds is not recoverable from the record itself.
- Under Fig 12 :117 the two are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. A register that names only the atoms (a "data-sharing system") cannot tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost. This is Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**But the stronger claim is not made.** Isomerism requires the same atom set. A benefits record and a targeting record may differ in which atoms are present (one includes an appeal route, the other does not). Where the atom sets differ, they are two members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124) rather than isomers. This is the weaker, safer reading: both are instances of the form `visibility → decision`, with different qualifiers in the decision slot.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single description of the instrument, naming only its atoms, predicts both outcomes with no reference to the bond or the residual, the isomer reading fails and the tools are one thing with two uses rather than two substances. The functional-group reading stands either way.

### Q2 - Why a list of "good administrative tools" cannot settle this

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
- Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*

A policy list ("build a registry, join the databases, train the staff") names ingredients. It cannot name the route from visibility to a fair outcome, because a route requires an ordered bond and a goal requires a comparison. The ICTD article's six governance dimensions are the same point in field language: legal clarity, oversight, recourse and inclusiveness are not substitutable ingredients; leaving one out changes the substance. So the answer cannot be a checklist of capacities, and the `gap` in F10 is not a gap in effort but a limit of ingredient vocabularies.

### Q3 - Dependency order among competing explanations

Four explanations compete for the same observation, "capacity grew and harm grew too". They are not rivals of equal rank; they sit at different links (Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*).

| explanation | link it addresses | instrument needed | figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| State cannot see (C1 failure) | 1 perception | case-level ground verification | Fig 3 :4-5; Fig 6 :23 |
| State has no terms for what the case affords (middle failure) | 2 vocabulary | a term for "this use is false" | Fig 3 :6-7; Fig 3 :27; Operators :58 |
| State cannot say what progress counts as | 3 objective | a comparison on fairness and harm | Fig 3 :9-11; Fig 12 :87 |
| Checker can author what it checks (C2 failure) | cross-link | external, non-authoring verifier | Fig 10 :42-43 |

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** (Fig 3 :29)

The usual reform failure is at link 2 or 3: the state has more data but no term for what the data affords the citizen and no comparison that counts harm as failure. That is where the project should look first, and it is exactly the link with no instrument (Fig 3 :27). **Falsifier:** a documented reform in which perception, vocabulary and objective all measure as working and the failure is located at planning or at the checker. That would falsify the "usually at the middle" claim while leaving the dependency ordering intact.

### Q4 - Is this a partition or a union, and what is the order of composition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

C1 and C2 overlap: checking is exercised inside the administrative loop, and the records that make service possible are the records that can be misused. The overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either condition alone. A single lever, "more oversight" or "more data", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

Order of composition: settle C1 first, because a checker cannot range a case outcome it cannot see; then build C2 over the settled visibility. This is Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** The checking seat composes over a visibility that has already been settled against the ground. It is not a larger version of the agency and not a replay of the agency's own review (Fig 10 :68, *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between"*). **Falsifier:** a case in which a checker reliably detects misuse of a visibility that was never settled at case level. That would reverse the dependency order and require a re-census.

### Q5 - What would convert the shape into a name?

- Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
- Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
- Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

A name would require a source-discriminating instrument: one that can distinguish, for the same administrative reading, honest implementation from suppressed complaint, from patronage, from an oversight body blind to its own agency. The source essay's metamerism point applies: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*. No such discriminator is held here. A state-level outcome statistic cannot do it, because it collapses the slots (Fig 1 :22). So the honest report is `gap` for the specific institution, and `specified` for the two-condition shape.

## CONCLUSION

The dual-use phenomenon is an isomer claim at its strongest and a functional-group claim at its safest: the same atoms under a different bond, or the same form with a different qualifier. Either way the difference is carried by the arrangement and the residual, not by the ingredient list, so no list of administrative tools is an answer. The dependency order puts perception and vocabulary before objective, and checking after a settled visibility. The whole is a union of two conditions with different instruments, and a single lever leaves the intersection standing. Converting the shape into a named institution requires a source-discriminating instrument that is not held here; that is a declared `gap`, not silence.
