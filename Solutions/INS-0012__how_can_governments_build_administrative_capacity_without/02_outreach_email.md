**Subject:** Check one output on state capacity before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about public administration. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** build the state's administrative capacity and the same move that improves service delivery and tax collection can also produce targeting, surveillance or patronage. The framework's rule is that the residual R = |prediction - outcome| is indexed per case, never aggregated into a national "capacity" score. So "capacity" is not one quantity. It is a family of case-level signals, and a blended score hides the live one.
>
> What follows from that is a conjunction of two conditions, kept separate:
>
> 1. **A maintained channel to a ground that settles each case.** Per-case outcomes have to be verified from a source the agency cannot move. Agency self-report is not an anchor, because it moves when the agency pushes on it.
> 2. **A checking seat that cannot author what it checks.** The same record that delivers a benefit can target a person, so the tool has no fixed valence. The capacity to refuse a use is structurally different from the capacity to act and has to be built as such.
>
> A state failing the first condition is blind. A state failing the second sees everything and may use it against people. Those are different compounds and need different instruments; one lever does not clear both.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular agency, oversight body, statute or staffing rule is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that this is settled by measurement. It is a `specified` prediction of a shape, and the specific institution that would fill the slot stays open until a ground settles it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. Per-case service and tax performance unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes citizen cooperation or the ground's payment for the agency's work.
> 2. An aggregate capacity index that predicts, prospectively and per named citizen, who will be harmed by a new power.
> 3. An agency-set metric that predicts independently verified case outcomes with no outside ground contact.
> 4. A state that renews its reach with no new atom from a differing closure and still passes the case-level test.
> 5. An administrative tool whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the residual.
> 6. Citizens harmed while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure measure high.
> 7. An oversight body that can author the decision it reviews and loses no detection power.
> 8. A single intervention that restores both conditions and leaves no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figures with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
