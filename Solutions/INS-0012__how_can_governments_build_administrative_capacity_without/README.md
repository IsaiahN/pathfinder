## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of a state's administrative capacity but *which slot's residual is shrinking*. In effect terms: R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`).

- **b** is the government's belief about what citizens and firms need and what will happen.
- **a** is what the state does: register, assess, collect, inspect, enforce, hire, promote, deliver.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the state's administrative library of routines, records, rules and staff predicts will happen.
- **o′** is what actually happens per case: the benefit arrives, the assessment is correct, the right is respected, no one is harmed, no job is given by favour.
- **s** is the citizen, firm or case. R is indexed per citizen and per case, never aggregated into a single national "capacity" score.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The question as posed assumes one lever, "capacity", with two side effects, one good and one bad. The figures refuse that framing. More information gathering, more staff, better records and stronger enforcement shrink R on the service and tax slots. The same moves can grow R on different slots: a person targeted, a favour dispensed, a right overridden. Those are different residuals with different anchors, and a single averaged "capacity" number hides the live one (Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "capacity is a stock the state owns and repression is its misuse": leads to the prediction that the trade-off is a matter of degree and can be averaged.
- Option B, "capacity is a set of slot-indexed relations that must be split, and one of the conditions is a checking seat that can refuse a use": leads to the prediction that the two effects have different anchors, different instruments and different failure modes, and that a blended capacity score destroys the diagnostic.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): **a conjunction of two conditions, (C1) a maintained channel to a ground that settles per-case service and tax outcomes, and (C2) a checking seat that cannot author what it checks, plus an instrument that discriminates a C1 failure from a C2 failure.** Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. Which specific institution settles the case is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the governance question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of state capacity.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable public administrators manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
