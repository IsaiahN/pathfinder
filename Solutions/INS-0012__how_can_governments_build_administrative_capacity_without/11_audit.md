## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19-22 (per-slot residual), Fig 11 :39-51 (capability and valence), Fig 10 :42-43 (checker non-access), Fig 9 :54-57 (union unbundling).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01` and `07`: capacity is not one stock; keep a case-level channel and a checking seat separate; tools change sign; one lever does not clear both.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** any concrete institutional answer, for example "create an independent audit body with statutory access to case data and a public correction procedure".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the anchor**, which can silently become a governance index rather than a verified case outcome;
- the **non-access rule**, which a real audit body often violates by being able to recommend or reverse the underlying decision;
- the **source-discriminating instrument**, which is absent; so the concrete design cannot be shown to name the source of a reading rather than the reading itself.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a case-level channel and a checking seat are different conditions with different failure modes, and a tool's valence is relative to the residual. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no case-level ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating a condensed formula as identical to the law set would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
