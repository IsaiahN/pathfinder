# WATSON answer: INS-0012, administrative capacity without repression or patronage

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

How can governments build administrative capacity without creating tools for repression or patronage?

Restated as an effect: why does the same capacity-building move shrink R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| on some case slots and grow it on others, and what would have to be true for the first without the second? R is indexed per citizen and per case, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: the independently verified case outcome, not agency self-report and not consensus (Fig 2 :29, :35). Option A: treat "capacity" as one stock whose misuse is a matter of degree, and average. Option B: split the union into a maintained case-level channel and a checking seat that cannot author what it checks. A leads to a blended score that hides the live harm slot; B leads to two conditions, two instruments and a recorded gap for the specific institution.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Administrative capacity built without repression or patronage is a conjunction of two kept-separate conditions: C1, a maintained channel to a ground that settles per-case outcomes from a source the agency cannot move; C2, a checking seat that cannot author what it checks and can refuse a use. The tools have no fixed valence, so they change sign with the residual and no list of administrative instruments is an answer. The two conditions have different instruments and different failure modes; a single lever clears one region and leaves the intersection. Which specific institution fills the checking slot is a `gap`, not a hidden answer. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. The world sources corroborate the mechanism but do not decide it; the figures do.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected reform. A state failing C1 would be sold oversight bodies it cannot feed with reliable case outcomes; a state failing C2 would be given more data and more staff, which increases its capacity to harm. A blended national capacity score would hide both. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible if the two conditions are kept separate.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The shape and its eight falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). No institution is named, so nothing has to be withdrawn by name.
- **reversed by:** any of the eight pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) per-case performance unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes citizen cooperation, (2) an aggregate index that prospectively predicts per-citizen harm, (3) an agency-set metric that predicts verified outcomes with no outside ground contact, (4) reach renewed with no import from a differing closure, (5) invariant tool valence across a residual change, (6) harm while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure measure high, (7) a checker that can author what it checks with no loss of detection, or (8) one lever restoring both conditions with no different residue. A genuinely independent evidence pool showing that per-case rights outcomes are fully predicted by averaged governance scores would also reverse it.
