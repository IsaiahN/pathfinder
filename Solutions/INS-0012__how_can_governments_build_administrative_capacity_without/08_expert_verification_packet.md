# Expert verification packet - the Tether on administrative capacity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public-administration researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No case-level institutional measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Administrative capacity is not a single stock. R = |prediction - outcome| is indexed per citizen and per case, never averaged into a national capacity score, so a data link or new inspectorate that closes the residual "the benefit did not arrive" can open the residual "a person was targeted or a favour was dispensed". Building capacity without repression or patronage is therefore a conjunction of two conditions kept separate: C1, a maintained channel to a ground that settles each case from outside the agency; C2, a checking seat that cannot author what it checks and can refuse a use. The tools have no fixed valence, they change sign with the residual, so no list of administrative instruments is the answer. Which specific institution fills the slot is not claimed; that is a declared gap. What is offered is the shape, two distinct failure modes and eight written refuters.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and method-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** R is per case, never a national average, and the anchor is the independently verified case outcome, not agency self-report. The question is a union of two conditions that must be unbundled.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Refuter:** an aggregate capacity or governance index that predicts, prospectively and per named citizen, who will be harmed by a new administrative power; or a demonstration that the two conditions are a partition rather than a union, such that one lever restores both with no categorically different residue.

### C2 - checker-facing; checkable by an auditor, ombudsman or oversight researcher

- **Statement:** the body that checks misuse must be unable to author what it checks, and the capacity to refuse a use is structurally different from the capacity to act.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43 - *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."* Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*; Fig 5 :53 - *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."*
- **What to check:** in a real oversight arrangement, does the checker have power over the underlying decision, and can it issue a refusal that binds the agency? Is refusal budgeted and staffed as a separate capacity, or is it assumed to arise from the same unit that delivers the service?
- **Refuter:** an oversight body that can author or reverse the decision it reviews and loses no detection power thereby; or a system that adds administrative powers with no refusal mechanism and in which measured harm does not rise.

### C3 - field-facing; checkable against known cases and data

- **Statement:** the same administrative instrument changes valence when the residual changes, so a tool cannot be certified safe by name. Quick failure is channel failure, not ground decay.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*
- **What to check:** find a case where a data-sharing system, register or inspectorate improved delivery and was later used for targeting or favour. Did the sign of the tool flip with the objective, or was the harm always latent in the same objective? The ICTD Robodebt discussion is one public instance.
- **Refuter:** an instrument whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual; or a case where citizens are harmed while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything. No final room; the specific institution stays open.
- Not that centralization is always wrong, or that any constitutional form is specified.
- Not that a national capacity or governance score can tell us which citizen is safe.
- Not that one lever achieves both conditions. The question is a union, not a partition.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an institutional measurement.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only true account of state capacity.
- Not a claim that any named oversight body, statute or merit system is the answer. Those are bindings, provenance owed.
- Not a claim that the world sources prove the shape. They corroborate; the figures decide.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*; a spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the colour case. Here:

1. **Per-case outcome test:** for a named citizen and a named act, was the benefit delivered and the right kept, verified by a source the agency cannot move?
2. **Valence test:** does the same tool's measured sign flip when the residual changes from "benefit failed" to "person targeted"?
3. **Checker non-access test:** can the checker author or reverse the decision it reviews, and does detection degrade if it can?
4. **Two-condition intervention test:** does one lever restore both conditions with no categorically different residue?
5. The eight standing falsifiers in `01` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :42-43, Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :117. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one oversight arrangement you know: can the checker author what it checks, and can it refuse?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate honest implementation from suppression or patronage for a single case, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
