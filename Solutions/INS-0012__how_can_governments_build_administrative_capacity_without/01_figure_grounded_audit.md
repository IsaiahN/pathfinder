## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the government's belief about what citizens and firms need and what will happen, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what the state does: register, assess, collect, inspect, enforce, hire, promote, deliver a benefit.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the state's library of routines, records, rules and staff predicts will follow.
- **o′** is what actually happens per case: the benefit arrives, the assessment is correct, the right is respected, no one is harmed, no job is given by favour.
- **s** is the citizen, firm or case. R is indexed by case and by time, never aggregated into a national "capacity" score.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is not how much capacity a state has but **why the residual falls on some slots and rises on others under the same capacity-building move**. A census, a register, a data link, a new corps of inspectors, a digital tax platform: each shrinks R where the state was blind, and each can grow R where the state now sees too much or rewards the wrong person. Call the first set of slots `S_service` and the second `S_harm`. The question asks for a way to move the first without moving the second.

State the residual as: *why does a state's improved prediction of citizens and firms sometimes coexist with, or produce, harm to those same citizens, and what would have to be true for the first to hold without the second?* This is an effect description. It names no theory, no "checks and balances", no "rule of law", no "civil service merit". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a conjunction of (C1) a maintained channel to a ground that settles per-case service and tax outcomes, and (C2) a checking seat that cannot author what it checks, together with an instrument that discriminates a C1 failure from a C2 failure. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 4 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names candidates: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* For this question the anchor is the independently verified per-case outcome: did the benefit arrive, was the assessment correct, was a person harmed, was a post filled by favour. Agency self-reports and expert consensus move when pushed, so they are instruments at best. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Viable anchor: case-level outcome verified by a source the agency cannot move.

**Strain S2, named here and resolved in section 6.** "Citizen preference" moves with culture and is therefore not an anchor. The resolution is Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Preferences are habitat; the settled case outcome is ground. The anchor is not "what people say they want today" but whether the promised benefit arrived and the right was kept.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a three-place relation: state Γ, action a, citizen outcome o′, indexed by slot s and ordered by time t. It is a sequence rather than a set, so it carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) and a **comparison** bond for "correct" or "impartial" (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87). It is not symmetric: the state and the citizen are not interchangeable.
- **Symmetry.** "Without repression or patronage" is a threshold and a prohibition, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :85-89; Operators table :46-51). Harm is asymmetric in time: a job given by favour is not undone by reversing the appointment. The negation matters: Operators table :58, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single agency's step-by-step loop (Fig 4 one-agent panel), the population of citizens and firms with *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5), and the time channel across successive administrations. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The administrative loop** (one agency, one scale) | officials, routines, records, the agency's Γ | actions and residual reduction per case | yes: the verified case outcome | admitted |
| **D2 The political habitat** (population and power) | citizens, firms, officials, politicians, courts, media, auditors | compliance, trust, legitimacy, survival of the government | yes: independently verified citizen or firm outcome; the public as ground (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| **D3 The reach library** (what the state can see and do) | atoms (records, legal authority, staff skill, data) and the bonds between them | reach (what can be composed and done), |φ| cost | yes: settlement, *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"* (Fig 5 :32) | admitted |
| **D4 The checking seat** (oversight and separation) | auditors, ombudsman, courts, legislature, free press | contact with the ground; ability to refuse a use | yes: the ground, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 Administrative capacity as a single stock the state owns** | the state | capacity | **no** anchor independent of the per-case outcomes | not a domain yet |
| **X2 Repression as mere overuse of capacity** | the same instruments as D1 and D3 | harm | measurable, but this is already the valence of D3 actors inside D2 and D4 (Fig 11 :49-51) | absorbed, not separate |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that state capability is unreal. X2 fails the separate-domain test: the same record that delivers a benefit can target a person, so repression is a relation, not a different kind of instrument.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: Γ is inside the agency loop, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D2 and D4 overlap: checking is exercised inside the political habitat, so that overlap is a second compound. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: **(C1)** maintained contact with a ground that settles per-case service and tax outcomes, and **(C2)** a checking structure that keeps the state's visibility from being used for targeting or favour, able to refuse a use. A state failing only C1 (blind, cannot deliver) and a state failing only C2 (sees everything, uses it against people) are not different degrees of one failure. They are different compounds and need different instruments. The Library of Babel essay states the same stop condition: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the state see the citizen and the local condition?) → 2 vocabulary (does Γ have terms for what citizens afford and need?) → 3 objective (can it say what would count as progress, namely a benefit delivered fairly and no harm?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. Most reform effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). The ICTD opinion on tax data makes the same point in the field: *"citizens need clearer ways to understand and challenge how their data is used."*

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about administration, `grounded` only if settled by an institutional measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: Capacity is a property of state-and-habitat, not of the state alone

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low overlap: habitat valence vs membrane size rule).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

A state's ability to collect, deliver and see is not located in the state considered alone. Adding staff, software or legal powers is an agent-only improvement unless it changes the state's contact with the people and conditions that settle the case. Fig 11 :72 gives the standing refuter in the figure's own words: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim.

**Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which per-case service and tax performance is unchanged across a habitat substitution that demonstrably removes citizen cooperation or the ground's payment for what the agency does. If it survives, F1 is false.

**World bearing (secondary only):** the Wikipedia synthesis of state capacity reports that *"State capacity is distinct from political control, as the latter refers to the tactics that states use to gain compliance from society"* (Hassan, Mattingly and Nugent 2022, cited there). That is a field-level corroboration of the per-slot split between delivering and coercing. It is one witness, reading from the same broad political-science pool as the other governance sources.

### F2: Never explain this by averaging across citizens or agencies

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for method. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap).**

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)

A single "state capacity index" scores the frame with a quantity the frame also produces. It is a filter for where to look, not a witness. The specified consequence: any answer to INS-0012 that blends service delivery, tax collection and rights protection into one score has already thrown away the signal that says which citizen is being harmed and where.

**Refuter:** an aggregate capacity index that predicts, prospectively and per named citizen or case, who will be harmed by a new administrative power. If one exists, F2's methodological consequence is false.

**World bearing:** the critique of the World Wide Governance Indicators reports that the six indexes *"do not discriminate usefully among different aspects of governance"* and that they show *"Lack of actionability"*, giving the example of a rule-of-law indicator about how secure business people feel rather than *why* they feel that way. That corroborates the anti-aggregation and source-discrimination warning. It is a measurement critique, not a verdict about any country, so it is used as a filter and one witness (Fig 9 :60).

### F3: The anchor must not update; agency self-report is frame-internal

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 1, low overlap: anchor rule vs frame-internal quantities).**

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :30)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)

If the agency's own metric of "capacity built" rises whenever the agency pushes on it, it is not an anchor. The anchor is the case outcome verified from outside the agency: the benefit arrived, the debt was correctly raised, the appeal was heard. A regulator that grades its own regulatory quality is panel 1 of Fig 2, not panel 3.

**Refuter:** an agency-set metric that predicts independently verified case outcomes prospectively, across a change of administration and without ground contact from the checker. If it does, F3's anchor requirement is too strong.

**World bearing:** Ogembo and Niesten (ICTD, 2026) write that *"taxpayers, courts, and oversight bodies cannot check rules or assessments that are not public"* and that *"Success should be assessed through both administrative results and taxpayers' experience"*. That is a field-level corroboration from outside the framework, but it shares the public-administration evidence pool with F5 and F7, so it is one witness across those findings.

### F4: Renewal of reach requires contact with a differing closure

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 13, three figures with distinct roles).**

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)
> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** (Fig 8 :32)
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** (Fig 13 :115)

A state can optimise inside its own library forever and never add an atom. To see local conditions it must take in records, reports and challenges from closures that differ from its own: citizens, firms, local knowledge, audit, courts, other agencies. Scott's *Seeing Like a State* supplies a low-overlap warning from history and political ecology: state simplification *"blinds the state to the complex interactions which happen within nature and society"*, and *"the eradication of local differences and silencing of local expertise can have adverse effects."* The ICTD authors make the operational version: *"Implementation also requires listening to those who use and are affected by these systems."*

**Refuter:** a state that renews its reach solely by composing inside its own closure, with no new atom entering from a differing closure, and that still passes the per-case ground test across a change of habitat. That breaks idempotence and F4 with it.

### F5: The same instrument changes valence when the residual changes

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations vs composition structures).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)

This is the dual-use claim stated in effect terms. A data link, a register or a new inspectorate is a benefactor to the residual "benefit did not arrive" and a malfactor to the residual "person was targeted or a favour was dispensed". Nothing in the instrument's description records which it is. Repression and patronage are not different kinds of tool; they are relations between a tool and a different residual. Hence X2 was absorbed into D2 and D4 in the census.

The world corroborates directly. Ogembo and Niesten write *"More connected data means more public power"*, and: *"Data availability should not be treated as proof that is use is lawful, appropriate, or sufficiently reliable."* Their Robodebt case is the figure's valence switch observed: information collected for one purpose was reused for another, producing around 470,000 wrongly raised debts, which the Royal Commission found *"neither fair nor legal"*. This is a single witness from the tax-administration field, not three.

**Refuter:** an administrative instrument whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the state's residual. If no such change of valence can be found anywhere, F5 weakens to `general`.

### F6: Quick failure is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the ground

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 7, low overlap: ground maintainer vs room chain).**

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)
> **"you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :28)
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"** (Fig 10 :29)

A state loses the ability to govern fairly when its contact with what settles the case degrades while the ground itself keeps settling other cases. The named decay modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings. "Pooled readings" is the administrative version of aggregating across slots (Fig 1 :22).

Fig 7 adds the two-factor test:

> **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"** (Fig 7 :37), **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"** (Fig 7 :38), **"the ground's pressure on the room"** (Fig 7 :39)

A decade of legitimate administration is a chain of handoffs across elections and administrations. If either the bracket's fidelity or the ground's pressure goes to zero, the state runs in place. Ogembo and Niesten describe the channel failure exactly: *"an error in one institution may also travel to other institutions and influence further decisions"*, and *"While the administration gains a wider view of the taxpayer, the taxpayer may see only the resulting demand."*

**Refuter:** a documented case in which citizens are harmed while bracket fidelity and ground pressure are independently measured as high and non-declining. If both stay high, F6's mechanism is not the cause.

### F7: The checking seat must be unable to produce what it checks

**Mode: `general` structural, `specified` in application. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2, low overlap: verifier non-access vs anchor triangulation).**

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** (Fig 10 :42)
> **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."** (Fig 10 :43)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)

The body that checks whether an administrative power is being misused must not be the body that can author the decision being checked. If it can, it has reproduced the frame inside itself and its clearance is a convention that has decided an outcome. This is why D4 is a separate domain with its own anchor rather than an extension of D1. The figure's own constraint applies to this derivation too: the checking seat is a position defined by what it cannot see, not by who occupies it.

The field corroborates in operational language: *"Independent oversight bodies need the powers, expertise, and resources to examine whether those limits are respected"*, and *"meaningful human review requires staff who can question the output and reconsider the case"* (Ogembo and Niesten 2026). The ASPA *Code of Ethics* and public-service framing of *"effective, ethical and equitable governance"* show the profession's own venue-level statement of the norm, but carry no verdict weight.

**Refuter:** an oversight body that can author or reverse the underlying decision it reviews and loses no detection power thereby. Under Fig 10 :42 that should be impossible; if observed, F7 is wrong.

### F8: A vocabulary that can only accumulate cannot refuse a use

**Mode: `general`. Domains D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 5 amendment and Operators table, two figures with distinct roles).**

> **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"** (Fig 5 :53)
> **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."** (Fig 5 :55)
> **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"** (Operators table :58)

A state that only adds powers, records and metrics has no term for "this use is false, stop". Negation is a unary operator; it is not a seventh bond (Fig 12 amendment, Operators table :70-78). The practical consequence is that the capacity to refuse a use is structurally different from the capacity to act, and must be built as such. The economics literature supplies a low-overlap corroboration: the principal-agent problem *"worsens when there is a greater discrepancy of interests and information between the principal and agent, as well as when the principal lacks the means to punish the agent"*, and the *"multiple principal problem is particularly serious in the public sector."*

**Refuter:** a system that adds administrative powers with no mechanism to refuse a use and in which measured harm does not rise across a documented change of residual. That would weaken the negation requirement to `general` bookkeeping.

### F9: The union must be unbundled before a lever is chosen

**Mode: `specified`. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, but the essay is framework-internal, so the external witness count is one).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 (quoted in section 1.3) are the governing lines. The two conditions have different instruments:

- A C1 failure shows up as a case outcome that does not arrive, a closure that cannot see local conditions, a bracket that no longer matches the residual (Fig 10 :26, Fig 7 :38, Fig 6 :21).
- A C2 failure shows up as a tool whose valence has flipped, an oversight body that can author what it checks, a record that travels without a rule for its reuse (Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 10 :42).

A single lever, for example "hire more inspectors" or "join up the databases", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55). The ICTD article's six governance dimensions, *"from legal clarity and oversight to recourse and inclusiveness"*, are a bundle of this kind: a data-sharing agreement without a correction procedure fixes visibility and leaves recourse unaddressed.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. Per-case service and tax performance is unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes citizen cooperation or the ground's payment for what the agency does (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. An aggregate capacity index predicts, prospectively and per named citizen, who will be harmed by a new administrative power (breaks F2, Fig 1 :22).
3. An agency-set metric predicts independently verified case outcomes prospectively with no ground contact from the checker (breaks F3, Fig 2 :29).
4. A state renews its reach with no new atom from a differing closure and still passes the per-case ground test across a change of habitat (breaks F4, Fig 8 :32).
5. An instrument's measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual (breaks F5, Fig 11 :49-51).
6. Citizens are harmed while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining (breaks F6, Fig 7 :37-39).
7. An oversight body that can author or reverse the decision it reviews loses no detection power thereby (breaks F7, Fig 10 :42).
8. A single lever restores both conditions and leaves no categorically different residue (breaks F9, Fig 9 :57).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until an independent, case-level ground measurement has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of public administration (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep the two conditions separate in every report. A blended "capacity and integrity score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Do not publish a single national capacity number as if it settled which citizen is safe. Use such numbers as a budget filter only (Fig 9 :60).
4. Ask, at each reform handoff, which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
5. Treat any list of administrative tools as Γ, that is, as a candidate library whose reach and valence must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27, Fig 11 :51).
6. Build the power to refuse as a separate capacity, not as an afterthought inside the power to act (Fig 5 :53, Operators :58).
7. Use the governance literature as one corroborating witness and a budget filter, never as the verdict. The Wikipedia governance pages, Scott, and the ICTD opinion draw on overlapping public-administration pools (Fig 2 :31, :40).
8. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
9. Stop at the shape. Do not name which agency, which oversight body or which statute is the answer; the framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
