# Constant versus evolving dark energy: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The literature treats "constant w" and "w0-wa evolution" as competing fitted models. Two structural questions are owed: (1) do they differ by an atom or by a bond, and (2) what is the dependency order of the observations that could decide between them?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. A constant dark-energy term and an evolving one both compose from the same six atoms if the only difference is whether a gradient is bonded to Time.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the difference an atom or a bond?

- Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- Constant Λ: Energy bonded to Time with no gradient over time. A first-order cross-kind composition.
- Evolving component: Energy bonded to a Gradient bonded to Time. Also a first-order cross-kind composition, with an extra structure operand.
- Both are legal. So the difference is a **bond**, not a new atom. No import is needed to state the distinction.

**Falsifier written before the run:** if either model requires a substrate member the other does not (a seventh term, or the removal of one of the six), the difference is an atom difference, not a bond difference, and this derivation fails.

### Q2 - Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- If both are written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}` and differ only in whether Gradient is bonded to Time, they are **isomers** under the figure's definition: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour (constant density versus varying density).
- If they are written as the literature writes them (ΛCDM with one parameter and w0waCDM with two), they share only the observable expansion history and differ in the number of fitted parameters. That is not the figure's isomer test. It is a **functional group** at best: the group says "dark energy", the qualifier says which model.
- **Falsifier:** if the per-epoch variation can be entirely removed by a pivot reparameterization that changes the reported mean offset from -1 while preserving the fit, the two are different spellings of one term under the same bond, not isomers. Cortes and Liddle's pivot analysis is exactly this kind of check: they find the evidence lives in the derivative of w rather than its mean offset. That does not by itself break the isomer claim, because the presence or absence of the gradient bond is structural, but it does mean the fitted significances are not a reliable reading of the bond. Recorded as a strain, not hidden.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- The chain for this question: (1) perceive the expansion per redshift bin; (2) hold a vocabulary term for w(z); (3) state what counts as progress, namely a residual that persists and does not trend down across frames; (4) plan which observation reduces the residual; (5) carry the result to the next epoch.
- The middle link (2 to 3) is where this question currently strains. We have a vocabulary term (w0, wa) and a statistic (sigma), but the objective, "persistence under frame change", is not yet an instrument. More high-z data optimises a link that already works (1) while the middle link stays uninstrumented.
- **Falsifier:** an instrument that directly measures the persistence of the residual across frames, with a stated threshold, would move link 3 from assumed to measured. Until then, any claim that "more data will settle it" is a reading taken below the break.

### Q4 - What does the low-overlap analogue license?

- The Noether result (Baez 2020) states: *"Time translation symmetry gives conservation of energy; space translation symmetry gives conservation of momentum, rotation symmetry gives conservation of angular momentum, and so on."*
- This licenses describing the constant case as time-translation symmetric at the level of the term and the evolving case as broken symmetry. It does **not** license concluding that the symmetry is broken in nature. A symmetry principle is a structure, not a measurement. Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Here the mathematics and the cosmology arrive at the same distinction from different fields, which is why the analogy is worth keeping, but the analogy carries no verdict.
- **Falsifier:** a derivation from symmetry principles alone that picks one of the two models would be a category error under Fig 13 :115 (*"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*). If it appeared, it would falsify the claim that this is a ground question.

### Q5 - Does the settled-molecule rule help?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
- ΛCDM is settled over decades of observation and can serve as an atom at the next level. The evolving models are not settled. So a second-order composition between them is not available yet: one operand has not been admitted. That is a structural reason, independent of the statistics, for refusing to composite them into a single "consensus" model now.
- **Falsifier:** a ground reading that settles one of the evolving models, making it available as an atom for a next-level composition.

### Q6 - Round trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- Sent up: the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 13 :76 (gradient over time is a different term), Fig 12 :117 (isomer) and Fig 9 :60 (filters versus witnesses).
- Abstracted: "constant versus evolving is a bond-to-Time distinction, and significance levels are filters."
- Brought down: the concrete world claim "DESI shows evolving dark energy at 2.8 to 4.2 sigma".

R_T is nonzero. Three things the abstraction holds do not survive the trip: the prior dependence of the signal (Cortes and Liddle), the shared evidence pool among the datasets (Fig 2 :40), and the unresolved high-redshift channel (Fig 6 :34). One thing survives cleanly: the structural distinction between a term with a gradient bonded to Time and one without. That is the residue worth sending. R_T is recorded qualitatively; no numerical round trip was measured because no ground reading exists.

## CONCLUSION

Constant and evolving dark energy differ by a bond to Time, not by a substrate atom, and under the full shared atom set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. Their dependency chain currently stops at the objective link, because "persistence under frame change" has no instrument yet. The Noether analogy licenses the symmetry description but not a verdict. The second-order composition is unavailable because one operand is unsettled. Everything specific about which microphysical field fills the slot remains an import with no source gate.
