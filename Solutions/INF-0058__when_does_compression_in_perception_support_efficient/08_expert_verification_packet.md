# Expert verification packet: compression in perception

**What this is:** a claim in a form a perception or neuroscience researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural separators, not `grounded`.** No experiment or dataset is claimed to have settled anything. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Compression in perception cannot be scored by the amount of compression achieved, because that is a quantity the frame produces and uses to score itself. The surviving boundary is four conditions: the residual stays indexed per object slot; the round trip from signal to compressed description and back is measured and its gap reported; the description cost plus the residual left unexplained is less than the uncompressed residual, with a one-occasion fit refused; and the typed relations between features are retained or their loss recorded. Compression supports cognition when a ground that does not move pays for the result under those conditions. It distorts systematically when the description shrinks while one condition is silently lost, through an atom that no further search in the same closure can recover, a dropped transient in a decaying channel, or coherent drift that a same-pool mirror cannot correct.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The self-score is disqualified, and the readout must stay per slot

- **Statement:** a bits-saved score cannot decide whether compression helps. The witness is a per-slot residual against an anchor that does not update.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :25 *"coverage, and compression achieved"*; Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*; Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** in your preparation, does a global reconstruction-quality score locate the failing region after compression as reliably as a per-slot readout? Does the "ground" you would use actually stay fixed when the observer changes its mind?
- **Refuter:** a frame can settle the question using only its own compression ratio with no ground reading; or a pooled readout locates the failing slot as well as a per-slot readout at the same ratio.

### C2. The round trip and the bargain are both required

- **Statement:** a compression is licensed only if the abstraction concretisation gap is measured and the MDL bargain is paid. A perfect fit to one occasion is refused.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*; Fig 4 :31 and :32 *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."*; Fig 5 :43 *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."*; Fig 5 :44 *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*
- **What to check:** is there a defined concretisation from the compressed description back to the original situation? Is the gap reported anywhere, or only the rate? Does the compression survive a second scene that the first fit did not see?
- **Refuter:** the transform is reversible at that level so the gap is zero; or a term whose cost plus leftover exceeds the uncompressed residual is retained and nevertheless pays at the ground.

### C3. Bond retention separates the two outcomes, and lost atoms need import

- **Statement:** over a fixed atom set, an efficient compression and a distorting compression are isomers: same features, different typed relations, different substance. If the loss is an atom rather than a bond, more search inside the same closure cannot recover it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*; Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*; Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*; Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** does a bond-preserving compression at equal bit cost outperform a feature-only compression on a task where the relation matters? If the missing detail is absent from the feature vocabulary altogether, has anyone proposed a new instrument or an imported atom, rather than more training on the same compressed material?
- **Refuter:** a feature-only description behaves identically to a bond-preserving one on a relation-sensitive task at the ground; or repeated search inside the same compressed closure recovers the missing atom and pays.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal ratio, threshold, bit budget or feature list.
- Not that compression is harmful, or that more detail is always better. The lossy transform is what makes a representation reapplicable.
- Not a named optimal code. Sparse coding, predictive coding and rate coding are bindings from your field with provenance owed, not figure derivations.
- Not that the six-term physical substrate governs perceptual features. That mapping was tried and withdrawn; only the general state/structure and discarded-detail-as-noise claims are used.
- Not that pooled expert agreement is corroboration. Agreement from one evidence pool is one witness.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet has been run.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument that discriminates at the slot, not a further argument. Three candidate checks are already proto-instruments: change detection (does a localizable transient survive compression?), metamerism matching (does the compressed match fail under a changed illuminant or observer?), and an explicit abstraction concretisation round trip (what does the gap hold?). Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, :25 and :27, Fig 4 :30 to :32, Fig 5 :43 and :44, Fig 12 :5 and :117. Eight short passages.
2. Say whether the bits-saved score is used as a verdict anywhere in your field. If it is, name the ground that backs it.
3. Check C3 against your own preparation: is there a known case where two compressed representations share features and differ in a relation, with different behavioral outcomes?
4. Name one proto-instrument already returning something at the slot but failing to resolve it. If none exists, say so; that is a `gap`, not a defeat.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
