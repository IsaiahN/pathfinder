# The Tether's answer: when classifications reveal and when they obscure

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the operational tests, not `grounded`.** This is a set of conditions and a route, not a verdict about any particular taxonomy. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A classification is a partition of a domain into classes. The residual is the difference between two outcomes that can coexist: a cut that runs along ground-paying differences (reveal) and a cut fixed by the classifier's frame that hides the variation it claims to organise (obscure). The two are not a single question with a single answer, because a class can reveal at one scale and obscure at another. Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the classification frame.**

1. **A class must pay the description-length bargain.** Fig 5 :42 "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold." Stating the classes plus what remains unexplained must cost less than the confusion they remove. A class that explains everything by saying nothing fails.
2. **A class must preserve the bond and the slot.** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A feature list and an average each drop a different half of what mattered.
3. **A union must not be worn as a partition.** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Overlapping conditions left in one bin leave their intersections standing.
4. **A frame cannot certify its own classes.** Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

**D2, the ground.**

5. **Reveal requires an anchor that does not move when classifiers agree.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
6. **Independent frames, not repeated frames.** Fig 8 :48-49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Convergence at maximum independence is genuine corroboration, but only the record of where each came from separates it from an adopted cut (Fig 8 :50-52).
7. **A discriminating instrument, not a better name, closes a class.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument "cannot comment on the source"; "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case.

**D3, the users and verifiers.**

8. **Keep the convention verdict and the truth verdict apart.** Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"; Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
9. **A convention becomes a distortion at the moment it decides an outcome.** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." This is the precise tipping point the question asks for.
10. **No final classification.** Fig 7 names "the limit: no last room"; Fig 9 :41 "so keep every cut ranked and reversible"; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,".

## 3. The one-line answer

A classification reveals meaningful patterns when its cuts are ground-anchored by independent frames, preserve bonds and individual cases, form a true partition, and cost less than the confusion they remove. It imposes divisions that obscure complexity when it is a feature list or an average, a union worn as a partition, a self-certifying closed vocabulary, or a convention that has been allowed to decide an outcome. Because these conditions live in different domains, the verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named taxonomy is correct. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that there is a final or best classification. There is no last room and every cut is reversible.
- Not that agreement among experts makes a cut real. Correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention.
- Not that more categories means finer truth. Arrangements are unbounded, kinds are not (Fig 13 :115-118).
- Not that the four conditions are measured. They are `candidate` until a ground pays.
- Not that this derivation settles anything. It is a simulation checked for internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

- An instrument that discriminates whether a class boundary tracks a real joint, so the classes support novel ground-paying predictions on held-out slots.
- A demonstration that a flat ingredient-only classification resolves bonded differences without an added relation (would break F3).
- A demonstration that a single-frame, ungrounded classification reliably predicts across domains (would break F1 and F5).
- A demonstration that a convention deciding an outcome improves contact with the ground rather than only coordinating action (would break F5).
- A derivation of a new kind by rearrangement alone inside one closed vocabulary (would break F6).

Agreement, authority and the number of people who use a taxonomy are not such instruments.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Each class is "a human's careful guess" at where a difference lives; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A classification is not simply true or false. It reveals a pattern when four things hold: its cuts are checked against something that does not move when the classifiers agree; the cut keeps the arrangement and the individual case rather than a feature list and an average; it is a real partition rather than several overlapping conditions folded into one bin; and stating the classes costs less than the confusion they remove. It obscures complexity when any of these fail, and most of all when a convention that is useful for coordination is allowed to decide an outcome. The same scheme can reveal at one scale and hide at another, so verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. What is offered is the shape a good classification must have, four refuters written in advance, and the kinds of instrument that would close it. The final taxonomy stays open until a ground settles it.
