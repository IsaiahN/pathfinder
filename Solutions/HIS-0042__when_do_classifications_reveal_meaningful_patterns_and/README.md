## What is being decided, in effect terms

A classification is a partition of a domain into classes. The question asks what makes such a partition earn its keep. Two outcomes are possible and they can occur at once in different domains:

- **Reveal:** the cut runs along differences that do work against a ground, so members of a class behave alike on the axes that matter, and the classes support induction, prediction and intervention.
- **Obscure:** the cut is fixed by the classifier's frame (a shared ingredient list, a convention, an algorithm that always returns groups), so members of a class differ on the axes that matter, and the classes hide the variation they claim to organise.

What an answer would look like: a set of conditions that separate the two outcomes, stated per domain, with a refuter written before the run. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` section 6 so it can accrue a residual like anything else.
