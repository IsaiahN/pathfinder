# Outreach draft: check one claim about a classification before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this email carries a claim about classification, not a request to adopt a framework. If a reader cannot tell what would refute it, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** A testable claim about when a classification hides more than it shows

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one claim about classification that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the tests, not `grounded`):** a classification reveals a real pattern only when its cuts are ranged against something that does not move when the classifiers agree, when it preserves the arrangement and the individual case rather than a feature list and an average, when it is a real partition rather than several overlapping conditions folded into one bin, and when stating the classes costs less than the confusion they remove. The same classification starts to obscure complexity at the exact moment a convention, useful for coordination, is allowed to decide an outcome. One scheme can reveal at one scale and obscure at another, so the verdicts have to be kept separate.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular taxonomy is the correct one, that there is a final classification, or that agreement among experts makes a cut real. The framework returns the shape a good classification must have, not the name of the right one. Agreement is efficient for a shared meaning and disqualifying as evidence about the world when the agreeing parties draw on the same material.
>
> **What would refute the claim (written before I ran this):**
> 1. a classification drawn from a single frame, with no ground reading, that reliably supports novel ground-paying predictions across domains;
> 2. a feature-list classification that discriminates bonded isomers or per-slot signals on a ground-paying axis, without an instrument that resolves the bond or the case;
> 3. a convention that decides an outcome and is shown to improve contact with the ground rather than only coordinate action;
> 4. a genuinely new kind derived by rearrangement alone inside one closed vocabulary.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture (Medium): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a classification you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the claim, the four refuters and the request for a failure. Record the reader's verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, if asked.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Nothing here is sent by this agent. The reader hosts and sends.
