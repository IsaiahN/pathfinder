## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what distinguishes a classification that reveals a pattern from one that imposes divisions hiding complexity. Option A: treat the question as a single search for the correct partition. Option B: treat it as a union of conditions across three domains, and state the shape a class must have in each. What each leads to: A spends budget searching inside a closed vocabulary, mistakes agreement for evidence, and never says what would refute the cut; B yields four testable conditions, a convention-versus-verdict distinction, written refuters, and an honest stop where no instrument can discriminate.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a cut that pays against the ground and a cut fixed by the classifier's frame. Arity: binary ("same class" joins two particulars), with "similar to" non-transitive while "same class" must be transitive. Symmetry: grouping is symmetric, but the bond that makes two things the same substance can be ordered. Scale: a cut made at one level becomes an operand at the next. Fig 9 names the question a compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **D1, the classification frame.** Actors: categories, membership rules, chosen features, the partition. Currency: description length and internal consistency. Anchor: none of its own, Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Question type: not a self-grounding domain; partition versus union inside the cut.
- **D2, the ground of the particulars.** Actors: particulars, their properties, causal powers and arrangements. Currency: settlement. Anchor: the ground, which does not update when classifiers change their minds, Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Question type: genuine domain; partition when the cut tracks a joint, union when a class bundles conditions.
- **D3, users and verifiers.** Actors: inquirers, institutions, induction-makers. Currency: successful induction and coordination. Anchor: the ground for a verdict, the shared meaning for a convention. Question type: union of two currencies; Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it".
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive differences per slot, bind a vocabulary and a criterion of progress, describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), refuse the wrong held class before minting, partition, then name and stamp each cut (Fig 3, Formula step 2, Fig 9 :41).
- **Strain test applied.** Fig 12 :104 (settled molecule becomes an atom) versus Fig 13 :116-118 (no new kind by rearrangement) was resolved by re-census: one governs recursion across levels, the other governs membership of the generating set. Different quantities. No strain survived.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the search for the correct classification as a single question does not. A classification reveals pattern when its cuts are ranged against an unmoving ground by independent frames, preserve bonds and individual cases, form a real partition rather than a union worn as a partition, and cost less than the confusion they remove. It obscures complexity when it is a feature list or an average, a closed self-certifying vocabulary, or a convention that has been allowed to decide an outcome. D1, D2 and D3 are kept separate and never averaged. Which specific classification is correct in any given domain is a `gap`, not a silence: the framework returns a shape and stops where instruments stop discriminating. This is a `general` and `specified`, ungrounded, `candidate` result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because the four refuters were written before the run. The real cost is spending a reader's independence by sending a finished taxonomy instead of a testable shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the claim and the refuter. If the shape is wrong in the other direction, a genuine ground-paying class may be dismissed as "just a convention", which loses a term that pays (Fig 5 bargain).
- **undoable:** yes. A classification is a set of cuts plus bindings; it can be re-cut without withdrawing the ground. Fig 9 :41 "so keep every cut ranked and reversible"; Fig 12 :104 lets a settled class become an atom for the next level. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that discriminates whether a boundary tracks a real joint; a flat ingredient-only classification that resolves bonded differences without an added relation (breaks F3); a single-frame ungrounded classification that predicts across domains (breaks F1 and F5); a convention deciding an outcome that improves ground contact (breaks F5); a new kind derived by rearrangement alone (breaks F6); or a re-census that removes the D1 and D2 strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
