## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Classification is structure-imposing even when it is used as structure-seeking.** The SAGE chapter says it plainly: "The strategy of cluster analysis is structure-seeking although its operation is structure-imposing." Huber's biology text adds that algorithms "will always find clusters - even where there are none", and Thrun's benchmarking study shows why: "each global clustering criterion imposes a particular structure on the data, and only if the data happen to conform to the requirements of a particular criterion are the actual clusters recovered." This is the outside-field version of F1 and F5. It is not a figure quote and is recorded as secondary corroboration in `09`.

2. **The history of chemistry supplies a clean case of a classification that recorded ingredients and lost the substance.** Science History Institute: isomers "led chemists to suspect that substances are defined not simply by the number and kind of atoms in the molecule but also by the arrangement of those atoms." Wentrup (2022, opened 2026-11-23) records that after Wohler and Liebig, "a classification of chemical compounds into organic and inorganic in the earlier sense had no natural basis." This is Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The figure text itself cites Liebig and Wohler (Fig 12 :97-98), so this is a genuine historical check with low overlap with the framework's other evidence.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending the full architecture before a reader has described their own residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A reader who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** Ask what they would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 is Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source" and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: a reader's agreement after seeing the theory is not evidence (Fig 2 :40). Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted by Fig 4 :22, but each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter, because `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. It is a `candidate` with four live refuters; no result has been gained by having more people read it.
