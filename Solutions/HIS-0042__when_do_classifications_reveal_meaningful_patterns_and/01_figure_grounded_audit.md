## 1. The residual, stated in effect terms

Given a domain of particulars and a proposed partition into classes, two outcomes are possible:

- **A (reveal):** the boundaries coincide with differences that do work against a ground. Members of a class behave alike on the axes that matter, so the classes carry induction, prediction and intervention.
- **B (obscure):** the boundaries are fixed by the classifier's own frame (shared ingredients, an interest, an algorithm that must return groups). Members of a class differ on the axes that matter, so the classes hide the variation they claim to organise.

The residual is: **which properties of a classification decide between A and B, and how can a reader tell which case they are in without already holding the right classification?**

A third possibility must be kept in view: a single classification can reveal at one scale and obscure at another. That makes the question a union compound, not one hard problem (Fig 9).

### Description of R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** "Same class" joins two particulars, so the grouping relation is binary. Its companion relation "similar to" need not be transitive, while "same class" must be. That gap is where a classification manufactures a cut (see the clustering impossibility result in `09` WORLD).
- **Symmetry.** "Same class" is symmetric: k spellings, one term under a symmetric bond. Fig 12 :92-93 "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term". But the arrangement that makes two things the same substance may be ordered, and order carries information a symmetric grouping drops.
- **Scale.** A classification is cut at one level, while the ground-paying difference may live at another. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." So a class settled at one level becomes an operand at the next, and a cut that is harmless below can be destructive above.

### Domain census (actors / currency / anchor, with partition or union and dependency order)

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the classification frame** | categories, membership rules, chosen features, the partition | description length and internal consistency | none of its own; Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." | not yet a domain with its own ground; it must range against an outside anchor |
| **D2, the ground of the classified particulars** | particulars, their properties, causal powers and arrangements | settlement (does a prediction or intervention pay?) | the ground, which does not move when classifiers change their minds, Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." | genuine domain; partition when the cut tracks a real joint, union when a class bundles overlapping conditions |
| **D3, the users and verifiers of the classification** | inquirers, institutions, induction-makers | successful induction and coordination | for a verdict, the ground; for a convention, the shared meaning | union of two currencies; Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" |

**Partition or union.** The question itself is a union: "when does it reveal" and "when does it obscure" are two conditions, and a case can satisfy both (Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"). The same distinction applies inside a classification: a scheme that treats overlapping conditions as mutually exclusive bins is a union worn as a partition (Fig 9 :54).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception of difference (D2) precedes a vocabulary of features (D1), which precedes a criterion of progress (D1), which precedes the partition, which precedes naming and carrying (D3). Fig 3 :26-27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A classification with no stated criterion of progress is a middle link nobody has looked at. Describe the effect before choosing a frame: Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A classification reveals pattern when its cuts are independent frames that each predict a distinct residual (triangulated)

Fig 8 :48-49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 13 :38-39: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything."

Two classifications that divide the same corpus by the same features agree by construction, and their agreement is the corpus talking. A reveal requires that the classes be ranged separately against an anchor that neither classifier can move. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

- **Mode:** `general` (structural).
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated, Fig 8 (union surplus) and Fig 2 (valence of correlation), low overlap between the two figures.
- **Refuter:** produce two classifications from independent instruments whose agreement persists on a held-out ground-paying slot and whose shared evidence pool is demonstrably empty. If that fails here, this finding stands.

### F2 - A classification reveals pattern when the cut is a real partition, not a union worn as a partition (triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

A class that bundles two conditions looks well formed on each subset and will not converge as a whole, which is the diagnostic in Fig 9 :4-5. The fix is not a finer cut of the same bin but an unbundling into the two conditions and a separate loop for their compound. The Library of Babel essay (read 2026-11-23) puts the same point in search terms: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."

- **Mode:** `specified` (operational test).
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated, Fig 9 and Fig 8 (where the union's surplus lives), with the essay as a low-overlap narrative corroboration.
- **Refuter:** a classification in which every member falls in exactly one class on the axis that matters, no residual persists inside a class, and no second condition binds in the intersections.

### F3 - A classification reveals pattern only when it preserves arrangement and per-slot signal (triangulated)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

A classification by shared ingredients records the atoms and drops the bond. Silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different behaviour, and the history of chemistry is the record of a classification failing on exactly this point (world sources in `09`, Wentrup 2022 and Science History Institute, both read 2026-11-23).

At the measurement layer the same failure has a second form. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A class average replaces the one live object with a mean, which is the mechanism by which a classification obscures complexity while appearing to summarise it.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated, Fig 12 and Fig 1 (different figures, different quantities), plus chemistry history as a genuine outside witness with low overlap with the framework.
- **Refuter:** a feature-list classification that discriminates bonded isomers, or an aggregate statistic, on a ground-paying axis without an instrument that resolves the bond or the slot.

### F4 - A classification earns its keep only under the description-length bargain (single-source)

Fig 5 :26: "and then: does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?" Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold." Fig 5 :43-44: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."

A class that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a class that fits one occasion perfectly. The bargain is relative, not absolute, which is why there is no fixed number of classes that is correct for every purpose.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation status:** single-source (Fig 5 and the formula's MDL step are one evidence pool). The independent clustering literature converges on a similar economy but proves an impossibility result, not this bargain (see `09`).
- **Refuter:** a classification whose total description length exceeds the confusion it removes while still supporting ground-paying prediction.

### F5 - A classification obscures complexity when a convention is promoted to a verdict (triangulated)

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

This is the sharpest answer to the question. A classification is legitimate as a convention: a shared meaning that lets people coordinate. It becomes a distortion at the exact moment it decides an outcome, for example a diagnostic category that determines treatment, or a species concept that determines what gets protected. The valence of agreement flips: useful for the convention, disqualifying for the verdict. Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one."

- **Mode:** `general` for the rule, `specified` for the test of whether a given class decides an outcome.
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated, Fig 2 and Fig 10 (separate figures, separate quantities).
- **Refuter:** a class whose boundaries are demonstrably fixed by interest and yet are used to settle a ground question, where an independent instrument redraws the same boundary. Until such an instrument exists, the case remains `candidate`.

### F6 - A classification obscures complexity when its frame certifies its own categories (triangulated)

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 13 :116-118: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

Refining a taxonomy indefinitely inside its own vocabulary extends the arrangement, never the set of kinds. The classifier cannot see this from inside, because the frame cannot certify its own limit (Fig 8 metaframe rules). What is owed is contact with a frame whose closure differs, a provenanced import with a source gate and a shadow test, not another level of the same hierarchy.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated, Fig 6 and Fig 13; the frame-limit claim is a proved metaframe result, not an empirical generalisation.
- **Refuter:** derive a genuinely new kind by rearrangement alone inside one closed scheme, or show a frame certifying its own limit.

### F7 - A classification obscures complexity when it declares a category instead of grounding it (triangulated)

Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 13 :38-39 makes independence a condition on the set itself: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything". The ISKO encyclopedia entry on facet-analytic approaches (read 2026-11-23) records the same hazard from knowledge organisation: the approach rests on "the problematical assumption that relations between concepts are a priori and are therefore not established by the development of models, theories and laws."

The world source is not a corroboration of the figures in a strong sense (its field is library and information science, low overlap with the framework), so the agreement is informative rather than expected.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated, Fig 10 and Fig 13 plus a low-overlap world source.
- **Refuter:** an instrument that discriminates the declared categories against the ground, showing they track a real difference.

### F8 - A classification reveals pattern at one scale and obscures it at another; verdicts must not be averaged (general)

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A class settled and useful at one level becomes an operand at the next, and its adequacy at the lower level does not carry up. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A taxonomy that is cheap to use is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation status:** triangulated, Fig 12 and Fig 9.
- **Refuter:** a class whose ground-paying adequacy is scale-invariant across two levels of composition.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is: a classification reveals pattern when its cuts are (i) ranged against an unmoving ground by independent frames, (ii) bond-preserving and slot-preserving, (iii) a real partition rather than a union worn as a partition, and (iv) cheaper than the confusion they remove; it obscures complexity when any of those fail, above all when a convention decides an outcome or a frame certifies its own categories.

This shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A classification drawn from a single frame, with no ground reading, reliably supports novel ground-paying predictions across domains. (Breaks F1 and F5.)
2. A flat ingredient-only classification discriminates bonded isomers or per-slot signals on a ground-paying axis without an instrument that resolves the bond or the slot. (Breaks F3.)
3. A convention that decides an outcome is shown to improve contact with the ground rather than merely coordinate action. (Breaks F5.)
4. A genuinely new kind is derived by rearrangement alone inside one closed vocabulary. (Breaks F6.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of it may be labelled `grounded`. Written before the WORLD search and before `07` and `09` were drafted.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask not "is this classification true" but "what does it range against, and what did it drop to fit". The first question has no ground in the general case.
2. State the criterion of progress before the partition (Fig 3 :26-27). A taxonomy with no stated purpose is a middle link nobody has looked at.
3. Split before you search (Fig 9 :4-5). If categories keep disagreeing on different subsets, the class is several questions.
4. Preserve the bond and the slot (Fig 12, Fig 1). Publish the lineage of each cut so a reader can see what was dropped.
5. Decide explicitly whether the classification is a convention or a verdict (Fig 2 :40-41, Fig 10 :47). If it decides an outcome, it owes a ground.
6. Keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41). No final room (Fig 7).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: reveal and obscure are not properties of a classification alone but outcomes of a classification meeting (or failing) four conditions across three domains, and they must be judged per domain and never averaged. The specific claim that any scheme is simply "true" does not survive. No verdict here is `grounded`; all are `candidate` pending a ground reading.
