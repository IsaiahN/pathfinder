### Q2 - What is the symmetry of the distinguishability relation?

- Operators table :25-27: `≡` identity, "two names, one referent", with the test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." Symmetric.
- Operators table :47-51: `⋛` comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities", with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Ordered.
- Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term.
- **"Distinguishable from at I" is symmetric.** If model A's predicted vector is separated from model B's by more than I's resolution, the reverse holds. It is a comparison (`⋛`) used as a threshold on a symmetric distance, so the ordering of the operands does not change the verdict. One term, not two.
- **"Same prediction at I" is an equivalence** (`≡`): two names, one distinguishable class at I. The class is the referent; the model names are the spellings. Fig 12 :22-23: "one substance", "this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term".
- **Careful, this is not a free pass to identify the models.** Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." The equivalence is at the level of the *instrument's reading*, not at the level of the mechanisms. The lineage keeps them separate; the ground does not.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a calibrated instrument for which "A is distinguishable from B" holds but "B is distinguishable from A" does not. That would make the relation ordered and break the threshold reading. This is not a hypothetical: parity-odd observables can be sign-sensitive, so the test must be applied to a *distance* or a two-sided criterion, not to a signed asymmetry. If a signed criterion were used, the falsifier would fire and the bond would have to be re-censused.

### Q3 - Are two inflation models isomers or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"

Three cases, kept separate:

1. **Broad family (single-field slow-roll).** Shared substructure `state -> structure -> state` with slow-roll conditions. This is a functional group: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The kind is single-field slow-roll; the member is Starobinsky, Higgs, monomial, hilltop, and so on. The substructure fixes adiabatic, nearly Gaussian, nearly scale-invariant predictions as a class; the qualifier decides which slot moves.
2. **Fixed atom set, different arrangement.** If two models are written over the same declared atom set (for example the scalar degree of freedom, the Einstein-Hilbert term, a non-minimal coupling, a plateau parameter) and differ only in how those atoms are bonded, they are isomers in Fig 12's sense: "same atoms, different bond, different substance." The classic case is Starobinsky `R^2` versus Higgs inflation with a large non-minimal coupling: the LiteBIRD paper lists both under exponentially approached plateaus and notes that in the Starobinsky model "the Planck scale does not occur by accident, but appears because the characteristic scale and the Planck scale are set by the same dimensionful coefficient in the action." Same plateau substance, different microscopic bond to gravity.
3. **Different atom sets.** Single-field models versus axion-SU(2) sourced-tensor models or multi-field models do not share the same atoms. They are not isomers under Fig 12's definition. Calling them isomers would import an atom set that one of them lacks, which Fig 6 prices above starting from primitives.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two models over the same declared atom set have identical arrangement but different observable predictions, the isomer definition fails and the functional-group reading stands alone. If two models over *different* atom sets are shown to be isomers, the atom-set requirement is wrong and must be replaced by a behaviour-based criterion.

**Strain, named:** "same atoms" needs a fixed atom set, and the field does not supply a canonical one. The isomer claim is therefore valid only within a restricted family whose atom set is declared in the claim. This is a strain on scope, not a contradiction, and it is recorded rather than hidden.

### Q5 - Analogues that carry, and one that does not

- **Metamerism (colour measurement).** Carries directly: one reading, several sources, an instrument that cannot comment on the source; resolved by the spectrometer, not by argument (*The Hard Problem is About Source*). Used in `05` section 4.
- **Library of Babel search.** Carries: describe the sought-for shape in borrowed vocabulary, recurse, stop when the finish line becomes the starting place (*The Library of Babel*).
- **Chemical isomerism.** Carries within a fixed atom set, fails across different atom sets (Fig 12 :97-98: "silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"). Used in Q3 with the scope restriction stated.
- **Analogue that does not carry: a horse race between models.** A race has a finish line independent of the timing instrument. Here the instrument's resolution partly *constitutes* the classes being compared (Fig 8 :49: overlap at a resolution makes two models one class). Using the race analogy would hide the instrument dependence, which is the whole result. Declined.

## CONCLUSION

The distinguishability relation is symmetric as a threshold on a distance, and "same prediction at I" is an equivalence whose classes are instrument-relative. Within a fixed atom set, models related by different arrangements are Fig 12 isomers; across the broad slow-roll family they are members of a functional group; across different atom sets neither term applies. Naming imported slots and instruments is permitted with provenance and is not a claim to move the wall, while naming a winning model from the figures remains forbidden. The dependency order puts the criterion before the experiment schedule, and a live re-census removed CMB-S4 from link 4. One strain (atom set for isomerism) is live and scoped; one signed-observable caveat is named and tested.
