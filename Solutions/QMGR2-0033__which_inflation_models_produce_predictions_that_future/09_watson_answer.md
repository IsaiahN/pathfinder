## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that some inflation models produce predictions future observations can distinguish. Option A: treat this as a single question about which model is right, and let an evidence ranking or a consensus of model builders answer it. Option B: treat it as a union compound about separability at a named instrument, keep the residual per model and per slot, collapse prediction-overlapping models into one class at that instrument, and leave the model list to an import from cosmology that waits for the ground. What each leads to: A bundles two conditions, disqualifies itself by selecting agreeing frames, and issues a verdict without an instrument; B issues a checkable shape, a pre-registered refuter, and an honest `gap` where the figures cannot name.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: binary between models, with a three-way dependency on model predictions, instrument resolution and ground. Symmetry: "distinguishable from at I" is symmetric as a threshold on a distance; "same prediction at I" is an equivalence. Scale: first order for a model's predicted vector, second order for the comparison with an instrument error and between two models. Fig 9 names a union compound, not one hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain A, the candidate model family (frames under test, not a free-standing domain).** Actors: Starobinsky `R^2`, Higgs inflation with non-minimal coupling, `alpha`-attractors, monomial chaotic potentials, natural inflation, hilltop models, axion-SU(2) sourced-tensor models, feature models. Currency: predicted observable vectors plus reheating uncertainty. Anchor: none. Per the census rule a set with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet; it is the family of frames to be ranged.
- **Domain B, the observational ground.** Actors: COBE, WMAP, Planck, BICEP/Keck, Simons Observatory, LiteBIRD. Currency: measured `n_s`, limits on `r`, `f_NL`, B-mode spectra. Anchor: the sky, which does not change because a frame prefers a model. CMB-S4 is removed from the live set: its own site states DOE and NSF "can no longer support the CMB-S4 Project" as of 2025-07-09.
- **Domain C, the resolution and comparison frame.** Actors: instrument error forecasts, foreground residuals, delensing assumptions, Bayesian evidences. Currency: separability in sigma. Anchor: the ground reached through instruments. Question type: union compound, so it must be unbundled before searching (Fig 9 :54).
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); fix the anchor before ranging models (Fig 2 :45); keep residuals per slot (Fig 1 :19); run inward precision and outward imports as separate tracks (Fig 6 :31); eliminate against the ground (Fig 9 :38); repeat with the changed reach.

Strain test applied: Fig 8 :49 (overlap collapse) and Fig 8 :50 (convergent derivation at maximum independence) were initially read as a contradiction. Re-census resolved it: the first governs the instrument's distinguishable classes, the second governs the independence between frames. Different objects, no strain after re-census. The isomer atom-set dependence remains live and scoped.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the specific model list does not come from the figures. Distinguishability is a per-model, per-slot relation against a fixed observational ground, evaluated at a named instrument. Prediction-overlapping models form one distinguishable class at that instrument while remaining independent frames with separate provenance. Reach and separation form a union compound that must be unbundled. Inward precision on `n_s` and `r` and outward imports of `f_NL`, features, isocurvature and stochastic gravitational waves are separate tracks. Within a declared atom set, models are isomers or functional-group members; across different atom sets neither term applies. The framework returns this criterion and its refuter, not the names of winning models. Status: `specified` and ungrounded; the model list is a `gap` filled only by a provenanced import.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, instrument dependence, scope strain and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a model list or a consensus instead of a criterion, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The other cost is building an experiment schedule on CMB-S4 after its withdrawal, which this package's habitat re-census removes.
- **undoable:** yes. Imported slots, instrument names and the predicted separation thresholds are removable and revisable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove the model-name claim and the CMB-S4 assumption. A refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and nothing is deleted from the record (Fig 6 :42-43: the reach falls because something was added, not because something was struck out).
- **reversed by:** the ground, not agreement. LiteBIRD detecting primordial B-modes above about 0.01 would separate the monomial class from the plateau class; an upper limit near `r < 0.002` would leave the plateau cluster as one class at that instrument, a verdict conditional on that instrument. A second discriminator (`f_NL`, features, isocurvature, parity-odd correlators, a stochastic gravitational-wave background) could split that cluster. A calibrated instrument that separates models with identical predictions, a ground value that moves with the assumed model, or a figure re-census that admits the figure lines were aimed at different quantities would each reverse the corresponding structural claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
