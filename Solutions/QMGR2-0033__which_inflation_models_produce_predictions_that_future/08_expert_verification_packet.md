# Expert verification packet: the Tether on inflation model distinguishability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist or CMB experimentalist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the specific model list. Not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed to have settled anything.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Two inflation models are distinguishable at a named instrument I if and only if their predicted distributions on at least one observable slot (`n_s`, `r`, `alpha_s`, `beta_s`, `f_NL`, a feature template) differ by more than I's resolution on that slot. Four structural consequences follow. The residual must be kept per model and per slot, never as one averaged fit statistic. Models whose predictions coincide at I form one distinguishable class at I, whatever their microscopic provenance. "In reach of I" and "separated from a neighbour" are two conditions forming a union compound, so they must be unbundled before searching. Sharpening an existing slot and importing a different observable are separate tracks. The figures give this criterion and its refuter; they do not give the model list, which is imported from cosmology and waits for the ground.

- **Refuter:** exhibit two models with identical predicted distributions on every slot at a stated resolution that a correctly calibrated instrument nevertheless separates; or show a correct distinguishability decision made from one aggregated fit statistic alone; or show the measured `n_s` changing when the assumed model changes.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable by a cosmologist against the forecast literature

- **Statement:** reach and separation are two conditions, and different instrument axes answer different questions. A better `r` error bar clears monomial models (predicting `r` above about 0.01) and does not by itself split the small-`r` plateau cluster (Starobinsky, Higgs, exponential `alpha`-attractors around `r` of 0.003 to 0.005). Precision on `n_s` may be more decisive for single-field selection than precision on `r`; a different observable such as `f_NL` or a feature template can separate models with identical power spectra.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 - "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 6 :31 - "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."
- **What to check (with the sources opened and quoted in `10_source_ledger.csv`):**
  - LiteBIRD Collaboration, PTEP 2023, 042F01: the mission requirement is `delta r < 0.001`; the paper states "different classes of models of inflation make different predictions for r" and "For a given reheating history, a model makes a definitive prediction, corresponding to a point in the n s - r plane."
  - Escudero et al, arXiv:1509.05419: "the measurement of r will not be enough to discriminate between the models on the (r,n s ) plane."
  - Hardwick, Vennin and Wands, JCAP 05 (2018) 070: "a decrease in the measurement uncertainty of the scalar spectral index would be more decisive than a decrease in the uncertainty in the tensor-to-scalar ratio."
  - Braglia et al, JCAP 2023 (03) 014: "two models with identical power spectra lead to primordial non-Gaussianities of different amplitudes."
- **Refuter:** show that improving a single observable separates every currently overlapping model pair, with no intersection left standing; or show that precision on one slot and the addition of a different slot are the same operation, so that the two tracks collapse into one.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a winning inflation model, and not a Bayesian evidence ranking as the ground.
- Not a proof that inflation occurred, and not a derivation of `n_s`, `r` or an inflaton potential from the six-term substrate.
- Not a fixed instrument list. CMB-S4 is out of the live set per its own site statement; LiteBIRD and Simons Observatory are the named forward instruments.
- Not a single-observable lever, and not a `grounded` result.
- Not a claim that the figures name models. They return a shape; the model list is an import with provenance.
- Not a claim that models converging on the same prediction are the same theory. They remain distinct frames with distinct lineages; they are only one class at that instrument.

## 6. What would settle it

1. LiteBIRD detects primordial B-modes at `r` above about 0.01: monomial models separate from plateau models, a class-level result.
2. LiteBIRD returns an upper limit near `r < 0.002`: the plateau cluster remains one distinguishable class at that instrument; the verdict "unreachable at this instrument" is recorded, not a silence.
3. A second discriminator splits that cluster: `f_NL`, feature-template shape, isocurvature, parity-odd correlators, or a stochastic gravitational-wave background. Braglia et al (2023) show the principle on power spectra versus non-Gaussianity.
4. Any of the six refuters in `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 5 fires against the structural claim.
