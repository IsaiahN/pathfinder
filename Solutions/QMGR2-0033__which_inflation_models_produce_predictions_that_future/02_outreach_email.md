# Outreach draft: check the criterion before you decide about the model list

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the instrument and its resolution are named in the attached packet (`08_expert_verification_packet.md`, C2). A distinguishability claim without an instrument is unbound and collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable point about distinguishing inflation models, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow methodological question: which inflation models produce predictions that future observations can actually distinguish? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A pair of inflation models is distinguishable at a named instrument I if and only if their predicted distributions on at least one observable slot, for example `n_s`, `r`, `alpha_s`, `beta_s`, `f_NL` or a feature template, are separated by more than I's resolution on that slot. Four consequences follow, and these are the part to check:
>
> 1. The residual must be kept per model and per observable slot. A single averaged "inflation fits the data" number throws away exactly the signal the question asks for. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
> 2. Models whose predictions coincide at I form one distinguishable class at I. They are not two independent witnesses just because they come from different microscopic mechanisms. Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."
> 3. "In reach of an instrument" and "separated from a neighbour" are two conditions, not one. Improving `r` alone clears the monomial models and leaves the plateau cluster standing, so the bundle has to be split before it is searched.
> 4. Instruments are sharpened versions of instruments already returning something: COBE to WMAP to Planck to BICEP to LiteBIRD. Sharpening an existing slot and adding a new slot (non-Gaussianity, features, isocurvature) are separate tracks and should not be read against each other.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming which inflation model is correct. I am not claiming the framework can name a model; it returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that a Bayesian evidence ranking is the ground; it is a frame that scores models with a quantity it computes. I am not claiming this is settled by experiment. The specific model list has to be imported from cosmology with its provenance stated, and it is not a figure derivation.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. Two models with identical predicted distributions on every slot at a stated resolution are nevertheless separated by a correctly calibrated instrument.
> 2. A correct distinguishability decision is obtained from a single aggregated fit statistic, with no per-model, per-slot residual.
> 3. The ground value moves when the model is changed, so that the measurement depends on which theory you assume.
> 4. A future instrument separates a pair the criterion declared indistinguishable at that instrument.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding point. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - LiteBIRD Collaboration, "Probing Cosmic Inflation with the LiteBIRD Cosmic Microwave Background Polarization Survey", PTEP 2023, 042F01: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02773
> - Hardwick, Vennin and Wands, "The decisive future of inflation", JCAP 05 (2018) 070: https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09491
> - Braglia, Chen, Kumar Hazra and Pinol, "Back to the features", JCAP 2023 (03) 014: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07028
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The source essay warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula" and that "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
