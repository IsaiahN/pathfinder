## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, in the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a government's policy prediction and observed employment and price outcomes. Arity: four indexed slots, employment, inflation, fiscal-resource, habitat-implementation, not one number. Symmetry: "stabilize" is a comparison against a target, so the bond is ordered/comparative, not a symmetric conjunction; the buffer's action is ordered in time. Scale: programme, macro and time-channel, with a lossy membrane between them. I derived this from Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per-slot indexing) and from the Operators table's comparison entry (Operators :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"), not from the primary's §1 table.
- **D1 Employment slot.** Actors: workers, employers, JG authority. Currency: jobs, hours, wage floor. Anchor: employment readings independent of the government's claim. Admitted.
- **D2 Inflation slot.** Actors: price setters, workers, central bank. Currency: price level and wage demands. Anchor: a price reading that does not update on the policy. Admitted.
- **D3 Fiscal-resource slot.** Actors: treasury, bond markets, real capacity. Currency: funds and resources. Anchor: observed resource use. Admitted. "Government can always afford it" has no nameable anchor unless read as capacity; absorbed.
- **D4 Habitat-implementation.** Actors: local labour markets, skills, administrators. Currency: contact and matches. Anchor: actual placement. Admitted.
- **D5 Verification frame.** Actors: researchers and reviewers. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground. Admitted, with zero independence in this simulated run.
- **Union, not partition.** D1 and D2 intersect at the wage floor; D3 and D4 condition both. One lever leaves the intersections. Derived from Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Order by dependency.** Read each slot, find terms for what a buffer affords, state the objective as a comparison, set the rule, carry the result (Fig 3 :20). Describe the residual before choosing a school (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second.").

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)), measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 :29/:33 (non-updating anchor), Fig 10 :47 (convention that decides an outcome), Fig 11 :61 (asymmetric introduction), Fig 12 :117/:121 (isomer and cycle).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the headline separator: two slots, no averaging; external price anchor; habitat introduction with two silent failures; real-capacity test; bond choice; declared numerical gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** an actual national programme with a named wage, buffer ratio, eligibility rule, price index and evaluation protocol.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the separator holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **source discrimination** required to tell a genuine buffer shift from a reclassification or a suppressed labour supply. The separator says "non-updating reading"; a policy has to build an instrument, and the figures do not specify it;
- the **tuning rule** for wage and buffer ratio. The separator says these are a gap; a government must pick numbers, and the choice is ground-bound rather than figure-derived (Fig 6 :32);
- the **non-updating status of the price anchor** once a government has an electoral interest in the reported number. Fig 2 :34 names the drift; the design must install a seat that cannot move the reading (Fig 10 :24);
- the **union intersections**. A real programme acts on employment and prices at once, so the two per-slot verdicts interact in a way the split derivation cannot recover by addition (Fig 9 :57).

Conversely, three things survive cleanly: the refusal to average, the requirement that the price anchor be external, and the asymmetry of introduction versus exclusion. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no policy ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the separator as if it were the policy would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## Strains and re-census

- **Physical six-term mapping.** The primary's early reading mapped employment and inflation onto the six physical terms and was retracted. Re-census assigns the slot/anchor work to Figs 1 and 2, union to Fig 9, habitat to Fig 11, bond and cycle to Fig 12. The canonical rule says a reading that makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain. No strain after re-census, retraction preserved in `01` §1 and §6.
- **MMT fiscal claim versus real-capacity anchor (live).** Mitchell's "no financial constraint as the issuer of the currency" and Sawyer's productive-capacity constraint are not the same quantity. One is a statement about the currency issuer's balance sheet, the other about real resources. The figures anchor D3 in real capacity (Fig 2 :29), so the strain is kept open with a refuter rather than smoothed over. This is the one live strain.
- **F3 anchor type (narrowed).** The price anchor must be a measurement that does not update; the JG wage is a convention. MMT literature calls the fixed wage a "nominal anchor", which is a different use of the word. Re-census: the figure's anchor is the thing that settles the question, not the number the policy fixes. The word collision is named, not hidden.
- **World conflict returned to census.** Mitchell's claim that "in general, there cannot be inflationary pressures arising from a policy that sees the Government offering a fixed wage to any labour that is unwanted by other employers" conflicts with Sawyer's constraint and with the buffer-stock article's note that schemes can create huge stockpiles. Returned to the census as a question about where the loop sign comes from (D3), and did not flip the verdict.
- **Isomer identification (single-source, not strained).** Fig 12 :117 gives the definition. Whether JG and benefits are economically isomers is an unmeasured empirical question; the falsifier is written. `single-source` on the figure side, not `triangulated`.
- **After re-census: one live strain, one narrowed word collision, one retracted mapping, one world conflict kept visible. None hidden.**
