# Outreach draft: check the separator before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until §3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been read and the seven refuters are understood. If the residual cannot survive the refuter, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable separator on job guarantees, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A government considering a job guarantee is trying to close a gap between what its policy predicts and what happens, separately in two slots: employment and inflation. Those slots have different currencies and different anchors, so they cannot be averaged into one "stabilization works" verdict. Fig 1 :19 says the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated. Fig 9 :55 says a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.
>
> **The output, stated before the theory:** a job guarantee can close the employment slot when the employment reading does not move because the government claims success. It does not by itself close the inflation slot, because that slot needs an anchor that does not update on the policy, and the programme's fixed wage is a convention rather than a ground. Fig 2 :33 says "The anchor must not update." Fig 10 :47 says "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." The resource question is real capacity, not the treasury's balance sheet. Implementation has two silent failures: what the programme failed to reproduce and what it unintentionally introduced. None of this returns a wage, a buffer ratio, or a programme size. Those are declared as a gap.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that the job guarantee is or is not desirable in the abstract; not that it settles inflation by wage declaration; not that MMT is right and mainstream macro is wrong; not that a cost table decides the question; not that this is a measurement. The figure set returns a shape and a route, not a policy number. My derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency against figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the output (written down before this was sent):**
> 1. a single aggregate "stabilization" measure that treats employment and inflation as one slot;
> 2. an inflation anchor that updates on the government's own announcement and still predicts out of sample;
> 3. a demonstration that a declared wage settles the price level by declaration alone;
> 4. evidence the programme adds no new composable term to employment, or that it adds a new atom to the inflation mechanism itself;
> 5. evidence that job guarantee and unemployment benefits differ only in degree, with identical behavioural effects;
> 6. evidence that the two schools range against the same anchor and the disagreement shrinks with more data;
> 7. a ground-validated controller tuning rule that holds across regimes.
>
> If any of these holds, the relevant part is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text and line numbers: local file `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as Fig N :line.
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Architecture essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The architecture essay warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula", so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
