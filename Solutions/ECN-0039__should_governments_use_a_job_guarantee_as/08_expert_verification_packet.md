# Expert verification packet: the Tether on a job guarantee for stabilization

**What this is:** a separator in a form a labour economist, macroeconomist or policy evaluator can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the numerical wage, buffer ratio and programme size. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a policy result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Employment and inflation are two slots with different anchors, so they cannot be averaged into one stabilization verdict (Fig 1 :19, :22). A job guarantee is a habitat introduction that can close the employment slot when the employment reading does not update on the government's own claim and the wage does not compete with the market wage structure. It does not by itself close the inflation slot: its wage is a convention, and "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" (Fig 10 :47), so the price slot needs a non-updating external anchor. The resource test is real capacity; implementation has two silent failures; the choice against unemployment benefits is a bond choice (isomer), not a degree. The wage, buffer ratio and programme size are a declared gap. Seven refuters were written before this run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal and economic; checkable against the figures and the programme record
- **Statement:** the two targets form a union of slots, not one residual; the instrument changes the habitat, not the worker in isolation, and introduction is available while exclusion is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **What to check:** do the employment and inflation readings have different anchors in the policy under discussion? Does the programme add an employer of last resort, and can it subtract the private sector or the skills structure? Verify against MGNREGA: "the key benefit of the scheme lies in the reduction of wage volatility" alongside "only 30.3 per cent of planned works had been completed".
- **Refuter:** a single aggregate stabilization measure with one anchor that one lever provably clears; or a JG that adds no new composable term to employment (pure relabel of unemployment benefits); or an implementation with neither silent failure over a full cycle.

### C2 Economic; checkable against wage-setting and price measurement
- **Statement:** the inflation slot needs an anchor that does not update on the policy announcement. The declared wage is a convention and cannot settle the price level by declaration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Also the world record: "There is an issue about the validity of an unchanging nominal anchor in an inflationary environment" (Wikipedia job guarantee).
- **What to check:** in the policy design being assessed, is the price index used to judge success independent of the wage announcement and of the programme's own reports? Does the wage floor compete with the private wage structure, or is it set below it ("hire off the bottom", Mitchell 2013)? Does the design survive a supply shock rather than only a demand shortfall?
- **Refuter:** an inflation anchor that updates on the government's own announcement and still predicts out of sample; or a demonstrated case where a declared wage settles the price level by declaration alone.

### C3 Framework-internal derivation with an economic check; the loop and the bond
- **Statement:** JG and unemployment benefits are isomers over a fixed fiscal atom set (Fig 12 :117), and the wage bill to demand to employment loop is a cycle whose sign is decided at the resource ground, not by the nominal wage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** do the two instruments differ in bond (wage-for-work versus transfer) rather than only in amount? Is the loop sign sensitive to spare capacity, as Sawyer's constraint ("the programme would require productive capacity in the right quantities and places") suggests, or is it fixed by the nominal wage, as Mitchell's design claim suggests? Are cost estimates being used as budget filters rather than as the verdict?
- **Refuter:** identical behavioural effects of JG and benefits at equal spend; a controlled demonstration that the loop sign is fixed by the nominal wage alone regardless of spare capacity; a cost threshold shown to determine the per-slot verdicts.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a yes or a no on job guarantees in the abstract, and not a policy recommendation without a ground.
- Not that a job guarantee controls inflation by wage declaration.
- Not that MMT is right and mainstream macro is wrong, or the reverse.
- Not a wage level, buffer ratio, NAIBER value, or programme size. No figure returns these.
- Not that a cost table, a single pilot, or a school consensus decides the question.
- Not `grounded`. No employment or price measurement was taken; the derivation is a simulation checked against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." The instruments owed:

1. An employment reading that does not update on the government's own claim of success.
2. A price reading independent of the policy announcement, plus a discriminator between a genuine buffer shift, a reclassification and a suppressed labour supply.
3. A real-capacity measurement that fixes the sign of the wage bill to demand loop.
4. A regime-valid tuning rule for the wage and the buffer ratio.

Any of the seven refuters in `01` §4 would instead withdraw the corresponding claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :55, Fig 2 :33 and Fig 10 :47. Four short passages.
2. Check C1 against one real programme: does it add an employer of last resort, and what did it fail to reproduce or unintentionally introduce?
3. Check C2 against the price-measurement arrangement: does the success metric update on the policy?
4. Check C3: is the JG/benefit difference a bond difference, and is the loop sign sensitive to spare capacity?
5. Name one instrument that would discriminate a genuine buffer shift from a reclassification, or say why none is possible.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
