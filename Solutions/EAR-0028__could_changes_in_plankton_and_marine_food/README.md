## The question restated as an effect

R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the ocean state: temperature, stratification, dissolved inorganic carbon, nutrient supply, plankton community composition.
- **a** is what happens: a change in plankton abundance, size structure, or food-web coupling.
- **Γ(b,a)** is the library's prediction from its pumps: solubility, soft-tissue, carbonate counter-pump, circulation and ventilation.
- **o′** is the observed air-sea CO2 flux and ocean carbon inventory.
- **s** is the ocean region and the carbon pool. R is indexed per region and per pool, never averaged into one global sink (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

The word *substantially* is a threshold comparison (Fig 12 :87, ⋛), asking whether a biological perturbation is large relative to the whole ocean carbon sink. The answer would be a shape, bound before the run (Formula, "Direction"): an enumeration of the distinct conditions that bear on the ocean's carbon role, the sign of each biological effect on two different quantities (the natural surface-to-deep gradient and the anthropogenic sink), and an instrument that discriminates a biological change from a physical one. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

- Option A, "plankton changes can substantially alter the ocean's carbon role as a single lever": predicts that one biological variable predicts the sink across regions and pools.
- Option B, "the ocean's carbon role is a union of conditions, and plankton changes enter with a sign that depends on the residual and the stratum": predicts two different quantities and two different instruments, with the biological effect large for the natural gradient and small for the contemporary anthropogenic sink.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the marine question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of the ocean carbon cycle.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
