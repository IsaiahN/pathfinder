## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One quantity, two readings, no contradiction.** The central apparent conflict in this question is between two true statements. Resplandy, Lévy and Bopp 2026 show that biological change contributes *"human-driven changes in the biological carbon pump have only a small effect on the air-sea CO2 flux [<0.15 GtC/year]"* against a sink of 2.70 GtC per year, while the same review states that *"The biological carbon pumps explain ~90% of the present-day surface-to-deep increase in carbon, while the solubility pump only explains the remaining 10%."* Both are right because they are about different quantities. The figures' union rule (Fig 9 :54-57) and level-relativity (Fig 12 :104, Fig 13) require them to be kept separate. This is a case where the world source did not flip the verdict; it forced a better census.

- **Global synthesis (earth system science).** Resplandy, Lévy and Bopp 2026 identify the error directly: *"The misconception that ecosystems and the biological pump contribute to the ocean carbon sink largely stems from the conflation between the mean state of the carbon cycle... and the human-driven changes in these fluxes"*. It corroborates the unbundling in F1, but it is one witness and its authors work within the marine-carbon synthesis pool.
- **Assessment (climate science).** IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 5 says *"There is low confidence in how changes in the biological pump will influence the magnitude and direction of the ocean carbon feedback."* This corroborates the F7 `gap`: the numeric threshold is not held. Low confidence is a verdict about an instrument, not silence.
- **Regional observation (marine ecology).** Ostle et al. 2022 combine the CPR plankton record with SOCAT pCO2 and find that *"marine primary production does not simply affect the (sub-) annual ocean uptake of CO2 from the atmosphere, but it also significantly correlates with the long term variability of this uptake."* They also find opposing trends in subpolar and subtropical North Atlantic, which corroborates F8 (per-slot, never a global mean). They explicitly decline to separate biology from physics: *"our correlation analysis does not permit us to further untangle the non-thermal drivers and quantify the relative contributions of biology and physics separately"*, which is exactly the missing discriminator named in F7.
- **Global species modelling (macroecology).** Benedetti et al. 2021 project that *"nearly 40% of the phytoplankton and zooplankton assemblages are replaced by poleward shifting species"* and that *"the biological carbon pump is weakened under higher levels of plankton diversity, which are promoted by higher temperature and resource limitation. Alternatively, the biological carbon pump is more efficient under species-poor communities characterized by higher proportions of larger species."* This corroborates F4 (valence relative to the residual) with a different method and basin set from the North Atlantic record.
- **Molecular ecology and networks.** Lin et al. 2017 show that a few OTUs explain most export-potential variance and that *"ecosystems with lower export potential have more tightly coupled communities"*. This is the isomer/functional-group claim of F6 tested with co-occurrence networks. It is a low-overlap witness for the bond-reading.
- **Earth system modelling.** Armstrong McKay et al. 2021 supply the sign-flip case: adding temperature-dependent remineralisation increased export but reduced the sink, while adding trait-based plankton ecology decreased export but increased the sink. They also state that the pump is a transient, not equilibrium, sink, which corroborates F5.
- **Data-constrained attribution.** Carroll et al. 2022 show biology is a small term at full depth and the largest loss at 100 m. This is F8's level-relativity stated in numbers, and it warns against any single "plankton effect" number.
- **Mesocosm ecology.** The OCB summary of Bach et al. reports the 15-fold versus 3-fold result, corroborating F6: composition controls the fate of sinking material more than sinking speed alone.
- **Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. They are not independent world witnesses, because their author wrote the figures. That overlap is recorded in the ledger.

**Overlap honesty.** Ostle, Benedetti, Lin, Armstrong McKay, Carroll and the OCB highlight all work on marine carbon and plankton. They do not share one dataset, but they do share a disciplinary frame and citation chain. Fig 2 :31 applies: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* The metatheory essays are framework-internal, so they carry no independent corroboration weight. No world source conflicts with a figure verdict after re-census; the one apparent conflict was returned to the census in F9.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28:** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if the residual is not described first. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.

**A hard practical constraint.** The MEPS homepage links an Artificial Intelligence policy for authors under "For Authors", and ASLO and the Copernicus journals have comparable policies. This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude. Any submission must be rewritten and owned by a human author who can defend the derivation, with the AI contribution disclosed under the applicable policy. The individual policy pages were listed but not opened, so treat this as a constraint to verify before acting, not as a quotation of a specific rule.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the three source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
