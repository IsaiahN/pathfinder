# The Tether's answer on plankton, food webs and the ocean carbon cycle

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No source-discriminating measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would explain why the gap between what a physics-and-pumps library predicts the ocean will do with carbon and what the ocean actually does changes when plankton and food webs change, and whether that part of the gap is large relative to the whole sink. R is indexed per region and per pool, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about **threshold comparison**, not about a single global number.

The union must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is two quantities, not one hard question:

- **Q1:** the natural surface-to-deep carbon gradient, mostly set by biology.
- **Q2:** the contemporary anthropogenic sink, mostly set by physics and chemistry.

## 2. The shape

1. **The ocean's carbon role is community-and-habitat, not plankton alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A list of species is Γ, a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, not the answer.
2. **The habitat is enumerated, and contact cascades.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :47 names the relations: *"predation, competition, dependency"*. Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A change at the plankton base is not confined to the base.
3. **The two quantities must be kept separate.** Biological change explains most of the natural gradient but only a small part of the contemporary sink. Resplandy, Lévy and Bopp 2026 report ~90 percent for the former and <0.15 GtC per year against 2.70 GtC per year for the latter. Both are true of different quantities. A single "plankton effect on the sink" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26).
4. **The sign is not a property of a species.** Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual, and *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Armstrong McKay et al. 2021 observed opposite sign responses for export and for the sink in the same model. The same biological change can strengthen one and weaken the other.
5. **The pump is a cycle, not a chain.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Carbon is fixed, exported, remineralised and returned; whether the loop sequesters depends on the sign and on the timescale, not on the existence of the pump.
6. **Bonds matter as much as atoms.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information."* Two communities with the same species but different coupling are isomers (Fig 12 :117). A species inventory alone cannot predict export.
7. **The measured inventory is the anchor, not model agreement.** Fig 2 :29 names *"a measurement"* as an anchor; :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* SOCAT pCO2, GLODAP DIC, sediment traps and the plankton record are instruments ranged against the ground; model consensus is not the ground.
8. **The break is usually in the middle of the chain.** Fig 3 :24 and :27: the vocabulary and objective links have no instrument, and a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at. More budget lands on flux measurement and model intercomparison because those instruments already exist.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No source-discriminating measurement has paid for this shape.
- Not a name. It does not say which species, pump or group is decisive. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not a number. It does not say what percentage change counts as "substantial". That is a `gap`.
- Not an average. It makes no claim about a global "plankton fraction of the sink". Such numbers are filters for budget, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a claim that plankton are irrelevant to the natural gradient. They set most of it. The claim is that the natural gradient and the anthropogenic sink are different quantities (Fig 12 :104).
- Not that physics alone settles everything. Physics controls Q2, and it also gates Q1 through nutrient supply and stratification (Fig 11 :40).
- Not corroboration by agreement. The marine-carbon sources share syntheses and a disciplinary frame (Fig 2 :31, :40). The metatheory essays are framework-internal.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*. Here the readings are air-sea CO2 flux, DIC inventory and export flux; the candidate sources are warming-driven solubility loss, stratification and nutrient supply, community composition and size, and remineralisation depth.

The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. a plankton change whose cascade stops at the first trophic step;
2. a plankton-only change that moves the sink with the physical habitat held fixed;
3. a plankton group whose valence on export never changes sign across a documented change of context;
4. a basin where the pump is a one-way chain with no return flux;
5. two communities with identical atoms and bonds whose export diverges with no difference in habitat;
6. one lever changing both Q1 and Q2 by the same factor everywhere.

A prospective study that measures Q1 and Q2 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, follows the cascade beyond the first trophic step, and attributes an observed flux change to a named source would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing things are a discriminator of sources and a threshold definition. Naming a winner or a number from a reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The eight points in section 2 are a shape; the ninth thing, the decisive actor and magnitude in a given basin, is not held and is not pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the plankton group that wins and it does not give a percentage for "substantial". It says that the ocean's carbon role is a union of conditions held by a community and its habitat, and that the natural surface-to-deep carbon gradient and the contemporary human-caused sink are two different quantities that must not be averaged: biology sets most of the first, physics and chemistry set most of the second. Plankton changes propagate through an enumerated food web whose relations are predation, competition and dependency, and the effect that matters can be several steps from the cell that changed. But the sign is not fixed. The same change can strengthen export and weaken the sink, because the pump is a cycle whose loop can flip and because two communities with the same species but different coupling are different substances. The measured carbon inventory, not model agreement, is the anchor. What is offered is that shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which species and what magnitude stay open until a source-discriminating instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather than filled.
