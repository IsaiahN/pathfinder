## Q1 - Are two plankton communities with the same species isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Two communities with an identical species list have the same atoms. If their bonds differ, that is, if one is tightly coupled through a microbial loop while the other is loosely coupled and dominated by large, fast-sinking cells, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: the difference is invisible in the species inventory and decisive in the export. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not just who is there, it is who eats whom", and it is stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance carrying information the ingredients do not.

If the communities share only a pattern, such as large cell plus grazer plus fast sinking, while differing in which species occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable, and the world has run a version of the test. Lin et al. 2017 found that *"ecosystems with lower export potential have more tightly coupled communities"* and that using specific OTUs and network properties *"substantially improved organic carbon flux predictions"*, raising explained NCP variance from 35 percent to 92 percent. A species inventory alone would have missed the difference. The Woods Hole OCB summary of Bach et al. adds the mechanism: *"degradation rates changed maximally 15-fold over the course of the study while sinking speed changed only 3-fold"*, so the fate of the particle is set more by the ecological bond than by the ingredient.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two communities with identical atoms and identical measured bonds nevertheless diverge in carbon export with no difference in habitat contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2 and D3), which moves the explanation out of D4.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and the Lin et al. network result, different fields), single-source for any basin-specific application. The Benedetti et al. 2021 finding of ~40 percent species turnover and *"potentially novel species interactions"* is a further corroboration from macroecology, but it is a projection, not a measurement of export.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for a plankton community facing a changed ocean:

1. **Perception.** Can we measure the flux and the plankton at all? This link has SOCAT, GLODAP, CPR and DNA. Its failures are visible and have obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for what the changed community affords: size, coupling, valence, remineralisation depth? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can we state a comparison that would count as "substantial", and say which quantity it compares? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only species names cannot state a threshold.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information: which instrument to buy, which basin to sample.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry it to the next basin.

The two quantities from the primary audit map onto this chain. Q1 (the natural gradient) is mostly a failure or success of links 2 and 3: the terms and the objective. Q2 (the anthropogenic sink) is mostly a failure of link 1 and of the physical domain D3. That mapping is why one lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Link 1 has flux networks and satellites; link 5 has almost no sustained plankton-and-export record outside a few time series. Budgets therefore pile up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 3. Ostle et al. 2022 report exactly this: satellite chlorophyll *"are not yet able to differentiate the plankton community composition"*, which is a vocabulary failure, not a perception failure, and they call for *"sustained and coordinated monitoring of these oceanic biogeochemical parameters and plankton communities"*.

**Falsifier:** a basin whose chain demonstrably works at links 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at link 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit, though not the dependency ordering.

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first two are different quantities, the middle three are one marine-carbon pool (Fig 2 :31), and the low-overlap witnesses corroborate mechanism rather than a basin-level number.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the biological-pump emphasis on export, as Q1 and Fig 12 :121;
- the solubility-and-circulation emphasis on the anthropogenic sink, as Q2 and Fig 13 :76;
- the PFT emphasis, as atoms in Γ whose reach must be tested, not as the answer;
- the food-web-restructuring emphasis on relations, as D4 and Fig 11 :47;
- the community-coupling emphasis on bonds, as Fig 12 :5 and :117.

It cannot generate the *name* of the species or group that will matter next in a given basin, and it cannot generate the *number* that would make a change "substantial". Those are the two `gap` rows in F10 of `01`. They are gaps at the instrument boundary, not silences: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a flux *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two communities with the same species but different coupling are isomers under Fig 12 :117; communities sharing only a `large cell plus grazer plus fast sinking` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why Q1 and Q2 need different instruments. The standard marine-carbon explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name or a number would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the two naming and magnitude gaps are declared rather than filled.
