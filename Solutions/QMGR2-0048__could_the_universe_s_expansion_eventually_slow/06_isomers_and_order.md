# The three fates as isomers, and the order of the dependency chain

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Can the three candidate fates (slow / recollapse, continue forever, dramatic change) be handled by the figure's own structural vocabulary, and if so in what order must they be settled? Two questions are owed: (1) are the model families **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what is the dependency order that leads from measurement to fate?

## DERIVATION

### Q1  /  Are the model families isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

- ΛCDM, quintessence and phantom are written over the same shared atom set {Ω_m, Ω_k, w(z)}. They differ in the value and time behaviour of w and in the curvature term.
- Over the observed redshift range their predictions are close enough that current data cannot separate them at high confidence. DESI 2024 VI reports a "2.6σ" preference for evolving w with DESI+CMB, rising to "3.9σ" with DES-SN5YR. That is a live discriminating signal, not a settled separation.
- Their asymptotic behaviours are categorically different: eternal dilution, an eternally accelerating de Sitter-like end, or a finite-time Big Rip. This is "decisive in the behaviour."
- ⇒ Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The difference is invisible in the current data and decisive in the future.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two families are shown to have the same asymptotic fate and the same second-order behaviour (so they are the same substance under Fig 12's bond tests), the isomer claim fails and they are one model under two names. Equally, if a figure line is found that forbids arranging these atoms into more than one asymptotic behaviour, the claim fails. Until one fires, this is `specified`, unmeasured at the ground.

### Q2  /  Are they a functional group instead?

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

If the families differ only in one slot (the value of w) while sharing all others, they are members of a functional group, "state → structure → state" analogues with different qualifiers. The stronger isomer reading requires the full shared atom set arranged differently, not just a different number in one slot. The honest position: as currently parameterised they are a **functional group**; the **isomer** claim is the stronger form worth testing. Both are recorded; neither is blended into the other.

### Q3  /  What order settles them?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

| link | content | status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | measure H(z), supernova distances, BAO, CMB acoustic scale | working, returning data |
| 2 vocabulary | define Ω_m, Ω_k, w(z) | working |
| 3 objective | state what counts as a fate (sign of the second derivative, finite-time singularity) | working as a comparison (Fig 12 :112, "a goal is a comparison") |
| 4 planning | extrapolate to t going to infinity | **the break**: depends on link 1 resolving w(z) and Ω_k |
| 5 learn and carry | settle one model and carry it as a head start | not reached |

The break is at link 4, but its cause is upstream: the instrument has not resolved w(z). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Here the instrument exists and is returning something that fails to resolve (DESI's non-trivial but sub-decisive preference), so by Fig 6 :34 the edge to extend is present: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

### Q4  /  Cycle and catalyst in the cosmology frame

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

The asymptotic fate feeds back into what observations are possible: a phantom Big Rip ends the observers; eternal expansion dilutes the signals; a recollapse reverses the arrow. The loop's sign decides whether the extrapolation closes into an observable end or runs open. This is a structural observation about the question, not a cosmological prediction. Mode `general`.

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." In this frame the cosmological constant (if it is a true constant) acts like a catalyst in the equations: it persists across the expansion rather than being spent. Whether dark energy is a catalyst or a spent gradient is exactly the w(z) question. Fig 13 :46: "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent." If w is exactly -1, nothing is spent; if w evolves, something is. That is a grounded question for the instrument, not a figure verdict. This is the one place where the figure vocabulary genuinely bears on the physics, and it still returns a shape (a distinction to measure), not a name (a fate).

### Q5  /  Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

1. **Sent up (x):** the three fates as isomers over {Ω_m, Ω_k, w(z)} and the dependency chain of Q3.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "the fates are isomers and the break is the w(z) measurement."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim such as "DESI's preference means phantom energy will cause a Big Rip."

**R_T is nonzero.** What is dropped on the way back down: the statistical weakness of the preference (2.6 to 3.9 sigma is not settlement), the fact that isomer status is a structural analogy and not a physical derivation, and the dependency order that makes extrapolation illegitimate before measurement. What survives cleanly: the need to discriminate w(z) before naming a fate. R_T is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints; it was not measured numerically because no ground reading of the asymptotic fate exists. Treating the shorthand as identical to the derivation would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## CONCLUSION

The three fates are **isomers** over the shared cosmological atom set in the strong form, and a **functional group** in the weaker parameterised form. They are not three versions of one question and not one averaged question: Fig 9 :57, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Their dependency order puts the w(z) and curvature measurement upstream of any extrapolation. The figure vocabulary (isomer, cycle, catalyst, gradient) genuinely bears on the structure of the question, but it returns only the distinction to measure, never the fate. The isomer claim has a falsifier; the order claim has a dependency break; neither is `grounded`.
