**Subject:** One checkable claim about investment screening, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with a policy position. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` structurally, `specified` for the policy, not `grounded`):** "should countries restrict foreign investment or technology transfers in national security industries" is not one question. It is a union of at least three conditions: the measured capability gap in a named sector, the need for a foreign atom to close that gap, and the dependency created by admitting it. One lever, restrict or do not restrict, clears the cases where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing. So a single yes or no is the wrong shape of answer. The defensible shape is per-sector verdicts with a nameable anchor: a security outcome that does not move when the screening authority is questioned.
>
> **What follows from that, and what I want checked:** capability belongs to the sector and its habitat, not to the domestic firms considered alone. A change that does not change contact changes no capability, however much legislation is passed. Import is the only operator that moves the wall of a frame's own closure. A sealed closure cannot manufacture a capability atom it does not hold, but an imported atom can be the wrong atom if it gives a foreign frame a channel it can close. So conditional admission with sunset clauses is the shape where the import is needed, and refusal is the shape where the dependency itself moves the anchor. Neither is a categorical rule.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures say "do not restrict." They do not hand down a yes or no. They rule out the agent-only improvement, the aggregate national-security average, and the designation that decides an outcome. Which specific sectors qualify, and whether a given restriction net-increases real security, are open. I have marked them as open rather than filled them with a preference.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. A sector's measured capability under adversarial shock rises while its contact surface with foreign frames is strictly reduced and no new atom is imported.
> 2. A purely internal composition adds a genuinely new capability atom absent from the pre-policy closure, with no contact with a differing closure.
> 3. A screening designation settles outcomes while its anchor moves with the designator's stated preference, and is still counted as decisive.
> 4. One lever clears all three conditions across sectors with no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, that part of the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - UNCTAD, World Investment Report 2025, chapter 2, investment screening data: https://unctad.org/publication/world-investment-report-2025
> - Godsell, "National security-related foreign investment screening laws and investment efficiency", Review of Accounting Studies 2026: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-026-09956-1
> - GAO-24-107358, CFIUS mitigation agreements: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-107358
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
