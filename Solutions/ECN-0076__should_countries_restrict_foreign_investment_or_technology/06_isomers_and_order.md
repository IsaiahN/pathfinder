# Structure, order and analogues: the membrane, the union and the two kinds of inflow

**Mode: `specified` for the policy application, `general` for the structural claims.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question treats "foreign investment" and "technology transfer" as one object and asks whether to close the boundary. Two structural questions are owed: (1) what kind of thing is the boundary, a wall or a filter with a direction; (2) are capital and know-how the same atom under a different name, or different atoms whose different bonds change the substance? A third question is about order: does refusal come before or after the search for a substitute?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in D2: capital, know-how, control, access to data, access to a supply channel. Bonds available from the Operators table: `+` (both hold, symmetric), `→` (sequence, ordered), `⇒` (production, ordered), `−` (absence of an operand, ordered), `⋛` (threshold comparison, ordered), `¬` (refusal, unary). ## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the boundary a wall or a filter?

Fig 4 :21: *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

A blanket ban treats the boundary as a wall. The figure defines it as a filter with a direction. A filter admits some things and refuses others, and the direction matters. A tariff-like condition on flow `X` is not the same operator as a prohibition on control `Y`, even if both are called "restriction." The policy vocabulary that collapses them into one lever has recorded the ingredient and lost the bond (Fig 12 :5 about the bond being the rest of the information).

**Reading:** the operable object is a set of directional filters with named operands, not a wall. **Falsifier:** a restriction that blocks all cross-boundary flows identically with no operand-specific effect and no direction. If such a thing exists and works, the filter reading is empty.

### Q2: Are capital and know-how isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."*

Consider two inflows over the atom set {capital, know-how, control, access}:

- A minority equity stake with no board seat and no access rights: capital `+` absence-of-control.
- A technology licence with source access and a maintenance channel: know-how `⇒` production-capability `+` a channel the licensor can close.

Same broad vocabulary word ("foreign investment"), different atoms and different bonds. By Fig 12 they are not two spellings of one thing; they are different substances with different failure modes. The first is a `−` (absence of an operand), the second is a `⇒` plus an open `→` channel. A policy that treats them identically is a flat formula: Fig 12 :14, *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not."*

The stronger isomer claim would require both inflows to be written over the full shared atom set and to differ only in arrangement. As written above they share the set but bind different atoms, so the weaker and safer claim is that they are different molecules in the same functional group, not isomers. **Which one governs is an empirical question about the actual transaction, not a figure question.**

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two inflows that differ only in bond produce identical measured effects at the ground across sectors, the bond distinction carries nothing and the isomer/functional-group framing collapses into the flat formula.

### Q3: What is the order of refusal and mint?

Formula step 2: REFUTED sorts by what is WRONG; the fifth bin is *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."* `¬` is a unary operator, *"this does not hold"*, and *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* (Operators table :57-58). Fig 6 :42 records that the reach falls because something was added, not deleted: a refusal adds a term and removes standing to compose over the refused one.

So the order is: refuse the false term first ("all cross-border capital in sector `s` is a security threat" or "all inflows are harmless"), then mint the discriminating instrument. Minting before refusal builds on a term that should have lost standing, so the reach is computed over a corrupted library. Most screening reform effort lands at the minting stage (new rules, new thresholds) while the false term remains held. That is Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

**Falsifier:** a case where minting a new instrument before refusing the false term closes `R_s` and the false term was not in fact false. Then the refusal-first order is not universal.

### Q4: What is the order of the union?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Dependency order for ECN-0076:

1. **Perception:** is there a measured capability gap in sector `s`? Without it there is no residual.
2. **Vocabulary:** do we have terms for the imported atom, the dependency channel, and the threshold? Fig 3 :7 about *"terms for what things afford"*.
3. **Objective:** what counts as security progress in our own terms? This is the middle link that usually has no instrument (Fig 3 :24-27).
4. **Planning:** choose admit, condition, or refuse.
5. **Learn and carry:** verify at the ground and carry the method, not the recording.

The common policy sequence is the reverse: plan first (pass a screening law), then look for a metric, then discover the objective was never instrumented. A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can (Fig 3 :22-23).

**Falsifier:** a screening regime whose objective link was demonstrably uninstrumented and whose planning link nevertheless closed `R_s` at the ground. That would mean the middle link is not load-bearing.

### Q5: Analogues, and what they do not license

- **Membrane (Fig 4):** a filter with a direction. Corroborates Q1 at the shape level. It does not license a particular threshold.
- **Isomer and functional group (Fig 12):** licenses the claim that bond choice changes the substance. Does not license naming which bond is "the security one."
- **Habitat substitution and test harness (Fig 11 :66-70):** a domestic pilot line is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability. World corroboration from Pham and Scholten 2026: *"If anyone is removed, the chain breaks"*; a self-contained operation *"would actually favor a Chinese invasion."*
- **Weaponized interdependence (Farrell and Newman 2019):** the chokepoint effect corroborates that an imported channel can be closed by the foreign frame. This is a low-overlap witness for the mechanism, not a verdict.

No analogue supplies a name for the missing atom. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## CONCLUSION

The boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall. Capital inflows and technology licences are different molecules in the same functional group, not interchangeable spellings. Refusal comes before mint, and the dependency chain runs perception to vocabulary to objective to planning, never in reverse. These three structural results support `01` F1 to F10 without adding any new primitive or any country-specific policy prescription. The one live strain is the isomer question at the transaction level, which needs the actual bond structure of the deal, not a further argument.
