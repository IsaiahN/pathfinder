## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the form an answer must have, statable before it can be filled. It constrains; it does not decide. For this question the shape is:

- per-sector-slot, not a national average (Fig 1 :19);
- a union of capability, import need, and dependency, kept separate (Fig 9 :55);
- anchored to a measured security outcome, not to a designation (Fig 2 :29);
- directional: admit, condition, or refuse are different bonds, not three spellings of one policy;
- mode-labelled and refutable.

The forbidden fillers are "restrict" and "do not restrict" as universal verdicts, and the naming of any particular industry or technology as *the* missing atom.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30 and :31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"* and *"rather than the cause."* The effect here is delivery under adversarial pressure, not the statutory word "security."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Applied here: say what a sector would look like if the policy were working (a measured continuity of supply, a verified absence of a capturable switch), not whether the statute was passed.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, because by then the searcher has *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* For this question the stop is the declared `gap` in F9 of `01`: the shape is complete when we can say exactly what instrument is missing.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A measured security outcome under adversarial pressure would name the atom. No such measurement is in this package.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51 about convergent derivation and adopted import looking identical in the contents).

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism for why a reading is not a name. Metamerism: a color instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* The source might be one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Applied to ECN-0076: a sector that looks secure on a compliance checklist is a projection. Several underlying sources (genuine capability, an untested dependency, a captured channel that the foreign frame has not yet used) give the same reading. The screening checklist cannot discriminate among them, so it cannot name the source. Naming "foreign ownership" as the source of the failure is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a name.

## 4. The specific forbidden error for this question

**"The figures tell us whether to restrict."** This is forbidden in two ways:

1. The figures return a shape, not a name or a yes/no verdict on a policy. The canonical README: *"a generator of the shape of an answer at any level."*
2. A frame cannot certify its own direction. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Metatheories cannot validate themselves."* The framework cannot certify that its own application to national security is the right frame. That is a seat's office, and the seat must range against the ground.

A second forbidden error is **"we measured security because the law was passed."** Fig 13 :16: *"it names what is being measured"*; Fig 13 :26: *"it decides what the measurement would mean."* A statute names neither. Counting screened transactions is a reading of the channel, not a measurement of the ground.

## 5. What the shape means for the answer file

`07_the_tether_answer.md` therefore does not answer "yes, restrict" or "no, do not restrict." It answers with:

- the union decomposition (three conditions, intersections named as compounds);
- a per-sector test: does this sector have a measured ground, a missing atom that must be imported, and a dependency that can move the anchor?
- the directional default: condition where the import is needed, refuse where the dependency is the threat, both revisable at the ground;
- an explicit `gap` for which sectors qualify and for the net empirical sign;
- four refuters written before the conclusion.

That is the shape. Filling it with a country or an industry would be a name, and names here are not licensed.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

For this question: a well-known economist, a former intelligence official, or a prominent think tank can give a screening proposal a budget to be tried. None of them can settle whether the sector still delivers under pressure. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is high, so the check should be bought from low-overlap frames, not from the most prestigious voices in the FDI policy community.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The essay calls simulated output *"simulacra"*.

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `09_watson_answer.md`, and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of any screening regime and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on. The verdict is `general` and `specified`, never `grounded`.
