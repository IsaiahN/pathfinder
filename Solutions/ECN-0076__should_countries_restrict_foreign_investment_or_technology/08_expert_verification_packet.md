# Expert verification packet: the Tether on investment screening and national security

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher or policy auditor can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the policy application, not `grounded`.** No security outcome is claimed to have been measured.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Should countries restrict foreign investment or technology transfer in national security industries" is a union of three conditions, not one question: the measured capability gap in a named sector, the foreign atom needed to close it, and the dependency created by admitting it. Capability belongs to sector and habitat, so an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Import is the only operator that moves a frame's closure, so a sealed sector cannot manufacture a missing capability atom, but an imported atom can be the wrong atom if it creates a capturable channel. The boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing contact. The default is conditional admission where the import is needed and refusal where the dependency is the threat, both revisable at a measured ground; the designation itself cannot be the ground. Which sectors qualify is an open `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: capability is contact-dependent, not domestic-agent-dependent

- **Statement:** a restriction that leaves a sector's contact with the ground and with other frames unchanged changes no capability, however much law is written. A capability claim must be measured as delivery under adversarial pressure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **Check:** is the capability claim in the case at hand a property of the domestic firms in isolation, or of those firms plus their supplier, talent and channel habitat?
- **Refuter:** a measured capability gain under adversarial shock with a strictly reduced foreign contact surface and no new atom imported. If observed, C1 is false.
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 and Fig 4 :21, low overlap).

### C2: the closure is a barrier, and the boundary is a filter, not a wall

- **Statement:** no amount of domestic search inside a sealed sector adds a capability atom the sector does not hold; capital, know-how and channel access are different atoms with different bonds, so one lever cannot clear them all.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 4 :21 *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*
- **Check:** name the atom the restriction is supposed to protect or supply. Is it already in the domestic closure? If it is absent, what instrument would bring it in, and what channel would the import create?
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that purely internal composition added a genuinely new capability atom absent from the pre-policy closure, with no contact with a differing closure. That breaks idempotence.
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 8 and Fig 6, different figures, low overlap).

### C3: the designation cannot be its own anchor

- **Statement:** if the screening authority's own label of "national security" settles each case, the anchor updates with the frame and the check becomes a convention that has stopped being one. A stakeholder interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying and must be declared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :43 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
- **Check:** what measured outcome would change the authority's decision, and would that outcome change if the authority were told the designated label? GAO-24-107358 found that *"it's unclear how they make enforcement decisions"* and that CFIUS has no documented process for periodic relevance review, which is a channel failure, not a ground failure (Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*).
- **Refuter:** a designation demonstrably unaffected by the designator's preference and validated by an external measurement. If that exists, C3 does not govern the case.
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 10, low overlap).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the figures say restrict, or do not restrict. They return a shape, not a verdict.
- Not which sector or technology is the right one. That is a name and no figure line licenses it.
- Not that foreign control is always a threat, or that openness is always safe. Both are terms to be refused first (formula step 2).
- Not that a low rejection rate proves safety. It is a prevalence filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a domestic pilot line proves capability. It proves wiring (Fig 11 :70).
- Not `grounded`. No security outcome was measured in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A per-sector delivery-under-pressure measurement independent of the screening authority.
2. An instrument that distinguishes genuine capability from an unused but capturable dependency in a low-rejection sector.
3. A detection test for a closable channel in the imported technology before it is used.
4. The six standing falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 8 :32, Fig 4 :21, Fig 2 :29, :43 (five short passages).
2. Take one sector you know. Name the atom the restriction is meant to protect or supply, and say whether it is already in the domestic closure.
3. Say what measurement would change your mind about that sector, and whether the authority's designation would move it.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish capability from an untested dependency, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
