## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured, not assumed zero. I ran the trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39-40 (contact-dependent capability), Fig 6 :14 and Fig 8 :32 (import and idempotence), Fig 4 :21 (filter), Fig 2 :29 and :43 (anchor and convention), Fig 9 :55 (union).

3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a national investment-screening policy for a named sector.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **difference between capital, know-how, control and channel access** (Fig 12 bond rule), which the shorthand flattens into "foreign investment";
- the **dependency cost of an accepted import** (Fig 6 :24), which the shorthand names but does not price;
- the **unstated stake of the screening seat** (Fig 10 :59), which the shorthand turns into a procedural recommendation;
- the **empirical net sign**, which the shorthand cannot produce and which `07` correctly leaves as `gap`.

What survives cleanly: per-slot indexing, contact-dependent capability, the barrier against internal manufacture of a missing atom, isolation as substitution, and the anchor rule. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero, the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
