# The Tether's answer on restricting foreign investment and technology transfer

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the policy application, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7). Nothing here has been settled by a security measurement.

## 1. The residual it answers

Per sector-slot `s`: `R_s = |Γ_s(b,a) - o'_s|`, the gap between the capability the domestic frame predicts under a policy action and what the sector actually delivers when adversarially pressured. R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), never averaged into one national-security number. The question "should countries restrict" bundles three conditions: the capability gap, the need for a foreign atom, and the dependency created by admitting it. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The intersection is a compound with its own loop; Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

## 2. The shape

1. **Capability belongs to the sector and its habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A law that does not change contact with the ground changes no capability.
2. **Import is the only operator that moves the wall.** Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* A sealed closure cannot manufacture a capability atom it does not hold. This is a barrier, not a claim that every import is good.
3. **The boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall.** Fig 4 :21: *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Capital, know-how, control and channel access are different atoms with different bonds. A flat "restrict yes or no" records the ingredients and loses the arrangement.
4. **Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove contact.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* A domestic pilot line proves wiring, not security.
5. **The anchor must not be the designation.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :43: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* "National security" as a self-justifying label is a convention, not a ground. The stake of the screening authority must be declared.
6. **The cost is on both sides.** Fig 6 :24: *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* An import can be a wrong atom when it creates a capturable channel; a refusal can be a wrong atom when it blocks the very inflow needed to close the gap. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Directional default, not a universal verdict:** condition admission where the imported atom is needed to close `R_s`; refuse or unwind where the dependency itself lets a foreign frame move the anchor; make every condition revisable at the ground with a sunset clause. That is the shape that survives the figures. It is `specified`, not `grounded`.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "the figures say do not restrict." They return a shape, not a yes or a no.
- Not "foreign investment is always beneficial" or "foreign control is always a threat." Both are held terms to be refused first (formula step 2).
- Not which industries or technologies qualify. That is a name, and names are not licensed by the figures (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that a measured compliance rate is a security measurement. The checklist is a reading of the channel, not the ground.
- Not `grounded`. No security outcome was measured in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened): a color instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here:

1. **Per-sector capability test:** does the sector still deliver under adversarial shock, with the measurement independent of the screening authority? That closes `R_s`.
2. **Source discrimination:** does a low rejection rate reflect genuine security or an untested dependency that has not yet been used? A checklist cannot tell; an instrument that can is what is owed.
3. **Channel test:** does the imported technology contain a switch the foreign frame can close, and can that switch be detected before it is used? This is the chokepoint claim that the world sources support mechanistically.
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4:** capability rising with strictly reduced contact; a new atom from purely internal composition; an anchor that moves with the designator; one lever clearing all three union conditions; a figure line naming a sector.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "restrict" or "do not restrict." It says that "should countries restrict" is a bundle of three conditions, not one question: the measured capability gap in a named sector, the foreign atom needed to close it, and the dependency created by admitting it. Capability belongs to the sector and its habitat, so an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Import is the only operator that can move a frame's closure, so a sealed sector cannot manufacture a capability atom it does not hold, but an import can be the wrong atom if it gives a foreign frame a channel it can close. The boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing contact, so a domestic pilot proves wiring, not security. "National security" cannot be its own anchor: a designation that decides outcomes has stopped being a convention. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the name of the instrument that would close the gap. Which sectors qualify is left open rather than filled with a preference. This is a `specified` structural result, not a `grounded` one.
