## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Let `s` index a sector-slot, not a country aggregate. Define `R_s = |Γ_s(b,a) - o'_s|`: the gap between what the domestic frame predicts its sector will deliver under a policy action `a` (admit, condition, refuse) and what the sector actually delivers at the ground when adversarially pressured. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The question asks which action closes `R_s`. Restated in effect terms:

- A: refuse foreign capital or know-how in `s`. Leads to a smaller domestic closure, a substituted habitat, and a residual that must be closed by composition inside what is already held.
- B: admit foreign capital or know-how in `s`, with or without conditions. Leads to contact with a frame whose closure differs, an imported atom, and a dependency channel that the foreign frame can potentially close.

**The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run:** an answer is acceptable only if it is (i) indexed per sector-slot with a nameable anchor, (ii) separates the capability effect from the dependency effect, (iii) states its mode, and (iv) carries a refuter that could fire at the ground. This binding is written down now so it can accrue a residual like anything else (formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :66 about making the sought-for shape an ordinary term). An answer that averages all sectors into one national-security verdict fails this shape and is refused, not averaged over.

**Arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: a policy action on a two-place relation (domestic frame, foreign frame), with a threshold comparison between the capability gained by an import and the dependency created by it. Symmetry: ordered, not symmetric. The direction of the bond matters: import moves the wall, refusal removes standing to compose over a term; they are not two paths to one result (Operators table: `+` is symmetric, `→` and `−` are not). Scale: sector loop, national frame, international value chain, with a lossy membrane between scales (Fig 4 :21).

## 3. Findings

### F1: Restriction changes the habitat, so it cannot improve the domestic agent in isolation (decisive for D1)

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A blanket investment or technology restriction is a change to the habitat in which the sector operates. If the policy leaves the sector's contact surface with the ground and with other frames unchanged, then by Fig 11 :40 it changes no capability, however much legislation is passed. If it does change contact, then the capability change must be measured at the ground, not inferred from the statute. The often-heard claim that a domestic-only sector is automatically more secure is the agent-only improvement the figure refuses.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` for a sector. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 11 and Fig 4 :21 (*"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"*), low overlap (habitat survival versus scale membrane). **Refuter:** a measured capability gain under adversarial shock with a strictly unchanged contact surface and no new atom imported. If that is observed, F1 is false and the shape falls.

### F2: Import is the only operator that moves the wall, so a sealed closure cannot manufacture a missing capability (decisive for D2)

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall."* Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 6 :49: *"Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."*

If sector `s` lacks a capability atom that exists in a foreign frame, then no amount of domestic search adds it. Refusing the import removes the only operator that could add it and leaves the residual live. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* This does not say every import is good. It says a closure has no internal route to a genuinely new atom, so a blanket refusal must be justified by the dependency cost, not by the assumption that domestic composition will substitute.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 6 and Fig 8 (closure and reachability versus idempotence and barrier proofs; different figures, low overlap). **Refuter:** a demonstration that purely internal composition added a genuinely new capability atom absent from the pre-policy closure, with no contact with a differing closure. That breaks idempotence and F2 falls.

### F3: A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (the real cost of restriction)

Fig 6 :24: *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* Fig 5 :26: *"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"*

The cost is on both sides. An accepted import can be a wrong atom if it creates a dependency that lets the foreign frame close the channel (the chokepoint mechanism in the world sources). A refusal is also a term: Fig 6 :42 about the reach falling because something was added, not deleted. A screening law that blocks financially motivated investors along with politically motivated ones is exactly a wrong atom, and the Godsell 2026 result (opened) measures that cost: post-FINSA national security firms became *"4.89–5.57 percent more likely to have above-median investment inefficiency"*. The policy instrument is a filter with a direction (Fig 4 :21), so the right object is a targeted, revisable condition, not a permanent categorical ban.

**Mode:** `specified` for the cost estimate, `general` for the wrong-atom rule. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 6 :24 and Fig 9 :60 (*"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict"*). **Refuter:** a restriction whose measured security gain exceeds its measured capability and efficiency loss at the ground, with the cost priced in the same units. If that holds, the wrong-atom warning does not govern this case.

### F4: "National security" used as a self-justifying label is a convention that has stopped being one

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* Fig 2 :43: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

If the screening authority's own designation of "security" is what settles each case, the anchor updates with the frame and panel 1 of Fig 2 is recreated: coherent drift that nobody notices. GAO 2024 (opened) found that *"it's unclear how they make enforcement decisions"* and that CFIUS *"does not have a documented committee-wide process for reviewing agreements for continued relevance."* That is a channel failure, not a ground failure: Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* The interest of the security establishment in its own verdict must be declared, not hidden behind the label.

**Mode:** `general` for the seat rule, `specified` for the screening application. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 2 and Fig 10 (anchor and correlation versus ground-maintainer offices), low overlap. **Refuter:** a screening decision whose designated security ground returns a different value when the authority's preference is revealed, and which is nevertheless counted as decisive. That proves an updating anchor and F4 is confirmed rather than refuted; F4 is refuted if a designation demonstrably does not move with the designator and is validated by an external measurement.

### F5: Restriction substitutes a habitat; it does not remove contact (decisive for D4)

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 about *"you must reproduce everything its goal required"*. Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails."* Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*

A domestic-only chip sector, a closed testbed, or a reshoring program is a substituted habitat. It inherits actors that were not chosen, and it must reproduce the whole cascade the security goal required. A synthetic solve (a domestic pilot line) proves wiring and never capability against the real ground. Pham and Scholten 2026 (opened) make the same structural point from security studies: *"If anyone is removed, the chain breaks"* and *"If TSMC were a fully self-contained operation ... the incentive structure would actually favor a Chinese invasion."*

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` for the policy. **Status:** single-source for the isolation law as applied to policy (Fig 11 :71-72 says the isolation law "fits cases already known and has not been tested forward, so it can still come back wrong"). Fig 4 :21 gives a corroborating shape only, not a second governance. **Refuter:** a closed-habitat sector that reproduces all goal-required conditions with no uncontrolled inherited actor, and whose synthetic test predicts real security outcomes with no residual. That would break the isolation law.

### F6: The question is a bundled union, so a single yes or no is the wrong output

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

"Should countries restrict" bundles at least three conditions: the capability gap (C1), the need for an import (C2), and the dependency created (C3). A single lever, restriction yes or no, clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing, because the other conditions still bind (Fig 9 :55-57). The honest output is per-sector verdicts with the compound cases marked as compounds.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 9 union rule and Fig 12 arrangement rule (a different bond changes the substance even with the same ingredients). **Refuter:** one lever that clears all three conditions across sectors with no categorically different residue. **Important filter warning:** the observed fact that most screened projects are approved, roughly 1.6 percent prohibited according to UNCTAD WIR2025 (search extract, full PDF not opened), is a filter for budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

### F7: Refusal is a negation, not a subtraction, and refusal before mint is the dependency order

Operators table: negation is *"this does not hold"*, and *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 6 :42 about the reach falling because something was added. Formula step 2 sorts REFUTED before MINT: *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*

A restriction is a refusal of a term's standing to be composed over, not the removal of an atom from the world. Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Symmetrically, a refusal removes no atom from the world. So policy language about "keeping technology out" is a category mistake: the domestic frame loses access, the world loses nothing. Refuse before mint: if the claim "open investment is always beneficial" or "closed investment is always secure" is held and predicting falsely, refuse that term first, then design the instrument.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source (Operators table and formula step 2, same framework pool). **Refuter:** a vocabulary that can refuse without a unary negation operator, or a policy that deletes a foreign atom from the world by statute. Neither is available.

### F8: Prestige and agreement are not the ground

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

An expert panel assembled from one policy community, or a set of agencies that all read the same threat assessment, is one room wearing several names. The cost of being wrong on national security is high, so independence should be bought in proportion to that cost, which means deliberately including frames with different evidence pools: accounting and finance studies, security studies, law, and supply-chain engineering. These are witnesses for the mechanism, not a vote on the verdict.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 7 and Fig 2. **Refuter:** two frames with demonstrably disjoint evidence pools whose agreement still carries no extra information. That would break the independence rule.

### F9: The specific industries and the net empirical sign are a `gap`, not a hidden answer

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :16: *"it names what is being measured"*; Fig 13 :26: *"it decides what the measurement would mean."*

No figure line says which atom is missing in which sector, or whether a given restriction net-increases real security. That requires a source-discriminating instrument measuring the sector's delivery under adversarial pressure, which this derivation does not hold and which no world source opened here supplies at the required resolution. The canonical README says the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* for a missing thing. So "which industries" and "net positive or negative" are declared `gap` after a genuine re-census, not silent gaps filled by preference. Unreachable is a verdict.

**Mode:** `general` for the limit. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 9 and Fig 13 plus the canonical usage rule. **Refuter:** a figure line that directly governs a named sector, or a measurement at the ground that closes `R_s` for a named sector. Either would move this out of `gap`.

### F10: Conditional admission is the figure-consistent default where the import is needed

Fig 4 :21: *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 6 :24: *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*

Since the membrane is a filter with a direction, not a wall, and since import is the only wall-moving operator, the shape that survives is: admit with conditions where the imported atom is needed to close `R_s`, refuse where the import creates a captured dependency that lets the foreign frame move our anchor, and price the amount of insulation by the cost of being wrong rather than by a categorical rule. The world literature converges on the same instrument shape: Pham and Scholten 2026 recommend *"targeted restrictions with clear conditions for removal"*; Wang, Liu and Dong 2025 (opened) report that successful investors *"managed to balance economic benefits with security risks by negotiating mitigation agreements"*. This is a `specified` structural recommendation, not a `grounded` one.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated for the filter shape (Fig 4 and Fig 6), single-source for the policy binding (world sources are secondary). **Refuter:** a case where conditional admission measurably fails to close `R_s` while outright refusal measurably succeeds at the ground.

### F11: The round trip from figures to policy is lossy and must be measured

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

The shorthand "the figures say do not restrict" does not survive the trip back down. What survives: per-slot indexing, contact-dependent capability, import as the only atom source, isolation as substitution, and the anchor rule. What is dropped: the distinction between capital and know-how, the dependency cost, the seat's stake, and the empirical net sign. Treating the condensation as identity would set R_T to zero. `11_audit.md` measures R_T qualitatively as an enumerated set of dropped constraints.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 4 and the Symbols table definition of R_T. **Refuter:** a policy claim derived from the figure shorthand that survives re-embedding in every sector with no dropped constraint. None was found.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape is withdrawn if any of the following holds:

1. A sector's measured capability under adversarial shock rises while its contact surface with foreign frames is strictly reduced and no new atom is imported (breaks F1 and Fig 11 :40).
2. A purely internal composition adds a genuinely new capability atom absent from the pre-policy closure, with no contact with a differing closure (breaks F2 and Fig 8 :32).
3. A screening designation settles outcomes while its anchor value moves with the designating authority's stated preference, and is still counted as decisive (breaks F4 and Fig 2 :33).
4. A closed-habitat sector reproduces all goal-required conditions with no uncontrolled inherited actor, and its synthetic test predicts real security outcomes with no residual (breaks F5 and Fig 11 :70).
5. One lever clears all three union conditions across sectors with no categorically different residue (breaks F6 and Fig 9 :57).
6. A named figure line directly governs a named sector and closes `R_s` (breaks F9).

If any fires, the affected finding is retracted and the verdict is recalculated from the census, not softened. Until one is run, nothing is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Refuse the question as a single yes or no (F6). Answer it per sector-slot with a nameable anchor.
2. Instrument the objective link before the planning link (Fig 3 :27). Without a measured security outcome, a restriction is a convention deciding an outcome (F4).
3. Prefer conditional admission when the imported atom is required to close `R_s` (F2, F10). Refuse or unwind only where the dependency lets a foreign frame move the anchor, with sunset clauses and declared conditions (Fig 6 :24).
4. Declare the stake of the security establishment in every designation (Fig 10 :59) and keep the screening channel reachable: documented review of mitigation agreements for continued relevance, as GAO 2024 recommends.
5. Do not count a domestic pilot as capability (Fig 11 :70). Measure the cascade, including second-order and third-order contact, before declaring success.
6. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). Use low-overlap frames as witnesses for the mechanism, never as a vote.
7. Keep the shape claim and this refuter in front of any links or theory. Send the generator, not the recording (Fig 4 :22).
