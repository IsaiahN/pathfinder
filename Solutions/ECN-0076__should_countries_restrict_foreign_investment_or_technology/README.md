## The question restated as an effect

A state must keep a capability in a named sector such that the sector still delivers under adversarial pressure. Two options are on the table. Option A closes the boundary to foreign capital and know-how in that sector. Option B admits them, possibly with conditions. In effect terms: does closing the boundary raise the sector's measured capability at the ground, or does it substitute a habitat in which the capability must be reproduced from a smaller closure? What an answer would look like: per-sector-slot verdicts, each with a nameable anchor (a measured security outcome), a stated mode, and a refuter written before the conclusion. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` section 0 and is therefore refusable by the same test as every other term (formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that restriction is right or wrong.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes agreement worthless.
2. Fig 2 :43: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Selecting checkers by desired verdict turns the check into a convention.
3. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: public opt-in institutional channels avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM style failures that unsolicited bulk outreach to harvested addresses creates.
