# Outreach draft — validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader can state the shape claim and its refuter in their own words. The output must survive its own check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how institutions claim authority

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a historical question: how rival institutions compete to define
> authoritative knowledge. I have one output I would like you to check against what
> you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not
> asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is
> nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** authority is not a stock an
> institution holds. It is maintained contact with a ground that does not change
> its answer because of what the institution thinks. That gives four checkable
> expectations.
>
> 1. An institution that certifies its own authority using its own publication count,
>    citation share, or prestige has made itself its own anchor. The anchor must not
>    update on the frame it is supposed to settle.
> 2. Two institutions have a real surplus to fight over only where their closures
>    differ at the evidence level. Two bodies reading the same reviews and using the
>    same instruments are one frame wearing two names. Their agreement is not corroboration.
> 3. A convention such as peer review or credentialing is harmless while it only
>    coordinates meaning. The moment it decides who is published or funded, it is a
>    verdict and must be answerable to a ground, not defended as a convention.
> 4. A sequence of like-minded institutions is one long room: motion, not progress.
>    The next room must differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the winning institution, that any
> particular academy is authoritative, that citations measure truth, or that the
> conflict is mostly sociological rather than about the world. The framework returns
> a shape, not a name. Where it cannot name a victor it says so.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an institution whose authority survives a non-updating change in the ground,
>    with its channel open and its own credibility held fixed;
> 2. an authority convention that demonstrably changes no outcome and cannot be
>    checked against any ground;
> 3. a historical run of near-identical institutions, with real ground pressure,
>    that still progresses;
> 4. an institution whose authority rises while its contacts, instruments, and
>    habitat are held fixed.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract
> the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement.
> If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public source links (the
> figures extract, the canonical usage rule, and the metalaw note). The figures are
> the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the eight-step loop is public in the figures
> folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write
> down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of
> what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the loop as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode (Formula "Direction"; Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"*).

Nothing here asks a recipient to trust the framework, and nothing here is sent by this agent. Hosting and sending are yours.
