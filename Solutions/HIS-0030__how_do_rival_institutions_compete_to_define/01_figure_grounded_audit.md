## 0. The question as an effect

> How do rival institutions compete to define authoritative knowledge?

Restated in effect terms. Two or more institutional frames, each with its own library Γ_i (criteria, methods, credentials, journals, standards), each claiming the standing to say what counts as knowledge, meet over a contested object: the definition of authoritative knowledge. What is being decided is not which institution wins a vote. It is (a) whether each claimant ranges against a ground that does not move when the claimant is pushed, (b) whether the rival closures differ at the evidence level so a union surplus exists, (c) whether an institution's authority convention has crossed the line into deciding an outcome, and (d) whether, across time, one room is selected over another by the ground rather than handed a closed label.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a **per-domain shape** saying what authority must consist of in each domain, a refuter per domain, and an honest stop where no figure line selects a particular historical victor. This is the sought-for shape written down so it can accrue a residual like anything else (Fig 5 :65-66).

Option A: treat "authoritative knowledge" as a property of statements that institutions possess in differing degrees, and explain rivalry as a contest for a share of that property. Option B: treat it as a relation between a frame and a non-updating ground, and explain rivalry as the maintenance of contact plus selection between differing rooms. Option B is the one derived below.

## 1. Residual and domain census

**R, in effect terms.** The gap between what a frame predicts its own authority rests on and what the ground actually pays. Indexed per institutional slot s, never aggregated into a single "authority of science" number (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**
- *Arity:* N ≥ 2 rival frames plus one contested object. Binary at minimum, usually plural.
- *Symmetry:* each claim has the same surface form ("we are authoritative"), but authority is a threshold relation, not a symmetric join. The contest is not a negotiation between equal parties (Fig 2 :35).
- *Scale:* intra-institutional (one frame settling its own members), inter-institutional (the surplus between frames), and diachronic (selection between rooms over time). Recursive: a settled institution becomes an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104).

**One line per domain. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the intra-institutional frame | members, editors, reviewers, credentialing bodies, the institution's criteria | credibility, predicates minted, citations, symbolic capital | a genuine ground iff it does not update on the institution's own verdict (Fig 2 :29-30) | partition: which compositions are legal inside the frame, plus the anchor test |
| D2, the inter-institutional contest | rival institutions, each a closure Γ_i with its own atoms and bonds | authority claims, which operate as conventions that decide outcomes | the external ground common to the contest, iff the contest is constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36) | union compound: overlapping conditions with an unassignable shared fraction (Fig 9 :54-57) |
| D3, the authority convention and its maintainer | the seat that authors conventions across a seam | legitimacy, standing, provenance | the ground, never agreement (Fig 10 :93) | seat office, not loop: what may be authored and what may not |
| D4, historical selection among rooms | successive institutional rooms, habitat of practices, funders, journals, academies | survival and arrival of methods and institutions | the ground, which does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24) | selection between rooms, not negotiation within one (Fig 7 :35) |

**Partition or union.** D1 is a partition question (what composes across the two kinds inside one library). D2 is a **union**, not a partition: rival institutions can be differently anchored (one to experiment, one to revelation or tradition, one to a market or a regulator), so their "disagreement" is not one quantity (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). D3 and D4 are distinct loops with distinct anchors and must not be averaged into D2.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Per Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* First establish whether a ground exists and is reachable (perception and anchor, D1). Second, give the frame terms for what the rival's claim affords (vocabulary). Third, say what would count as progress in the contest (objective). Fourth, plan the comparison (D2). Fifth, name what worked and carry it to the next room (D4). The maintainer seat sits outside this chain (D3). Effort spent planning a comparison before the anchor is established arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :21).

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Authority is not a stock held by a frame; it is a relation to a ground that does not update (`general` shape, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

An institution that certifies its own authority by its own publication count, citation share, or prestige has installed a frame as its anchor. Fig 1 :27 is exact: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

So the first thing the competition is *not* is a contest over a stock of authority. Authority is the name we give to maintained contact with a ground. When the institution moves, the contact changes; the ground does not (Fig 10 :24 **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**).

**Refuter for F1:** show an institution whose authority survives a demonstrated, non-updating change in what the ground returns, with its channel to that ground still open and its own credibility variable held fixed. If that is possible, authority is a stock, and this finding fails.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 2, Fig 10 and Symbols table, with low overlap between the per-slot residual rule and the anchor definition.

### F2 — Rivalry is triangulation only where closures differ, and is one frame wearing two names where they do not (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :19: **"closures differ, so a surplus exists"**; :45 **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**

Fig 8 :13-15 names the collapsed case: *"same atoms, different names / their union adds nothing / two names for one frame."* Two rival institutions that recruit from the same pool, read the same reviews, and use the same instruments have high overlap. Their public disagreement is variation, not a verdict.

But Fig 8 :50 blocks the easy conclusion: **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."** Two institutions can arrive at the same claim by genuinely independent instruments. That is the opposite of a rivalry, yet it looks identical in the contents. Fig 8 :51-52 supplies the discriminator:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

So the competition is decided by **provenance of the closure**, not by the claim text. This is the same distinction the exemplar used for the GR/QM mapping, applied here to institutions.

**Refuter for F2:** exhibit two institutions with demonstrably low overlap (different instruments, different evidence pools, different closures) whose union produces no term either could reach alone, and whose agreement still tracks the same ground. That would show surplus without rivalry (corroboration) and force a re-census of what "compete" names.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 2 :31-32 (*"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"*).

### F3 — The competition is not negotiation; agreement selected for receptiveness is a disqualifying correlation (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** Fig 2 :46: **"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."**

Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention. Agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool; agreement about a shared meaning is the product."*

This is the sharpest answer to the question. Rival institutions *can* negotiate a shared meaning, and that is a legitimate convention. They *cannot* settle what is authoritative by negotiating, because the thing being contested is constitutively about a ground (Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*), and a negotiated compromise can move. Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

The Mertonian counter-norms make the mechanism concrete in the world: Mitroff's Apollo scientists countered universalism with particularism, assessing knowledge "based on the reputation and past productivity of the individual or research group." That is the frame scoring itself with a quantity it produces, exactly F1. (World source, secondary; see `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD.)

**Refuter for F3:** show a case where the authoritative definition is constitutively a convention about shared meaning (a unit, a classification, a naming rule) and where no ground outside the agreement is appealable. Then negotiation *is* the legitimate mechanism and Fig 2 :41 governs instead: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* This refuter is real, and §7 keeps it as a live boundary condition rather than erasing it.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Seat Map :163, and the Merton counter-norm report (low overlap: the world source is a 1974 survey of Apollo scientists, not the figure pool).

### F4 — An authority convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 10 :45: **"It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."** Fig 10 :46: **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does."**

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** The amended Seat Map states the same law at :163 with the final clause: *"and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

This is where most real institutional rivalry lives. Peer review, credentialing, journal scope, and funding criteria begin as conventions (shared meanings about what will count as evidence). The moment they decide who is published, hired, or funded, they are verdicts. They must then be corrected by the ground, not accepted as conventions. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** And :61: **"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."** An academy whose prestige is the thing being defended cannot also be the anchor that settles the dispute.

**Refuter for F4:** exhibit an authority convention whose operation demonstrably changes no outcome (no gatekeeping, no resource allocation, no credential), merely coordinating meaning across a seam. Then Fig 10 :47 does not bite and the convention remains legitimate. Recorded as a live boundary for institutions that are purely standard-setting bodies.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 2 :43 (*"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*), and the Seat Map.

### F5 — Institutions are closures; re-arranging them cannot add a ground (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

A rival institution can restructure its committees, merge departments, adopt open peer review, or change its journal's scope. All of that is arrangement inside the closure. It never adds a ground. What can add an atom is contact with a frame whose closure differs: an import, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13 *"source gate: provenance / shadow test: explains a gap"*).

Fig 6 :24 prices the import: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."** This is why simply copying the rival's criteria is the expensive move: it both imports a possibly wrong atom and spends the independence that made the comparison informative.

**Refuter for F5:** show an institution that adds a genuine new ground (not a new criterion) by internal re-arrangement alone, with no contact with a differing closure. If that occurs, closure is not idempotent for institutional frames and Fig 6 :21 is misapplied here.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13. No strain after re-census: these govern closures at any stratum, and an institution is a bounded system with a library (Symbols table :111-112).

### F6 — A chain of agreeing institutions is one long room; progress requires low overlap and ground pressure (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."** Fig 7 :30: **"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives."** Fig 7 :32: **"→ one long room: motion, no progress."**

Fig 7 :35 is decisive: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Progress per handoff has two factors (Fig 7 :37-39): the bracket's fidelity across the handoff and the ground's pressure on the room. Zero either and the chain runs in place. Institutional history that narrates a succession of like-minded bodies "advancing knowledge" is a mirror chain unless the next body differed and matched the residual the last one surfaced.

Fig 10 :72: **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

**Refuter for F6:** exhibit a historical sequence in which rooms were near-identical in closure (high ρ), the ground's pressure was demonstrably nonzero, and the field still progressed. That would falsify the two-factor claim as applied to institutions.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 7 and Fig 10.

### F7 — Prestige and citation counts are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 9 :51: **"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."** An institution's fame, its prize count, its address, its founding date: evidence bearing on its maker, not on the residual. The outside frame is denied it (Seat 4 "must not hold the interiors, the records, the anchor, any stake, or any authority").

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."**

The Matthew effect makes the failure concrete in the world: Merton and Zuckerman coined the term for the way "eminent scientists will often get more credit than a comparatively unknown researcher, even if their work is similar." A competition in which the already-authoritative institution wins because it is already authoritative is the prestige loop, not a ground reading. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap gives the rule in the framework's own vocabulary: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

**Refuter for F7:** show a case where a prestige proxy is itself a non-updating measurement of the residual (for example, a certified reproduction count that cannot be gamed by the measured frame). Then it is an instrument, not a filter, and may be admitted as a witness. This is a live possibility, not a rhetorical aside.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 2, Seat Map :163, and the two world sources (low overlap: 1968 and 2026 sociology versus the figures).

### F8 — The habitat, not the institution in isolation, is what can change (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

An institution surrounded by funders, journals, schools, regulators, and a public is a habitat. Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed. / List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** The competitive field is read off the world, not composed from a theory of what institutions ought to be.

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** A regulator that is a benefactor to one residual is a malfactor to another. There is no standing list of "good" institutions.

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** A competing institution can introduce an actor (a new journal, a new standard, an open archive). It cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult it.

**Refuter for F8:** show an institution whose authority increased while its contacts, instruments, and habitat were held fixed. Then capability is agent-alone and Fig 11 :40 is refuted as applied here.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 10 :24.

### F9 — Different institutional forms can be isomers or a functional group, and the distinction is checkable (`specified`, single-source for the figure definitions, physics/analogue check unmeasured)

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two institutions built from the same atoms (a set of journals, a credential, a peer-review process, a funding stream, a public mandate) but with a different bond between them are isomers. "Rival university" and "rival academy" are not distinguished by their ingredient list. Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

If they share only a form (each has "a review process") and differ in atoms, they are a functional group: Fig 12 :124 **"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Fig 12 :119 names the **catalyst**: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* An institution that survives its own use (a standard body, a registry) behaves differently across disputes from one that is spent by them. Fig 12 :121 names the **cycle**: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Peer review that feeds its own output back as input is a cycle; its sign decides whether it closes.

**Refuter for F9:** if two institutions can be shown to share all atoms and all bonds yet behave differently, the bond is not the whole difference and Fig 12 :5 fails for institutional compositions.

**Status:** single-source for the figure definitions (Fig 12), with the institutional application unmeasured. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

### F10 — What the figures cannot do: name the winning institution (`gap`, plainly stated)

Fig 9 :32: **"→ you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Fig 13 :137-138: *"And it can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."* and :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

After a genuine re-census, no figure line selects a particular historical victor, a date, a percentage of credit, or a ranking of institutions. The figures return the shape any answer must have: non-updating ground, differing closures, convention-under-audit, selection between rooms, habitat contact. They do not return the name. Fig 8 :56 even warns that the triangulation claim itself *"fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward."*

This is a `gap` of the **shape-only** kind, not underivable in principle and not unreachable-at-this-budget in the sense of Formula step 7. There is no ground reading here for this agent to take. A historian with archives could move it. We say `gap` plainly rather than manufacturing a victor. ## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor test fails.** An institution's authority survives a demonstrated, non-updating change in what the ground returns, with the channel open and its own credibility held fixed (breaks F1).
2. **Convention test fails.** An authority convention demonstrably changes no outcome and still cannot be checked against any ground, so it is a legitimate pure convention rather than a verdict (breaks F4).
3. **Room test fails.** A historical sequence of near-identical rooms, with nonzero ground pressure, still progresses (breaks F6).
4. **Habitat test fails.** An institution's authority increases while contacts, instruments, and habitat are held fixed (breaks F8).
5. **Lineage test fails.** Two institutions share all atoms and all bonds yet behave differently, so the bond is not the whole difference (breaks F9).
6. **World/figure conflict that survives re-census.** A well-sourced historical case shows rivalry settling a knowledge claim by negotiation against a ground that genuinely does not update and cannot be ranged against separately. That would send the census back to D2 rather than flip the verdict (Fig 2 :35).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing is `grounded`. The refuter is recorded here before the findings were finalized; findings F1 to F10 each carry their own local refuter in §3, and the global list binds them together.

## 5. WATSON order when no candidate closes R

Formula step 7 splits "no φ in closure(Γ) closes R" into three questions. Asked first, cheapest, and assuming the other two:

- **SEARCHED.** Is the victor already in our closure and unfound? The relevant closure here is the historical record: archives, minutes, correspondence, funding files. We did not search it. This is a budget limit, not an underivability claim. Fig 8 :39 *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* So the honest report is `unreachable at this budget` for a victor, not `unreachable in principle`.
- **INWARD.** Extend the instrument from one already returning something. The instruments available here are the already-returning historical proxies (citation data, archive catalogues, peer-review records). Sharpening them in our own terms would tell us more about who won, but not about what authority is.
- **OUTWARD.** Import. Characterize R in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. That frame is the sociology of scientific authority, STS, and the history of science. Source gate (provenance) and shadow test (does it explain a gap?) are applied in `09_watson_answer.md`. Import is debited against independence (Fig 6 :24).

The three are kept distinct. We did not let OUTWARD hand us a verdict; the verdict comes from the figures, and the world sources are secondary.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape, not a named victor (F10). Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
2. Ask, per institution, whether its ground updates on its own verdict (F1). If it does, it is a frame, not an anchor.
3. Demand provenance of closure before calling two institutions rivals (F2). Convergence and adopted import look identical in the contents.
4. Treat authority conventions as verdicts the moment they decide an outcome (F4), and route them through a maintainer who has no stake in the reading (Fig 10 :59, :93).
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), and never let a filter (prestige, citations, age) hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
6. Keep the three venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` and the outreach order in `02_outreach_email.md`: residual and refuter first, theory only after a verdict is recorded.
7. Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** Send the generator (the shape and the refuter), not a history of who won.

### Strains

- **S1, Kuhnian incommensurability versus Fig 2 triangulation.** Kuhn's *"Like the choice between political institutions, that between competing paradigms proves to be a choice between incompatible modes of community life"* (SEP, quoting *Structure* 1970, 94) looks like a conflict with Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Re-census dissolves it: Kuhn describes the frame-to-frame relation inside two closures (no shared standard), while Fig 2 describes the frame-to-ground relation outside both. Different objects. **The world source is not allowed to flip the verdict**; it corroborates the "no neutral comparison inside the rival frames" part only.
- **S2, convention as product versus convention as verdict.** Fig 2 :41 (*"efficient for a convention"*) and Fig 10 :47 (*"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*) are in tension for peer review, which is both.
