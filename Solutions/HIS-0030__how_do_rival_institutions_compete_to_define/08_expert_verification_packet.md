# Expert verification packet — the Tether on rival institutions and authoritative knowledge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or sociologist of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No archival reading is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Authority is not a stock an institution holds. It is maintained contact with a ground that does not change its answer because of what the institution thinks. Two rival institutions have a real surplus to contest only where their closures differ at the evidence level, because convergence and adopted import look identical in the contents and only provenance separates them. A convention such as peer review is legitimate while it only coordinates meaning, but once it decides an outcome it is a verdict answerable to the ground. A sequence of like-minded institutions is one long room, motion without progress, unless the next room differs and matches the residual the last one surfaced. The framework does not name a victor. It offers the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and an honest stop.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus historical; checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 1
- **Statement:** authority is contact with a non-updating ground, not a self-score. An institution that certifies itself with its own credibility has become its own anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 1 :27 — *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :33 — *"The anchor must not update."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, identify the ground that actually settled the claim. Did it move when the institution's own verdict changed? If it did, it was a frame, not an anchor.
- **Refuter:** an institution whose authority survives a demonstrated non-updating change in the ground, with its channel open and its credibility held fixed.

### C2 — the rivalry claim; checkable against two institutions and their records
- **Statement:** rivalry has a surplus only where closures differ at the evidence level. Two bodies reading the same reviews with the same instruments are one frame wearing two names, and their agreement is not corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :45 — *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :50 — *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 8 :51-52 — *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** for two rival institutions, list their evidence sources and instruments. Is the overlap high or low? If high, can either produce a term the other cannot? Is the similarity of their claims convergence or adopted import? Only the record answers that.
- **Refuter:** two institutions with demonstrably low overlap whose union yields no new term, yet whose agreement tracks the same ground. That shows corroboration, not rivalry, and forces a re-census of what "compete" names.

### C3 — the convention and history claim; checkable against institutional rules and a sequence of rooms
- **Statement:** a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; and a chain of agreeing institutions is one long room, motion without progress.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 — *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :93 — *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Fig 7 :35 — *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36 — *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- **What to check:** pick a credentialing or publication rule. Does it allocate an outcome? If yes, is it tested against a ground or defended as "just a convention"? Then pick three successive bodies in one field. Did the third differ from the second and match the residual the second surfaced?
- **Refuter:** an authority convention that demonstrably changes no outcome and cannot be checked against any ground, or a run of near-identical rooms with nonzero ground pressure that still progresses.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of institutions, a winning academy, a date, or a share of credit. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not "sociology explains it all." The question is constitutively about a ground (Fig 2 :36), so negotiation cannot settle it. World sources are secondary only.
- Not "peer review is corrupt." While a convention only coordinates meaning it is the product, not a corruption (Fig 2 :41).
- Not a claim that prestige measures truth. Prestige is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that institutions literally occupy Seat 1 to 4. The seat map governs the checker, not the historical actors.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an archive or an instrument this agent read.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

1. An archive-based test of whether a given institution's ground moved when its own verdict changed.
2. A low-overlap comparison of two institutions' evidence pools and instruments, with the provenance of the claims recorded so convergence can be separated from import.
3. A test of whether a specific credentialing rule allocates outcomes, and if so whether it is answerable to a non-updating ground.
4. A three-room sequence scored on difference and residual-match.

A checklist of the form *"how many historians agree"* is not one of these. It is a pool talking (Fig 2 :40).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 1 :27, Fig 8 :45 and :50-52, Fig 10 :47, Fig 7 :35-36. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one case you know: name the ground and say whether it updates.
3. Check C2 against two rival institutions: is the overlap high or low, and is the similarity convergence or import?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a convention from a verdict in your field, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
