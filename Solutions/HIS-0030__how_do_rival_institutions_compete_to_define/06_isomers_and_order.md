## Q1 — Are rival institutions isomers? CORRECTION TO MY OWN §1/§3 of `05`

## Q2 — Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- **Strong reading:** two institutions built over the same full atom set (a review process, a credential, a journal, a funding stream, a mandate, a practitioner body), differing only in which bond joins which atoms, are **isomers**. Same ingredients, different arrangement, different behaviour. This is the claim worth testing.
- **Weak reading:** if they share only a form ("both have a review process") and differ in atoms, they are a **functional group**: Fig 12 :124 **"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** "Rival institution" then names the kind, not the substance.
- The difference is decisive because the competition looks identical from the outside in both cases. Only the lineage distinguishes them (Fig 13 :128 *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."*).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two institutions share every atom and every bond yet behave differently under the same residual, the bond is not the whole information and the isomer claim fails.

## Q3 — Which bond is doing the work?

The seven bonds are not interchangeable (Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*).

- **Sequence (`→`)** between a criterion and a verdict: the verdict needs the criterion to have happened. Ordered bond.
- **Production (`⇒`)** when a new journal or standard brings a term into existence that was absent before. Ordered bond, and the test is *"is there a B before A fires?"*
- **Subtraction (`−`)** when an institution is defined by the absence of another's criterion (a journal that publishes what the rival rejects). Ordered bond; absence is the point.
- **Comparison (`⋛`)** when two institutions are ranked against a threshold. Operators table :48-51: *"a threshold relation between two quantities / is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Authority is this kind of quantity, not a stock.
- **Conjunction (`+`)** and **identity (`≡`)** are symmetric. Treating a rivalry as a `+` of two institutions overcharges the ordering and undercharges the bond term (Fig 12 :92 *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"*).
- **Negation (`¬`)** is the eighth operator and not a bond. A refutation of an institution's authority claim is a negation, which is why a vocabulary of ingredients alone "can only accumulate" (Operators table :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*). This is the operator the competition most needs and most lacks.

**Strain, named:** applying ⋛ to two institutions at the historical scale needs both to count as quantities at that level. Fig 12 :103-104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom, but whether an institution counts as a quantity for comparison needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a live type question, not hidden.

## Q4 — Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

| link | question | domain | failure if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | is anything returning a residual here? | D1 | comparing conclusions that no ground ever touched |
| 2 vocabulary | do we have terms for what a rival claim affords? | D1/D2 | "authority" stays a vague stock; no slot to index |
| 3 objective | what would count as progress in this contest? | D2 | no way to say whether a handoff moved anything |
| 4 planning | which room to compare, and against which ground? | D2/D4 | a comparison chosen for agreement, not for the residual |
| 5 learn and carry | what worked, and was it settled before being carried? | D4 | a recording carried upward as if it were a method |

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Link 3 (the objective) is the usual break, because "what would count as authoritative" has no instrument inside a frame that produces its own credibility.

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Adding more archival detail (link 1) cannot rescue a missing definition of what would count as a settlement (link 3).

## Q5 — Analogues that can be carried, and one that must be refused

- **Catalyst** (Fig 12 :119 *"A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*): a standard body or registry survives its own use; a one-off commission is spent. The two behave differently across disputes and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list. Carried as a structural distinction, not a label.
- **Cycle** (Fig 12 :121 *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*): peer review that feeds its output back as input is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes into a self-certifying room. This is the institutional form of Fig 7's mirror chain.
- **Room chain** (Fig 7 :35 **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**): carried as the diachronic domain D4.
- **Seat map analogue, carried carefully.** Fig 10 :42 **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A historical checker who has absorbed the institution's own criteria cannot verify the institution's authority claim. This is a structural requirement, not a personal judgement. But the seat map is a checking arrangement, not a history, so it governs how we test a claim about institutions, not how institutions actually behaved. Writing as if institutions occupied Seats 1 to 4 would be an analogue promoted past its source. **Refused as a historical description.**

## CONCLUSION

Rival institutions are **isomers** if they share the full habitat atom set and differ only in bonds, and a **functional group** if they share only a form. The isomer claim has a stated falsifier. Authority comparison is a `⋛` threshold, not a `+` aggregate, and the missing operator in most institutional vocabularies is negation (refusal), not more ingredients. The four domains must be read in dependency order, with link 3 (the objective) the likely break. Catalyst, cycle and room chain carry as structural distinctions; the seat map carries only as a rule for the checker, not as a description of the historical actors.
