## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the figures say about rivalry over authoritative knowledge. Option A: treat authority as a stock that institutions possess in differing degrees, and explain rivalry as a contest for a share of that stock. Option B: treat authority as maintained contact with a non-updating ground, and explain rivalry as differing closures plus selection between rooms, with the convention-under-audit and the habitat as separate loops. What each leads to: A ends in a prestige-weighted vote and a named victor the figures cannot return; B ends in a per-domain shape, four refuters, and an honest stop where a historian's ground would be needed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what an institutional frame predicts its authority rests on (its criteria, credentials, citation share) and what a non-updating ground actually pays. Arity: N greater than or equal to 2 frames plus one contested object. Symmetry: each claim has the same surface form, but authority is a threshold relation, not a symmetric join. Scale: intra-institutional, inter-institutional, and diachronic, with recursion because a settled institution becomes an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104).
- **Domain 1, the intra-institutional frame.** Actors: members, editors, reviewers, credentialing bodies. Currency: credibility, predicates minted, citations. Anchor: a genuine ground only if it does not update on the institution's own verdict. Question type: partition plus anchor test.
- **Domain 2, the inter-institutional contest.** Actors: rival institutions, each a closure with its own atoms and bonds. Currency: authority claims operating as conventions. Anchor: the external ground common to the contest, only if the question is constitutively about it. Question type: union compound, not partition (Fig 9 :55).
- **Domain 3, the authority convention and its maintainer.** Actors: the seat that authors conventions across a seam. Currency: legitimacy, standing, provenance. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: seat office, not loop.
- **Domain 4, historical selection.** Actors: successive institutional rooms, funders, journals, academies. Currency: survival and arrival of methods. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24).
- **Order by dependency.** Establish the anchor and its reachability, give the frames terms for what a rival claim affords, say what would count as progress, choose the comparison, then carry what was settled to the next room (Fig 3 :20). The maintainer seat sits outside this chain.

Strain test applied: Kuhnian incommensurability versus Fig 2 triangulation was returned to the census and dissolved (different objects: inside two closures versus outside both). The convention as product versus convention as verdict tension is a live boundary, not a smoothed agreement. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Authority is maintained contact with a non-updating ground, not a stock. Rival institutions have a contestable surplus only where their closures differ at the evidence level, and provenance of the closure, not the similarity of the claim text, separates convergence from adopted import. A convention that decides an outcome is a verdict answerable to the ground, and a keeper with a reputation to protect is disqualified. A chain of agreeing institutions is one long room; progress needs a differing room that matches the surfaced residual. Prestige is a budget filter, never a witness. Capability lives in the institution-and-habitat. No victor, date or credit share is returned: that is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` on the four domains, `general` on the shape, ungrounded, candidate. This is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, four refuters and the provenance of every quote, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending them a history of who won instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The victor gap is removable without touching the four domain verdicts, because it comes from an archive this agent did not read, not from a primitive that would have to be withdrawn. A refuter that fires narrows one domain; nothing has to be silently erased, and the retraction log in `01` keeps earlier readings visible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An archive test showing an institution's authority surviving a non-updating change in the ground would break the authority-as-relation verdict. A low-overlap comparison showing no surplus but tracking agreement would split convergence from rivalry. A three-room sequence with high overlap and real ground pressure that still progresses would break the room verdict. An instrument that distinguishes a pure meaning-coordinating convention from an outcome-deciding one would clear the S2 boundary. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
