# The Tether's answer: could self-sustaining chemical networks have formed before genetic molecules?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain shape, `general` on the dependency and recursion structure, `gap` on the historical sequence and the identity of the first network. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is usually posed as a historical either/or. In effect terms it is: can a chemical network's residual R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| stay small across successive turnovers while Γ holds no heritable genetic template? R is indexed per network-and-compartment slot (Fig 1 :19, :22), and the ground is the only metric (Fig 1 :23). The "either/or" is a union of four overlapping conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :55, :57), so there is no single lever and no averaged verdict.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **D2, the driver.** A network cannot be driven by ingredients alone. The first condition is a gradient that can be spent. Fig 13 :22 and :46: *"a difference that can be spent"*; Fig 13 :47: *"If the whole carries a property neither part has, the whole is not the parts arranged."* The gradient is an anchor in the Fig 2 sense: it does not move when a frame changes its mind about it.

2. **D1, the network.** Self-sustaining is a cycle, not a chain: Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Its catalysts survive their own use: Fig 12 :119, *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A linear sequence of syntheses is the degenerate case and does not close.

3. **D3, the boundary.** Capability is a property of network-and-habitat, not of the network alone: Fig 11 :39, *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The compartment keeps products in contact with catalysts. Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one (Fig 11 :66). A laboratory harness is a substituted habitat, which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and not capability.

4. **D4, the channel.** Selection does not wait for a genetic author: Fig 11 :36, *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Differential persistence among compartments is where the ground votes (Fig 7 :35). What the template-free account still owes is an account of channel decay: Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. A genetic molecule is one solution to that decay, but the figures do not say it is the only one.

5. **The transition, if it happens.** If a genetic molecule is a new kind relative to a network's atoms, a template-free network cannot mint it from inside its closure: Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* It would be an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8). One direction is already supported by world evidence: autocatalytic networks generate RNA monomers, but monomers alone do not regenerate the networks (see `09`). That makes the monomers in-closure and the polymer a later composition, not a wall-moving atom.

6. **The ordering.** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The compositional order is gradient, cycle, compartment, compounding channel. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A settled network can therefore serve as the operand on which heredity composes. That is the metabolism-first ordering as a **composition relation**. It is atemporal: it does not say what happened at 4 Ga.

7. **The candidates.** Iron-sulfur surfaces, alkaline vent micropores, vesicles and abstract RAF sets are isomers over a shared atom set (Fig 12 :117), or members of a functional group (Fig 12 :123) if the atom set is not shared. The figures do not select among them; naming one is a provenanced binding from the world frame, not a derivation.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that metabolism first is historically true. The historical order is a `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget, not as silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).
- Not that RNA or genetics was absent early, or that RNA cannot catalyze. The claim removes a claimed *structural necessity* for a template before selection can begin; it does not date anything.
- Not that a particular vent, mineral or vesicle was the first network.
- Not that the first metabolism resembled a modern pathway. That is a retrodiction from modern metabolism, a recording carried upward rather than a generator (Fig 4 :22, :25).
- Not a derivation of one chemical network from the figures. No instrument, no measurement, no ground reading.
- Not `grounded`. The verdict is candidate until the ground pays (Formula, mode rule).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Closure test:** in a flow reactor or vent mimic, does a template-free catalytic network's R stay flat across turnovers while the gradient, the compartment and the selection channel are measured as present? If R grows without bound, D1 fails.
- **Threshold test:** does production exceed catalyst loss, and does the catalyst survive its own use across repeated cycles? That is the Fig 12 :121 sign of the loop.
- **Channel test:** do compartment variants compound differentially without a heritable template? If not, D4 fails and the template is required before evolution can start, even if networks can persist.
- **Ordering test:** can a heritable template be derived entirely inside a template-free closure? If yes, it is not an import and F10 falls.
- **Standing figure refuters:** a settled network that cannot become an atom at the next level; a catalytic cycle that cannot close; a union collapsed into one lever. All five are pre-registered in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The four-condition shape is what the figures can return. The date, the first network and the historical order require an instrument that does not exist in this run. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable; *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A conflict between the metabolism-first and genetics-first frames sends us back to the census, never to a flip of the verdict.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "metabolism first, yes or no". It unbundles the question into four conditions that must co-hold for a chemical network to stay self-sustaining without a genetic template: a gradient that can actually be spent, a catalytic cycle whose parts survive their own use, a boundary that keeps products in contact with catalysts, and a channel in which differential persistence can compound. Selection can run without a genetic author, and once a network is settled it can become the atom on which heredity later composes. That is the metabolism-first ordering as a composition relation, not a date. The framework refuses to name the first network or the historical order, because it returns a shape, not a name. Iron-sulfur surfaces, alkaline vent pores, vesicles and autocatalytic sets stand to each other as isomers or as members of a functional group, to be separated by an instrument. What it offers is that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it. The historical center stays open until the ground settles it.
