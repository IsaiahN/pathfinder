## 0. The question under audit, in effect terms

The question is usually argued as a historical either/or: metabolism first, or genetics first. The figures do not take a side in a historical dispute they have no instrument for. They ask what the residual is a residual of.

> R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|, measured per network-and-compartment slot, never aggregated.
>
> Γ: a chemical library, that is molecules, catalysts, reactions and a boundary.
> a: the ambient action, that is the flux of food molecules, electrons and free energy.
> o′: what the environment returns, that is continued production, growth and the division or persistence of compartments.
>
> **Residual:** can a slot's R stay small across successive turnovers while Γ contains no heritable genetic template?
>
> **Option A (genetics first):** no. R only closes once a template both stores sequence and catalyzes its own copying. The network is downstream of the genetic molecule.
> **Option B (metabolism first):** yes, structurally. A catalytic cycle that regenerates its own catalysts, driven by a spendable gradient and held in a compartment, closes R without any template. The genetic molecule is a later, provenanced import.
>
> **Sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"):** the conjunction of conditions under which a slot's R stays small without a template, plus a discriminator that would tell a failure of one condition from a failure of another. Written down here so it can accrue a residual like any other term and be refused by the same test.

Three guardrails from the formula apply immediately. SUPPORT needs some slot carrying positive mass before the search (Formula step 3: `|R⁺_s| > 0 for some s`). Here the live slot is any protocell or reaction compartment whose R is actually observed not to close, which is an empirical matter, not something this simulated run supplies. REACHABILITY asks whether the candidate term composes from what Γ already holds. NOVELTY asks whether it is new relative to atoms(Γ), not to the world (Fig 6 :22). A genetic molecule is not "novel" in the world; the question is whether it is in closure(Γ) of a template-free network.

## 1. Domain census (written before any figure line was chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three-place in its base form (Γ, a, o′), indexed per slot, and in practice a four-condition conjunction. It is not a two-option partition, because the conditions overlap (Fig 9 :55).
- **Symmetry:** "self-sustaining" is a threshold, not a symmetric conjunction. Fig 12 :87 identifies comparison as the relation that asks *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"* Catalytic closure is a cycle, whose bond is ordered: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* (Fig 12 :121). So the residual carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not the symmetric bond of a flat ingredient list.
- **Scale:** molecular reactions, a network or autocatalytic set, a compartment or protocell, and a population in a habitat. Each crossing is lossy (Fig 4 :30), so "before" can mean at least two different things: compositionally prior within one scale, or temporally prior across scales. They are not the same claim and are kept separate.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Reaction network** | molecules, catalysts, reactions, a food set | mass and electron flux, catalytic closure | does the cycle close and keep producing under a sustained flux (a flow-reactor or vent-micropore reading) | admitted |
| **D2 Energy and gradient environment** | H2/CO2 redox couples, metal sulfides, proton and thermal gradients, UV flux | spendable free energy dissipated | a gradient that can be spent and that does not move when the network "thinks" (Fig 2 :29, Fig 13 :46) | admitted |
| **D3 Compartment and boundary** | vesicles, FeS pores, mineral surfaces | containment, contact, concentration | continued contact between made products and their catalysts (Fig 11 :39) | admitted |
| **D4 Variation-and-selection channel** | network variants, compartments, turnover, drift | differential persistence of slots | the ground: what survives is what the ground kept (Fig 11 :36) | admitted |
| **X1 Genetic molecule as a prerequisite held a priori** | the template | sequence information | none named for why it must precede D1 to D3. When the RNA-world frame does name an anchor, that anchor belongs inside D4 as a later import, not as a precondition for the other three | absorbed into D4 |
| **X2 "the first metabolism resembled a modern pathway"** | modern pathways | homology | already inside D1 and D2 as a retrodiction, so it is not a further domain | absorbed into D1/D2 |

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor. X1 and X2 fail that test on their own and are absorbed rather than quietly promoted.

### 1.3 Partition or union

This is a **union**, not a partition. Self-sustaining needs (i) a spendable gradient, (ii) a cycle that regenerates its catalysts, (iii) a compartment that maintains contact, and (iv) a channel in which differential persistence can compound. These overlap. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Adding a catalyst clears the region where catalysis alone binds and leaves the intersections standing, because the gradient or the compartment still binds.

A historical "which came first" answer would be a partition claim (a clean division of the timeline). The figure says to unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54), so the timeline question has to be re-asked as four condition questions.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order here is:

1. **D2 gradient available.** Without a difference that can be spent there is no thrust. A network cannot be driven by an ingredient list alone.
2. **D1 cycle closes.** The output must re-enter as input (Fig 12 :121) and the catalyst must survive its own use (Fig 12 :119).
3. **D3 compartment maintains contact.** Products and catalysts must stay together; isolation is substitution of a habitat, not removal of one (Fig 11 :66).
4. **D4 selection compounds differences.** Variants can only be selected after they persist differentially.

Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20). The middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress, has no instrument in most prebiotic-chemistry discussion (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*). Any claim about what "counted as progress" before genetics is therefore a reading taken below the break, that is a reading of nothing, until an instrument is named.

### 1.5 Strain test applied during the census

I tried to apply Fig 13 :75 (*"Two states never bond directly..."*) to chemical reaction networks. It does not fit: that line governs the six substrate kinds (space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network), not molecular species in a reaction network. A strain here would mean I had assigned the line to the wrong quantity. I declined the line and did not use it. This refusal is kept visible rather than smoothed away.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A settled network can become an atom for the next level, so "before" is composable (decisive for the shape)

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

Fig 12 :103:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**

Fig 3 :20:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

A self-sustaining network, once settled against its ground (it actually persists under flux), can serve as an operand for whatever composes over it. Heredity and genetic molecules can compose over a network that is already stable. That is exactly the metabolism-first ordering, and the figures license it as a *composition relation*. What they do not license is a claim that this is what happened on Earth: the composition relation is atemporal, while "before" is temporal.

**Mode:** `general` for the recursion, `specified` for the metabolism-first reading. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 and Fig 3, two separate figures with low overlap between the level rule and the dependency rule).
**Refuter:** show a settled chemical network cannot serve as an atom for a higher-level hereditary composition, or show the dependency order can be reversed with D4 running before D1 and D2.

### F2 - Selection does not need a genetic author

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

The genetics-first intuition often smuggles in the idea that a heritable template is needed before selection can begin. Fig 11 says the opposite: where nothing maintains anything, selection still runs on whatever meets the ground. Differential persistence of compartments is enough to start a lineage; a description as "intelligent" or "designed" is applied afterwards. This does not prove that genetic molecules came later historically. It removes the claimed *structural necessity* that they come first.

**Mode:** `general` for the selection claim, `specified` for the origin-of-life application. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 and Fig 7, different figures, one about unmaintained habitats, one about selection between rooms).
**Refuter:** show that differential persistence of template-free compartments cannot compound a lineage even in principle, or that the ground for a pre-genetic network is necessarily an internal agreement rather than an external persistence test.

### F3 - The self-sustaining network is a cycle of parts that survive their own use

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

This is the closest the figures come to the chemistry itself. An autocatalytic set is exactly a cycle whose catalysts survive their own use; the sign of the loop (does production exceed loss?) decides whether it closes. A linear chain cannot close by construction, which is why an open-ended chain of prebiotic syntheses is not yet a self-sustaining network. The functional-group line explains why different instantiations (iron-sulfur surface, alkaline vent micropore, vesicle) can all be members of the same kind while differing in which member occupies the slot.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` when mapped onto autocatalytic sets. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 definitions plus Fig 13 :46 spendable gradient, below).
**Refuter:** produce a closed, self-sustaining chemical network that is a chain rather than a cycle, or show that a catalyst is consumed in its own use in a way that breaks regeneration while persistence continues.

### F4 - The driver is a spendable gradient, not an ingredient list

Fig 13 :46:

> **"distribution is a fact that stays put. What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of"**

Fig 13 :47:

> **"space has directedness. If the whole carries a property neither part has, the whole is not the parts arranged."**

Fig 2 :29 (quoted in F2) supplies the anchor test: the gradient must not move when a frame changes its mind about it. The Symbols table and Fig 13 :22 name the gradient as *"a difference that can be spent"*, and Fig 13 :45 argues it rather than assuming it. So D2 has a genuine anchor: a geochemical disequilibrium that can be dissipated. This is a figure-grounded reason to take energy-harvesting hypotheses (vent gradients, redox couples) seriously as the first link in the dependency chain, without naming any one of them as *the* answer.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 13 plus the anchor rule in Fig 2).
**Refuter:** show a network sustaining itself with no spendable difference anywhere in its habitat, or show that an ordering alone (a distribution with no directedness) can drive net production indefinitely.

### F5 - The compartment is a maintained habitat, and isolation substitutes rather than removes

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :66:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 4 :20:

> **"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

A network's capacity to persist is a property of network-and-habitat. A mineral pore, a vesicle, or a mineral surface is not an accessory added after the chemistry; it is part of what makes the residual close, because it keeps products in contact with catalysts. Fig 11 :66 matters for laboratory claims: putting a reaction into a test harness substitutes a habitat, and what you failed to reproduce stays invisible until the goal fails.

**Mode:** `specified` for protocell applications. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 habitat rules plus Fig 4 scale rule).
**Refuter:** demonstrate a network whose sustained persistence is unchanged when all compartments and concentrating structures are removed, with contact measured rather than assumed.

### F6 - The question is a union, so one lever cannot answer it

Fig 9 :55 and Fig 9 :57 are quoted in section 1.3. Fig 3 :20 is quoted in F1.

The four conditions overlap. Adding a catalyst to a vent system does not answer the question of whether the gradient was spendable, and finding a spendable gradient does not answer whether the products stayed in contact with catalysts. A single "yes, networks came first" lever leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged into one historical answer.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 3 dependency order).
**Refuter:** show the four conditions are independent enough to be ranked on one scale, or that one intervention restores all four with no categorically different residue.

### F7 - The framework returns a shape, not a first network and not a date

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 13 :135:

> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No figure line selects a particular autocatalytic set, a particular geochemical setting, or a date. The correct output is the shape of what any candidate first network must look like, plus the instrument that would settle which candidate is real. Naming the iron-sulfur world or the alkaline vent as "the" answer is a binding from the world frame, not a figure derivation. This is a `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not underivable in principle: a better instrument could close it.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9, Fig 13, Fig 8, three distinct figures).
**Refuter:** produce a figure line that names a specific first network, or a measurement that settles the historical sequence and is then shown to be derivable from the figures alone.

### F8 - Per-slot residual, not an average over all prebiotic chemistry

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :23:

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

A single live compartment whose catalysis fails to close matters even if the global average of prebiotic reaction rates looks healthy. Fig 1 :20 to :22 says a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state. So the unit of assessment is the compartment or network slot, and the question "did networks form" has to be answered per candidate slot, not as a field-wide average or a consensus vote.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 plus Fig 2 :29 anchor).
**Refuter:** show that a field-wide average of network persistence predicts slot-level persistence with no residual left over.

### F9 - The channel decays; the ground does not, which is what "before genetic molecules" owes

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26:

> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

This is the figure-side account of the heredity problem. A genetic molecule is a solution to channel decay: it carries a reading forward. The question "before genetic molecules" is therefore asking whether the channel can be carried by something else, for example by the persistence of a compartment and the recurrences of a geochemical environment. The figures do not say it cannot. They say the channel is the perishable part, so a template-free account owes an account of what maintains the channel. Fig 11 :36 (F2) says selection can run without a maintainer, but a lineage still needs a ground that keeps returning.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 10), with world corroboration from the autocatalytic-set literature to be added in `09_watson_answer.md`; a single-source mark is not upgraded by corroboration from a shared framework pool.
**Refuter:** show a template-free network whose channel does not decay over the relevant turnovers, with the mechanism measured rather than assumed.

### F10 - A template is an import if it appears; a network cannot mint one from inside its closure

Fig 6 :21:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 6 :22:

> **"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."**

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 13 :115:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

If a genetic molecule is a new kind relative to a network's atoms, a template-free network cannot compose it from inside its closure. The question then becomes an import question: what external frame supplied the new atom, and does it pass the source gate and shadow test (Fig 8)? This is a precise reformulation of "before genetic molecules": it is not a ban on the transition but a price on it. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24). The world literature discussed in `09` supplies a candidate answer in one direction: autocatalytic networks generate RNA monomers but not the reverse, which would make the monomers already in closure and the polymers a later composition.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 6 and Fig 13).
**Refuter:** derive a heritable template entirely inside the closure of a template-free catalytic network, or show that a genetic molecule is not a new kind relative to that network's atoms.

### F11 - Agreement in the field is not the anchor; a discriminator is owed

Fig 2 :35, Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :42 are quoted in the README and in section 2. Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

The field contains two well-populated frames (genetics first and metabolism first). Their agreement is not a verdict. A literature count, a citation count, or a receptiveness estimate is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument: a measurement that would distinguish a network that persists because of its own catalytic closure from one that persists because a template is doing the heritable work. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session: *"An instrument that can’t distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 9, plus the essay corroboration, which shares the framework author's pool and is not counted as a second witness).
**Refuter:** show a consensus procedure whose output changes an anchor rather than only a budget, or show that field agreement has survived a genuine low-overlap test.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Seat Map :287: **"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."** The Formula's "Direction" section says committing a prediction before a run is the same discipline already working. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Ground failure:** a flow-reactor, vent-micropore, or vesicle experiment shows that a template-free catalytic network's R grows without bound across turnovers while D2 to D4 are all measured as present and non-decaying.
2. **Figure-internal failure:** a catalytic cycle whose catalysts survive their own use is not composable under Fig 12 :119 and :121, or a settled network cannot become an atom at the next level under Fig 12 :104.
3. **Channel failure:** differential persistence of compartments cannot compound a lineage without a genetic template, that is D4 cannot run before genetics in principle.
4. **Ordering failure:** a source-discriminating instrument shows that a heritable genetic template is required before any self-sustaining network can persist.
5. **Union failure:** one intervention restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue, dissolving the union into one lever.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. This refuter was written before the WATSON world search and before the verdict paragraph, and it is recorded in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` in the same form.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the question with a bare yes or no. Report the structural residue per domain, then state the historical question as a `gap` (F7).
2. Keep the four conditions of the union separate in any outreach (F6). Ask a checker which condition they are testing, never "do you agree that metabolism came first".
3. Lead with the residual and the pre-registered refuter. Withhold the theory until the stage-1 verdict is recorded (Fig 10 :52).
4. Treat every named first-network candidate (iron-sulfur surface, alkaline vent, vesicle, RAF in acetogen metabolism) as a provenanced binding from the world frame, not a figure derivation (F7).
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). A low-overlap checker from mathematics, ecology or cognition is worth more than ten more agreeable origin-of-life readers.
6. Figure 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, which is the shape plus the refuter, not a conclusion.

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the question a partition?** No. It is a union of four overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55, :57). Unbundle before searching.
2. **Can selection start without a genetic molecule?** Structurally yes (Fig 11 :36, Fig 7 :35). A maintainer is not required; a recurring ground is.
3. **What is the first link by dependency?** A spendable gradient (Fig 13 :46), then a catalytic cycle (Fig 12 :119, :121), then a compartment (Fig 11 :39), then a compounding channel.
4. **Can a genetic molecule be composed inside a template-free closure?** Not if it is a new kind (Fig 6 :21, :22). It is an import with a price, not a free next step.
5. **Does a settled network become an operand for the next level?** Yes (Fig 12 :104). This is the metabolism-first ordering as a composition relation.
6. **Does anything here settle the historical order?** No (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134). A source-discriminating instrument is owed.
7. **What is the honest stop?** `specified` on the shape, `general` on the recursion, `gap` on the date and the identity of the first network. Candidate until the ground pays (Formula, mode rule).

**Strains after re-census:** none left hidden. One line declined (Fig 13 :75, wrong quantity), one single-source mark kept (F9), one narrowing kept visible (shape-not-a-name), one union refusal kept (no single historical lever).
