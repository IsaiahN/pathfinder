**Subject:** One checkable claim about pre-genetic chemical networks, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** self-sustaining here means a chemical network whose own products regenerate the catalysts it needs, driven by a gradient that can actually be spent, and held somewhere that keeps products in contact with those catalysts. Under the figure framework I am using, three structural facts follow. First, a catalyst is *"a part that survives its own use"* and the network is a *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Second, selection can run where nothing maintains anything: *"Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* So a heritable template is not structurally required for differential persistence to begin. Third, once such a network is settled it *"becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"*, so heredity can compose over a network that already persists. This is a shape and a dependency order, not a date and not a claim that this is what happened on Earth.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a particular setting (an iron-sulfur surface, an alkaline vent, a vesicle) was the first one; that RNA or any other genetic molecule is ruled out as an early player; that the historical order is settled; or that this derivation is a measurement. The framework returns a kind of thing, not the answer itself. Naming a specific first network would be a binding from the laboratory frame, and it would need provenance.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a flow-reactor, vent-pore, or vesicle experiment in which a template-free catalytic network's residual grows without bound across turnovers while the gradient, the compartment and the selection channel are all measured as present;
> 2. a demonstration that a catalytic cycle whose catalysts survive their own use is not composable, or that a settled network cannot serve as an atom for a higher-level hereditary composition;
> 3. evidence that differential persistence of compartments cannot compound a lineage without a genetic template;
> 4. a source-discriminating instrument showing that a heritable template is required before any self-sustaining network can persist.
>
> I wrote these down before making the claim. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Full figure text with line numbers (local framework extract, `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`).
> - Autocatalytic chemical networks at the origin of metabolism (Xavier, Hordijk, Kauffman, Steel and Martin, Proc R Soc B 2020): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7126077
> - On the origin of biochemistry at an alkaline hydrothermal vent (Martin and Russell, 2007): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2442388
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step formula is in the framework figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
