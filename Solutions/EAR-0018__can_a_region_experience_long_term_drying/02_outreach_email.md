# Outreach draft - check the residual first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the structural claim and its refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-read and the mode labels are intact. The offer collapses on contact if the first stage carries theory instead of the residual.

---

**Subject:** One claim to check: can a region dry while the global mean rises?

> Dear [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can a region experience long-term drying while global average rainfall increases?" bundles two different objects. One is a global mean, a single number formed from many regional slots. The other is one slot, the region you actually care about. A weighted mean can rise while one of its components falls, so the two trends are jointly legal states. The framework's own bookkeeping makes this explicit: the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated, and aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. Nothing here claims that the mean is wrong. It claims that the mean is the wrong instrument for a question about a region.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular region is drying, that a specific percentage applies, or that the figures can predict a regional trend. Those are ground questions for gauges, reanalysis and models. I also am not claiming that "wet gets wetter, dry gets drier" is a law; current assessments call it too simplistic. The physical mechanism is a union of energy-budget and moisture-transport conditions, and I am not collapsing it into one cause.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. Show that the global mean is invertible to its slots, so the aggregate trend determines the sign of every regional trend.
> 2. Show a downscaling operator that recovers each region from the mean with zero round-trip gap.
> 3. Show that one physical condition, rather than a union of conditions, accounts for every persistent regional drying trend.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** is the residual I have described the residual your field actually works on, or have I mischaracterised it? A correction on that point is the result I am after.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three short sources, with the note that the figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> Thank you for your time.

## Two-stage offer, explicit

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the verdict before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three source links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. The figures are the authority, not this email.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.
