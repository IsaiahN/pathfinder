# Aggregates, isomers and order: the supporting derivation for EAR-0018

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order rule, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. The 3000-4000 km break is reported from world sources, not derived here.

## RESIDUAL

Two readings of one period disagree: a global mean precipitation trend (up) and a regional slot trend (down). The question asks whether the second is possible given the first. The supporting derivation owes three things: (1) whether the two readings are the same term under a different name, (2) what order the reasoning must run in, and (3) what the cross-scale gap is made of.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: N regional slots with their precipitation values; the weighted-mean operator; the global total; transport structure between slots. Bonds held: the symmetric `+` that forms the aggregate, the ordered `→` for the dependency chain, and `⋛` as a threshold comparison when the aggregate is compared with a slot. No new primitive is needed. Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Sort: **partition** for aggregate versus slot (two objects), **union** for the physical mechanism (thermodynamics plus dynamics plus aerosol plus land use). Order: legality first, mechanism second, outreach third (Fig 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the mean and the slot the same term under two names? No.

- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :14 "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".
- The mean records total water; the slot records where it fell. Same ingredients, different bond, different substance. Under the symmetric `+` bond, "k operands have k! spellings and one term" (Fig 12 :92-93): reordering the regions does not change the mean. A term that cannot distinguish the reorderings cannot answer a question about one named region.
- Therefore the two readings are **not** `≡` (identity). Calling them the same quantity is the flat-formula collapse. Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 1 :19 plus Fig 12 :5, :14, low overlap).

**Falsifier:** exhibit an invertible mean, or show that the `+` operator preserves the arrangement of its operands. Both are refused by Fig 12.

### Q2 - Are they isomers? Yes, at the level of fixed total.

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Write two worlds with the same global total precipitation:

- World W1: the total is spread evenly across all slots. Each slot's trend matches the mean.
- World W2: the total is redistributed so one slot loses water and another gains more than the mean gain. The mean is identical to W1; the slot trend has the opposite sign.

W1 and W2 have the same atoms (same total water, same set of regions) and different arrangements (different transport bond). By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, and the difference is "invisible in the formula" (the mean) and "decisive in the behaviour" (drought in that region). This is the cleanest structural statement of the answer to EAR-0018, and it needs no climate physics to see. Mode `specified` (the isomer reading is a claim about the terms), triangulated with Fig 1 :22. The climate question of whether W2 is physically realised is a separate, ground question.

**Falsifier:** if two worlds with the same global total always produce the same trend in every slot, the isomer reading fails and the mean would be a complete description. That would refute Fig 12 :117 as applied here.

### Q3 - What order does the reasoning run in? Legality, then mechanism, then verification.

Fig 3 :20:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Fig 3 :19:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The dependency chain for this question:

1. **Perception:** is a per-slot trend distinguishable from a global trend at all? Yes (gauges, reanalysis).
2. **Vocabulary:** do we have terms for both the aggregate and the slot? Yes (Fig 1 :19; Symbols `s`).
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress? A characterisation of the slot residual and the transport that sets it. This is the middle link with no obvious instrument in many arguments, and the one Fig 3 :25-27 warns about.
4. **Planning:** which frame supplies the transport mechanism? The energy-budget and moisture-budget frame, imported with provenance (Fig 6 :23).
5. **Learn and carry:** record the break scale and the refuter for reuse on the next regional question.

Asking step 4 before step 3 is how a debate about "global warming means more rain" gets started and never resolved. Mode `general`, single-source for the chain rule (Fig 3), but corroborated by the canonical usage rule's "describe the effect before you go looking."

**Falsifier:** a case where the physical mechanism can be specified without first distinguishing the aggregate from the slot, with no loss of meaning. None is supplied.

### Q4 - What is the cross-scale gap made of, and where does it close?

Fig 4 :31-32:

> **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**

The abstraction T_A is the weighted mean of the slots. The concretisation T_E is asking what the mean says about one slot. Three things are dropped going up and cannot come back down:

1. **Which region** is which (the ordering information, Q1).
2. **The transport bond** between slots, which is what moves water into or out of a slot.
3. **The divergence terms** of the water and energy budgets, which vanish only in the global aggregate.

The empirical counterpart is reported, not derived: Dagan, Stier and Watson-Parris (2019) locate the water-budget closure scale near 3500-4000 km; Dagan and Stier (2020) put the combined energy-and-water closure scale near 3000 km. Above that scale, precipitation change approaches the small global mean (1-3% per K); below it, divergence permits much larger changes of either sign. This is the physical content of R_T and the reason the question's two readings can diverge. Mode `specified` for the structural gap, `grounded` only in the cited sources' own measurements, not by this run.

**Falsifier:** a downscaling operator with zero R_T that recovers each slot from the mean. That would also falsify Q1 and Q2.

### Q5 - Two budgets, one reading each, never averaged.

IPCC AR6 WG1 Ch8 reports that the global mean is constrained by the atmospheric **energy** budget (1-3% per K), while regional changes are governed by the **water** budget and moisture transport. These are two constraints on two different objects, not two estimates of one quantity to be averaged. Fig 12 :125-127 holds two currencies apart: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." The use here is an **analogue** about holding two readings apart, marked `specified`; the figures' two currencies are bits and actions, not energy and water. This is the second declared strain after F10, and it is recorded rather than hidden.

**Falsifier:** a derivation that merges the energy and water constraints into one scalar that settles both the mean and the slot. If that worked, the two-object claim would collapse.

## CONCLUSION

The mean and the slot are not the same term (Q1), they are isomers at fixed total (Q2), and the reasoning must settle legality before mechanism (Q3). The cross-scale gap is made of location, transport and divergence (Q4), and closes empirically at a few thousand kilometres. Two budgets read two different objects and must not be averaged (Q5). The structural answer to EAR-0018 is yes: the joint state is legal, and W2 is the shape any mechanism must produce. Whether W2 is realised in a particular region is ground work, not figure work.
