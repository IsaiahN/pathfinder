## Key verdict (internal consistency only)

The question is answerable in shape. A checkable history of failed science research is a **lineage
record**, not a highlight reel. It keeps refusals in the archive (Fig 6 :42-43), prices results in
bits and actions separately (Fig 12 :125-126), locates chance in an already-returning instrument
(Fig 6 :34-35), distinguishes refuted from unreachable-at-budget from no-proto-instrument
(Fig 8 :39-43), keeps the past ground from updating on the present (Fig 2 :33-34), and reports what
could have been concluded and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ
(Fig 13 :140-141). The three items in the question are a union, so one lever cannot clear them
(Fig 9 :54). Mode: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No verdict about any named discovery, program or historian. The framework returns a shape, not a
  name, and the dated primary record is what settles an episode.

The reasons are in the figures: Fig 2 :40 (*"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing
frames share an evidence pool"*), Fig 2 :35 (*"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror
cannot correct you."*), and Fig 10 :42 (*"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify
it."*).
