## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A historian holds a finished record: surviving papers, instruments, notebooks, correspondence, and the present body of accepted claims. The question asks how that record should account for three things that a success-only narrative tends to drop:

1. **Chance discoveries** (a result registered where the probe was not aimed).
2. **Failed projects** (a term was tried and did not pay or did not settle).
3. **Abandoned research programs** (search stopped short of settlement).

Stated as a residual: the finished record shows the settled result but systematically omits the *generative process*, namely the actions spent, the refuse-branches, the residuals that registered on an instrument aimed elsewhere, and the programs that stopped for budget or instrument reasons. The residual `R` is the gap between the record as it stands (`Γ`) and the process that produced it. If a history drops that gap, it cannot reconstruct the counterfactual, and the present outcome begins to look inevitable. That is the failure the world literature names Whig history or presentism (Butterfield, 1931; see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"):** a history is a *lineage record* that keeps refusals in the archive, prices results in two currencies, distinguishes three different reasons a program stops, refuses to let the past ground update on the present outcome, and reports what could have been concluded and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ. That shape is bound here so it can accrue a residual and be refuted, rather than remaining an unrefusable preference.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**
- **Arity:** three conditions are bundled in the question (chance, failure, abandonment). Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* This is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a list of three separable topics. Each needs its own lever.
- **Symmetry:** the three are **not interchangeable**. They occupy different bins of the loop: NOVEL (chance), REFUTED (failure), and unreachable-at-budget or lost channel (abandonment). Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms (Operators table :61: *"Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms, and charging for the sequence is correct."*). Order is load-bearing.
- **Scale:** the research episode is first order; the narrative composed over settled episodes is second order. Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*

## 3. Findings

### F1 (D1) Refusals must stay in the record; a reach that deletes them is not computable

Fig 6 :42-43:

> **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."**
> **"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."**

Fig 10 :62-63:

> **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom. The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."**

A history that prunes failed projects to make a clean story treats the archive as a store that deletes. The figures forbid this for a structural reason: a store that deletes cannot tell never-held from held-and-given-up (Fig 6 :45-46: *"A store that deleted instead would have neither property, and could not tell never-held from held-and-given-up."*). So a history which omits an abandoned program makes it impossible to decide whether the field never tried it or tried and refused it.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural rule about records), `specified` when applied to a given archive.
- **Refuter:** show an archive that reconstructs the counterfactual correctly while recording no refusals or stopped branches. If the reach can be computed without them, F1 is false.
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 6 on the reach and Fig 10 on provenance, low overlap: one is about closure, the other about a checking seat).

### F2 (D2) Price a discovery in two currencies, not one

Fig 12 :125-127:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them."**

Fig 12 :107:

> **"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."**

A published result is the bits. The weeks of failed runs, the wrong instrument, the abandoned branch are the actions. A success-only history records the bits and drops the actions, then presents the result as if it were cheap. Fig 12 :128-129 adds that the two readings come apart in both directions: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."* So word count is not a proxy for cost, and a one-line discovery can be the most expensive one.

- **Mode:** `general` for the two-currency rule, `specified` for any particular cost attribution.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a history that reports the actions as fully recoverable from the final description, with no residual cost left over. If the description carries its establishment cost, F2 is false.
- **Status: single-source for the figure (Fig 12), corroborated at low overlap by publication-bias research.** Corrected from "triangulated" after the second-seat re-census in `11_audit.md`: both figure lines are in Fig 12, so this is one figure, not two. The world source finds the bit-only record is biased.

**Retraction guard.** I initially wanted to say "histories should list every failed experiment." That is a filler reading of a shape: it tries to name the content rather than keep the quantity. Corrected to "keep the actions and refusals such that the reach and the counterfactual stay computable." The corrected form is a shape, not a name. The earlier wording is preserved here rather than erased.

### F3 (D2) Chance discovery is a residual on an instrument already returning something

Fig 6 :34-35:

> **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

Fig 6 :33:

> **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."**

A "chance" discovery is not a bolt from nowhere. It is a residual that registered where the probe was not aimed, on an instrument already returning something that failed to resolve. The historian's job is to record *what was already returning a reading* and what the observer was primed to see, not to label the event "luck" and stop. This matches the world evidence: Baumeister (2006) writes that *"Absent sagacity, a chance observation of an important phenomenon will have no impact, and the observer may be denied historical attribution for the discovery."*

- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded for any single episode; the general shape is that chance requires a pre-existing instrument and a prepared observer.
- **Refuter:** show a documented discovery in which no instrument at any resolution was returning a reading that failed to resolve. If chance can register where nothing was already returning something, F3 is false.
- **Status: single-source for the figure** (Fig 6 :34-35), corroborated at low overlap by cognitive-science world sources (Dunbar and Fugelsang; Baumeister). Corroboration is secondary and is not a second figure.

### F4 (D2) Abandonment is not proof of impossibility

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 8 :40-43:

> **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."**

An abandoned program can stop for at least three structurally different reasons, and a history that labels all three "failed" loses the distinction:

1. **Refuted:** a held claim was wrong; what is owed is a refusal (Formula step 2, Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*).
2. **Unreachable at this budget:** the term was in the closure and not found; the remedy was more search, not a new atom (Fig 8 :41-42).
3. **No proto-instrument anywhere:** nothing at any resolution was returning a failing reading (Fig 6 :34-35), which is a legitimate stop, not a defect.

Note the danger in the middle case: importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed, and Fig 6 prices *"a wrong atom"* above starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24). A historian who declares a program impossible because it was unfunded commits the budget/absence confusion. Cold fusion is the standing world example: expectations diminished through failed replications and retractions, not through a proof of impossibility (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

- **Mode:** `general` for the distinction, `specified` when classifying a given program.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where "we searched and found nothing" legitimately proves absence. If it can, Fig 8 :39 is false and F4 falls.
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 8 on underivable/unreachable plus Fig 6 on the proto-instrument stop, two distinct figures with low overlap).

### F5 (D3) The anchor must not update on the present outcome

Fig 2 :33-34:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

The ground for a history of science is the dated record of what was available then. Presentism rewrites that record so it leads to today's outcome: the anchor updates on the answer. By Fig 2 :34 the result is coherent drift, and the drift is invisible from inside because the narrative and its ground now agree. Fig 7 :12-13 names the same failure across handoffs: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A chain of textbooks repeating the present consensus is one long room: motion, no progress.

- **Mode:** `general` (preservation of the anchor), `specified` for a given retrospective claim.
- **Refuter:** show that the past ground legitimately changes its answer when the present answer changes. If it does, the anchor is a frame and the presentist reading is not drift.
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor rule plus Fig 7 mirror chain; low overlap between an anchor figure and a handoff figure).

### F6 (D3) The line of descent is the proof, including the counterfactual

Fig 13 :140-141:

> **"not only what was concluded, but what could have been concluded, and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ."**

Fig 13 :128-129:

> **"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."**

Fig 13 :130:

> **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."**

A finished result does not say which route produced it. Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* The route is carried by the lineage, so a history that presents only the artefact has recorded half of the thing. What a history owes is the residue, the taken route, the refused alternatives, and the condition under which the answer would have been different. This is the paragraphs that world historiography calls "the accidental and contingent nature of historical events" (Butterfield, 1931).

- **Mode:** `general` for the lineage rule, `specified` for a given narrative.
- **Refuter:** show a composed term from which the route and the counterfactual are recoverable by inspection, with no lineage. If the artefact carries its own route, F6 is false.
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 13 lineage plus Fig 10 :62 provenance, different figures).

### F7 (cross-domain) Unbundle the union before searching

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :57:

> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The Library of Babel essay states the same discipline in the search vocabulary: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually"*. Applying one lever to all three items fails: a sagacity lever addresses chance (F3), a refusal lever addresses failure (F1, F4), and a budget/instrument lever addresses abandonment (F4). A history that explains all three with "the zeitgeist was right" is a single lever clearing the region where only one condition holds and leaving the intersections standing.

- **Mode:** `general` for the unbundling rule, `specified` for the three-way split claimed here.
- **Refuter:** show a single lever that clears all three conditions including their intersections. If one does, the union reading is false.
- **Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 5 fifth bin, two figures with low overlap).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach (Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An archive reconstructs the reach and the counterfactual correctly while recording no refusals or stopped branches (breaks F1, Fig 6 :42-43).
2. A discovery is documented in which no instrument at any resolution was returning a failing reading (breaks F3, Fig 6 :34-35).
3. "We searched and found nothing" is shown to prove absence (breaks F4, Fig 8 :39).
4. The past ground is shown to legitimately update on the present outcome, so presentism is not coherent drift (breaks F5, Fig 2 :33-34).
5. The artefact is shown to carry its own route and counterfactual, so no lineage is owed (breaks F6, Fig 13 :128-129).
6. A single lever clears all three conditions including their intersections (breaks F7, Fig 9 :54).

If any fires, the prescriptive shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Keep refusals and stopped programs in the archive as refusals with provenance, so the reach and the counterfactual remain computable (F1).
2. Price results in bits and actions separately; never let the published description stand in for the cost of establishing it (F2).
3. Record for each chance event what instrument was already returning a failing reading and what the observer was prepared to see (F3).
4. Label an ended program as refuted, unreachable-at-budget, or no-proto-instrument; do not collapse the three into "failed" (F4).
5. Keep the past ground from updating on the present; report what was knowable then and declare the historian's stake in the reading (F5, Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it"*).
6. Report the lineage: what route was taken, what was refused, and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ (F6).
7. Unbundle the question before applying any single explanation (F7).

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the method of checking (how to keep the reach computable), not a curated list of great episodes.

## 7. Strain test and re-census

**Apparent strain.** Fig 8 :39 says a search finding nothing is never proof of absence. Fig 6 :34-35 says that where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from. Read together, they seem to say both that absence is never provable and that absence can be a legitimate stop.

**Re-census.** They govern different objects. Fig 8 is about the *underivability* of the metaframe: no search inside a closure can prove what is outside it (Fig 8 :32-37, Gödel, Tarski, Chaitin). Fig 6 :34-35 is about *instrument availability*: whether anything at any resolution is already returning something that fails to resolve. One is a logical limit on proof, the other an empirical claim about detectors. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. This matches the README's rule.

**Second apparent strain.** Fig 6 :42 says the reach can fall when a refusal is added, while Fig 13 :134 says *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Re-census: one is about the derived reach of a library under new information, the other about what a frame may honestly report. A falling reach and an honest "unreachable" verdict are the same event read from two seats. No strain.

**After re-census: no hidden strains.** One qualified strain is recorded and not hidden: the world sources on chance are mostly by historians and psychologists of science whose agreement with the figure shape is expected given a shared evidence pool (see `09_watson_answer.md`). That is not corroboration, and it is declared.

## 8. Verdict summary

The question is answerable in shape. It is not a `gap`. The shape is: a history of failed science research is a **lineage record**, not a highlight reel. It keeps refusals and stopped programs (F1), prices results in bits and actions (F2), locates chance in an already-returning instrument and a prepared observer (F3), distinguishes refusal from budget-limit from absent instrument (F4), refuses to let the past ground update on the present (F5), reports what could have been concluded and what would have had to differ (F6), and unbundles the union before explaining (F7).
