## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to
look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence. It cannot say which primitive is
missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small
and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your
question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains;
it does not fill. For this question, the shape is the set of constraints a history must satisfy to
stay checkable and correctable. It is not a list of which famous discoveries were lucky, and it is
not a periodization.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for
  recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you
  are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it
  in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than
  the cause."* The historian's categories (progress, revolution, serendipity) are borrowed words.
  The effect is what the archive shows.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain
  description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This
  is the operational test for the shape claim in `02_outreach_email.md`: the five refuters are
  plain descriptions of what would be seen if the claim were false.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also
  be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that you have *"already composed the answer through
  the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."*
   The README says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can
   distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For a history, the ground is the
   dated primary artifact and the then-available instrument reading.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a
   source gate and a shadow test. Butterfield's Whig history is such an import from historiography,
   used to corroborate Fig 2's anchor rule, not to replace it.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the status of the primitives: *"Declaring the primitives does not
make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible
choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot."* A historian's periodization is likewise a
visible choice, not a discovery of nature's own categories. Recording it is what makes it corrigible.

## 4. Why the "list of failures" reading is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot
comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet
discovered. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior,
then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better
instrument exists."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a published result is the reading; the route that produced it is the source. A history
that lists the successful artefacts and calls that a history of science has the metamerism error.
Several routes can produce the same published result, and the artefact cannot say which. What is
owed is the lineage, not a larger pile of artefacts.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* states the same refusal: *"The projection that no instrument
distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even
when the copy is perfect."* And: *"indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 5. What the essays say specifically about failure and chance

This question is unusual because the essays speak to it directly, not only by analogy.

- *They're Scanning Books*: *"Discovery under consequence = a random walk plus a model that can be
  surprised."* A chance discovery is the walk; the prepared observer is the model. And the standard
  is failure quality: *"General intelligence is not reliable success on the novel, nobody has that.
  It is the right kind of failure: making progress through search-and-adaptation rather than
  confabulation. The bar is whether failure accumulates traction, not whether the answer arrives."*
  A history of failed research should report whether the failure accumulated traction.
- *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"Bad news travels in the same lane as good news."* And:
  *"Things fade, so they can be tried again."* The essay's decay rule is exactly the distinction
  between a refusal and a deletion: an old verdict loses weight, but the record is never deleted.
  It also says *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different"*, which is why a
  single consensus narrative cannot check a program.
- *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* on the separations: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can
  give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle
  it."* A famous historian's verdict funds a narrative; it does not settle it.
- *Arrival of the Fittest*: the figure rule *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is
  a description of one occasion."* A list of great discoveries is a recording of one occasion each,
  not the method that checks them.

## 6. Two consequences for this solution

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*:
*"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory,
cannot validate itself."* And *Arrival of the Fittest*: when an LLM simulates the framework,
*"the outputs generated must be seen as simulacra."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated
derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the
historiographic claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be
allowed to validate an action directly."* A list of prestigious historians who agree with the shape
is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to be tried against the
dated record, and the ground, not a reputation, decides.

## 7. Correction to my own §3 (recorded, not erased)

I first wrote that the shape-not-a-name rule forbids naming any particular structure or route in the
answer. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs *"a missing thing"*, a primitive not held in
Γ. Naming a route that is actually in the lineage is provenance, which the framework requires. What
remains forbidden is presenting the name as if the framework derived it, or using the name to replace
the lineage. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost. This mirrors the exemplar's
own §5b correction on naming Gradient.
