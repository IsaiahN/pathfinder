## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **The present is not a proxy for the ground.** Butterfield's Whig history is the historical name
   for the anchor updating on the outcome. This is Fig 2 :33-34's coherent drift, arrived at
   independently in a different field. Cite it when explaining why a success-only narrative is a
   structural failure, not merely an incomplete one.
2. **The record is biased before the historian arrives.** Publication bias and the file-drawer
   effect mean the archive itself has already dropped negative results. This is Fig 12's
   two-currency problem at one level up: the surviving bits do not carry the actions. Registered
   Reports (COS, F1000Research) are the institutional attempt to fix the channel, which is Fig 10's
   seat office: keep the anchor reachable rather than adjudicate what it would have said.
3. **Chance is not pure chance.** Dunbar, Fugelsang and Baumeister make chance discovery depend on
   a prepared mind and an already-returning instrument. That is Fig 6 :34-35 in cognitive-science
   vocabulary. It is also the reason a history should record the instrument and the expectation,
   not just the anecdote.

Source independence note: the Wikipedia pages and essays were read directly. Several of the world
sources draw on the same reviews (Merton and Barber, Dunbar and Fugelsang, Rosenthal, Sterling), so
their agreement is partly one evidence pool. The ledger records overlap per row. No world source
overrules a figure line, and no conflict with the figure verdict was found.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** : *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending a curated list of famous discoveries before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** : *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** : *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see in a history of a failed program before showing the framework.
- **Fig 2 :40** : *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; **Seat Map :163** : *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :60** : *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* A historian's stake in a preferred narrative is structural and should be named.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the figure links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## Two-stage offer, and the boundary on what this agent does

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim, refuters. No theory. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** offer the three source links. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes
