## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are D2, D3 and D4 isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

As the domains are usually written, D2 is `{signal, source, causal bond}`, D3 is `{signal, community, conventional bond}`, D4 is `{signal, agent, interpretive bond}`. They share only the signal slot, so by Fig 12's definition they are **not** isomers; they are three members of a **functional group** (signal bonded to a ground) with different atoms in the other slots.

The stronger form: write all three over a full shared atom set `{signal, typed bond, ground}`, differing only in which typed bond holds and which ground settles it. Then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the claim worth testing, because it makes the difference invisible in a flat formula and decisive in behaviour, exactly as Fig 12 says.

- Chemistry is the precedent the figure cites: Fig 12 :97-98, "silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation. Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; named isomerism by Berzelius, 1830."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either domain can be written without an atom the others require, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Equivalently, if two domains share all atoms and all bonds and all grounds yet yield different semantic content, a new atom is owed (an import), not a rearrangement.

### Q2. Symmetry decides why Shannon cannot be aboutness

Fig 12 :59: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Fig 12 :74: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." The Operators table gives the same tests at :15 and :33.

Shannon mutual information is symmetric: I(X;Y) = I(Y;X) (fetched Wikipedia Information theory article). Aboutness is not symmetric: a smoke signal is about fire, not fire about the smoke. Wikipedia also notes that "In contrast to mutual information, directed information is not symmetric." Directed information is therefore a better **shape** for aboutness, though it is still a syntactic measure and still lacks the bond to a referent (F1).

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." The Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay gives the small-scale case: heteronyms such as "live" versus "live," where "the position of a word decides what it means." Same ingredients, different arrangement, different meaning. That is Fig 12's isomer rule at the scale of a sentence.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a symmetric quantity that carries aboutness, invariant under swapping signal and referent while still distinguishing which is about which. If one exists, this finding fails.

### Q3. The order of the four strata

Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

Dependency order:

1. **Shannon level.** D1 is settled by proof. Its output, bits, becomes an atom at the next level.
2. **Coded-message level.** Bits bonded into symbols and sequences. Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A message without bonds is an inventory, not a route to a referent.
3. **Semantic-content level.** The message bonded to a referent, settled by a ground. This is where F1's missing half lives.
4. **Interpretive-uptake level.** The settled content becomes an atom for a receiver or a community. Fig 13 :110 "And each composition becomes an operand for whatever composes over it."

Each level's irreducibility is local to that level. Shannon bits are irreducible at level 1 and mere ingredients at level 3. This is the recursion the figures name, not a hierarchy with a top rung.

- **Falsifier:** show that one level can be composed without the output of the level below it (for example, semantic content with no coded message at all, rather than a coded message with no content). If so, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4. The analogue that must not be over-read

The Hard Problem is About Source gives metamerism: a spectrometer reads green and "cannot comment on the source." Several distinct sources give one reading. The reading does not determine the source. This is analogous to mutual information reading a correlation without commenting on aboutness. But it is an **analogue from a different domain**, not a figure line governing semantic information. Per Fig 9 :60, "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." It is recorded as corroboration for F4, not as a source of the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The three domains are a **functional group** as the world writes them and a real **isomer claim** when written over the full shared atom set. Shannon's symmetric measure has the wrong symmetry for aboutness, and directed information fixes the symmetry but not the missing bond. The four strata stand in a local, recursive dependency order, with each settled level becoming an atom for the next. The isomer claim, the symmetry claim and the level-order claim each carry a written falsifier.
