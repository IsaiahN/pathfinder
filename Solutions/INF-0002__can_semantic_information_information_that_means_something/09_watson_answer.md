## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a definition of semantic information, information that means something, can be given with no appeal to an interpreting agent. Option A: yes, meaning is fixed by the signal and its source, and the interpreter is only an instrument. Option B: no, semantic content is constitutively relative to an interpreter or to a convention standing in for one. What each leads to: A gives a world anchor and a definitional project; B gives an agent-relative anchor and a per-domain answer. The figures decide neither for the unqualified question, because that question is a union of overlapping conditions. They decide the shape and the verdict in each unbundled domain.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between Shannon's syntactic measures and aboutness. Arity: signal, referent, bond, and optionally an interpreter; the question asks whether the fourth term can be eliminated. Symmetry: aboutness is directed, Shannon mutual information is symmetric. Scale: four strata, signal distribution, coded message, semantic content, interpretive uptake. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." R is a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, Shannon / syntactic.** Actors: source, channel, receiver, probability distribution, code. Currency: bits, entropy, mutual information, capacity. Anchor: Shannon's coding theorems, a proof that does not move with opinion. Genuine domain, upstream and settled. Supplies ingredients, not aboutness.
- **Domain 2, causal / informational aboutness.** Actors: signal, source state, causal link, function. Currency: causal dependence and covariance. Anchor: the source state and its causal history, nameable when a stable link is measurable. Genuine domain. Interpreter eliminable in principle, but a bond and a ground remain required.
- **Domain 3, convention / shared meaning.** Actors: parties to a convention, code, community. Currency: shared meaning. Anchor: agreement itself, legitimate for a convention by Fig 2 :41. Genuine domain. Parties are constitutive.
- **Domain 4, interpretive / agent-relative meaning.** Actors: interpreter, response, behaviour, fitness. Currency: uptake and adaptive response. Anchor: behaviour, fitness, and human judgment where the question is constitutively about persons. Genuine domain. Agent is the legitimate anchor.
- **Domain 5, framework composition.** Actors: atoms, bonds, ground. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ) then settlement. Anchor: figures as metalaws for design questions, ground for measurement. Governs the shape and returns a constraint, not a definition.

**Partition or union.** D2, D3 and D4 overlap and form a union compound. D1 is upstream; D5 is the grammar applied to the union. **Order by dependency:** settle D1 before D2 through D4; unbundle the union before choosing a frame; settle each bond before composing across levels (Fig 12 :104).

**Strain test.** Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 appear to conflict about agreement. Re-census via Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." resolves it: the two lines govern a verdict about the world and a product about shared meaning, respectively. No strain remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The unqualified question has no single yes/no answer; it is a union, and per-domain verdicts must be kept separate. Causal aboutness can be defined without an interpreting agent in principle, but only with a typed bond to a source and a ground that settles it, and Shannon's symmetric measure is the wrong shape for it. Conventional meaning cannot be defined independently of the parties, because agreement is the product. Interpretive meaning cannot be defined independently of the agent, because the question is constitutively about that agent. Semantic information is the bond, not the ingredient list; a declaration does not ground meaning; and the framework returns a four-part shape, not a definition. Status: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. This is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, four refuters and the provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished definition instead of a shape and a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the unbundled claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A domain binding can be corrected without touching the shape. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove the two over-broad readings recorded in the retraction log. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent packet can be superseded.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that distinguishes aboutness from mere correlation would settle D2 and name the missing ground; a demonstration that shared meaning persists with no parties would break the D3 verdict; a partition proof for the three domains would break the union verdict; a derivation of directed aboutness from signal statistics alone would break the bond verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
