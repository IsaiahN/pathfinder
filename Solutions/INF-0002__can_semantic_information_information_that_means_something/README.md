## What this solution decides, in effect terms

The question asks whether a definition of semantic information can be built with no appeal to an interpreting agent. Option A says yes: meaning is fixed by the signal and its causal source alone, and the interpreter is only an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Option B says no: semantic content is constitutively relative to an interpreter, or to a convention that stands in for one. What each leads to: A yields a nameable anchor in the world and a definitional project; B yields an anchor in an agent or a community and a per-domain answer. The figures do not choose A or B for the unqualified question, because the unqualified question is a **union** of at least three overlapping conditions. They decide the shape any answer must have, and the verdict in each unbundled domain. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading and not an original reading of Isaiah's architecture.
