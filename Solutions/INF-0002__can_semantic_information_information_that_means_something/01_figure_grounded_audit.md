## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Shannon's theory measures transmission: entropy, mutual information, channel capacity. It brackets meaning. The residual R is the gap between that syntactic currency and what we call semantic information, information that means something, information that is *about* something.

- **Option A (yes).** Semantic content is fixed by the signal and its causal source, with no essential reference to an interpreter. The interpreter is only an instrument, and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). Leads to a definitional project with a world anchor.
- **Option B (no).** Semantic content is constitutively relative to an interpreter, or to a convention that does the interpreter's work. Leads to a per-domain answer and to the question of which anchor is legitimate.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a shape with four parts, (i) a signal, (ii) a typed bond to what it is about, (iii) a ground that settles which bond holds, (iv) a stratum at which the settling occurs. The sought-for shape is written down here so it can accrue a residual like anything else (Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM").

**R is not one question.** It bundles at least three conditions with different anchors. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Unbundle before searching.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity.** Semantic content is a relation with at least three terms (signal, what it is about, the bond between them) and often a fourth (who or what takes it as about that). The question asks whether the fourth term can be eliminated. This is an arity question, not a difficulty question.
- **Symmetry.** Aboutness is directed: the signal is about the source, not the source about the signal. Shannon mutual information is symmetric. A symmetric measure has the wrong symmetry to carry aboutness. Fig 12 separates symmetric bonds (test: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No.") from ordered bonds (test: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes.").
- **Scale.** Four strata: signal distribution (Shannon), coded message (syntactic composition), semantic content (bond to a referent), interpretive uptake (agent or community). Composition is recursive across them (Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible.").

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means not yet a domain.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Shannon / syntactic | source, channel, receiver, distribution, code | bits, entropy, mutual information, capacity | Shannon's coding theorems (a proof, nameable and unmoved by opinion) | upstream, settled; supplies ingredients, not aboutness |
| D2 causal / informational aboutness | signal, source state, causal link, function | causal dependence, correlation, covariance | the source state and its causal history, nameable when a stable link is measurable | genuine domain; interpreter eliminable in principle, bond still required |
| D3 convention / shared meaning | parties to a convention, code, community | shared meaning | agreement itself; Fig 2 :41 makes it legitimate for a convention | genuine domain; parties are constitutive, not optional |
| D4 interpretive / agent-relative meaning | interpreter, response, behaviour, fitness | uptake, interpretation, adaptive response | behaviour, fitness, and human judgment where the question is constitutively about persons (Fig 2 :37) | genuine domain; agent is the legitimate anchor |
| D5 framework composition | atoms, bonds, ground | composition inside closure, then settlement | the figures as metalaws for design questions, the ground for measurement | governs the shape, returns a constraint not a definition |

**Partition or union.** D2, D3 and D4 overlap and are a **union compound**, not a partition (Fig 9 :54, :55, :57). D1 is upstream and settled; D5 is the grammar applied to the union. D1 supplies the atoms that D2 through D4 bond.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Settle D1 before asking about D2 through D4 (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."). Unbundle the union before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Settle each bond before composing across levels (Fig 12 :104).

**Strain test applied:** the anchor clause in Fig 2 :36 and the convention clause in Fig 2 :41 at first appear to conflict. Re-census shows Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." They govern different quantities: a verdict about the world versus a product about shared meaning. Not a strain after re-census.

## 3. Findings

### F1. Shannon is the ingredient list; semantic information is the bond (decisive for Option A as stated)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :47: "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

Shannon entropy is computed from the signal's own distribution. It records the ingredients (symbols and their frequencies). Aboutness is the typed relation to what the signal is about. A definition made only from the signal's statistics has recorded half of itself and scores itself with a quantity it produces.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural shape).
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 (bond composition) and Fig 1 (ground as only metric), two figures with low overlap. World corroboration: Fulbright (arXiv 2026) states that "Traditional information theory provides powerful tools for analyzing statistical properties of information but offers limited insight into meaning and semantic transformation." Different pool, corroborates only.
- **Refuter:** derive directed aboutness from a signal's distribution alone, with no bond to a referent and no interpreter, and show the derivation preserves the direction of aboutness. If that works, F1 is false.

### F2. The symmetry mismatch

Fig 12 :59 (symmetric test): "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Fig 12 :74 (ordered test): "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Wikipedia's Information theory entry states that mutual information is symmetric, I(X;Y) = I(Y;X), while directed information is not.

Aboutness survives no swap: a smoke signal is about fire, not fire about the smoke. Aboutness therefore behaves like an ordered bond, which a symmetric measure cannot be.

- **Mode:** `specified`, unmeasured.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure test (Fig 12), corroborated by a world source from a different field. Not averaged with F1.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a symmetric quantity that carries aboutness (that distinguishes "X is about Y" from "Y is about X" while being invariant under swapping X and Y). If such a quantity exists, F2 fails.

### F3. The anchor test, not a blanket yes or no, decides the agent question

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :38: "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

So the role of the interpreter is decided by the sub-question, not by the word "semantic". Where the question is constitutively about an agent (D3, D4), the agent or the community is the legitimate anchor and cannot be eliminated without changing the question. Where the question is constitutively about a source state (D2), the source is the anchor and the interpreter is only an instrument. The unqualified question mixes both, so it has no single answer.

- **Mode:** `general` (the rule), `specified` in each domain below.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 (ground/anchor) and Fig 10 (ground maintainer, "a declared meaning is not a grounded one" at :18, "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." at :49), two figures with low overlap.
- **Refuter:** show a case where the question is constitutively about an agent but an agent-free definition still settles every instance of it, with no residue. If so, Fig 2 :36 does not govern here.

### F4. A declaration does not ground meaning

Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Fig 10 :19: "→ install what can be violated". Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."

This cuts both ways. An interpreter saying "this signal means X" does not ground X; nothing in the declaration is violable. Equally, a signal's structure saying "this is what I mean" does not ground X. What grounds a bond is something that can fail to pay.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 5's MDL bargain), low overlap.
- **Refuter:** show a case where a bare declaration of meaning is itself the ground, with a violable consequence and no further test. If so, F4 narrows.

### F5. Convention: agreement is the product, not the corruption

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".

For D3, asking for a definition independent of the parties is asking for a convention with no parties. The agreement is the product. This is not a defect to be repaired, and the essay "Decoding Tacit Knowledge" says the same in plain words: "Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10 plus an essay from a different medium. The apparent :40 versus :41 conflict was resolved by re-census (Fig 2 :39).
- **Refuter:** show a convention whose shared meaning is settled with no parties and no agreement, and which still fixes what a signal means to that community.

### F6. The capability analogue, recorded as an analogue and not a witness

Fig 11 :39: "the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."

The question "can semantic information be defined independently of an interpreting agent?" has the same shape as "can capability be defined independently of the habitat?". Fig 11 dissolves the latter into a contact question. Applied as an analogy, meaning lives in a contact system, not in the signal and not in a bare agent. The biosemiotics source states: "From a biosemiotic perspective, meaning cannot be reduced to signal transmission or statistical regularities, but emerges from the relational dynamics between organisms and their Umwelten."

- **Mode:** `general` as an analogue only.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure analogy (Fig 11); world corroboration from biology. Per Fig 9 :60, "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." This finding does **not** hand the verdict to Option B.
- **Refuter:** show a capability, or a meaning, that changes with the agent in isolation while contact is held fixed. Fig 11 :72 names that as the refutation of the capability claim.

### F7. The union must be unbundled before an answer is attempted

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". The Library of Babel essay says the same operationally: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."

The SEP entry on Information reports that the leading definitions "have next to nothing in common. Some are even conflicting (truth vs. probability, deterministic computing vs. probability)." That is the footprint of a union, not a single hard question.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay plus SEP (different pools).
- **Refuter:** show that D2, D3 and D4 partition rather than overlap, so one definition can cover all cases without residue.

### F8. The framework returns a shape, not a definition

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". Fig 6 :26: "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access." Fig 5 :62: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."

The figures can say what any candidate definition must contain: a signal, a typed bond to what it is about, a ground that settles the bond, and a level. They cannot supply the definition, because that requires a ground reading (a measurement, a proof, an instrument that discriminates sources). A search that finds no such definition is not proof none exists (Fig 8 :39).

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 13 plus canonical README), low overlap between the "kind of thing" limit and the "unreachable is a verdict" rule.
- **Refuter:** derive a complete, agent-independent definition of semantic content from the six-term set alone, with the bond type and its ground specified, and show it settles every case in D2, D3 and D4.

### F9. The habitat of a checker is enumerated, not composed

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Everything in contact with this residual: information theorists (syntax), philosophers of information (definition), biosemioticians and animal-behaviour researchers (non-human meaning), semantic-communication engineers (operational meanings), and cognitive scientists (interpretation). This list drives `03_public_contact_points.csv`. It is a contact list, not an evidence list.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 11), operationalised in the CSV.
- **Refuter:** show a class of checkers in contact with the residual that is omitted because a name or a discipline looked unfamiliar.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim (that semantic information is a bonded arrangement with a ground and a level, and that the unqualified question is a union) is refuted if any one of the following holds:

1. **Bond refuter.** A directed aboutness relation is derived from a signal's own distribution alone, with no bond to a referent and no interpreter, and the derivation preserves the direction of aboutness. Breaks F1 and F2.
2. **Union refuter.** D2, D3 and D4 are shown to partition rather than overlap, and a single definition covers all of them without residue. Breaks F7 and the per-domain verdicts.
3. **Anchor refuter.** A definition settles every case of D3 or D4 with no parties and no agent, while leaving the meaning claim intact. Breaks F3 and F5.
4. **Declaration refuter.** A bare declaration of meaning is itself the ground, with a violable consequence and no further test. Breaks F4.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Unbundle before arguing (F7). Name which sense of "semantic information" is at issue.
2. Lead with the shape and the pre-registered refuter, not with a definition (F8, Fig 4 :22).
3. Do not let the Shannon result hand a verdict by analogy (Fig 9 :60). Syntactic success is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the semantic claim.
4. Treat agreement among researchers as a contact signal, never corroboration (Fig 7 :12-13, Fig 2 :40).
5. Where an operational definition is offered, as in semantic-communication engineering, record which domain it belongs to and what its ground is. The arXiv relay paper defines meaning "by classification labels," which is a D3-style convention internal to a task, not a D2 source bond.
