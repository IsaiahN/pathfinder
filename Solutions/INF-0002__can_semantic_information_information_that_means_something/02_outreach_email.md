# Outreach draft: check the unbundled claim before deciding about the definition

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the reader gives a verdict on stage 1 before any framework material is shown. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about semantic information, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about semantic information that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "semantic information" is not one question. It bundles at least three conditions with different anchors. Fig 9 says "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either," and the right move is to unbundle before searching. The three are:
>
> 1. **Causal aboutness:** a signal carries information about a source state. Here the source is the anchor, and an interpreter is only an instrument, and instruments "can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). So, yes in principle, this can be defined independently of an interpreting agent, but not from the signal's statistics alone.
> 2. **Conventional meaning:** a signal means what a community agrees it means. Here "agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" (Fig 2 :41). The parties are constitutive. No, this cannot be defined independently of them.
> 3. **Interpretive meaning:** a signal means something *to* an agent. Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." No, this cannot be defined independently of the agent.
>
> The structural claim underneath all three is that Shannon's measure is the ingredient list and semantic content is the bond: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording" (Fig 12 :5). A symmetric measure such as mutual information cannot carry a directed aboutness relation. The framework returns a shape, not a definition: a signal, a typed bond to what it is about, a ground that settles which bond holds, and a level at which it is settled.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have produced the definition, that the unqualified question has a single yes/no answer, that no definition is possible, or that agreement among researchers settles any of this. A search that finds nothing "is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. Derive directed aboutness from a signal's own distribution alone, with no bond to a referent and no interpreter, preserving the direction of aboutness.
> 2. Show the three domains partition rather than overlap, so one definition covers them without residue.
> 3. Settle every case of conventional or interpretive meaning with no parties and no agent, while leaving the meaning claim intact.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then, here are the sources.** The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Philosophy of Information, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
