# Expert verification packet: the Tether on semantic information

**What this is:** a claim in a form an information theorist, philosopher of information or biosemiotician can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No instrument or proof settles a definition here.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Semantic information" is a union of at least three conditions with different anchors, so the unqualified question has no single yes/no answer. Shannon's measure is the ingredient list; semantic content is the typed bond to what a signal is about, and a symmetric measure cannot carry directed aboutness. Where the question is constitutively about a source state (causal aboutness), an agent-free definition is possible in principle, though a bond and a ground are still required. Where it is constitutively about a community (convention) or an agent (interpretive meaning), the parties or the agent are the legitimate anchor, so no agent-free definition is available. A declaration, by an interpreter or by a signal, does not ground meaning. The framework returns a four-part shape, not a definition.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 1

- **Statement:** semantic information is the bond, not the ingredients; Shannon's distribution-internal score cannot grade semantic content.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5, "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :47, "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." Fig 1 :27, "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
- **Refuter:** derive directed aboutness from a signal's distribution alone, with no bond to a referent and no interpreter, and show the direction survives. If that works, C1 is false.

### C2. Cross-field; checkable against the symmetry of the measures

- **Statement:** aboutness is an ordered relation, so a symmetric measure cannot be it. Shannon mutual information is symmetric, I(X;Y) = I(Y;X); directed information is not (fetched Wikipedia Information theory article).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :59, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Fig 12 :74, "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Fig 12 :94, "The arrangement is the substance."
- **What to check:** is the smoke/fire example faithful? A smoke signal is about fire; fire is not about the smoke. Does any symmetric quantity carry that asymmetry?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a symmetric quantity that distinguishes "X is about Y" from "Y is about X" while remaining invariant under swapping X and Y. If one exists, C2 fails.

### C3. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** anchor legitimacy is question-relative, so the agent is eliminable only where the question is not constitutively about an agent. A declaration does not ground meaning.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36, "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :41, "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 10 :49, "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- **What to check:** does the distinction between a source-state question and an agent-question survive in your field? In biosemiotics, does the organism's Umwelt play the anchor role, or the sign relation itself?
- **Refuter:** settle every case of conventional or interpretive meaning with no parties and no agent while leaving the meaning claim intact; or show a bare declaration that is itself violable and needs no further test. Either breaks C3.
