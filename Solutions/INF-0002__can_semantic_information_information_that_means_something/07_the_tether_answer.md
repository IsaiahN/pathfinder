# The Tether's answer on semantic information

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a definition. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Shannon's theory measures transmission and brackets meaning. The residual is the gap between a signal's statistics and aboutness. The question asks whether that gap can be closed with no appeal to an interpreting agent. The unqualified question is a **union** of at least three conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :54-57), so it has no single yes/no answer. Unbundle first.

## 2. The shape

1. **Semantic information is a bond, not an ingredient list.** Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Shannon entropy records the ingredients. Aboutness is the typed relation to what the signal is about. A definition made from the signal's statistics alone has "recorded half of itself" (Fig 12 :47).
2. **Aboutness has the wrong symmetry to be Shannon mutual information.** Fig 12 :59 versus :74 distinguish symmetric from ordered bonds. Mutual information is symmetric; aboutness is not. Directed information is closer in shape but still lacks referent and ground.
3. **The anchor decides the agent question, per domain.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Where the question is constitutively about agents (convention, interpretive meaning), the agent or community is the legitimate anchor. Where it is constitutively about a source state (causal aboutness), the source is the anchor and the interpreter is only an instrument that "can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38).
4. **A declaration does not ground meaning.** Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Neither an interpreter's declaration nor a signal's structure settles what it is about. Something must be violable.
5. **Agreement is the product for convention.** Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." This is not a defect in the convention domain; it is what a convention is.
6. **The framework returns a four-part shape, not a definition.** Signal, typed bond to what it is about, ground that settles the bond, level at which it settles. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that semantic information is undefinable. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that no interpreter is ever needed, or that one is always needed. The answer is domain-specific.
- Not that mutual information is useless. It is a settled measure of transmission, an atom at the next level, not the whole molecule.
- Not that this derivation settles anything. It is simulated and unmeasured.
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 4 :33: "Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress." Each level is local.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The Hard Problem is About Source: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source," and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Discriminator test:** is there an instrument that distinguishes "signal is about source S" from "signal is merely correlated with S" without appealing to an interpreter? If yes, D2 becomes `grounded`.
- **Convention test:** can a community's shared meaning be shown to persist with no parties and no agreement while remaining the same meaning? If yes, F5 and the D3 verdict break.
- **Level test:** can semantic content be composed with no coded message at all? If yes, the dependency order in `06_isomers_and_order.md` is wrong.
- **Pre-registered falsifiers from `01`:** derive directed aboutness from signal statistics alone; partition the three domains; settle conventional or interpretive meaning with no agent or parties.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four-part shape is what the figures license. Which definition fills it is a choice of ground and of domain, and the choice of which sense of "semantic information" is at issue is a seat's office, not something the loop can check: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." The lineage, the refuters and the failed fetches are recorded so a later reader can locate the error. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not define semantic information once and for all, and it does not answer the unqualified yes/no question. It says that "semantic information" is a bundle of three different questions. For causal aboutness, an agent-free definition is possible in principle, but only with a bond to a source and a ground that settles it; Shannon's symmetric measure records the ingredients, not the bond. For conventional meaning, the parties are constitutive, because agreement about a shared meaning is the product. For meaning to an agent, the question is constitutively about that agent, so the agent is the legitimate anchor. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, the per-domain verdicts, and four refuters written in advance. The slot stays open until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.
