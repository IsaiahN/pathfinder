### Q2 : Are the drivers isomers, or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 13 :75 to :76: **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."** and **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

Reading the three drivers over the six-term set:

| driver | minimal figure reading | bond emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| ocean circulation / sterodynamic | Energy + a gradient over space, composing a flow; density changes are a spectrum-like ordering of the same state | → sequence and ⋛ comparison across a spatial field |
| gravity, rotation and deformation | mass moved from ice to ocean changes a relation between near field and far field; the sign reverses with distance | ⋛ comparison, and network between source and ocean |
| vertical land motion | a change of the land state at the slot relative to the sea-surface state | − subtraction of one height from another |

- **As commonly written** (dynamic sea level, gravitational fingerprint, vertical land motion), the three share only the observed relative sea level. Under Fig 12 they are **not isomers**; they are members of one **functional group**, the recurring `state → structure → state` arrangement that shifts a relative sea level.
- **In the stronger form**, if all three are written over the full shared atom set and differ only in which structure mediates which state pair, they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different regional substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it is the one that makes their fingerprints distinguishable in principle.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one driver can be written without an atom the others require (for example gravity with no Network relation, or circulation with no Gradient), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. This is the same falsifier shape the exemplar used for GR versus QM, applied to a union rather than a pair.

### Q3 : What order is the chain?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Dependency order for this question, upstream first:

1. **Ice and land-water mass state.** Settled by gravimetry and mass-balance records. If this link is missing, no downstream fingerprint can be attributed.
2. **Gravity, rotation and deformation response.** Settled by GRD models with gravimetric and geodetic checks. Produces the near-field fall and far-field excess.
3. **Ocean dynamic and steric redistribution.** Settled by altimetry, Argo and circulation arrays. This dominates the annual to decadal pattern, so it can mask the step-2 signal.
4. **Regional mean sea level at the slot.** Settled by combining altimetry with GNSS-corrected tide gauges.
5. **Extreme still water level and flood frequency.** Settled by tide-gauge extremes. This is the third-order residual named in F4.

A reading taken at step 5 without steps 1 to 4 is not a weak reading of impact; by Fig 3 :22 to :23 it is a reading of nothing, because the input that step needs never arrived. Conversely, effort spent refining step 1 cannot rescue a broken instrument at step 4.

### Q4 : Which gradient is which?

- The spatial pattern is a gradient bonded to Space. The temporal rate is a gradient bonded to Time. Fig 13 :76 forbids treating them as one term.
- IPCC AR6 WG1 Ch9, world source: "Temporal variability in ocean dynamics dominates regional patterns on annual to decadal time scales". A pattern and its rate have different dominant causes at different timescales, which is what the figure predicts when the bonded states differ.
- **Falsifier:** if one field is sufficient for both the pattern and the rate with no loss in either, the two-gradient distinction fails.

## CONCLUSION

The three drivers are a **union**, not a partition (Q1). As usually written they are members of one **functional group**; over the full shared atom set they are a real **isomer** claim with a stated falsifier (Q2). The chain from mass loss to coastal flooding is **ordered by dependency**, and the decisive middle link is the gravity/rotation/deformation step, which has no single dedicated coastal instrument and is therefore the place a reading is most likely to be taken below the break (Q3). The spatial pattern and the time rate are distinct terms (Q4).

This framing keeps what the figures license without importing any new primitive. The specific coefficients, and the dominant driver at any named coast, remain world-frame results and are not asserted here.
