**Subject:** One checkable claim about regional sea level, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a claim about how sea-level rise varies regionally. I would like you to check one specific output against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` in shape, `specified` in the binding, not `grounded`):** the live signal is indexed by coastal location, not by the global mean. A near-zero global residual with one live coastal residual is a legal state, so a well predicted global mean and a badly predicted coast can coexist. That is not a paradox; it is what "indexed by slot, not aggregated" means. The residual the framework returns is a per-coast vector.
>
> The three named drivers, ocean circulation, gravity and land movement, are then a **union of overlapping conditions**, not three separable problems and not one hard problem. Their sign is **relative to the coast**: ice loss can lower relative sea level near the ice sheet and raise it above the global average thousands of kilometres away. And the causal chain **cascades**: ice mass changes gravity, rotation and deformation; that redistributes water into a fingerprint; that shifts regional mean sea level; that shifts extreme still water levels and flood frequency at the shore. A model scoped only to the coast misses the driver.
>
> What this claim explicitly does **not** say: that the global mean is useless (it is a legitimate aggregate indicator, just not the per-slot residual); that the framework produces a numerical regional map (it does not, and I record that as a gap); that the three drivers are figure-derived (they are an assessed world result, treated here as secondary corroboration); or that the structure is settled by agreement.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I ran this:**
> 1. A global mean plus fixed local offsets predicts observed sea level within instrument error at every coast.
> 2. Circulation, gravity/deformation and land motion act on disjoint coast sets with empty intersections.
> 3. One ice-loss event raises sea level by the same amount at every coast, with no near-field fall and no far-field excess.
> 4. Tide gauges and satellite altimeters draw on the same evidence, so their agreement is one pool talking.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it; the framework's own rule is that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.
