**Subject:** Check one output about migration and identity before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am sending one specific output about how a change of social setting alters identity, belonging, and emotional life. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** for a person s, the gap is between what their self-and-belonging library predicts will happen in a social setting and what that setting actually returns: do others grant belonging, do their emotion terms map onto ours, does the person's own story still fit. That gap must be measured per person. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Averaging people into "the migrant experience" throws away the only signal that says where to look.
>
> **The output we want checked:** identity, belonging, and emotional life are a property of person-and-habitat, never of the person alone (Fig 11 :39). Social change and migration alter them by changing contact. Migration is a substitution of one habitat for another: the person must reproduce what their identity goals required and inherits actors they did not choose (Fig 11 :66-67). What changes the person's reach is contact with a frame whose closure differs, because that is the only operator that moves the wall (Fig 6 :23). The person's own verdict and actual social treatment are the legitimate anchors here, because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37). Researcher agreement is not an anchor.
>
> **What this is not claiming:** that a particular acculturation strategy (integration, assimilation, separation, marginalization) *is* the answer. Those are names from one discipline's library. The framework returns a shape, not a name: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). It does not name which practice, category, or belief a given person should adopt. It also does not claim that migration always damages identity, or that a stable sense of self is a fixed inner core that migration merely uncovers. That idea fails the anchor test and was excluded at the census.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these before writing the output, and before contacting you:
> 1. A habitat substitution that removes the social payment for what a person does, with identity and belonging measured as unchanged.
> 2. A person who reproduces none of their old identity-supporting conditions and inherits none of the new habitat's actors, yet shows no failure on any identity, belonging, or emotion goal.
> 3. An identity change with no new atom entering from a differing closure, pure reflection inside the original library, that still passes the ground test.
> 4. Belonging lost while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure measure high and non-declining.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule and the shape/name limit: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The essays warn that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula" and that "the output is only as good as the validator." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and residual first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter before the conclusion | Seat Map design-instrument rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`, *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Person-level indexing, never an average | Fig 1 :19, :22. An average is how a live signal disappears. |
| Human judgment named as anchor, but researcher agreement refused | Fig 2 :37 and Fig 2 :40. The question is about people, so person reports are the anchor; agreement among a shared pool is not corroboration. |
| Discipline strategy names withheld | Fig 9 :32, shape not a name; Fig 8 :50-52, convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, only the record separates them. |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask users to write their own refuter | Formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; commit the prediction before the run. |
| One to one, never bulk | Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A receptive list is a convention, not a witness. |
