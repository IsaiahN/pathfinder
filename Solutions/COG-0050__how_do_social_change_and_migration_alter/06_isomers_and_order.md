### Q3 - Are two migrants with the same atoms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- Two siblings inherit the same cultural atoms (language, rituals, schemas, emotion concepts) but arrange them differently: one keeps the heritage atom as the bond between self and family, the other bonds it to the host language; one treats a display rule as a resource, the other as a constraint. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bonds, different identity substance. As a weaker reading, if only the functional group is shared (`state → structure → state` in some form) but the atoms differ, they are members of one functional group, not isomers.
- The heterogeneity is corroborated from inside the field: Kreienkamp et al. 2024 find *"large variations in how people acculturate and in how well they adapt to this process."* That is a phenomenon report, not the figure's mechanism, so it is single-source for the isomer claim.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two people with identical atom sets and identical bond arrangements return identical readings across all three conditions (identity, belonging, emotional life) under a documented change of habitat, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands instead.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** (sequence, an ordered bond).
- Fig 7 :12: **"same closure, handed along;"** and Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**: a migration is a series of handoffs, not one event; a repeated room stops progress.
- Kreienkamp et al. 2024 give the field's own ordered phases: *"acculturation conditions [ABCDs prior to contact], (b) acculturation response [ABCDs during contact], and (c) acculturation outcome [ABCDs following the contact]."* That is a sequence, not a set, and it matches Fig 3's dependency order (perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry).
- So the alteration is ordered: prior conditions set what can be perceived; contact produces a response; the response settles into an outcome; the outcome becomes a condition for the next handoff. Each turn begins from the residual the last one left (Fig 13 :88-89: *"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."*).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a documented migration produces identical outcomes regardless of the measured order of conditions, response, and outcome, the sequence claim fails and the union must be re-censused as a partition.

## CONCLUSION

The personal and collective selves are two levels sharing a kernel, with methods crossing and a measured round-trip gap. Two people with the same cultural atoms under different bonds are genuine isomers under Fig 12's definition; if only the functional group is shared, they are weaker cousins. The union runs in a dependency order (conditions, contact response, outcome, next handoff), not as a simultaneous blend. Three falsifiers are recorded above before the conclusion. The S1 strain (physical primitives on identity content) is resolved by restricting Fig 13 to the honest-stop role; the one live strain is the level-relativity of the anchor, named in `11_audit.md`.
