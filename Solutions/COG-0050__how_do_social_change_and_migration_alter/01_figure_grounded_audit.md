## 0. The residual in effect terms

Formula step 1: `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per slot s. Here:

- **b** is the person's belief about who they are and where they belong. It is formed from private history and inherited priors, mixed by the weighting **w**. Symbols table: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* and *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* This is the structural reason two people from one origin diverge under the same migration.
- **a** is what the person does: moving, learning a language, adopting or refusing norms, maintaining or dropping practices, telling or retelling their story.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the person's library predicts. It holds self-concepts, autobiographical memory, cultural schemas, and emotion concepts, with the bonds between them.
- **o'** is what the social environment actually returns: whether belonging is granted, whether others' categories match one's own, whether the emotion terms one carries are legible, whether the self-narrative still pays.
- **s** is the person. R is indexed by person and ordered in time, never collapsed into an average.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is not whether identity changes, but **how** it changes: what alters the relation between the person's prediction and the social return, and why the alteration is quick in some cases and slow in others. State it as: *why does the gap between what a person's self-and-belonging library predicts and what a new social environment returns widen, narrow, or change kind after a change of social setting?* The effect description names no theory, no "acculturation style," no "bicultural identity." Those are candidate names, withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a pair of conditions whose conjunction keeps contact with a paying ground reachable and moves reach outward through a differing closure, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from the other. Until bound, the shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so section 4's refuter can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* This question is constitutively about people, and Fig 2 :37 says: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* So the person's own verdict and the actual social response are legitimate anchors here. They settle "does this belong and does this fit." But the same line adds that human judgment is *"only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Cross-cultural comparison uses person reports as instruments, so they can be wrong on the very axis being compared. Researcher consensus, publication counts, and self-report scales the frame itself produces are frame-internal: Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a three-place relation (person Γ, action a, social return o'), indexed by person and ordered in time. It carries an **ordered** bond, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72), and a **comparison** bond for "belonging granted" and "superior fit": *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87). It is not symmetric: person and environment are not interchangeable, and a lost belonging is not recovered by reversing the move.
- **Symmetry.** Belonging is a threshold relative to a social field, not an absolute quantity. Loss in time is asymmetric, which is why "returning home" does not undo the change.
- **Scale.** Four scales are in view. The single person's step-by-step loop (Fig 4 one-agent panel); the habitat or population with *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5); the boundary between personal and collective identity, a membrane that is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21); and the temporal channel across a life and across generations, where *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24). The transform between scales is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: Γ sits inside the person's loop and also crosses the scale membrane, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D2 and D4 overlap: contact with the ground is exercised inside the habitat, so that overlap is a second compound. Identity, belonging, and emotional life are therefore a **union of overlapping conditions**, not three independent aspects and not one hard problem.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The Library of Babel essay names the same stop: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* The unbundled conditions are (C1) contact with a paying ground stays reachable, and (C2) reach moves outward through contact with a closure that differs. A lever that only repairs C1 leaves C2 standing, and the residue is categorically different, not a smaller dose of the same failure.

There is a second, necessary split. **Social change** alters the habitat around a person who stays put; **migration** substitutes the habitat for a person who moves. Both change contact, but they are different operations with different instruments, and their verdicts are kept separate below. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the person read the new social response?) → 2 vocabulary (does Γ have terms for what the new habitat affords, and for its emotion categories?) → 3 objective (can the person say what would count as progress or belonging there?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what the situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the instrument-poor one. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :115: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Most diagnosis effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29). Kreienkamp et al. 2024 observe the instrument gap from inside the field: *"few empirical studies have actually considered the theoretical implications of migration as a process and even fewer have methodologically followed the trajectories of migrants over time."*

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about persons, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: Identity and belonging are properties of person-and-habitat, not of the person alone

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low topical overlap: habitat relations vs scale membrane).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :19-20)

The answer to "how does migration alter identity" cannot be a list of inner traits considered alone. It is a change in the relation between a person and the field that pays their self-prediction. The habit of answering with attributes (resilience, openness, biculturalism) is an agent-only improvement that may not change contact.

**Refuter:** a controlled habitat substitution that demonstrably removes the social payment for what the person does, with identity and belonging measured as unchanged on all three conditions. If it survives, F1 is false.

**World bearing (secondary only):** Ward and Szabó 2023 write *"Intercultural contact produces changes in an individual's culturally grounded beliefs, behaviours and identities, a process known as acculturation."* Barua and Maheshwari 2025 write that *"migration can become a catalyst for rethinking and renegotiating one's identity."* Both corroborate the direction of change, not the figure's mechanism. They share the acculturation evidence pool, so they count as one witness (Fig 2 :31).

### F2: Migration is habitat substitution, with two silent failure modes

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and the animal-behaviour source Larue et al. 2018, genuinely low overlap).**

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** (Fig 11 :66)
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."** (Fig 11 :67)
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"** (Fig 11 :68)
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"** (Fig 11 :69)

Migration does not delete a person's habitat; it replaces it. The person must reproduce what their identity goals required (language, ritual, recognition, role, relationship) inside a field whose actors they did not choose. The two failures are invisible until they act: what could not be reproduced, and what was carried in unnoticed. This is the figure-grounded reading of culture shock, homesickness, and "returning home is not the same."

**Refuter:** a person who reproduces none of the identity-supporting conditions of their old habitat, inherits none of the new habitat's actors, and shows no failure on any identity, belonging, or emotion goal across a documented move. That breaks F2.

**World bearing (secondary, low overlap):** Larue et al. 2018, studying woodland caribou, report that *"subadult caribou often repeated the habitat selection tactics they had used as calves."* This is an independent witness, from animal behaviour, that early habitat experience carries into a new habitat after dispersal. It corroborates the *shape* of carried preference and substitution, not the human content, so it is single-source for the human claim and triangulated for the mechanism shape.

### F3: The alteration runs through contact with a differing closure; import is the only operator that moves the wall

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 8, distinct roles).**

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)
> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** (Fig 8 :32)

A person can keep composing inside their inherited self-library forever and never add an atom. What changes identity is contact with a closure that holds different atoms: another language, another emotion category, another rule for who counts as kin or neighbor. The wall moves outward by import, not by more introspection. Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

**Refuter:** a documented identity change in which no atom enters from a differing closure, pure composition inside the original Γ, and that still passes the ground test. That breaks idempotence and F3 with it.

**World bearing (secondary):** Kreienkamp et al. 2024 state that *"the prerequisites of culture and contact are central to understanding how the psychological experience emerges."* Hu and Cheung 2024 find that *"social identity can affect social integration directly without any moderators."* These corroborate the primacy of contact from inside the field, but they are one evidence pool (migration social psychology), so one witness.

### F4: The weighting w makes two people from one origin diverge under the same contact

**Mode: `general` structural from the Symbols table, with a `specified` consequence for persons. Domain D1. Status: single-source (framework only).**

Symbols table: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* and *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Two siblings in the same household inherit the same priors but have different private histories, so their self-beliefs lean differently and the same new environment returns different residuals. This is a structural prediction, not a measured one.

**Refuter:** two people with identical private histories and identical w who diverge in identity outcome under identical, simultaneously measured contact. That removes w as the divergence parameter.

**World bearing (secondary):** Heine's cultural psychology review documents two self models, *"One way is that the self can largely derive its identity from its inner attributes"* and *"A second way people can conceptualize themselves is to view the self as largely deriving its identity from its relations with significant others."* This corroborates that self-models differ across cultural settings, but it does not corroborate the weighting parameter w itself. Marked single-source for w.

### F5: A carried practice changes valence when the residual changes

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 habitat relations vs Fig 12 composition structures, low overlap).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** (Fig 12 :119)

A practice that produced belonging in the old habitat can become a liability in the new one without anything about the practice changing. A family obligation, a display of deference, a way of claiming status: each can regenerate or become spent across uses, and nothing in its description records which. This is the figure-grounded form of "identity conflict," stated as a relation rather than a trait.

**Refuter:** a practice whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the person's residual and habitat. If none can be found, F5 weakens to `general`.

**World bearing (secondary):** Mesquita, Vissers and De Leersnyder write that *"Culturally adaptive emotions are more prevalent and intense."* That is the same valence-relative-to-fit claim in the emotion domain, from an independent lab, so it corroborates F5 across a different field.

### F6: Emotional life changes when emotion concepts must cross a closure

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D3. Status: single-source for the figure mechanism; two independent world witnesses for the phenomenon, but one broad emotion-culture pool.**

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information"** (Fig 12 :5)
> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."** (Fig 12 :46)
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** (Fig 13 :115)

Emotion concepts are atoms in Γ and the relations among them are bonds. A new culture may hold a different ordering of those atoms, so arriving there either imports new atoms (new categories, new display rules, new appraisals) or leaves the person reading a emotion term with no matching source. Fig 9 :31: *"rather than the cause"* and Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The anchor rule matters here: a person's report is the right anchor for "is this a good answer for this person," but an instrument for cross-cultural comparison, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37).

**Refuter:** an emotion category that maps one to one across two cultures in frequency, intensity, appraisal, and display rule, with no residue left over. If found, F6 fails for that category.

**World bearing (secondary):** Mesquita et al. write that *"the emotional constituents are assembled in culture specific ways that are meaningful and predictable, resulting in systematic cultural differences in 'emotional practices'"* and Heine writes that *"display rules are the culturally specific rules that govern when, how intensely, and what facial expressions are appropriate in a given situation."* Both corroborate the arrangement claim. They are independent of the Tether but share a broad emotion-and-culture pool, so they are one witness, not two.

### F7: The three conditions must be unbundled before a lever is chosen

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` method consequence. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the independent meta-analysis Hu and Cheung 2024).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 (quoted in section 1.3) are the governing lines. The two conditions have different instruments:

- A C1 failure shows up as saturated metrics, pooled readings, a bracket that no longer matches the social response (Fig 10 :26, Fig 7 :38).
- A C2 failure shows up as a closure that keeps handing itself the same room, no import, and a reach that stops moving while the library keeps growing (Fig 6 :21, Fig 7 :12).

A single lever, for example "learn the host language," clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55). This is why the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue left over. That would collapse the union into a partition.

**World bearing (secondary):** Hu and Cheung 2024 report that *"the usefulness of analyzing different aspects of social identity on social integration is challenged."* This is an independent quantitative corroboration that the "aspects" overlap rather than separate cleanly. Corroborates, does not conflict.

### F8: Quick loss is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the capacity to belong

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 7, low overlap: ground maintainer vs room chain).**

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"** (Fig 10 :29)
> **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"** (Fig 7 :37)
> **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"** (Fig 7 :38)
> **"the ground's pressure on the room"** (Fig 7 :39)

A person loses belonging quickly when contact with what pays degrades while the field keeps paying others. The named decay modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, and pooled readings. "Pooled readings" is the person-level version of aggregating across slots (Fig 1 :22): the signal that says where to look disappears into an average such as "how migrants are doing." "Returning home" fails because the bracket no longer matches the person the ground now sees; it is a room handed along unchanged (Fig 7 :36).

**Refuter:** a documented case in which belonging is lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining. If both stay high, F8's mechanism is not the cause.

**World bearing (secondary):** Kreienkamp et al. 2024 note the field has *"a crucial disconnect between theory and practice"* and few longitudinal trajectories. That corroborates the channel-instrument gap, not the mechanism itself.

### F9: Personal and collective identity share a kernel; only methods cross the membrane

**Mode: `general`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 4 and Fig 12, distinct roles).**

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** (Fig 4 :22)
> **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."** (Fig 4 :27)
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** (Fig 4 :30)
> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"** (Fig 12 :104)

An individual identity change and a collective identity change run the same loop with different actors, currency, and anchor. What crosses from one to the other is a method that can be reapplied, not the recording of one person's experience. A personal narrative carried upward as if it were a group's identity is a recording, and replayed downward it produces a person repeating a past success and unable to produce a new one (Fig 4 :25). R_T measures what the coarser collective description cannot hold.

**Refuter:** a recording of one person's identity change that, carried to the collective level and back, is reapplicable with zero measured gap. Under Fig 4 :30 that should be impossible; if observed, F9 is wrong.

**World bearing (secondary):** Heine's two self models (independent and interdependent) show that the same person-and-relation structure is described at both individual and cultural scale. Weak corroboration of the kernel, single-source for the membrane rule.

### F10: Which specific atom a person needs is a gap

**Mode: `general`. Domain all. Status: figure-derived stop, triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 13).**

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** (Fig 9 :27-28)
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"It is recoverable from the lineage."** (Fig 13 :129)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** (Fig 13 :134)

The framework returns the shape of the alteration, not the name of the practice, category, or belief a given person should adopt. Naming it requires an instrument that discriminates among sources of the same reading. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The lineage, that is the route by which a person arrived at their identity, is part of what the framework can report, because *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available"* (Fig 13 :128).

**Refuter (lifting the gap):** a source-discriminating instrument that names the needed atom for a named person and the ground pays for it. Until then the honest entry is `gap`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A habitat substitution that removes the social payment for what a person does, with identity and belonging unchanged (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. A person who reproduces none of the identity-supporting conditions and inherits none of the new actors, with no failure on any goal (breaks F2, Fig 11 :66-69).
3. An identity change with no atom entering from a differing closure, pure composition inside Γ, still passing the ground test (breaks F3, Fig 8 :32).
4. Two people with identical histories and identical w diverging under identical measured contact (breaks F4, Symbols table w).
5. A practice whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual and habitat (breaks F5, Fig 11 :49-51).
6. An emotion category mapping one to one across cultures in frequency, intensity, appraisal, and display rule with no residue (breaks F6).
7. A single intervention restoring both conditions and leaving no categorically different residue (breaks F7, Fig 9 :57).
8. Belonging lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining (breaks F8, Fig 7 :37-39).
9. A personal identity recording carried across the scale membrane and back with zero R_T (breaks F9, Fig 4 :30).
10. Fig 13's six terms derived from five, or Fig 6's idempotence failing inside a closed set (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a ground measurement has paid for the shape per person.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of culture (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep C1 and C2, and the three conditions, separate in every report. A blended "adaptation score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Never publish an average as an answer. Per-person indexing is the instrument (Fig 1 :19).
4. Ask at each handoff which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
5. Treat any list of cultural traits as Γ, a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27).
