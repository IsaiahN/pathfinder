## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how information theory should model communication that changes the beliefs or behavior of its participants. Option A: extend the Shannon pipeline with a semantic term, keeping the anchor at fidelity of symbol reproduction. Option B: keep the Shannon channel for the symbol layer and re-anchor the effect layer on a per-participant change settled at the ground, with the bond included in the message and agreement about code separated from evidence about world. What each leads to: A reports a high-information message that can change nothing and cannot distinguish an echo chamber from a correct belief; B delivers a checkable shape, pre-registered refuters, and an explicit account of what instrument is still owed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the effect a message is predicted to have on a participant and the effect actually observed at a ground neither communicator can move. Arity: at least two participant frames plus a ground; the message is an operand and each participant is a separate slot. Symmetry: the message-to-effect bond is ordered (sequence, production) and comparative (threshold), not symmetric; the shared-code bond is symmetric (identity). Scale: first order is a mint inside one receiver's library; second order is the settled change becoming an atom that propagates through a network.
- **Domain 1, encoding and transmission.** Actors: source, transmitter, channel, noise, receiver. Currency: bits, code length, distortion. Anchor: the sent message, reproduced at the receiver. Question type: overlaps D2; a single lever here clears only the transmission-only region.
- **Domain 2, receiver as a frame.** Actors: the receiver's library Γ, belief b, weighting w, live residual R⁺_s, candidate term. Currency: belief update and description length. Anchor: an observed action or outcome that neither frame can move. Question type: overlaps D1; this is where effect lives.
- **Domain 3, convention and meaning.** Actors: sender, receiver, shared code, mutual knowledge. Currency: agreement about meaning. Anchor: a convention, explicitly not a ground; the shared code is the reference and has no truth value. Question type: overlaps D1 and D2; its product must never be promoted into D2's verdict.
- **Domain 4, habitat and propagation.** Actors: other participants, network, conditions, relations. Currency: influence, valence relative to the residual. Anchor: a behavioral outcome at population scale, such as a market, an election or an experimental result. Question type: overlaps D2; depends on D2's settled change becoming an atom.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. A message can be reproduced exactly and change nothing, or be garbled and still move behavior. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54-56).
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the effect, acquire terms for it, state the objective, then design the message, then carry the method. The chain breaks at the objective (Fig 3 :20-21, :27), not at the channel.

Strain test: Fig 2 :40 versus :41 is not a strain; the first governs world claims and the second governs shared meaning. Fig 12 two currencies is not a strain either; it governs two quantities that must be held apart.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Keep the Shannon channel for the symbol layer and add an effect channel anchored on a per-participant change settled at the ground. Measure per participant, gate the message by support, reachability and novelty, include the bond as part of the message, keep code-agreement separate from world-evidence, and price description and settlement in separate currencies. The output is a shape with six constraints and five pre-registered refuters, not a fitted law. A specific measure of persuasion is not named by the framework; a discriminating instrument is owed. This is `general`/`specified`, candidate, ungrounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by asking them to endorse a conclusion instead of checking an effect, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The re-frame adds a channel rather than withdrawing the Shannon layer, and the refuters are removable and testable independently. A superseded packet can be sent without retracting any primitive.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-participant instrument that separates message-driven change from receiver-history-driven change would name the measure; an already-held message with no live residual that still moves behavior would break the three guards; a shared-evidence population that tracks a movable ground better than a low-overlap frame would break the valence law; a frame manipulation with identical atoms that produces no discriminable difference would break the bond claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
