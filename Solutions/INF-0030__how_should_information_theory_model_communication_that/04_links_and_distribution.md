## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Shannon's anchor.** The classic model asks for reproduction of the selected message at the destination. It is the correct anchor for coding and the wrong anchor for effect. Cite it as the baseline being re-anchored, never as a refutation of the question.
2. **Equivalency framing.** The same facts under gain and loss frames reverse preference. This is Fig 12 :117 (*"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*) instantiated in a field with no contact with the figures. It is the strongest low-overlap corroboration of the bond claim.
3. **Grounding in conversation.** Clark and Brennan's two-phase account (presenting, then accepting with evidence of understanding) is a per-receiver check, not a channel property. It corroborates the three guards in F3 from cognitive science.
4. **Social influence.** Lorenz et al. show diversity collapsing while collective error does not improve. This is Fig 7 :13 (*"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*) measured in a laboratory with money at stake.
5. **Animal signalling.** ethology defines communication by its effect on receiver behavior, which is a corroboration of F1 from a field that does not use information theory's fidelity anchor.

The eight essays are by the framework's author. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight. The five sources in items 1 to 5 are from different fields and are treated separately in `09_watson_answer.md`, with overlap recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full framework before a recipient has described the effect sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the residual and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :39** - *"Correlation has valence."* This is not a general warning against agreement. Agreement about the shared code is the product of communication; the ban applies only to using agreement as a verdict about the world.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim; (2) the pre-registered refuter; (3) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (4) only then the four links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the loop as a method if they ask.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 2 :45 - frames range against the ground separately. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - methods cross up. The loop is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user still writes down what would refute their own result before running it, because a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is a shape, not a measurement; the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 is what makes it checkable. Do not omit it.
