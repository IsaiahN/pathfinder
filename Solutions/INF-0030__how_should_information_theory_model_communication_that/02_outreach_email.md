# Outreach draft - check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 has been copied verbatim into the first message. The offer collapses on contact if the refuter is withheld until after the theory.

---

**Subject:** One output to check: does a message change a receiver, or only reproduce itself?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (stated as an effect):** information theory usually asks whether the receiver reproduced the message that was sent. That question is settled by fidelity. My question is different: what changed in the participant, and what at the ground settles whether that change happened? A receiver can repeat a message perfectly and never act on it. A garbled rumor can still move a market. These are not the same measurement.
>
> **The shape claim (`general`, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The anchor of the model has to be the observed effect on each participant, not fidelity of reproduction at the channel output.
> 2. That effect is measured per participant, never as a campaign average. A mean near zero can hide one strongly moved person.
> 3. A message changes a receiver only if three conditions all hold: the receiver has a live unresolved question; the message composes with what the receiver already believes; and the message is not already in the receiver's library. Any one at zero and the message is inert.
> 4. The effect is carried by the arrangement, not just the ingredients: framing, order and relation. The same facts under a different frame are different messages with different effects.
> 5. Agreement about a shared code is the product of communication; agreement about the world is not a verdict when the two sides share an evidence pool. These are two quantities and must not be pooled.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not offering a new formula for persuasion, not claiming to know which particular belief measure is correct, and not claiming a measurement was run. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
>
> 1. A message already in the receiver's library, with no live unresolved question, still produces a per-participant change tracking the message rather than the receiver's history.
> 2. A group drawing on one shared evidence pool returns a belief about the world that tracks a movable ground better than an independent frame with separate evidence.
> 3. A framing manipulation with identical facts and a different frame produces no discriminable difference in belief or behavior.
> 4. The per-person effects are flat while the aggregate effect is large and survives a ground test.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Message about a shared meaning versus message about the world, Figure 2: [Figures text, local extract]
> - The bond carries the effect, Figure 12: [Figures text, local extract]
> - Grounding in communication, Clark and Brennan 1991: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grounding_in_communication
> - How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect, Lorenz et al. 2011: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299
>
> **If you want to use this as a method:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, state whether your claim is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`, and keep agreement about a code separate from evidence about the world.

## Stage 2 and stage 3, opt-in only

- **Stage 2:** send the four links only after the recipient has recorded a verdict on stage 1. Once they have absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3:** offer the loop as a reusable method. A frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question"* (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction"), so each user writes down what they expect and what would refute it before running it.
- **Never pool** a later agreement as corroboration. Agreement and trust are not witnesses here.
