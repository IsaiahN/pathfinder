**Subject:** Check one output about the cosmological constant before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the observed dark-energy density is an anchor, a measurement that does not move when a theory re-describes it. The "theoretical estimate" is not its equal. It is a family of frame-internal returns whose value changes with the regulator: roughly 120 orders of magnitude above the observation with a Planck cutoff, about 56 with dimensional regularisation, and lower still for the properly renormalised free-theory value. That variation is the first thing to check, because an estimate that moves when you push on the frame is not an anchor.
>
> Second, "why is it so small" is not one question. It bundles at least three conditions with different grounds: why the vacuum gravitates at all, why the renormalised value is so tiny relative to the cutoff, and why the dark-energy density is of the same order as the matter density *today*. A single lever, such as renormalising to zero, clears one condition and leaves the others standing. They need separate verdicts and must never be averaged.
>
> Third, the headline gap is a cross-scale projection, not a same-level mismatch. The prediction is formed at the ultraviolet end; the observation is read at the infrared end. A transform between those sizes is lossy by construction, and the honest report is the measured round-trip gap, not a forced identity. Treating the transform as lossless is dead reckoning.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework computes Lambda, that it selects among unimodular gravity, trace-free Einstein gravity, light-front quantisation, holographic bounds, or anthropic selection, or that it settles whether dark energy is a constant or a field. It returns a shape, not a value. After a genuine re-census no figure line yields the number or names a mechanism, and I record that as `gap`, not silence. I am also not claiming that all anthropic reasoning is empty; a bound stated before the measurement is a prediction and I treat it as such.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a direct ground instrument that returns the absolute vacuum energy rather than a difference, so the estimate is an anchor after all;
> 2. a single lever that shrinks all three bundled conditions together in a measured way;
> 3. a lossless cross-scale map with a round-trip gap of zero at the required precision;
> 4. a derivation, inside one closure and without fine-tuning, that lands on the observed value.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. The physics reviews are secondary corroboration and I have not treated them as witnesses.
> - Cosmological constant problem, review of the regulator dependence and the competing proposals: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_constant_problem
> - Carroll, "The Cosmological Constant", Living Reviews in Relativity 4, 1, 2001: https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0004075
> - Martin, "Everything You Always Wanted To Know About The Cosmological Constant Problem", Comptes Rendus Physique 13, 566, 2012: https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3365
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an output can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
