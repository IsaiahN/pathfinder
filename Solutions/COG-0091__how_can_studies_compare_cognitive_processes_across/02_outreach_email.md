# Outreach draft : validate the shape of a comparison before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three conditions C1, C2 and C3 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the recipient's own terms. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 shape is not checkable without the framework.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about comparing cognition across cultures

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape I would like you to check against what you already know about cross-cultural method. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The shape (`general` structurally, `specified` for method, not `grounded`):** a comparison of cognitive processes across cultures is legitimate only if three conditions hold at once.
>
> 1. The effect is described first in terms that travel, that is, what you would expect to see, in a form anyone can check against the world. No culture's instrument is named until that description is fixed.
> 2. The anchor in each site is a local outcome that the test designer cannot move. A test validated only on its authors' own culture is not an anchor for a cross-cultural claim; it is the frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces.
> 3. The frames compared draw on low-overlap evidence. Agreement among people who share one educational and media pool is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other. Convergence reached separately is the evidence; the record of how each site arrived at the construct is what separates it from a shipped instrument.
>
> In addition, the habitat is enumerated in each culture rather than filled in from a universal list, the investigator's stake in the reading is declared rather than hidden behind a claim of culture-freedom, and verdicts are kept per site and per task rather than averaged into one score.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular culture is more or less intelligent or rational; that a single construct is universal; that a named test is culture-free; or that the framework picks which construct belongs in the slot. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The question "what counts as intelligence or rationality" is, in this reading, the wrong question. The answerable question is what makes a system capable in a habitat.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a single culture's test, validated only on itself, shown to predict an independent ground in every culture with full equivalence against that ground;
> 2. two sites with demonstrably non-overlapping evidence pools failing to agree against the ground in a way that cannot be repaired by re-describing the effect;
> 3. a fixed a priori list of cognitive domains that leaves no residual when applied across habitats;
> 4. a cognitive strategy ranked identically across habitats whose residuals are measured as different;
> 5. an aggregated index that prospectively identifies, per site, where a comparison is failing with local-ground confirmation.
>
> I wrote these down before this message. If any holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The canonical figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - An independent open-access treatment of the insider/outsider problem: https://www.anthroencyclopedia.com/entry/emic-and-etic
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a comparison you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
