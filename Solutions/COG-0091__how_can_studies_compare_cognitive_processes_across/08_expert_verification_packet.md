# Expert verification packet - the Tether on cross-cultural cognitive comparison

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural methodologist, anthropologist or psychometrician can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on its consequences for method. Not `grounded`.** No local-ground measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "what counts as intelligence or rationality?" is the wrong question; the answerable one is what makes a system capable in a habitat. A cross-cultural comparison is legitimate only when three conditions hold at once: the effect is described in terms that travel before any culture's instrument is named; the anchor in each site is a local outcome the instrument's authors cannot move; and the frames compared draw on low-overlap evidence, so their agreement is triangulation rather than the pool talking. Cross-cutting requirements are that the habitat is enumerated rather than composed from a universal list, the investigator's stake is declared rather than hidden behind a claim of culture-freedom, and verdicts are kept per site and per task rather than averaged. The framework returns this shape and its refuters. It does not name which construct is the right one.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and conceptual; checkable by reading Figures 11, 2 and 1

- **Statement:** a ranking of cultures by "intelligence" or "rationality" answers a dissolved question, and a test that validates only on its authors' own culture is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :41 - *"Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird."* Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **What to check:** is the dissolution warranted? Fig 11 :72 gives the figure's own standing refuter: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim. The anchor test at Fig 2 :29-30 says an anchor that moves when you push on it is not an anchor.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single culture-invariant construct that predicts locally valued outcomes in every habitat with the same mapping, demonstrated against a ground that neither the construct nor its authors can move. Or exhibit an agent-only improvement that moves the ground. Either breaks C1.

### C2 - methods-facing; checkable by a cross-cultural researcher against study design

- **Statement:** independence, not sample size, is what makes cross-cultural agreement worth anything; convergence reached separately is evidence, while a shipped instrument is an adopted import that looks identical in the contents. Sites sharing one educational and media pool are one frame wearing many names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 8 :50 - *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 8 :51 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them,"* Fig 2 :42 - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **What to check:** in a study you know well, were the sites' constructs arrived at separately or shipped? Does the record show the route? Would the same agreement have been obtained from two samples inside one pool?
- **Refuter:** show that sample size alone, with evidence pool held constant, changes the verdict about the comparison; or show two sites sharing instruments and training whose agreement predicts an independent local ground as well as two independently derived sites do. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - measurement-facing; checkable by a psychometrician or anthropologist

- **Statement:** a declared construct does not ground itself; an operational definition must be violable by a ground reading in each site, and a score cannot comment on its own source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18 - *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :19 - *"→ install what can be violated"*; Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. The source-discrimination rule is stated in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."*
- **What to check:** for any published cross-cultural comparison, can you name the observation that would have shown the construct inapplicable in one site? Would the study have recorded it, or excluded the site? If no observation could violate the definition, the definition is a constant authored by the instrument designer (Fig 10 :48).
- **Refuter:** exhibit an operational definition declared in advance that is violated by a ground reading in one of the sites and whose violation is handled by revision rather than exclusion. If such handling is routine, C3 is procedural only.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a culture ranking. No culture is asserted to be more or less intelligent or rational.
- Not a claim that a universal cognitive capacity does not exist. The claim is about what may serve as an anchor, not about what exists.
- Not "measurement invariance is the answer". That is a binding from the psychometrics pool, provenance owed, not a figure-derived name.
- Not a culture-free instrument. Fig 10 :54: *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."*
- Not a derivation of one culture's cognition from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement.
- Not a claim that the construct in the slot is known. The instrument that would discriminate among the sources of a score is not held here.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Can an instrument distinguish a locally valued capability from a test-taking convention, a translation artifact and a motivation to perform, all of which produce the same score?
2. **Anchor test per site:** name the outcome in each habitat that the instrument's authors cannot move; show it does not move when the instrument is revised (Fig 2 :29-30).
3. **Independence audit:** show that sites' evidence pools are disjoint by contact history rather than by national label, and that agreement survives against separate grounds.
4. **Violability test:** state which observation would show the construct inapplicable in a site and show the study would record it rather than exclude the site.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39 and :41, Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :36 and :40, and Fig 8 :50-51 - six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a study you know: were the sites' constructs derived separately or shipped? Does the record say which?
3. For one published cross-cultural comparison, name the observation that would have violated its construct in one site. If you cannot, that is the finding.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
