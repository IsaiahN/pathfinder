## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the researcher's belief about what a cognitive process is: a construct inherited from a training tradition.
- **a** is the act of comparing: select an item, set a rubric, translate, sample, score, rank.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the researcher's library of constructs and instruments predicts participants will do.
- **o′** is what participants actually do in their own habitat.
- **s** is the cultural slot. R is indexed by culture and by task, never aggregated into a single "human cognition" score.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."** (Fig 1 :28)

The defect named by the question is now stateable without naming a theory: the instrument that produces the reading is authored inside one frame, validated on that frame, and then applied to another. The reading is therefore a projection of the instrument as much as a reading of the participant. The frame is scoring itself with a quantity it also produces:

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"coverage, and compression achieved"** (Fig 1 :25)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)

State the residual as: *why does the gap between what a comparison instrument predicts and what participants in another habitat actually do stay small in some slots and grow in others, when the instrument was built and validated inside a single frame?* This is an effect description. It names no culture, no test, no "intelligence", no "rationality". Those are candidate names, withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a conjunction of conditions under which a cross-cultural comparison is legitimate, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of another. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 3 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names candidates: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* A test score that changes when the test author revises the rubric is not an anchor. Expert consensus about what "intelligence" means is not an anchor. Viable anchor: locally nameable outcomes in each habitat, what actually pays there, measured per slot. This is a structural constraint, not a list of cultures.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a three-place relation: frame Γ, action a (the test), observed behaviour o′, indexed by cultural slot s and ordered by time t. It is a sequence, so it carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) and a **comparison** bond for "more rational" or "more intelligent" (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87). It is not symmetric: tester and tested are not interchangeable, and reversing them changes what the score means.
- **Symmetry.** "Superior" is a threshold relative to a reference group, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :85-89; Operators table :46-51). A score is not invariant under a change of reference group. Comparison is asymmetrical in time too: a culture's score once published does not unpublish itself.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single participant's step-by-step loop (Fig 4, one-agent panel), the population of participants with a shared instrument and *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5), and the habitat across generations. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured rather than assumed zero (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The measuring frame** (the instrument's authors) | test designers, item writers, rubric, the discipline's Γ | internal scores, published norms, construct validity within the frame | yes but scope-limited: predictive validity for local outcomes inside that culture, a genuine ground for the local question only (Fig 2 :36-38) | admitted with a scope restriction |
| **D2 The measured habitat** (each culture) | participants, local practices, conditions and relations enumerated from the world | locally valued outcomes: what actually pays, survival, competence as locally read | yes: the local ground, read off the habitat (Fig 11 :43-48) | admitted |
| **D3 The comparison frame** (the cross-cultural study) | researchers, translated instruments, multi-site samples | equivalence claims, measurement invariance, group differences | **not yet legitimate**: the anchor currently is often D1's test, a convention deciding an outcome (Fig 10 :47) | admitted as the domain under repair |
| **D4 The verification frame** (people checking the comparison) | cross-cultural methodologists, indigenous psychologists, reviewers | agreement and residual | should be the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :45). In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so independence is low (Fig 2 :31-32) | admitted, independence declared low |
| **X1 "Culture-free universal intelligence" as a free-floating property** | an instrument with no author | IQ points | **none independent of the test that produces it**; a frame scoring itself with its own output (Fig 1 :27) | not a domain. Excluded before any figure line is applied |
| **X2 "Culture" as one frame with one anchor** | a reified national culture | a single national score | not nameable: the habitat updates, the ground does not (Fig 11 :73) | absorbed into D2 as many habitats |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded structurally. This is not a claim that no general cognitive capacity exists; it is a claim that a construct with no independent ground cannot serve as the anchor of a comparison. X2 fails because a culture is a habitat, a field of contact that updates, not a settled ground (Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*).

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: the comparison uses instruments authored in D1, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D2 and D3 overlap: the comparison ranges over D2 populations, so that overlap is a second compound. D1 and D4 overlap whenever the verifier was trained in the measuring frame. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the"** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are:

- **C1 anchor legitimacy.** The instrument must range against a ground, not against the frame that authored it (Fig 2 :29-30, :36-38; Fig 1 :27).
- **C2 independence.** The frames compared must draw on low-overlap evidence, so agreement is triangulation and not the pool talking (Fig 2 :31-32, :35, :40; Fig 8 :48-49).
- **C3 effect-first description.** The target cognitive effect must be described in terms that travel before any culture's frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-31; Fig 6 :30).

C1, C2 and C3 are not three degrees of one failure. A study failing only C1 (well-described construct, independent sites, but one culture's rubric is the anchor) needs a different instrument from one failing only C2 (good local grounds, well-described construct, but all sites share one evidence pool). A single lever, for example "translate the test more carefully", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55). This is why the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the effect be observed at all in each habitat?) → 2 vocabulary (do we have terms for what the task affords locally, without importing one culture's categories?) → 3 objective (can we say what would count as capable in each habitat?) → 4 planning (choose sites, instruments, sampling) → 5 learn and carry (name what worked, verify, carry to the next comparison).

The middle link is where cross-cultural method usually breaks: having terms for what a situation affords locally and being able to say what would count as progress there. Fig 12 :113-115 says a vocabulary holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it, and that *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Most methodological effort lands above the break, where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30: *"practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."*).

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable consequence for method, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1 : "What counts as intelligence or rationality?" is the wrong question; the answerable question is what makes a system capable in its habitat

**Mode: `general`, with a `specified` consequence for method. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4 and Fig 2, three figures with distinct roles and low topical overlap).**

> **"What is intelligence?"** (Fig 11 :34)
> **"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."** (Fig 11 :35)
> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."** (Fig 11 :36)
> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird."** (Fig 11 :41)
> **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

The question as posed assumes that there is a thing called intelligence or rationality, that cultures possess it in differing amounts, and that a test can read it. Fig 11 declines the substratum question and substitutes the contact question. A ranking of cultures by "intelligence" is an agent-only improvement of a reading: it changes the description and does not change contact with what pays in either habitat. Fig 11 :41 names the exact move: ask what generates the observed capability rather than whether the observed behaviour qualifies as the label.

**Specified consequence:** a comparison that reports a single rank order of cultures by a construct has answered a dissolved question. The defensible report is per habitat: what makes a system capable here, in contact with what, and what would break that capability.

**Refuter:** a culture-invariant measure that predicts locally valued outcomes in every habitat with the same construct and the same mapping, demonstrated against a ground in each habitat rather than against the test itself. If such a measure exists, F1's dissolution is too strong. This is a ground reading, not an argument.

**World bearing (secondary only, one witness).** The *Psychometrics* article reports that *"critics increasingly question the assumption that intelligence can be measured as a culturally neutral and objective biological trait"* and that *"different cultures, socioeconomic conditions, educational systems, and social environments may value different forms of reasoning, adaptation, communication, and problem-solving."* That is the F1 distinction observed from inside the measurement field. It corroborates the shape, not the figures' authority. Overlap: the Wikipedia measurement pool is independent of the framework but overlaps the cross-cultural article, so it is one witness.

### F2 : A culture's test that validates only on itself is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces

**Mode: `general`. Domain D1. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 10, low overlap: agent metric vs ground maintainer).**

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)
> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** (Fig 10 :18)
> **"→ install what can be violated"** (Fig 10 :19)

A test designed in frame A, normed on frame A and used to declare frame A more rational than frame B is precisely a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces. The score, the norm and the interpretation all come from the same library. Fig 10 :19 gives the remedy: install a meaning that can be violated, meaning an operational definition tied to a ground that can return "no".

**Refuter:** a test whose scores predict an independent, locally named ground in each culture it is applied to, with the mapping specified in advance and shown to be violable. If this is demonstrated, F2 does not indict that test.

**World bearing.** The *Psychometrics* article states: *"a test is not valid unless it is interpreted and used in the manner for which it was designed"* and notes that *"test scores represent estimated interpretations shaped by theory, methodology, culture, environment, and context."* The *Cross-cultural psychology* article reports the pseudoetic failure directly: *"Irvine and Carroll brought an intelligence test to another culture without checking whether the test was measuring what it was intended to measure."* These two are distinct fields (measurement theory and cross-cultural method) but share a Wikipedia evidence pool; counted as one witness.

### F3 : The anchor is legitimate only when the question is constitutively about it; human outcomes qualify, one culture's definition does not

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, low overlap: anchor vs convention).**

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :30)
> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** (Fig 2 :37)
> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."** (Fig 2 :37-38)
> **"The anchor must not update."** (Fig 2 :33)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)

This is the most important distinction in the folder and the one most easily lost. The cross-cultural question IS constitutively about human cognition, so human outcomes are a legitimate anchor in the sense of Fig 2 :36-37. But a *particular culture's* definition of rationality is not the human outcome; it is an instrument. Fig 2 :37-38 says instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure, and a culture's test does not measure the axes on which another culture's competence lies. When that test decides the outcome of the comparison, it has stopped being a neutral convention (Fig 10 :47).

**Specified consequence:** the anchor in each slot must be named locally and independently of the test, before scoring. A cross-cultural study that names "score on the adapted instrument" as its outcome has no anchor at all; it has one frame's instrument.

**Refuter:** show that a single culture's rubric is wrong on no axis it does not measure when applied elsewhere, that is, full equivalence against a shared ground that neither the rubric nor its authors can move. Until then the scope restriction stands.

**World bearing.** The *Cultural relativism* article, in Boas's methodological form, states the same structural requirement: *"This view of culture confronts anthropologists with two problems: first, how to escape the unconscious bonds of one's own culture, which inevitably bias our perceptions of and reactions to the world, and second, how to make sense of an unfamiliar culture."* It also quotes Ruth Benedict: *"any scientific study requires that there be no preferential weighting of one or another items in the series it selects for its consideration."* That is Fig 2 :29-30 and Fig 10 :47 in the words of the field. Independent of the framework; shares the Boasian pool with the emic/etic sources.

### F4 : Comparison must be triangulation, not negotiation; samples drawn from one evidence pool are one frame wearing many names

**Mode: `general`. Domains D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 7 and Fig 8, three figures, distinct mechanisms).**

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** (Fig 2 :31)
> **"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."** (Fig 2 :32)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** (Fig 2 :42)
> **"same closure, handed along;"** (Fig 7 :12)
> **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :13)
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** (Fig 7 :36)
> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost"** (Fig 8 :48)
> **"completely are one frame wearing two names."** (Fig 8 :49)

A multi-site study that recruits hundreds of participants from the same educational and media ecosystem has a larger sample, not more frames. The agreement among them is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40). Fig 8 :48-49 gives the positive condition: the surplus, the extra reach that makes comparison worth doing, exists only where the closures differ. Fig 7 :36 adds the directional condition: the next site must differ from the last *and* match the residual the last one surfaced. Adding a site chosen for convenience is booking the same room.

**Specified consequence:** independence is a design variable to be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and the independence of sites must be described, not assumed from their national labels. Two neighbouring countries with shared curricula and media may be one frame; two regions within one country with different contact histories may be two.

**Refuter:** a study in which sites demonstrably share no evidence pool and nevertheless fail to agree against the ground in a way that cannot be repaired by re-describing the effect. More simply: demonstrate that sample size alone, with evidence pool held fixed, changes the verdict. Under Fig 2 :40 it should only change the precision of one witness.

**World bearing.** The *Cross-cultural psychology* article names the WEIRD problem: *"some psychologists became concerned that constructs and phenomena accepted as universal were not as invariant as previously assumed, especially since many attempts to replicate notable experiments in other cultures had varying success."* It also notes that *"Cross-cultural psychologists are turning more to the study of how differences (variance) occur, rather than searching for universals in the style of physics or chemistry."* The *Animal cognition* article gives a low-overlap analogue of the same design error in a different field: *"one of the main risks in this sort of work is anthropomorphism, the tendency to interpret an animal's behavior in terms of human feelings, thoughts, and motivations."* Different evidence pools, so two witnesses here, one from human cross-cultural method and one from comparative cognition.

### F5 : Describe the cognitive effect in terms that travel before any culture's instrument is chosen

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` method consequence. Domains D1, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 6, low overlap: leave/arrive vs membrane).**

> **"describe it before you go looking, or"** (Fig 9 :27)
> **"any frame you pick will seem to fit"** (Fig 9 :28)
> **"matching is a one-pass check, not a search"** (Fig 9 :29)
> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"** (Fig 9 :30)
> **"rather than the cause"** (Fig 9 :31)
> **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** (Fig 6 :30)
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."** (Fig 9 :44)

The description must be in effects: what would you expect to see if the process were present, in a form anyone can check against the world. If the researcher starts from "we will measure analytical reasoning with this matrix task", any culture that performs differently will seem to lack analytical reasoning, because the frame was chosen first and the effect was never described. Fig 6 :30 says the description travels when cast in effect terms and fails when sharpened into terms only we can read. Fig 9 :32 gives the limit: the comparison returns a kind of thing, not the thing itself.

**Specified consequence:** the pre-registration of a cross-cultural study should contain an effect description written before any instrument is named, and the instrument should then be selected for its ability to detect that effect in each habitat. Reversing the order is apophenia by construction.

**Refuter:** a comparison that fixes the construct in one culture's terms first and then demonstrates measurement invariance against a shared ground without re-describing the effect. If this succeeds, the "describe first" requirement is a preference rather than a constraint.

**World bearing.** The essay *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened directly, gives the same rule and its anthropological instance: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* It also states: *"Culture is a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge. Its members can hold very different mindsets; what they share isn't a set of beliefs but the backdrop they read each other against."* This is framework-internal corroboration, declared as such. The independent witness is Mostowlansky and Rota's *Open Encyclopedia of Anthropology* entry: Pike's emic standpoint *"would dispense with a priori means of classification. Focusing on one culture at a time, its goal would be to discover and describe the structured patterns of mental and bodily activities that the members of that culture, consciously or unconsciously, regard as distinct and significant for their system of behaviour."*

### F6 : Enumerate the habitat; do not compose the list of "cognitive universals" from one culture's theory

**Mode: `general`. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and the open-access anthropology source, different evidence pools; within the figures, single-source).**

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** (Fig 11 :43)
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"** (Fig 11 :45)
> **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"** (Fig 11 :46)
> **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"** (Fig 11 :47)
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** (Fig 11 :48)

A universal battery is a composed list: it asserts in advance which cognitive domains exist and then looks for them. Fig 11 requires the opposite order. Read off what actually acts on the capability in each habitat, including conditions and relations, then follow contact outward until the cascade stops mattering. What cannot yet be perceived or measured is the residual, not evidence of absence (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).

**Specified consequence:** domain sampling should be reported as an enumeration with provenance (who named each domain, from what contact), not as an application of a universal taxonomy. A capability present in one habitat and absent from the battery remains a live residual.

**Refuter:** a fixed a priori list of cognitive domains that, applied across habitats, leaves no residual, that is, misses no locally named capability and predicts none that is not found. If such a list exists and is invariant, F6 weakens to a caution about method rather than a constraint.

**World bearing.** Mostowlansky and Rota report that Pike's emic approach *"would dispense with a priori means of classification"* and that *"two etically identical behaviours can in fact differ profoundly, depending on the meaning and purpose of the actors."* The *Cultural relativism* article records Boas's objection to the museum method: *"Our objection...is, that classification is not explanation."* Two distinct fields, different authors, corroborating the enumeration order. They share a Boasian background, so this is closer to one evidence pool with two faces; declared as such.

### F7 : A cognitive strategy's value is relative to the local residual; no standing list of "rational" strategies survives a change of habitat

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations vs composition structures).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)

What counts as rational is a relation between a strategy and a residual, not a property of the strategy. A behaviour that is efficient in one habitat can be a malfactor in another without anything about the behaviour changing. Fig 12 :124 supplies the honest middle term: strategies can share a functional group, the kind of thing that solves a class of problems, while the qualifier, which member of the group, is habitat-specific. This refuses both extremes: there is no universal list of rational acts, and there is no incomparable chaos either.

**Specified consequence:** a comparison may legitimately claim shared functional groups across cultures. It may not rank the members of a group on a single scale without naming the residual each member serves.

**Refuter:** a cognitive strategy ranked identically in two habitats whose residuals are independently measured as different. If its valence does not change, F7 is false.

**World bearing.** The *Animal cognition* article supplies a low-overlap instance: *"Barbados bullfinches (Loxigilla barbadensis) from urbanized areas are better at innovative problem-solving tasks than bullfinches from rural environments, but that they did not differ in colour discrimination learning."* Same species, different habitat, different valence by task, exactly the functional-group-with-qualifier reading. It also states that *"learning and other cognitive abilities are shaped by the structure of environmental change"*, which is Fig 11 :49-51 in the animal-behaviour field.

### F8 : A declared construct does not ground it, and a definition that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 1, low overlap: seat rules vs agent metric).**

> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** (Fig 10 :18)
> **"→ install what can be violated"** (Fig 10 :19)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"** (Fig 10 :48)
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** (Fig 10 :49)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)

A definition of intelligence written into a test manual is a convention. It becomes a constant authored by the test designer the moment it decides which culture wins the comparison. Fig 10 :19 gives the test for an honest convention: can it be violated? If no observation could show the definition wrong, it is not measuring anything; it is legislating.

**Specified consequence:** every construct in a cross-cultural comparison should be paired with the observation that would refute its applicability in a given habitat. Without that, the construct is unfalsifiable and carries the authority of its author rather than the verdict of a ground.

**Refuter:** a study in which the operational definition is declared in advance and is then violated by a ground reading in one of the sites, with the violation handled by revision rather than exclusion of the site. If such handling is routine, F8's warning is already satisfied and the point is procedural only.

**World bearing.** The *Psychometrics* article quotes the Standards: *"validity refers to the degree to which evidence and theory support the interpretations of test scores entailed by proposed uses of tests"* and adds *"a test is not valid unless it is interpreted and used in the manner for which it was designed."* That is precisely a convention whose legitimacy depends on the use, not a property of the item set. Independent witness, measurement field.

### F9 : The "culture-free" observer is an idealisation no real occupant can meet; declare the stake instead

**Mode: `general`. Domain D4. Status: single-source within the figures (Fig 10), corroborated from two independent fields.**

> **"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."** (Fig 10 :53)
> **"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position. Before enforcing an absolute, ask what would have to be true for a real occupant to meet it."** (Fig 10 :54)
> **"A seat with no stake at all does not exist."** (Fig 10 :58)
> **"The condition is that nothing about which value the ground returns today serves the occupant: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** (Fig 10 :59)
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."** (Fig 10 :60)

A researcher who claims a culture-free instrument is claiming a seat no one can occupy. Fig 10 :54 says that a rule no real occupant could meet is an idealisation, and that idealisations license inferences no real position would support. The honest replacement is a declaration of stake: state which reading of the ground would serve the authors, then arrange for the ground to be read by frames that cannot be moved by that interest (Fig 2 :45-46).

**Specified consequence:** methodological transparency reports should include the investigators' prior commitments about the construct, not only their procedures. The commitment is modelled by readers whether or not it is declared (Fig 10 :60).

**Refuter:** exhibit an actual instrument with no author stake in what it returns, demonstrated by a construction record rather than a claim. If one exists, F9's "cannot" needs weakening to "has not yet happened".

**World bearing.** Two independent fields. The *Cross-cultural psychology* article: *"Some critics have pointed to methodological flaws in cross-cultural psychological research, and claim that serious shortcomings in the theoretical and methodological bases used impede, rather than help, the scientific search for universal principles in psychology."* The *Cultural relativism* article: *"Boas pointed out that scientists grow up and work in a particular culture, and are thus necessarily ethnocentric."* Mostowlansky and Rota sharpen the same point into a definition: for Pike, *"the etic categories devised by the scholars had no special status, but amounted to nothing more than the emic perspective of a scientific community."* That last sentence is Fig 10 :47 and Fig 1 :27 stated by an anthropologist with no knowledge of the figures. It is the strongest single world corroboration in this folder.

### F10 : Convergence at maximum independence is the evidence; shared atoms alone are not

**Mode: `general`. Datasets D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 8 and Fig 2, low overlap: union surplus vs anchor triangulation).**

> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** (Fig 8 :45)
> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** (Fig 8 :47)
> **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."** (Fig 8 :50)
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them,"** (Fig 8 :51)
> **"Residual first, frame second."** (Fig 8 :53)

Two cultures arriving at the same cognitive structure through separate contact is strong evidence, because nothing in the arrival of one depends on the evidence of the other. Two cultures arriving at the same structure because the same test was translated and shipped is no evidence at all; the atom was an adopted import. The contents look identical. Only the provenance separates them (Fig 8 :51).

**Specified consequence:** cross-cultural agreement counts as corroboration only when the record shows separate derivation. A study should therefore report how each site's construct was arrived at, not only that the scores align. Shared items are not the signature; separate routes to a comparable effect are.

**Refuter:** show two sites that share instruments and training but whose agreement predicts an independent ground as well as two independently derived sites do. If so, provenance is not the deciding variable, and Fig 8 :50-51 needs re-census.

**World bearing.** Mostowlansky and Rota describe Pike and Harris reaching related structures from different fields: *"I could prove I had not copied her because my paper trail was different, and my vocabulary was different. But the structures matched."* That sentence, from the framework author's essay *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, is framework-internal and marked as such. The independent instance from the encyclopedia entry is the Pike-Harris debate itself: two scholars, *"two basic standpoints from which a human observer can describe human behavior, each of them valuable for certain specific purposes"*, arriving from linguistics and ecology respectively. Different routes, comparable structures, recorded provenance.

### F11 : Keep verdicts per cultural slot; never average into one "human cognition" score

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` method consequence. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap).**

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)
> **"A global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"** (Fig 1 :13)

A pooled cross-cultural index is a filter: it tells a funder where to look and consumes none of the residual that says which site has lost contact with what pays. Worse, a global score near zero is a legal state with one live slot residual (Fig 1 :13), so the average can look healthy while a single site is failing. Fig 9 :60 forbids letting that filter hand you the verdict.

**Specified consequence:** report per-culture and per-task residuals, and keep the conditions C1, C2 and C3 separate in every report. A blended "culture-fairness index" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.

**Refuter:** an aggregated index that prospectively and per site identifies where the comparison is failing, with the failure confirmed against a local ground. If one exists, F11's methodological consequence is false.

**World bearing.** The *Cross-cultural psychology* article notes that *"Cross-cultural psychologists are turning more to the study of how differences (variance) occur, rather than searching for universals in the style of physics or chemistry."* That is a field-level move away from the aggregate. It is corroboration of method shape, not of the figure verdict.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A single culture's instrument, validated only on itself, predicts an independent ground in every culture with full equivalence demonstrated against that ground (breaks F2 and F3, Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :36-38).
2. Two sites with demonstrably non-overlapping evidence pools fail to agree against the ground and the disagreement cannot be repaired by re-describing the effect in effect terms (breaks F4 and F5, Fig 2 :40, Fig 6 :30).
3. A fixed a priori list of cognitive domains applied across habitats leaves no residual, missing no locally named capability (breaks F6, Fig 11 :43-48).
4. A cognitive strategy is ranked identically across habitats whose residuals are measured as different (breaks F7, Fig 11 :49-51).
5. An operational definition declared in advance cannot be violated by any ground reading in any site, and is still shown to predict those grounds (breaks F8, Fig 10 :19).
6. A genuinely culture-free instrument with no author stake is exhibited with a construction record (weakens F9, Fig 10 :53-60).
7. An aggregated index prospectively and per site identifies where a comparison is failing with local-ground confirmation (breaks F11, Fig 9 :60).
8. Fig 11's capability claim is refuted: an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (the figure's own standing refuter, Fig 11 :72).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a local-ground measurement has paid for the shape in more than one habitat.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Reframe the question before designing the study. Ask what makes a system capable in each habitat, not which culture has more of a substance (Fig 11 :39, :41).
2. Write the effect description first, in terms anyone can check against the world, then select instruments for their ability to detect that effect locally (Fig 9 :27-31, Fig 6 :30; *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world"*).
3. Name an anchor in each slot that the instrument's authors cannot move, and report the scope of each anchor (Fig 2 :29-30, :36-38; Fig 10 :24).
4. Buy independence deliberately. Describe each site's evidence pool, treat low overlap as the design goal, and count convergence at maximum independence as the evidence (Fig 2 :42; Fig 8 :48-50).
5. Enumerate the habitat per culture, including conditions and relations and two steps out, and report the provenance of each named domain (Fig 11 :43-48, :52).
6. Declare the stake in the reading. Do not claim culture-freedom (Fig 10 :58-60).
7. Attach a violability condition to every construct: what observation in this site would show the construct does not apply here (Fig 10 :19).
8. Keep verdicts per culture and per task, never averaged. Treat any pooled index as a budget filter only (Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :60).
9. Treat the cross-cultural claim as a union and unbundle C1, C2 and C3 before choosing a lever (Fig 9 :54-57).
10. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode (Fig 4 :22, Formula "Direction").

## 7. Recursive derivation summary

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :54-57 plus the *Library of Babel* bundle passage: it is a union of anchor legitimacy, frame independence and effect-first description, each with its own instrument.
2. **What is the anchor?** Locally nameable outcomes in each habitat, not a test score (Fig 2 :29-30, :36-38). The measuring frame's own validity is scope-limited to its own culture.
3. **Is the question well posed?** No. "What counts as intelligence" dissolves into "what makes a system capable in its habitat" (Fig 11 :34-41).
4. **Why does a transported test fail?** It is a frame scoring itself with its own output, and a declared meaning that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 1 :27, Fig 10 :47).
5. **How should the effect be described?** In terms that travel, before a frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-31, Fig 6 :30).
6. **What makes comparison legitimate?** Low-overlap frames ranging separately against a ground they cannot move; convergence at maximum independence with provenance (Fig 2 :31-42, Fig 8 :48-53).
7. **What is the habitat?** Enumerated per culture, including conditions, relations and second-order contact, not composed from a universal list (Fig 11 :43-48, :52).
8. **What can be said at the stop?** A conjunction of conditions, three failure modes, a pre-registered refuter and the honest admission that which construct occupies the slot is a `gap`. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives: **a cross-cultural comparison is legitimate only when the cognitive effect is first described in terms that travel, each culture's own ground is named as the anchor and the instrument's authors cannot move it, the frames compared are independent enough that their agreement is triangulation rather than the pool talking, the habitat is enumerated rather than composed from a universal list, the observer's stake is declared rather than hidden behind "culture-free", and verdicts are kept per cultural slot and never averaged. The question "what counts as intelligence or rationality" is the wrong question; the answerable question is what makes a system capable in a habitat.** Which particular construct is the right one is a `gap`, not a silence: the framework returns a kind of thing, and the instrument that would discriminate among sources is not held here. Mode `general` on the shape and `specified` on the method consequences. No world source was allowed to flip a verdict; the one genuine strain (anchor legitimacy) was returned to the census and narrowed. No unresolved strain remains after re-census.
