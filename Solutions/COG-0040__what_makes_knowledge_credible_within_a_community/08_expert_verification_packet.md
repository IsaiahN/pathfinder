# Expert verification packet: credibility standards in a community

**What this is:** a claim in a form a social epistemologist, cultural-cognition researcher or science-studies scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three domain verdicts, `gap` on the content of a particular standard. Not `grounded`.** No community was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A community cannot certify its own knowledge by its own credit assignments. A claim is credible when a frame has ranged it against an anchor that does not change its answer because of what the community thinks, and when at least one checking frame does not share the makers' evidence pool. Agreement has valence: shared meaning is the product of a convention, while agreement about the world from one pool is disqualifying as a verdict. Standards change by selection between rooms that differ against the residual a room surfaced, by refusing held terms, and by importing from closures that differ with provenance and a shadow test. The record grows while the reach can fall; there is no last room. The framework returns that shape and five refuters, not a named institution.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and social-epistemology-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** a community credibility score computed from its own credit assignments is frame-internal and cannot serve as the metric. Credibility requires a non-updating anchor and at least one frame independent of the makers' evidence pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :26 *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*; Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*; Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*; Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*; Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **What to check:** is the distinction between evidence overlap and opinion difference recoverable in your field's own practice? Fricker's credibility deficit and excess (IEP, opened 2026-11-22) assign credibility by identity power; does that belong on the hearer side of this line?
- **Refuter:** a credibility score computed entirely from a community's own credit assignments predicts independent ground outcomes as well as a ground-anchored score; or two frames demonstrably drawing on one evidence pool, with the anchor excluded, converge on ground outcomes better than either alone.

### C2 - the valence split; checkable against the convention/verdict distinction

- **Statement:** agreement has opposite valence in the two operands. In a convention about shared meaning agreement is the product. The same agreement is disqualifying for a world verdict when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Over a fixed atom set the two are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."*; Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** can a case be found where a community's shared interpretive standard is also settling a world claim, and the two cannot be separated? That is the compound where the valence rule is most likely to fail.
- **Refuter:** a shared-meaning agreement and a world-verdict agreement have the same bond to a non-updating ground, measured by whether each changes when the ground changes; or a convention that decides an outcome still tracks the ground at the same rate as a community that keeps the two separate.

### C3 - the dynamics; checkable in science studies and historical epistemology

- **Statement:** standards change by selection between rooms that differ against a surfaced residual, by refusal and by import from a differing closure. They do not change by accumulated assent. The record grows while the reach can fall, and there is no last room.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*; Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*; Fig 7 :12-13 *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 12 :140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*; Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*; Fig 10 :72 *"When the question changes, the room should change."*
- **What to check:** does a documented change of standard in your field fit selection between differing rooms plus refusal, or is it better described as pure replacement (Kuhn 1962) or pure accumulation (the 2024 Royal Society cumulative-progress result, partial read, reduced weight)? The figures take a third position: cumulative record, non-monotone reach.
- **Refuter:** a chain of rooms with near-identical closures, no surfaced residual and no ground pressure becomes more accurate by an independent measure; or a community with no way to withdraw standing corrects an error without layering a new standard over it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a community trust score, and not a claim that residuals may be averaged (Fig 1 :19).
- Not that consensus is worthless. It is the product in D2 and may be corroboration at maximum independence in D1 (Fig 8 :50).
- Not that any named institution is the ground, and not that an instrument is legitimate on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :37).
- Not a standing roster of credible sources (Fig 11 :51).
- Not a Kuhnian replacement and not pure accumulation; the empirical magnitude is contested and left to measurement (`01` S5, S6).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the institutional form of retraction is literally the unary negation operator. That is a live type question (`01` S4).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Anchor test:** does the proposed ground move when the community pushes on it? (Fig 2 :29-30)
2. **Independence test:** do the checking frames draw on different evidence? (Fig 2 :31)
3. **Valence test:** is the agreement producing shared meaning or settling a world claim? (Fig 2 :39-41, Fig 10 :47)
4. **Refusal test:** can the community withdraw a held claim's standing without deleting the record? (Fig 12 :140)
5. **Per-slot test:** does the assessment name the failing claim? (Fig 1 :19)
6. **The five standing falsifiers** in `01` section 4 and `07` section 5, all written before this packet.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :26-27, Fig 2 :29/:31/:39-41 and Fig 7 :35-36. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one case in your field where credibility was assigned by a source property rather than by a ground reading.
3. Check C2 against one case where a shared standard also decided a world claim. Was the bond changed?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish an independent check from a shared-pool reading, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
