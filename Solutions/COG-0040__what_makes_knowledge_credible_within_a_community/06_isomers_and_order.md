# Isomers and order: convention versus verdict, and the order of standard change

**Mode: `general` on the structural derivation, `specified` on the per-domain claims, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The community calls both "a convention about what counts as a good explanation" and "a verdict that this claim is true" by the same name: a credibility standard. The residual is whether these are one thing at two strengths, or two different substances that happen to share ingredients. A second residual is the order in which the pieces of standard change can occur.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network) plus, at the social scale, the library of available claims, the bond vocabulary (seven bonds and negation) and a ground that is not in the library. Bonds held: `+` symmetric, `∥`, `≡`, `→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`, and `¬` unary. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).

## Q1 - Are a convention and a verdict isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."** Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**. Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**.
- Both objects use the same visible ingredients: members of a community, agreement, a record, a vocabulary, a history. What differs is the bond to the ground. A convention is bonded to the community by a symmetric sharing relation; a verdict is bonded to the ground by an ordered ranging relation. Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

⇒ Over a fixed atom set, convention and verdict are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their written formula (a shared standard) cannot tell them apart, which is exactly why an unlabelled "consensus" is ambiguous. The behaviour distinguishes them: one coordinates a community and the other settles a claim about the world.

- What breaks the isomer relation: if a convention is promoted so that it decides an outcome. Fig 10 :47 **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. The bond has changed, so the substance has changed. This is not a stronger convention; it is a verdict wearing a convention's notation.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a community's shared-meaning agreement and its world-verdict agreement have the same bond to a non-updating ground, measured by whether each changes when the ground changes, then they are not isomers and this claim fails. The functional-group reading (shared ingredient set, different arrangement kinds, Fig 12 :124 **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**) stands instead.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 12 :117 (definition), Fig 2 :39-41 (valence) and Fig 10 :46-47 (promotion failure). Three figure locations with different subject matter, low redundancy.

## Q2 - What is the dependency order of standard change?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** The order is derived, not chosen.

1. **A residual must be described before a frame is selected.** Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. Asking "which community should we study" before stating which claim failed produces a study of whichever community was named.
2. **Refusal precedes minting.** Formula step 2 routes REFUTED before offering a new term. Fig 5 :53 **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**. A community that adds a new guideline while keeping the standing of the old error has layered, not corrected.
3. **The ground must be non-updating before frames can be compared.** Fig 2 :33 **"The anchor must not update."** Fig 2 :34 **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**
4. **Independence is purchased before agreement is read.** Fig 2 :42 **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Fig 8 :48 **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**
5. **Rooms must differ before selection can do work.** Fig 7 :36 **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
6. **Settlement precedes composition at the next level.** Fig 12 :104 **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**. A standard that has not been paid for by the ground cannot serve as a premise for a higher standard.

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Most community self-assessment instruments sit at link 1 and 5, perception and carry, while the objective link (what would count as a change in this standard) has no instrument. That is where the chain breaks.

**Verdict: `general`, single-source for the ordering sequence as such (Fig 3, Formula step 2) but triangulated for each individual step across Fig 7, Fig 9, Fig 2, Fig 8 and Fig 12.**

## Q3 - What is the analogue from collective navigation, and where does it stop?

Simons (2004) and Mann, Bailey and Codling (2024) were opened 2026-11-22. The many-wrongs principle: groups of agents with noisy independent estimates can navigate more accurately than a single agent, by pooling. Mann et al. add that *"when agents can accurately observe each other the correlations that emerge between agents reduce the effective number of independent samples that the group represents"*, and that individually rational agents free-ride on social information, so the group attends less to environmental cues than is collectively optimal.

This is a close structural analogue of Fig 2 :31 and Fig 6 :53, and a low-overlap corroborator for the independence parameter. It is **not** an identity:

- the animal ground is a target direction that does not update on what the group thinks, so the analogy holds at the ground half;
- the animal group has no operator for refusing a held estimate, so the analogy stops before Fig 12 :140 and Fig 5 :53;
- the animal group cannot import a new sense from another closure by a source gate and shadow test, so the analogy stops before Fig 6 :23.

The analogue corroborates the independence condition; it does not corroborate the refusal and import conditions. That boundary is recorded rather than smoothed.

**Verdict: `specified`, `single-source` on the figure side for the independence condition, with one genuinely low-overlap world witness.**

## Q4 - Does a standard change by replacement or by accumulation?

Both, in two different quantities. Formula "The record and the reach": *"What is recorded only grows; what is reachable is derived and can fall."* A refutation adds a term that refuses and removes nothing. The reach falls because something was added, not because anything was struck out.

For a community:

- the **record** grows: retracted papers stay in the literature, failed replications stay cited, the lineage of a standard stays available;
- the **reach** can fall: claims that were composable over a now-refused premise lose their standing without being deleted;
- **motion without progress** is possible: Fig 7 :12-13 **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"** and Fig 7 :32 **"one long room: motion, no progress"**.

This is the point at which Kuhn (1962) and the 2024 cumulative-progress paper conflict (recorded in `01` section 5, S5). The figures take a third position: cumulative record, non-monotone reach. Kuhn's *"no neutral algorithm for theory choice"* is refused because the figures require a non-updating ground; the pure accumulation picture is refused because a refusal lowers the reach. Neither world source is allowed to flip the figure verdict.

**Verdict: `general` on the two-quantity structure, `gap` on the empirical magnitude of any particular community's standard change.**

## Q5 - What is the order of the two quantities in time?

Fig 13 :4-5: **"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."** Applied at the social scale: the record (state-like, what a community is at a moment) and the standard (structure-like, how that record is arranged) alternate. Each step changes the other. Fig 13 :31: **"And because each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next, it is also where the randomness comes from"**.

So the answer to "how do standards change over time" is not a sequence of replacements with a final state. Fig 7 :6 names **"the limit: no last room"**. A community's credibility standard is always a room in a chain, and the next room is selected by the residual the last one surfaced.

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 13 :4-5 and Fig 7 :6, with the canonical README's *"there is no top and no infinite regress"* as a third framing.**
