**Subject:** One checkable claim about how a community decides what counts as known

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read an account of the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the three domains, not `grounded`):** a community cannot certify its own knowledge by its own credit assignments. A claim becomes credible when a frame has ranged it against something that does not change its answer because of what the community thinks, and when at least one checking frame does not share the evidence pool of the claim's makers. Agreement inside one pool is not a second witness. That is a structural claim about the shape of a credibility standard, not a measurement of any particular community.
>
> The second half follows from the first. A standard changes when a room that differs is selected against the residual the last room surfaced, when a held term can be refused, and when something is imported from a closure that differs with a recorded source and a shadow test. It does not change merely because personnel turn over, citations accumulate, or everyone agrees more. A chain of agreeing rooms can move without travelling.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a scientific consensus is worthless. Shared meaning is the product of a community, and agreement about a shared meaning is efficient for a convention. The same agreement is disqualifying only when it is used as a verdict about the world and the agreeing parties draw on one evidence pool. I am not claiming that any particular institution is the ground, that a fixed roster of trusted sources is possible, or that a community-wide trust score measures anything at the claim level. I am not claiming to have measured your field.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a community credibility score computed from its own credit assignments predicts independent ground outcomes as well as a ground-anchored score;
> 2. two frames drawing on one evidence pool, with the anchor excluded, converge on ground outcomes better than either alone;
> 3. a chain of rooms with near-identical closures, no surfaced residual and no ground pressure nonetheless becomes more accurate by an independent measure;
> 4. a community with no way to withdraw the standing of a held claim corrects an error without merely layering a new standard over it;
> 5. an anchor that changes with community pressure keeps discriminating the same claims correctly.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the relevant part of the shape is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract from the canonical SVGs): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked account of the architecture: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula and the figure set are in the links. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A Stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration, Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A Stage 1 refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
