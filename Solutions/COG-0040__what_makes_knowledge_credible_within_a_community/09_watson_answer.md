# WATSON answer: what makes knowledge credible within a community, and how do those standards change over time

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the content of a particular standard. Not `grounded`.**

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the figures say about community credibility. Option A: credibility is what the community agrees on and standards move by negotiation and turnover. Option B: credibility is contact with a ground that does not update on what the community thinks, checked by frames that do not share the makers' evidence pool, and standards move by selection between differing rooms against surfaced residuals, by refusal and by import. What each leads to: A scores a frame with a quantity it also produces (Fig 1 :26) and produces coherent drift that nobody notices (Fig 2 :34); B keeps an anchor, an independence parameter, a refusal operator and a checkable lineage.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Which specific claim failed to resolve for which community, and why the standard for resolving it moved. Arity: at least two operands, a world verdict and a shared-meaning convention, so a union compound (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Symmetry: agreement is symmetric under `+` (Fig 12 :59 "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."), while triangulation is ordered ranging against an anchor (Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."). Scale: one frame, a community of frames, a chain of communities over time.
- **Domain 1, the verdict about the world.** Actors: frames and one ground. Currency: residuals ranged against the ground, agreement weighted by independence. Anchor: the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.
- **Domain 2, the convention about shared meaning.** Actors: members co-authoring a convention. Currency: coordination and shared vocabulary, which is the product itself. Anchor: none in the world, legitimately, because a convention makes no world claim (Fig 10 :46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.
- **Domain 3, the change of standards over time.** Actors: rooms in a chain and the ground that selects between them. Currency: progress per handoff (Fig 7 :26 "progress per handoff: R = h² × S"). Anchor: the ground that votes between rooms (Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."). Verdict: `specified` on mechanism, `general` on shape, triangulated.
- **Cross-cutting condition, the refusal seat.** Without an operator that withdraws standing, D1 cannot correct and D3 cannot move (Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"). Its institutional form is a live type question, not a dissolved strain.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); keep the anchor non-updating before comparing frames (Fig 2 :33); buy independence before reading agreement (Fig 2 :42); require rooms to differ before selection can work (Fig 7 :36).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 are not in conflict after re-census because they govern different quantities, as Fig 2 :39 states ("Correlation has valence."). Fig 11 :73 and the claim that standards change are not in conflict because the habitat updates while the ground does not. Two live type questions remain and are named in `01` section 5.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Credibility is not a community's own credit score. It is a relation between independent frames and a ground that does not change its answer when the community pushes on it. Agreement is split by quantity: shared meaning is the product of a convention, world agreement from one pool is disqualifying as a verdict, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Standards change by selection between differing rooms against a surfaced residual, by refusal and by import with provenance and a shadow test. The record grows while the reach can fall, and there is no last room. No figure line names the particular standard a community should adopt, so that item is a `gap` reported as unreachable, not silence. The whole result is `specified` on the three domains, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the name, and not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, five refuters and the two live type questions before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is mistaking a shared-pool consensus for corroboration and thereby certifying a wrong claim with a larger audience, which is the Fig 2 :40 collapse.
- **undoable:** yes. The two live type questions are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state them as open. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, no primitive has to be withdrawn, and a refused term stays in the record so the correction is auditable.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor test showing that the proposed ground moves when the community pushes on it; an independence test showing the checking frames share evidence; a valence case where a convention that decides an outcome still tracks the ground; a refusal test showing a no-refusal community correcting an error; or a per-claim measurement showing a community average resolving failing claims as well as per-slot residuals. Any one of these fires the pre-registered refuter. A figure re-census that admits self-scored credibility as a ground metric would also reverse the verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
