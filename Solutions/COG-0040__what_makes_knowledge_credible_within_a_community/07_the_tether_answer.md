# The Tether's answer: credibility in a community, and how its standards move

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the three domain verdicts, `gap` on the content of any particular standard. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A community needs to decide which claims it may treat as known, and it needs its standards for that decision to be revisable. Two readings compete: credibility as agreement, and credibility as contact with a non-updating ground by independent frames. The residual is per claim, not per community (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). A community-wide trust score near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state.

The question bundles two operands with different anchors, so it is a **union compound**, not one question with opposite answers (Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Verdicts below are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Credibility is not a community's own credit score.** Fig 1 :26 names *"credibility accrued"* and Fig 1 :27 disqualifies it: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A claim earns standing when a frame ranges it against an anchor that does not move when the community pushes on it, Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
2. **Agreement is not a second witness when it comes from one pool.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* The parameter is evidence overlap, not headcount and not apparent opinion diversity.
3. **The same agreement has opposite valence in the two operands.** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* For a convention about shared meaning, agreement is the product, Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. For a verdict about the world from one pool, it is disqualifying, Fig 2 :40. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention, Fig 10 :47. Over a fixed atom set the two are isomers, `06` Q1.
4. **Standards change by selection between rooms, not by accumulation of assent.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Both factors are required, Fig 7 :37-39: *"Zero either and the chain runs in place"*.
5. **Standard change needs refusal and import.** A community with no way to withdraw standing can only accumulate, Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Only import moves the wall, Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* An import needs provenance and a shadow test, Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
6. **The record grows while the reach can fall.** A refutation adds a refusing term and removes nothing. Standards can be corrected without deleting history (Formula "The record and the reach"). Motion without progress remains possible, Fig 7 :32: *"one long room: motion, no progress"*.
7. **No standing roster of credible sources.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Credibility excess and testimonial deficit (Fricker 2007, opened 2026-11-22) are hearer-side assignments, not properties of the source.
8. **The community updates; the ground does not.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Cultural cognition (Kahan et al. 2011/2015, opened 2026-11-22) is a channel failure. An anchor whose keeper benefits from a particular answer is disqualified, Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a general trust score. Residuals are per claim (Fig 1 :19).
- Not that consensus is worthless. It is the product in D2 and can be corroboration at maximum independence in D1 (Fig 8 :50).
- Not that any named institution is the ground. Instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37).
- Not that all agreement is corrupt, and not that all disagreement is healthy. Disagreement from one pool is still one pool.
- Not that a standard change is a Kuhnian replacement or a pure accumulation. The figures allow cumulative record with a falling reach, and the world literature conflicts on the magnitude (`01` S5).
- Not that this is settled. No community was measured. Nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Anchor test.** Does the proposed ground's answer move when the community pushes on it? If yes, it is a frame (Fig 2 :29-30).
- **Independence test.** Do the two checking frames draw on different evidence, or are they two readings of the same report? (Fig 2 :31)
- **Valence test.** Does the agreement produce shared meaning (D2) or settle a world claim (D1)? If a convention is deciding an outcome, the bond has changed (Fig 10 :47).
- **Refusal test.** Can the community withdraw the standing of a held claim without deleting the record? (Fig 12 :140, Fig 5 :53)
- **Per-slot test.** Does the assessment resolve which specific claim failed, or only report a community average? (Fig 1 :19, :22)
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: a self-computed credibility score that predicts ground outcomes as well as a grounded one; one-pool convergence that beats independent frames; a mirror chain that becomes more accurate; a no-refusal community that corrects; a movable anchor that keeps discriminating.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures return the shape a standard must have, the conditions under which agreement counts, and the operators that move a standard. They do not name the next standard. The Kuhn/cumulation conflict and the institutional form of negation are live and recorded, not resolved by assertion. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not offer a trust score for a community. It says that a claim is credible when a frame has ranged it against something that does not change its answer because of what the community thinks, and when the check does not reuse the makers' evidence. Agreement is not a second witness when it comes from one pool; the same agreement is legitimate when it is only a shared meaning, and becomes a corruption the moment it is used to settle a fact. Standards change not by accumulating assent but by selecting rooms that differ against the residual each room surfaced, by refusing held terms, and by importing from closures that differ with a recorded source and a shadow test. The record grows while the reach can fall, and there is no last room. What it returns is the shape any credible standard must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The particular standard stays open until a ground settles it.
