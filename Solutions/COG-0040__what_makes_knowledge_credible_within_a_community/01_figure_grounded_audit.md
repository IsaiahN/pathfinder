## 0. The question in effect terms

> What makes knowledge credible within a community, and how do those standards change over time?

**Option A.** Credibility is what the community agrees on. Standards change by persuasion, negotiation and generational replacement. What this leads to: the claim is scored by a quantity the frame itself produces. Fig 1 :26 names that quantity, *"credibility accrued"*, and Fig 1 :27 disqualifies it: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

**Option B.** Credibility is a frame's contact with a ground that does not change its answer because of what the frame thinks. Agreement counts only when it is triangulation between independent frames. Standards change when rooms differ, are selected against the residual they surfaced, and import atoms from closures that differ. What this leads to: an anchor, an independence parameter, a refusal operator and a lineage that a later reader can check.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running.** Formula step "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* Bound shape: *credible knowledge is a settled term whose standing comes from a non-updating anchor reached by at least one independent frame, and whose standard changes only through refusal and import on a growing record.* That shape is refutable in section 3.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** The residual "credible knowledge in a community" takes at least two operands: a verdict about the world and a convention about shared meaning. Kuhn's case study (opened 2026-11-22, SEP entry on incommensurability) reports exactly this split as *"changes in the list of problems that a theory must resolve and a corresponding change in the standards for the admissibility of proposed solutions."* Two operands with different anchors is a **union**, not a partition (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Adding the diachronic question gives a third operand, the room chain, which is the dynamics of the union rather than an independent fourth condition.
- **Symmetry.** Agreement is a symmetric relation. Fig 12 :59 names the test for `+`: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Triangulation is not a symmetric joining of two claims; it is two frames ranging separately against an anchor (Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*). Reading an ordered relation (ground settles claim) off a symmetric one (claim agrees with claim) is a type error, not a small approximation.
- **Scale.** One knower (a frame with a library), a community (a population of frames, a room), and a chain of communities over time (Fig 7, the room chain). Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The loop is the same at all three scales.

1. **Refuse before mint** (Formula step 2). A community that cannot refuse a held term cannot correct a standard; it can only add to it.

3. **Settle D1 before promoting D2 into an outcome** (Fig 10 :47). A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
4. **D3 presupposes rooms that differ.** Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
5. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance, Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - "Credibility accrued" is a frame-internal quantity, and is disqualified as a metric

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 1 :25-27 lists what does not count, including *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*, then states *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

When a community says a claim is credible because it is widely credited, it scores itself with a quantity it also produces. The score rises when the community's own attribution rises, whatever the ground returns. That is the named collapse, not a measurement. This is the single most direct figure line for the question, because the word *credibility* is literally named as disqualified.

**Mode:** `general`, structural, triangulated across Fig 1 :23 and Fig 1 :26-27 (two different moves within the same figure: the metric declaration and the frame-internal exclusion). Low overlap, since one is an assertion about the ground and the other an exclusion of self-produced quantities.

**Refuter:** show a community credibility score that improves its prediction of an independent ground outcome while remaining computable entirely from the community's own credit assignments. If it does, Fig 1 :27 is wrong about the class, and the finding is withdrawn.

### F2 - Agreement is disqualifying for a verdict when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.

The relevant parameter is evidence overlap, not the number of people, not the apparent diversity of their opinions. Fricker's *Epistemic Injustice* (opened 2026-11-22 via the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy) describes credibility being assigned by *"identity power"* and *"prejudice causes the hearer to give less credibility to a speaker than is deserved"*. That is a hearer scoring a speaker by an attribute that does not bear on the claim, which is the same class of failure as a community scoring a claim by its own credit assignments. Kahan, Jenkins-Smith and Braman (2011, *Journal of Risk Research*, opened 2026-11-22) found *"the tendency of individuals to form risk perceptions that are congenial to their values"* and that *"cultural cognition shapes individuals' beliefs about the existence of scientific consensus"*, so two groups can agree internally and disagree with each other while both are reading the same consensus reports. Agreement inside either group is the pool talking.

**Mode:** `specified` on the community application, `general` on the valence rule. Triangulated: Fig 1 :27 (self-scoring) and Fig 2 :40 (shared pool) are different arguments, and the world sources add a third frame from cultural cognition (low overlap with the figures).

**Refuter:** show two frames that converge on a ground outcome while demonstrably drawing on one evidence pool and with the anchor excluded from both. If the convergence still tracks the ground better than either alone, Fig 2 :40 is too strong for this case and the finding is narrowed to the cases where the pool is wrong.

### F3 - Correlation has valence: the same agreement is a corruption in D1 and the product in D2

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40 disqualifies shared-pool agreement *"for a verdict"*. Fig 2 :41 says *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.

This dissolves the apparent contradiction between "agreement is worthless" and "agreement is the product". They govern different quantities. A shared vocabulary is the thing being produced in D2. The same shared vocabulary is a contaminant when it is used to certify a claim about the world in D1, unless it was reached by convergence at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50).

The live failure is the overlap: a community treats a convention about what counts as a good explanation as if it were a measurement of the world. Merton (1942, *The Normative Structure of Science*, opened 2026-11-22) names the institutional counterpart: *"truth-claims, whatever their source, are to be subjected to preestablished impersonal criteria: consonant with observation and with previously confirmed knowledge."* That is a D1 standard. Merton also records that the ethos fails when *"the larger culture opposes universalism"*, which is a D2 convention invading D1.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :39-41, Fig 10 :46-47 and the seat map law at Seat Map :163 (independence from the checking arrangement, which is framework-internal and counts as one pool with the figures). World corroboration from Merton is low overlap.

**Refuter:** show a community where a shared meaning decides a world verdict and the verdict still tracks the ground at the same rate as a community that keeps the two separate. If so, the valence distinction has no purchase here and F3 is narrowed to the cases where the ground is contestable.

### F4 - Standards change by selection between rooms, not by accumulation of agreement

Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :32: *"one long room: motion, no progress"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :37-39 requires both *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"* and *"the ground's pressure on the room"*, with *"Zero either and the chain runs in place"*. Fig 7 :42: *"The chain approaches the truth only as the world runs out of ways to refuse it."*

A community whose new members are selected for agreement with the old, whose instruments read through the same channel, and whose questions stay fixed, can change personnel and vocabulary indefinitely without changing its standard. That is motion, not progress. A standard moves when a room that differs is selected against the residual the last room surfaced, and the ground votes through that selection. Fig 10 :72: *"When the question changes, the room should change."*

Kuhn (1962, via the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on incommensurability, opened 2026-11-22) supplies a low-overlap historical witness: *"these differences arise because of changes in the list of problems that a theory must resolve and a corresponding change in the standards for the admissibility of proposed solutions."* That is a change in what a community will count as an admissible answer. It corroborates the shape, not the figures' mechanism. The figures require an unchanging ground and selection between rooms; strong Kuhnian incommensurability denies a neutral standard. The conflict is handled in section 5, not hidden.

**Mode:** `specified` on the change mechanism, `general` on the shape. Triangulated across Fig 7 :35-36 (selection) and Fig 10 :72 (question moves room), with world corroboration from Kuhn at low overlap.

**Refuter:** show a chain of rooms with rho approximately 1, no surfaced residual, and no ground pressure, whose credibility standard nonetheless becomes more accurate by an independent measure. If so, Fig 7 :35 is not governing this domain and F4 is withdrawn.

### F5 - Only import moves the wall, so a standard changes when a differing closure is brought in with provenance

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :14 calls import *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names"*. Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

Re-arranging a community's existing standards cannot enlarge what they can certify. A new standard that genuinely reaches further must arrive from a closure that differs, and it must carry a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (it explains a gap the community already had). Without the record, a convergent derivation and an adopted import are indistinguishable by content, which is exactly why an unlabelled "consensus" cannot be trusted.

Simons (2004, *Trends in Ecology and Evolution*, opened 2026-11-22) supplies a low-overlap mathematical-biology witness: the many-wrongs principle, where *"navigational accuracy increases with group size"*, but only when individual errors are not perfectly correlated. Mann, Bailey and Codling (2024, *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, opened 2026-11-22) sharpen it: *"when agents can accurately observe each other the correlations that emerge between agents reduce the effective number of independent samples that the group represents."* That is Fig 2 :31 and Fig 6 :53 written in the language of collective navigation.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 :21/:23 (closure) and Fig 8 :45/:51-52 (surplus and provenance), with two genuinely low-overlap animal-behaviour witnesses.

**Refuter:** show a community that enlarges the set of claims it can certify by re-arranging its existing standards alone, with no contact with a differing closure and no imported atom, measured against a ground it could not previously reach. If so, Fig 6 :21 fails for this domain.

### F6 - A community that cannot refuse cannot correct: the refusal operator is load-bearing for standard change

Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely."* Fig 12 :140: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Fig 7 :42: *"The chain approaches the truth only as the world runs out of ways to refuse it."*

Standards change downward as well as upward. A refutation adds a term that refuses and removes nothing, so the record grows while the reach falls (Formula "The record and the reach": *"What is recorded only grows; what is reachable is derived and can fall."*). A community with only accretion mechanisms (more papers, more citations, more members) has no operator for removing the standing of a held standard. It can add a new guideline and still compose over the old error.

A live type question is recorded rather than smoothed: real communities implement refusal with retractions, failed replications, dissenting minorities and formal votes of no confidence. These are not literally the unary operator `¬`, which acts on a single claim (Operators amendment :70-74). Whether an institutional retraction has the same logical effect as a negation is a question about the community's notation, and needs an instrument, not an argument. This is a **strain**, named in section 5.

**Mode:** `general` on the operator requirement, `specified` on the institutional binding. Triangulated across Fig 5 :53, Fig 12 :140 and Operators :58 (three places in the framework, low redundancy between the pipeline and the operator table).

**Refuter:** show a community with no mechanism that can withdraw the standing of a held claim, whose standards nonetheless correct a known error without merely adding a new layer over it. If so, the operator requirement is not necessary for this domain.

### F7 - The ground must not update, and a self-certifying institution is not a ground

Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*. Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap (opened 2026-11-22) states the same law in plain terms: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."*

For a community's credibility standard this means the anchoring institution cannot be one whose answer moves when the community pushes on it. A journal, a committee or a citation index can be an instrument, and instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :37). If its keeper benefits from a particular reading, the keeper is disqualified from tending the ground. The honest move is to declare the stake and keep the anchor elsewhere.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :29/:33-34 (definition and failure), Fig 10 :59/:93 (stake and disqualification) and the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap (low overlap in venue, maximal overlap in author, so counted as one witness).

**Refuter:** show an anchor whose answer changes with community pressure and that nonetheless continues to discriminate the same claims correctly on an independent measure. If so, the non-updating condition is not necessary.

### F8 - There is no standing list of credible knowers; valence is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Symbols :104 defines the ground as *"Whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Fricker's *testimonial injustice* and *credibility excess* (IEP, 2026-11-22) are the human-face version: a hearer assigns too little or too much credibility because of *"identity power"*, and the result is *"a loss of knowledge, since a low biased judgment of the speaker's credibility results in failure to learn what the speaker may know."* The figures add that the failure is not merely moral but structural: the same speaker can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another (Fig 11 :50). A permanent roster of "trusted sources" is therefore a category error even when every name on it was once earned.

Kahan's 2015 essay *"What is the science of science communication?"* (opened via public PDF text, direct PDF fetch unsupported, recorded with reduced weight) gives the mechanism: *"Motivated reasoning refers to the tendency of people to conform their assessments of all sorts of evidence to some goal unrelated to accuracy"*, with the Hastorf and Cantril case of rival-college students seeing opposing outcomes in the same disputed football film. The credibility of a source is read off group fit, not off the claim.

**Mode:** `specified` on the human application, `general` on the valence rule. Triangulated across Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 2 :31, with low-overlap world corroboration from social epistemology and cultural cognition.

**Refuter:** exhibit a fixed list of sources whose credibility ranking predicts ground contact equally across three residuals that differ in kind. If so, valence is not residual-relative in this domain.

### F9 - The credibility residual is per claim, not per community average

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :26 names *"pooled readings"* as a channel failure.

A single number for "how credible is knowledge in this community" aggregates away the only signal that says which claim is failing. The operational residual is indexed per claim slot: which specific assertion failed to resolve against what the ground returned, for whom, at what time. A global credibility score near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). Reporting only the global number is how a live failure becomes invisible.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side for the per-slot rule but triangulated with Fig 10 :26 pooled readings. World corroboration from the source-discrimination essay (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened 2026-11-22): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*.

**Refuter:** show a community-level credibility average that predicts which individual claims will fail on a ground as well as per-claim residuals do. If so, aggregation is not losing the signal in this domain.

### F10 - Cultural cognition is a channel failure, not a ground failure

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

The cultural-cognition literature (Kahan and colleagues, 2011/2015/2016, opened 2026-11-22) repeatedly finds that more information does not produce more agreement across cultural groups, because the same evidence is credited or discredited according to group commitments. This is not evidence that the ground moved. It is evidence that the channel to it is correlate with what the reader wants. Fig 2 :34 explains why nobody notices: *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

A genuine conflict was found and is recorded rather than smoothed. Van der Linden (2016, *Science Communication*, opened 2026-11-22) argues that *"the extent to which true attitude polarization occurs in response to 'mixed evidence' ... is vastly overestimated"* and that consensus messaging often **reduces** polarization. This conflicts with the strong cultural-cognition prediction. It does not flip the figure verdict, because the figures govern the structural condition (shared pool plus an anchor that updates with the reader's commitments), not the empirical magnitude of polarisation. The claim is narrowed in section 5 to the structural form, and the empirical magnitude is left to measurement.

**Mode:** `specified` on the failure class, `general` on the channel/ground distinction. Triangulated across Fig 10 :24/:26 and Fig 11 :73; the world evidence is internally conflicted and carries reduced verdict weight.

**Refuter:** show a case where the ground itself changes its answer when a community pushes on it, and the credibility failure is fully explained by that change rather than by the channel. If so, the channel/ground split misdescribes this domain.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim (credibility = non-updating ground reached by independent frames; standards change by refusal and import on a growing record) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A community credibility score computed entirely from its own credit assignments predicts independent ground outcomes as well as a ground-anchored score does. (breaks F1, Fig 1 :26-27)
2. Two frames that demonstrably draw on one evidence pool, with the anchor excluded, converge on ground outcomes better than either alone. (breaks F2, Fig 2 :40)
3. A chain of rooms with rho approximately 1, no surfaced residual and no ground pressure changes its credibility standard in a way that an independent measure calls more accurate. (breaks F4, Fig 7 :35-36)
4. A community that cannot withdraw the standing of a held claim corrects a known error without merely layering a new standard over it. (breaks F6, Fig 12 :140)
5. An anchor whose answer moves with community pressure continues to discriminate the same claims correctly on an independent measure. (breaks F7, Fig 2 :33)
6. A fixed roster of credible sources predicts ground contact equally across three residuals of different kinds. (breaks F8, Fig 11 :51)

If any fires, the shape is wrong in that domain and the affected finding is withdrawn with the failure recorded here. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is credibility an anchor or a frame?** A frame produces *"credibility accrued"* (Fig 1 :26), so it is a frame-internal quantity, not an anchor. Credibility must be re-grounded in contact with a non-updating ground (Fig 2 :29).
2. **Does agreement supply the anchor?** No. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Agreement is symmetric (`+`, Fig 12 :59); triangulation is ordered.
3. **Is any agreement usable?** Yes, in D2, where agreement is the product (Fig 2 :41), and in D1 when reached by convergence at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50). The record separates the two (Fig 8 :51-52).
4. **What is the residual, and can it be aggregated?** It is per claim slot (Fig 1 :19) and must not be averaged (Fig 1 :22).
5. **How do standards change?** By refusal (Fig 5 :53, Fig 12 :140), by import from a differing closure (Fig 6 :23) and by selection between rooms against a surfaced residual (Fig 7 :35-36). Not by accumulating agreement (Fig 7 :12-13).
6. **What if the anchoring institution is interested?** Fig 10 :93 disqualifies its keeper. Declare the stake (Fig 10 :60) and keep the anchor non-updating (Fig 2 :33).
7. **Can the framework name the next standard?** No. Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134. The gap is a verdict. What remains outside is a seat's office, not the loop's.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: credibility is a relation between independent frames and a non-updating ground, never a community's own credit score; standards move by refusal, import and selection between differing rooms, on a record that grows while the reach can fall. Strained after re-census: two live type questions, the institutional form of negation (S4) and the empirical Kuhn/cumulation magnitude (S5), both named. Nothing is `grounded`.
