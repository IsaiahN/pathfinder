## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

A **shape** is therefore the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name its occupant. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- Shape: *a state keeps legitimacy while a per-slot channel to a non-updating governed ground stays reachable, its lever is introduction rather than exclusion, and its legitimacy convention is not cashed out as a credential.* It loses legitimacy when standing gives its seat a stake in the reading and its instruments pool the live signal away.
- Not a shape: "country X is legitimate because of reform Y". That is a name with a date and a place, and the figures do not supply it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrap vocabulary by proximity. Use the terms you already have that are closest to the thing, note which recurring new terms overlap or correlate with them, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* Applied here: "trust", "compliance", "capacity", "bureaucracy" are the near terms; "anchor", "channel", "stake", "per-slot residual" are the recurring new ones that narrow the space.
- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. So the question is carried into political science, social psychology and political ecology in effect terms, not as "anchor theory applied to states".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is exactly what the five refuters in `01` and `08` are: plain descriptions of what would count as failure.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* Here the finish line is the shape plus its refuter; anything further is a country name, which is F8's gap.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, neither of them inside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* A per-slot longitudinal instrument that discriminates sources of legitimacy would license a name here. None was run.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Scott's legibility thesis and Habermas's input-crisis thesis were read as corroboration of the shape, not imported as new primitives into the framework.

So "which reform restores legitimacy in country Z" is not answered by this package. It is F8's `gap`, unreachable at this budget.

## 4. The metamerism warning: why a national legitimacy score is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it.

A national trust score is the same metamerism error. High average trust can come from broad genuine contact, from system justification among the disadvantaged, from fear of reprisal, or from a small elite's acquiescence. The reading does not determine the source. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* What is owed is a discriminator, not a name, and not a better average.

The same essay's warning about constructed limits applies: Chalmers *"stipulat[ed] a limitation rather than prov[ing] one"*. Do not stipulate that legitimacy cannot be measured per slot. The absence of an instrument today is `unreached`, not `unreachable in principle` (Fig 8 :40-43).

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* The essay's diagnosis of rot is exactly F3 at the state scale: *"someone let prestige stand in for proof... someone let reputation substitute for result... The prestige loop confirms itself... The ground is never consulted. The error compounds... Nobody notices because the prestige loop looks like agreement, and agreement feels like truth."*

For this question: a state's international standing, its rankings and its own credibility accrued are frame-internal (Fig 1 :26-27) and can fund a reform but cannot settle whether legitimacy improved. The ground does that. This is also why this package withholds names of countries and reform packages: naming an admired case would be prestige standing in for proof.

## 6. Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote §3 as if "shape, not a name" forbade naming any specific element of the answer. That was too broad. The canonical README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* It governs a primitive not held in Γ. Anchors, channels, stakes and per-slot residuals are **already in the figure library**, so using them in the shape is not inventing missing primitives. What remains forbidden is (a) claiming a local reform is derived from the figures, and (b) naming the occupant of a missing instrument that has not been built. Both claims are now recorded, so neither the overreach nor its correction is silently lost.

A second correction: in an earlier draft I treated "system justification among the disadvantaged" as a rival explanation that conflicted with F2. It does not conflict; it is a low-overlap corroboration of the claim that low capacity need not produce delegitimation. The apparent conflict came from reading a world mechanism as if it were a figure line. Re-census: different domains.

## 7. This is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And the framework's own rule: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. ... You may all agree and be wrong."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of any state, and it cannot settle the question it reports on. It offers a shape and a refuter, and it stops at F8's gap. Mode `specified` on the mechanisms, `general` on the structure, nothing `grounded`.
