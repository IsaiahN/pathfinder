## 0. The question under audit

> How do weak states maintain legitimacy, and how do capable states lose it?
>
> Restated as an effect: why does the gap between a state's claim to rightful rule and the governed's actual compliance stay small in some low-capacity slots and grow in some high-capacity slots? The gap is indexed per citizen or group slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22).
>
> Option A: legitimacy is a stock that capacity accumulates and protects. Weak states lack it, capable states have it. The remedy is more capacity, measured by outputs.
>
> Option B: legitimacy is a relation of contact with a ground that does not update on what the state thinks. Capacity is a contact-changing lever with residual-relative valence. Weak states keep legitimacy by keeping the channel reachable; capable states lose it when the seat acquires a stake in its own reading and the metrics pool the signal.

An answer would look like: (i) a separation of the two quantities with their anchors named, (ii) one loop per domain giving the maintenance and loss mechanisms, (iii) a refuter that would fire if the separation were wrong. That shape is bound as an ordinary term here, so the search can refuse it (formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-63).

## 1. Residual, stated in effect terms

**R** is the gap between what the state's library predicts (compliance, deference, voluntary cooperation) and the observation at the ground (what citizens actually do and report per slot). Its three descriptors:

- **Arity.** Two conditions, not one quantity. Capacity and legitimacy have different anchors (Fig 2 :29-30; Fig 9 :55). The question is a **union compound** whose overlap has its own loop, not a single hard question with a smooth trade-off curve (Fig 9 :54-57).
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric. Introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). A state can add a service, a voice, an instrument; it cannot subtract the habitat or remove a citizen's contact with the ground. The comparison between capacity and legitimacy is also not a symmetric join: capacity is an action quantity, legitimacy is a ground-contact quantity, and they are held apart (Fig 12 :125-126).
- **Scale.** Three scales: the single citizen or group slot, the population aggregate, and the time channel across regimes. The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-32); the round trip from a national legitimacy score back to a citizen slot does not preserve what was averaged away (Fig 1 :22).

A global legitimacy reading near zero residual with one live group slot is a legal state, not an inert one (Fig 1 :13, :20-21). This is the single most important measurement consequence of the census: a national trust index cannot close R.

## 4. Findings

Each finding states its mode, its verbatim figure lines, its triangulation status and its refuter. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

### F1 : Capacity and legitimacy have different anchors; they are a union, not a stock and its measure

**Mode: `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for the state application.**

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

**Reading.** Capacity is an action quantity: what the state can do, tax, enforce, deliver. Legitimacy is a ground-contact quantity: whether the governed treat the state as the rightful anchor. They do not share an anchor and they do not add. A "legitimacy stock" that capacity fills is a category error in the figure's own terms, because it treats a contact relation as if it were a second budget line. The question is a union of two conditions with an overlap that has its own loop.

**Triangulation status: triangulated.** Fig 2 (anchor rule) and Fig 9 (union/partition rule) are low-overlap: one governs what settles, the other governs how a question splits. Fig 12 adds a third, distinct price argument.

**Refuter:** a measurement in which legitimacy, defined as voluntary per-slot compliance with a non-updating ground, moves monotonically with capacity while contact is held fixed. That would collapse the two anchors and falsify the union reading.

### F2 : How weak states maintain legitimacy: contact, introduction and habitat

**Mode: `specified`.**

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :32: *"→ introduce, never subtract"*

**Reading.** A weak state is low in own-capability but can still be high in capability-as-contact, because capability lives in the state-and-habitat pair. What it must protect is the channel: the street-level percepts, the local votes, the audits that still return something from the ground. Its available lever is introduction, adding a service, a voice, an instrument, not subtracting an actor from the habitat. This is *permanent work*, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*). A weak state that tries to manufacture legitimacy by internal signalling alone has improved the agent in isolation, which changes nothing (Fig 11 :40).

The world corollary (secondary, not a figure line): system justification research finds that people defend and rationalise the prevailing system even when it disadvantages them, which is one reason low capacity does not mechanically produce delegitimation. See `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD.

**Triangulation status: triangulated.** Fig 11 (capability/contact and asymmetry) and Fig 10 (channel decay) are different figures with different offices. The world source is low-overlap corroboration, not a third figure.

**Refuter:** a controlled comparison in which a state raises its own internal capacity metrics with no change in any contact measure, and per-slot voluntary compliance rises and persists across a documented habitat substitution. That would refute the contact claim. A second refuter: exclusion of a habitat actor that increases per-slot legitimacy monotonically with no new contact, which would refute the asymmetry claim.

### F3 : How capable states lose it (mechanism 1): standing becomes a stake in the reading

**Mode: `specified`.**

Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*

Fig 10 :58: *"A seat with no stake at all does not exist."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

**Reading.** Capability accumulates standing. Standing is a stake that survives the outcome it earned, so the capable state's officers come to benefit from the value the ground returns today. The state then starts updating on its own reputation: its metrics are tuned to what confirms its standing, its critics are absorbed, and the anchor becomes another frame. The drift is coherent, which is exactly why nobody notices, including the state's own monitors. By Fig 10 :93 the keeper of a ground with a reputation to protect is disqualified as keeper. Legitimacy is not taken by an enemy; it is spent by the seat that holds it. The honest move is declaration (Fig 10 :60), not denial.

**Triangulation status: triangulated.** Fig 10 (seat, stake, standing) and Fig 2 (anchor collapse, coherent drift) are low-overlap: one is a rule about offices, the other a rule about what settles a question.

**Refuter:** a capable state whose officers have no stake in the reading and whose anchor demonstrably does not update, yet whose per-slot legitimacy falls at the same rate as comparable states with high standing. That would remove the stake mechanism.

### F4 : How capable states lose it (mechanism 2): aggregation throws away the live signal

**Mode: `specified`.**

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 10 :25: *"stale percepts, dropped transients,"* Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"* Fig 10 :27: *"the output is not a verdict, it is"* Fig 10 :28: *"you have lost touch"*

**Reading.** Capacity buys statistics. Statistics aggregate. The moment a state's legitimacy reading becomes a national average, the live group slot that is about to defect disappears into the mean, exactly as a global residual near zero can hide one live slot (Fig 1 :13). The channel then decays in the specific ways Fig 10 names: stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings. The state still receives an output, but the output is not a verdict; it is the signal that the state has lost touch. This is why capable states can be the last to know. The world corollary is Scott's legibility argument: standardised, centralised knowledge blinds the state to local conditions (secondary, see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Triangulation status: triangulated.** Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 10 (channel decay) are different figures with different objects.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the population average predicts per-slot defection better than per-slot measurement, or a state whose metrics are not pooled and saturated yet loses contact at the same rate. Either would falsify the aggregation mechanism.

### F5 : Legitimacy is a convention, so agreement is the product; a state that treats it as a verdict has stopped having it

**Mode: `specified`, with `general` convention rule.**

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*

Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

**Reading.** This is the discriminating line of the whole audit. If legitimacy were a verdict about the world, agreement inside the state would be worthless because it shares one evidence pool. But legitimacy is constitutively a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption. So a state's citizens agreeing that the state is rightful is not a biased measurement; it *is* the thing being measured. The failure comes when the convention is used to decide an outcome, when legitimacy is cashed in as a credential that settles a disputed policy, a budget or a refusal to answer. At that moment, by Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47, it has stopped being a convention. It has become a verdict the state is handing itself, and a room's agreement is not a verdict. The capable state's characteristic error is therefore not lying; it is treating its own legitimacy as a settled object rather than a relation it must keep enacting.

**Triangulation status: triangulated.** Fig 2 (valence of correlation) and Fig 7 (agreement is variation) are low-overlap.

**Refuter:** a state whose legitimacy-as-shared-meaning routinely decides contested outcomes and whose per-slot legitimacy nonetheless persists, measured against a ground that does not update. That would show a convention can decide outcomes and remain a convention.

### F6 : The anchor for "is this state legitimate?" is the governed, not the state's own metrics

**Mode: `general` for anchor selection, `specified` for the state case.**

Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :46: *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."*

Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

**Reading.** Legitimacy is constitutively about the governed, so the governed are the anchor for this question. The state's own satisfaction survey is an instrument for a different question and can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :37-38). The governed and the state must each be corrected separately by a ground neither can move. This is also why the habitat is enumerated, never composed: which groups and conditions are in contact with R is read off the world, and what cannot yet be perceived is the residual (Fig 11 :48). A state that picks its own friendly respondents is authoring the room rather than reading the habitat.

**Triangulation status: triangulated for "read it off the world" (Fig 2 + Fig 11); single-source for the specific anchor selection (Fig 2 :36 only).** Marked as such rather than inflated.

**Refuter:** a case in which the state's own metric, shown to be movable by state effort, is nonetheless the correct anchor for legitimacy by a measurement that does not reduce to circular self-report.

### F7 : What is refused: frame-internal prestige cannot score the state

**Mode: `general`.**

Fig 1 :24: *"Everything else either produces a residual or consumes the library. These do not count:"* Fig 1 :25: *"coverage, and compression achieved"* Fig 1 :26: *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

**Reading.** International reputation, awards, rankings and the state's own "credibility accrued" are frame-internal. They can fund an action, that is, give a reform a budget to be tried, but they cannot settle whether the reform worked. A state that cites its standing as evidence of legitimacy is scoring itself with a quantity it also produces. The same refusal applies to this package: this simulated derivation is frame-internal and cannot corroborate itself.

**Triangulation status: triangulated** (Fig 1 + Fig 2). **Refuter:** a demonstration that a frame-internal quantity settles a legitimacy question without any ground reading, which would break Fig 1 :27.

**Mode: `general`, verdict `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind.**

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**Reading.** After a genuine re-census, no figure line names which service, which decentralisation, which anti-corruption body or which electoral timetable restores legitimacy in a given country. The figures fix the shape: a non-updating anchor, a reachable per-slot channel, introduction rather than exclusion, a seat able to refuse, and a convention not cashed out as a credential. They do not name the occupant of those slots. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." This is not underivable in principle; it is unreachable at this budget because the missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument for the local case, not a new primitive.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Per-slot voluntary compliance rises monotonically with own-capacity while the contact measure is held fixed, over a documented habitat substitution (breaks F1 and F2).
2. A state's own legitimacy metric moves when citizens push on it and still settles the question (breaks the anchor rule, Fig 2 :30).
3. A population average predicts per-slot defection better than per-slot measurement, or a state with unpooled, unsaturated metrics loses contact at the same rate (breaks F4).
4. A capable state with no reputation stake and no standing-derived interest in the reading loses legitimacy at the same rate as one with high standing (breaks F3).
5. A state whose legitimacy convention routinely decides contested outcomes retains per-slot legitimacy against a non-updating ground (breaks F5).
6. A figure re-census yields a line that directly governs a specific institutional design in this field, closing F8 as a figure result rather than a gap.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is rerun. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`, and the whole package stays `candidate`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F5: two anchors, and a convention that must not be cashed. These are the strongest figure-derived points and they are checkable by reading four short passages.
2. Keep the two loss mechanisms separate (F3 and F4). Do not merge them into "elite capture", which is a name, not a shape.
3. State the mode on every sentence (Seat Map :281: *"An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."*).
4. Put the derivation before any country example, with dates, so the provenance of every binding is checkable (Fig 8 :52).
5. Declare the stake: this package is produced inside the framework it evaluates, so its agreement with the figures is internal consistency, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40; Fig 10 :60).
6. Send the generator, the shape claim and the refuter, not a dossier of agreeable country stories. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

## 9. Closing statement

The figures decide a shape, not a country verdict. A weak state keeps legitimacy while its per-slot channel to a non-updating ground stays reachable and its lever remains introduction. A capable state loses legitimacy when standing gives its seat a stake in its own reading and the instruments pool the live signal away. Both are `specified` mechanisms with written refuters. No figure line names the reform that would restore legitimacy in a particular place, so that question is reported as `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind. Everything here is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
