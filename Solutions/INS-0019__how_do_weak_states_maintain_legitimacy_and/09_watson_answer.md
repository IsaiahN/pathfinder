## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which shape is figure-licensed for the pair of observations: weak states sometimes retain legitimacy, and capable states sometimes lose it. Option A treats legitimacy as a stock that capacity accumulates and protects, so the remedy is more capacity read off outputs. Option B treats legitimacy as a relation of contact with a ground that does not update on what the state thinks, so capacity is a contact-changing lever whose valence is residual-relative. What each leads to: A gives a monotone curve and recommends output metrics; B gives a union of two conditions with different anchors, per-slot measurement, two separate loss loops, and a declared gap where a specific reform would be. The figures decide B and refuse A as written.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot gap between a state's claim to rightful rule (prediction) and what the governed actually do and report (observation). Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55, :57), not one hard question. Symmetry: introduction available, exclusion not (Fig 11 :61); capacity (actions) and legitimacy (contact) are different currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125-126). Scale: citizen or group slot, population aggregate, and the time channel across regimes; the scale transform is lossy (Fig 4 :27-32).
- **Domain 1, state as frame.** Actors: agencies, officials, routines, budgets, self-description. Currency: actions, extraction, enforcement, information. Anchor: the ground of what the governed actually do. Question type: partition inside closure, then ground contact.
- **Domain 2, governed as frames.** Actors: citizens, groups, local and informal authorities. Currency: belief, compliance, voluntary cooperation. Anchor: their lived conditions and whatever settles for them. Question type: triangulation with D1 against one ground.
- **Domain 3, channel and instruments.** Actors: censuses, surveys, audits, street-level percepts, national averages. Currency: contact, or its loss. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay while the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Question type: instrument question (Fig 3 :27).
- **Domain 4, checking seat.** Actors: researchers, reviewers, public record. Currency: agreement versus ground contact. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run every domain draws on one evidence pool, so independence here is zero.
- **X1, state prestige as anchor.** No nameable anchor; it moves when frames push on it (Fig 2 :30). Not a domain yet, refused.
- **X2, national legitimacy score.** A lossy transform inside D3, absorbed rather than admitted as a separate ground.

Order by dependency: perceive the per-slot residual, acquire vocabulary for what the state affords, state the objective, plan, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20); keep the anchor reachable before spending on capacity (Fig 10 :29); describe the effect before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Strain test applied: capacity as action currency versus legitimacy as contact currency is an analogue from Fig 12, named as strain S1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §8, not smoothed into an identification.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-licensed answer is Option B, with its two mechanisms kept separate. Legitimacy and capacity are different quantities with different anchors, so the question is a union, not a stock-and-output curve. The residual is per slot. A weak state maintains legitimacy by keeping the channel to a non-updating governed ground reachable and by introduction rather than exclusion; a capable state loses it through two independent loops, standing becoming a stake in the reading and pooled metrics erasing the live signal, and additionally by cashing a legitimacy convention as a credential that decides an outcome. Weak and capable are not opposite kinds but isomers over a shared atom set, so the question's grammar misleads. No figure line names a specific reform, reported plainly as `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, records five refuters before the conclusion and keeps the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The avoidable cost is spending outside readers' independence by sending a country dossier instead of a refutable shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The substantive cost is a wrong diagnosis of why capable states lose legitimacy, which would misdirect reform toward more capacity where the channel was the broken link (Fig 3 :19).
- **undoable:** yes. The refused stock reading is removable without touching the figure lines; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No primitive is withdrawn and no country claim needs retracting, because none was made. A sent packet can be superseded by the corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot longitudinal instrument that discriminates sources of legitimacy would name what F8 leaves open; a monotone capacity effect at fixed contact would break the two-anchor claim; an updating anchor, an average that beats per-slot measurement, equal loss rates with and without a stake, or a convention that decides outcomes and remains legitimate would each fire a written refuter. A figure re-census that closes F8 or clears analogue strain S1 would also change the verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
