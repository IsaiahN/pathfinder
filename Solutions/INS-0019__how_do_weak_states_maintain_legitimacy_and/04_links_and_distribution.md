## 2. Wider literature that bears on this question

The substantive world sources split across low-overlap fields, so agreement between them is worth more than agreement among papers in one pool.

- **Political philosophy / normative legitimacy (home field, high overlap with itself).** SEP's entry distinguishes descriptive from normative legitimacy, records Weber's belief account and Beetham's second-order condition, and notes Rawls's claim that legitimacy makes weaker demands than justice. Prediction: legitimacy cannot be read off a state's own output metrics; a second-order justification is required. Corroborates F5 and F6.
- **Comparative politics / public administration (home field).** State capacity is distinct from the tactics used to gain compliance, and is multidimensional (territorial, economic, infrastructural, symbolic). Prediction: capacity and compliance are not one variable, so the two anchors in F1 are not an artefact of vocabulary. Corroborates F1.
- **Social psychology (low overlap).** System justification theory predicts that people defend the system even when it disadvantages them, especially under threat. Prediction: low capacity need not produce delegitimation, which corroborates F2; also, when a capable state suffers a visible failure, citizens may restore legitimacy by victim blaming rather than by changing the state, which is a world-level analogue of F3's coherent drift.
- **Anthropology / political ecology (low overlap).** Scott's legibility thesis predicts that a capable state's standardised knowledge is precisely what blinds it to local conditions. Prediction: capable states lose contact through the instrument they trust most. Corroborates F4 strongly. Note Scott's own caveat that the state is sometimes necessary; the claim is about centralised knowledge, not about state abolition.
- **Sociology / critical theory (low overlap).** Habermas's legitimation crisis is a loss of confidence that coexists with retained legal authority: an input crisis, not an output collapse. Prediction: a capable state can keep every formal competence and still lose legitimacy. Corroborates F3 and the whole A-versus-B split.
- **Development survey research (measurement ground).** Afrobarometer and V-Dem publish disaggregated public data. Prediction: national aggregates can hide group-slot residuals; these projects are the natural place to look for the instrument F8 owes. Corroborates the per-slot requirement and is not itself a verdict on this derivation.

All political-science sources (SEP, the two overviews, the venue pages) share a broad evidence pool and count as **one witness** for corroboration purposes. The genuinely low-overlap witnesses are social psychology, political ecology and critical theory. The framework essays all share the framework author and carry little independent weight.

## 3. Distribution rules: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** : *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator (the shape claim and its refuter) before any theory or country dossier.
- **Fig 10 :42** : *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** : *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source links.
- **Fig 2 :40** : *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Never rank or select recipients by receptiveness. Never pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*).
- **Fig 2 :42** : *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a wrong diagnosis of why capable states lose legitimacy, so independence is worth buying through public, non-partisan venues.
- **Fig 13 :134** : *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* so say plainly that no specific reform follows from the figures, and ask for an instrument instead of inventing a name.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the source links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## 4. The two-stage offer, restated

Stage 1 checks the output against the ground. Stage 2 is opt-in reading of the theory. Stage 3 offers the formula as a reusable method with the recipient's own refuter written first. Stage 1 must precede stage 2 (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*), stage 2 is opt-in by construction (a recipient who has absorbed the theory stops being an outside frame), stage 3 sends a generator and is permitted (Fig 4 :22), and stage 3 still needs a ground per problem because a loop cannot check its own question direction.

## 5. Submission constraint on AI-assisted work

This package is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation. IPSA's site lists a "Journal Guidelines on Generative AI" resource and APSA publishes research-ethics and data-policy material; both should be read before any submission. Do not present this work as a ground reading or as the product of field research. Its honest status is a checkable shape plus a pre-registered refuter, offered to a public venue for refutation. If a venue forbids AI-assisted submissions, do not submit; take it to an opt-in commentary route instead and label it accordingly.
