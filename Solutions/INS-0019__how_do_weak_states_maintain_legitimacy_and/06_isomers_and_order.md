# Union, order and isomers: are weak and capable states opposites?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary phrasing "weak states maintain legitimacy, capable states lose it" invites an opposition: two ends of one capacity axis, with legitimacy as the dependent variable. The residual is whether that opposition is a real partition or a union of overlapping conditions with different orders and different anchors. Two questions are owed: (1) can the four cases (weak/high-legitimacy, weak/low-legitimacy, capable/high-legitimacy, capable/low-legitimacy) be written from one held atom set, and (2) if so, are they isomers under Fig 12's definition or merely members of a functional group?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held: states and structures from the graph of K (states: capacity, contact, standing; structures: channel, stake, convention, metric). Bonds held: the seven plus negation, including `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, `−` subtraction and `¬` refusal. Anchors: the governed ground for legitimacy, the market/outcome for capacity. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is the question a partition into two opposite kinds? NO.

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- ⇒ Capacity is an actor whose valence flips with the residual. Spent on contact it maintains legitimacy; spent on standing it corrodes legitimacy. There is no fixed "weak = legitimate" or "capable = illegitimate" mapping.
- **Reading:** the four cases form a union of two conditions (contact maintained, reach renewed), not a partition by capacity. This is the structural reason the question's own grammar misleads.
- **Falsifier:** a dataset in which capacity alone, with contact and stake held fixed, perfectly separates the four cases. Then the opposition is real and the union reading fails.

### Q2 : What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Order for this question:
  1. **Perceive** the per-slot residual (which group, which service, which failure).
  2. **Vocabulary** for what the state affords (which lever is introduction, which is subtraction).
  3. **Objective** stated in the state's own terms (what would count as legitimacy here, per slot).
  4. **Plan** under incomplete information (channel-first, not budget-first).
  5. **Learn and carry** a method that can be reapplied (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).
- A state that buys capacity at step 4 while step 1 has no instrument is spending above the break. This is the figure-grounded reason that "build capacity, legitimacy follows" is the wrong order.

### Q3 : Are the four cases isomers over a shared atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Write the four cases over the shared atom set `{Capacity, Contact, Standing, Channel, Stake, Convention, Metric}`:

| case | arrangement (bond sketch) | figure reading |
|---|---|---|
| weak, high legitimacy | low Capacity `+` live Channel `→` Contact `+` Convention not cashed | a molecule whose contact substructure dominates; intro available, subtraction refused (Fig 11 :61) |
| weak, low legitimacy | low Capacity `+` broken Channel `→` no Contact `+` Convention cashed as credential | same atoms, channel bond broken, convention bond changed substance |
| capable, high legitimacy | high Capacity `→` Channel `→` Contact, Standing declared not held as stake | capacity spent on the channel; standing present as an atom but not bonded to the reading |
| capable, low legitimacy | high Capacity `→` Metric `+` Stake `¬` Contact, pooled reading | standing bonded to the reading; negation of contact; same atoms as the capable/high case |

- By Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers**: same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The difference is invisible in a one-line "capacity score" and decisive in behaviour.
- The weaker claim, if the atom set is not shared in a given comparison, is a **functional group**: all four contain a `state → structure → state` legitimacy molecule, differing in which structure occupies the slot (Fig 12 :124).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any case requires an atom the others do not (for example, legitimacy with no channel at all, or capacity with no contact relation), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. A second falsifier: if swapping two bonds leaves behaviour unchanged, the bond carries nothing and Fig 12's pricing claim does not apply here.

### Q4 : Order of the two loss mechanisms: are they one compound or two?

- Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* (stake mechanism)
- Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (metric mechanism)
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- ⇒ They are **two loops** in a union. Either can fire without the other. A state can lose contact through pooled metrics while its officers remain disinterested, and can acquire a stake while its per-slot instruments stay sharp. A single "delegitimation score" would add quantities with different anchors, which Fig 12 :125 (*"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add"*) refuses at the structural level.
- **Falsifier:** a measured case in which the stake mechanism never fires without the metric mechanism and vice versa, across many cases, would collapse the union into one compound. Honest reading: the figure gives no forward test of this; it is a structural claim, `specified`.

## CONCLUSION

Weak and capable are not opposite kinds. They are two values of one atom whose valence flips with the residual (Fig 11 :49). The four cases are isomers over a shared atom set when the set is shared, and members of one functional group when it is not. The dependency order is perception of the per-slot residual first, capacity spending last; buying capacity above a broken channel is effort that arrives at a step that cannot use it. The two loss mechanisms are a union of separate loops and must be reported separately.

This rescues what is defensible in the question's own phrasing without the part that fails: "how do weak states maintain legitimacy" is a question about a contact-preserving arrangement; "how do capable states lose it" is a question about two different bond changes. Neither needs a new primitive, an import that moves the wall, or a national legitimacy score.
