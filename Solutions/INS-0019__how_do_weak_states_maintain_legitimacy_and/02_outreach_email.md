**Subject:** One checkable claim about state legitimacy, before any theory

> Hello [name],
>
> I am writing about one specific output, not asking you to read a framework. I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** capacity and legitimacy are not one quantity with a stock that fills up. They have different anchors. Capacity is what a state can do; legitimacy is whether the governed treat the state as a rightful anchor that does not move when they push on it. Because the anchors differ, the question is a union of two conditions, not one curve. The observable is indexed per citizen or group slot, never averaged into a national trust score, because averaging is how a live signal disappears into a mean.
>
> From that separation two mechanisms follow, and they are separate loops:
>
> 1. A low-capacity state can keep legitimacy by keeping the channel to the ground reachable and by adding services, voices or instruments. It cannot manufacture legitimacy by internal signalling, because an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Its power is asymmetric: it can introduce, it cannot subtract the habitat.
> 2. A high-capacity state can lose legitimacy two ways. Standing becomes a stake in the reading, so the state starts updating on its own reputation and drifts coherently with its own monitors. And its statistics pool the live per-slot signal away, so it receives an output that is not a verdict but a sign that it has lost touch. A legitimacy convention that gets cashed in as a credential to settle an outcome has stopped being a convention.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular country is an instance, that a specific reform restores legitimacy, that legitimacy is a measurable national score, or that this is grounded. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that prestige or international reputation makes a state legitimate; those are frame-internal and cannot settle the question.
>
> **What would refute it (written before I ran this):**
> 1. per-slot voluntary compliance rises with own-capacity while the contact measure is held fixed;
> 2. the state's own legitimacy metric moves when citizens push on it and still settles the question;
> 3. a population average predicts per-slot defection better than per-slot measurement;
> 4. a high-capacity state with no standing-derived stake loses legitimacy at the same rate as one with high standing;
> 5. a legitimacy convention that routinely decides contested outcomes still retains per-slot legitimacy against a non-updating ground.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am asking for at stage 1:** only your reading of the shape and the refuters above, recorded before you see where they came from. What would you count as a failure here? Which of the five would you expect to fire in the cases you know?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README (canonical usage rule): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
