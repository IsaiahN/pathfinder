# Expert verification packet : the Tether on state legitimacy

**What this is:** a claim in a form a political scientist, public-administration scholar or survey researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanisms, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No country measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Legitimacy and capacity are different quantities with different anchors, coupled by a channel to a ground that does not update on what the state thinks. The residual must be indexed per citizen or group slot, never averaged into a national trust score. A weak state keeps legitimacy while that channel stays reachable and its lever is introduction rather than exclusion; a capable state loses it through two separate loops, standing becoming a stake in the reading, and instruments pooling the live signal away. Legitimacy is a convention, so a state that cashes it as a credential to settle an outcome has stopped having it. Weak and capable are not opposite kinds but isomers over a shared atom set. No specific reform follows; that is recorded as a gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** capacity and legitimacy have different anchors, so the question is a union of two conditions, and the residual is per-slot, not a national aggregate.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :29 : *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
  - Fig 9 :55 : *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
  - Fig 12 :125 : *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
  - Fig 1 :22 : *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **Refuter:** per-slot voluntary compliance rises monotonically with own-capacity while the contact measure is held fixed, across a documented habitat substitution. That would collapse the two anchors. A second refuter: a population average predicts per-slot defection better than per-slot measurement.

### C2 : state-facing; checkable against cases a political scientist knows

- **Statement:** the weak-state maintenance mechanism is contact plus introduction, and the capable-state loss mechanisms are (a) standing as a stake in the reading and (b) pooled metrics erasing the per-slot signal. These are separate loops.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :40 : *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
  - Fig 11 :61 : *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
  - Fig 10 :61 : *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*
  - Fig 2 :34 : *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
  - Fig 1 :22 : *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
  - Fig 10 :26 : *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- **What to check:** in cases you know, does capacity spent on the channel preserve per-slot compliance while capacity spent on institutional standing does not? Do the two loss mechanisms fire independently, or always together? Is the per-slot signal actually destroyed by aggregation, or does the average carry it?
- **Refuter:** a capable state with no reputation stake and no standing-derived interest in the reading loses legitimacy at the same rate as one with high standing (breaks mechanism a); or a state whose metrics are unpooled and unsaturated nonetheless loses contact at the same rate (breaks mechanism b); or a weak state that raises internal capacity with no contact change and per-slot legitimacy rises and persists (breaks the maintenance claim).

### C3 : framework-internal derivation, with a world-facing consequence

- **Statement:** legitimacy is a convention, so agreement about shared meaning is the product; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one, and a room's agreement is not a verdict. The specific reform that would restore legitimacy in a given country is therefore a declared gap, not a figure result.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :41 : *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*
  - Fig 2 :43 : *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
  - Fig 7 :35 : *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
  - Fig 9 :32 : *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
  - Fig 13 :134 : *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **Refuter:** a state whose legitimacy convention routinely decides contested outcomes and whose per-slot legitimacy nonetheless persists against a ground that does not update; or a figure re-census that yields a line directly governing a specific institutional design in this field.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a national legitimacy score, a stock, or a reservoir that capacity fills.
- Not that all weak states are legitimate or all capable states illegitimate. Valence is residual-relative (Fig 11 :49); counterexamples are expected.
- Not that capacity is harmful. Capacity spent on the channel is the legitimate use.
- Not that coercion, interests and habit are irrelevant. They are real; only frame-internal self-scoring is refused here (Fig 1 :27).
- Not a country verdict, a reform recommendation or a league table.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not a claim that this framework is the only one producing the shape. That is a uniqueness claim over a census nobody ran (Fig 9 :44).

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot, source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Per-slot longitudinal contact measure** across a change of government. Does the average hide a live slot (Fig 1 :13, :20-21)?
2. **Stake discriminator.** Does an officer's legitimacy reading move when their own standing is at issue, independently of what the ground returns (Fig 10 :93)?
3. **Metric resolution test.** Does the instrument retain per-slot resolution after aggregation (Fig 1 :22)?
4. **Convention-outcome test.** Can the state cash its legitimacy as a credential and still show per-slot legitimacy against a non-updating ground (Fig 2 :43)?
5. The five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5, written before the run.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :34, :41, :43, Fig 1 :22, Fig 11 :40 and :61, Fig 10 :61, Fig 7 :35. Nine short passages.
2. Check C2 against one case you know well: identify the channel, the stake and the metric, and say which of the two loss loops fired.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a live group slot from a national average, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
