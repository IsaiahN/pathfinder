# Access and settlement: order, structure and analogues

## RESIDUAL

The question gives two effects, "what people could observe" and "what they considered real." Are these two names for one change, an ordered pair, or two independent quantities? And are the historical cases (telescope, spectroscope) different *kinds* of thing under the figure's composition rules, or the same thing with different labels? Two questions are owed: (1) what is the order of access and settlement, and (2) are the cases isomers.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are access and settlement one quantity or an ordered pair?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- A reading must exist before it can be settled. Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part." The part here is the reading.
- But the converse does not hold: a reading can arrive and be refused, as the Carnegie Galileo page reports for the moons of Jupiter among contemporary observers. Settlement is a separate threshold, not the automatic tail of access.
- Fig 6 :28: "Which makes reachability two questions, not one. The instruments are opposed." The figure's two instruments here are inward (extend access) and outward (find a frame that holds the term). They map to our two ledgers: inward changes observation, outward changes what can be admitted.
- ⇒ The pair is **ordered** (settlement consumes a reading) but **not reducible** (a new reading need not settle). Write it as `reading → settlement` with the arrow meaning sequence, not production: Fig 12 :72 "A then B; B needs A to have happened." B does not follow automatically.
- **Falsifier:** if a case is found where a term is settled with no prior reading at all, or where every new reading necessarily settles within one generation, the ordered-pair claim fails and the two halves must be re-censused as one quantity. Single-source for sequence (Fig 3 and Fig 12, low overlap); unmeasured at the ground.

### Q2: Are the telescope and spectroscope isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Read at the level of the six atoms, both cases are the same first-order molecule: a state, a structure, a state. The telescope composes space (a distant point) with a structure (lenses and their ordering) into a new spatial reading. The spectroscope composes energy (light) with a structure (a prism's spectrum) into an ordering that identifies a source. Different atoms, different structures in the slot, but the same **functional group**: `state → structure → state`.
- They are **not** isomers under Fig 12's definition as actually written, because they do not share the same atom set. Calling them isomers would be a strain. Per the strain test, the line then governs another quantity, so re-census: the isomer claim is available only at a **higher level**, where both instruments have settled and become atoms for whatever composes over them (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"). At that level both are "an instrument that extends a frame's reach," same atoms, different bond (one extends spatial access, one discriminates sources). That is a real isomer claim.
- **Falsifier:** if one instrument cannot be written as `state → structure → state` at first order, or if at the settled level they do not share the same atoms (one adds a primitive the other does not), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3: What is the order of the union across a historical community?

- Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." The residual (the failing reading) selects the instrument; we do not pick a famous instrument and then find a residual for it. This is the ordering the audit's method section 1 obeys.
- Once a new term is settled, Fig 12 :104 makes it an atom at the next level. So the historical process is recursive: each generation inherits settled terms as head starts, not as recordings. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 5 :30 "the machinery worked; the answer was already known." A generation that only inherits the recording of a previous generation's observation mints nothing; its machinery fires and the answer is already in the library. This is why a list of instruments is not a mechanism.
- Fig 13 :124: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." Likewise, "objectivity" and "progress" are what a particular instrument-and-settlement composition leaves behind, not additional primitives to be named as the cause.
- **Falsifier:** if a community can inherit a settled term without any method and still mint new readings at the same rate, the recursion claim fails. Unmeasured here.

### Q4: What the order forbids

- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- So "instruments improved observation, and that is progress" uses a word (`progress`) that is a **subtraction** of earlier from later reach. It is not a primitive and not an instrument. Stating it as a cause is the flat-formula error: the ingredients are recorded, the arrangement is not. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

## CONCLUSION

Access and settlement form an **ordered pair** (`reading → settlement`, sequence, not production): settlement consumes a reading but does not follow from one. The telescope and spectroscope share a **functional group** at first order and are **isomers** only at the settled level where both have become atoms of the same kind. The historical union is recursive (Fig 12 :104, Fig 4 :22), with each generation inheriting methods and head starts rather than recordings. Both falsifiers are written before the run. Nothing here is `grounded`.
