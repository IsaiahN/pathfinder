## Three things in the fetched pages that bear directly on the question

1. **The source essay separates reading from source.** The green reading is a projection; the spectrometer discriminates among sources that the eye cannot. This is the concrete mechanism behind "what people considered real": the better instrument does not argue, it discriminates. It matches Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" and Fig 2 :38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Cite the essay for the mechanism, not for the figure verdict.

2. **The spectroscope case is a settlement case, not just an observation case.** SHI reports that cesium and rubidium were discovered with the instrument and inaugurated a new era in finding elements; Waterloo adds that chemists could detect quantities before weighable amounts were prepared. So the instrument widened access (faint lines became readings) and then a new term entered the settled library (two new elements). That is the two-ledger shape in one historical episode. But SHI and Waterloo share the same case and a similar public audience, so count them as **one witness**, not independent corroboration.

3. **Galileo is an independent-frames case, with a caution.** Carnegie says Galileo did not invent the telescope and that his *interpretation* of what he saw changed the view of the cosmos. That matches Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something" and Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." The caution: the page is an educational timeline, not a scholarly monograph, and it is used here as an illustration of a shape, not as the ground that settles the history.

## The two-stage offer

**(1)** Check the output against the ground. **(2)** Only if curious, read the framing that produced it. **(3)** Optionally reuse the formula on another problem, writing a fresh refuter and stating a mode.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source" and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence, so the stage-1 verdict must be recorded first and never pooled (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted by Fig 4 :22, but each user still needs a ground and a written refuter, because the Formula "Direction" section says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. This output is a simulated derivation and is labelled as such. If a checker shows that access and settlement always move together, or that an instrument can add a world primitive, the email does not go out in its current form.
