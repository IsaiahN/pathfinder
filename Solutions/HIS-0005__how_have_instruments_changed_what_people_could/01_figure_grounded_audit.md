## 0. The residual stated in effect terms

The question as asked: "How have instruments changed what people could observe and what they considered real?" (source line 8). Stated as an effect, not as a topic:

> An instrument is inserted between a frame and its ground. Two changes follow. (1) Readings arrive that the old frame could not represent, so the set of observables widens. (2) Some of those readings are settled and become terms that later frames can compose over, so the set of things treated as real widens. The world itself is not credited with new primitives.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a **two-condition account** with access and settlement kept in separate ledgers, each with its own anchor, plus a source-discrimination condition that decides what a reading means. The binding is allowed to accrue a residual. If it fails, the shape claim fails with it.

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** two conditions (access, settlement) plus a third that governs their interface (source discrimination). A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either: Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". This is a **union compound**, not one big question and not a partition.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The instrument produces the reading; the reading does not rebuild the instrument. Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Source discrimination is one-way too: a reading does not determine its source without a better instrument.
- **Scale:** intra-frame (one observer's library Γ) and inter-frame (a community across historical time), with recursion at the second scale because a settled result becomes an operand for the next generation (Fig 12 :104).

## 1. Domain census, written before any figure line was chosen

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

**Domain 1, access and the instrument.** Actors: the instrument, its worse predecessor, the reading, the per-slot residual. Currency: representable states, what can be registered at all. Anchor: the ground, reached through a reading that already returns something and fails to resolve (Fig 6 :33 to :34). Nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: extension of reach.

**Domain 2, settlement and what is "real".** Actors: the frame, its library Γ, settled molecules, the community of frames. Currency: settled terms and what can be composed over them. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :45). Nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: settlement, a threshold on evidence. "Real" is treated as a frame's settled reach, not as a metaphysical verdict.

**Domain 3, source discrimination and provenance.** Actors: the instrument, candidate sources, the record of the route. Currency: discriminating readings, and the cost of a reading that cannot separate sources. Anchor: the ground via a better instrument. Nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: which reading licenses which claim about what produced it.

**Partition or union:** union. The three conditions overlap at the reading, but each has a different anchor and a different failure mode. A single lever that fixes access does not fix settlement: a telescope can return a reading that a community refuses to settle, as theGalilean moons episode shows (Carnegie source, `04`). An overlap is a compound with its own loop, so it is kept as its own domain rather than folded into either neighbour.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3):** access first, because perception and vocabulary precede the objective and planning (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."); settlement second, because a term must be settled before it can be composed over (Fig 12 :104); source discrimination third, because it asks what an already-settled reading means. History and community transmission are not a fourth domain: they are this same loop iterated, because a settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it.

**Strain test applied during the census:** reading "what people considered real" as "what people agreed to" would make Fig 2 :35 ("Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.") contradict the historical record of communities changing their minds under instrument pressure. A line that only fits with that strain governs another quantity, so the census was re-run: agreement is a **convention** (Seat Map line 163), settlement is a **verdict** and the two are separate ledgers. No strain remains after the re-census. This retraction is preserved in section 6.

## 3. Findings, one loop per domain

### F1: An instrument changes the frame's access, not the world's atoms (Domain 1, decisive for "observe")

Fig 6 :25 to :26:

> "An import adds no atom to the world."
> "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."

Triangulation: Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." and Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The three lines come from different figures with low overlap: one about the wall of a closure, one about capability as contact, one about a dependency link. They agree on the same quantity, which makes this **triangulated**, not a single-source echo.

The effect is therefore precise. A telescope, a microscope or a spectroscope does not add a new kind to the world; it moves the boundary of what a frame can represent and thereby changes which readings can arrive. The old frame was not wrong about the world in the sense of having fewer atoms; it was limited in reach. This is why "what people could observe" is the right first half of the question and why it must not be merged with the second half.

Refuter (kept for the packet): show an instrument adding a primitive to the world rather than to a frame, that is, a new kind that was not composable from the six and did not come from another frame. If that can be demonstrated, F1 fails.

### F2: Instruments grow out of worse instruments already returning something (Domain 1, historical order)

Fig 6 :32 to :34:

> "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
> "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."
> "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."

Mode: `specified`, ungrounded. This is the figure's own historical claim, not a claim that this agent verified against a telescope history. The world source from Carnegie (`04`) is consistent with it: Galileo "did not invent the telescope, and may not even have been the first to use it to observe the sky," and improved an existing instrument.

**The strain that was found and re-censused.** Fig 6 :38 says "Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point" instruments. Read naively, that contradicts the claim that instruments are never specified from a description. Re-census shows two quantities: the **existence of a sensor** (Fig 6 :32 to :34, access) and the **direction of search** (Fig 6 :38, where to aim an instrument you already have). Theory can supply the second without supplying the first. Neptune was found by pointing an existing instrument at a predicted position. The figures are not in conflict; the naive reading had conflated aim with access. No correction to F2 is owed, and the strain is recorded as resolved by re-census rather than hidden.

Refuter: exhibit a case where a new sensor was built directly from a description with no prior instrument returning any reading at all, not merely a case where theory redirected an existing sensor. That would break Fig 6 :32 and F2 with it.

### F3: "Considered real" is the settled reach, not a vote (Domain 2, decisive for "real")

Fig 12 :104:

> "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"

Fig 2 :45:

> "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Seat Map line 281: "An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict." (This is a local framework file quote, not a figure line.)

The operational reading: a thing becomes "real" for a frame when the ground pays for it and later frames can compose over it. Before settlement it is a candidate. Agreement alone cannot do this work: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Triangulation: Fig 12 (recursion and settlement) and Fig 2 (anchor and non-negotiation) are different figures with different subjects, so the "reality is settled reach" verdict is **triangulated** within the framework. It is not corroborated externally by the history sources: they document cases, they do not decide what "real" means. The philosophy-of-science source (`04`, SEP "Experiment in Physics") supplies a low-overlap analogue: Ackermann is quoted there saying instruments "create an invariant relationship between their operations and the world," so the datum stays the same while its significance changes under a new theory. That is a distinct witness for two quantities (datum and significance), not for the framework's settlement rule.

Refuter: show a term treated as real by a community with no ground contact, and show that the framework must admit it as composable anyway. Or show a term settled by the ground that cannot be composed over. Either breaks the settlement-to-reality mapping and sends us back to census.

### F4: A reading cannot comment on its source; the better instrument discriminates (Domain 3)

Fig 8 :51:

> "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

Fig 2 :38:

> "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

Fig 10 :24 to :26:

> "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
> "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

This is the point where the question's second half becomes concrete. An instrument returns a reading; the reading is a projection. Several sources can produce the same reading (metamerism). The source essay (world source 1, `10_source_ledger.csv`) states it directly: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." And: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." The spectroscope is the historical instance: a flame colour that the eye reads as one thing is resolved by a spectral instrument into lines that identify cesium and rubidium (SHI and Waterloo sources, `04`).

The figure line that governs this is Fig 8 :51 (provenance: the contents alone do not tell you where a claim came from), supported by Fig 2 :38 (an instrument has axes it does not measure) and Fig 10 :24 (the channel decays even though the ground does not). Called **triangulated** for the structure, with the concrete historical claim recorded as single-source per case: SHI and Waterloo share the spectroscope case and are one witness, not two.

Refuter: show a reading that determines its source with no discriminating instrument and no record, in a case where multiple sources are known to produce the same reading. That breaks Fig 8 :51 as applied here.

### F5: New instruments create new independence axes (Domain 3, corroborating)

Fig 2 :31:

> "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."

Fig 2 :35:

> "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

When a new instrument enters a community, it is not another opinion. If it draws on a different physical interaction from the older one, it is a second frame pointed at the same ground, and its agreement or disagreement with the older reading carries information. The spectroscope and the balance are such a pair: the spectroscope detects trace quantities before weighable amounts can be prepared (Waterloo source, `04`). The agreement is evidence only because the evidence pools differ. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Conversely, ten observers reading the same spectroscope trace are one frame with ten names, and Seat Map line 163 ("High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention") applies.

Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 2 independence plus Fig 9 split rule). The historical fact that instruments are treated as independent frames is single-source per case in these sources.

Refuter: show two instruments whose readings are generated by the same hidden source, with no independent channel, yet whose agreement is counted as corroboration. That is the common-cause collapse of Fig 2 panel 2, and it would break the leverage this finding claims.

### F6: Some non-observations are genuine absence of a proto-instrument, not bad instruments (Domain 1, stop condition)

Fig 6 :40:

> "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."

Fig 13 :134:

> "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

When nothing, at any resolution, is returning a failing reading, there is no edge to extend and no proto-instrument to sharpen. That is different from a hard measurement problem where a worse instrument is already returning something. The practical consequence for the historical question: "they could not observe X" has two distinct causes, only one of which is improved instrumentation. Conflating them is the same error as conflating underivable with unreachable-at-budget (Fig 8 :40-43).

Verdict: **single-source** on Fig 6 :40, with Fig 13 :134 governing the reporting form (verdict rather than silence). No world source here decides it.

Refuter: exhibit a case where a sensor was built for a quantity that had never registered anywhere at any resolution, with no predecessor reading at all. F6 and Fig 6 :40 would both need revision.

### F7: What persists across generations is the method and the record, not a past reading (cross-domain)

Fig 4 :22:

> "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

Fig 4 :25:

> "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

Fig 13 :126 to :130 (the lineage is the proof): "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." and "Which makes two different questions answerable, and the second is the one that is usually not."

An instrument's historical effect is carried by methods (how to build, calibrate and read it) and by the record of which residual it was built to close. A table of past readings is a recording: it tells a later frame what happened once, not what to do next. This is why the answer to "how have instruments changed what people could observe" cannot be a list of discoveries. A list is the recording; the generator is the loop that produced them.

Verdict: **single-source** across Fig 4 and Fig 13, with low overlap between the membrane figure and the lineage section, so weak triangulation inside the framework. The history-of-instruments guide (Cambridge HPS, `04`) corroborates the practice from a different field: it treats an instrument as something that must be read with its materiality and context, not as a bare reading.

Refuter: show a case where a bare recording, with no method and no lineage, reliably generated a new observations in a later frame. That would break the membrane rule as applied here.

### F8: Mode check and the two quantities that must never be averaged

Every claim above is `specified` or `general`, none `grounded`. Nobody measured whether a given community changed its ontology because of a given instrument. The figures permit a shape and a route; the history sources report particular cases; the two are kept in separate columns.

Critically, the two halves of the question are two quantities, not two ends of one scale. Widening access (F1, F2) and settling new reality-status terms (F3, F4) can move independently: a new reading can arrive and be refused, and a term can be settled long after the instrument that produced its reading was forgotten. Averaging them into "instruments made science more objective" would be the Fig 1 error: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Verdict: **general** for the two-quantity shape, **specified** for each finding. No `gap` remains after the census: figure lines govern all three domains. The historical detail (which instrument, which community, which decade) is a **gap of the world**, not a figure gap, and this package says so rather than inventing it.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before the WATSON run, per Seat Map line 287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An instrument is shown to add a primitive to the world rather than to a frame's reach (breaks F1 and Fig 6 :25).
2. A new sensor is built from a description with no worse instrument already returning something (breaks F2 and Fig 6 :32).
3. A term is settled as real by agreement alone with no ground contact, and the framework must admit it as composable (breaks F3 and Fig 2 :45).
4. A reading is shown to determine its source with no discriminating instrument and no record in a known metamer (breaks F4 and Fig 8 :51).
5. The two halves of the question are shown to be one quantity by a case where access and settlement always move together across all tested frames (breaks F8 and Fig 1 :19).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Keep access and settlement in separate ledgers. Never report "instruments improved observation and therefore changed reality" as one sentence; the warrant for each half is different.
2. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a list of famous instruments. A list is a recording (Fig 4 :25); the generator is the loop.
3. When a reading is offered as evidence, state which other instrument or record would discriminate its source (F4). If none can be named, mark the claim `unreachable`, not false.
4. Use filters for the venue budget and witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."). The public contact points in `03` are a budget filter, not a panel of believers.
5. State the mode on every substantive sentence. An unmeasured historical claim is a specification of what to check, not a verdict (Seat Map line 281).

### Round trip note

Condensing the figures into the shorthand "instruments extend reach, settlement sets the real, the world does not change" has a nonzero round-trip gap R_T. Three constraints are dropped on the way down: the per-slot indexing of the residual (Fig 1 :19), the requirement that a new instrument have a worse predecessor already returning something (Fig 6 :32), and the provenance condition on a reading (Fig 8 :51). They are enumerated in `11_audit.md`; treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: instruments change a frame's access, settlement changes what a frame can treat as real, and the world does not gain primitives. Per-domain verdicts are kept separate: access triangulated, settlement triangulated, source discrimination triangulated in structure but single-source per historical case, stop condition single-source, method persistence weakly triangulated. Strains after re-census: none hidden, one recorded as resolved (Neptune aim versus sensor access), one recorded as live for the world rather than the figures (which instrument settled which term in which community is a historical ground question this package does not answer). Mode `specified` on domains, `general` on shape, not `grounded`, candidate.
