# The Tether's answer: instruments, observation and the real

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the two-ledger shape, not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No instrument or historical episode was measured by this agent.

## 1. The residual it answers

An instrument is inserted between a frame and its ground. Two changes follow: readings arrive that the old frame could not represent, and some readings get settled into terms that later frames compose over. The question asks how instruments changed both. The two effects are a **union compound**, not one question: Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Per-slot indexing matters, because averaging the two halves loses the only signal that says where to look: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 2. The shape, with verdicts kept separate

**Domain 1, access. Verdict: triangulated.** An instrument changes a frame's access, not the world's atoms. Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :26 "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The three figures differ in subject and overlap, so this is triangulation inside the framework. Historical note, `specified` and single-source per case: instruments are improved from worse instruments already returning something, Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." and Fig 6 :33 "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."

**Domain 2, settlement and what counts as real. Verdict: triangulated.** "Real" is the frame's settled reach, not a vote. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom" and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Agreement cannot do settlement's work: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." A new reading may arrive and be refused; the figures give no rule that it must settle.

**Domain 3, source discrimination. Verdict: triangulated in structure, single-source per historical case.** A reading is a projection and does not name its source. Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" and Fig 2 :38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" and Fig 10 :25-26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" describe how the channel, not the world, fails. A better instrument discriminates sources that a worse one merges. The spectroscope case is the concrete instance from the world sources, counted once even though two pages report it.

**The stop. Verdict: single-source on the figure, with a second form from the essays.** Some non-observations are absence of any proto-instrument, not bad instruments: Fig 6 :40 "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it." Report that as a verdict, not silence: Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The essay form is the moving union horizon: a bigger instrument finding nothing is indistinguishable from a trace it still cannot reach, so the honest report is "nothing found yet."

**Cross-domain consequence.** Access and settlement are an ordered pair (`reading → settlement`, Fig 12 :72 "A then B; B needs A to have happened"), not one scale. Across generations, what carries is the method and the record, not the past reading: Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." and Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that instruments create reality, or add primitives to the world (Fig 6 :25).
- Not that a community's agreement settles a term. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Seat Map line 163 adds that high correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention.
- Not that a reading determines its source without a discriminating instrument (F4).
- Not that any named instrument is figure-derived. Telescope and spectroscope are world-source illustrations, not selections by the framework. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room"; every room closes some of the gap and no finite number arrives.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles any domain verdict.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would refute it

The ground settles it, not more agreement: Fig 9 :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides". Concretely:

1. **Refuter 1:** demonstrate an instrument adding a primitive to the world rather than to a frame's reach (breaks F1).
2. **Refuter 2:** exhibit a new sensor built from a description with no worse instrument already returning something (breaks F2 and Fig 6 :32).
3. **Refuter 3:** show a term settled as real by agreement alone, with no ground contact, which the framework must still admit as composable (breaks F3).
4. **Refuter 4:** show a reading determining its source with no discriminator and no record in a known metamer (breaks F4).
5. **Refuter 5:** show that access and settlement always move together across all tested frames, collapsing the two quantities (breaks F8 and Fig 1 :19).

These were written in `01` section 4 before the WATSON run. A report of any one of them, with provenance, reverses the affected verdict. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached on the world side, not unreachable.** The figures fix the shape and the per-domain verdicts. They do not say which instrument settled which term in which community in which decade. That is a `gap of the world`: it requires a historian reading instruments, records and ground, not another derivation. This package stops at the limit of what these instruments (text, figures, public pages) can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *Indiscernibility is not identity*: a shape that no instrument distinguishes from the answer is still a shape. The lineage is the proof (Fig 13 :126-130).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> An instrument does not add a new kind to the world; it extends what a frame can represent. So the first half of the question, what people could observe, is a change of reach, and it happens by improving a worse instrument that was already returning a failing reading. The second half, what people considered real, is a different ledger: a thing becomes real for a frame when the ground settles a reading and later frames can build on it, and a community's agreement cannot do that work. Where several sources produce the same reading, the reading cannot name its source, so a better instrument rather than a better argument is what changes the settled list. The two ledgers are ordered but not reducible; a new reading can arrive and be refused. Across generations what carries is the method and the record, not the list of past discoveries. This is a shape with written refuters, not a history and not a measurement, and it stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing.
