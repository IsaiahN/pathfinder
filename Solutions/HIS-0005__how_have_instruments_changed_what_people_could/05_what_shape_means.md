## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

*The Library of Babel*: "This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** here is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the particular instrument, community or century. It constrains; it does not fill. "An instrument widened access and a settlement changed the real" is a shape. "The spectroscope did it in 1860" is a name.

The same essay ties the limit to instruments directly: "If you don't have the answer to a question, it's because you are lacking certain things in the library (instruments, information) that would help, or perhaps you are asking the wrong question altogether." That is Fig 6 in narrative form: the missing thing is in the library or it is not, and the two cases have different instructions.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* proximity bootstrap: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." Applied here: start with the effect words (reading, residual, source, settlement), then let the figure lines narrow the space, rather than starting with a list of famous instruments and mapping backwards. The latter is the apophenia of Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is why the outreach in `02` asks what a failure would look like instead of asking for agreement.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* on the three parts of contact: "So contact isn't one simple thing. It is a question, an encounter and an interpretation, and it can fail in all the ways the combinations table lists: a measurement with no question is noise, and a reading with no encounter is projection." An instrument supplies the encounter; a frame supplies the question and the interpretation. The question's two halves are therefore not detachable: access without interpretation is noise, interpretation without an encounter is projection.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." And the paradox: "the mystery of The Library of Babel is that you enter as a researcher looking for a book, and if successful, you will leave as the author."

## 3. What licenses a name in this question

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :22-23, Fig 8 :51).

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And it names the limit that matters for instruments: "Instruments extend the sensory window but not the conceptual one, they reach what we cannot see but can imagine measuring." Plus: "Every instrument is a crystallized hypothesis about what is worth measuring, and the hypothesis comes from human awareness." So an instrument can widen access only along an axis the frame already has a primitive for. That is the essay-level version of Fig 6 :40 "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."

The same essay adds the honesty condition: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Declaring the primitives "does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." This Solution does not pretend the two-ledger shape is nature's own list. It is a visible choice, recorded as such.

## 4. The source essay is the key text for "what people considered real"

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source." Several underlying causes create one reading: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The eye cannot settle the source; the spectrometer can. And: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." The essay's verdict is operational: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."

It also states the world-versus-frame distinction that governs this whole question: a thing "existed whether you had the instruments and tools to measure its existence or not." That is the essay form of Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." So the honest answer to "instruments changed what people considered real" is not "instruments created the real." It is: instruments changed which sources could be discriminated, and discrimination is what the frame uses to settle a term.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* adds the union-horizon caution, which is the strongest sentence in the eight essays for this question: "This Union Horizon is always moving because instruments improve, and better properties get discovered, so the horizon is a statement about the current state." And: "A bigger and better instrument finding nothing is indistinguishable from a trace it still can't reach. You can only ever say nothing has been found yet." This is why the audit's F6 is a `specified` stop condition, not a metaphysical claim about what does not exist. It also says: "The projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect." That is Fig 8 :51 in another voice.

## 5. Answer to the question the shape is asked

**What does "shape" license us to say about instruments?** Exactly three things, and no names:

1. An instrument changes a frame's reach, not the world's atoms (Fig 6 :25-26; essay 7).
2. A thing enters the "real" ledger only when the ground settles a reading, and settlement needs a discriminator when several sources can give the same reading (Fig 8 :51; Fig 12 :104; essay 7).
3. The stop is a report about the current union horizon, not a verdict about absence (Fig 6 :40; essay 1).

**What does it forbid us to say?** That a community's agreement grounds a term (Fig 2 :35; Seat Map line 163); that a new sensor can be read off a description with no predecessor instrument (Fig 6 :32); that a reading determines its source without a discriminating instrument (essay 7); that a list of famous instruments is the answer (Fig 4 :25).

**Does this Solution commit the forbidden errors?** Access and settlement are kept in separate ledgers (F1, F3), the world is not credited with new primitives (F1), a discriminator is required for source claims (F4), and the historical detail is recorded as a `gap of the world` rather than invented (F8). The one place where a name appears, the spectroscope and telescope cases, is explicitly marked as an illustration from the world sources, not a figure-derived selection.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers famous instrument historians to agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *Arrival of the Fittest* is blunter about this exact practice: attaching the formula and figures to a prompt gives "a plausible simulated output to an issue in any given domain," and "due to the black-box nature could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the historical claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any instrument in this Solution. That was too strong. The README's phrase governs "what a **missing** thing would have to look like," a primitive not held in Γ. Naming a spectroscope is not a new primitive, it is a binding from the world frame onto an existing account of access and settlement. What remains wrong would be to claim the figures *select* the spectroscope, or that naming it moves the wall. Both claims are refused in F2 and F3. The narrowed form is recorded here rather than the earlier overstatement being quietly erased.
