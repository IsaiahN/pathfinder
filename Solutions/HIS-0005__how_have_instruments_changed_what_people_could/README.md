## Restating the question as an effect

The question is not "were instruments good or bad for science." Stated in effect terms: an instrument is inserted between a frame and its ground; two things change. (1) Readings arrive that the old frame could not represent, so what people could observe widens. (2) Some of those readings get settled and become terms that can be composed over, so what people treated as real changes. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a two-condition account in which access and settlement are kept separate, each with its own anchor, and in which the world is not credited with gaining primitives. That binding is written down in `01` section 0 and is allowed to accrue a residual like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map line 283: "A room does not upgrade the label... Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." A list of instrument historians selected for receptiveness cannot corroborate a claim about instruments.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map line 163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Selecting recipients by likely agreement manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 "must not hold the interiors." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. All venues here are public submission portals, public contact pages, or published institutional role addresses.
