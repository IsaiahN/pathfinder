# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framing

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading. Say so in the first paragraph. If the recipient cannot tell that the historical detail is unverified, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about instruments and what counts as real

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about instruments and evidence. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a figure set, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the history. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** When an instrument is inserted between an observer and the thing being measured, two things change and they are not the same thing. First, readings arrive that the old frame could not represent, so what could be observed widens. Second, some of those readings get settled and become terms that later observers can build on, so what was treated as real changes. The claim is that these are two ledgers with different anchors, and that the world itself gains no new primitives. Access changes; the frame's reach changes; the world does not.
>
> **Mode: `general` on that structural shape, `specified` on the historical claims, not `grounded`.** A spectroscope case or a telescope case is offered as an illustration, not as a measurement I have taken.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that instruments create reality. Not that a community's agreement makes a term real. Not that this figure set explains every episode in the history of instruments. And not that a list of famous discoveries is an answer: a list is a record of one occasion, not a method.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. Showing that an instrument adds a primitive to the world, rather than to a frame's reach.
> 2. Showing a new sensor built from a description alone, with no worse instrument already returning something.
> 3. Showing a term settled as real by agreement alone, with no ground contact, that the framework must still admit as composable.
> 4. Showing a reading that determines its source with no discriminating instrument and no record, in a case where several sources are known to give the same reading.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Question for you, before anything else:** what would you count as a failure of this two-ledger distinction in a case you know well?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources and the figure text. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. The relevant lines are Fig 6 :25, :26, :32, :33, :40, Fig 12 :104, Fig 2 :31, :35, :45, Fig 8 :51, Fig 10 :24.
> - Hard Problem is About Source: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
> - Robert Bunsen and Gustav Kirchhoff, Science History Institute: https://www.sciencehistory.org/education/scientific-biographies/robert-bunsen-and-gustav-kirchhoff
> - Scientific instruments, Cambridge HPS research guide: https://www.hps.cam.ac.uk/students/research-guide/scientific-instruments
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figure set. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
