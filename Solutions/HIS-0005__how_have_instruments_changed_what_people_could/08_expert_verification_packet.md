# Expert verification packet: instruments, observation and the real

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of scientific instruments can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. This is the file to send.

**Mode: `specified` on the domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a historical episode is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the historical question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An instrument changes a frame's access, not the world's atoms. A telescope or spectroscope extends what can be represented; it does not add a new kind to the world. Separately, a thing enters what a frame treats as real when the ground settles a reading and later frames can compose over it; agreement alone cannot do that. Where several sources give the same reading, the reading cannot name its source, so an instrument that discriminates sources changes the settled list where an argument cannot. Access and settlement form an ordered pair, not one scale: a new reading can arrive and be refused. Across generations, methods and records carry; a list of past observations is a recording, not a generator. The framework returns this shape with refuters written in advance, not a history of particular instruments.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework access claim; checkable by reading Figure 6

- **Statement:** an instrument extends what can be represented; it adds no primitive to the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :26 "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all." Triangulation: Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." and Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate an instrument adding a primitive to the world rather than to a frame's reach; or exhibit a sensor built from a description with no worse instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something.").

### C2: settlement claim; checkable by reading Figure 12 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** "real" is the frame's settled reach, not its agreed list. A reading must exist before it can be settled, but a settled term does not follow automatically from a new reading.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom" and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Against the agreement reading: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** is "settled" being used as a threshold on ground contact, not as a synonym for consensus? Does the figure's settled-molecule rule actually license composing over a term, and does it require a prior reading?
- **Refuter:** show a term settled as real by agreement alone, with no ground contact, that the framework must still admit as composable; or show a term settled with no prior reading at all; or show a case where every new reading necessarily settles within one generation.

### C3: source-discrimination claim; checkable by reading Figure 8 and by the spectroscope case

- **Statement:** a reading is a projection and does not determine its source; a better instrument discriminates sources that a worse one merges. This is where an instrument changes what a frame counts as real.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" and Fig 2 :38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Also Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- **World corroboration, secondary:** Science History Institute, "Robert Bunsen and Gustav Kirchhoff": "From 1860 the search was on for trace elements detectable only with the help of specialized instruments like the spectroscope." Isaiah N, "The Hard Problem is About Source": "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." The two web pages counting the spectroscope case are the Science History Institute and the University of Waterloo, and they are one witness, not two.
- **Refuter:** show a reading determining its source with no discriminating instrument and no record, in a case where multiple sources are known to give the same reading; or show that the historical case is misdescribed, that is, that the new elements were not settled through spectral discrimination.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that instruments create reality, or that they add kinds to the world.
- Not that agreement grounds a term. A convention is not a verdict (Seat Map line 163).
- Not that a reading names its source without a discriminator.
- Not that the telescope or the spectroscope was selected by the figures. They are world-source illustrations; the framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that any specific community changed its ontology in any specific decade. That is a gap of the world, left open.
- Not a theory of everything, and no last room.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A historian's ground, not a vote. Concretely: 1) a documented case where an instrument added a world primitive rather than a representational reach; 2) a documented case of a sensor built from a description with no predecessor reading; 3) a documented case of a community settling a term with no ground contact and the framework admitting it anyway; 4) a documented metamer resolved without a discriminator. Absence of such cases in the pages read here is a search result, not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").
