**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## What this folder is

It is a figure-grounded derivation of a question that arrives as a single yes/no with a follow-up. The derivation finds that the question is a **union of two conditions with different anchors**, not one question with two halves. "Appears across all societies" asks for an invariant registered per society-slot against a ground that does not update on any culture's own account. "Cultures interpret them differently" asks about shared meanings, where agreement is the product rather than a corruption. The two verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

The short result: a universal moral concern is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), a substructure whose presence fixes the kind of moral judgment, with the cultural interpretation as the qualifier that says which member. Two cultures holding the same concerns under different bindings are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117) over a shared atom set. The standing list of which concerns are universal is a declared `gap`, a shape with an owed discriminator, not a silence and not an invitation to assert one.

## What an answer would look like (Direction, bound as an ordinary term)

Before the run, the sought-for answer is written down so it can accrue a residual like any other term: "a per-slot invariant over moral judgments, expressible as a functional group, with cultural variations as bindings over the same atom set, and a stated instrument that would discriminate among the sources of an identical reading." That is the shape. Anything that fills the slot with a named list without a discriminator is refused as a binding without provenance.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, no receptiveness rank, no bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the framework settles which moral concerns are universal. That is the `gap`.
- No pooling of later agreement as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- No `grounded` verdict. No cross-cultural measurement was taken in this derivation.
