## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that some moral concerns appear in all societies and that cultures interpret them differently. Option A: assert a list of universal concerns and treat variation as decoration. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, split the question into two domains with different anchors, and declare the list a `gap`. What each leads to: A returns a `general`-mode verdict with no source discriminator, mistakes a convention for a ground, and spends the independence of anyone asked to check it. B returns a checkable shape, per-slot verdicts, written refuters, and an owed instrument.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The unassigned structure is which part of a moral judgment is invariant across societies, which part is a cultural binding, and what would count as the same concern under two interpretations. Arity: two conditions joined, universality and variation, with the linking mechanism inside the join. Symmetry: recurrence is an invariance over society-slots (symmetric), interpretation is an ordered binding (asymmetric). Scale: per judgment and per society, not one scalar. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Domain 1, individual moral cognition.** Actors: persons judging in a slot. Currency: per-slot prediction residuals. Anchor: observed judgment or behavior that does not update on what the judge thinks it should be. Live domain.
- **Domain 2, cultural interpretation.** Actors: societies, traditions, institutions, shared backdrops. Currency: shared meanings and qualifiers. Anchor: the shared meaning itself, legitimate because the question is constitutively about human meaning (Fig 2 :36). Live domain, different anchor from Domain 1.
- **Domain 3, cross-cultural research and verification.** Actors: researchers, instruments, samples, peer frames. Currency: data and residuals. Anchor: an independent measure that does not update on the researcher's preferred universal. Live domain, frequently compromised by a shared evidence pool.
- **Domain 4, the standing list.** Actors, currency and anchor: none nameable. Not a domain yet. Declared `gap`, which is a verdict and not a silence.
- **Union, not partition.** The domains overlap; a judgment is at once an individual act, a cultural interpretation and a research observation. One lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Register per slot, acquire terms for what a moral situation affords, state what counts as invariant versus bound, then compare across rooms. The chain breaks at the vocabulary-to-objective link: there is no agreed operational term for "the same concern under a different interpretation" (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

Strain test applied: the six physical terms of Fig 13 do not compose moral concerns. Re-census assigns Fig 13 to the substrate and Fig 12 :104 to settled molecules becoming atoms at the moral level. Strain dissolved, not smoothed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union with two anchors, not one claim with two halves. The cross-society invariant is a functional group whose qualifier is the cultural interpretation. Cultures sharing the concerns under different bindings are isomers over the shared atom set; if the atom sets differ, the relation is composition plus import. Agreement has opposite valence in the two halves, so no single universalism score is admissible. The standing list is a `gap` with a refuter and an owed source-discriminating instrument, reported as a verdict rather than silence. This is `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`. The strongest figure point is the anchor split (Fig 2 :36-41); the strongest owed thing is the discriminator that would fill the `???` slot.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the claim, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a list instead of a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The candidate bindings (harm, fairness, authority) are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument showing one source for an apparent universal everywhere it appears would fill the slot; an independent reading showing a society-slot with no shared functional group would break the recurrence claim; a demonstration that the two halves share one legitimate anchor would collapse the union; a standing list derived with no import and stable valence across two residuals would close the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
