# The Tether's answer: moral concerns across societies

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the standing list, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8). No cross-cultural measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 1. The residual it answers

The observed pattern is that some moral judgments recur across societies while the same concerns are prioritized, bounded and applied differently. What is not given is which part of a judgment is invariant, which part is a cultural binding, and what would count as the same concern under two interpretations. The question is a **union compound**, not one yes/no with an appendix. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Its two live conditions have different anchors, so one verdict cannot be averaged over both.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, recurrence across societies (`specified`, triangulated).**
The invariant is a **functional group**, a substructure whose presence fixes the kind of moral judgment. Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The cultural interpretation is the qualifier. The invariant is registered per society-slot (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."), so no global "universal percentage" is returned. Recurrence is symmetric: swapping two societies leaves the group unchanged.

**D2, interpretation across cultures (`specified`, triangulated).**
Cultures holding the same concerns under different bindings are **isomers** over the shared atom set. Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." A list of which concerns are present cannot see the difference, because the bond is not in the ingredient list (Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."). The condition is that both cultures are written over the same atom set; otherwise the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. Interpretation is ordered: the qualifier binds to a concern in a setting, and swapping changes the term.

**D3, verification (`specified`, triangulated on the rule, unmeasured in fact).**
Agreement has opposite valence in the two halves. Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". For "does it recur", agreement drawn from one pool of samples, instruments and reviews is Fig 2's common-cause collapse, not corroboration. For "how is it interpreted", the question is constitutively about human meaning, so human judgment is the right anchor (Fig 2 :36-37) and agreement about a shared meaning is the product.

**D4, the standing list (`gap`, reported as a verdict).**
No figure line names which concerns are universal, and no instrument here discriminates the sources of an identical reading. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The slot stays `CONCERN(???)CULTURE`. Harm, fairness, authority and purity are candidate bindings from the psychology frame, provenanced as such, not figure-derived primitives and not settled universals.

## 3. The load-bearing point

The same word "agreement" is evidence in one half of the question and corruption in the other. That is why the answer cannot be the single sentence "yes, moral universals exist, and cultures interpret them differently." That sentence takes a `convention` verdict in the interpretation domain and presents it as a `world` verdict in the recurrence domain. Seat Map :281 calls that a mode stated as a verdict. The honest form is two verdicts with two anchors, kept separate.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of universal moral concerns. That is the `gap` (D4).
- Not that all moral judgment is relative. The functional-group invariant is real at the shape level; what is refused is the standing list.
- Not that cross-cultural research has settled the split. No measurement was taken; the Moral Foundations record of measurement-invariance failures is a warning about the anchor, not a settlement.
- Not that agreement is always worthless. It is the product for shared meaning and disqualifying for a world verdict (Fig 2 :40-41).
- Not a derivation of one culture's code from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it (the ground)

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. From *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "indiscernibility is not identity." Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Source Test:** does an identical cross-cultural reading come from one concern or from several? An instrument that discriminates sources would fill the `???` slot. The anthropology source states the need independently: "The observation of the same or similar behavior in different cultures does not prove that they are the results of a common underlying psychological mechanism."
2. **Independence Test:** are the frames ranging against a non-updating ground, or reading the same samples and instruments? Multiple studies using the same questionnaire and the same WEIRD samples are one witness wearing many names (Fig 2 :31-32).
3. **Isomer Test:** can two cultures be written over the same atom set with different bonds? If one requires an atom the other lacks, the isomer claim fails and the relation becomes composition plus import.
4. **Standing refuters:** a society-slot with no shared functional group under an independent reading; a single anchor that legitimately settles both halves; a standing list derivable from the figures with no import and stable valence across two residuals.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** "Across all societies" is an unbounded census, and no frame is a sealed room (Fig 9 :44). *They're Scanning Books*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." The candidate concerns are a visible guess drawn from one research tradition, not nature's own list. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

The author's stake is declared: the question is constitutively about human values, and an interest in the outcome is unavoidable while an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59). The refuter in `01` §4 was written before this conclusion to keep that stake from deciding it.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand back a list of universal moral concerns, and it does not say morality is only local. It says the question is two conditions with different anchors. The invariant that recurs across societies is a functional group, a substructure that fixes the kind of judgment, with the culture-specific rule as a qualifier that says which member; it is registered per society, never averaged. Cultures that hold the same concerns under different bindings stand as isomers, same atoms arranged differently, so a list of which concerns are present cannot see the difference. The same word "agreement" has opposite valence in the two halves: agreement from one pool of samples is disqualifying for the claim that a concern recurs, while agreement about a shared meaning is the product for the question of interpretation. The framework refuses to name the concern in the slot, because what is missing is a source-discriminating instrument, not another list. It offers the shape, four refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
