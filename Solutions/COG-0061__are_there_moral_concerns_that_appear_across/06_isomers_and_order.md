# Functional groups, isomers and order in the moral-universal question

## RESIDUAL

The census in `01` left three questions open: (1) what kind of thing a cross-society invariant is; (2) in what relation two cultures stand when they share concerns but differ in interpretation; (3) what order the composition has, given that "appears across all societies" and "interpreted differently" are not the same level of description. This file derives those three and attaches a falsifier to each.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is the invariant a named universal or a functional group?

Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- A named universal would be a single atom, say `AUTHORITY`, present in every society. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" refuses that form: valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- A functional group is a substructure: `state → concern → qualifier`, whose presence fixes the kind of judgment. The specific concern is the qualifier, which "says which member."
- The Moral Foundations source supplies the empirical analogue: candidates are admitted by criteria including "Presence across multiple cultures", with Shweder's "autonomy, community, and divinity" as clusters of concern. The cluster is the group; the culture-specific rule is the qualifier.
- **Conclusion:** the invariant is a functional group, not a standing atom. **Mode:** `specified`. **Falsifier:** exhibit a society-slot where the recurrent substructure is absent under an independent reading that does not import the researcher's category. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 plus the psychology source, low overlap).

### Q2 : In what relation do two cultures stand?

Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

- Commit to the stronger claim condition: both cultures are written over the **same atom set**, the candidate concerns held by the research frame at this level.
- If they bind those atoms differently, for example one culture bonding purity to divine order and another bonding it to bodily discipline, they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different moral substance.
- If instead one culture requires an atom the other does not, the relation is **composition plus import**, not isomerism. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The import then needs a source gate and a shadow test.
- The low-overlap corroborator is the Evolution of morality source, which reports "premoral sentiments" shared across social animals, including "attachment and bonding, cooperation and mutual aid, sympathy and empathy, direct and indirect reciprocity", and a human layer of enforcement and reason. Shared atoms, different arrangement, different substance.
- **Conclusion:** same-concern/different-interpretation is an isomer relation **conditional on a shared atom set**. **Mode:** `specified`. **Falsifier:** show one culture requires an atom the other cannot be written with, which kills the isomer claim and leaves composition plus import. **Status:** triangulated for the definition, unmeasured for any particular culture pair. The condition is named, not hidden.

### Q3 : Why the two halves use different bonds

Operators table: the test for conjunction is "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." The test for sequence is "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."

- **Recurrence** is symmetric. Swapping two society-slots leaves the invariant unchanged. It behaves like identity (`≡`) or conjunction (`+`).
- **Interpretation** is ordered. A qualifier binds to a particular concern in a particular setting. Swapping the setting changes the term. It behaves like `→` (sequence) or `⋛` (comparison, a threshold relation between two quantities).
- Putting both in one sentence with one verb invites the error of treating a symmetric invariance and an ordered binding as the same relation. Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- **Falsifier:** show that swapping two cultural settings leaves the binding unchanged, so interpretation is symmetric after all. Then the bond distinction collapses and this finding is withdrawn.
- **Status:** single-source for the bond taxonomy (Operators table plus Fig 12, same framework pool), corroborated structurally by the moral-foundations data that concerns cluster differently under different political and cultural contexts. World corroboration is weak and is recorded as such.

### Q4 : What order does the composition have?

Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Fig 12 :103 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."

1. **First order, per society-slot:** a concern composes a judgment with its situation. The psychological record gives the candidate atoms.
2. **Second order, within a culture:** first-order judgments settle into a moral code, which becomes an atom for that culture's further composition. The code is a molecule that can be composed over because it settled against a ground (repeated social enforcement and judgment, per the Evolution of morality source).
3. **Third order, between cultures:** two settled codes are compared. Their comparison is a relation at the next level, not a merging into one code. Fig 12 :104-105 makes irreducibility a fact about a level: "there is no bottom for the same reason Figure 4 says there is no top."
4. **The substrate re-census:** none of this uses the six physical terms as atoms. Fig 13 governs the substrate the loop assumes; Fig 12 :104 governs the demotion of settled molecules to atoms at a higher level. The earlier strain (mapping harm onto gradient) was dissolved by this re-census in `01` §1.5.

**Mode:** `general` for the recursion, `specified` for the three-level reading. **Falsifier:** show a settled moral code cannot serve as an atom for a between-culture comparison, so the third order is illegal. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 4 level rule). No measurement of the three levels was taken.

### Q5 : Does "across all societies" add a further order?

No. It is a **census over slots at the first order**, not another compositional level. Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

So the universal claim is an unbounded enumeration under a per-slot invariant, not a higher molecule. Treating "Universality" as an atom that composes with "Culture" is a category error and is refused. The `gap` in `01` F6/F7 is exactly the refusal to turn an unbounded census into a named term.

**Falsifier:** produce a closed sealed sample of all societies such that the census terminates. That does not exist, so the finding stands as a limit.

## CONCLUSION

The cross-society invariant is a functional group (Fig 12 :123), not a standing atom. Two cultures holding the same concerns under different bindings are isomers over the shared atom set (Fig 12 :117); otherwise the relation is composition plus import. Recurrence and interpretation use different bonds, symmetric and ordered respectively. The composition is three-level (concern, settled code, comparison) and "across all societies" is a per-slot census, not a fourth level. The standing list remains `gap`.

What survives is the shape with its conditions. What does not survive is any named universal and any claim that a list of concerns was derived from the figures. Harm, fairness and authority are bindings from the psychology frame, provenanced as such, not figure-derived primitives.
