## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question arrives as one sentence with two clauses. Read as an effect, the observed pattern is:

> Some moral judgments recur across societies, while the same recurring concerns are prioritized, bounded and applied differently under different cultural interpretations. What is not given is (a) which part of a judgment is the invariant, (b) which part is the cultural binding, and (c) what would count as the same concern under two different interpretations.

Call that unassigned structure `R`. It is the residual. In the notation of the Symbols table, `R` is "the gap between prediction and observation" and "it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read." Here the slice is the source of an apparent cross-cultural regularity: the same observed judgment can be produced by more than one underlying concern, so the reading does not determine the source.

**What an answer would look like, written down before the run (Direction).** A shape with four properties: (1) the invariant is registered per society-slot, not averaged; (2) it is a functional group, not a named content; (3) cultural variations are bindings over the same atom set, so two cultures are isomers; (4) an instrument is named that would discriminate among the candidate sources of an identical reading. This bound term is allowed to accrue a residual and be refuted like anything else (Formula, Direction). It is not a preference smuggled in after the fact.

**Options and what each leads to.** Option A: assert a list of universal moral concerns and treat cultural differences as decorations on that list. This returns a `general`-mode verdict with no discriminator, mistakes a convention for a ground, and would spend the independence of anyone asked to check it. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, split the question into two domains with two anchors, and declare the list a `gap`. This returns a checkable shape, a per-slot method, and written refuters. The derivation below chooses B and says why.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two conditions joined, universality and variation, with a third condition (the linking mechanism) inside the join. That makes it a **union compound**, not one hard question with a yes/no answer. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Symmetry:** the recurrence claim is an invariance over society-slots. It behaves like identity (`≡`) or conjunction (`+`): swap two societies and the invariant should not change. The interpretation claim is ordered: a qualifier binds to a particular concern in a particular setting, so swapping changes the term. Operators table: the test for `+` is "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No"; the test for `→` is "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." One symmetric and one ordered relation in the same sentence is a second reason to unbundle.
- **Scale:** per judgment (the individual slot) versus population (the society or room). Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." So the same loop runs at both sizes, but the anchor must be renamed at the second.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor. No anchor, no domain yet.

| **D2, cultural interpretation** | societies, traditions, institutions, shared backdrops | shared meanings, conventions, qualifiers that bind a concern to a setting | the shared meaning itself. ### 1.3 Partition or union

Union, not partition. The three live domains overlap: a judgment is simultaneously an individual act, a cultural interpretation and a research observation. They cannot be separated into exclusive boxes. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Library of Babel states the same failure in plain words: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." Consequence: one lever does not clear the region, and verdicts must be kept per domain.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can we register a moral judgment per slot, and tell it apart from the one before? Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
2. **Vocabulary:** do we have terms for what a moral situation affords? Fig 3 :7 "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". This is where the question breaks. We have terms for "concern" and "interpretation" but no agreed operational term for "the same concern under a different interpretation." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
3. **Objective:** can we say what would count as progress, that is, what would count as invariance versus binding? Not yet, because step 2 has no instrument.
4. **Planning:** compare across rooms only after 1 to 3 are settled. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
5. **Learn and carry:** carry methods, not recordings of one culture's success (Fig 4 :22).

### 1.5 Strain test and re-census

First pass strain: the six-term substrate of Fig 13 is physical (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network), and moral concerns are not compositions of those six. Forcing "harm = gradient" or "fairness = spectrum" is a strain, so by the strain rule it governs another quantity. Re-census: Fig 13 governs the substrate the loop assumes; a moral concern at the human scale is a **settled molecule** that becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." "Irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." The strain is dissolved by assigning each figure to its proper object, not by softening either. Fig 12 :104 is used at the moral-composition level; Fig 13 is used at the substrate level. No hidden strain remains from this source.

## 3. Findings

### F1 : The question is a union, not a yes/no partition (decisive for structure)

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` that this question instantiates it. **Refuter:** show the two clauses share one anchor and one bond, so that a single measurement would settle both. If that were shown, the split would collapse and this finding would be withdrawn. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and the essay (low overlap between a figure about composition limits and an essay about search vocabulary).

### F2 : A universal concern is a functional group, not a named content

Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Read the cross-cultural record as molecules. The invariant across societies is not a particular rule such as "do not eat your dog" or "respect elders." It is a substructure whose presence fixes the kind of judgment: a concern about harm, or about reciprocity, or about authority, or about purity, any of which recurs as a *kind* while the specific rule varies. The cultural interpretation is the qualifier that says which member of the group is instantiated here.

The Moral Foundations theory source gives the empirical shape. Haidt and Joseph proposed five criteria for a candidate foundation, including "Presence across multiple cultures", and Shweder's "big three" were "autonomy, community, and divinity". The same source says morality diverges because different cultures use the same building blocks differently. That is a functional group plus qualifiers, arrived at from inside psychology, not from the figures.

**Mode:** `specified` for the shape, `general` for the claim that a group-and-qualifier reading fits the recurrence. **Refuter:** exhibit two societies where the recurrent substructure is absent from one of them at the per-slot level, with an independent reading that does not use the researcher's own category. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 plus the psychology source, different evidence pools). The specific list is NOT established here; see F6 and F7.

### F3 : Same concern, different interpretation is an isomer relation

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

If two cultures hold the same set of concerns (harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity in the psychological literature) but bind them differently, for example one binding purity to divine order and another to bodily discipline, then over that shared atom set they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different moral substance. The difference is invisible in a list of ingredients, which is exactly why a survey that reports only "which concerns are present" cannot see it.

Corroboration from a genuinely low-overlap field: the Evolution of morality source reports "premoral sentiments" shared by humans and other social animals, including "attachment and bonding, cooperation and mutual aid, sympathy and empathy, direct and indirect reciprocity, altruism and reciprocal altruism, conflict resolution and peacemaking", and notes that human morality adds rigorous enforcement, reputation and judgment. Shared atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That source is animal behaviour and evolutionary biology, not cross-cultural psychology, so it is a different evidence pool.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** show that the two cultures do not share an atom set, that is, one requires a concern the other cannot be written with. Then the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism, and the claim is narrowed rather than silently kept. **Status:** triangulated for the isomer definition (Fig 12 plus animal-behaviour source). The binding in any particular pair of cultures is unmeasured.

### F4 : The reading does not determine the source (metamerism)

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 6 :30 "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

The Hard Problem is About Source essay gives the mechanism: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." The instrument reads green and "it cannot comment on the source." Green can be one light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The essay adds: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

Apply this to the question. An observed cross-cultural regularity, say that a taboo is treated as morally wrong in many societies, is a reading. The source could be harm avoidance, authority, purity, a learned local convention, or a common external history. The Cultural universal source states the same limit in anthropology: "The observation of the same or similar behavior in different cultures does not prove that they are the results of a common underlying psychological mechanism. One possibility is that they may have been invented independently due to a common practical problem." It adds that outside influence can explain some universals, so universals are not proof of innateness.

**Mode:** `specified` for the error shape, `general` that an instrument is owed. **Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that, with independent readings in two low-overlap frames, assigns the same single source to the regularity in every culture where it appears. That would close the metamerism gap and this finding would be narrowed. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 6, the source essay and the anthropology source. This is the central "owed instrument" of the whole solution.

### F5 : The two halves have different anchors, so agreement has opposite valence

Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

This is the finding that keeps the two verdicts separate.

- For the **universality** half, the claim is about the world (does the concern recur independently of our descriptions?). Agreement among researchers who read the same WEIRD samples, the same questionnaires and the same review articles is Fig 2's second collapse. Fig 2: "they never talk, and still agree" and "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Independence is "how little they draw on the same evidence" (Fig 2 :31). The Moral Foundations source records exactly this problem: measurement invariance of the MFQ fails across demographic groups and cultures, a 27-country invariance study exists, and the authors themselves argue MFQ-2 is useful for cross-cultural work partly because earlier structure was inconsistent. Shared instrument, shared pool, weak verdict.
- For the **interpretation** half, the claim is constitutively about human meaning ("how do cultures interpret them"). Here human judgment is the right anchor, not merely an instrument (Fig 2 :37), and agreement about a shared meaning is the product (Fig 2 :41). The Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay puts it plainly: "Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources." That is Fig 2 :40-41 restated. The same essay adds: "Culture is a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge. Its members can hold very different mindsets; what they share isn't a set of beliefs but the backdrop they read each other against."

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** show that a single anchor legitimately settles both halves, or that agreement has the same valence in a convention question and a world question. Until then, averaging the two into one "moral universals exist, interpreted differently" verdict is the named output-layer error: a mode stated as a verdict (Seat Map :281). **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, the tacit-knowledge essay and the Moral Foundations measurement record.

### F6 : No standing list of universals survives a change of residual

Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 13 :134 "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."

A concern's valence is relative to the residual: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries" (Fig 11 :49). Whether "authority" is a universal binding concern or a local contamination depends on which residual is being tracked. The Cultural universal source lists hundreds of candidate items under Brown's "no known exception" definition, including "Moral sentiments", "Distinguishing right and wrong, good and bad", "Incest prevention or avoidance", "Redress of wrongs, sanctions" and "Shame". That list is a habitat to enumerate, not a standing answer to compose. Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

**Mode:** `general` structural, with the specific list declared `gap`. **Refuter:** derive a fixed list of universal concerns from the figure set alone, with no import and with stable valence across two different residuals. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 11), corroborated by the Brown list as evidence that enumeration grows rather than settles. The list itself remains `gap`.

### F7 : "Across all societies" is an unbounded census, and absence of a finding is not absence

Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

No finite set of societies is a sealed room. The question "appears across all societies" therefore cannot be closed by enumeration; it can only be approached by a shape plus an owed discriminator and a recorded lineage. The framework's own limit (Fig 9 :32) is that it returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The distinction between "underivable in principle" and "unreachable at this budget" matters here: the list is unreachable at this budget and with these instruments, not proven underivable. Fig 8 :40-43 warns that importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

**Mode:** `general` on the limit, `gap` on the enumeration. **Refuter:** a closed, sealed sample of all societies with a non-updating anchor. Such a sample does not exist, so the finding stands as a limit rather than a measurement. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 8, Fig 9, Fig 13).

### F8 : The linking mechanism is an import and needs a source gate and shadow test

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

The mechanism that links a universal concern to its cultural interpretation is not in the cross-cultural questionnaire frame. Candidate imports are evolutionary biology and animal behaviour (why the building blocks recur), cultural psychology and anthropology (how qualifiers are learned and transmitted), and measurement theory (how to tell a shared atom from a shared instrument). Each must be brought in with provenance and a shadow test: does it explain a gap the current frame registers, or does it merely rename it? If two imported sources draw on the same evidence pool, they are one witness, not two (Fig 2 :31-32).

**Mode:** `specified` on the requirement, not on any particular import. **Refuter:** show the mechanism is derivable inside the cross-cultural frame with no import and no loss. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 2).

### F9 : Register per slot and refuse the average

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".

A "percentage of moral concerns that are universal" is an average across slots. It hides the one society-slot where a concern is live and differently bound. The Society of Agents essay carries the same rule into groups: a method that is brilliant in one setting must not be drowned by an average across all of them, citing Fig 1 :22. Verdicts in this solution are therefore per domain and per slot, never averaged into a universalism score.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** show the universal-vs-variable distinction is a single scalar over societies with no per-slot structure. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 1), with the group essay as a same-author restatement, so effectively one pool.

### F10 : Prestige cannot settle a universal

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." The same essay asks: "What does it mean to align an AI to human values when human values are a moving target? You cannot align to something that changes."

A list of universals endorsed by famous universalists is prestige, not ground. The legitimate ask is a budget to try a claim against a non-updating measure. This is a constraint on verification and outreach, not a substantive moral claim. **Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** none needed within the derivation; it is a rule the framework imposes on itself. **Status:** single-source (framework essay), maximal overlap with the figures.

### F11 : Mode discipline is itself a finding

Seat Map :281: "an unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict." The questions "which concerns are universal" and "how much variation is cultural" are unmeasured here. Stating either as `grounded` would be the named output-layer failure genus. Every substantive file in this folder carries a mode line for this reason, and the packet (`08`) repeats it before the claim.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a measurement in which the universal-vs-variable split is settled by a ground, at which point the relevant claim can be promoted from `specified` to `grounded`. **Status:** single-source (seat map), framework self-rule.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim (a functional-group invariant, isomeric cultural bindings, an owed source discriminator, no standing list) is refuted or narrowed if any of the following holds:

1. **Per-slot recurrence fails.** A society-slot shows a moral concern with no shared functional group in any other slot under an independent reading that does not import the researcher's own category.
2. **Isomer claim fails.** Two cultures hold different moral codes with no shared atom set, so the relation is composition plus import rather than isomerism.
3. **Metamerism closes.** A source-discriminating instrument assigns one and the same source to a cross-cultural regularity in every culture where it appears, with independent readings in low-overlap frames.
4. **Anchor split fails.** A single anchor legitimately settles both the world claim (universality) and the meaning claim (interpretation), so agreement has the same valence in both.
5. **List gap closes.** A fixed standing list of universal concerns is derivable from the figure set alone, with no import and stable valence across two different residuals.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction log in section 7 is updated. No finding is quietly retained.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Split before answering.** Answer D1 (per-slot invariant as functional group), D2 (interpretation as binding, convention anchor) and D3 (research frame, independence required) separately. Never average them into a universalism percentage (F1, F5, F9).
2. **State the shape, not a list.** Say that the invariant is a functional group with cultural qualifiers; leave the slot named `???` until a discriminator pays for it (F2, F7).
3. **Name the owed instrument.** The next useful thing is a source-discriminating measure that can tell whether an identical cross-cultural reading comes from one source or several, run in low-overlap frames (F4).
4. **Audit the evidence pool.** Before citing cross-cultural agreement as corroboration, ask whether the frames share samples, instruments or review articles (F5, F8).
5. **Send methods, not recordings.** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Lead outreach with the shape and the refuter, then offer links only if asked.
6. **Declare the stake.** The question is constitutively about human values; the author's interest in an answer is unavoidable. Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Declare it and keep the refuter in front of the claim.

## 8. Status

The structure survives: a union compound, a per-slot functional-group invariant, isomeric cultural bindings, two anchors with opposite agreement valence, and a `gap` for the list.
