# Expert verification packet: the Tether on moral universals and cultural interpretation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural researcher, moral psychologist or cultural anthropologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the standing list, not `grounded`.** No cross-cultural measurement was taken. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of two conditions with different anchors, not one question with two halves. The invariant that recurs across societies is a functional group: a substructure whose presence fixes the kind of moral judgment, with the culture-specific rule as a qualifier that says which member. Cultures holding the same concerns under different bindings are isomers over a shared atom set, so a list of which concerns are present cannot see the difference. Agreement has opposite valence in the two halves: shared evidence pools disqualify the recurrence verdict, while agreement about shared meaning is the product for the interpretation verdict. The framework does not name the concern in the slot, because what is missing is a source-discriminating instrument, not another list. It offers the shape, refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the cross-society invariant is a functional group, not a standing atom; its cultural interpretation is a qualifier that says which member.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Also Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** is the invariant better described as a recurring substructure with a variable binding than as a fixed list of contents? A researcher can check this against their own domain without accepting the rest of the framework.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a society-slot where the substructure is absent under an independent reading that does not import the researcher's own category; or derive a fixed standing list with stable valence across two different residuals.

### C2 : framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by a cultural researcher

- **Statement:** two cultures with the same concerns under different bindings are isomers over the shared atom set, same atoms arranged differently; if the atom sets differ, the relation is composition plus import instead.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Also Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **What to check:** pick two cultures you know. Can both be written over the same set of concerns? If yes, is the difference a binding rather than an extra atom? If no, the isomer claim is narrowed for that pair and the import claim applies.
- **Refuter:** show one culture requires an atom the other cannot be written with. That kills the isomer claim for that pair and leaves composition plus import.

### C3 : the load-bearing claim; checkable against method and evidence provenance

- **Statement:** agreement has opposite valence in the two halves. For the world claim (does a concern recur independently of our descriptions), agreement drawn from a shared pool is disqualifying. For the meaning claim (how is it interpreted), agreement about a shared meaning is the product.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Anchor legitimacy: Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
- **What to check:** in a study you know, did the apparent cross-cultural convergence come from independent readings, or from a shared questionnaire, sample and review pool? Did the interpretation claim use the shared meaning itself as the anchor? Those are different standards and should not be scored on one scale.
- **Refuter:** show one anchor that legitimately settles both halves, or that agreement has the same valence in a convention question and a world question.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of universal moral concerns. No figure line returns one, and none is asserted here.
- Not that moral judgment is purely relative. The functional-group invariant is real at the shape level.
- Not that cross-cultural research has settled the recurrence verdict. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not that agreement is always worthless. It is the product for shared meaning (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that harmony, fairness or any other named concern is the universal slot filler. Those are bindings from the psychology frame, provenanced as such and unproven.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Source Test:** does an identical cross-cultural reading come from one concern or several? An instrument that discriminates sources would fill the `???` slot.
2. **Independence Test:** are the frames reading a non-updating ground, or the same samples and instruments? Shared pools are one witness, not several (Fig 2 :31-32).
3. **Isomer Test:** can two cultures be written over the same atom set with different bonds? If not, the isomer claim is narrowed for that pair.
4. **Standing refuters:** a society-slot with no shared functional group under an independent reading; a single anchor that settles both halves; a standing list derivable with no import.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :123, :117, :5 and Fig 2 :40, :41, :36. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against two cultures you know: same atom set, different binding, or an extra atom?
3. Name one study where apparent cross-cultural agreement may have come from a shared instrument or sample rather than an independent ground.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
