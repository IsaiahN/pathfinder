# The Tether's answer: are standard cost-benefit analyses adequate?

**Mode: `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the claim that adequacy is relative to a ground. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A standard CBA returns one scalar: discounted monetised benefits minus discounted monetised costs. The decisions it is asked to inform have effects that are uncertain, unequal across people, and difficult to value in money. The residual is the gap between the scalar and what those decisions need: a per-person distributional reading, a robustness reading over contested futures, and a separate statement of effects with no money price. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* So it is three loops with separate verdicts, never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The money-only closure has a boundary.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Money can price commensurable, reversible effects. Distribution across persons, contested futures and non-market values are not atoms of that closure. No amount of internal refinement adds them (Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*).

2. **Unequal effects fail because the residual is per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A symmetric `+` over affected people records the amounts and loses who bears them. Verdict for this domain: **not adequate**, triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 12 (bond symmetry and the flat formula) and Fig 9 (union unbundling). The repair is an import: a distributional weight with recorded provenance.

3. **Deeply uncertain effects fail because the probability atom is the wrong atom.** Calculable risk is inside the closure, so SEARCHED or a budget limit is the honest answer. Where the model of the system, the probability distribution or the weights over outcomes are disputed, Fig 6 :29 applies: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."* Another discount rate is refinement on an axis that cannot carry the residual. What is owed is robustness across futures, imported from a frame whose closure holds it. Verdict for this domain: **adequate for calculable risk, not adequate for deep uncertainty**, triangulated across Fig 6 :29-30 and Fig 8 :40-43.

4. **Hard-to-value effects fail because the two currencies do not add.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* A WTP reading is an instrument, and Fig 2 :37-38 says *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Verdict for this domain: **not adequate**, triangulated across Fig 12 (units) and Fig 2 (instrument versus anchor). The honest report where no discriminating instrument exists is `unreachable` (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*).

5. **The verdicts are held apart.** Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* The two answer forms, one scalar and three loops, are isomers (Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*). A composite "adjusted BCR" restores the symmetric bond and destroys the arrangement that carried the information. There is no single adequacy percentage.

6. **Repairs are owed in dependency order.** Unbundle, then SEARCHED, then refuse before mint, then INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks, then settle each loop before composing. Filters are for the budget, never for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that CBA is useless. It is the right instrument inside its closure: commensurable, reversible, small effects with low distributional stakes.
- Not that a BCR is never informative. It is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*), so it is a witness, not the anchor.
- Not that a particular welfare weight, discount rate, robustness criterion or valuation method is the answer. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this run settles adequacy for any particular decision.
- Not a single composite metric. Two currencies in different units are held apart, not summed.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A ground reading, not more agreement. The instruments that would close each domain:

- **Distribution:** a decision in which the per-person incidence is measured and the weighted and unweighted assessments are both shown, so a checker can see what the weight changed.
- **Uncertainty:** a case where the competing conceptual models are stated, the plausible futures enumerated, and the policy's performance measured across them, rather than at one best estimate.
- **Valuation:** a source-discriminating instrument, in the sense of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the spectrometer resolved the case. Here, an instrument that separates a real valuation from moral satisfaction or framing would name the slot.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** (1) a money-only CBA registering all three conditions without import; (2) values invariant to income, framing and instrument; (3) a decision where a bare ratio ranks exactly as the multi-frame assessment and the differences carry no decision-relevant information.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in general.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The three imports have not been tested against a particular decision in this run, so no claim is made about any specific project. Where no instrument discriminates, the answer stops at `unreachable at this budget` and records the lineage. Indiscernibility is not identity: a monetised placeholder that looks like an adequate answer is still a placeholder.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A standard cost-benefit analysis is adequate inside a narrow closure: effects commensurable in money, small and reversible, with distribution that does not matter. It is not adequate when effects are uncertain in the deep sense, unequal, or hard to value in money, because those are three different missing atoms, not three harder versions of the same calculation. Money-only CBA adds effects and people under a symmetric plus sign, so the identity of who bears a cost disappears into the average; it holds one probability atom, so contested futures cannot be represented; and it has one currency, so an effect with no price cannot be added without fabricating one. The Tether's answer is a shape, not a replacement metric: keep three readings separate, with a per-person distributional weight, a robustness test across plausible futures, and a non-monetary valuation whose source is recorded; report unreachable where no instrument discriminates; and never sum the currencies into one score. Refuters were written before the run. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
