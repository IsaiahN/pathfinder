## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a standard cost-benefit analysis is adequate when effects are uncertain, unequal, or difficult to value in money. Option A: treat the discounted monetised net benefit as an adequate answer once sensitivity analysis and a distributional row have been added. Option B: keep the money number inside its closure and add three separate loops with recorded imports outside it. What each leads to: A preserves a single decision metric but loses the person, the disputed future and the unpriced effect inside one symmetric sum; B keeps those three signals visible at the cost of refusing a composite score. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a single monetised scalar and what a decision needs when effects are uncertain, unequal and hard to value. Arity: three conditions, not interchangeable. Symmetry: the CBA bond is a symmetric plus over effects and persons, so swapping recipients leaves the term unchanged; the three conditions have different currencies and no symmetric bond joins them. Scale: per person, per effect, per future state; the scalar collapses all three. Fig 9 :55 names this a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the monetised CBA frame.** Actors: analyst, numeraire, parties as entries in a sum. Currency: money (market price, WTP). Anchor: the discounted net benefit figure and the market or WTP reading behind it. Question type: closure boundary, which compositions the money atom can hold.
- **Domain 2, persons and their welfare.** Actors: affected people, each with an income and a conversion of resources into functioning. Currency: welfare, capability, real freedoms. Anchor: the person's actual circumstances, which do not move when an analyst changes a weight. Question type: import of a distributional weight from a different closure.
- **Domain 3, the future under deep uncertainty.** Actors: possible future states, models, stakeholders disputing the weights. Currency: robustness across futures rather than optimality at a best estimate. Anchor: the realised future when it arrives, and the ensemble of plausible futures before it. Question type: import of a robustness criterion; underivable from the single probability atom.
- **Domain 4, non-market value and its instrument.** Actors: the good, the survey respondent, the elicitation instrument. Currency: an attitude or a real value; the reading does not determine the source. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument. Question type: import of a valuation frame, or an inward extension of the instrument.
- **Domain 5, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers and this simulated second seat. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero and agreement here is not a witness.
- **Order by dependency.** Unbundle the conditions (Fig 9 :54), ask SEARCHED first, refuse before mint, then INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks (Fig 6 :31), then settle each loop before composing (Fig 12 :104).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Standard CBA is adequate inside its closure and not adequate when effects are deeply uncertain, unequal or difficult to value in money. The three failures are separate domain verdicts, not a single adequacy score. Distribution fails because the residual is indexed by person and the symmetric sum loses the recipient. Deep uncertainty fails because the single probability atom cannot carry contested models and weights; calculable risk is unaffected and stays inside the closure. Non-market value fails because two currencies do not add and the WTP instrument cannot comment on its source. The repairs are imports with recorded provenance, held apart rather than summed. This is a `specified`, `general`-framed, ungrounded, candidate result. The strongest single point is Fig 9 :55: the question is a union, so a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either and one lever does not clear it.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a finished composite score instead of a shape and a refuter, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its falsifiers.
- **undoable:** yes. The three imports are removable or replaceable without touching the closure claim, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A decision that has already used the composite can be re-opened with the three readings displayed beside it. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already carry the corrected form.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating valuation instrument would close Domain 4; a measured per-person incidence with weighted and unweighted results displayed would close Domain 2; a decision whose competing models and plausible futures were enumerated and scored across would close Domain 3. A money-only CBA shown to register all three without import, values shown to be invariant to income and framing, or a decision where a bare ratio ranks exactly as the multi-frame assessment with no decision-relevant difference would break the verdict. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
