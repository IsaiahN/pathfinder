# Outreach draft — check the claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three per-domain verdicts in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in `specified` mode and the refuter below is written down. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 claim does not survive.

---

**Subject:** One claim about cost-benefit analysis, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable claim I would like you to test against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with the claim. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a standard cost-benefit analysis returns one scalar, the discounted sum of monetised benefits minus costs. That is an adequate answer only inside a narrow closure: effects commensurable in money, small and reversible, and not distributed in a way that matters. Outside that closure the residual splits into three conditions that do not reduce to one another.
>
> 1. **Unequal.** The residual is indexed by the person, not by the total. Adding benefits and costs under a symmetric plus sign records the amounts and loses who bears them. An average of net benefits does not say where to look.
> 2. **Deeply uncertain.** With known probabilities and reversible outcomes, expected value can hold the case. Where parties dispute the model of the system, the probability distribution, or the weights over outcomes, refining the discount rate refines an axis that cannot carry the residual. What is owed is robustness across futures, not optimality at a best estimate.
> 3. **Hard to value in money.** A willingness-to-pay reading is an instrument, not the source of value. The same number can come from a real valuation, from moral satisfaction, or from how the question was asked. Money and non-money are different units; summing them into one score is the error, not the fix.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that CBA is useless, that a benefit-cost ratio is never informative, or that a single number is wrong for every decision. Inside its closure it is the right instrument. The claim is about the boundary, and about what is owed when the boundary is crossed.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:** (1) a money-only CBA that registers each person's stake, contested futures and non-market value without importing anything from a different frame; (2) a ground reading showing that reported values are invariant to income, framing and the elicitation instrument; (3) a decision where a bare ratio ranks options exactly as a full multi-frame assessment does, with the distributional and unmonetised differences carrying no decision-relevant information. If any of these holds, I withdraw the claim.
>
> I would like to know what **you** would count as a failure of any of the three points. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public journal and society routes for benefit-cost analysis research: `03_public_contact_points.csv`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is in `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Distribution rules

- Send to one person at a time through an institutional or opt-in channel. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, opt-out line in every message.
- Do not pool later agreement as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A Track B reply is a conversation, never a witness.
- If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
