## Wider literature that bears on the three conditions

These are grouped by the condition they check, not by discipline, so a reader can see which figure line each one addresses.

**Unequal.** Sen's capability approach attacks sum-ranking directly: maximising total welfare ignores the pattern of distribution and the different abilities of people to convert resources into functioning. Arrow's impossibility theorem is the formal version of the same boundary: there is no rule for combining many individual orderings into one social ordering under the stated conditions. The 2026 Green Book is the practice-side concession: an extra pound is worth more to a low-income household, so weights are required and weighted estimates must be shown beside unweighted ones. Read these three together with Fig 1 :19 and Fig 12 :92.

**Uncertain.** RAND's DMDU programme and the Marchau, Walker, Bloemen and Popper volume (Springer, 2019) separate calculable risk from deep uncertainty and replace predict-then-act with robust decision making. The Green Book distinguishes known-unknowns from unknown-unknowns and prescribes sensitivity analysis, scenario planning and optimism-bias adjustments for the latter. Read with Fig 6 :29-30 and Fig 8 :40-43. Overlap note: RAND and the Springer volume come from the same research programme, so they are one witness with two pages, not two independent witnesses.

**Difficult to value.** Kahneman and Knetsch (1992) and Kahneman and Ritov (1994) show that willingness-to-pay responses track moral satisfaction or attitude more than economic value. The 1999 CRS report records the legislative consequence: nonuse values are real, but the measurement methodology is contested and cannot be assumed reliable. Circular A-4 warns against treating unmonetised as unimportant. Read with Fig 2 :37-38 and Fig 12 :125-130. Overlap note: the two Kahneman papers share an author and a research programme, so they count as one witness.

**Practice-level ground.** The Green Book 2026 and Circular A-4 2023 are different jurisdictions and independent policy documents. Their agreement that a bare monetised ratio is not the decision rule is two witnesses from a shared professional tradition, not a proof, but it is the best available report of what standard practice itself concedes.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25 —** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42 —** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 —** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Seat Map :163 —** *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send list selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42 —** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a decision with irreversible or unpriced effects, so independent checkers are worth more than a larger agreeing audience.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the claim in `specified` mode with its three conditions; (2) the pre-registered refuter and the question "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction to state a mode and write a refuter before running it.

## The two-stage offer

**(1)** Check the claim against the ground. **(2)** Opt in to the framework only if the claim survives and curiosity remains. **(3)** Reuse the loop on another problem with its own ground and its own refuter.

- Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* — and Fig 2 :45 — *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- Stage 2 is opt-in by construction. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded first and their later agreement never pooled (Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted (Fig 4 :22). The loop is a generator, not this answer.
- Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user writes down what would refute their result before running it.
