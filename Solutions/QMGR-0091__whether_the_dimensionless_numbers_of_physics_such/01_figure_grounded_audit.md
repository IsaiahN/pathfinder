## 0. The question under audit

> "Whether the dimensionless numbers of physics, such as the mass ratios, have a common origin"
>
> Source: `Questions/QM & GR Questions.txt` line 116. Section: Fundamental Constants and Symmetries.

Stated as an effect: we hold a list of measured pure numbers. Each is settled by an instrument, yet each is a free slot in the theory that contains it, meaning the theory returns no value for it. "Common origin" is the claim that a single generative frame predicts the pattern of those slots, so that some or all of them are compositions rather than primitives. Option A, assert a common origin, leads to a derivation or a selection story that reduces the number of independent inputs. Option B, refuse the claim, leads to accepting the numbers as empirical facts. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): a frame whose `closure(Γ)` contains a composition predicting a relation among at least two constants, settled at the measurement anchor, with cost paid under the acceptance bargain.

## 1. Residual in effect terms

- **Arity.** Many, not one. Sources actually read give between 19 and 31 dimensionless constants. Baez (2011) guesses 26 and notes that the count depends on how the pie is sliced. Tegmark, Aguirre, Rees and Wilczek (2006) identify 31 required by particle physics and cosmology. The Wikipedia extract gives 25 for the Standard Model proper. So even the residual's arity is frame-dependent, which is itself a finding (F5).
- **Symmetry.** Each constant is a scalar. The relation among them, if any, is a threshold or a binding, not a symmetric join of peers. Operators table :11-15 lists `+` as the flat conjunction, Fig 12 :92-93 warns that under a symmetric bond k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a "common origin" that merely conjoins constants would undercharge the arrangement.
- **Scale.** First order inside a theory (a coupling, a mass ratio), second order at a generative frame that would produce them. Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
- **Anchor.** Measurement. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A proposed origin is a frame; the measured numbers are the anchor it must range against.

## 4. Findings, each with a refuter

### F1 — Irreducibility is a fact about a level, not about the world (decisive, structural)

Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

Fig 13 :3: **"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."**

The constants are settled at the anchor (domain A), so each can serve as an atom for whatever composes over it. Whether they are atoms at a deeper level is a fact about that level, not a fact about the world. So "do they have a common origin" is well-posed only after a level is named. At the level of the Standard Model they are atoms; at a hypothetical GUT or landscape level some may be molecules. The figures do not answer yes or no; they decide the **shape** of the question.

Status: `triangulated` (Fig 12 :103-104 and Fig 13 :3 are low-overlap routes: composition recursion versus minimal-set structure). Mode `general` on the structural claim.

**Refuter:** show a quantity that is irreducible at one level and also irreducible at every deeper level as a theorem (not a budget limit), or derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five. Either breaks the level-relativity and the finding is withdrawn.

### F2 — A common origin is an import, not a mint inside the current closure

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :24: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**

Within `closure(Γ)` of the Standard Model, minting explores arrangements of the held parameters and cannot add a new primitive that predicts their values. A common origin therefore arrives as an import from a frame whose closure differs (a GUT, a compactification, a selection measure). Fig 8 attaches a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (explains a gap) to any import. A slogan "they share a common origin" supplies neither gate.

Status: `single-source` for the closure mechanic (Fig 6), corroborated in kind by the world sources (GUTs reduce independent inputs; Baez calls the all-derived theory *"merely a dream"*), which is low overlap. Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a derivation inside the Standard Model closure that predicts one of the free constants from the others with no new atom and no empirical input. Then the origin is a mint, not an import, and F2 is narrowed.

### F3 — The MDL bargain is the test of whether an origin is worth having

Fig 5 :43: **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."**

Fig 5 :45: **"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."**

A common-origin theory pays if `cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R)`. Restating the 26 numbers with 26 new parameters fails this: it explains everything by saying nothing, which Fig 5 :43-44 explicitly fails. The world literature gives the two halves of the evidence. GUTs predict relations among fermion masses (electron/down, muon/strange, tau/bottom) and gauge couplings *"nearly, but not quite"* meet, so a partial compression exists. But *"Some of these mass relations hold approximately, but most don't"* and *"there is no generally accepted GUT model."* On the other side, attempts since Eddington *"have yet produced convincing results or gained wide acceptance among theoretical physicists."*

Status: `triangulated` (Fig 5 MDL rule plus the independent physics record). Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** a candidate origin that reproduces all measured constants while adding at least as many free parameters as it removes fails the bargain and is not a common origin in this sense. Presenting it as one refutes the claim that MDL governs this question.

### F4 — The constants are a union, so a single origin may be the wrong question shape

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

From *The Library of Babel* (essay, read 2026-11-23): *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

The first 22 or so constants are particle-physics parameters; the cosmological constant and the density parameters are cosmological; the initial conditions are a third condition. Baez counts 26 with the cosmological constant included, Tegmark et al. count 31 by adding cosmology and selection. A single lever (one origin) may clear one region and leave the intersections. So before searching for "the" origin, unbundle. This is the cheapest figure-grounded move and it is owed before F1 to F3 are applied.

Status: `triangulated` (Fig 9 union rule plus the essay's bundle passage). Mode `general` on the shape, `specified` on the partition of this particular list.

**Refuter:** show that particle-physics and cosmological constants are one condition, so that changing the origin term moves both together with no residue. Then the union reading is wrong and a single loop is correct.

### F5 — Different parametrizations are isomers or identities, not new physics

Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Operators table :23-27: identity, **"two names, one referent,"** with test **"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."**

Baez writes: *"There are different ways of slicing the pie. Instead of the electromagnetic coupling constant together with the masses of the W, Z, and Higgs, we could have used 4 other constants... Either way we slice it, we're now up to 12 + 2 = 14 fundamental constants."* The count changes with the parametrization while the theory does not. Under operators these are either `≡` identities (two names, one referent, no new substance) or Fig 12 isomers (same atoms arranged differently). The famous question "how many constants are there" is therefore partly a notation question, and the figures already price it: a flat formula records ingredients and not arrangement (Fig 12 :14-17).

Status: `triangulated` (Fig 13 :121 plus Operators `≡` plus the independent Baez observation). Mode `specified`.

**Refuter:** exhibit two parametrizations related by neither a definitional identity nor a different bond of the same atoms, so that the count change carries new physical content rather than notation. Then the isomer/identity reading fails.

### F6 — Candidate origins that fit the same numbers are one witness without provenance

Fig 8 :51-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

A GUT mass relation, a landscape selection argument and a numerological fit can all return the same measured number. Their agreement does not corroborate a common origin unless their evidence pools differ and each ranges against the anchor separately. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): several underlying causes can create identical readings, and *"the instrument... cannot comment on the source."* The constants are readings; a common origin is a claimed source. The record of which frame produced which prediction is the only discriminator available until an instrument discriminates sources.

Status: `triangulated` (Fig 8, Fig 2 and the essay's metamerism argument, three low-overlap routes). Mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show that two candidate origins draw on genuinely different evidence pools and still make the same novel prediction that is then confirmed at the anchor. That is convergence at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50), and it would upgrade this from a warning to a corroboration.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim of section 7 is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. **Level-relativity fails.** A constant is shown to be irreducible at every level as a theorem (not merely at this budget), or one of the six substrate terms is derived from the other five. Breaks F1.
2. **The bargain fails to govern.** A candidate origin reproduces all measured constants while adding at least as many free parameters as it removes, and is nevertheless counted as a successful common origin by the field. Breaks F3.
3. **The union fails.** Particle-physics and cosmological constants are shown to be one condition, so a single lever moves them together with no residue. Breaks F4.
4. **Provenance is shown unnecessary.** Two origins drawing on one evidence pool are accepted as independent corroboration. Breaks F6.
5. **A name is derived.** A specific origin primitive is derived from the figures and settled at the anchor, exceeding the documented shape-not-a-name limit. Breaks F7.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the recommendation is rewritten. Until one is run, no part of this audit is `grounded`.
