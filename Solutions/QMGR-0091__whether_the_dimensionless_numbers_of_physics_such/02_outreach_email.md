# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 to F5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are recorded as `specified`, not as measurements. The output must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the dimensionless constants, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on a figure-grounded framework and I have one structural claim about the dimensionless constants that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "do the constants share a common origin" is not one question. It is a union of at least three conditions: particle-physics parameters (alpha, mass ratios, the strong coupling, mixing angles), cosmological parameters (the cosmological constant and density ratios), and initial conditions. A single lever can clear the region where only one condition holds and leave the intersections standing. So the first move is to unbundle, not to name an origin.
>
> The second claim is about level. Whether a number is irreducible is a fact about the level you are standing on, not a fact about the world. At the level of the Standard Model the constants are free inputs. A "common origin" is a claim about a deeper level where some of them would be compositions. The framework does not say which deeper frame, and I am not claiming it does.
>
> **What would count as a common origin here.** A frame whose closure predicts a relation among at least two constants, settled against the measured values, and cheaper to state than the list it replaces. A theory that reproduces 26 numbers by adding 26 new parameters does not count. A relation that fits one set of data but has no provenance (no record of which frame produced it and what gap it explains) does not count as evidence yet.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that there is a common origin. Not that there is not one. Not that gauge coupling unification, GUT mass relations, or anthropic selection settle it. Those are candidate frames in the physics literature, and the figures do not name among them. I am also not claiming the constants are fine-tuned for life; that is a neighbouring question with its own ground.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before writing to you:
> 1. Show that particle-physics and cosmological constants are one condition, so a single origin moves them together with no residue.
> 2. Show that a constant is irreducible at every level as a theorem, not merely at our current budget.
> 3. Show that a candidate origin reproduces all measured constants while adding as many free parameters as it removes, and that this is still counted as a successful origin.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local figure folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an output contrived to fit the formula proves nothing. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
