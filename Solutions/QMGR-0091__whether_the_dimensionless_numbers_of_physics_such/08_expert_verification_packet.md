# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the dimensionless constants

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a constant is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do the dimensionless constants share a common origin" is a union of conditions, not one list: particle-physics parameters, cosmological parameters and initial conditions must be unbundled before a single lever is sought. Irreducibility is a fact about a level, so a constant that is a free input in the Standard Model may be a composition at a deeper level, but the framework does not name that level. A common origin would be an import from a frame whose closure differs, owing provenance and a shadow test, and it earns its place only under the minimum-description-length bargain: cheaper to state than the list it replaces, with a relation predicted and settled at the measured values. Derivation and selection are separate mechanisms with separate grounds, and agreement among candidate origins is not corroboration without independent evidence pools. The constants are readings; the origin is a claimed source, and only a source-discriminating instrument can settle the source.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12
- **Statement:** the constants must be unbundled before an origin is named, and irreducibility is level-relative.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 — *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 12 :103 — *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* Fig 12 :104 — *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- **Refuter:** show the three conditions are one, so a single parameter change moves them together with no residue; or show a constant irreducible at every level as a theorem, not merely at this budget.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable against the actual constants
- **Statement:** a common-origin hypothesis is admissible only if it pays the description-length bargain: it predicts a relation among at least two constants, and its own cost plus what it leaves unexplained is less than the cost of listing the constants separately.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :43 — *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."* Fig 5 :45 — *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."*
- **What to check:** do the GUT fermion mass relations (electron/down, muon/strange, tau/bottom) actually predict measured values, and how many of them survive? Do the three gauge couplings meet at one scale without adding an assumption? Does the proposal add as many free parameters as it removes?
- **Refuter:** a candidate that reproduces all measured constants while adding at least as many parameters as it removes, and is still counted as a successful origin, refutes the claim that this bargain governs the question.

### C3 — framework-internal provenance claim
- **Statement:** derivation and selection are different loops, and competing origins that fit the same numbers are one witness without provenance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51-52 — *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
- **What to check:** does a proposed common origin state which frame produced it, what gap it explains, and on what evidence it draws? If two proposals share a review or a dataset, they are one witness.
- **Refuter:** two origins with genuinely independent evidence pools that make the same novel prediction and are confirmed at the anchor would upgrade agreement to corroboration and narrow this claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no last room (Fig 7 :6 — *"the limit: no last room"*).
- Not a name for the origin. SU(5), SO(10) and the landscape are provenanced candidates in the physics literature, not figure derivations.
- Not that the constants have a common origin, and not that they do not.
- Not that gauge coupling unification or a fermion mass relation settles the question. They are partial compressions; most such relations fail.
- Not that the constants are fine-tuned for life (that is the neighbouring question QMGR-0092).
- Not that the figure's six-term substrate is nature's own list. It is a declared, revisable guess.
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Concretely:

1. A GUT mass relation predicted and confirmed at the measured values with the prediction dated before the measurement.
2. Gauge couplings demonstrated to meet at one scale, or demonstrably not.
3. A precision constancy test finding variation in a dimensionless constant (see Uzan 2025).
4. A derivation inside the Standard Model closure predicting a free constant from others with no new atom.
