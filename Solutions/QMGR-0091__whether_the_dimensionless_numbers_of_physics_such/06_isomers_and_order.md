## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are different parametrizations isomers?

- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Operators table :23-27: identity is **"two names, one referent,"** tested by **"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."**
- Baez (2011), fetched 2026-11-23: *"There are different ways of slicing the pie. Instead of the electromagnetic coupling constant together with the masses of the W, Z, and Higgs, we could have used 4 other constants... Either way we slice it, we're now up to 12 + 2 = 14 fundamental constants."*
- ⇒ A list using `α, m_W, m_Z, m_H` and a list using `g_1, g_2, m_H, v` can be related either by **identity** (a definitional change of coordinates, `≡`, no new substance) or by **isomerism** (same physical theory, different bond, different written substance). The count changes without the theory changing. Under Fig 12 :14-17 a flat formula records ingredients and not arrangement, which is why the count question can look like a discovery when it is a notation choice.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two parametrizations related by neither a definitional identity nor a different bond over the same atoms, such that the count change carries new physical content. Then the isomer/identity reading fails. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 13 plus Operators plus Baez, low overlap).

### Q2 — What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Dependency order for this question: (1) settle each constant at the measurement anchor; (2) describe the residual, that is, which constants fail to be derived and where; (3) unbundle particle, cosmological and initial-condition conditions (Fig 9 :55); (4) only then choose a candidate frame (Fig 9 :27-28); (5) price it under the MDL bargain (Fig 5 :43); (6) attach provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8); (7) ask what instrument would discriminate sources (essay, section 4 of `05`).
- A candidate origin proposed before step 3 is an echo without a shadow: a structure found elsewhere and given somewhere to live. The world literature shows the failure mode. Eddington fixed α⁻¹ = 136 by argument and *"when its value was discovered to be closer to 137, he changed his argument to match that value"* (Wikipedia extract). That is a frame picking its target after seeing the reading.
- **Falsifier:** show a candidate origin whose prediction was committed before the measurement it matches, with the commitment date in the record. Then the order objection is answered for that case. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 3, Fig 9, Wikipedia history).

### Q3 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Fig 9 :59: **"not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."**
- World evidence: Baez arrives at 26 by including the cosmological constant; Tegmark et al. arrive at 31 by adding cosmology and selection effects to particle physics. The count jumps when conditions are added, which is the signature of a union rather than a longer partition of one list.
- ⇒ Treat the constants as a union of at least three conditions. A single common origin clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The residue after unbundling is a specific compound, not a harder version of one problem.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a single parameter change that moves all three conditions together and leaves no residue. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus the two independent counts).

## CONCLUSION

Different parametrizations of the constants are identities or isomers, and the count question is partly notation (Q1). Dependency runs from measurement, through unbundling, to frame choice and pricing; a candidate named early is an echo (Q2). The constants form a union, so a single lever clears only one region (Q3). The partial mass relations and coupling convergence are functional groups, the landscape is a selection mechanism, and Eddington's number is refused (Q4). None of this derives or denies a common origin. It gives the shape such an answer would have and the order in which the checks must be made.
