# The Tether's answer: which insights genuinely transfer across domains?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts and the four-condition separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal standing list. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

The residual is not "do epidemics and finance look alike". It is `R(S -> T)` for each ordered pair of domains: the gap between an insight that pays in the source and what pays when it is applied in the target and checked against the target's own counter. Arity is a five-way invariant; symmetry is ordered (source to target); scale is second order over five first-order frames (Fig 10 :68). Fig 9 :55: *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The question is a union of method, habitat and ground conditions, not a partition into transferring and non-transferring lists.

## 2. The shape

1. **What crosses is a method, not a recording.** Fig 4 :22-23: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. / upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* The method is: name actors, currency and anchor; enumerate the habitat; describe the residual; price the lever at the target's counter. The levers themselves (immunise hubs, diversify, meter, cull, organise through weak ties) are one-occasion recordings (Fig 4 :25).

2. **A transfer must enter a reproduced habitat.** Fig 11 :39-40: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61); the target habitat's unchosen actors come with it (Fig 11 :66-69).

3. **The target's ground decides, per domain.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Five domains agreeing in vocabulary is one evidence pool, not a witness (Fig 2 :40). Verdicts are indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19) and never averaged (Fig 1 :22).

4. **An imported insight needs two gates and carries a debit.** Fig 8 :12-13: *"source gate: provenance"*, *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. Fig 6 :24: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame’s independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*

5. **No standing list.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The literal list is a declared `gap`, a verdict and not silence (Fig 13 :134).

6. **Shared ingredients are a functional group, and possibly isomers.** Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Over a full shared atom set the five domains become an isomer claim with a falsifier (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1), not a single substance. Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a catalogue of universal insights; no figure line returns one.
- Not that all five domains are the same system. They share a functional group of ingredients, not a settled bond arrangement.
- Not that a power law or the word "network" proves a common mechanism. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."*
- Not that a transfer failure proves impossibility. Fig 8 :39: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"*; unreachable-at-this-budget differs from underivable-in-principle.
- Not that cross-domain agreement is corroboration. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
- Not `grounded`. No counter was moved by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator at each domain's counter, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the answer is *"unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the operations are:

1. **Lever test:** apply one named lever with the same binding arrangement in all five domains, register it on each domain's own non-updating counter, and record sign and magnitude per slot. If the signs agree, the residual-relative valence claim is refuted.
2. **Method-versus-recording test:** repeat a transfer once with the generator and its provenance intact and once with the bare recording; the recording must fail where the generator pays.
3. **Channel test:** before blaming an insight's failure on the domain, check the counter's channel for saturated metrics and pooled readings (Fig 10 :26).
4. **Import test:** for each borrowed insight, require the source gate and the shadow test, and record the debit to the importer's independence.
5. **Standing refuters** from `01` §3: a named figure list, carried valence with no contact change, independent corroboration from a shared pool, a domain with no anchor, or a reach that grows without import.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The five-domain census uses the complexity vocabulary because that is the vocabulary available; it is a visible guess, not nature's list (*They're Scanning Books*, carried). The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer (Fig 9 :32), and stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, recording the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A figure re-census that returned the named list, or a per-domain measurement of the levers, would move this beyond the current stop. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a list of insights that transfer from epidemics to finance to traffic to ecosystems to social movements, and it does not treat the five as one system wearing five names. It says that what genuinely crosses is a re-applicable method, never the recording of a success in one habitat; that the target habitat and its own counter, not cross-domain agreement, decide whether the transfer pays; that a borrowed insight is an import needing a source gate, a shadow test and a debit; and that the same lever can help in one domain and harm in another because its valence is relative to the residual. The five share a functional group of ingredients and, over a fixed atom set, form a testable isomer claim, not a single substance. The literal list is a declared gap, a verdict rather than silence. What is offered is the four-condition separator, five written refuters, and the instruments that would close it.
