## 0. The claim under audit and the residual

### 0.1 The claim as posed

> Which insights about epidemics, financial crises, traffic, ecosystems, and social movements genuinely transfer across domains?

Read as an ordinary claim, this asks for a list: take an insight that works in epidemics, carry it into finance, traffic, ecology and social movements, and keep the ones that still work. The word "genuinely" is the load-bearing term. It cannot be settled by resemblance between domains; it asks what survives contact with each target's ground.

### 0.2 The residual stated in effect terms

For an ordered pair of domains (source S, target T), let `R(S -> T)` be the gap between an insight asserted and paid for in S and what actually pays when the same insight is applied in T and checked against T's own anchor. The question asks for the invariant over all five domains: what would still pay after being carried to any of them.

- **Arity:** this is a relation over domains, not a property of one. With five domains it is at least a pairwise invariant, and the question implicitly asks for a five-way common kernel. Symbols table :38-40: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*
- **Symmetry:** transfer is ordered (source to target), like the sequence bond `→` or an import, not the symmetric conjunction `+`. Operators table :29-33: sequence is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, with the test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Swapping source and target is a different claim, so "insights transfer" cannot be scored by a symmetric similarity coefficient.
- **Scale:** each domain is a first-order frame settled against its own ground; the transfer relation is second order (Fig 4 membrane, Fig 12 :104 settled-molecule rule). Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."*

### 0.3 Domain census before choosing any figure line

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet (Fig 2 :29-30).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 epidemics | pathogens, hosts, vectors, public-health seats | transmission events, R | case counts, contact-tracing records, outbreak data (yes) | one frame, own ground |
| D2 financial crises | banks, funds, regulators, counterparties | credit, exposure, capital | prices, defaults, clearing records (yes) | one frame, own ground |
| D3 traffic | drivers, vehicles, road network, signal controllers | flow, density, travel time | loop-detector counts, travel-time measurements (yes) | one frame, own ground |
| D4 ecosystems | species, resources, conditions, managers | biomass, energy, population counts | field censuses, abundance time series (yes) | one frame, own ground |
| D5 social movements | people, organisations, media platforms | attention, participation, recruitment | event participation and mobilisation records (yes) | one frame, own ground |
| D6 the transfer relation itself | candidate insights or methods, importer seats | whether an insight pays when re-grounded | the target domain's own ground, never agreement (yes) | union of conditions, not a partition |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** All five named domains have counters. But "complexity science" as a single generic frame does not: it has no instrument of its own, only the instruments of D1 to D5. So the question cannot be answered inside a generic "complex systems" frame. That is the first substantive result of the census.

**Partition or union.** The question presumes a partition: insights that transfer versus insights that do not. Fig 9 :55 refuses that: *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*, and :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Transfer is a union of at least three conditions, (a) the thing carried is a re-applicable method, not a one-occasion recording (Fig 4), (b) the target habitat reproduces what the insight required (Fig 11), (c) the target ground pays for it at its own anchor (Fig 2). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-57). **Verdict: union.**

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20).** *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The loop must (1) describe `R` in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), (2) enumerate the target habitat before importing (Fig 11 :43-44), (3) refuse before mint (FORMULA step 2), (4) settle each first-order domain before composing a transfer claim (Fig 12 :104), (5) keep the transfer verdict indexed per slot rather than pooled (Fig 1 :19). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

## 2. Findings

### F1 - What transfers is the kernel or method, never the recording (decisive)

Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

The canonical README (cited as README, not as a figure line): *"Domain-agnostic. What changes between domains and levels is only the cast, never the rules."* Fig 10 :68-69: *"a generator crosses, an instance does not"*.

Applied to the census: the method that crosses is *name the actors, the currency and the anchor, enumerate the habitat, describe the residual, then price the intervention against the target's ground*. The recordings that do not cross are the particular levers: "immunise the hubs", "diversify the portfolio", "metering", "cull", "organise through weak ties". Each is a description of one occasion in one habitat. Fig 13 :3: *"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."*

**Verdict (triangulated: Fig 4 and Fig 10, low overlap between the between-scale membrane and the between-level ground maintainer): `general` shape, `specified` for each domain.** Refuter: exhibit an insight that pays in a new domain with the binding arrangement dropped, i.e. as a bare recording, and with no re-derivation against the target's ground. If that is possible, F1 is false.

### F2 - Resemblance is not transfer; the residual must be described first

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. Fig 8 :19: *"closures differ, so a surplus exists"*, :21: *"nothing was aimed; the union is mute"*.

All five domains have been described with the word "contagion", a power law, or a network. That is a resemblance at the vocabulary level, which Fig 8 :12-13 marks as the collapse to vocabulary: *"source gate: provenance"*, *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. Two frames with high overlap are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49). The surplus exists only where closures differ and a residual has been aimed.

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* So a shared curve cannot tell us whether the same mechanism was reached independently or a name was borrowed. Without provenance the transfer claim carries zero evidentiary weight.

**Verdict (triangulated: Fig 8 and Fig 9): `specified`.** Refuter: show two domains whose agreement about a transfer claim is backed by independent instruments at low overlap, with the residual described before the frame was chosen and provenance recorded. That would establish a genuine witness.

### F3 - No standing list of transferable insights survives a change of residual (decisive against the literal question)

Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*.

The question asks for insights that transfer, i.e. for a standing list. Fig 11 :51 refuses the form of the question before any content is examined. The same intervention is benchmarked here:

| candidate lever | D1 epidemics | D2 finance | D5 social movements | why the sign flips |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| immunise / protect the highest-degree nodes | targeted immunisation is efficient where global degree is known (world source: Scale-free network) | "too connected to fail" protection is a systemic-risk target, but protecting one node can migrate the risk to a less regulated sector (world source: Systemic risk) | removing central accounts can slow both recruitment and counter-mobilisation | the residual changes: protect hosts, contain default, or suppress mobilisation |
| dense connectivity / many ties | raises the basic reproduction number; immunisation target | absorbs shocks below a tipping point, amplifies above it (world source: Systemic risk) | weak ties and small worlds spread simple contagions but **slow** complex contagions (world source: Complex contagion) | simple versus complex contagion is a different mechanism in the same network shape |
| diversify / spread exposure | strain diversity can escape a vaccine | diversification is ambiguous: shock-absorber below a threshold, shock-amplifier above | broad Coalition membership dilutes commitment and legitimacy | the currency differs (transmission, exposure, participation) |

Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A manager can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. So a lever that worked in one habitat cannot be exported by subtraction of the unwanted parts.

**Verdict (triangulated: Fig 11 plus three low-overlap world sources): `specified`, world-corroborated.** Refuter: a controlled cross-domain trial in which one lever pays with the same sign in all five domains against each domain's own non-updating anchor, with the habitat reproduced. That would falsify the residual-relative valence claim.

### F4 - The target's ground decides; agreement among domains is not a witness

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The five domains are usually said to "share the same insights" because the same complexity literature describes them. That is one evidence pool. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. What would constitute an anchor here? For D1 a case count from a contact-tracing record; for D2 a clearing/default record; for D3 a loop-detector count; for D4 a field census; for D5 a participation record. Each is a counter that does not move when a complexity theorist changes their mind. If the purported shared insight cannot be registered on any of these counters, no transfer has been claimed, only a metaphor.

Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."*

**Verdict (triangulated: Fig 2 and Fig 10): `specified`.** Refuter: a case where an insight is validated by cross-domain agreement alone, with no domain-specific counter, and where that agreement later survives an independent measurement in each domain.

### F5 - The residual is per slot; a global "transfer coefficient" destroys the signal

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

An answer of the form "complex systems insights transfer 60 percent of the time" aggregates D1 to D5 into a single number. By Fig 1 :22 that throws away the only signal that says where to look. The honest output is five separate verdicts plus the transfer-relation verdict, each with its own anchor. The exemplar's discipline applies: verdicts per domain, never averaged.

**Verdict (single-source on the figure side, Fig 1; corroborated by the per-domain world results): `general`.** Refuter: a measurement that assigns a single cross-domain transfer score and whose per-slot residuals all point in the same direction with the same magnitude. Then the aggregate would carry the signal.

### F6 - Borrowing an insight is an import: source gate, shadow test, and a debit

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :24: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame’s independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* Fig 8 :12-13: *"source gate: provenance"*, *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. Fig 6 :30: *"Cast it in effect terms."*

When epidemics lends "herd immunity" to finance, or ecology lends "resilience" to social movements, an atom crosses a closure boundary. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* So this is an import, not a mint inside the target. Two gates apply: where did the atom come from, and does it explain a residual the target actually has? A wrong atom costs more than rebuilding from the target's own primitives.

Fig 8 :39: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* So a failed transfer attempt in one domain does not prove the insight cannot work; it may be unreachable at this budget, which has a different instruction (Fig 6 :49-50).

**Verdict (triangulated: Fig 6 and Fig 8): `specified`.** Refuter: an import into a target domain that moved that domain's reach, with recorded provenance, a shadow test on a named residual, and no debit to the importer's independence. Then the standard import model is wrong.

### F7 - Check which link the target domain stops at, not how good the insight is

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

A transferred insight can only pay if the target chain reaches the link it needs. D5 social movements, for example, often has a vocabulary for participation and a measure of events, but no instrument for the objective that would count as progress toward a movement's goal. Adding a method from D1 above that break does not rescue it (Fig 3 :19). The order of the transfer test is therefore: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :4-17), and each link must be read before a lever is borrowed.

**Verdict (triangulated: Fig 3 and Fig 12): `general`.** Refuter: a domain whose transfer fails even though every link from perception to learn-and-carry has an instrument and was measured. Then the dependency-chain ordering does not govern transfer.

### F8 - Five domains with shared ingredients are an isomer or functional-group case, not a shared substance

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*

Each domain holds the same coarse ingredients: a network, a gradient of some currency, a threshold, a cascade, an intervention seat. Under Fig 12 these ingredients are a functional group, *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*. The differences between D1 and D2 are carried by the bond: in D1 a contact transmits a pathogen; in D2 a exposure transmits a liability. Same atom, different typed relation, different substance. Over a fixed shared atom set the domains can be read as isomers, an claim developed with its falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* and *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* A shared vocabulary is cheap in bits and may be expensive in actions; the two readings come apart, so a qualitative resemblance cannot stand in for a measured transfer.

**Verdict (single-source for the figure definition, Fig 12; world corroboration for the sign flips, not for the bond typing): `specified`.** Refuter: two domains with the same atom set and the same bond arrangement whose behaviour nonetheless differs, with the difference measured at the ground. Then the bond does not carry the difference.

### F9 - The literal list is a `gap`: a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*

No figure line enumerates which named insight transfers to which of the five domains, and Fig 11 :51 structurally refuses a standing list. What the figures return is the shape any genuine transfer must have: method not recording, reproduced habitat, non-updating anchor, provenance and shadow test, per-slot verdict. The literal list is therefore a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind. This is a verdict, not an omission or an invitation to invent a catalogue.

**Note on the correction.** The canonical README's phrase is *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. It governs a primitive not held in the library. Naming an already-available binding, such as "network" or "threshold", is provenance, not the invention of a seventh primitive. See `05_what_shape_means.md` §5b and `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1. The `gap` here is about the list of *levers*, not about naming atoms already held.

### F10 - Filters are for the budget; witnesses are for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 1 :24-27: *"Everything else either produces a residual or consumes the library. These do not count: coverage, and compression achieved predicates minted, and credibility accrued."*

A cross-domain citation count, the number of fields in which a concept appears, or how widely a term is used are filters for where to spend attention. They do not decide whether an insight transfers. Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success."* The popularity of "network" across five domains is a count of the denominator.

**Verdict (single-source, Fig 9, corroborated by Fig 1): `general`.** Refuter: a filter whose value determines a per-domain verdict with no measurement at that domain's anchor. Then the filter has become a witness and F10 is false.

### F11 - When transfer fails, suspect the channel, not the ground

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 10 :28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is / you have lost touch"*. Fig 10 :29: *"→ keep the anchor reachable"*.

This gives a specific failure mode for transfer. The target's counter did not stop functioning; the path from the domain to that counter was saturated or pooled. Pooled readings are exactly what a cross-domain average produces (F5). The remedy is to keep the anchor reachable per slot, not to replace the insight with a newer one.

**Verdict (single-source, Fig 10): `specified`.** Refuter: a domain whose ground demonstrably changed while the channel and the insight stayed fixed, and whose transfer failure is attributable to the ground. Then the channel diagnosis is not universal.

### F12 - Contact cascades: the relevant effect is often three steps out

Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :44-45: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

The question's five domains are all cases where an intervention in one part of a habitat registers elsewhere. A transfer test scoped to direct contact will miss the effect that matters. This is why F3's lever table compares only first-order targets; a full enumeration would need the cascade, and that is a budget question (Fig 9 :60).

**Verdict (single-source, Fig 11): `general`.** Refuter: a stable transfer in which all effects terminate at first-order contact across all five domains, with second-order effects measured and absent.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat-map rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **A named standing list.** A figure line is found that enumerates which named insights transfer to which of the five domains, or a cross-domain trial shows one lever paying with the same sign and magnitude in all five domains against each domain's own non-updating anchor.
2. **Carried valence.** A lever pays in a new habitat with no change in contact and without re-grounding (breaks Fig 11 :40, :49-51).
3. **Agreement as witness.** Two domains with demonstrably shared evidence are shown to corroborate a verdict about the world, and the verdict survives an independent instrument (breaks Fig 2 :40).
4. **No anchor.** One of the five domains turns out to have no nameable counter that fails to move when a frame pushes on it; it is then not a domain and the census must be redone (Fig 2 :29-30).
5. **Reachable without import.** An insight transfers purely by composition inside the target's closure, adding no atom, and the target's reach demonstrably grows (breaks Fig 6 :21, :23).
6. **The gap closes.** A figure re-census yields a line that names the missing cross-domain term, converting the `gap` into a name. Until one fires, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Do not answer with a list. Answer with the separator (F1, F3, F9) and keep five verdicts plus the transfer-relation verdict separate (F5).
2. Describe the residual in effect terms before naming any candidate domain (Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise any of the five frames will seem to fit (F2).
3. Treat every borrowed insight as an import with a source gate and a shadow test (F6); debit the importer's independence.
4. Ask which link the target domain stops at before transferring a method (F7). Check the channel before blaming the ground (F11).
5. Enumerate the habitat outward; a first-order scope misses the effect that matters (F12).
6. Send the four-condition separator and the pre-registered refuter, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

Here the search is not for more domains to compare; the target ground has to be reached. INWARD is a better instrument in the target domain; OUTWARD is a provenanced import. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts.

The word "genuinely" is treated as the ground test (Fig 2 :29), not as a rhetorical intensifier. "Transfer" is treated as ordered (Operators table :29-33), not symmetric.

### Final audit verdict

The shape survives: a genuine transfer is a method, not a recording; it crosses into a reproduced habitat through a channel to a non-updating target ground, with provenance, a shadow test and per-slot verdicts. The literal list does not; it is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, and Fig 11 :51 refuses its form. Strains after re-census: one live (the physical six-term substrate applied to social and financial domains, narrowed to a visible guess and held as an arrangement), one live (whether held atoms count as the same substance across domains requires a measured bond, not a shared word). Both are named in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `11_audit.md`, neither smoothed over.
