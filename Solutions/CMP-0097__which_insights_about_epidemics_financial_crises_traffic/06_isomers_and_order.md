## RESIDUAL

The five domains share a conspicuous ingredient list: nodes and links, a difference in some currency, a threshold, a cascade, an intervention seat. The question is whether this makes them the same kind of thing (a transferable insight follows), or whether the ingredient list hides a bond difference that decides the behaviour. Two questions are owed:

2. What is the **order** of a transfer, and which operators may carry it?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the five domains isomers? (with falsifier)

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- As the domains are ordinarily described, they do **not** share one atom set. D1 has pathogen and host; D2 has exposure and liability; D3 has vehicle and flow; D4 has species and resource; D5 has person and participation. By Fig 12's definition they are **not** isomers; they are members of a **functional group**, a `network with a spendable difference and a threshold`, with different atoms in the slots. *"The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The stronger form: if all five are rewritten over the **full shared atom set** `{network, gradient, spectrum, threshold, cascade, seat}` and differ only in which bond connects which pair, then they **are** isomers in the figure's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, and it is `specified`, not settled.
- Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* So a shared curve does not decide the case either way; two different bond arrangements can compose functionally identical terms, and the converse also holds.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any two domains can be written with the same full atom set **and the same bond arrangement** while their measured behaviour at their own anchors still differs, the isomer framing fails and the functional-group reading stands as the whole story. Equally, if a domain requires an atom none of the others holds and that atom is load-bearing in the transfer, the isomer claim fails on the atom set rather than the bond.

### Q2 - What order is a transfer?

- Transfer is ordered. Operators table :29-33: sequence is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, with test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Carrying D1 into D2 is not carrying D2 into D1; the same atom arrives into a different closure.
- The correct order of operations is the dependency chain of Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Concretely: describe the residual (Fig 9 :27-28), enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43-44), assess which link the target stops at (Fig 3 :27), import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :12-13), and only then compose the transfer claim.
- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A transfer plan is a sequence; a target's goal is a comparison between what it has and what it needs; the improvement is a subtraction of residual. A vocabulary with only ingredients can name a shared concept and cannot name a route from one domain to another.

### Q3 - Which operator carries the crossing?

- A mint composes inside the target closure and adds no atom: Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* So a transfer that merely renames an existing target term is not a transfer of an insight.
- An import adds an atom: Fig 8 :12-13 *"source gate: provenance"*, *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. Fig 6 :24: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame’s independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*
- A generator crosses; an instance does not (Fig 10 :68-69 *"a generator crosses, an instance does not"*). So the carried object is the method that can be re-applied, not the recording of the success in the source domain.
- **Refusal is available and is not a bond.** Fig 12 amendment: *"Without a unary operator a system can write what a situation contains, what follows what, and how far along it is - and it cannot write that any of those is false."* A transfer claim has to be refusable; the four refuter conditions in `01` §3 are the unary operator in use.

### Q4 - Two currencies, and why a shared word is cheap

- Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* / *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- "Network" is cheap in bits across all five domains and may cost a full budget to settle in any one of them. Fig 12 :128-129: *"The two readings come apart in both directions."* So a concept's cross-domain frequency is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*), never a witness for transfer.
- World corroboration for the price split: the arc-welding study's selected spectral bands were cheap to state once chosen and expensive to re-establish when material and process changed (Garcia-Allende et al. 2009, opened and quoted in `10_source_ledger.csv`).

### Q5 - Order versus recursion: what settles a transferred term?

- Fig 12 :103-104: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* / *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*.
- A domain that has settled its own terms can serve as an atom for the cross-domain composition. This is why the transfer relation is second order and why the five first-order verdicts must be settled separately before any cross-domain claim (Fig 1 :19).
- Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A transfer that only rearranges the vocabulary above the chain's break has not moved the ground (Fig 3 :19).

## CONCLUSION

The five domains are **not** a single substance distinguished by degree. As ordinarily described they are a **functional group** over different atoms; rewritten over the full shared atom set they become a genuine **isomer claim** with a stated falsifier, which is the strongest checkable structural form. Transfer is **ordered** (source to target), proceeds by the Fig 3 dependency order, and is carried by an import with two gates and a debit, not by a mint inside the target. The shared word is cheap in bits and expensive in actions, so it is a budget filter and not a witness. The isomer claim needs a bond, not a resemblance; the bond is currently a `specified` candidate, not a measurement.

This rescues what is defensible in the ordinary "complex systems insights transfer" claim without the part that fails: the transferable object is a generator with a recorded route, and the test is per-domain and per-slot. It does not produce the standing list the question asked for; that remains a `gap` (Fig 13 :134).
