# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are on the page. The offer collapses on contact if the output cannot survive checking.

---

**Subject:** Check one per-place claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in permafrost carbon research: how thawing ground releases CO2 and methane, and how those releases vary by place. I have one specific `specified` output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a single global release number is the wrong answer shape for the "by place" half of this question. The residual is indexed by place, gas, pathway and time. Aggregating across places is how the live signal disappears into an average. So a complete answer must have four parts, in this dependency order:
>
> 1. an enumerated list of the conditions in contact with the frozen carbon at each place (temperature, ground ice, water saturation, soil, topography, vegetation, snow, and the second-order effects of fire and coastal erosion), with no condition assigned a fixed sign in advance;
> 2. a thaw pathway that treats gradual active-layer deepening and abrupt thermokarst as overlapping conditions, not two items on a list;
> 3. a production step in which microbes make CO2 where oxygen is available and methane where it is not. The gas did not exist before that step fired;
> 4. a ground reading per place (chamber or tower fluxes, thaw depth, isotopic source signatures) that settles magnitude, timing and the CO2/CH4 split.
>
> That is a shape and an order, not a forecast. I am explicitly **not** giving you a PgC number. The figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself. The published range for CO2 release, 3 to 41 PgC per degree of warming by 2100, is world evidence with low confidence in timing and in the CO2 versus CH4 split. It is not a result this derivation produced.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this framework settles how much carbon will be released, that a model ensemble is an anchor, that one emission factor works across places, or that the framework can name which condition dominates at a given site. Agreement among models that draw on the same measurements is one evidence pool, not independent confirmation. The anchor is the measurement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a single global flux closes the residual and every per-place residual is near zero;
> 2. at a given place, CO2 and CH4 are governed by one redox condition with no overlap, or gradual and abrupt thaw never co-occur;
> 3. measured per-place flux is independent of the enumerated contact conditions;
> 4. the gas released is already free before thaw, so no production step fires.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public permafrost carbon assessment, for the confidence language and the numbers I am declining to reuse: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-5/
> - NSIDC overview of the oxygen/no-oxygen switch and the place dependence: https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/frozen-ground-permafrost/why-frozen-ground-matters
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Records to keep on the receiving side

1. The stage-1 verdict, in their words, **before** any theory is shown.
2. Which of the four refuters they think is most likely to fire, and why.
3. Whether they name an instrument that would discriminate CO2 from CH4 sources at a place (isotopic or flux-based), or say why none exists.
4. Their later agreement, if any, filed separately and never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
