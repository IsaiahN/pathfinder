## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question: how thawing permafrost will release carbon dioxide and methane, and how those releases will vary by place. Option A: report a global release estimate with a regional breakdown and treat that as the answer. Option B: report the shape a complete answer must have, unbundle the overlapping conditions, state the per-place grounds that would settle magnitude and timing, and mark the quantitative slots `gap`. What each leads to: A answers a different question (how much in total) and averages away the live per-place signal (Fig 1 :22); B answers the question as asked, keeps verdicts separate, and remains checkable because its refuters were written in advance.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a forecast of gas release and the flux the ground returns. Arity: place x gas x pathway x time, at least four indexed slots. Symmetry: CO2 and CH4 are not interchangeable, different redox origin, different atmospheric lifetime and forcing. Scale: microbe, soil profile, landscape, region, globe, with a lossy transform between scales. Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A global number is a legal quantity but not the quantity this question asks for.
- **Domain 1, ground thermal state and frozen carbon stock.** Actors: frozen organic matter, ground ice, mineral soil, active layer. Currency: carbon mass and heat. Anchor: boreholes, active-layer thickness, carbon inventories. Question type: partition boundary of a stock.
- **Domain 2, thaw pathway.** Actors: gradual active-layer deepening, thermokarst and abrupt thaw, ice-rich yedoma, hydrology. Currency: phase-change energy, landscape change. Anchor: measured thaw depth, thermokarst maps. Question type: **union** compound, not a list.
- **Domain 3, microbial gas production.** Actors: microbes, oxygen availability, water saturation. Currency: carbon transformed into gas. Anchor: chamber and tower fluxes, isotopic source signatures. Question type: production bond (⇒).
- **Domain 4, spatial variation.** Actors: local climate, soil type, ground-ice content, topography, vegetation, snow, drainage, plus second-order fire and coastal erosion. Currency: valence of each actor relative to R. Anchor: per-place flux measurements. Question type: habitat enumeration.
- **Domain 5, forecast verification.** Actors: flux towers, chambers, remote sensing, models, assessment panels. Currency: residual between prediction and observation. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: triangulation.
- **Partition or union.** D1 to D3 form a dependency chain. D2 and D3 each contain a union of overlapping conditions (oxic and anoxic microsites; gradual and abrupt thaw). Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Order by dependency.** Place-level perception and vocabulary before a place-level objective; thaw before substrate availability before gas production before verification. Fig 3 :20 - *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

Strain test: two candidate strains were found and dissolved by re-census. The thermal-gradient reading is a binding from the physics frame onto a held atom, not a derivation of thaw rate. IPCC's abrupt-thaw uncertainty governs magnitude, while Fig 3 governs dependency order. Different objects, so no strain remains after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the global number does not. Per place, the habitat is enumerated, the two gases and the two thaw pathways are unbundled as overlapping conditions, gas generation is a production event, the feedback closes with a positive sign, and the per-place measurement is the anchor. A single global flux averages away the live signal and answers the wrong half of the question. Magnitude, timing and regional ranking are `gap` at this budget: no figure line governs them, and the published range is world corroboration with a source gate, not a figure result. This is `specified` and ungrounded. The strongest figure-grounded point is D4, triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 11, because it is the part that answers "vary by place" directly.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, keeps per-domain verdicts separate, and carries five refuters written before the run, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a global number or a theory before the stage-1 verdict. Fig 2 :42 prices that: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* It is avoidable by leading with the per-place claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four figure-derived shape findings can be revised or withdrawn without touching the world sources or the ground data. No primitive is withdrawn; F8's binding is recorded with provenance and can be re-cast if a better instrument discriminates the source of a flux reading. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A chamber or tower record showing per-place residuals near zero and one global number closing the residual would break D4. Isotopic and flux discrimination showing one redox condition governing both gases would break D2 and D3. Field evidence that gradual and abrupt thaw do not co-occur, or that they require different atom sets, would break the union and isomer claims. Evidence that a model ensemble updates on disjoint measurements and still fails to track withheld flux observations would be convergence at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50) and would revise D5. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
