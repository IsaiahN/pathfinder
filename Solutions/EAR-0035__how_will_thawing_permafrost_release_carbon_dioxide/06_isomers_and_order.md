## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond makes the gas: production, sequence or subtraction?

Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** (production, ⇒). Fig 12 :74: sequence is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (→). Fig 12 :82: subtraction is *"A with B removed or absent"* (−).

The CO2 and CH4 molecules released from old soil carbon were not waiting in the ground as free gas. They appear when microbial metabolism fires. The second operand is absent before the first fires, which is the test for ⇒ in the Operators table: *"is there a B before A fires? No."* So the bond is **production**, not sequence.

A sequence reading would imply the gas already exists and is merely released later, which is the free-gas or clathrate case. That case exists in the world (NSIDC discusses subsea methane clathrates) but it is a **different residual** and must be split off under Fig 9 :54 before the search continues.

**Falsifier Q1:** if, at a site, the dominant released gas is demonstrably already free before thaw and the microbial production step contributes negligibly, ⇒ is the wrong bond there and the finding is withdrawn for that site.

### Q2 - Union or partition for CO2 and CH4?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Oxic and anoxic conditions overlap in one soil profile. NSIDC *Why Frozen Ground Matters* states the switch: *"If there is oxygen available, the microbes make carbon dioxide. But if there is no oxygen available, they make methane."* The same place can produce both. Gradual active-layer deepening and abrupt thermokarst also overlap in one landscape. So the split is a **union of two overlapping conditions**, not a partition into two populations of sites.

The practical consequence: a single lever (one emission factor per degree) clears the one-condition region and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :55). The union needs its own loop and its own ground.

**Falsifier Q2:** if a field campaign finds that oxic and anoxic production never co-occur at any sampled place, and that gradual and abrupt thaw never co-occur, the union collapses to a partition and this finding is withdrawn.

### Q3 - What is the order, and where is the break likely to be?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

The chain for this residual:

1. perception: boreholes, active-layer probes, flux chambers at a place;
2. vocabulary: terms for what each place affords (ice-rich soil, drainage, oxygen availability, microtopography);
3. objective: what counts as a release at that place, in the place's own terms;
4. planning: which thaw pathway and which production bond apply;
5. learn and carry: name what worked, verify it against a fresh ground reading at another place.

Fig 3 :25-27 names link 2 to 3 as the usual break: having terms for what a situation affords and saying what counts as progress there have neither obvious failures nor obvious instruments. The NOAA Arctic Report Card shows that links 1 and 5 are well instrumented (GTN-P boreholes, CALM active-layer grids), while the per-place objective (link 3) is where an emission factor silently substitutes for an observation.

**Falsifier Q3:** if a program improves its emission factor (above the break) and the per-place residual drops with it, then the break is not where this derivation says it is. The census must be redone.

### Q4 - Which Fig 12 structures actually apply?

Fig 12 :117 defines **isomer**: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Gradual thaw and abrupt thermokarst can be written over the same atom set (frozen carbon, ground ice, heat, microbes, water) with a different arrangement: conduction through an active layer versus collapse and exposure of previously frozen material. As written, they are a real isomer claim, with a stated falsifier below.

Fig 12 :124 defines **functional group**: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The production motif (organic carbon, microbes, oxygen availability, gas) is a functional group repeated across places. The qualifier (oxic or anoxic) says which member. This is the weaker and safer reading.

Fig 12 :121 defines **cycle**: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** The permafrost carbon feedback is explicitly a cycle: released CO2 and CH4 warm the atmosphere, which warms the ground, which thaws more carbon. The sign is positive, so the loop closes and amplifies. NSIDC states it: *"the increased abundance of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere will only exacerbate warming and continue permafrost thaw."* A chain representation (thaw -> gas -> atmosphere) cannot capture this; a cycle can.

Fig 12 :119 defines **catalyst**: *"A part that survives its own use."* Microbial populations are regenerating rather than spent; they persist across uses. This matters because a one-shot reagent model would misstate the production step. Recorded as `specified`, unmeasured at this budget.

**Falsifier Q4:** if abrupt thaw requires an atom absent from gradual thaw (a distinct heat source or a distinct substrate class that the gradual case does not contain), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. If released carbon does not re-enter as ground warming at a site (loop sign zero or negative), the cycle claim fails there.

### Q5 - Two currencies, read separately

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**

A global emission factor is cheap in bits (one number) and expensive in actions (a whole campaign to settle whether it holds at a place). A per-place table is expensive in bits and may cost one chamber measurement to refute at a site. Fig 12 :128: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."* Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read. The choice cannot be made from description cost alone.

## CONCLUSION

The gas-generation bond is production (⇒), with the free-gas case split off as a different residual. The two gases and the two thaw pathways are a union compound, not a partition. The dependency order runs from place-level perception to a place-level objective before planning, and the likely break is at the vocabulary-to-objective link. Over Fig 12's structures, gradual and abrupt thaw support a real isomer claim with a falsifier, the production motif is a functional group, the feedback is a positive-sign cycle, and microbes behave as a regenerating part. The isomer and cycle claims are `specified` and ungrounded. The falsifiers in Q1 to Q4 were written before this conclusion.
