# Expert verification packet - the Tether on permafrost carbon release

**What this is:** a claim in a form a permafrost carbon researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** No flux measurement, thaw rate or methane yield is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The residual in this question is indexed by place, gas, pathway and time, so a single global release number is the wrong answer shape for the "by place" half. A complete answer must enumerate the conditions in contact with the frozen carbon at each place, with no condition assigned a fixed sign in advance; treat gradual and abrupt thaw, and oxic and anoxic gas production, as overlapping conditions that must be unbundled; represent gas generation as a production event rather than a delayed release; and settle magnitude, timing and the CO2/CH4 split with a ground reading per place. The feedback closes as a positive-sign cycle. The anchor is the measurement, never agreement among models that share an evidence pool. The figures return that shape and its dependency order. They return no number, and the magnitude and timing slots are reported as `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** the "by place" half cannot be answered by a global average. The residual is per-slot and the habitat is enumerated, never composed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 - **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 11 :43 - **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :49 - **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
- **Triangulation:** two figures with low overlap. Fig 1 is about measurement indexing, Fig 11 about contact topology. Both give the same verdict.
- **What to check:** does a single global flux close the residual at any site you know, or does the live signal sit in the per-place differences? Does any habitat condition carry a fixed sign across sites?
- **Refuter:** a forecast in which one global number closes the residual and every per-place residual is near zero; or measured flux independent of the enumerated conditions; or a condition whose valence does not change with the residual.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable by a researcher against field and process knowledge

- **Statement:** gas generation is a production event (⇒), not a sequence or a delayed release, and CO2 versus CH4 is a union of overlapping oxic and anoxic conditions, not a partition of sites.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :77 - **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**. Operators table :37, same line. Fig 9 :55 - **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."** Fig 9 :57 - **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- **What to check:** at a site you know, do oxic and anoxic production overlap in one soil profile? Do gradual active-layer deepening and abrupt thermokarst co-occur in one landscape? Is the released gas newly produced from old carbon, or is a pre-existing free-gas reservoir dominant?
- **Refuter:** one redox condition governs both gases with no overlap, or gradual and abrupt thaw never co-occur (union fails); or the gas is already free before thaw and microbial production is negligible at that site (production bond fails).

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about verification

- **Statement:** the anchor is the per-place measurement. Agreement among models sharing calibration data and process assumptions is one evidence pool, not independent corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :40 - **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** Fig 9 :42 - **"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"** Fig 7 :13 - **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
- **What to check:** do the models in a published ensemble use disjoint measurements, or the same calibration set and the same abrupt-thaw parameterisation? IPCC AR6 WGI Ch5 states: *"Thawing terrestrial permafrost will lead to carbon release (high confidence), but there is low confidence in the timing, magnitude and the relative roles of CO2 versus CH4 as feedback processes."* Is that low confidence a report of a weak ground, or of agreement inside one pool?
- **Refuter:** an ensemble whose members update on disjoint measurements and whose agreement still fails to track withheld flux observations. That would be convergence at maximum independence, which Fig 8 :50 allows, but it would need the provenance record.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a forecast. No PgC value, thaw date, methane yield or regional ranking is asserted by the figures. The IPCC range is world corroboration with a source gate, not a figure result.
- Not `grounded`. No chamber, borehole, tower or isotopic reading was taken in this derivation.
- Not a claim that any place dominates or that any condition has a fixed sign. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not a claim that a model ensemble or a review panel is an anchor (C3).
- Not a claim that the six-term substrate derives soil physics. The thermal-gradient mapping is a binding from the physics frame onto a held atom, recorded with provenance in F8 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, and it adds no atom (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*).
- Not a theory of everything, and not a final answer. The magnitude and timing slots remain `gap`.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Per-place flux:** a chamber or tower record showing whether per-place residuals carry live mass or one global number closes the residual.
2. **Redox discrimination:** isotopic and flux measurements that separate old-carbon CO2 from old-carbon CH4 at the same place, and test whether the two overlap.
3. **Pathway discrimination:** field evidence on whether gradual and abrupt thaw co-occur and share an atom set.
4. **Cycle sign:** evidence on whether released carbon re-enters as ground warming at the site, closing the loop with a positive sign.
5. **Habitat test:** whether per-place flux is independent of the enumerated contact conditions.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 11 :43, :49 (four short lines). Ask whether the per-place claim matches your field experience.
2. Check C2 against your own sites: production versus delayed release; overlap versus partition.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate CO2 from CH4 sources at a place, or say why none is available.
4. Check whether the published ensemble you know uses independent measurements or one shared pool.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Records to keep:** the stage-1 verdict in the recipient's words, before any framework material is shown. Later agreement is filed separately and is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
