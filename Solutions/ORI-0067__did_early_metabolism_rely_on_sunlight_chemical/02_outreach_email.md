# Outreach draft - check the shape before you decide about the history

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter below is checked for internal consistency. If the shape claim fails, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide which early energy source mattered

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in early energy sources research: did the earliest self-maintaining chemical systems rely on sunlight, on chemical reactions, or on several sources? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape):** the figures require that any usable energy source present a *spendable gradient*. Energy by itself is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (the Symbols table), and a gradient is *"a difference that can be spent"* (Fig 13 :46). Separately, the energy source is a *habitat condition*, and the habitat is *"enumerated, never composed"* (Fig 11 :43). Light is one item in that enumeration, sitting beside water, temperature, terrain, and the redox couple. The figures do not rank those items.
>
> The second output is about the question's form. The three options are not three members of one list. They are a **union of overlapping conditions**: photothioautotrophs use light *and* a chemical electron donor, and there is a published proposal that the first light-driven metabolism arose at hydrothermal vents under low-intensity geothermal light. Fig 9 :55 says *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* So "multiple sources" is not a third peer of the other two; it is a re-description of the question as a compound with its own loop.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures tell us which source came first. They return a shape, not a name: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). The historical reading in my notes is `specified`, not `grounded`: geochemical redox gradients (H2/CO2, serpentinisation, natural pH gradients) appear to precede biological phototrophy, with light entering first as a habitat condition and later as a reaction centre, and atmospheric photochemistry feeding H2 back into surface ecosystems. That is a world reconstruction with provenance, not a figure verdict. If it conflicts with your reading of the rock or genome record, I want the conflict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before I sent this):**
> 1. a clean partition, with no earliest system coupling two sources and no overlap between the light and chemical conditions;
> 2. a self-maintaining system that spends free energy with no gradient of any kind;
> 3. a figure line that ranks habitat conditions by energetic contribution;
> 4. an instrument that dates the first light coupling before the first redox coupling at the ground.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The eight-step formula: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a report of failure is more useful to me than a report of success.
