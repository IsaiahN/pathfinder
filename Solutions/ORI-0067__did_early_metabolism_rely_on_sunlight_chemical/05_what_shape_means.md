**Mode: `general`.** Sources read this session: the canonical figures README (local copy), the verbatim figure text (local), and two of the eight essays opened directly: *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (Isaiah N, 19 Nov 2026) and *The Library of Babel: Locating the Book of Books* (Isaiah N, 2 Oct 2026). ## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for ORI-0067 a **shape** is: *any usable energy source must present a spendable gradient across a boundary, and the candidate sources are habitat conditions to be enumerated, not ranked.* That constrains the answer. It does not fill in which source won on early Earth. The distinction matters here because the question invites a name (sunlight, or a vent) where the figures only license a constraint.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* For this question the starting vocabulary is "energy", "gradient", "light", "redox", "habitat", all already in the figure set and the question text.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* So describe the effect: a difference that can be spent, across a boundary, continuously. Do not begin by naming serpentinisation or photosystem II.
- *Library of Babel* on the bundle: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is the exact structural finding in `01` F1. The shape includes a union/partition judgement before any source name.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"* and *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here the finish is a constraint (spendable gradient, enumerated habitat, union), not a date.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For this question, a source-discriminating isotope or petrographic result would name the source.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and shadow test. The vent/H2 geochemistry papers are such imports. They are recorded with provenance and overlap in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

Until one of those fires, "H2/CO2 at alkaline vents" and "sunlight in the photic zone" are bindings supplied by the world frame, not derivations from the six-term set. Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

## 4. Why the "which one source" phrasing is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (listed in the exemplar, not re-opened this session) gives the general mechanism: a reading does not determine its source; several sources can produce the same reading, and an instrument that cannot distinguish them demonstrates its own inadequacy. Applied here: the presence of carbon isotope fractionation in ancient rocks is a reading compatible with several primary producers. Naming the source from that reading alone is the metamerism error. The remedy is a better instrument, not a name.

The figures say the same in their own vocabulary. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* A claim that "the figures showed chemical reactions won" erases the record and converts an import into a derivation.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid saying anything about energy sources at all?

No. It forbids naming a *missing* primitive as if the framework had produced it. The constraint is a shape: a spendable gradient, an enumerated habitat, a union. The world frame then supplies names with provenance. Two records stay distinct:

| record | content | mode |
|---|---|---|
| figure record | gradient required; light is one condition; options form a union | `general` |
| world record | H2/CO2 redox and natural gradients appear first; phototrophy later, possibly under vent geothermal light; photochemistry recycles H2/HCN | `specified`, ungrounded |

Collapsing the second row into the first is the error. Keeping them separate is the whole point of the WATSON ordering (figures primary, world secondary).

## 6. Two consequences for this folder

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* It adds: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* So `01` is checked for internal consistency against the figures and cannot settle the question it reports on. This must be stated on every substantive claim.

**Prestige is not action.** Same essay: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous origin-of-life researchers who agree with the chemical-first narrative is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the refuter against a ground.
