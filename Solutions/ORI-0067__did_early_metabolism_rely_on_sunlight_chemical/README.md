## The question restated in effect terms

Given the energy-conversion machinery of the earliest self-maintaining chemical systems, which external free-energy source or sources did those systems couple to, and what does each candidate answer commit us to?

- **Sunlight (phototrophy).** Commits to a photic habitat, to pigment reaction centres appearing very early, and to a surface or shallow-water setting.
- **Chemical reactions (chemolithotrophy).** Commits to a redox couple such as H2/CO2, to natural pH or redox gradients, and to a vent or crustal setting.
- **Multiple sources.** Commits to a union of overlapping conditions with its own loop, not to a third item on the same list. Fig 9 :54 names this case: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

What an answer would look like: a shape stating what any energy source must supply before it can drive self-maintenance, plus a per-source verdict kept separate, plus the ground that would decide which source actually operated first. That shape, bound as an ordinary term, is the tether for this question (formula step "Direction").
