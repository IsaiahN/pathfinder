## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are sunlight and chemical redox isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

- Under Fig 12's definition, two entries are isomers only if they share an atom set and differ in the bond. The two energy options as named do not share an atom set: one has photons and a pigment, the other has H2, CO2 and a mineral catalyst. They are **not isomers** as named.
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The shared substructure here is *a source that supplies a spendable gradient*. Both options are members of that functional group. The group says what kind (gradient supply); the qualifier says which member (light-driven or redox-driven).
- The stronger form: if both are written over the shared atom set {energy, gradient, habitat boundary}, differing only in which structure delivers the gradient, then they are isomers in the figure's sense. That is a checkable structural claim, not the claim as the question writes it.

**Falsifier (written before the run):** if one option can be written without an atom the other requires, with no shared gradient substructure, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading stands alone. Current world sources already indicate a shared gradient substructure (both make a proton motive force), but the figure derivation does not depend on that.

### Q2 - Union or partition, and which bond expresses it?

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The question implicitly writes a symmetric disjunction: sunlight `∥` chemical redox, with "multiple sources" as an afterthought. Fig 12 :61-62 defines `∥` as *"either suffices; not both required"*, test: *"remove one. Does it still work? Yes."* That is wrong for the early-Earth case, because photothioautotrophs use light **and** H2S, and vent phototrophy proposals put light **inside** a chemically powered habitat.

Fig 12 :111-112: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** The trichotomy names states. What the question wants is a route, which needs an ordered bond. Fig 12 :70-72: `→` is sequence, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, and the test is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* A chemical-first reading is `chemical → light`; a photosynthesis-first reading is `light → chemical`. These are different things, so the order is not cosmetic.

**The figure does not supply the order.** It supplies the requirement that an order exists and that the members overlap. The order itself is a world claim, recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` as `specified`. Fig 12 :115: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."*

**Falsifier:** if the two conditions are proved disjoint, with no system ever coupling both and no shared gradient substructure, then the symmetric disjunction is restored and the union reading fails.

### Q3 - Why the source must be continuous: cycle, not chain

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A self-maintaining system is a cycle. A one-off pulse of energy cannot close a cycle; the source must keep arriving. This is why the figure places continuity in the shape, ahead of the name. Sousa et al. (2013) report that serpentinisation *"has probably been taking place since there was liquid water on the Earth"*, and Schwander et al. (2023) note that vent discharge can persist for tens of thousands of years. Those are world confirmations of continuity, not figure outputs.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate a self-maintaining chemical cycle driven by a single finite energy pulse whose output re-enters indefinitely. That would break the cycle requirement.

### Q4 - Order by strata, not by importance

Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."** Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Four strata are in play: prebiotic synthesis, the first self-maintaining system, LUCA, and the first phototroph. A settled result at one stratum becomes an atom at the next. Moody et al. (2024) reconstruct LUCA as *"a prokaryote-grade anaerobic acetogen"* at ~4.2 Ga already embedded in an ecosystem. That is a stratum above the origin, so it cannot by itself settle the pre-LUCA coupling. Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

**Falsifier:** show that a reconstruction at a later stratum determines a coupling at an earlier one without an import. That collapses the level distinction and breaks Q4.

## CONCLUSION

The three options are a functional group of gradient suppliers, not isomers as named and not a symmetric disjunction. The question's structure is a union of overlapping conditions whose members need an ordered bond to express a historical route. The figures supply the gradient requirement, the overlap warning, the cycle requirement, and the level rule. They do **not** supply the order. The chemical-first order is a `specified` world reading with stated provenance and stated overlap; phototrophy at vents under geothermal light and photochemical H2 recycling are the reasons the union cannot be flattened to a single source.
