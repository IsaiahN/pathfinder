# The Tether's answer on early energy sources

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the historical reading, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which energy source early metabolism relied on. The residual is the gap between what the rock and genome record return and what we can say about the source coupled to the earliest self-maintaining systems. Slots: (s1) gradient available, (s2) coupling without modern enzymes and membranes, (s3) temporal order of adoption, (s4) the instrument that discriminates sources. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

## 2. The shape (one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate)

1. **Any usable source must present a spendable gradient.** Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."* Energy alone is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (Fig 13 :13-14). Light can make a gradient in a pigment; serpentinisation can make one across a vent wall. The requirement does not pick a winner. **Domain D1, triangulated** (Fig 13 plus Symbols table plus Fig 11 enumeration).
2. **The source is a habitat condition, enumerated and not ranked.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :46 lists *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*. Light is one item. No figure ranks it first. **Domain D2, triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 9 union rule).
3. **The three options are a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Photothioautotrophy couples light to a chemical donor; vent geothermal light puts the first phototrophy inside a chemical habitat. "Multiple sources" is not option three, it is a re-description of the question as a compound with its own loop. **Domain D1/D2 overlap, triangulated.**
4. **Capability belongs to the agent-and-habitat pair.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. A redox couple is not intrinsically "the source for life"; its valence is relative to the system that couples to it. **Domain D2, triangulated.**
5. **The historical attribution is a ground question.** No figure line names which source came first. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* **Domain D3, `gap` at the figure level**; `specified` world reading below.

## 3. The `specified` historical reading (world, secondary, provenanced)

Kept on a separate line from the figure shape:

- Geochemical redox disequilibrium (H2/CO2, serpentinisation, natural pH and redox gradients, inorganic microcompartments) is the best-supported candidate for the *earliest self-maintaining* coupling. Sousa et al. (2013), Weiss et al. (2016), Martin, Bryant & Beatty (2018), Schwander et al. (2023), Moody et al. (2024).
- Phototrophy is ancient but probably not among the earliest metabolisms. Blankenship (2010): *"photosynthesis began early in Earth's history, but was probably not one of the earliest metabolisms"*. Its timing remains disputed. Cardona (2019) calls the anoxygenic-before-oxygenic premise *"the chief unproven assumption in the evolution of photosynthesis."*
- Light was not absent: Martin, Bryant & Beatty (2018) place the first chlorophyll phototrophy at vents under *"low-intensity, long-wavelength geothermal light"*; Moody et al. (2024) model photochemical H2 and HCN recycling into the surface ecosystem.
- Moody et al. (2024) state that LUCA's H2 and CO2 *"could have come from both geochemical and biotic inputs."* Even at the LUCA stratum the sources are mixed.

This is **corroboration, not a figure verdict**. Four of those papers share an author group and evidence pool; their agreement is one witness. Blankenship, Cardona and Moody reach overlapping conclusions from different frames.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the figures proved chemical reactions came first. They return a shape, not a name.
- Not that sunlight was unimportant. It is a habitat condition and later a biological energy converter.
- Not that "multiple sources" means an even mixture. It means a union with overlapping conditions and a `specified` order still open at the ground.
- Not that LUCA was the first life. It is a later stratum.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here discriminates the first coupling.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

- A source-discriminating instrument: isotope or petrographic evidence that ties a specific carbon or sulfur fractionation to a phototrophic versus a chemolithoautotrophic producer. Fig 6 :34: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
- A direct date for the first light-driven reaction centre relative to the first acetyl-CoA-type coupling.
- A demonstration of a clean partition (no overlap, no dual coupling), which would refute the union reading.
- A demonstration that energy can be spent with no gradient, which would refute the shape.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The figure set can say what the answer must look like and what would reverse it. It cannot name the source that actually operated first. The stop is recorded here so the limit is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick sunlight or chemical reactions as the one early energy source. It says that any source must supply a spendable gradient, that the source is one habitat condition among several, and that the three options in the question form a union of overlapping conditions rather than a list of peers, so "multiple sources" is a re-description of the problem rather than a third answer. The figures return that shape, not a name: which source actually came first is a ground question. The world literature, read second and with provenance, favours geochemical redox gradients (H2/CO2, serpentinisation, natural proton gradients) as the earliest coupling, with phototrophy arriving later and perhaps first under geothermal light at vents, while atmospheric photochemistry fed H2 back into surface ecosystems. That historical reading is `specified`, not `grounded`. What the package offers is the shape any answer must have, a refuter written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
