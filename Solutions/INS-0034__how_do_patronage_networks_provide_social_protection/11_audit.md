## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure lines on actor valence, anchor, stake, union and closure.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07`, one enforcement capacity, two residuals, lever at the membrane.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a specific anti-corruption programme (audit, external allocator, entitlement).

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot requirement** (Fig 1 :19), since a programme report almost always aggregates across households;
- the **provenance requirement** on imports (Fig 8 :50-52), since a universalist rule can be reported without its origin;
- the **ban on naming an institution** (Fig 9 :32), since a programme must name something;
- the **distinction between an interest in the outcome and an interest in the reading** (Fig 10 :59), since programme documents routinely collapse both into a conflict-of-interest clause.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a structure must be read against two residuals, and discretion that buys credibility also disqualifies the patron as an anchor. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
