## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks how one structure, the patronage network, can do two things at once: supply social protection and political coordination where formal institutions are thin, and at the same time degrade impartial governance. Stated as an effect rather than a name:

> In a polity where formal insurance, contract enforcement and dispute resolution do not reach every household, a personalistic network supplies material help, information and coordination. The same network also routes public resources, offices and judgements to insiders on the basis of particularistic obligation. The residual is the **co-occurrence**: why do the protective effect and the governance-weakening effect travel together, and why does adding formal rules inside the network not keep the first while removing the second?

What an answer would look like was bound in `README.md`: one mechanism, two separate verdicts, no averaged net-welfare score, and an instrument that could settle it. The two tempting non-answers are (a) "patronage is a necessary evil, a trade-off" and (b) "patronage is just corruption with a nicer name." Both collapse the residual before measuring it. (a) averages across slots; (b) names a moral verdict where a mechanism is owed.

Per `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2, the sought-for shape is a **preference**, not a term, so no internal check can see it. Binding it above is the discipline that makes it refusable.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity: two conditions.** Protection is present *and* impartial governance is degraded. They co-occur in the same actors and the same enforcement capacity. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* This is a **union compound**, not one hard question and not a list of two independent problems.
- **Symmetry: asymmetric and ordered.** The patron-client link is dyadic, contingent, hierarchical and iterated (Hicken, 2011; Stokes et al., 2013, via the Clientelism source). It is `→` sequence or a `⋛` comparison of dependence, never a symmetric `+` conjunction of equal parties. Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Both sides benefit, but benefit is not symmetry.
- **Scale: two strata with a membrane.** The network operates at the household, neighbourhood or faction stratum; impartial governance claims the polity-wide stratum. Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The boundary between them is *"a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), not a wall.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. No nameable anchor, no domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Informal protection and coordination** | patrons, brokers, clients, households | reciprocal obligation, information, reputation, sanction, access to goods | **yes:** material counters (did the household eat, was the loan repaid, was the dispute settled, did the job arrive) | a partition question: which exchanges can be enforced locally at all |
| **D2 Impartial governance** | officials, rules, courts, auditors, citizens | universal rules, due process, public goods, merit | **yes:** impartial application, measured by counters (equal treatment, audit outcomes, turnover of office) | a partition question: which allocations can be made without regard to identity |
| **D3 Verification and observer frame** | researchers, datasets, reviewers | evidence and residuals | **yes:** ground data, not agreement | a triangulation question: which frame can correct which |

D3 is admitted with a warning. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so its independence is zero (Fig 2 :40). It is a seat in form only.

### 1.3 Partition or union

The two effects are a **union**, not a partition. They share the enforcement capacity: the same reputation, information and sanction that make a promise credible inside the network are what let the patron direct an office or a benefit to an insider. Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

Consequence for method: a reform that adds formal rules to the region where only patronage binds will leave the intersection standing, because the personal obligation still binds there. The residue after such a lever is smaller and *"categorically different"* (Fig 9 :57). It must be re-censused, not treated as a smaller version of the same problem.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the observer tell who received what, and tell it apart from the previous period?
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what the relation affords: insurance, enforcement, contingency, exclusion?
3. **Objective:** can it say what would count as progress here, per slot, in its own terms?
4. **Planning:** can it route a lever to the overlap without destroying the protection?
5. **Learn and carry:** can it name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next locality?

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Most reform effort lands at step 4, where measurements already exist. The middle links, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, have the fewest instruments. This is where the question actually breaks.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - One actor, two valences: the dual effect is not a contradiction (decisive)

Fig 11 :49:
> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :50:
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :51:
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 2 :39:
> **"Correlation has valence."**

The patron is not a benefactor who secretly becomes a malfactor, nor a malfactor doing good by accident. Against D1's counter (household insurance) the relation is beneficial. Against D2's counter (impartial application) the very same relation is harmful. The two verdicts are measured on different anchors and must be kept separate. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated. Fig 11 governs actor valence in a habitat; Fig 2 governs the valence of correlation in a verdict. Different actors, different currency, different anchor, low overlap. The agreement is structural, not a pooled observation. **Refuter:** a case where the same measured relation is beneficial and harmful *on the same anchor at the same slot*, with no change of residual, would collapse the two domains into one and break this finding.

### F2 - Why the protection is credible: an instrument aimed at a counter

Fig 2 :29-30:
> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 11 :39:
> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**

Fig 11 :40:
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The protection effect has a real anchor: did the household survive the shock, was the dispute settled, was the loan repaid. The network's enforcement (reputation, repeated dealing, exclusion) is an instrument aimed at that counter. It is credible precisely because it is local, information-rich and repeated. Hicken (2011) defines the core as *"the combination of particularistic targeting and contingency-based exchange"*; Trivers (1971, via the Reciprocal altruism source) lists cheater detection and indefinite repeated exchange as conditions for reciprocity; Axelrod (1984, via The Evolution of Cooperation source) gives the shadow of the future and recognition of past partners as requirements. These are world corroboration only and carry no verdict weight.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 11 for capability as agent-and-habitat); Fig 2 supplies the anchor definition for a different quantity, so the combined claim is weakly triangulated. **Refuter:** a case where households receive reliable protection from a network with no repeated contact, no cheater detection and no material counter would break this finding.

### F3 - Why the same capacity weakens impartial governance: the interested seat (decisive)

Fig 10 :47:
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 10 :59:
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**

Fig 2 :33:
> **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :34 (partial):
> **"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"**

Impartial governance needs an anchor that does not move when a party pushes on it. The patron has a stake in which value the counter returns today: the allocation is to insiders, the office goes to a client, the judgement favours the network. A normative convention about who deserves help, once it decides an actual allocation, has stopped being a convention and has become a verdict authored by an interested seat. The network cannot be its own ground maintainer. Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* The patron's stake is structural and cannot be refactored away inside the network.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated. Fig 10 governs the seat's interest in the reading; Fig 2 governs the anchor's refusal to update. Different figures, different quantities, low overlap. **Refuter:** a construction in which the patron's stake in the value returned is removed while the protective effect is unchanged (for example, an anonymous, rotating, audited allocator using the same local information) would break this finding.

### F4 - It is a union compound, not a trade-off to be averaged

Fig 9 :54:
> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :57 (partial):
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

Fig 1 :22:
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A single "net welfare" score that adds protection and subtracts governance corruption throws away the only signal that says where a lever would act. The overlap region, where both personal obligation and formal rule bind, is *"categorically different"* (Fig 9 :57) from either condition alone. Abolish patronage wholesale and the protection counter may fall; add formal rules only and the overlap may keep the old allocation under new wording.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the prediction that levers behave differently in the intersection. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union/partition and Fig 1 per-slot residual, different domains). **Refuter:** a measured case where a single lever improves both counters monotonically in every slot, with no residue in the intersection, would break the union reading.

### F5 - Why reform from inside the network cannot deliver both: closure

Fig 6 :21:
> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 6 :23:
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :32 (partial):
> **"no search, however long, adds an atom"**

The network's closure contains local information, reputation, obligation and discretionary sanction. It does not contain an impartial anchor, because an anchor is defined as something that does not update on the frames (Fig 2 :29-30, :33). Composing those atoms more carefully produces better patronage, not impartiality. Only an import from a frame whose closure differs moves the wall, and that import carries a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :11-14). Fig 8 :32 makes this definitional, not empirical.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 says what moves the wall, Fig 8 says why nothing else can; different figures, low overlap). **Refuter:** a demonstration that an impartial allocation anchor is constructible from network-local atoms alone, with no import and no external instrument, would break this finding. Such a construction would also break Fig 2's anchor definition, so a re-census would be owed rather than a simple reversal.

### F6 - The import can be misidentified: provenance is the whole issue

Fig 8 :50-52:
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 9 :27-28:
> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

A formal rule that appears in a network-governed locality may be a genuine import (a rule whose closure differs and whose anchor cannot be moved by patrons), or it may be the network's own discretionary allocation written down in the language of universal rules. The contents alone cannot tell them apart. The record of origin, date and residual answered can. This is why the first contact with any checker carries the residual and its refuter, not the theory.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 8), with Fig 9 governing the search order. **Strain:** convergence and adopted import are indistinguishable in contents, so a field observation cannot by itself decide which occurred. Named, not hidden. **Refuter:** a case where provenance is complete and the two remain indistinguishable in behaviour *and* the distinction makes no difference to any counter would make this finding idle rather than false.

### F7 - The lever sits at the membrane, not inside either frame

Fig 2 :25:
> **"pinned: it does not update on A or B"**

Fig 10 :29:
> **"keep the anchor reachable"**

Fig 2 :42:
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 4 :22:
> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

The figure-derived shape of a remedy is: preserve the protection function's counter (material outcomes, measured per household), and move the selection and sanction function to a frame whose closure differs, with an anchor neither patron nor client can move. That means an independent audit of allocation, a portable entitlement that does not pass through the patron, and a dispute route outside the network. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

This is a shape, not a policy name. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Choosing a particular institutional design is a binding from the development-policy frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation from the six-term set.

**Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor and Fig 4 membrane, different figures). **Refuter:** a case where the protection counter collapses as soon as selection moves to an external anchor, across every measured locality, with no compensating instrument, would break this shape claim.

### F8 - The checker's trap: one evidence pool is one witness

Fig 2 :40:
> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 7 :13:
> **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 7 :35:
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

This audit, the second seat in `11_audit.md`, the world sources and the framework essays all draw on overlapping material. Their agreement is one pool talking. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A list of corroborating authors selected for receptiveness is a budget filter, not a witness.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 correlation valence and Fig 7 room chain). **Refuter:** an independent checker working from a different evidence pool who returns the same shape claim for a different reason would be the first real second witness. No such checker was used in this run.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. The Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Same-anchor reversal.** A measured case where the same relation is beneficial to household insurance and harmful to impartial application on the *same* counter at the *same* slot, with no change of residual. This breaks F1 and the union reading.
2. **Internal anchor construction.** A demonstration that an impartial allocation anchor is constructible from network-local atoms alone, with no import and no external instrument. This breaks F5.
3. **One-for-one substitution.** A ground measurement in which an external impartial allocation displaces the network's protective effect with no residual loss of insurance across every measured locality. This breaks F7's membrane claim.
4. **Stake removal without loss.** A construction where the patron's stake in the value returned is removed while the protective effect is unchanged. This breaks F3.
5. **Additivity.** A single lever that improves both counters monotonically in every slot with no intersection residue. This breaks F4.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn and the domain census is rerun. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F3: one actor, two valences, two anchors. Do not lead with a moral verdict about corruption.
2. Keep verdicts per domain separate and never average them (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. Unbundle the union before searching for a lever (Fig 9 :54). Re-census the residue after any partial lever.
4. State mode on every claim. An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict (Seat Map :281).
5. Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
6. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). For a question about impartial governance, that cost is high.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: one enforcement capacity produces two effects because the actor's valence is relative to the residual, and the capacity that makes protection credible is exactly what disqualifies the patron as an impartial anchor. The compound is a union, so no single lever clears both conditions. The remedy is a shape at the membrane, not a named institution. Strains after re-census: one live strain, the provenance/contents indistinguishability at F6, named and not smoothed over.
