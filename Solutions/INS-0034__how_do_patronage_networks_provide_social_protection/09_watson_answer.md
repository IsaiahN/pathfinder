## QUESTION

Option A: treat this as a trade-off, net the two effects, and prescribe a reform that trades a little protection for a little integrity. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any named institution does not. The protective and governance-weakening effects are one enforcement capacity read against two different anchors. The patron's valence is relative to the residual, so the two verdicts are separate and must never be averaged. The discretion that makes protection credible is exactly what disqualifies the patron as an impartial anchor, because an anchor must not update on the parties. Internal composition cannot add the missing anchor, so the lever lies at the membrane: keep the protection counter visible per household, move selection and sanction to a frame whose anchor neither party can move, and measure the round trip. The compound is a union, so no single lever clears both conditions and a universalist rule may simply carry the network's interest under another name. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded but real. The packet is mode-labelled, states five refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The larger cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a named reform instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The five refuters are stated separately, so a single failure removes one claim without collapsing the others. No primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and the retraction log in `01` keeps earlier readings visible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measurement showing one lever improving both counters monotonically in every slot with no intersection residue would break the union claim. A construction of an internal impartial anchor from network-local atoms would break the closure claim. A case where removing the patron's stake leaves protection unchanged would break the interested-anchor claim. Two independent frames with low evidence overlap returning the same shape for different reasons would be the first real second witness; no such frame was used here.
