## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two arrangements isomers?

Fig 12 :117:
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :94:
> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

The two arrangements as normally described do **not** share every atom. The personalistic arrangement uses obligation and identity as load-bearing atoms; the formal impartial arrangement uses codified rule and audit. On Fig 12's strict definition they are therefore **not isomers** as written. They are members of a **functional group**:

Fig 12 :123:
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The shared substructure is `authority → sanction`, an enforcement relation that reaches a counter. The qualifier differs: particularistic contingency in one, universal category in the other. *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"* (Fig 12 :123).

The stronger isomer claim is available but unmeasured. If both arrangements are written over the full shared atom set `{authority, information, sanction, resource, obligation, identity}` and differ only in which bond attaches obligation to allocation, then they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that swapping the bond while holding the atoms fixed changes both counters in opposite directions.

**Status:** functional-group reading `triangulated` (Fig 12's strict definition plus the primary-field descriptions from Hicken 2011 and the Clientelism source); isomer reading `single-source` for the figure definition and unmeasured. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either arrangement can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, a formal allocation with no information atom, or a network allocation with no sanction atom), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 - Where does the dependency chain break?

Fig 3 :20:
> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Fig 3 :27:
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The chain for this question runs: perception of who received what (link 1), vocabulary for insurance and contingency (link 2), objective stated per counter (link 3), lever design (link 4), carry and verify (link 5). Most published reform effort lands at link 4, where instruments already exist. The break, if any, is at link 3: having terms for what the relation affords and being able to say what would count as progress are two different things, and the second has the fewest instruments. Fig 3 :25-26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

A reading taken at link 4 when link 3 has no instrument is not a weak reading of the lever. It is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :23). This is why the outreach packet asks what a checker would count as failure before offering any design.

**Status:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 3 and Fig 9 :27-28 on describing the effect before choosing a frame). **Falsifier:** a case where a working counter for impartial application already exists in every locality, with no vocabulary-building step needed, would move the break and break this ordering claim.

### Q3 - The reciprocity loop and its sign

Fig 12 :121:
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A patronage relation is a cycle, not a chain: help today re-enters as support tomorrow, which re-enters as access to further help. The negative case is exclusion: a client who fails to reciprocate is cut off, and the loop closes against them. This is why the protective effect is selective and why the governance effect is structural. A chain cannot produce either closure.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition, with Trivers's cheater detection and Axelrod's shadow of the future as low-overlap world corroboration for the loop's persistence conditions. **Falsifier:** a one-shot, non-iterated exchange that still yields both the protective and the governance-weakening effect would break the cycle reading.

### Q4 - Reputation as a catalyst

Fig 12 :119:
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Reputation in the network is a catalyst: it is spent in each allocation and regenerated by the visible act of allocation. The patron's standing survives the use that consumes it. Fig 12 :119 warns that *"nothing records which a term is"*, so an audit that treats reputation as an ordinary consumable resource will misprice it. This is a structural observation, not a measurement.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source. **Falsifier:** a case where reputation is depleted by use and not regenerated, while the dual effect persists unchanged, would break this analogue.

### Q5 - The round trip and what it drops

Fig 4 :30:
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Sent up: the shape (one capacity, two residuals, union, membrane). Brought back down: a specific institutional intervention. Three things do not survive: the per-slot measurement requirement, the provenance requirement on imports, and the ban on naming a specific institution. The gap `R_T` is nonzero and is enumerated qualitatively in `01` section 6 and checked in `11_audit.md`. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## CONCLUSION

The two arrangements are a **functional group**, not isomers as normally written. The stronger isomer claim needs a shared atom set and an instrument. The dependency chain most likely breaks at the objective link (link 3), not at the lever link. The relation is a **cycle** whose negative sign produces exclusion, and reputation is a **catalyst** that survives its own use. All four analogues are structural; none is a measurement. They support the shape in `07` without adding a primitive or naming an institution.
