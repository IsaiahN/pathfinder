**Subject:** One checkable claim about patronage, clientelism and impartial governance

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in institutional analysis and I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** in places where formal insurance, contract enforcement and dispute resolution do not reach every household, a personalistic network often supplies real material help and coordination. The same network also routes offices, benefits and judgements to insiders. The question is why those two effects travel together, and why adding formal rules inside the network does not keep the first while removing the second.
>
> **The output, in one paragraph:** the two effects are not a trade-off on one welfare scale. They are two residuals measured on the same actor. The patron-client relation is beneficial when the counter is household insurance and harmful when the counter is impartial application. The capacity that makes the relation credible as insurance, local information, reputation and discretionary sanction, is exactly what disqualifies the patron as an impartial anchor, because the anchor must not update on what the parties think. So the protection effect and the governance-weakening effect are one mechanism read against two different anchors, not two independent facts that happen to coincide. The practical consequence is a shape: keep the protection counter visible per household, and move selection and sanction to a frame whose anchor neither patron nor client can move. This is a shape, not a named policy.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that patronage is everywhere or always harmful; that every network provides protection; that a particular reform design follows from this; that the six-term substrate forbids or requires any institutional form; or that this is settled. No measurement in my material settles the binding.
>
> **What would refute the claim.** I wrote these down before writing the derivation:
> 1. a measured case where the same relation is beneficial to insurance and harmful to impartial application on the *same* counter at the *same* slot, with no change of residual;
> 2. a demonstration that an impartial allocation anchor is constructible from network-local atoms alone, with no import and no external instrument;
> 3. a ground measurement where external impartial allocation displaces the protective effect with no residual loss of insurance in any locality;
> 4. a construction where the patron's stake in the value returned is removed while protection is unchanged;
> 5. a single lever that improves both counters monotonically in every slot with no intersection residue.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (public repository, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
