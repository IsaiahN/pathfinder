# Expert verification packet - patronage, social protection and impartial governance

**What this is:** a claim in a form a political scientist, institutional economist or development practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle the binding. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The protective effect and the governance-weakening effect of patronage networks are not a trade-off on one welfare scale. They are two residuals measured on one actor against two different anchors. The relation is beneficial when the counter is household insurance and harmful when the counter is impartial application. The enforcement capacity that makes it credible as insurance, local information, reputation and discretionary sanction, is exactly what disqualifies the patron as an impartial anchor, because the anchor must not update on what the parties think. The compound is a union, so a lever that clears the region where only patronage binds leaves the intersection standing. What follows is a shape rather than a policy: keep the protection counter visible per household, move selection and sanction to a frame whose anchor neither patron nor client can move, and measure what the move costs. Five refuters were written before the derivation.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** an actor's valence is relative to the residual, so the same patron can be a benefactor on one counter and a malfactor on another without contradiction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."*
- **What to check:** read those three lines in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Are they present verbatim? Does the two-anchor reading follow without forcing? Is there any figure line that assigns a fixed valence to an actor independent of a residual?
- **Refuter:** a measured case where the same relation is beneficial and harmful on the *same* counter at the *same* slot, with no change of residual. Also: a figure line that gives an actor an intrinsic valence.

### C2 - the structural core; checkable by reading Figure 10 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the patron's stake in which value the allocation counter returns disqualifies the network as its own impartial anchor, and a local convention that decides an allocation has become a verdict rather than a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*; Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* Seat Map :171: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
- **What to check:** is the analogy faithful to the institutional case? In clientelism, delivery is contingent on the recipient's support and iterated; the patron monitors and rewards or punishes. Does that constitute an interest in the reading of who qualifies? Or is it merely an interest in the outcome, which the figures permit?
- **Refuter:** a construction where the patron's stake in the value returned is removed while the protective effect is unchanged (an anonymous, rotating, audited allocator using the same local information). If that works, C2 fails.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about reach; checkable by reading Figure 6 and Figure 8

- **Statement:** composing inside the network's closure cannot add an impartial anchor; only an import from a frame whose closure differs moves the wall, and convergent derivation and adopted import are indistinguishable in their contents.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*; Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*; Fig 8 :32 *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*; Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** does the idempotence claim apply to a human institutional frame at all, or only to formal closures such as a typed DSL? This is the main transfer risk. If it does not transfer, C3 is idle for this question even if it is true inside the framework.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that an impartial allocation anchor is constructible from network-local atoms alone, with no import and no external instrument; or a case where complete provenance and identical behaviour make the convergence/import distinction irrelevant to every counter.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that patronage is universal, always harmful, or that every network provides protection.
- Not that the state is absent, or that formal and informal institutions are separate in practice. The compound is a union (Fig 9 :54).
- Not a net-welfare score or a single trade-off (Fig 1 :22).
- Not a derivation of any specific anti-corruption programme, civil-service reform, conditional transfer or audit design. Those are bindings from a world frame.
- Not that the six-term substrate forbids or requires an institutional form.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles `left(R,φ)`.
- Not that imported formal rules are automatically improvements (F6's live strain).
- Not corroborated by a second independent witness. Every source in this folder shares an evidence pool to some degree (Fig 2 :40).

## 4. Five pre-registered refuters (written before the derivation)

1. Same-anchor reversal: the same relation beneficial and harmful on the same counter at the same slot, with no change of residual.
2. Internal anchor construction: an impartial anchor built from network-local atoms with no import.
3. One-for-one substitution: external impartial allocation displaces protection with no residual insurance loss in any locality.
4. Stake removal without loss: the patron's stake in the reading removed while protection is unchanged.
5. Additivity: one lever improves both counters monotonically in every slot with no intersection residue.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn and the census is rerun. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
