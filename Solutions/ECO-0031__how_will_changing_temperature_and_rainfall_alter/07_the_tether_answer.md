# The Tether's answer on climate and species interactions

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three-route decomposition, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Range models predict where each species can live. The question asks what happens to the **relations** among species when temperature and rainfall change. A species can stay in place while its partner, prey, competitor or predator changes; the same pair can relate differently under a new regime; and a change can propagate several steps through the network. Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step."* Here the object is the interaction.

The residual is indexed by interaction, never by species count or aggregate network score, and never averaged across the two conditions (Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :57).

## 2. The shape

1. **Interactions are relations, not properties of species.** Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 12 :46 says the bond *"is not recoverable from the operands."* So a range shift does not entail an interaction prediction.
2. **Three routes, kept separate and never averaged.**
   - **Range-mediated contact change:** species move at different rates, so who meets whom changes. First order, and the best instrumented.
   - **Condition-mediated valence change:** the same pair under a changed temperature or rainfall can change interaction type or strength. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
   - **Cascading indirect effects:** Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* The consequential change can be far from the species that first felt the warming or drying.
3. **The routes are a union compound, not a list.** Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Temperature and rainfall interact; warming plus drought is not the sum of warming and drought.
4. **Track per interaction.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A network index can stay nearly constant while links are lost and replaced.
5. **The interaction link is the unmeasured middle link.** Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
6. **Capability and effect are agent-and-habitat, not agent alone.** Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A range shift that leaves all contacts unchanged is not an answer to this question.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which named interaction will strengthen or weaken, where, or when. That direction is a `gap` owed to field measurement, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not that temperature and rainfall can be treated as two independent single-driver problems (F6).
- Not that a change in range is a change in interaction (F9), and not that a relocated species keeps its old partners (F10, Fig 11 :66).
- Not a new mechanism and not a theory of everything. The framework returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement of a specific interaction under a specific regime is claimed here.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

The anchor is field observation of the interaction: who visits whom, who eats whom, who suppresses whom, under a measured temperature and rainfall (Fig 2 :29). Concretely:

- **Route 1 test:** do the co-occurrence and timing changes predicted from range and phenology data match observed partner turnover, per interaction?
- **Route 2 test:** hold occurrence constant and change the condition. Does the same pair change interaction type or strength, as Fig 11 :49-50 predicts? The temperature-dependent competition manipulations in Åkesson et al. 2021 (opened) are a model-based version of this test.
- **Route 3 test:** does a change in one link propagate to third parties, as the DDT case in Fig 11 :52-54 does? Wang et al. 2023 (opened) report exactly such cascading effects from warming plus drought in a desert stream.
- **Instrument test (Fig 3 :27):** name an instrument that measures interaction change at the resolution of range change. Until one exists, route 2 and route 3 remain under-measured relative to route 1.

Standing pre-registered falsifiers are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` section 2.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39 forbids reading a failed search as proof of absence. The shape is a checkable result; the direction requires an enumerated habitat and a measurement. The derivation stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

Two honest uncertainties from the opened literature are recorded rather than smoothed: Ockendon et al. 2014 find indirect biotic mechanisms dominate; Thierry et al. 2021 (as reported in a citing source) found experimental warming acting through direct performance rather than altered competition. Kharouba & Wolkovich 2023 report a lack of evidence for match-mismatch across terrestrial trophic interactions. These conflicts mark exactly where the ground must decide, and none of them is allowed to flip the figure verdict (Fig 2 :35).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not predict that a named pollinator will stop visiting a named flower. It says that species interactions are relations, not fixed properties of species, so a range prediction does not entail an interaction prediction. Climate change alters interactions through three routes that must be kept separate: species moving change who meets whom; the same pair can relate differently under a new temperature or rainfall; and a change in one link can cascade several steps through the network. Each route must be tracked per interaction, because a single network score can stay flat while links are lost and replaced. The framework returns that shape, names the instruments that would close it, and refuses to fill the direction with a guess. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
