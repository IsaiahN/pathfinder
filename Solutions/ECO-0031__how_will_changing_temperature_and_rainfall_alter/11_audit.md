## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the question line alone, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a range prediction and an observed interaction change under a change in temperature and rainfall. Arity: the relation joins two species but the consequential effect runs three steps out, so effective arity is at least three. Symmetry: relations are typed and directed, and valence is relative to the residual rather than carried by the actor. Scale: occurrence and phenology at first order, pairwise interaction at second, cascade at third. I read the two conditions themselves as a union compound before looking at the primary's F6.
- **Domain 1, conditions.** Actors: temperature, water or rainfall, season. Currency: environmental forcing. Anchor: instrument records. Upstream.
- **Domain 2, occurrence.** Actors: species, populations, phenologies. Currency: range, abundance, timing. Anchor: monitoring data. Well instrumented.
- **Domain 3, interactions.** Actors: species pairs in typed relations. Currency: presence, strength, valence. Anchor: field observation of the interaction outcome. Compound, not a sum of domain-2 slots, and the unmeasured middle link.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: ecologists. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence zero in this simulated run.

Order by dependency: conditions → occurrence → pairwise interaction → cascade → community outcome. Describe the residual before choosing a subfield (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse before mint where a held term is false (Formula step 2).
