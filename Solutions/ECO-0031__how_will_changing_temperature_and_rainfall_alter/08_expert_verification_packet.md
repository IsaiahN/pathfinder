# Expert verification packet — the Tether on climate and species interactions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate-ecology researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. One-to-one use only; no bulk send.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three-route decomposition, not `grounded`.** No field measurement of a named interaction is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Changing temperature and rainfall alter species interactions through three routes that must be kept separate and tracked per interaction: range-mediated contact change, condition-mediated valence change, and cascading indirect effects. Interactions are relations, not fixed properties of the species involved, so a range prediction does not entail an interaction prediction. The routes form a union compound, not a list; warming plus drought is not the sum of warming and drought. The framework does not name which interaction changes direction in a given place. That is a `gap` owed to field measurement, stated as a verdict rather than filled with a guess. What it offers is the shape any adequate answer must have, its refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and ecology-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** mutualism and parasitism are relations, not fixed kinds of species; an actor's valence is relative to the residual it is being measured against.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Also Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Cross-check from a different figure family, Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- **What to check:** can a pollinator or predator change from beneficial to harmful under a temperature or rainfall shift without changing range? If yes, the relation is not a fixed species property.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that an ecological interaction type is a function of the two species' identities and traits alone under every temperature and rainfall regime, so mutualism and parasitism are fixed kinds rather than relations.
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low-overlap figure families).

### C2 — the decomposition claim; checkable against field and model literature

- **Statement:** three routes are needed and cannot be averaged: range-mediated contact change, condition-mediated valence change, cascade. They must be measured per interaction, not as an aggregate network index.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **What to check:** do the opened sources show link loss/emergence/strength change among species that remain co-present (route 2), and third-order propagation (route 3)? Marjakangas et al. 2025 and Wang et al. 2023 do. Does an aggregate metric stay nearly constant while links change? Rahimi & Jung 2026 report that bipartite metrics changed little while whole network cells disappeared.
- **Refuter:** show that an aggregate network metric fully determines which single interaction fails, or that condition effects never propagate past direct pairwise contact, or that temperature and rainfall effects are additive and separable in all cases.
- **Status:** route 1 `single-source` (Fig 11, plus strong world corroboration); route 3 `single-source` (Fig 11 DDT, corroborated by Wang et al. 2023); union `single-source` (Fig 9).

### C3 — framework-internal limit; checkable by reading Figures 3, 9 and 13

- **Statement:** the direction of a named interaction change is not derivable from the figure set. It is a `gap` owed to an enumerated habitat and a field measurement. The interaction link is the unmeasured middle link of the dependency chain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*; Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **What to check:** can anyone derive the sign of a specific interaction response from the six substrate terms and the two condition values alone? If not, the gap is correctly recorded. If yes, this sub-claim is refuted and the framework is stronger than reported.
- **Refuter:** produce that derivation with no field input, or name an interaction instrument at range-resolution that makes route 2 and route 3 as measurable as route 1 (which would close the gap rather than refute the shape).
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9, Fig 13, Fig 3).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a prediction of the direction or timing of any named interaction change.
- Not that temperature and rainfall act independently; they form a union compound.
- Not that co-occurrence implies interaction (the sources warn against this directly).
- Not that a range shift is an interaction change, or that a relocated species keeps its original partners.
- Not that the seven bonds of Figure 12 are ecological interaction types. That literal mapping was attempted and retracted; Figure 12 is used only for the structural isomer point.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that a failed search proves no answer exists (Fig 8 :39).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Specifically:

1. A per-interaction field dataset measuring interaction type and strength under contrasting measured temperature and rainfall, with occurrence held constant where possible (closes route 2).
2. A documented third-order propagation case with the intermediate link measured (closes route 3).
3. An instrument that measures interaction change at the resolution of range change (closes the Fig 3 :27 break).
4. A derivation of direction from the six terms alone (would close the gap from inside and refute C3).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :47-54 and Fig 1 :19-28, two short passages.
2. Check C1 against one system you know: can the same species shift from mutualist to antagonist as conditions change?
3. Check C2 against one dataset you know: do aggregate network metrics hide per-link turnover?
4. Name the instrument that would close route 2 or route 3, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
