**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about climate and species interactions, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the decomposition, not `grounded`):** changing temperature and rainfall alter species interactions through three routes that must be kept separate, not averaged into one climate effect.
>
> 1. **Range-mediated contact change.** Species move at different rates, so who meets whom changes. This is the route that range and phenology instruments already see.
> 2. **Condition-mediated valence change.** The same two species can relate differently under a different temperature or rainfall regime. Mutualism and parasitism are relations, not fixed kinds of species. A partner can change sign without changing its range.
> 3. **Cascading indirect effects.** A change in one interaction propagates through the network, so the effect that matters can be several steps from the species that first experienced the warming or drying.
>
> Each route has to be tracked per interaction. A single network-level score can stay almost constant while individual links are lost and replaced. The framework does **not** tell you which named interaction will strengthen or weaken in a given place. That direction is owed to field measurement, and I mark it as a gap rather than filling it with a guess.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. An interaction type is recoverable from the two species' identities and traits alone under every temperature and rainfall regime, so mutualism and parasitism are fixed kinds rather than relations.
> 2. A condition change demonstrably alters no interaction beyond direct pairwise contact in any system.
> 3. An aggregate network metric fully determines which single interaction fails, with no per-link information needed.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the decomposition is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage discipline

Stage 1 is the residual, the three-route shape and the refuter. The recipient's verdict on stage 1 is recorded **before** any theory is shown. Stage 2 is opt-in: only if they are curious do the three links go out, with the note that the figures are the authority. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A Track B style reply, if one is ever used, is a conversation and carries zero verdict weight.
