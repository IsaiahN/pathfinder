# Supporting derivation: structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the order claim, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit established that interaction change is a relation change and that direction is a gap. Two supporting questions remain:

1. **Structure.** Is a change in the interaction set a change of *kind* (new species present) or a change of *arrangement* (same species, different bonds)? If it is arrangement, then range-only predictions are structurally unable to see it.
2. **Order.** In what dependency order do the three routes run, and can a later route be assessed before an earlier one?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: the species present in a locality (D2) and the conditions acting on them (D1). Bonds held: the ecological relations named in Fig 11 :47, *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*. Anchor: field observation of the realized interaction. Currency: interaction presence, strength and valence. No new primitive is available without import from field ecology.

**Important boundary.** The seven composition bonds of Fig 12 are operators over a typed DSL, not ecological interaction types. They are used here as an analogue for a structural point, not as a literal taxonomy. The literal mapping was attempted and retracted in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is an interaction change a change of kind or a change of arrangement?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :118 region, chemical case: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."* (Fig 12 :97-98)
- Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Take two localities with the same species list. Under a warmer, drier regime the pollination link is lost and a competition link forms between the same two species. The atom set is unchanged; the bond changed. By Fig 12 :117 that is a **different substance**, invisible in a species-count formula and decisive in behaviour. This is the structural reason range-only prediction cannot answer the question: it records the ingredients and not the arrangement.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the interaction set is always a function of the species set alone, so that identical species lists always yield identical interaction arrangements under every temperature and rainfall regime, the isomer claim fails. The world sources on interaction rewiring (`09_watson_answer.md`) are the check: Marjakangas et al. 2025 report loss, emergence and strength change of links among species continuously present in the community, which is exactly an arrangement change at fixed atoms. That is corroboration, not a figure verdict.

### Q2 — What is the order of the three routes?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
- Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Dependency order:

1. **Conditions (D1)** act on occurrence and phenology (D2). This is the well-instrumented first-order link.
2. **Occurrence and phenology** determine which pairs co-occur and when (second order). This is the unmeasured middle link of Fig 3.
3. **Pairwise interaction change** propagates to third and further parties (the cascade of Fig 11 :52).

The routes are not interchangeable. You cannot assess a cascade before you know which pairwise link changed; you cannot attribute a pairing change to climate without first separating range shift from condition-mediated valence change. Effort spent measuring conditions more finely cannot rescue a broken interaction instrument (Fig 3 :19).

**Falsifier:** if an experiment shows that a third-order interaction effect is fully predictable from condition values without measuring the intermediate pairwise link, the dependency order is wrong and the routes can be collapsed.

### Q3 — What analogue does Fig 12 actually license?

The chemical isomer case (Fig 12 :97-98) licenses exactly one structural claim here: **same ingredients, different arrangement, different behaviour, invisible in the flat formula.** It does not license mapping competition onto subtraction, predation onto production, or mutualism onto conjunction. Those are different operator families. The retraction in `01` keeps that boundary visible.

A second analogue is the DDT case in Fig 11 :52-54: *"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."* The residual was three steps out. That is an ecological cascade example inside the figure itself, not an external analogue, and it is the strongest support for route 3.

### Q4 — Does a settled range result become an atom for the interaction level?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

A species' range, once settled against observations, can serve as an operand for the interaction composition. But Fig 12 :106 says *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."* A range projection that has not been settled cannot be composed over. This is why an unvalidated SDM output is a weak atom for interaction prediction: the world sources record that SDMs assume species independence and therefore misrepresent tightly linked species (Rahimi & Jung 2026, opened).

**Falsifier:** show that an unsettled range projection composes as reliably as a settled observation into interaction prediction. If that holds, the level rule is violated for this domain.

## CONCLUSION

The interaction change is primarily a **change of arrangement**, not a change of kind: the same species under different conditions can carry different relations, and that difference is decisive in behaviour and invisible in a species list (Fig 12 :117, Fig 11 :50). The three routes run in dependency order conditions → occurrence → pairwise interaction → cascade (Fig 3 :20, Fig 11 :52). The chemical and DDT cases are analogues and an in-figure example respectively; neither is used to hand a verdict about a named interaction. Two falsifiers are written above and stand before any conclusion. The structure is `general`, the order `specified`, nothing `grounded`.
