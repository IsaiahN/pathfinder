## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, then order by dependency (Fig 3).

- **R (the residual).** The gap between range-based prediction and observed interaction change under a change in temperature and rainfall. Arity: a relation joins two species at first order, but contact cascades to third parties, so effective arity is at least three. Symmetry: interactions are typed and often directed, and valence is relative to the residual rather than carried by the species. Scale: first order occurrence and phenology, second order pairwise interaction, third and higher order cascade. The conditions themselves form a union compound (Fig 9 :57).
- **Domain 1, conditions.** Actors: temperature, water or rainfall, season (Fig 11 :46). Currency: environmental forcing. Anchor: instrument records of temperature and precipitation. This is the upstream domain.
- **Domain 2, occurrence.** Actors: individual species, populations, phenologies. Currency: range position, abundance, timing. Anchor: census and monitoring data. Well instrumented.
- **Domain 3, interactions.** Actors: species pairs in typed relations (Fig 11 :47). Currency: interaction presence, strength, valence. Anchor: field observation of the interaction outcome, which is the ground for this question. This is the unmeasured middle link (Fig 3 :27) and it is a compound, not a sum of domain-2 slots.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: climate ecologists, reviewers, data managers. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Conditions → occurrence and phenology → pairwise interaction → cascade → community outcome (Fig 3 :20). Refute before mint where a term is held falsely (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a subfield (Fig 9 :27-28).

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. They are independent of the Tether framework, which is a genuine strength, but they are all ecology or climate biology and several draw on shared literature, so overlap is declared per row.

- **Global change ecology.** Tylianakis, Didham, Bascompte and Wardle, "Global change and species interactions in terrestrial ecosystems", Ecology Letters, 2008 (opened via SLU record 2026-11-23): "how biotic and abiotic context alters the direction and magnitude of GEC effects on biotic interactions" and "extrapolating these complex impacts across entire networks of species interactions yields unanticipated effects on ecosystems". Predicts context-dependence and unanticipated network effects; corroborates F1, F2, F3. Overlap: shares the network-ecology literature with Marjakangas 2025; moderate.
- **Meta-analysis, population responses.** Ockendon et al., "Mechanisms underpinning climatic impacts on natural populations: altered species interactions are more important than direct effects", Global Change Biology, 2014 (opened via PubMed 2026-11-23): "significantly greater support for indirect, biotic mechanisms than direct, abiotic mechanisms as mediators of the impact of climate on populations". Predicts that interaction-mediated effects dominate direct ones; corroborates the question's own "not just ranges" premise and F9. Overlap: distinct author group and method; low.
- **Network ecology.** Marjakangas, Dalsgaard and Ordonez, "Fundamental Interaction Niches", Ecology Letters, 2025 (opened via PMC 2026-11-23): "Global change will create new species interactions and alter or eliminate existing ones, a process known as interaction rewiring". Defines loss, emergence and strength change of links among continuously present species; corroborates F1 and Q1 of the supporting derivation. Overlap: cites Tylianakis; moderate.
- **Long-term field observation.** Wang, Grimm, Lawler and Dong, "Changing climate and reorganized species interactions modify community responses to climate variability", PNAS, 2023 (opened via PMC 2026-11-23): "Diversity loss and new establishment of competitors reorganized species interactions" and "reorganized species interactions switched from stabilizing to destabilizing influences, further amplifying community fluctuations". Reports warming of 1 C plus a 40% precipitation reduction and cascading effects through the food web; corroborates F3, F6 and the union claim. Overlap: cites Tylianakis and network literature; moderate.
- **Spatial ecology.** Carroll, Abrahms, Brodie and Cimino, "Spatial match-mismatch between predators and prey under climate change", Nature Ecology & Evolution, 2024 (opened 2026-11-23): "Variability in the rates, magnitudes and directions of species' shifts can alter spatial overlap between predators and prey, with the potential to decouple trophic interactions". Predicts route 1 and a route 2/3 interaction; corroborates F1 and F9. Overlap: cites Tylianakis and Wang; moderate.
- **Theoretical eco-evolutionary modelling.** Akesson et al., "The importance of species interactions in eco-evolutionary community dynamics under climate change", Nature Communications, 2021 (opened via PMC 2026-11-23): "interactions between trophic levels as well as temperature-dependent competition within a trophic level mitigate the negative impact of climate change on biodiversity, emphasizing the importance of understanding biotic interactions in shaping climate change impact". Predicts that interaction terms change the outcome; corroborates F1. Overlap: distinct modelling tradition; low.
- **Ecological modelling.** Rahimi and Jung, "How can we incorporate species interactions into SDM-based climate change modelling?", Community Ecology, 2026 (opened 2026-11-23): "Most SDMs operate under the assumption of species independence, neglecting the fact that the presence or absence of one species often depends on the occurrence of others". Reports that aggregate bipartite metrics changed little while 204 grid cells lost all interactions; corroborates F4 and F5. Overlap: cites network literature; moderate.

**Conflicts recorded, not hidden.** Thierry et al. 2021, as reported in a citing source encountered during search, found experimental warming acting through direct species performance rather than altered competition and parasitism. Kharouba and Wolkovich 2023 report a lack of evidence for match-mismatch across terrestrial trophic interactions. These conflict with the stronger forms of routes 2 and 3 and are exactly why direction is left to the ground. Per the conflict rule, they sent the analysis back to the census; the census was re-run and the three routes were kept separate rather than one being promoted. These two were not opened as primary rows and are not used as witnesses.

No opened world source conflicts with the figure verdict on shape. Agreement here is from genuinely independent authors, but all seven sources are in one broad field, so they are one literature, not seven independent worlds.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures govern the shape directly and strongly through Fig 11, with triangulation from Fig 1, Fig 3, Fig 9 and Fig 12. Interaction change is a change of relation, not a change of species kind. It decomposes into three routes: range-mediated contact change, condition-mediated valence change, and cascading indirect effects. The routes form a union compound and must be tracked per interaction, never averaged. The directional question (which named interaction strengthens or weakens, where and when) is a `gap` owed to field measurement, recorded as a verdict with the instruments that would close it. The result is `general`/`specified`, ungrounded, and simulated. It is internally consistent with the verbatim figures and does not settle the question it reports on.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second cost is practical: if route 2 is wrong and models keep averaging across interactions, monitoring effort goes to range tracking while the interaction break stays unmeasured (Fig 3 :27).
- **undoable:** yes. The three routes are separable, so a failed route can be withdrawn without touching the others. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already withhold direction, so no primitive must be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected decomposition.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-interaction field dataset with occurrence held constant would settle route 2; a documented third-order case with the intermediate link measured would settle route 3; an instrument at range-resolution would close the Fig 3 :27 break; a derivation of direction from the six terms alone would close the gap from inside and refute C3. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige and agreement are not such instruments.
