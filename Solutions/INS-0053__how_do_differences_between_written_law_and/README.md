## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether written law matters. It is which signal a person's controller actually ranges against when the statute book and the enforcement record disagree. In effect terms:

- **b** is the person's belief about what the rule requires and what will happen if they act, formed from private experience and inherited priors (Symbols table, `b`, `w`).
- **a** is what the person does: comply, evade, avoid the domain, use a connection, shift to a different forum.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the written rule predicts should happen.
- **o′** is what actually happened at the person's slot: a sanction, a warning, a ignored violation, a favour, a fine.
- **s** is the person, the legal domain, and the concrete case. R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is measured per slot, never averaged into a society-wide "compliance rate" (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, "the written rule determines expectations and behavior."** Leads to the prediction that changing the text alone moves behavior at the slot, even while the enforcement channel stays as it was.
- **Option B, "the reachable enforcement signal determines expectations and behavior, and the written rule is a declared meaning that must be grounded to count."** Leads to the prediction that behavior tracks what the ground actually pays for at the slot, that the text becomes a convention rather than a verdict when it is treated as settling the outcome by itself, and that the two conditions (grounded text, reachable channel) have different instruments and must be kept separate.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conditions under which the written rule and the enforcement record stop disagreeing at a slot, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of the declared-meaning condition from a failure of the channel condition. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The universal number ("how much" behavior changes per unit of textual change) is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of law in practice.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
