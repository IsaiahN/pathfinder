# Expert verification packet - the Tether on written law and everyday enforcement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a sociolegal scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal effect size. Not `grounded`.** No enforcement measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A written rule is a declared meaning, and a declared meaning does not ground itself. Where the statute book and everyday enforcement diverge, people's expectations and behaviour follow the signal whose channel to the ground stays reachable; the written text functions as a convention rather than as a verdict about what happens. Divergence persists when expectation and enforcement mutually update without a non-updating anchor. Agreement between the two frames is evidence only when they do not share an evidence pool. The same statutory atoms under a different enforcement bond are a different social substance, which is why identical text produces different expectations across offices. The question is a union of three conditions, each with its own instrument, and the framework returns their shape and their falsifiers, not a universal percentage or a named rule.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and sociolegal; checkable against Fig 10 and the anchor rule

- **Statement:** a declared meaning does not ground itself, and the anchor is the realized enforcement event plus behaviour, not an official opinion about the rule.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*; Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*; Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
- **Refuter:** change the written text alone, hold the enforcement channel demonstrably constant, and show expectations and behaviour at the named slot changing with the text rather than the event. Also: show the official statement of the rule changing when people's reported expectations are pushed, which would make it a frame and not an anchor.

### C2 - mechanism-facing; checkable by a sociolegal researcher against field data

- **Statement:** quick collapse of expectation is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the rule, and divergence persists through mutual update when no anchor is pinned.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :27: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*; Fig 2 :13: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*; Fig 2 :15: *"is whoever had the most influence"*; Fig 7 :37: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, :38 *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"*, :39 *"the ground’s pressure on the room"*.
- **What to check:** in a case you know, are the bracket fidelity (how faithfully the declared rule is transmitted across cohorts) and the ground's pressure (what actually happens at the slot) separately measurable? Do expectation and enforcement priority move together, and if so, is there an outcome neither updates on?
- **Refuter:** expectations collapse while both factors measure high and non-declining; or expectation and enforcement are independent low-overlap frames whose divergence persists without either moving.

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading Figures 8 and 12

- **Statement:** same statutory atoms under a different enforcement bond are a different social substance, and agreement between frames is a verdict only where closures differ.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*; Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*; Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."*; Fig 8 :52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **Refuter:** a pair of slots with identical statutory atoms and different enforcement bonds whose expectations are invariant; or a case where text and enforcement demonstrably share no evidence pool and a change in one still predicts nothing about the other.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal percentage, and not a claim that a particular amount of textual change moves behaviour by a particular amount.
- Not "enforcement is everything." Text is a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground (Fig 1 :27).
- Not a standing list of good laws or a transplant recipe (Fig 11 :51).
- Not a derivation of one legal system from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No enforcement event or expectation was measured in producing this packet.
- Not the claim that "law in action" is the framework's answer. It is the realist school's binding, recorded as provenance (Fig 9 :32).

## 5. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :18, :24, :26, :47, :49 and Fig 2 :29, :33, :40, :41. Six short passages.
2. Name one slot you know where the written rule and enforcement disagree. Say which signal people there actually use, and what would show you were wrong.
3. Say whether the statute and the enforcement record in that slot share an evidence pool. If they do, your agreement about "the law" is a convention, not a verdict.
4. Try one refuter from section 2. Send the result. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
