## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question as given: "How should researchers address the risk that study participants change their responses because they know they are being studied?" (cognitive science.txt line 118, section 10, Methods, comparison, and theory).

Restated as an effect: a researcher holds a belief `b` about a participant's cognition or behaviour and a hypothesis encoded in the library `Γ`. The researcher acts by observing (`a`), and gets an observation `o'`. The residual is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot `s`. The trouble is that `o'` is not a passive reading of the participant. The act of observing enters the system being observed: the participant forms an interpretation of the situation, adopts a role, and changes the response. So the quantity the researcher wanted as a ground moves when the frame pushes on it.

Three things follow immediately, before any figure is read:

1. This is a **channel** problem first, not a truth problem about the participant. The thing that decays is the contact between the frame and the ground.
2. The residual is **per slot**, not one global number. Different participants, items, cultures and settings carry different live mass. "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19) and "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." (Fig 1 :22).
3. The sought-for object is a **shape of a remedy**, not a named technique. We must write down what a successful answer would look like before choosing one (formula step "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refusable).

**What an answer would look like, bound in advance:** a set of design moves such that (i) the anchor used to settle a claim does not change because a participant believes something about the study, (ii) the residual from awareness is measured rather than assumed away, (iii) methods that agree do so from genuinely different evidence, (iv) the habitat the response came from is stated, (v) the ethical cost of the moves is declared rather than hidden, and (vi) the whole is recorded so a later reader can locate the error. If no figure line can license such a set, the result is `gap`, reported as a verdict.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** The residual is not unary. At minimum it has four operands: awareness of observation, the participant's interpretation of the setting (demand), the social presentation the response is tuned to, and the habitat the response is elicited in. They overlap. By Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." This is a **union compound**, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Symmetry.** The reactive change is directional. A participant may move toward pleasing the experimenter, toward avoiding a bad score, toward suspicion, or toward the peer group's norm. It is not a symmetric join of two equal operands. "Correlation has valence." (Fig 2 :39). The direction of the move is itself part of what has to be described, so a single signed "effect size" is already a lossy summary.
- **Scale.** The residual registers at the single response slot (one item, one participant, one moment) and at the lab-versus-field scale (the substituted habitat). These are two scales with a lossy transform between. "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." (Fig 4 :22).

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor. Where no anchor can be named, it is not yet a domain and is recorded as a gap.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Channel and observation | researcher, participant, hypothesis, the participant's interpretation | attention, trust, interpreted demand | the response that would occur without awareness of study (counterfactual), approached through instruments that do not carry the awareness cue | yes, as a limit approached, never directly held |
| D2 Habitat and transfer | participant, setting, cultural norms, observer, peer group | ecological transfer, valence relative to the residual | behaviour under ordinary contact in the unmodified habitat | yes |
| D3 Frames and corroboration | methods as frames: self-report, unobtrusive, implicit, physiological, archival, multiple experimenters | low-overlap agreement | the ground, never agreement | yes |
| D4 Ethics and consent | participant, IRB or ethics committee, researcher | consent, minimized harm, declared interest | the participant's autonomy and the declared stake of the checker | yes |
| D5 Naming and reporting | the literature's "Hawthorne effect", reviewer, editor, reader | verdict and lineage | the ground for a particular study, plus the record for the claim about methods | yes for studies; the single unified magnitude is a gap |
| D6 The remedy itself | the design moves, each a frame with a closure | reach of what can be corrected | an instrument that discriminates the reactive from the non-reactive response | yes, as the stop condition |

### Partition or union

D1 through D4 are a **union with overlaps**, not a partition. Awareness of observation (D1) is expressed through a habitat (D2), is detected by methods (D3), and is constrained by consent (D4). A single lever applied to one condition leaves the intersections standing. "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." (Fig 9 :55). D5 and D6 are second-order: they are about the record of the others and the choice of remedy, so they get their own loops rather than being folded into D1.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception / vocabulary.** Can the study even tell what is in front of it, and does it have terms for what the setting affords the participant? "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." (Fig 3 :20). "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." (Fig 3 :19).
2. **Objective.** State what would count as progress, in the study's own terms: a discriminated residual, not a procedural checkmark.
3. **Refuse before mint.** The claim "the observed response is the participant's response" is a held term that is false under awareness. Formula step 2 sorts it as `REFUTED`, owing a refusal first, not another explanation. "a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so" (Fig 5 :53).
4. **Enumerate habitat.** "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." (Fig 11 :43).
5. **Describe the residual before choosing a frame.** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28).
6. **Establish a non-updating anchor, then buy low-overlap methods.** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." (Fig 2 :42).
7. **Declare the stake and record the lineage.** "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." (Fig 10 :60).

Strain test applied during the census: the phrase "anchor must not update" (Fig 2 :33) seemed at first to forbid using any human response as a ground for a question about people. Re-census resolved it. Fig 2 :36-38 says an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it, and human judgment is the right anchor for "is this a good answer for a person." The strain was caused by assigning the participant's *aware response* to the anchor domain. It belongs in the channel domain. No figure was bent.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1. The observation channel is the failure, not the ground (governs D1)

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does."**

Fig 10 :25-28: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** and **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."**

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

When a participant changes their response because they know they are studied, the measurement has become a mutual-update loop: the reading changes the thing read, and the researcher's hypothesis and the participant's interpretation of the setting converge on a number that neither would have produced alone. This is Fig 2 panel 1 (collapse 1, coherent drift), not panel 3 (triangulation). It is dangerous precisely because it is coherent: the data look clean, the metric saturates, and nobody notices. The correct diagnosis is that contact has been lost, which is Fig 10 :28's output, "you have lost touch," not a verdict about the participant.

**Refuter for F1:** show an instrument for which the response is invariant to the awareness cue while the instrument independently discriminates the two states. If the gap is zero on a source-discriminating instrument, the channel diagnosis fails for that quantity.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2, two figures with low overlap: one about the maintainer's channel, one about the geometry of anchors).

### F2. The observed response is a refused term, not a mint (governs D1, D6)

Formula step 2 sorts a residual whose content is "a regularity we hold is false" as `REFUTED`. Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."** Operators table :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."**

The held term is "the observed response is the participant's natural response." Under awareness it is false. What is owed is a refusal of that term's standing to be composed over, not a new bias-correction term appended to the analysis. Nothing is deleted: Fig 5 :55 says "Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach." The practical consequence is that researchers should not add a "reactivity covariate" and carry on as if the original interpretation still stands. They must remove the standing of the naïve reading and rebuild from what survives.

**Refuter for F2:** show a decomposition in which the naïve reading remains composable over after the residual is admitted, with the same reach and no contradiction in the record.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 5 amendment and Operators table; formula step 2).

### F3. The "effect" is a bundled union, not one named thing (governs D5)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The question says "participants change their responses because they know they are being studied" as if this were one mechanism. The world literature does not support that. One open encyclopaedia entry describes reactivity as a family: the Hawthorne effect (attention), the John Henry effect (rivalry with the experimental group), the experimenter effect (subtle communication of expectations), the Pygmalion effect (teacher expectations), and reactivity of self-report measures such as confidence ratings and judgments of learning, each with its own cited studies. Demand-characteristics research adds the good-participant, negative-participant, faithful-participant and apprehensive-participant roles. Social-desirability research adds impression management and self-deceptive enhancement. These are overlapping conditions, so a single lever aimed at "awareness" will clear the region where only awareness holds and leave the intersections (awareness plus culture, awareness plus experimenter expectancy, awareness plus evaluation apprehension) standing.

World corroboration: Wikipedia "Reactivity (psychology)" and "Demand characteristics" and "Response bias" (opened 2026-11-22). A peer-reviewed randomised controlled trial (McCarney et al. 2007, BMC Medical Research Methodology) found only a small effect and explicitly noted the category is "effectively a catch-all term for labelling psychological and social factors that weren't controlled for or that cannot be explained." Nisbett is quoted in the same encyclopaedia entry calling it "a glorified anecdote." That is corroboration for the union reading, not a conflict: it says the single named thing is underdetermined.

**Refuter for F3:** a low-overlap demonstration that one mechanism accounts for all of the reactive variance across settings, cultures and measures with a stable sign and no named remainder.

**Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 9 union rule) but corroborated by multiple independent world sources with different evidence pools (methodology review, clinical RCT, social psychology). The specific claim that no single mechanism suffices is therefore weakly triangulated across figure and world, but the figure verdict itself is single-source.

### F4. The lab is a substituted habitat, and introduction is asymmetric (governs D2)

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

A study setting introduces the participant's awareness of being studied. It cannot then subtract that awareness by an act of will, because "contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you" (Fig 11 :62). The only available moves are to substitute a different habitat (field setting, unobtrusive observation, archival traces) and to enumerate what the substitution failed to reproduce and what it unintentionally introduced. Fig 11 :68-69 names both blind spots: "what you failed to reproduce" and "what you unintentionally introduced." A laboratory result that "proves wiring and never capability" is exactly the failure the question is about.

Cross-cultural bearing: a response that is stable in one habitat is not thereby stable in another. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." An observer, a questionnaire format, or an authority cue can be a benign actor in one culture and a reactive one in another. Fig 10 :73-74 requires the participant pool to be unauthored before it is examined: "the pool must be unauthored first and examined second." Selecting a convenient cultural sample and then interpreting reactivity within it is authored-room stacking.

**Refuter for F4:** demonstrate a setting in which the awareness actor can be subtracted without substituting a habitat, and in which the response is invariant across cultures after a provenanced translation round trip.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 habitat rules and Fig 10 selection rule).

### F5. Agreement inside one reactive frame is worthless (governs D3)

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :31-32: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not."**

Adding more participants to the same paradigm, or more items of the same self-report type, increases vocabulary and agreement without adding an anchor. Every method that depends on the participant knowing they are being studied (self-report, interview, observed performance under instruction) draws on the same awareness evidence. Their agreement is one pool talking. The remedy is not "more data" but "other instruments": unobtrusive traces (Webb, Campbell, Schwartz and Sechrest 1966, as reported in the public encyclopaedia entry), implicit measures, physiological or behavioural measures that do not require a report of intent, archival records, and different experimenters who do not know the hypothesis. Double-blind procedures reduce the experimenter-expectancy branch; they do not by themselves remove the participant's awareness branch.

Fig 8 :29: **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence."** Note the distinction at Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Two methods that converge from separately built instruments are corroboration at high independence; two methods that share a pool are one method wearing two names. The record, not the agreement, decides which.

**Refuter for F5:** show that the added instruments share a single evidence source with the self-report, and that the apparent corroboration disappears when the source is identified. In that case the independence claim is refuted and the remedy reduces to one frame.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 geometry, Fig 7 chain, Fig 8 union).

### F6. Methods are imports and must pass a source gate and a shadow test (governs D3, D6)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 6 :34: **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

A researcher cannot specify an unobtrusive measure from a description of reactivity. They start from an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve: an existing trace, an existing implicit task, an existing archival record, and sharpen it. This matters because the fashionable advice "use an unobtrusive measure" is a name, not an instrument. Fig 6 :34 supplies the test: is anything, at any resolution, already returning something that fails to resolve? If not, there is no edge to extend.

Every import is debited against independence (Fig 6 :24) and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24). So a researcher who imports a whole method because it is popular, without a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (does it explain this residual?), has added an atom that may not be needed.

**Refuter for F6:** exhibit an instrument built from a description alone that resolves the awareness residual with no prior proto-instrument and no import debit.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 inward/outward and instrument rules, Fig 8 surplus).

### F7. Deception and covert measurement trade a methodological gain against an ethical cost, and the two currencies do not add (governs D4)

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

The standard toolkit for reducing awareness effects includes deception about the hypothesis, anonymous or self-administered questionnaires, the ballot-box method, randomised response, the unmatched-count technique, the bogus pipeline, and unobtrusive measures. Each reduces the participant's ability to tune the response to a guessed hypothesis. Each also costs something: consent, trust, the possibility of debriefing, and in the case of public digital traces, the question of whether public data may be used without consent. The unobtrusive-research entry states the dilemma directly: "can all data collected in the public domain be used for research purposes? When should we seek consent, and is it realistic to require informed consent from sources of unobtrusively collected data?" The Belmont Report adds respect for persons, beneficence and justice, and its encyclopaedia entry records a critique that the three principles can be "one size fits all" and should be extended to cultural, gender, ethnic and geographical considerations (Shore 2006, as reported on the Belmont Report page).

The figure rule is that you may not sum the gain and the cost into one index and pick the maximum. "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." (Fig 12 :130). Keep them as two readings. A deception that buys a large reduction in demand characteristics and a large consent cost is not thereby "worth it"; it is a decision that must be owned.

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."** The researcher's interest in getting a clean effect is unavoidable. An interest in which value the instrument returns is disqualifying and must be declared. Preregistration, a hypothesis-blind experimenter, and an explicit record of which method was chosen before the result was seen are the operational forms of this rule.

**Refuter for F7:** produce a single currency in which methodological validity and consent cost are commensurable, with a rule that everyone accepts for weighting them. Until that exists, the two-reading rule stands.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 two currencies and Fig 10 stake rules; world: Belmont Report, unobtrusive research).

### F8. More of the same is a mirror chain, not a remedy (governs D5)

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."**

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

A common response to reactivity is to increase sample size, add a manipulation check, or replicate in the same lab with the same paradigm. Within a fixed closure these are more of the same search. They can establish that the residual persists, which is useful, but they cannot import the missing instrument. If the search finds no reactivity, that is a search report, not proof that reactivity is absent. "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." (Fig 9 :44).

**Refuter for F8:** show that the added sample reaches a different closure, that is, it uses an instrument that does not draw on the participant's awareness of the hypothesis, and that the residual changes sign or magnitude there.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 7, Fig 8, Fig 9).

### F10. Provenance warning: do not borrow the physicist's "observer effect" (governs D5)

Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."**

A public discussion page for the Hawthorne effect records an explicit warning: "Please do not link this article to the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle. It is a misconception to attribute observers interfering with the experiment as the reason behind HUP. Quantum mechanics is not applicable in these scenarios." A separate disambiguation page lists "observer effect" across the Hawthorne effect, software heisenbugs, information technology, physics, observer-expectancy effects and the observer's paradox. These are different frames that happen to share a name. Borrowing the authority of the physics sense for the cognitive-science claim would be an echo, not a shadow: a structure found elsewhere and given somewhere to live. The correct move is to describe the effect (a response changes with awareness of observation) and name the frame that predicts its shape (social psychology and research methods), not to import the term.

**Refuter for F10:** show a derivation in which the physics sense and the social-science sense share the same anchor and the same evidence, so that the transfer carries information rather than only a name.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion and before any outreach)

Required before any claim leaves the folder. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The derived shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Channel test.** An instrument is shown that discriminates observed from unobserved response independent of the participant's belief about being studied, and the gap is zero across all tested slots. That breaks F1 and the whole channel diagnosis.
2. **Union test.** One mechanism accounts for all reactive variance across settings, cultures and measures, with a stable sign and no named remainder under low-overlap confirmation. That breaks F3 and leaves a partition, not a union.
3. **Habitat test.** Awareness can be subtracted without substituting a habitat, and the response is invariant across cultures after a provenanced translation round trip. That breaks F4.
4. **Independence test.** All supposedly low-overlap methods are shown to draw on a single evidence source, and the corroboration vanishes once the source is named. That breaks F5.
5. **Stake test.** A method is shown to raise validity and lower consent cost on one accepted scale without any remainder, so the two currencies do add. That breaks F7.
6. **Reach test.** A single technique is shown sufficient in all habitats and cultures with a fixed magnitude, which would collapse the bundle to one term and make the remedy a name rather than a shape. That breaks F9's gap verdict.

If any fires, the remedy as stated is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. These same six conditions are carried into `02`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Refuse first.** State plainly that the observed response under awareness is not the natural response, and remove its standing to be composed over (F2). Do not append a covariate and continue as before.
2. **Split before searching.** Name the conditions separately: attention/demand, evaluation apprehension, social desirability, experimenter expectancy, habitat substitution, cultural response style (F3). Keep verdicts per condition.
3. **Enumerate the habitat.** List what the study setting introduced and what it failed to reproduce, including cultural and authority cues, before designing the remedy (F4, Fig 11 :43).
4. **Buy low-overlap instruments.** Use at least one method whose evidence does not depend on the participant's awareness of the hypothesis, sharpened from an instrument already returning something (F5, F6). Report which instruments share a pool.
5. **Keep the two currencies apart.** Report the validity gain and the ethical cost in separate columns; never sum them (F7). Declare the stake, preregister, and use hypothesis-blind contact where possible.
6. **Do not equate more data with a different frame.** Replication within the same paradigm tests persistence, not the remedy (F8).
7. **Record the lineage.** Report what was unreachable, what was imported and from where, what was refused, and what would reverse the verdict (F9, Fig 13 :127).
8. **Use the shape, not a name.** The framework returns a shape: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). Do not assert a single correct technique.
