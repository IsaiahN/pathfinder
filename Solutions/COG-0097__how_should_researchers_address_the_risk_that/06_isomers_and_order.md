# Isomers, functional groups, and order in the reactivity remedy

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5).

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after `01`:

1. If the literature's "Hawthorne effect" is a bundle of conditions (F3), what is the correct structural relation among those conditions: partition, union, or something else?
2. If several different methods are all offered as remedies, are they interchangeable spellings of one remedy (symmetric), or different remedies that share a structure but not an outcome (isomers or functional group)? What would make them non-interchangeable?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (already reduced in `01` section 1)

Atoms held: awareness of observation, demand interpretation, evaluation apprehension, social presentation, experimenter expectancy, habitat, cultural response style. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: correction of a residual. Anchor: an instrument that discriminates the reactive from the non-reactive response.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The conditions overlap. A good-participant role (demand) can be amplified by evaluation apprehension and expressed through a culturally patterned acquiescence style. Removing awareness alone does not clear the intersection. So the correct structure is a **union**, and each intersection becomes a specific compound with its own loop. The practical consequence: a method must be named for the condition it clears. "Reduce demand characteristics" is not the same claim as "reduce social desirability" or "restore field transfer."

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a low-overlap demonstration that one condition accounts for all reactive variance across settings, cultures and measures with a stable sign and no named remainder. If that lands, the union claim fails and a partition stands. This is refuter 2 in `01` section 4.

### Q2. Are the methods isomers or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (In this extract the line is numbered 123; the exemplar cites 124 for the same sentence. The text is unchanged and the citation difference is recorded rather than silently harmonised.)

Define a method by its atom set and its bond to the participant, not by its name:

| method | atoms it touches | bond to participant | clears which condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Double blind / hypothesis-blind contact | experimenter's knowledge, participant's guess | withholds the cue from the contact person | experimenter expectancy, part of demand |
| Unobtrusive / archival measure | trace, behaviour, no report of intent | removes the report step | awareness of being measured, evaluation apprehension |
| Implicit measure | task performance, no self-description | indirect access | social desirability, impression management |
| Randomised response / ballot box | response privacy, random device | masks the individual answer | social desirability, apprehension |
| Naturalistic / field setting | habitat, observer presence | substitutes habitat | transfer, part of demand |
| Cross-cultural response-style correction | cultural response style, item scores | statistical rebonding after collection | acquiescence, extreme responding |

Under Fig 12 :117, methods with the **same atom set** but a different bond are isomers: the behaviour differs even though the ingredient list looks the same. Randomised response and the ballot-box method both touch privacy and a response, but one bonds a random device and the other bonds a physical container; whether they behave identically in a given culture is an empirical question, not a naming decision.

All six methods share a **functional group**: they insert a barrier between the participant's guess about the hypothesis and the recorded response. "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The qualifier is the condition cleared and the habitat in which it is used. So the answer to Q2 is: the methods are members of one functional group, and specific pairs become isomers when they hold the same atoms under different bonds. They are not interchangeable spellings, because the bond carries the information the ingredient list does not.

- **Falsifier:** show two methods with the same atoms and the same bond that behave identically across all habitats and cultures, so the bond carries no information. That would collapse them to one term and make method choice a matter of taste.

### Q3. What is the order of the remedy?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

The dependency order is:

1. **Perceive and name the conditions.** Without terms for demand, apprehension, desirability, expectancy, habitat and response style, the search cannot aim (Fig 3 :5-8).
2. **Refuse the naïve reading.** "The observed response is the natural response" is a false held term. Fig 12 :112 distinguishes the negation from subtraction: this is not "response minus bias," it is a refusal of the claim. Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Doing this before minting matters (formula step 2).
3. **Enumerate the habitat.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." List what the setting introduced and what it failed to reproduce.
4. **Describe the residual in effect terms.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
5. **Choose instruments from an existing proto-instrument.** Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
6. **Buy independence.** Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
7. **Record.** Fig 13 :127: "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."

A study that jumps to step 5 (pick a fashionable method) while step 1 and step 2 are missing is optimising a link above the break. The chain does not carry the correction to where it is needed.

### Q4. Catalyst, cycle, and the trap of the self-validating check

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A post-experimental questionnaire such as the Perceived Awareness of the Research Hypothesis scale (cited in the demand-characteristics entry) is a candidate **catalyst**: if it survives its own use, the same instrument can be reused across studies. But the entry also notes that a pre-experimental questionnaire can itself create demand characteristics, and that a different experimenter should distribute the questionnaire. So whether it is a catalyst or a spent part depends on a fact the record usually does not contain: whether the act of checking changed the thing checked. Fig 12 :119 says exactly this, "nothing records which a term is."

Replication is a **cycle**. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes. If the next study is drawn from the same closure as the last, the loop is a mirror chain (Fig 7 :13, "agreement compounds, evidence does not") and it converges on agreement rather than on the ground. If the next study is selected against the residual the last one surfaced, the loop closes toward the ground (Fig 7 :36). The researcher must choose the sign; it is not given by the fact of replication.

- **Falsifier:** show a check instrument whose reuse demonstrably does not change the measured response, recorded in a way that distinguishes catalyst from spent part. Until then the catalyst status is `candidate`.

### Q5. The two-currency constraint on choosing among isomers

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**

Choosing a method costs description complexity (how many assumptions, how many conditions named) and action cost (how many participants, how much deception, how much consent negotiation, how much fieldwork). A short method over a costly habitat is cheap to state and expensive to settle; a long method over cheap parts is expensive to state and may take one check to refute. Neither figure predicts the other, so both must be read. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` and `04_links_and_distribution.md` keep contact cost in a separate column from evidence, and why the ethics cost is never folded into the validity gain.

## CONCLUSION

The reactive "effect" is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition, and its intersections are specific compounds. The methods offered as remedies form one functional group, barrier insertion between the participant's guess and the recorded response, and become isomers when they hold the same atoms under different bonds. The remedy has a dependency order: name, refuse, enumerate, describe, sharpen an existing instrument, buy independence, record. Jumping to a method without the first two links is optimising above the break (Fig 3 :19-20). A check instrument may be a catalyst or a spent part, and the record usually does not say which. Replication is a cycle whose sign the researcher chooses. Choice among methods costs in two currencies that do not add.
