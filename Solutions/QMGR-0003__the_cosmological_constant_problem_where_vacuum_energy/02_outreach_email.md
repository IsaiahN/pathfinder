# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in your own words and survive your own check. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** A checkable claim about the 120-order vacuum energy gap, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the cosmological constant problem I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, stated in effect terms (`specified`, not `grounded`):** one frame sums the zero-point energy of each field mode up to a cutoff and returns a density of order the cutoff to the fourth power. Another frame reads the expansion history and returns a density of order (2 meV) to the fourth power. The gap is about 10^120 in energy density. My output is structural, not a number: the 120 orders is an aggregate over mode slots, and aggregation is precisely the operation that hides which slot carries the live residual. The regulated sum is a harness, not a measurement of nature. The observed value is an anchor that fixes the magnitude but not the mechanism. The problem is a union of at least three conditions, so a single lever clears at most one of them. Within the closure I hold, the mechanism is unreachable at this budget, which I report as a gap rather than as a name for a new particle or symmetry.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that I have solved the cosmological constant problem; not that a particular new field or symmetry is the answer; not that the framework returns a name where it can only return a shape; not that the counterterm that fits the observed value explains anything. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in their contents and only the record separates them, so any provenance-free mapping carries zero weight with me.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
> 1. A per-mode vacuum-energy residual that matches the observed density at the ground with no counterterm fitted to that density.
> 2. A demonstration that the inferred dark-energy density changes when the theorist changes the cutoff scheme.
> 3. A derivation of the observed scale from inside the field-theory closure with no import and no fitted parameter.
> 4. A measurement showing that zero-point energy does not gravitate at the same coefficient as the observed term.
> 5. A single parameter change that sets the magnitude, removes the mode sum, and explains the coupling all at once.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. I am keeping this one-to-one and I am not sending it to a list.
>
> **Before you answer, what would you count as a failure?** I would like to record your verdict on the output before showing you where the output came from. That ordering is deliberate: once a reader has absorbed the source, their agreement stops being evidence.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule for the figures: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Public overview of the architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
