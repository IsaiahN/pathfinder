# Expert verification packet: the Tether on irrigation, fertilizer, runoff, water and climate

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in agricultural water and nutrient research can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.** No regional magnitude is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so it must be split into a field source loop, a basin receiver loop, an atmospheric loop and their coupling before it is searched. The residual is indexed by slot and must never be averaged: irrigation evapotranspiration cools locally while fertilizer-derived N2O warms globally, and these are different currencies. Contacts must be enumerated out to the cascade horizon, because the effect that matters is often the nutrient load, the bloom, the decomposition and the oxygen loss rather than the application itself. Valence follows the target residual, and management can introduce actors but cannot subtract what has already crossed into the basin or the atmosphere. Field-to-basin and basin-to-region handoffs carry round-trip gaps that must be measured. The framework returns this shape and these refuters; it does not return numbers.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal plus earth-system-facing; checkable by reading Figure 9 and by inspecting the region

- **Statement:** the case must be unbundled; it is a union compound, not one hard question and not a partition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :58 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- **What to check:** in a region you know, can one lever (say nitrogen reduction alone) clear the water-quality residual while the irrigation and transport conditions remain binding? If yes, the union reading is supported. If one lever clears every slot across seasons, it is refuted.
- **Refuter:** a single intervention clears the residual in all slots of a region and the effect persists across seasons.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable against Figure 1 and Figure 13

- **Statement:** per-slot residuals must not be averaged. The irrigation moisture/heat slot and the fertilizer radiative slot have opposite signs and different currencies, and a gradient over space is not a gradient over time.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 13 :76 *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- **What to check:** do the local cooling measurement and the N2O forcing calculation use the same currency and the same evidence pool? If they do, their netting is a legitimate single slot; if they do not, the netting is the disallowed operation.
- **Refuter:** all climate slots share one mechanism, one evidence pool and residuals that trend together under the same intervention. Even then they are one witness, not several (Fig 2 :40).

### C3: framework-internal plus cascade; checkable against Figure 11 and the field record

- **Statement:** contacts must be enumerated out to the cascade horizon, and managed systems have asymmetric power. The residual that matters is often three steps out; introduction is available, exclusion is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43-44 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-53 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :61-62 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
- **What to check:** in the basin you know, are the load, bloom, decomposition and oxygen-loss steps measured, or does the monitoring stop at the field edge? Can a manager actually remove a nutrient already in the groundwater, or only add a countermeasure?
- **Refuter:** second and third-order contacts are measured and contribute nothing while first-order contacts explain the residual; or an intervention genuinely subtracts an actor from the habitat rather than adding a counteractor.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a regional number of any kind: no cooling magnitude, nutrient load, N2O flux, hypoxia area or salinity change. The figures do not yield those; the numeric part is recorded as `gap` in `01` F9 and `09`.
- Not that nitrogen, irrigation or runoff is intrinsically good or bad. Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- Not that a calibrated regional model is the ground. Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."*
- Not that the three actors are isomers as currently written. They are members of one functional group; the stronger isomer claim needs the full shared atom set and has its own falsifier in `06`.
- Not that the six world sources are six independent witnesses. Four are one encyclopedia pool and two are one government pool; overlap is declared per row in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument in this run.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-58, Fig 1 :19-22 and Fig 11 :43-44 and :52-53. Five short passages.
2. For a basin you know, name one lever and say which of the three conditions (water, nutrient, transport) it does **not** clear. That is the union check.
3. Name one instrument that would partition a local temperature change into evapotranspiration, albedo and radiative forcing, or say why none is available. That is the per-slot check.
4. Say at which step your monitoring currently stops: field, ditch, stream, estuary, or coast. That is the cascade check.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no agreement will be pooled as corroboration.
