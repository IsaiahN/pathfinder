**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked against the verbatim figure text by you. The offer collapses on contact if a figure line has drifted.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about irrigation, fertilizer, runoff, water and climate

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on agricultural water and nutrient research and I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any framework first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A regional water and climate prediction made with no agricultural actors returns errors in distinct slots: stream nitrogen and phosphorus loads, dissolved oxygen, groundwater nitrate, aquifer level and salinity, evapotranspiration and near-surface temperature, and atmospheric N2O. The claim is that those slots must not be combined into one "agricultural effect". Irrigation, fertilizer and runoff are not three spellings of one input: irrigation changes water quantity and the surface energy flux, fertilizer adds reactive mass, and runoff is the transport relation that carries field losses to the basin. The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds.
>
> **What I am asking you to check (the three sub-claims):**
> 1. That the case must be split into field, basin and atmosphere before it is searched, rather than treated as one hard question.
> 2. That the residuals are indexed by slot and must never be averaged: local irrigation cooling and N2O warming are different currencies with opposite signs.
> 3. That the effect that matters is often three steps out. A first-order field census misses the nutrient load, the bloom, the decomposition, the oxygen loss and the downstream user.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. One lever clears the residual in all slots of a region across seasons.
> 2. Second-order and third-order contacts are measured and contribute nothing, while first-order contacts explain all of it.
> 3. A measured field-to-basin water saving maps with zero gap where return-flow users exist.
> 4. A proposed anchor changes its reading when the frame proposing it changes, or is itself one of the parties.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving you a number for regional cooling, N2O flux or nutrient load. The framework I am using returns a shape and an enumeration discipline, not physical magnitudes, and I am recording that part as a gap rather than filling it with a guess. I am not claiming nitrogen is simply "bad": its valence depends on which residual you are trying to reduce. I am not claiming that a calibrated model is a ground. And I am not asking you to trust or endorse anything.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figure repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked earth-system context: https://www.epa.gov/nutrientpollution/effects-environment and https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/deadzone.html
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the three sub-claims and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the source links, with the note that the figures are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A stage 2 or stage 3 agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; once a recipient has read the framework, that pool is shared.
