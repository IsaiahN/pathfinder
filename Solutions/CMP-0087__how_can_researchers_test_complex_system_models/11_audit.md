## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The missing observation that would close the gap between a model prediction and a target reading. Arity: model, target, channel, plus an absent intervention. Symmetry: asymmetric; the anchor must not update on the model (Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*). Scale: abstraction and concretisation with a round-trip gap R_T (Fig 4 :30).
- **Domain 1, model frame.** Actors: parameters, structure, Γ. Currency: fit, prediction, description length. Anchor: none. It is a frame, so it cannot serve as its own check domain. I reach the same boundary as the primary, but by Fig 1 :27 rather than by its census table.
- **Domain 2, target world.** Actors: entities, conditions, relations. Currency: outcomes. Anchor: the world, including *"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome"* (Fig 2 :29-30).
- **Domain 3, alternative ground.** Actors: exogenous events, routine readings, natural experiments, patient outcomes, markets. Currency: readings already returning something. Anchor: the same world contacted without a controlled intervention. This is the answer domain.
- **Domain 4, independent checking frames.** Actors: other models, instruments, disciplines, datasets. Currency: independence. Anchor: the ground. Same-pool agreement is one witness.
- **Domain 5, verification seat.** Actors: reviewers, refuters, provenance. Currency: refusal. Anchor: the ground. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"* (Fig 10 :42).

Union, not partition: physical impossibility, ethical unacceptability and non-isolability overlap, so a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). Dependency order: describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); instrument the observation that would change the model before planning a route (Fig 3 :27); refuse before mint; settle after the search (Fig 5 :32).

**Comparison with the primary census.** My boundaries match the primary's five domains and its union reading. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29-32 (anchor and independence), Fig 6 :23, :32, :34-35 (reach and instruments), Fig 9 :5, :54, :57 (union), Fig 11 :43-44, :70 (habitat and substitution).

3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete protocol for, say, an epidemiological or climate model: choose specific historical readings, declare which exogenous event is the natural experiment, list the independent data sources, and write the five refuters.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints in the shorthand do not survive the trip back down intact:

- the **anchor-must-not-update test** is easily collapsed into "use more data", which loses the asymmetry Fig 2 :33 requires;
- **independence between checks** is easily collapsed into "use several methods", which loses the evidence-pool test in Fig 2 :40;
- the **habitat-substitution point** is easily collapsed into "validate in simulation", which is exactly the error Fig 11 :70 names.

One thing survives cleanly: describe the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame, prefer a reading already returning something, and let the ground decide. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the law set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
