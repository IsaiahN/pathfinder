# The union of impossibilities, the three searches, and the order of checks

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

"Controlled experiments are impossible or ethically unacceptable" is one sentence covering several different structures. Two questions are owed: (1) is this a partition of separate cases or a union of overlapping conditions, and (2) in what order do the available searches run, so that a budget filter never hands us a verdict?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the anchor set (`Fig 2 :29`: a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome), the two independent instruments (inward, outward), the third question (more search at the same reach), the habitat and the round trip. No new validation primitive is available without import. Bonds held: union (`+`), sequence (`→`), comparison (`⋛`), negation (`¬`). So we can describe a compound, an order, a threshold and a refusal.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Partition or union?

- `Fig 9 :5`: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- `Fig 9 :54`: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- `Fig 9 :57`: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Physical impossibility (scale, irreversibility), ethical unacceptability, and non-isolability overlap in real cases. A climate model is both physically uncontrollable and ethically uncontrollable. ⇒ **Union.** Each condition keeps its own loop and its own possible anchor. The union is *"a specific compound"* (`Fig 9 :58`), not a mixture to be averaged.
- **Falsifier:** if the three conditions could be cleanly separated so that the same validation method worked identically on each, the union reading is wrong and the partition reading stands.

### Q2  -  What is the order of the three searches?

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 names three questions, not one. They are distinct and must not be read against one another.

1. **SEARCHED first.** `Fig 6 :49-50`: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."* and *"Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent."* If the reading already exists in held data and was missed, the remedy is more independent search, not a new method. `Fig 8 :41-42`: *"Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."*
2. **INWARD second.** `Fig 6 :32`: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Sharpen a reading that already returns something: routine monitoring, historical records, administrative counters, patient outcomes.
3. **OUTWARD third.** `Fig 6 :23`: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Import a frame that predicts the shape of the residual: natural experiments (de Vocht et al. 2021), synthetic controls (Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller 2007/2010), pattern-oriented modeling (Grimm et al. 2005), robust decision making across many futures (Lempert et al. 2013). **Import is debited against independence** (`Fig 6 :24`) and needs a source gate and a shadow test (`Fig 6 :11-13`).

- **Why this order.** SEARCHED is cheapest and the other two assume its answer (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7). INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed: one sharpens our own terms, the other casts the description in terms another frame can read (`Fig 6 :29-31`). Reading one against the other is the error.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a case where an imported frame settled a residual that was merely unfound in held data, and where no independence was lost. Under `Fig 8 :42-43` that would be a wrong atom costing more than starting from primitives.

### Q3  -  Are natural experiments and synthetic controls "isomers" of a controlled experiment?

- `Fig 12 :117`: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- `Fig 12 :123`: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- A controlled experiment, a natural experiment and a synthetic control share a **functional group**: an exogenous contrast between exposed and unexposed, read against an outcome. The group says what kind. `Fig 12 :123`.
- They are **not isomers** under the figure's definition, because they do not share the same atom set. A controlled experiment holds the assignment; a natural experiment finds an assignment already made by the world; a synthetic control constructs a comparison from donor units. The arrangement differs in the atoms, not only in the bond. Calling them interchangeable is the flat-formula error (`Fig 12 :94`: *"The arrangement is the substance."*).
- The stronger claim worth testing: over the shared atom set {exogenous variation, outcome, comparator, pre-period reading}, the three are arrangements of the same atoms with different bonds. That is a real isomer claim. It needs an instrument, not an argument.
- **Falsifier:** if one of the three lacks an atom the others require (for example no pre-period reading), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4  -  What is the order of checks inside one loop?

- `Fig 3 :19`: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Dependency order: describe R → instrument the observation that would change the model → choose an anchor that cannot be moved by the model → run independent checks → refuse or accept → record the lineage. `Fig 9 :43`: *"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."*
- Refusal before mint: if a held regularity is false, owe a refusal, not a replacement method (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2). Negation is not a bond (`Fig 12 :134-136`).
- Settlement after the search, never before: `Fig 5 :32`: *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a loop that skipped the effect description and still reliably selected an anchor that later discriminated across several targets.

## CONCLUSION

The impossibility is a **union**, not a partition. The searches run **SEARCHED → INWARD → OUTWARD**, kept distinct, with SEARCHED first because it is cheapest. Natural experiments, synthetic controls and synthetic-harness tests share a functional group with the controlled experiment but are **not** its isomers as usually practised; they differ in atoms, not only bonds. The checks inside one loop are ordered by dependency, and a link with no instrument is not a working link.

This rescues what is defensible in the question's premise without inventing a universal validation method: there is no single substitute for the controlled experiment, but there is a shape that any substitute must have. `Fig 9 :32`: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
