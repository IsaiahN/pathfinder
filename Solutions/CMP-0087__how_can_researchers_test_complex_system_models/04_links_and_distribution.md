## 2. What the wider literature contributes, and its limits

These sources are **secondary corroboration**. The figures decide, the world sources do not overrule them.

- **Sargent (2013)** and **Collins et al. (2024)** corroborate the separation between artefact verification and validity against the target, and the refusal to call a model validated. Low overlap with the framework.
- **de Vocht et al. (2021)** and **Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller (2007/2010)** corroborate the INWARD and OUTWARD routes: use exogenous variation when the researcher cannot assign exposure; build a comparison unit when random assignment is impossible. Low overlap.
- **Grimm et al. (2005)** corroborate testing a complex model against several observed patterns rather than one fitted curve. This is the ecology analogue of independence between checks. Low overlap.
- **Gutenkunst et al. (2007)** corroborate that a tight fit and a settled model are different things. Low overlap.
- **Lempert et al. (2013)** corroborate the budget/filter rule: use computation to find where a plan fails across futures, not to certify one forecast. Low overlap.
- The four essays are by the framework's author. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight; they are used for the meaning of "shape", not as witnesses.

No world source conflicted with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule (back to the domain census) was not triggered.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- `Fig 4 :22`  -  *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A full write-up sent before anyone has described a residual is a recording, not a generator. Lead with the residual and the refuter; offer links after.
- `Fig 10 :42`  -  *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- `Fig 9 :27-28`  -  *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- `Fig 7 :35`  -  *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Do not gather endorsements. Ask for a refutation.
- `Fig 2 :40`  -  *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A list ranked by receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 5. The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. The recipient checks the residual, the anchor test and the five refuters without holding the framework's interiors. `Fig 10 :52`: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
- **Stage 2, opt in only:** if curious, read the figures and the worked sources. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. Check it against your own field first. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
