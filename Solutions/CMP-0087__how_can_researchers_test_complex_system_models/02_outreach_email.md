# Outreach draft  -  check the residual and its refuter before deciding about the method

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 has been checked against your own field. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** What would count as a failure of this validation claim?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A complex-system model predicts an observation for a target system. The ordinary validator intervenes: hold the background fixed, change one thing, read the gap between prediction and outcome. Here that is impossible or unacceptable. You cannot randomise a climate or a city, you cannot assign a population to a harmful exposure, and you cannot hold an open system still. So the missing thing is not effort. It is the observation that would settle the question.
>
> **The output.** I claim that when the intervention is unavailable, a model can be tested only by ranging it separately against an anchor that did not move because of the model. The figures' own anchor list is not experiment-only: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* So the route is (1) describe the residual in effect terms before choosing any method, (2) look for a reading that is already returning something and sharpen it, (3) if necessary import a frame whose closure differs, (4) keep the verdicts separate and report `candidate / unreached` or `unreachable at this budget` rather than `validated`.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that a synthetic test harness validates the real system. A substituted habitat proves wiring, not capability. I am not claiming that an ensemble of models trained on the same data is several witnesses, or that a panel of experts who read the same literature can corroborate each other. Where the evidence pool is shared, the agreement is the pool talking. I am not claiming that surviving every available check proves a model valid. I am not offering a named universal validation technique. This is a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a valid model claim settled by agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool, with no independent anchor, where that agreement later survived contact with an outside ground;
> 2. a case where no reading returned anything at any resolution, yet the model was settled at that budget and the settlement survived an independent anchor;
> 3. success in a substituted habitat establishing capability in the original target without a subsequent reading of that target;
> 4. a finite accumulation of successful checks proving a model always valid;
> 5. a cost filter establishing truth by itself.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Verification and validation of simulation models, Sargent, Journal of Simulation 2013: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/jos.2012.20
> - Conceptualising natural and quasi experiments in public health, de Vocht et al., BMC Medical Research Methodology 2021: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7879679/
> - Evaluation of clinical prediction models, Collins et al., BMJ 2024: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10772854/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
>  -  [name]
