## 0. The residual in effect terms

A system holds a library Γ, a belief b formed from history and inherited prior, and takes an action a. It observes o′ and forms `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot s. The ordinary question asks how to split effort between two operations:

- **Act on what it knows.** Consume the library where its prediction already pays against the ground.
- **Seek new information.** Aim a probe where the library does not yet predict, to change what can be composed or what contact is reachable.

The conventional answer is a scalar: an ε, a temperature, a bonus coefficient, a fraction of trials. That answer treats the two operations as two ends of one dial. The residual asks whether they are the same kind of thing at all.

What an answer would look like: a routing rule indexed per slot, a payment test for the probe, a separation of the currencies, and an honest marked location where a universal number would go if any figure supplied one. Bind that shape as an ordinary term now, so it can accrue a residual like anything else (Formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question because the sought-for shape is not a term it holds).

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least three, not two. There is the operation that consumes the library, the operation that aims a probe, and the routing that assigns a gap to one or the other (plus the refusal case). The question's binary phrasing hides a union (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").
- **Symmetry:** both operations are ordered, not interchangeable. Acting consumes a settled prediction; probing aims at a live gap. A single symmetric mix would overwrite the ordering (Fig 5 :11 to :14 sort the gap into *kinds*, not weights).
- **Scale:** steps 1 to 5 close within one scale; step 6 crosses scales with `R_T` as its error signal. Exploration and exploitation both occur at every scale, and the membrane governs what crosses (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 single-agent loop | Γ, b, a, o′, R per slot, boundary diff | residual and settled terms (bits) | the ground as the only metric (Fig 1 :23) | partition: which bin receives this gap |
| D2 instrument and search | instruments, closure(Γ), searchers, SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD | reach, independence, actions | the ground, plus a proto-instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32) | union of three distinct questions |
| D3 world decision frame | arms, foragers, brains, niches | reward, energy, fitness, value of information | reward distribution / fitness / measured outcome | different closure, secondary only |
| D4 habitat and distributed search | searchers, habitat, introduced actors | independence and surplus | the ground (Fig 11 :73) | union with D2, independence read twice |

**Partition or union.** D1 is a partition of the *gap* into bins. D2 and D4 are unions of overlapping conditions, so one lever clears only the region where a single condition holds (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the"). D3 is a separate closure that corroborates but cannot decide.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Route the gap before choosing the operation (Fig 5 bins precede the drive). Settle before composing (Formula step 5: a gate is not the ground). Describe R before selecting a frame (Fig 9 :28 "any frame you pick will seem to fit"). Keep INWARD and OUTWARD as two tracks, never read one against the other (Fig 6 :31). Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1: The either/or is a union of operations, not one dial (decisive against the scalar)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the"**

Fig 1 :3:

> **"Everything here either produces a residual or consumes the library."**

These are two different functions of the loop, with different currencies. One produces the error signal; the other spends the library. Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies the usual way of scoring them:

> **"Everything else either produces a residual or consumes the library. These do not count:"**
> **"coverage, and compression achieved"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

So the fraction of time spent producing versus consuming is a frame-internal bookkeeping number. It cannot be the verdict about the balance. A single ε is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the answer (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

**Mode:** `general` for the structural refusal of the dial. Triangulated: Fig 1 plus Fig 9, low overlap between the agent-loop account and the leave/arrive account.

### F2: The routing signal is the residual per slot, never a global rate

Symbols table :38: *"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice:"*

Fig 1 :19, :22, :28:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

Symbols table :43: *"One object, region or entity being tracked. R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."* Symbols table :47: *"The part of the residual on slot s that is positive: something happened there the prediction missed."*

A global exploration rate is exactly the aggregation these lines forbid. The correct object is `R⁺_s` per slot: where the residual has positive live mass, a probe is owed; where it does not, the library may be consumed. Fig 1 :20 states the legal state that the global view misses: *"Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert"* (continuing *"one."*).

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 1 and the Symbols table, two different passages of the same primary source family but different quantities (indexing versus live mass). Marked `triangulated` because the claim is carried by two distinct figure lines with low conceptual overlap.

### F3: The four bins, and the curiosity drive when nothing is scoring

Fig 5 :11 to :14:

> **"held: the model was right, nothing owed"**
> **"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"**
> **"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"**
> **"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"**

Fig 5 :16 to :18:

> **"fires when nothing is scoring"**
> **"aimed at the new bin"**
> **"seeks the gap that is large and compressible"**

Fig 5 :46 to :49:

> **"When nothing is scoring, the drive manufactures a gradient."**
> **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."**
> **"Machinery firing is not the milestone. A running system can mint and still return nothing new, which is exactly what the third guard exists to record."**

This is the framework's own version of the explore drive, and it comes with a warning the bandit literature usually omits: the amount of exploring is not evidence that exploring worked. The gap sought is *"large and compressible"*, which is the same quantity the acceptance test scores (Fig 5 :47). So exploration is not random motion; it is aimed at a compressible gap.

There is also a fifth bin, a refusal (Formula step 2, Fig 5 :50 to :56). A term held may be false, and what is owed is a refusal, not more sampling. No amount of exploring reaches the fifth bin (Formula: *"the fifth by what is WRONG, and no arrangement of the other four reaches it"*).

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 5 plus the Formula amendment. World corroboration from Berger-Tal et al. 2014 and Cohen et al. 2007 (section 5), both low overlap with the framework.

### F4: Two incommensurable currencies: bits and actions

Fig 12 :125:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126-127:

> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."**

Fig 12 :130:

> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**

Information gain is a price in bits. Acting is a price in actions. The usual exploration bonus converts the second into the first by fiat, which is the named error. The value-of-information tradition corroborates the structure from outside: information has a price the decision maker would pay, and *"The VoI of observing two new evidence variables is not additive. Instead it is equivalent to observing one, incorporating it into our current evidence, then observing the other"* (Wikipedia, Value of information). Non-additivity is the world's version of "they do not add".

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 12 and the value-of-information source, genuinely low overlap (information economics versus the composition ledger).

### F5: The amount is set by a bargain, not a threshold

Fig 5 :42-43:

> **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."**
> **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing"**

Formula step 3:

> `accept iff  cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R)`
> **"gated by a product; any factor at zero forces inertness:"**

A probe is worth taking when its description cost plus what remains unexplained after it is below the current unexplained cost. There is no fixed proportion; there is a comparison that can be run per candidate. The three guards are a product (Fig 5 :33 *"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."*), so a system minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting (Fig 5 :41 *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."*).

**Mode:** `specified`. Single-source on the figure side (Fig 5 and Formula are the same primary family); the world corroboration is the non-additivity of VoI and the dynamic phase model of Berger-Tal et al., which also refuse a stationary fraction.

### F6: Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong

Fig 2 :39-40, :42:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 6 :50: *"Fails by spending budget on a region already swept. Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent."* Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."*

The number of probes is not the value of the probes. Searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names (Fig 6 :53). So the balance question includes a second axis: how independent the exploring frames are. That axis is priced by the cost of being wrong, not by a fixed rate.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 2 and Fig 8, two figures with low overlap (triangulation geometry versus union surplus), both governing the same independence parameter.

### F7: "Explore" decomposes into three opposed questions

Formula step 7 and Fig 6 :29-31:

> **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**
> **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
> **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**

Fig 6 :49-50:

> **"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."**
> **"Fails by spending budget on a region already swept. Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent."**

So "seek new information" is at least three different operations:

1. **SEARCHED:** more search inside the same closure, distributed and independent.
2. **INWARD:** extend an instrument from one already returning something (Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*).
3. **OUTWARD:** import from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23).

Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts with different instructions (Fig 8 :40-43). Applying more sampling when the right move is an import, or importing when the term is already in the closure and merely unfound, is the failure mode. A single exploration dial cannot see any of this.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 6 and the Formula step 7, plus Fig 8's scope rule.

### F8: Acting on what you know is valid only while the channel holds

Fig 10 :24-28:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients,"**
> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is"**
> **"you have lost touch"**

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33-34: *"The anchor must not update."* and *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

Exploitation is only contact with the ground if the channel still ranges against a non-updating anchor. If the metric saturated or the reading is pooled, acting on the stored prediction is replaying a recording, not using knowledge: Fig 4 :25-26 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."*

This reframes the balance: part of "exploit" is actually channel maintenance, which is a seat's office (Formula step 8: *"Nothing here maintains the ground. The ground does not decay; the channel does, and that is a seat's office, not the loop's."*).

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 10 and Fig 2, low overlap (channel maintenance versus anchor geometry).

### F9: Valence is relative to the residual, so there is no standing winner

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Exploration is not intrinsically good and exploitation is not intrinsically bad. A probe that does not change contact changes nothing. Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* So the verdict is per domain and per residual, never averaged into "some exploration is healthy".

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 11 and the Formula habitat rule.

### F10: The universal fraction is a `gap`, and unreachable is a verdict

No figure line returns an ε, a temperature, an optimal fraction, or a universal allocation across all slots and scales. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 13 :136: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

This is a shape-not-a-name gap, not an underivable-in-principle claim. The framework returns the routing rule, the payment test, and the three probe modes; it does not return the number. To invent the number would be to name what the framework says it can only characterise (canonical README: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*).

**Mode:** `gap` for the number, `general` for the structural claim that the number is not governed. Single-source on the figure side, corroborated by Cohen et al. 2007: *"There is no known generally optimal solution to the exploration versus exploitation problem, and a solution to the general case may indeed not be possible."*

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line returns a universal scalar exploration fraction, temperature or bonus coefficient for all slots and all scales.
2. A measurement shows that information gain alone settles the choice without any reference to the ground, contradicting Fig 1 :27 and Fig 5 :48.
3. A case is demonstrated in which the residual is genuinely global, not per-slot, and a global ratio is the correct object, contradicting Fig 1 :19 and :28.
4. SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD are shown to be the same operation, contradicting Fig 6 :31.
5. A measurement shows exploration that never changes ground contact nevertheless improves capability, contradicting Fig 11 :40.
6. A figure line is found that governs the numeric fraction after a genuine re-census, which would convert the `gap` to `specified`.

If any fires, the routing claim is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 6. Strains, re-census and confirmations

- **S1, word sense, not a conflict.** Fig 6 :26 says *"Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."* Here "explores" describes search inside a closure, not the policy term. Re-census: different sense, no strain. Kept visible because the word invites a false fit.
- **S2, the "nothing is scoring" reading.** Fig 5 :16 *"fires when nothing is scoring"* sounds like explore only after exploit fails. World sources (Berger-Tal's knowledge establishment and accumulation phases) show exploration before any exploitation pay-off. Re-census: the drive fires when nothing is scoring *in the current slot*, and Fig 1 :20 makes a live slot with a near-zero global residual a legal state. Not strained after per-slot indexing. Kept visible.
- **S3, stationary balance versus no general optimum.** Berger-Tal et al. model a time-varying optimal proportion; Cohen et al. say no general solution is known and a general solution may not be possible. This is a world/world tension, not a figure conflict. It is kept as evidence that the question is a union, not a scalar. The conflict rule was not triggered at the figure level.
- **S4, union surplus versus agreement worthlessness.** Fig 8 :45 and Fig 2 :40 appear to pull against each other. Re-census: independence is one quantity read twice (Fig 8 :47). Same parameter, different consequence. Not strained.
- **S5, the gap is not silence.** The absence of a scalar is a `gap` verdict under Fig 13 :134 and Fig 8 :39, not an omission. Confirmed by the canonical README.
- **S6, correction to my own first pass.** The first pass read F1 as refusing the question entirely. Re-census narrows it: the binary is refused as a description, but the residual and the routing rule survive. I kept the earlier reading in this log rather than erasing it.
