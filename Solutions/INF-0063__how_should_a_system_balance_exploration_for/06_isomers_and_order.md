# Isomers and order: why explore and exploit are not two ends of one dial

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `gap` on the numeric balance. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).**

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary phrasing asks for a *balance* between two quantities. In the figure vocabulary a balance is a comparison, ⋛. Two questions are owed:

1. Is ⋛ well typed when its operands are the two currencies of the loop?
2. Are exploration and exploitation two amounts of one substance, or two different substances over a shared atom set (Fig 12's isomer question)?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the live residual `R⁺_s`, Γ, b, a, o′, the ground, instruments, the boundary diff, settled terms. Bonds held: `+ ∥ ≡ → ⇒ − ⋛` plus the unary negation ¬. No new primitive is available without import. Currencies held: bits (description) and actions (settlement), which do not add (Fig 12 :125).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is "balance" a well-typed operator here?

The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Fig 12 :52 says *"a goal is a comparison"*, and the Operators table :51 repeats it.

Fig 12 :125-130:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**
> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**

So "balance exploration against acting" is ⋛ applied to quantities in different units. The comparison is mistyped until something supplies a common measure. The usual move is to convert actions into bits (an exploration bonus denominated in reward), which is the "one accounting" the figure forbids. Value-of-information gives a common monetary measure, but VoI is defined as what a decision maker would pay, and the value of two observations is *"not additive. Instead it is equivalent to observing one, incorporating it into our current evidence, then observing the other."* Even the common measure is sequential, not a single static ratio.

**Result:** the question as phrased is not answerable as a comparison until the two currencies are either kept apart or a source supplies a conversion with provenance. This is a type finding, not a refusal of the whole question. **Falsifier:** a figure line or a measured common unit that licenses ⋛ over bits and actions directly. None was found. `specified`, single-source on the figure side, with low-overlap world corroboration from information economics.

### Q2: Are the two operations isomers or amounts of one thing?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Write the two operations over the same atom set `{gap, Γ, prediction, observation, ground, instrument}`:

- **Act on what is known.** The held bin: *"held: the model was right, nothing owed"* (Fig 5 :11). The binding is closest to `≡` identity, *"two names, one referent"*, with the test *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* Prediction and observation substitute without loss; nothing is owed.
- **Seek new information.** The new bin: *"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"* (Fig 5 :12). The binding is `→` sequence, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, because the probe must precede the observation that resolves it. Where the gap is *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"* (Fig 5 :14), the bond is `⇒` production, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, since the term did not exist before the probe fired.
- **Refit.** *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"* (Fig 5 :13). The bond is `−` subtraction, *"A with B removed or absent"*, where the second operand's absence is the point.
- **Refuse.** The fifth bin (Formula step 2) is ¬ negation, *"this does not hold"*. Fig 12 amendment: negation is a unary operator, not a bond, *"it joins nothing, the symmetry question does not apply to it."*

Over a shared atom set, the first three are **isomers** in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The refusal is not an isomer, because negation is not one of the bonds. This is the structural reason a single dial fails: a dial can scale one substance up or down, but it cannot change a bond type.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if exploration and exploitation are shown to differ only in a scalar parameter over the same bond, the isomer claim fails and the single-dial reading stands instead. If the refusal can be written as a binary bond, the amendment is wrong.

### Q3: What order obtains between them?

Fig 12 :103-104:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**
> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

A settled act becomes an atom for the next probe. The loop alternates, but the alternation is not a ratio: it is a change of role. Fig 13 :27-31 states the same alternation for the six-term substrate: *"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."* and *"each step acts on one kind and yields the other."*

Fig 12 :111-112:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

A vocabulary holding only "explore" and "exploit" names two states and cannot name the route between them. What it lacks is the bond vocabulary, not a better setting on the dial.

**Order by dependency:** route the gap (Fig 5 bins); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); settle before composing (Formula step 5); then let the settled molecule become an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104). This ordering is the chain Fig 3 describes: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

### Q4: Does the world literature give the same shape?

Lower-overlap corroboration:

- **Foraging.** The marginal value theorem gives a *leaving* rule: the tangent to the diminishing-returns curve given travel time. That is a per-patch comparison with a stated currency and constraints, not a global fraction of time spent searching. (Wikipedia, Optimal foraging theory.)
- **Bandit methods.** Epsilon-greedy, epsilon-first and epsilon-decreasing are all scalar policies; the pricing strategies explicitly set *"the expected reward plus an estimation of extra future rewards that will gain through the additional knowledge"*. This is a conversion of actions into a single account, which is exactly the "one accounting" of Fig 12 :126. (Wikipedia, Multi-armed bandit.)
- **Cognition.** Cohen et al. 2007 distinguish expected from unexpected uncertainty and report frontopolar activity when subjects explore, alongside a statement that no general optimum is known. The distinction is a routing signal (which bin), not a ratio.
- **Multidisciplinary model.** Berger-Tal et al. 2014 produce four ordered phases with an optimal time-varying proportion. The phases are ordered states, which is the Fig 12 :111 shape, not a fixed amount of exploration.

These corroborate the shape from three genuinely different pools. They do not supply the numeric fraction.

## CONCLUSION

The "balance" is a ⋛ comparison whose operands sit in two currencies that do not add, so the comparison is mistyped until a source supplies a conversion with provenance. Over a shared atom set, acting, probing, refitting and refusing are different bonds (isomers for the first three, a unary refusal for the fourth), not amounts of one substance. What survives is a routing rule per slot, a two-currency ledger, and a three-mode separator for "explore". The universal fraction remains a `gap` and is reported as unreachable rather than as silence.
