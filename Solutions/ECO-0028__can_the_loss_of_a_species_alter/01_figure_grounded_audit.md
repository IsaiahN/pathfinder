## 0. The claim under audit (residual as effect)

> Can the loss of a species alter evolutionary trajectories enough to cause further losses?

Stated as an effect, not a topic:

- **R:** one primary extinction occurs; the residual is the unexplained variance in what happens next to the remaining species, their interactions, and their trajectories. `R` is indexed by object slot (species, relation, site), never averaged. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **Option A (cascade):** the loss removes an actor, the removal changes contact, the changed contact changes selection on survivors, and the change compounds until a second species can no longer persist. Leads to: priority on high-cascade actors and on enumerating interactions, and to permanent management.
- **Option B (absorption):** the loss is buffered by redundancy, rewiring, or abundance compensation, so no further loss follows. Leads to: species-by-species abundance triage and a one-off intervention.
- **The threshold:** `enough to cause further losses` is a comparison, not a quantity we hold. The framework returns a shape, not a name or a number: Fig 9 :32,  "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". The threshold is decided at the ground of a particular community.

**Direction discipline (Formula, `Direction`).** The sought-for shape is made an ordinary term before the run: a cascade answer must name (i) the removed actor, (ii) the changed contact, (iii) the compounded remainder, (iv) the second loss, and (v) the ground that would show the second loss was caused by the first. If any of the five cannot be stated, the shape has not been answered, only gestured at.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** at least two (a primary loss and a downstream response), often three or more, since the effect can be `three steps out` (Fig 11 :52). It is not a one-variable question.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric and ordered. Removal is not the inverse of addition: `The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not.` (Fig 11 :61). Isolation is substitution, not subtraction: `Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another.` (Fig 11 :66). The bond is therefore `→` sequence / `⇒` production, not `+` conjunction.
- **Scale:** crosses from one slot (a species) to a network of slots (a community), and from ecological to evolutionary time. The crossing is lossy and non-invertible: `The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside.` (Fig 10 :70).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (name it or it is not a domain) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 ecological habitat, unmaintained | species in contact, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) (Fig 11 :45-47) | contact and persistence | the ground: `Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept.` (Fig 11 :56) | enumeration, never composition |
| D2 evolutionary trajectory | lineages, heritable phenotypes, the remainder each turn leaves | heritable change; the residual that re-enters the next turn | the ground, which settles and does not decay: `the ground does not decay, the channel does` (Fig 10 :24) | non-invertible transform, compounding remainder |
| D3 managed/conservation frame | managers, instruments, introduced actors | introduction (available), not subtraction | the ground still decides: `the ground still decides` (Fig 11 :22); the seat authors conventions, not verdicts | permanent work, asymmetric power |

**Partition or union?** Union compound. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Ecological contact (D1) and evolutionary trajectory (D2) are two conditions in the same system, and management (D3) overlaps both. Fig 9 :58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". So there is no single lever, and no single verdict to average. Each domain gets its own loop.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order is: (1) perception, enumerate the habitat; (2) vocabulary, have terms for what the lost species afforded (pollination, predation, habitat); (3) objective, say what counts as a further loss; (4) planning, choose an intervention; (5) learn and carry. The chain usually breaks between 2 and 3, because counting species is easy while naming what one species afforded is hard. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." So effort spent on better abundance monitoring (link 1) cannot rescue a missing account of what the species did (link 2).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The habitat is enumerated, never composed (governs D1)

Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

The first move is not to model the cascade. It is to read off the cascade's habitat: actors, conditions, relations. Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." A model that assigns cascade probabilities without first enumerating contact has composed a habitat it did not author, which the figures forbid.

**Mode:** `general` (structural rule). **Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 9 :27-28,  "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit", two low-overlap routes). **Refuter:** exhibit a case where a cascade was correctly predicted from a habitat that was never enumerated, and where the prediction did not depend on any prior enumeration by another party.

### F2 - Contact cascades are real and can be third order (governs D1, decides A over B in principle)

Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles". The full line continues `first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out.` So a first-order reading of the habitat misses the effect that matters. Fig 13 :24 names the structure that carries it: `that entities relate, and influence travels`. Fig 13 :31 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos".

The structural verdict is that A is possible and B is not guaranteed: reach exists in the network, so a loss can propagate further than the direct neighbours of the lost species. But the figures also forbid turning this into a universal frequency claim, because `frequency` is a ground quantity, not a structural one (Formula step 5, `the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground.`).

**Mode:** `general` on possibility, `specified` on the shape of the propagation. **Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 DDT case plus Fig 13 network and chaos, low overlap between an ecological case and a substrate structure). **Refuter:** a demonstrated case where a primary loss changed contact in an enumerated habitat, the remainder compounded through a relation that carries influence, and no further loss occurred anywhere in the cascade, with the instrument's detection threshold stated.

### F3 - Valence is relative to the residual, so `which species matters` is a question, not a list (governs D1)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

So the answer cannot be a fixed list of `keystone` or `foundation` species. A species that stabilises one residual destabilises another. The figure's own warning applies: an actor's cascade role is a relation, and it changes when the residual changes. The world literature makes the same point quantitatively (Eklöf 2006, Gillis et al. 2025, ledger), and its disagreements are expected under this rule rather than surprising.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus the Operators table's ordered bonds, and world sources at low overlap with the figure text). **Refuter:** exhibit a standing list of species whose cascade role is invariant across at least three different residuals, measured, not assumed.

### F4 - A loss is a subtraction, and the aftermath is a sequence, not a conjunction (governs D2, bond choice)

Operators table: `−` means `A with B removed or absent`, with the test `is the second operand's absence the point? Yes.` This is the primary loss. The aftermath is not `+` (both hold at once) but `→` (`A then B; B needs A to have happened`) and often `⇒` (`A produces B; B did not exist before`), because a novel interaction can follow a removal. Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Counting species before and after names a state; it cannot name the route between them.

Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two communities with the same species list can have different cascade fates because the bonds differ. This is why a species count is not a sufficient answer.

**Mode:** `general` (notation), `specified` (the particular bond reading). **Verdict:** single-source for the bond definitions (Operators table / Fig 12, one instrument), corroborated in world by the network literature. **Refuter:** show that a species removal is adequately represented by a symmetric bond, or that two communities with identical species and identical bonds have identical cascade fates in every measured case.

### F5 - Trajectories are non-invertible; the loss changes what selection runs against (governs D2)

Fig 10 :68 "a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it." Fig 10 :70 "The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside." Fig 13 :28 "They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from." Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."

Therefore: yes, the loss alters trajectories, because it changes the habitat against which selection runs, and the transform back to the old trajectory does not exist. Reintroducing the species later is not playback. Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." This is the figure-grounded reason the word `enough` in the question is not a one-off threshold but a property of a changed field of contact.

**Mode:** `general` on non-invertibility, `specified` on the ecological reading. **Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 10 tree of life plus Fig 13 alternation plus Fig 11 selection, three distinct figure passages). **Refuter:** demonstrate an exact inversion, a restored community whose subsequent trajectory is identical to the counterfactual unloss trajectory under the same external conditions, with the matching instrument stated.

### F6 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, not a species trait (governs D1 and D2 together)

Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Applied here: a species is not `resilient` in isolation. Its capacity to persist is a property of the species plus its habitat. So a primary loss changes the capability of the survivors even if their own traits do not change. This is a figure-derived reason to reject option B's strongest form (the claim that redundancy alone absorbs the loss) without yet claiming a measured cascade frequency.

**Mode:** `general`. **Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 capability claim plus Fig 10 :68 level-relative transform). The figure itself marks this as a claim that fits known cases and has not been tested forward: Fig 11 :71,  "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim". **Refuter:** that exact case, an agent-only change that moves the ground.

### F7 - The managed response is asymmetric and permanent (governs D3)

Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :22 "instrument, distil, introduce; the ground still decides". Fig 11 :65 "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."

So conservation cannot undo a loss by subtracting the cause; it can only add, and it must keep adding. The failure mode is introduction with the cascade unenumerated, which the figure names explicitly. This is the figure's own ecological example, so D3 is `single-source` within the figures (Fig 11 only), with the world literature on ecosystem engineering as secondary corroboration.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Verdict:** single-source within the figures. **Refuter:** a managed ecosystem that reaches a solved state after a one-off subtraction, with no further introduction and no further ground pressure, sustained over a stated observation window.

### F8 - This is a union compound and must be unbundled before search (method)

Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". D1 (contact), D2 (trajectory), and D3 (management) overlap. A single lever, for example `protect the keystone species`, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The honest output is three verdicts, not one averaged verdict. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

**Mode:** `general` (method). **Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 9 union split plus Fig 1 per-slot residual). **Refuter:** a case where a single intervention settles all three domains at once and the three conditions are demonstrably non-overlapping.

### F9 - World sources corroborate the shape and expose a real conflict, which returns us to the census

The world literature opened for this audit is unusually rich and mostly agrees with the figure shape on possibility: coextinction amplifies primary loss (Koh et al. 2004; Strona and Bradshaw 2022, reporting about 184 percent amplification under an intermediate scenario), the loss of highly connected and low-trophic species triggers the most secondary extinctions (Eklöf 2006), and evolution itself can cause the loss of another species in the network (Loeuille 2019, `evolutionary murder`). Early-warning work (Scheffer et al. 2009) supplies the instrument class that could decide the threshold.

It also contains a conflict that must not be smoothed over. A 2022 review in *Trends in Ecology and Evolution* states that simulations predict cascades should be commonplace while field observations rarely match them, and a 2025 review of keystone predation found only 10 of 25 explicit manipulative experiments gave consistent support. Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The conflict does not flip the figure verdict. It sends us back to F1: unenumerated habitats and fixed rooms produce mismatches. Fig 10 :72 "A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

**Mode:** `specified` on the conflict reading. **Verdict:** world sources are corroboration only; their agreement with the figures is one evidence pool in broad ecology and is recorded as such in the ledger. **Refuter:** a body of field experiments in enumerated habitats that finds predicted cascades at the predicted rate and with the predicted actors, in which case the mismatch explanation is withdrawn.

## 4. Route when no line closes the residual (Formula step 7)

No figure line yields a numerical threshold for a named species, and that is a genuine limit rather than a silence. Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The three questions are kept distinct:

1. **SEARCHED?** Is the threshold in our closure and unfound? Partly. The coextinction and early-warning literatures (ledger) contain threshold estimators, but none is a figure line, and importing one with a source gate and a shadow test is a `specified` move, not a derivation. Fig 6 :14 calls import `the only operator that moves the wall`; it is debited against independence.
2. **INWARD?** Extend the instrument from one already returning something. Early-warning statistics (rising variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, critical slowing down) are instruments built on a reading that already resolves change. Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
3. **OUTWARD?** Find a frame whose closure predicts the shape. Field removal experiments and long-term observation (Paine's *Pisaster* removal, the 30-year bison study cited by Gillis et al. 2025) are the low-overlap frame that can carry the residual across. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

These are three tracks, never one, and INWARD and OUTWARD are never read against each other (Fig 6 :31).

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline that `a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument`. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A primary loss in an enumerated habitat, with influence-carrying relations present and a compounded remainder, is followed by no further loss in any downstream slot, with the detection threshold of the instrument stated. This kills F2.
2. An exact inversion is demonstrated: a restored community whose subsequent trajectory matches the unloss counterfactual under the same external conditions. This kills F5.
3. A standing list of species is shown to have invariant cascade valence across three or more residuals, measured. This kills F3.
4. A managed ecosystem reaches a solved state after a one-off subtraction, with no further introduction and no further ground pressure, over a stated observation window. This kills F7.
5. Two communities with identical species and identical bonds have identical cascade fates in every measured case. This kills F4.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the domain census is rerun. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Canonical usage check (figures README, read after sections 0 to 6)

The canonical README says the figures `outrank everything else in the repo: the code, the rulings and the docs answer to them... the quotes are the authority, not this summary` and that `a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain, not found a flaw`. The architecture essay (*Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture*, 2026-08-19) adds a direct warning for this question in its section `Three mappings offered and declined`: `The habitat as an ecological niche. A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes.` It concludes that `the resemblance is real and the definitions do not align`. So the figure's `habitat` must not be equated with an ecological niche; the relation is to a moving residual, not to a fixed organism-environment pairing. That is why Fig 11 :43 says `enumerated, never composed` rather than borrowing the ecological term. Three consequences were applied:

- The apparent conflict between Fig 11 (cascades happen) and the world reports of weak empirical support was resolved by re-census, not by softening either: Fig 11 governs structural reach; the field reports govern frequency at a ground. Different quantities.
- The framework returns `what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)`. So no figure line is used to name a particular species as the cascade trigger, and no number is presented as the threshold.
