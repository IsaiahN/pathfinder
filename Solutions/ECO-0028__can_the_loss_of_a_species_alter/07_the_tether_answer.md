# The Tether's answer: can one species loss cause further losses?

**Mode: `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No site was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

One primary extinction occurs. The unexplained variance is what happens to the remaining species, their interactions and their trajectories. Option A says the loss changes contact, the change compounds, and a second loss can follow. Option B says redundancy or rewiring absorbs it. The figures do not return a frequency or a named species. They return the shape any settled answer must have, plus the instrument that would close it. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

## 2. The shape, in three separate verdicts

### D1, ecological habitat: A is structurally licensed, B is not guaranteed

The habitat is read off the world, not composed: Fig 11 :43,  "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Influence travels through relations: Fig 13 :24,  "that entities relate, and influence travels". Contact can reach further than immediate neighbours: Fig 11 :52,  "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles". So a loss can alter the field of contact of species it never directly touched, and the remainder can compound (Fig 13 :31 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos").

But which species matters is a question, not a list: Fig 11 :49,  "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A high-cascade actor in one residual can be inert in another. **Verdict:** `yes, in principle; which loss, how often, and by how much are ground questions`. `triangulated` (Fig 11 case plus Fig 13 structure, low overlap).

### D2, evolutionary trajectory: yes, and the change is one-way

A loss changes what selection runs against: Fig 11 :36,  "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." The transform back is not available: Fig 10 :70,  "The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside." Fig 10 :68 "a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."

Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, not of the species alone: Fig 11 :40,  "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." So a primary loss changes the capability of survivors even if their own traits are unchanged. **Verdict:** `yes; trajectories change, the change is non-invertible, reintroduction is not playback`. `triangulated` (Fig 10 plus Fig 13 plus Fig 11).

### D3, managed response: additive and permanent

The seat can add and cannot subtract: Fig 11 :61,  "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Introduction with the cascade unenumerated is the named failure, Fig 11 :65. **Verdict:** `further losses can be buffered only by continued, enumerated introduction; there is no one-off undo`. `single-source` within the figures (Fig 11), with world corroboration recorded in `09`.

### Why the three verdicts are never averaged

Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single sentence answer that merges D1, D2 and D3 would hide the only checkable parts.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that cascades always happen, or happen at any stated rate. Frequency is a ground quantity (Formula step 5: `the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground.`).
- Not which species is the trigger. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not that the figure's `habitat` is an ecological niche. The architecture essay declines that mapping: `the resemblance is real and the definitions do not align`.
- Not that natural selection is the whole framework. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: `Natural selection is not the whole answer. It is a special case.`
- Not that restoring a species restores the old trajectory. Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is claimed for any community.

## 4. What would settle it (the ground)

The ground is a site, a removal experiment, a long-term series, a measured second extinction. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Three instrument classes are already returning something and can be extended inward (Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."):

1. **Enumeration instruments:** interaction observations that already record who contacts whom. Extend them from direct to second and third order, as the DDT case requires.
2. **Early-warning statistics:** rising variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, critical slowing down (Scheffer et al. 2009). These are a `specified` import with a source gate, not a figure derivation.
3. **Field removal experiments:** Paine's *Pisaster* removal and its 50-year audit trail provide a low-overlap frame. Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5: a cascading change with no second loss anywhere downstream; an exact inversion restoring the old trajectory; valence-invariant species across three residuals; a solved managed ecosystem after one-off subtraction; identical bonds and identical fates in every case.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is complete; the site-level threshold is not. Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figures stop at what the available instruments can distinguish and record the lineage so the stop is checkable. What is recorded here is the route, the refuters and the gap, not a verdict about a particular species. The world literature is unusually rich, but most of it shares an ecology evidence pool, and its own theory/field mismatch is a reason to enumerate more carefully, not a reason to flip the shape (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a species or a deadline. It says that a species loss changes the field of contact of the survivors, that influence can travel two or three steps out before a second loss appears, and that the trajectory a community then follows cannot be played back to the old one, because a loss substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. So yes, a loss *can* cause further losses, but which loss and how often are questions for a site, not for a framework. The answer also has a management half: you can add actors to a changed habitat and you cannot subtract the change, so stewardship is permanent work. What is offered is the shape, the three refuters written in advance, and the instrument classes that would close it. The threshold stays open until the ground settles it.
