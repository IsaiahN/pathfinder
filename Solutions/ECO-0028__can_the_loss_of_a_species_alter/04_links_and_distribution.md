## Wider literature that bears on the question

These are the world sources in `09_watson_answer.md` and `10_source_ledger.csv`, grouped by what they do. They are corroboration only and never overrule a figure verdict.

**Reach and amplification (corroborate D1).** Koh et al. 2004 (*Science*) recalibrate extinction estimates for coextinction. Dunn et al. 2009 (*Proc R Soc B*) state that losing a species is expected to cause cascading effects across trophic levels, while also noting that few coextinction events have actually been recorded. Strona and Bradshaw 2022 (*Science Advances*) report coextinctions increasing primary losses by about 184 percent under an intermediate scenario and a network-structure cost larger than the diversity cost. Kehoe and Frago 2021 (*Ecological Entomology*) describe waves of secondary extinctions as a hidden driver of insect decline.

**Which species matter (corroborates F3, strains standing lists).** Eklöf 2006 (*Journal of Animal Ecology*) finds that loss of highly connected and low-trophic species triggers the most secondary extinctions on average. Gillis, Thomsen and Tonkin 2025 (*Ecology and Evolution*) audit Paine's classic removal result and find consistent support in only 10 of 25 explicit experiments. Both can be true if cascade role is relative to the residual.

**The threshold and the conflict (ground questions).** Scheffer et al. 2009 (*Nature*) supply early-warning signals for critical transitions, an instrument class already returning something and therefore extendable inward (Fig 6 :32). Morton et al. 2022 (*Trends in Ecology & Evolution*) state that simulations predict frequent cascades while field observations rarely match them. That conflict sends us back to enumeration; it does not flip the figure verdict.

**The evolutionary half (corroborates D2).** Loeuille 2019 (*F1000Research*) introduces `evolutionary murder`, where evolution of one species causes the loss of another in the network. Bastazini et al. 2022 (*Ecological Modelling*) show that the mode of trait evolution changes network robustness. Both are low-overlap frames relative to pure ecology.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 11 :43** `The habitat is enumerated, never composed.` Enumerate the recipient's habitat (what they study, what residual their work touches) before choosing which frame to offer. Do not send a generic pitch to a generic list.
- **Fig 10 :52** `Bring a characterised residual, never the source.` The first contact carries the shape and the refuter, not the framework.
- **Fig 10 :42** `A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.` A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before answering has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** `describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit.` Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :35** `Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.` Do not ask for agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40** `agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.` Later agreement is not pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** `Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.` One-to-one contact, public opt-in venues, no bulk send.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45, `Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it.` The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors; once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Record the stage-1 verdict first.
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 `Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.` The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` `Direction`: a frame `cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds`. Each user writes a fresh refuter and states a mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. That is why the refuters are in front of the links and why no frequency or trigger species is asserted. The output is a shape, not a result.
