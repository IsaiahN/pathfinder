## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a primary extinction can change the trajectories of survivors enough to produce further extinctions. Option A: yes, structurally, because the loss changes a field of contact and the change compounds through influence-carrying relations. Option B: no, because redundancy, rewiring or abundance compensation absorbs the loss. What each leads to: A makes conservation about enumerating interactions and about high-cascade actors, and makes stewardship permanent work; B makes conservation a species-by-species abundance triage with a one-off intervention. The figures decide the shape. The ground of a particular site decides the number.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** Unexplained variance in survivors and their trajectories after one extinction. Arity: at least two, often three or more (Fig 11 :52, the effect `three steps out`). Symmetry: asymmetric and ordered; subtraction is not reversible (Fig 11 :61,  "Introduction is available; exclusion is not"). Scale: one slot to a network of slots, and ecological to evolutionary time (Fig 10 :70). Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **D1, ecological habitat, unmaintained.** Actors: species in contact, conditions, relations (Fig 11 :45-47). Currency: contact and persistence. Anchor: the ground, `what survives is what the ground kept` (Fig 11 :56). Union condition, not a partition boundary.
- **D2, evolutionary trajectory.** Actors: lineages, heritable phenotypes, the remainder each turn leaves. Currency: heritable change. Anchor: the ground, `the ground does not decay, the channel does` (Fig 10 :24). Non-invertible transform (Fig 10 :70).
- **D3, managed / conservation frame.** Actors: managers, instruments, introduced actors. Currency: addition, not subtraction. Anchor: the ground still decides (Fig 11 :22). Single-source within the figures (Fig 11 only).
- **Question type.** Union compound (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"), so unbundle before search. Never average the three verdicts (Fig 1 :22).
- **Dependency order.** Enumerate habitat, acquire vocabulary for what the loss afforded, state what counts as a further loss, then plan intervention (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."). The chain usually breaks at vocabulary (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**Yes in principle, per domain, and never averaged.** D1: a loss changes the field of contact and influence can travel two or three steps before a second loss, but which loss, how often and by how much are ground questions. D2: trajectories change and cannot be played back, because the transform is non-invertible and the habitat is substituted rather than removed. D3: further losses can be buffered only by continued, enumerated introduction, never by a one-off subtraction. The threshold `enough to cause further losses` is not a figure output; the framework returns a shape and stops. This is `specified` and ungrounded for any named site.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the shape is wrong, the failure is one of five pre-registered refuters in `01` section 5, each attached to a specific domain, so the error is findable rather than diffuse. The real avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion before the refuter. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 4 :30 requires the round-trip gap `R_T` to be measured rather than assumed zero.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no field is edited. A wrong finding is removed from the reach by adding its refusal (Fig 6 :41-42), and the corrected packet in `08` supersedes any earlier draft. A claim sent to a journal can be corrected or retracted. Real ecological losses are not undoable, which is exactly D2 and D3.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, never agreement. A site-level removal experiment in an enumerated habitat that shows a compounded remainder and no second loss anywhere downstream would kill F2. An exact inversion restoring the counterfactual trajectory would kill F5. A measured valence-invariant species list across three residuals would kill F3. A managed ecosystem reaching a solved state after one-off subtraction would kill F7. An early-warning instrument that forecasts the threshold at a declared error rate would move the threshold from ground question to `specified` instrument. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
