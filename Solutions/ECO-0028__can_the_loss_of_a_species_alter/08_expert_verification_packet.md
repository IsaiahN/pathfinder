# Expert verification packet: the Tether on species loss and further losses

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecosystem-function researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement at any site is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A species loss removes an actor and changes the field of contact of the survivors. In an unmaintained habitat, influence travels through relations and can reach two or three steps out before a second loss appears, so a loss *can* cause further losses. Which loss, at which site and with what threshold is a question for the ground, not for the framework; a standing list of `important` species does not travel between residuals, because an actor's valence is relative to the residual. The trajectory the community then follows cannot be played back, because the transform is non-invertible and the loss substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. The managed response is therefore additive and permanent: you can introduce actors into a changed habitat and you cannot subtract the change. What is offered is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument classes that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - ecology-facing; checkable against an enumerated habitat

- **Statement:** a primary loss can reach a second loss through relations that carry influence, beyond the immediate neighbours of the lost species. Reach is structural; frequency is a ground quantity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 `The habitat is enumerated, never composed.` Fig 11 :44 `List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering.` Fig 11 :52 `Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles` Fig 13 :24 `that entities relate, and influence travels`
- **What to check:** in a habitat you know, were the relations that carried the effect enumerated outward to the point where the cascade stopped mattering, or was the scope set at direct contact? Did the measured second loss sit at one step or several?
- **Refuter:** a primary loss in an enumerated habitat, with influence-carrying relations present and a compounded remainder, followed by no further loss in any downstream slot, with the instrument's detection threshold stated. Also relevant: Morton et al. 2022 report that simulations predict frequent cascades while field observations rarely match them; that is a frequency conflict, not a refutation of reach, and it is recorded as such.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against a standing species list

- **Statement:** cascade role is relative to the residual, so no fixed list of high-impact species survives a change of question. Two communities with the same species list can stand as isomers under Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 `An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries.` Fig 11 :51 `No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.` Fig 12 :117 `Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour.`
- **What to check:** does the species you would nominate as the trigger hold that role across at least three different residuals, measured? Eklöf 2006 reports that loss of highly connected and low-trophic species triggers the most secondary extinctions on average, while Gillis et al. 2025 find only 10 of 25 explicit keystone-predation experiments gave consistent support. Both can be true under this claim.
- **Refuter:** a standing list of species whose cascade role is invariant across three or more residuals, measured, not assumed; or two communities with identical actor sets and identical measured relations but identical cascade fates with no unmeasured condition left to explain it.

### C3 - framework-internal with an eco-evolutionary edge; checkable by reading

- **Statement:** the trajectory change is one-way. A loss substitutes a habitat; reintroducing the actor is an addition to a changed field, not playback. Managed response is permanent work.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :70 `The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside.` Fig 10 :68 `a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it.` Fig 11 :66 `Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another.` Fig 11 :61 `The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not.` Fig 11 :63 `Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state.`
- **What to check:** is there any case where restoring the lost actor restored the original trajectory under the same external conditions? Loeuille 2019 raises the matching eco-evolutionary point that evolution of one species can cause the loss of another in the network, which is a mechanism for one-way change, not a proof of it.
- **Refuter:** an exact inversion, a restored community whose subsequent trajectory matches the unloss counterfactual under the same external conditions, with the matching instrument stated; or a managed ecosystem reaching a solved state after a one-off subtraction over a stated observation window.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that cascades always happen, or at any stated rate. Frequency is a ground quantity.
- Not that a threshold can be computed from the figures. `you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself` (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a named trigger species. The figure's `habitat` is contact with a moving residual, not an ecological niche; the architecture essay declines that mapping with `the resemblance is real and the definitions do not align`.
- Not that natural selection is the whole framework. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: `Natural selection is not the whole answer. It is a special case.`
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was measured at a site.
- Not a claim that any expert should agree. Agreement is not the instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not a further argument. The figures name three instrument classes already returning something:

1. **Enumeration instruments:** interaction observations that already record who contacts whom; extend from direct to second and third order.
2. **Early-warning statistics:** rising variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, critical slowing down (Scheffer et al. 2009). A `specified` import with a source gate, not a figure derivation.
3. **Field removal experiments:** Paine's *Pisaster* removal and its long audit trail; a low-overlap frame for the same residual.

For each: state the detection threshold before the run, then report the first downstream loss or its absence. Fig 2 :29: `An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks.`

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-44, :49, :51, :52 and Fig 13 :24, five short passages.
2. Pick a habitat you know. Say whether the relations carrying a loss were enumerated outward, or scoped to direct contact.
3. Check C2 against a list of species you would nominate as high impact. Does any one hold the role across three different residuals?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a second loss from a detection limit, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
