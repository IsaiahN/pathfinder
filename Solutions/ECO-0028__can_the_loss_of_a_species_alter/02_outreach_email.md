# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the shape claim and its refuter in stage 1. The record of the stage-1 verdict must exist before any framework material is shown.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about species loss and further losses

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on three separate verdicts, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** the claim is that a species loss changes the field of contact of the survivors, and that influence can travel two or three steps out through relations before a second loss appears, so a loss *can* cause further losses. Which loss, at which site, and with what threshold is left open. The framework does not return a name or a number for that. It also says that the trajectory after the loss cannot be played back, because the loss substitutes a habitat rather than removing one, and that the managed response is therefore additive and permanent, not a one-off undo.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that cascades always happen, or happen at any measured rate; that any named species is the trigger; that the loss of species X will cause the extinction of Y by year Z. Cascade role is relative to the residual in question, so a standing list of high-impact species does not survive a change of question. Two communities with the same species list can still behave differently because the relations differ. I am also not claiming the figure's `habitat` is the same thing as an ecological niche. The framework's own essay declines that mapping.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. A primary loss in an enumerated habitat, with influence-carrying relations present and a compounded remainder, followed by no further loss anywhere downstream, with the detection threshold stated.
> 2. A species list whose cascade role is invariant across three different residuals, measured.
> 3. A restored community whose subsequent trajectory matches the unloss counterfactual under the same external conditions, or a managed ecosystem reaching a solved state after a one-off subtraction.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw the relevant part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three short sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers, especially Figure 11 (the habitat) and Figure 13 (the minimum viable set): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay, including the section where the habitat-as-niche mapping is declined: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - The worked derivation and the eight-step formula: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a habitat you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The architecture essay warns that simulated outputs `could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula`, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, framework second | Fig 10 :52 `Bring a characterised residual, never the source.` The reader checks the shape without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 `Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.` |
| Pre-registered refuter | The discipline that `a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument`. |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: `Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which.` |
| No named trigger species | Fig 11 :51 `No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.` |
| Habitat mapping explicitly declined | The architecture essay's `Three mappings offered and declined`: `the resemblance is real and the definitions do not align`. |
| No frequency claim | Formula step 5: `the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground.` |
| Links after a verdict | Fig 10 :42 `A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.` A reader who has absorbed the framework before answering has joined the frame being checked. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 `Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.` Giving away a generator does not give away an answer. |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` `Direction`: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |
| No prestige ask, no bulk send | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: `Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly.` Fig 2 :40: `agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool`. |

## Two-stage record to keep

1. **Stage 1:** send the paragraph labelled `The output` and the three refuters only. Record the recipient's verdict verbatim, with date. Do not show the links or the framework.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** if they are curious, send the three sources with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction to write a fresh refuter and state a mode per problem.
