## What is being decided, in effect terms

Option A: a primary loss changes the habitat (the field of contact of the remaining species), the change compounds through the interaction network, and it can cross a threshold into further extinctions. Option B: the loss is absorbed by redundancy, rewiring, or compensation, and no further loss follows. Each option leads to a different conservation rule: A prioritises the loss of high-cascade actors and the enumeration of interactions, B prioritises species-by-species abundance triage. The figures decide the shape of the answer; the ground of a particular community decides the number.

## Boundary and honesty

Everything here is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. By *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, LLM output is a simulacrum and `the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself`. This package cannot settle the ecological question it reports on. It returns the shape a settled answer would have and the instruments that would close it.

No figure was edited. No file outside `Solutions/ECO-0028__can_the_loss_of_a_species_alter/` was modified. Figure quotes were taken from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`. World sources were opened on 2026-11-23 and are listed with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35, `Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.` A list selected for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes agreement worthless (Fig 2 :40), and a recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).
