# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about corporate investment. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "does access to financial markets help firms invest better, or increase short-term pressure?" is not one question. It bundles two conditions that arrive through the same market: access to capital and a distributed price signal, and exposure to a frequent public performance number. The figures return a shape rather than a signed verdict:
>
> 1. Investment quality must be measured per project, against realised long-run outcomes. Aggregating the projects into one quarterly number is how a live positive-value project disappears from view.
> 2. A market price is an instrument for this question, not the ground. The ground is realised long-term value, which does not move when someone changes a forecast. If managers and the price update on each other with no separate anchor, they drift together and nobody inside notices.
> 3. When a quarterly consensus estimate decides which projects are funded, it stops being a coordination convention and becomes a substitute ground. That is the named failure.
> 4. Capital access can move a firm's reachable investment set when capital was the binding constraint. That is a benefit, but it does not cancel item 2 or 3, because the two conditions are a compound, not a list.
>
> So the claim is not "markets help" or "markets hurt." It is that any answer which does not keep those two verdicts separate has averaged away the thing that decides the question.
>
> **What would refute this shape:**
> 1. investment quality is genuinely one aggregate quantity with no per-project structure;
> 2. a market price is invariant to firms' actions on it and settles realised long-term value, so it is the anchor here;
> 3. price and managerial decisions are corrected separately against an immovable long-run measure, so no mutual-update pair forms;
> 4. the earnings target never changes project acceptance, so it stays a convention.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have measured a net effect, that every listing harms investment, or that any named study proves the mechanism. The figure lines are the authority; my summary of them is not. Agreement from people who already accept the framework is not evidence, so I am not collecting it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures and formula repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own article warns that simulated outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and refuter first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode stated on the output | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Two verdicts, not one signed verdict | Fig 9 :55 *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* |
| No claim of a measured net effect | Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* |
| Links only after stage 1 | Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it; only the ground settles it. |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask each user for their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

## The two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links. Note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because Fig 2 :40 says *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **Stage 3, opt in only.** Offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Nothing here is sent by this agent. You host and you send.
