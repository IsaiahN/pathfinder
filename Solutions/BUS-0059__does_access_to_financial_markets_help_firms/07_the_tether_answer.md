# The Tether's answer on financial market access and firm investment

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question "does market access help firms invest better or increase short-term pressure?" bundles two conditions that arrive through the same market: capital and price information become reachable, and a frequent public performance number becomes binding. Fig 9 :55 *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"* ; :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. It is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground, so a single signed verdict is the wrong answer form.

## 2. The shape

1. **Investment quality is indexed per project, not per quarter.** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A positive-net-present-value project is a live slot; pooling it into one quarterly number removes the only signal that says where to look. So "better investments" must mean better per-project selection against realised long-run outcomes.
2. **The market price is an instrument, not the ground.** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* The question is constitutively about real investment (Fig 2 :36), so price is an instrument that *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :37). The ground is realised long-term cash flow.
3. **Manager and price form a mutual-update pair unless a separate anchor is installed.** Fig 2 :13 *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* ; :34 *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"*. This is why the harm cannot be detected by asking the market whether it is myopic.
4. **A target that decides projects has stopped being a convention.** Fig 2 :43 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* ; the same rule at Fig 10 :47. The quarterly consensus estimate is a shared meaning until it decides which projects are funded; then it is a substitute ground.
5. **Capital access can move the wall, but capability belongs to the habitat.** Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"* ; Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. When capital was the binding constraint, listing imports it and expands the reachable investment set. That is a genuine benefit. It does not cancel items 3 and 4, because the two conditions are a compound (Fig 9 :55).
6. **Exposure cannot be subtracted afterward.** Fig 11 :61 *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* "Take the capital but ignore the price" is not an available move once listed.
7. **The net of the two verdicts is not a number the figures return.** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The two effects have different grounds and different units; no common currency for them is in the library.

## 3. Verdicts per domain, never averaged

| domain | verdict | mode |
|---|---|---|
| Capital and information access | **helps, conditionally** when capital or price information was the binding constraint | `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 11) |
| Short-horizon pressure | **harms, conditionally** when the quarterly number is the binding constraint | `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 + Fig 2) |
| Verification of either verdict | **no verdict from agreement**; only the ground settles it | `general`, single-source within the figures |

The answer to the original question is therefore: **not one or the other, but which condition binds, stated per firm and per project, with a `gap` for the net magnitude.**

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that every listing harms investment. The capital-import effect is real.
- Not that every public firm becomes myopic. The mechanism fires when the metric binds.
- Not that share price is useless. It is an information-bearing instrument, just not the anchor for this question.
- Not that any cited study proves the framework. Those studies are world sources, secondary and corroborating only.
- Not `grounded`. No firm-level measurement was gathered here.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Per-project, long-horizon measurement.** For matched public and private firms, measure project acceptance and realised returns project by project, not as an aggregate investment rate. This tests F2.
2. **Anchor test in practice.** Identify a change in long-run realised value that does not move with the quarterly number, and check whether managers range against it. This tests F3 and F4.
3. **Target-decides test.** Check whether variation in the earnings-response coefficient changes project acceptance while holding opportunity constant. Asker, Farre-Mensa and Ljungqvist do something close to this; a replication at low overlap with their data would be a second witness.
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** per-slot structure absent, price shown to be an anchor, mutual-update pair absent, target never decides projects, or sign independent of which condition binds.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The two verdicts are available as a shape. The net magnitude needs a ground reading, and the figures can only report what is owed. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"the recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* The record of the route is what keeps the stop checkable. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer whether financial markets help or hurt firms with one signed verdict, because the question bundles two conditions that arrive together. Capital and price information can expand what a firm can reach, and that helps when capital was the binding constraint. But a quarterly public number pools all projects into one average, and a live project disappears into that average; when managers and the price update on each other with no separate long-run anchor, they drift together without noticing, and a target that decides which projects are funded stops being a convention and becomes a substitute ground. So the answer is per project and per condition, measured against realised long-run outcomes rather than against the price. What the framework offers is that shape, four falsifiers written in advance, and the names of the instruments that would close it. It does not claim a measured net effect, and it does not claim that markets are only harmful.
