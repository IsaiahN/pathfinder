# Expert verification packet - the Tether on financial market access and firm investment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a corporate-finance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No net effect is asserted as measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question bundles two conditions that arrive through one market: capital and price information become reachable, and a frequent public performance number becomes binding. Investment quality must be measured per project against realised long-run outcomes, because pooling projects into a quarterly number removes the live signal that says where to look. The market price is an instrument for this question, not the ground; when managers and the price update on each other with no separate long-run anchor, they drift together and nobody inside notices. A target that decides which projects are funded stops being a coordination convention and becomes a substitute ground. Capital access can genuinely expand the reachable investment set when capital was binding. The two verdicts have different grounds and must not be averaged; the net magnitude is unreached.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2
- **Statement:** investment quality is indexed per project, not per quarter; an aggregate metric erases a live project signal; the ground for this question is realised long-run value, not the price.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- **Refuter:** show investment quality is one aggregate quantity with no per-slot structure, or exhibit a price invariant to firms' actions that settles realised long-term value.

### C2 - finance-facing, checkable against the literature
- **Statement:** the short-horizon pressure mechanism is real and conditional on how sensitive the price is to earnings news. Public firms invest less and respond less to opportunities than comparable private firms; a majority of surveyed CFOs would skip a very positive NPV project to meet the quarterly consensus.
- **What to check:** Asker, Farre-Mensa and Ljungqvist, Review of Financial Studies 28(2), 2015; Graham, Harvey and Rajgopal, Journal of Accounting and Economics 40, 2005; Stein, Quarterly Journal of Economics 104(4), 1989. Are these characterisations faithful? Is the identification credible? Does the earnings-response-coefficient test isolate the metric channel?
- **Source status:** these are world sources, secondary corroboration only. They do not prove the framework. Their finance-pool overlap is high, so they are one pool, not independent witnesses (Fig 2 :40).
- **Refuter:** a low-overlap replication showing project acceptance is independent of the earnings signal once opportunity is held constant.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation
- **Statement:** the two conditions form a union compound with two verdicts, and no net term is owed because no common currency for the two grounds exists.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"* ; Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* ; Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **Refuter:** show the capital channel and the metric channel reduce to one monotone condition over realised value, or exhibit a common currency in which the signed net is well defined.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a measured net effect and not `grounded`.
- Not that every listing harms investment, and not that share price is useless.
- Not that the cited studies prove the framework. They are corroboration from a different pool.
- Not that Goodhart's law or Campbell's law is a proved law settling C2's target point. The 2018 Significance article records a disputed attribution and says the originals were not read; the arXiv 2024 paper formalises the adage under tail conditions. Fig 2 :43 governs, and the analogue only corroborates.
- Not a new primitive, and not a theory of everything.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. Per-project, long-horizon measurement for matched public and private firms, so the per-slot signal is visible.
2. An anchor-in-practice test: a change in long-run realised value that does not move with the quarterly number.
3. A target-decides test: variation in the earnings-response coefficient at fixed opportunity.
4. The four standing refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22 and Fig 2 :29, :30, :43. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the three cited papers: are the findings stated correctly, and is the earnings-response test the right discriminator?
3. Name one instrument that would separate long-run value from the quarterly number at the project level, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
