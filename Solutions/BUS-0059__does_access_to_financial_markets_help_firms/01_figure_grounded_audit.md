## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question is a bundled "A or B" over one intervention: access to a public financial market. We do not ask which slogan is true. We ask what the intervention changes in the firm's contact with the ground.

**R:** the gap between what the firm's investment choices predict about long-run real value and what the ground (realised cash flows, realised project returns) pays. The market supplies a price and a quarterly number. The question is whether those are the ground for this decision or an instrument that can be wrong on the axis the decision needs.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": write down what an answer would look like so it can accrue a residual):
> An answer is admissible only if it names (i) the ground for investment quality, (ii) the unit at which investment quality is measured, and (iii) which of the two bundled conditions is binding. A single signed verdict that ignores (iii) is not an answer to this question; it is an average.

**What an answer would look like:** per-condition verdicts, each with its own ground and unit, and an explicit statement that the net of the two cannot be read off agreement about market virtue.

## 1. DOMAIN CENSUS (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity: two.** The intervention bundles two conditions: (a) access to capital and a distributed price signal, (b) exposure to a frequent public performance number. Fig 9 :54 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"* ; Fig 9 :55 *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"* ; Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. The two conditions overlap (both arrive through the same market) but are not the same condition. This is a **union compound with its own loop**, not one hard question.
- **Symmetry: not symmetric.** Condition (a) operates on the availability of capital and information; condition (b) operates on the horizon of the performance signal. One is about reach, the other about metric. They cannot be swapped without changing the question.
- **Scale: two levels.** Firm-level per-project slots (each investment opportunity is an object being tracked) and market-level aggregate (one price, one quarterly earnings number). Fig 1 :11 *"residual R indexed by object slot"* ; Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

No domain is admitted without a nameable anchor.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the firm's investment frame** | managers, project slots, capital, R&D and maintenance outlays | real returns over time; investment rate | realised long-run cash flows, a measurement that does not move because an analyst changed a forecast. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* |
| **D2, the financial market frame** | investors, analysts, price, quarterly target, transient institutional ownership | price, EPS, capital allocation | the market only if the question were constitutively about price. Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Here it is an instrument: Fig 2 :37 *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* |

### 1.3 Partition or union

Union compound across D1 and D2 (Fig 9 :55, :57). D3 is a checking frame that ranges against both, not a third condition. The overlap (capital and price both arrive through one market) is why the compound needs its own loop rather than two independent loops.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

1. Name the ground (long-run realised value) before measuring any effect (D3 depends on D1's anchor).
2. Separate per-slot signals from the aggregate before judging investment quality (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. Unbundle the two conditions before searching for a frame (Fig 9 :54, :55; formula step 7 says SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD).
4. Judge each domain's verdict separately; never average them.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Frame-internal scores cannot be the verdict (D1, D3; `general`)

Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
Fig 1 :24-27, the disqualified list: *"coverage, and compression achieved"* and *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*, then *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

A share price, an analyst coverage count, or an EPS streak is produced by the very frame whose investment quality it is asked to certify. It cannot be the verdict. The verdict is realised long-run value. This is structural, so mode `general`.

**Refuter:** show a market-generated number that does not change with what frames do with it and that settles realised long-run value. Such a number would be an anchor, not an instrument.

### F2 - The aggregate erases the per-project signal (D2; `general` for the rule, `specified` for its application to quarterly targets)

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :28 *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

Investment opportunities are slots. A single quarterly EPS number pools them. A live positive-net-present-value project (a live slot) disappears into the pooled average, and the firm acts on the average instead. This is the figure's structural reason short-term pressure harms investment, and it is why the correct unit for "better investments" is per project, not per quarter. The rule is `general`; calling the quarterly target the aggregate is a `specified` application.

**Refuter:** show that investment quality is genuinely a single aggregate quantity with no per-slot structure, so that averaging loses no live signal.

### F3 - The market price is an instrument here, not the anchor (D1/D2; `specified`)

Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :30 *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
Fig 2 :37 *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

**Strain test and re-census.** Fig 2 :29 lists *"a market"* among its examples of an anchor. Read literally, that strains against F3. Re-census resolves it: the question "does market access improve real investment?" is constitutively about real investment, not about the market. Moreover a price moves when firms act on it, so it fails :30. The anchor is long-run realised value; the price is an instrument that can be wrong on the horizon axis. This is a re-census, not a softening. The reading is recorded in §5 as a retracted first reading.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a price that is invariant to firms' actions on it and that is the constituted subject of the investment question.

### F4 - Mutual update produces coherent drift (D2; `specified`)

Fig 2 :13 *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*
Fig 2 :14-15 *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"*
Fig 2 :34 *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"*

When managers update on price and price updates on manager actions, with no separate anchor to long-run cash flows, the pair converges on whoever has the most influence. Because the drift is coherent, it is invisible from inside the firm and from inside the market. This is the figure-grounded form of the short-termism mechanism, and it is why the harm cannot be detected by asking the market whether it is myopic.

**Refuter:** show that price and managerial decisions are each corrected separately against an immovable long-run measure, so the two do not form a mutual-update pair.

### F5 - A target that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (D2; `specified`)

Fig 2 :43 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

The quarterly consensus estimate is a shared meaning, a convention. When it decides which projects are funded, it has stopped being a convention and has become a substitute ground. Fig 10 :47 forbids exactly this. A check: the two figure lines say the same thing, so this is **single-source**, not triangulated. The Goodhart/Campbell literature corroborates it (see `09`), but it is a different pool, not a second figure.

**Refuter:** show a firm where the earnings target coordinates expectations but never changes project acceptance, so it remains a convention and never decides an outcome.

### F6 - Market access is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (D3; `general`)

Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 9 :51 *"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*

A stock listing filters which firms get capital and how cheaply. That is a budget fact. It is not evidence that the firm's investments are better. Treating cheap capital or high coverage as a witness for investment quality is the named error. This also governs the outreach: a researcher's agreement is not the verdict.

**Refuter:** show an instance where the budget filter itself resolves the residual about real investment quality, with no separate ground reading.

### F7 - Capital access can move the wall; habitat changes are not automatically improvements (D1; `specified`)

Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*
Fig 6 :22 *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*
Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*
Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

When capital is not in the firm's closure, listing imports it, and import is the only operator that moves the wall. But capability belongs to the firm-and-habitat pair, so adding market contact changes capability without guaranteeing it improves it. The sign depends on which condition binds. This is the figure-grounded reason F7 and F2 to F5 are separate verdicts that must never be averaged.

**Refuter:** show a firm whose reachable investment set did not change when capital became available, or a case where the added contact changed nothing measurable.

### F8 - Exposure cannot be subtracted once installed (D2; `specified`)

Fig 11 :61 *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*

A listing introduces a market into the firm's habitat. The firm cannot then subtract it, because contact is a fact about the world. This is why "take the capital but ignore the price" is not a available move once listed. It also constrains the remedy: the fix is a different ground-maintainer arrangement, not a wish to un-list.

**Refuter:** show a firm that removed market contact from its habitat while keeping the capital, so exclusion is in fact available.

### F9 - The net effect is a union compound, not one number (cross-domain; `general` for the form)

Fig 9 :55 *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*
Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

The figures give the shape of the answer, which is: keep two verdicts, each with its own ground, and refuse the single averaged verdict. They do not give the net magnitude. That is `gap` for a single signed number, and it is a verdict, not a silence. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (this line concerns what cannot be named from inside a closure; here it licenses the honest stop on the net number).

**Refuter:** show that the two conditions reduce to one monotone condition over realised value, so a single signed verdict is well defined.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A firm's investment quality is shown to be a single aggregate quantity with no per-slot structure, so F2's averaging loss does not occur.
2. A market price is shown to be invariant to firms' actions on it and to be the constituted subject of the investment question, so F3's instrument reading fails.
3. Price and managerial decisions are shown to be corrected separately against an immovable long-run measure, so F4's mutual-update pair does not form.
4. The earnings target is shown never to change project acceptance, so F5's convention-to-ground transition does not happen.
5. An empirical result, measured against realised long-run outcomes and at low overlap with the sources in `10`, shows the sign of the effect does not depend on which condition binds.

If any fires, the compound reading is wrong and the single-verdict form is restored. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
