## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"* ; :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.
- Condition (a) is capital and information access. Condition (b) is exposure to the short-horizon metric. They overlap because both arrive through one market, so a partition is false.
- A compound is not a harder case of either; it is a case in both, with the intersection as its own object. ⇒ The single question "does market access help or hurt" is malformed as written.
- **Falsifier:** show an implementation of market access in which the capital channel and the metric channel are separable in fact, so a partition is correct.

### Q2 - Order of operations

Formula step 2: refuse a term held to be false before minting a new one. Formula step 7: SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, and never read INWARD against OUTWARD.

1. **Refuse** the term "the market is the ground for investment quality" before any measurement (Fig 2 :30, :36).
2. **Describe** the residual in effect terms before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).
3. **Settle each condition separately** against its own anchor (Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).
4. **Only then** ask whether the two verdicts can be composed into one. If they cannot, the honest answer is a compound with two verdicts, not an average.

Reversing steps 1 and 2 is the apophenia error: choose "market efficiency" as the frame, then read the residual into it. *The Library of Babel* calls the corrective exactly: describe the effect, then let the frame be selected.

### Q3 - Compound or isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Take the firm before listing and the same firm after listing over the atom set {firm, projects, capital, price, target, realised outcomes}. The atoms are the same; what changes is which bond is load bearing: the capital-import bond in the first configuration, the metric-feedback bond in the second.
- Under Fig 12's definition those two configurations are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is a stronger and more precise claim than "a compound," and it is testable.
- **Falsifier:** show that the listed and unlisted configurations require different atoms (for example that listing introduces a primitive that was not already reachable), which breaks the isomer claim and leaves the weaker compound claim standing.
- Caveat: this is a figure-defined structural claim, not evidence about any real firm. Its physics-facing analogue in the exemplar needed an instrument; this one needs a per-project dataset.

### Q4 - The cross-field analogue, tested rather than assumed

The Goodhart and Campbell laws, as quoted in Rodamar 2018 and the arXiv 2024 paper, say that a measure used as a target loses its meaning. Fig 2 :43 says *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

- The structure matches: a coordination device becomes a selector, and then stops carrying the information it carried as a coordination device.
- Low overlap: measurement theory in social science and monetary economics does not draw on the Tether figures. This is genuine corroboration of F5, not an echo.
- Strain: the adage's attribution and wording are disputed, and neither original was read for the 2018 article. The analogue is therefore corroboration of the figure line, not a replacement for it. Recorded as such in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- **Falsifier:** show a firm where the earnings target decides project acceptance and yet remains an accurate signal of long-run value, which would break the analogy and force a re-census of F5.

### Q5 - Is a net-effect term owed?

- Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (the original line concerns primitives that cannot be named from inside a closure; here it licenses the honest stop on a single number).
- The two verdicts in `01` have different grounds and different units. A term that combined them would need a third currency in which per-project real return and metric-induced distortion add. No such currency is in the library. ⇒ No net term is owed; the compound answer is complete as two verdicts.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a common currency in which both effects are measured and a signed net is well defined.

## CONCLUSION

The correct order is refuse, describe, settle each condition, then compose only if a currency exists. The before and after configurations are isomers over a shared atom set, which makes the structural claim testable against a per-project dataset. The target-corruption analogue corroborates F5 from a low-overlap field but does not govern it. No net-effect term is owed; the two verdicts are the answer.
