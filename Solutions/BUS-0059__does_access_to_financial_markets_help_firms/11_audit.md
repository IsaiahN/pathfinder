## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per-slot indexing), Fig 2 :29, :30, :36, :43 (anchor, invariance, legitimacy, convention), Fig 9 :55 (union), Fig 6 :14 and Fig 11 :39 (import and habitat).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: investment quality is per project; price is an instrument; manager and price drift together; the target becomes a ground; capital import helps conditionally; two verdicts, no net.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete reading a finance researcher would make: "public listings lower investment because managers chase quarterly earnings; private firms invest more."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints held in the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- **the conditionality** of both verdicts. The concrete reading turns "helps when capital binds, harms when the metric binds" into an unconditional "listings lower investment." That is the aggregation error the figures forbid.
- **the anchor test.** The concrete reading treats the quarterly number as the thing that decides, which is exactly the convention-to-ground transition Fig 2 :43 names. The shorthand keeps it as an instrument.
- **the refusal of a net term.** The concrete reading supplies a signed verdict; the shorthand refuses one because no common currency exists.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a live project signal is lost when pooled into an aggregate, so measurement must be per project. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no firm-level ground reading exists here; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the metalaws would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
