# Isomers, order and negation in the firewall compound

**Mode: `specified`, ungrounded.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The firewall question asks for one local state at the horizon, but the residual decomposes into three conditions (S1 radiation purity, S2 exterior EFT validity, S3 the infalling detector's reading). Three questions are owed:

1. Are the three conditions **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or do they share only a **functional group**?
2. What **order** do they compose in, and where is the dependency break?
3. Is "firewall" a **mint**, a **comparison**, or a **refusal**, and which operator actually applies?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the three conditions isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Write each condition as a first-order molecule over the six-term set:

| condition | first-order reading | atoms used |
|---|---|---|
| S1 radiation purity | `Energy → Spectrum → Time` (an ordering of radiated energy over time) | Energy, Spectrum, Time |
| S2 exterior EFT | `Space → Spectrum → Energy` (a local ordering over a spatial region with energy content) | Space, Spectrum, Energy |
| S3 infalling reading | `Space → Gradient → Time` (a directed crossing of a spatial boundary in time) | Space, Gradient, Time |

- S1 and S2 share Energy and Spectrum; S2 and S3 share Space; S1 and S3 share Time. No pair shares all three atoms, and the three together do not share one identical set.
- By Fig 12's strict definition they are **not isomers** as written. They are three members of the same **functional group**, `state → structure → state`, with different atoms in the slots. Fig 12 :124: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The stronger form: if all three are rewritten over the **full shared atom set** `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they **are** isomers, same atoms, different arrangement. That is the claim worth testing, and it carries the falsifier below.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any condition can be written only by adding an atom the other two do not require (that is, if S3 needs Network or a seventh term while S1 and S2 do not), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 — What order do they compose in?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The dependency order is not S1, S2, S3 by list order. It is:

1. **S2 first.** Exterior EFT is the link with an instrument (local field measurements, gravitational-wave observatories). It produces the vocabulary in which the horizon region can be described.
2. **S1 second.** Radiation purity is settled outside the horizon by correlation measurements on the exterior record. It consumes the S2 vocabulary.
3. **S3 last.** The infalling reading is a composition of S1 and S2 only if both first-order molecules settle; it then becomes a second-order term. It is the link with **no instrument**, so it is not a weak reading, it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." S1 and S2 are settled in their own domains, so each can serve as an atom at the next level. S3 is not settled, so the second-order composition is legal in form but `candidate` in status.

**Strain named:** writing S3 as `Space → Gradient → Time` assumes a structure in the slot. Fig 13 :75 licenses *a* structure but not *which* one. The slot stays marked `Space(???)Time` in the sense of the derivation essay, and the gradient reading is a binding, not a result.

### Q3 — Is the monogamy constraint a comparison bond?

Operators table :47-51: the test for ⋛ is "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 12 :86-87: "comparison / a threshold relation between two quantities."

The Coffman-Kundu-Wootters result states that "there is a trade-off between A's entanglement with B and its entanglement with C." That is a threshold relation over an entanglement budget: one tangle plus another cannot exceed a bound. It reads as ⋛, not as a symmetric join. So the D3 prediction is structurally a **comparison**, which is why it can conflict with a local smooth-crossing condition that is not itself a comparison. A comparison and a local field statement are different bond types; their collision is a type-level feature of the compound, not a paradox in one variable.

**Operand type strain, still live:** at second order the operands of the comparison are settled molecules-now-atoms, while the Operators table defines ⋛ over "two quantities." Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom. Whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded, not hidden.

### Q4 — Is "firewall" a mint or a refusal?

Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"

"Smooth horizon" is the held term whose residual persists and does not trend down. Under `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 the refusal is owed **before** a mint. The popular framing reverses this: it mints "firewall" as a new physical term. Under the guards, "firewall" is assembled from Energy and Network (no new atom), so it is not a novel primitive; and settlement is zero, so the mint is inert. The honest move is to refuse the smooth-horizon term's standing to be composed over, record the refusal, and leave the slot open until a ground pays for one of them.

Note the unary operator point (Fig 12 :135-136): negation acts on one operand and "joins nothing." A refusal does not add an atom and so does not move the wall (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."). It shrinks the reach, which is why the record must show it.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are a **functional group** as written and a real **isomer claim** only in the stronger full-atom-set form, with a stated falsifier. Their dependency order is S2, S1, S3, with the break at S3 because it has no instrument. The monogamy constraint is a ⋛ comparison over an entanglement budget, and the firewall term is a candidate mint with zero settlement, whereas the refusal of the held smooth-horizon term is what is actually owed. None of this selects a physical outcome. It prices the compound and names the break.
