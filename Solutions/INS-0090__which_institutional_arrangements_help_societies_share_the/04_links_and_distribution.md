## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The tradeoff is real and priced, not rhetorical.** Einav and Finkelstein (2018) state that moral hazard makes full insurance infeasible and that optimal contracts are incomplete, "striking a balance between reducing risk and maintaining incentives." This is the world-facing version of the figure finding that a distribution is not a gradient (Fig 13 :46). It supports partial, rule-based coverage over full equalisation.
2. **What bites is the social-insurance channel, not the headline stabiliser.** McKay and Reis (2013) find that programs affecting inequality and social insurance can have a large effect on volatility, while the US stabiliser set as designed had barely any effect. This supports the per-slot finding (Fig 1 :19): the aggregate instrument is the wrong place to look.
3. **Risk pooling has design principles, not just a label.** Cronk and Aktipis (2021) list seven principles converged on independently across societies: the pool covers unpredictable needs, giving creates no repayment obligation, helpers are not asked to help before their own needs are met, need is consensually defined, resources are visible, partners are choosable, and the network scale must match the risk scale. Principle 7 is the figure-grounded network-scale claim in world vocabulary; principle 2 is a concrete way not to flatten the incentive gradient.
4. **Diversifying support can cost short-term return.** Carter et al. (2017) found that bats investing in more nonkin did better only when a key donor was removed, not under normal conditions. Villa Martin et al. (2019) model the same shape: spreading risk can be optimal under slow, spatially structured variability and vanishes when change is too fast. Both are low-overlap corroboration that spreading and short-term return are two readings, not one.
5. **A genuine conflict.** Bliege Bird et al. (2002) found weak support for risk-reduction reciprocity among Meriam foragers. This does not overturn the figure verdict; it sends the census back to question whether "sharing" is the right actor-relation for a given residual. It is recorded as a conflict, not smoothed away.
