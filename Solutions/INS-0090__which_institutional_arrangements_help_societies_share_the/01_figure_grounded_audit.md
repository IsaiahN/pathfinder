## 0. The question under audit, stated as an effect

The question is: "Which institutional arrangements help societies share the costs of economic shocks without undermining long-term investment?" (source: `institutional questions.txt` line 109).

Stated in effect terms, not in the vocabulary of any economic theory:

- A shock is a residual arriving at a population of agents. It hits particular slots (a region, a sector, a household cohort), not a homogeneous mass. What is owed is a way to keep any one slot from bearing a catastrophic loss.
- "Share the costs" means the loss is spread through relations among agents so that consumption and capacity do not collapse in the hit slots.
- "Without undermining long-term investment" means the arrangement must not erase the directedness that channels current resources into future productive use. If every difference between states is evened out, there is no spendable difference left to drive investment.
- The word "without" makes this a conjunction of two conditions, not one quantity to be maximised. An arrangement that shares perfectly but kills investment fails; one that preserves investment but leaves a slot to collapse also fails.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the search (formula step "Direction"): a **shape** with four properties, (i) the shock is read per slot, (ii) it is spread through a network whose scale matches the shock's scale, (iii) a spendable gradient toward future use is preserved, and (iv) the arrangement is settled by measured outcomes rather than by expert agreement. This shape is a preference until it is bound; writing it down here makes it refusable.

## 1. Residual: arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two conditions. Sharing and investment preservation are two operands, not one score. Fig 9 :54 says "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again", and Fig 9 :55 notes "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones".
- **Symmetry:** risk pooling is symmetric among participants (each may give and receive, swap them and the relation is unchanged). Investment is ordered and directed (current resources are committed so that future resources exist; swap the times and the term changes). The two are not the same kind of bond.
- **Scale:** the same question recurs at household, firm, national and cross-border level. Fig 4 :19 says "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The loop does not change with size.
- **Strain test:** an early reading tried to make Fig 12's two-currency line govern the sharing/investment tradeoff. That line governs *bits* (cost of the description) versus *actions* (cost of finding out whether it holds), not two economic quantities. It strains, so it governs another quantity and the line was withdrawn. The sharing/investment distinction is governed by Fig 13's network-versus-gradient split. See section 8.

## 4. Findings

### F1: The residual is a union compound, not one hard problem (decisive for method)

Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again". Fig 9 :55: "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones". Fig 9 :56: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".

The question names two conditions joined by "without". Searching for a single master institution before splitting them is the collapse Fig 9 names: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. The overlap (an institution that both pools and changes incentives) is a compound with its own loop and its own ground. **Refuter:** a measurement in which sharing and investment preservation always move together with no independent variation would make this a partition; then the union claim is withdrawn.

### F2: Shocks must be read per slot, not averaged into a national mean

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

An aggregate shock figure hides which region, sector or cohort needs the transfer. This governs the *aim* of sharing, not its size. **Refuter:** a dataset in which the national aggregate predicts local shortfalls as well as local readings do would break this finding. It is an empirical question, not a figure question.

### F3: Pooling is a network; investment is a gradient; they are different structures

Fig 13 :24: "that entities relate, and influence travels" (network). Fig 13 :22: "a difference that can be spent" (gradient). Fig 13 :46: "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness." Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

Risk pooling spreads influence through relations. Investment needs a directed, spendable difference. Equalising every state produces a distribution, and a distribution is not a gradient. So full smoothing and investment preservation genuinely pull in different structural directions; this is not a rhetorical tradeoff but a consequence of the two-structure census. They compose through a state (for example, a credible commitment that turns a pooled surplus into a future project), not by being blended into one quantity. **Refuter:** derive directedness from "influence travels" alone, so that network and gradient are one structure, or show that full equalisation leaves measured investment unchanged. Either breaks the shape.

### F4: The ground is measured outcomes; agreement is disqualified as a verdict

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

The ground here is realised consumption smoothing and realised long-term investment. A consensus of economists, a prestige-weighted review panel, or a list of people selected because they like the conclusion is a room, not an anchor. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." **Refuter:** an instrument that moves when an expert frame pushes on it is not an anchor; if the only available "outcome" is an expert scorecard, the verdict is `gap`, not `grounded`.

### F5: The figures give a shape, not a named winner

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

The six structures are fixed; the institutional arrangements over them are not. Automatic stabilisers, unemployment insurance, deposit insurance, index insurance, sovereign stabilisation funds and mutual-aid networks are **bindings imported from the economics and anthropology frames**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). The figures can say what any viable arrangement must look like; they cannot say which one is best. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". **Refuter:** a figure line that names a specific institution would break this finding. No such line exists in the source text.

### F6: The channel to the ground, not the ground, is what decays

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Realised outcomes do not go stale; the statistics that report them do (stale series, pooled aggregates, lagged benefit data). A reform that improves a ministry's model without changing who actually receives a transfer in a bad year has improved the agent, not the contact, and changes nothing. **Refuter:** a shown case where a pure model improvement, with no contact change, moved realised investment would break this.

### F7: A neutral rule that decides an outcome has become a verdict

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

A guarantee that was a shared rule and now determines which firms survive a shock has crossed the seam: it is grading the thing it was meant to measure. Because exposure to shocks cannot be subtracted (contact is a fact), every such arrangement is permanent maintenance rather than a solved state. This is the figure-grounded form of the commitment problem. **Refuter:** an arrangement that selects no winners and no losers under any state of the world would not have crossed the seam; the finding would not apply to it.

### F8: Filters are not witnesses

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A tier label, a cost estimate or a contact score decides what gets tried first, not whether an arrangement works. Ranking institutions by how likely a panel is to approve them manufactures the disqualifying correlation. **Refuter:** none internal; this is a discipline, and a breach is visible as a receptiveness column entering a verdict row.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the Seat Map (local file): "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted, or sent back to census, if any of the following holds:

1. A figure re-census shows that network and gradient are not distinct structures, that is, that "influence travels" already carries directedness and spendability. Then the two-condition compound collapses and F3 falls.
2. A ground reading shows that full consumption equalisation across states leaves measured long-term investment unchanged or higher. Then the gradient-preservation half of the shape is false.
3. A dataset shows that a national aggregate shock predicts local shortfalls as well as local readings. Then Fig 1 :22 does not govern this residual.
4. A measurement shows the two conditions are a partition with no overlap. Then the compound is dissolved and the loops are run separately (the shape itself may survive).
5. A figure line is found that names a specific institution. Then F5 is false and the framework does more than return a shape.

Until one of these is run, nothing may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Unbundle the question into a sharing loop and an investment-preservation loop before searching (F1).
2. Aim transfers per slot, using local readings, not a national mean (F2).
3. Keep a spendable gradient: prefer partial, rule-based coverage over full equalisation, and keep the arrangement a replenishment cycle rather than a one-off (F3, `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
4. Treat named institutions as imports with provenance and a shadow test, never as figure derivations (F5).
5. Use budget and contact scores as filters only. Ask a checker what would refute the shape, record the verdict before showing any theory, and never pool later agreement as corroboration (F4, F8).
6. Watch the data channel and the seam where a rule starts picking winners (F6, F7).
