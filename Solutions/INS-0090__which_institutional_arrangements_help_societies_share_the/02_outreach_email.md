**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the findings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are repaired and the mode labels are attached. The output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about sharing shocks without killing investment

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A shock is not a uniform event. It hits particular regions, sectors and households. Two things are wanted at once: spread the loss so no single slot collapses, and keep the directed difference that turns current resources into future productive use. Full equalisation and a live investment incentive pull in different directions.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A figure-grounded derivation says this is a union of two conditions, not one optimisation problem. It must be unbundled before searching. Risk pooling is a network, influence travelling between participants; investment is a gradient, a spendable difference with direction. Those are different structures, so they cannot be merged into one score. Any viable arrangement must read the shock per slot, spread it through a network whose scale matches the risk, preserve a spendable gradient toward future use, and be settled by measured outcomes rather than by professional agreement. The framework supplies that shape. It does not name an institution.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework picks unemployment insurance, deposit insurance, stabilisation funds or any other named arrangement as the winner. Those are bindings imported from economics and anthropology, with provenance, not derivations from the figures. I am not claiming that agreement among experts settles the question. I am not claiming this is grounded; no measurement here settles it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. A re-census showing that network and gradient are one structure, so that "influence travels" already carries directedness and spendability.
> 2. A ground reading in which full consumption equalisation leaves measured long-term investment unchanged or higher.
> 3. A dataset in which a national aggregate shock predicts local shortfalls as well as local readings do.
> 4. A figure line that names a specific institution.
>
> If any holds, the shape is wrong or the claim is out of scope and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
