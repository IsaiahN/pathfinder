## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A shock arriving unevenly at a population. Arity: two conditions, spread the loss and preserve the directed difference that funds future use. Symmetry: pooling is symmetric among participants, investment is ordered in time. Scale: household, firm, national and cross border, with the same loop at each (Fig 4 :19 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."). Fig 9 makes it a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, household and firm pooling.** Actors: households, firms, mutuals, informal networks, insurers. Currency: transfers, premia, smoothed consumption. Anchor: realised shortfall frequency and consumption variance. Partition edge: who is in the pool.
- **Domain 2, public stabilisation.** Actors: tax and transfer systems, unemployment benefits, fiscal buffers. Currency: disposable income, aggregate demand, fiscal space. Anchor: measured volatility and whether buffers were replenished. Union with domains 1 and 3.
- **Domain 3, investment and incentives.** Actors: savers, firms, creditors, regulators. Currency: expected return, net present value, commitment credibility. Anchor: measured long term investment and capital flows. Union with domains 1 and 2.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers and editors. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: measured outcomes, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so this seat's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Per slot perception before vocabulary, vocabulary before the objective, the objective before planning. The chain breaks at the objective because no single instrument reads both conditions; Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Effort above the break cannot rescue it.

Strain test applied: an early reading used Fig 12's two currency line for the sharing and investment tradeoff. That line prices bits against actions, not two economic quantities. It was withdrawn and the tradeoff moved to Fig 13's network versus gradient split. No strain remains after re census.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only; they never overrule a figure. Each was opened on 2026-11-23 unless noted.

- **Macroeconomics, automatic stabilisers.** Alisdair McKay and Ricardo Reis, NBER Working Paper 19000, 2013 (published Econometrica, 2016). Quote: "tax-and-transfer programs that affect inequality and social insurance can have a large effect on aggregate volatility". Predicts that the social insurance channel, not the aggregate income channel, is where stabilisation bites. Corroborates domain 1 and the per slot finding. Overlap: high with the other economics sources, one pool.
- **Insurance economics, moral hazard.** Liran Einav and Amy Finkelstein, Journal of the European Economic Association 16(4), 2018, read via the PMC open access copy. Quote: "the presence of moral hazard leads optimal insurance contracts to be incomplete, striking a balance between reducing risk and maintaining incentives." Predicts partial coverage, never full equalisation. Corroborates domain 3 and Fig 13 :46. Overlap: high with other economics sources.
- **Public economics, corporate investment.** European Commission Economic and Financial Affairs, Economic Paper 452, 2012. Quote: "automatic stabilisers have no impact on corporate investment. Investment decisions in the model are determined by the net present value of investment projects over their whole lifetime". Predicts that smoothing consumption need not destroy the investment gradient. Corroborates the union claim. Overlap: high with the other economics sources. Read via the search integration excerpt; the PDF was not directly fetchable by this tool, recorded honestly.
- **Human behaviour, risk pooling design.** Lee Cronk and Athena Aktipis, Nature Human Behaviour 5, 825 to 833, 2021. Quote: "Risk-pooling systems are most effective when their participants adhere to several principles: (1) participants should agree that the pool is for needs that arise unpredictably, not for routine, predictable needs; (2) giving to those in need should not create an obligation for them to repay". Predicts covering unpredictable needs and avoiding a debt obligation that would flatten the incentive. Corroborates domains 1 and 3. Overlap: low, anthropology and psychology.
- **Animal behaviour, social bet hedging.** Gerald Carter, Damien Farine and Gerald Wilkinson, Biology Letters 13(5), 2017, read via the PMC record. Quote: "investing in quantity of social relationships at the expense of relationship quality ('social bet-hedging') can reduce the risks posed by unpredictable social environments." Predicts that a broader network pays off when a key partner fails, not in normal times. Corroborates domain 1. Overlap: very low.
- **Complexity science and biology, bet hedging.** Paula Villa Martin, Miguel Munoz and Simone Pigolotti, PLOS Computational Biology 15(4), 2019. Quote: "some individuals in the community are enabled to tackle adverse conditions, even at the price of reducing overall growth in the short term." Predicts that risk spreading can cost short term growth and depends on the rate and spatial scale of change. Corroborates the two condition tradeoff. Overlap: low.
- **Anthropology, conflict source.** Rebecca Bliege Bird, Douglas Bird, Eric Smith and Geoffrey Kushnick, Evolution and Human Behavior 23(4), 2002. Quote: "These results cast considerable doubt on RRR as an explanation of Meriam food sharing." Predicts that sharing is not always risk reduction. Conflicts with a naive "sharing equals insurance" reading. Per the rule, the conflict sends the census back to the actor relation; it does not flip the figure verdict. Read via the search integration excerpt; direct PDF fetch unavailable. Overlap: low.

No world source overturns the figure verdict. Agreement among the three economics sources is one evidence pool, not three witnesses. The low overlap anthropology, animal behaviour and complexity sources corroborate the shape independently of that pool.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the unique name does not. The question is a union compound and must be unbundled. Shocks are per slot. Pooling is a network and investment is a spendable gradient, so they are different structures and full equalisation cannot be the whole answer. The arrangement must keep the gradient alive, avoid becoming the rule that decides who survives, and be settled by measured outcomes rather than professional agreement. No figure line names an institution; unemployment benefit, deposit insurance, index insurance, stabilisation funds and mutual aid are imported bindings needing a source gate and a shadow test. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result with a `gap` verdict on the unique name.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending a checker's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The imported names are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A superseded claim can be replaced without withdrawing any primitive.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per slot dataset in which a national aggregate predicts shortfalls as well as local readings; a measurement in which full equalisation leaves long term investment unchanged or higher; a re census showing network and gradient are one structure; a demonstration that the two conditions are a partition; or a figure line that names an institution. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
