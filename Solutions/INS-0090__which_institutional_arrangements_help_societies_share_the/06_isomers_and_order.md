## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are shock-sharing institutions isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take two imported arrangements:

- **A: unemployment benefit.** Atoms: a pool (network), a contribution in good times (gradient over time), a transfer in bad times. Bond: a *conditional* transfer triggered by an observed state of need.
- **B: a stabilisation fund with rule-based payouts.** Atoms: the same pool, the same contribution in good times, the same transfer in bad times. Bond: a *threshold* release triggered by an aggregate indicator.

Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour: A reaches individual slots directly; B reaches them through an aggregate trigger. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not variants of one thing. This matters because a policy that swaps one for the other does not merely change the label; it changes the substance.

Weaker claim: if A and B do not share the full atom set (for example, one requires a payroll contribution and the other requires a sovereign balance sheet), they are not isomers but members of one **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Both are arrangements that pool and preserve; the qualifier says which one.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one arrangement requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If both arrangements can be written over the identical atom set and behave identically under every state, the isomer claim also fails (a bond that makes no behavioural difference is a free bond, which Fig 12 prices at zero).

### Q2: What order is a viable arrangement?

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input." Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."

A one-off emergency transfer is a **chain**: it runs from a donor to a recipient and stops. A rule-based pool whose payouts in bad times are replenished by contributions in good times is a **cycle**: the output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it sustains sharing.

A buffer that survives its own use is a **catalyst** in Fig 12's sense; a buffer that is spent and not replenished behaves differently across uses, and nothing in the bare name records which a term is. Cronk and Aktipis (2021, world source) state the same design rule in world vocabulary: the network scale must cover the risk scale, and transfers should not create a repayment obligation that would turn the pool into a debt relation.

**Falsifier:** an arrangement that shares shocks over many periods with no replenishment path would refute the cycle requirement. If it also keeps investment intact, both Fig 12 :121 and Fig 13 :46 would need re-census.

### Q3: Network and gradient are not interchangeable

Fig 13 :46: "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness." Fig 13 :24: "that entities relate, and influence travels." Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

A network spreads influence symmetrically; a gradient carries direction. Merging them into one "risk-adjusted incentive" measure would be the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 names: "the parts are not the thing." The world source Einav and Finkelstein (2018) corroborates in economics vocabulary: optimal insurance is incomplete because it must balance risk reduction against incentives, which is two readings held apart, not one sum.

### Q4: Where does the chain break?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

For institution choice the links are:

1. **Perception:** local shortfall is measured (Fig 1 :19).
2. **Vocabulary:** terms exist for pool, buffer, transfer, gradient, cycle.
3. **Objective:** what counts as success under both conditions? This link has no standard instrument, so it breaks here. Most effort lands above the break, where volatility measures already exist, and cannot rescue the missing middle.
4. **Planning:** choose an arrangement under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** verify and carry the method (Fig 4 :22).

A reading taken below link 3 is not a weak reading of the choice; it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22, "a reading of nothing"). The practical consequence is to build the instrument for the conjunction before ranking institutions.

## CONCLUSION

Shock-sharing institutions can be genuine **isomers** over the shared atoms of pool, contribution and transfer, with the bond deciding which behaviour appears. Their viability is **second order** in the sense that a settled first-order sharing term must re-enter as a replenishment cycle; a one-off chain cannot sustain it. Network and gradient are distinct structures and must be composed through a state, never blended. The dependency chain breaks at the objective link, because no single instrument currently reads "sharing without undermining investment" as one quantity. This rescues what is checkable in the question without importing a new primitive or naming a winner from the figures.
