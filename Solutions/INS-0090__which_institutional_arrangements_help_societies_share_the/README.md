# Solution INS-0090

**Question:** Which institutional arrangements help societies share the costs of economic shocks without undermining long-term investment?

**Source:** institutional questions.txt line 109

**Section:** 9. Economic institutions and distribution

**Effect restatement:** A shock is a residual that hits particular slots. The question asks for an arrangement that spreads the loss through relations while preserving the spendable difference that drives future investment. The word "without" makes it a union of two conditions, not one quantity to maximise. An answer would look like a shape: the shock is read per slot, spread through a network whose scale matches the risk, with a gradient toward future use preserved, and settled by measured outcomes rather than by agreement.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. A room does not upgrade the label. A panel of sympathetic economists is agreement, not an anchor (Seat Map, "A room does not upgrade the label"; Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").
2. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Seat Map: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention"; Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
3. Showing the framework before asking for a verdict spends the checker's independence (Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.").

Legally: scraping private addresses for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in submission venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
