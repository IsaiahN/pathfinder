# Are compliance, identification and internalization isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

One agreement reading recurs in slots where the private state that would produce it is absent. Two questions are owed: (1) under Fig 12's definition, are the three sources of that reading **isomers**, and (2) what bond runs from source to reading, and what order does that imply for a company's measurement? A third question follows: do the three sources form a partition or a union, and what does that decide about a single lever?

Atoms available in this frame: an induced value statement, an overt agreement response, a history of adopting it, a reward or sanction, a relationship to a person or group, and a value system already held. Bonds available: the seven, including production (⇒), sequence (→), identity (≡) and comparison (⋛). No new primitive is available without import, and Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." Order by dependency: perception of value-relevant behaviour, then vocabulary, then objective, then instrument (Fig 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three sources isomers? CORRECTION TO MY EARLIER READING

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- The atoms are shared: a stated value, an agreement response, a history of adopting it. The overt formula is the same. What differs is the bond: production from an external reward, production from a relationship, or content congruence with a value system. Kelman 1958, read 2026-11-22, gives the operative test in the business frame: "Responses adopted through different processes will be performed under different conditions, will be changed and extinguished under different conditions, and will have different properties."
- ⇒ Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not variants of one thing. This is the same structure as the Melt / Freeze / Boil case: Fig 12 :13 "three different changes, one written formula", Fig 12 :14 "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not", Fig 12 :16 "and nothing signals that anything was lost".
- **The correction.** An earlier reading called them "three levels of one attitude", which would make them a sequence under →. That would erase the bond and make the difference invisible by construction. Retracted: the difference between them is exactly what Fig 12 :117 says an isomer difference is. Triangulated (Fig 12 definition plus Kelman's consequent-history test, low topical overlap), but still `specified`: no company-level measurement has paid for it.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the three processes show no distinguishable consequent history under any change of reward, relationship or content, the isomer claim fails and they collapse into one source with three labels.

### Q2 - What bond runs from source to reading?

- Fig 12 :68 "two names, one referent" is **identity** (≡). Fig 12 :77 "A produces B; B did not exist before" is **production** (⇒).
- The company's inference treats "agrees with the values" and "holds the values" as identity: two names, one referent. That is the error. The correct bond is production: the private state produces the reading under conditions the reading does not record. Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A survey vocabulary names the state (agreement) and cannot name the route from a value to a costly choice. Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A value-consistent route is a sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72). The costly choice comes after the stated agreement; reversing them changes the thing being measured.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstrated reversible mapping from reading to private state that preserves all consequences of the reading. That would be identity, and Q2 would be wrong.

### Q3 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- A person who complies and identifies is not a more extreme complier. That person is a specific compound, and a lever that clears compliance alone leaves the compound standing. Allen and Meyer 1990, read 2026-11-22, found that affective and normative commitment were "empirically distinguishable constructs with different correlates" but "although distinguishable, appear to be somewhat related": a union, not a clean partition, at the commitment level.
- **Falsifier:** a clean partition in which no slot ever occupies two source conditions, and one lever clears every slot with no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union.

### Q4 - Functional group, catalyst, cycle and valence

- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." All three sources share a functional group: an adopted behaviour congruent with a stated value. The bond says which member. This is why one survey item cannot tell them apart.
- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Internalization is catalyst-like: it regenerates across uses. Compliance is like a spent part: it needs the reward renewed each time. `Specified` analogue, not a derivation from the figure to a person.
- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Internalization can close a self-sustaining loop; compliance under external reward is a chain that stops when the power stops. The sign of the loop, not the volume of the agreement, decides which one is present.
- Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Public agreement is a benefactor to a coordination residual and a malfactor to a dissent residual. There is no standing list of good survey items (Fig 11 :51).
- **Falsifier:** a survey item whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the residual. If no such change of valence can be found anywhere, Q4 weakens to `general`.

## CONCLUSION

The three sources are **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different bond, different consequent history, invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour. The bond from private state to agreement reading is **production (⇒), not identity (≡)**. The route from a stated value to a costly choice is a **sequence (→)**, which is why the order of measurement matters. The three form a **union**, so a single lever leaves the intersections standing. Their shared structure is a **functional group**, and their difference is the bond, which is exactly what the reading does not record. The analogues of catalyst and cycle are `specified` and must not be promoted to a claim about a named employee.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the company's question without pretending the survey can answer it: "do they agree?" is readable; "why do they agree?" is a bond question, and bonds are not recoverable from operands (Fig 12 :46). The gap is not silence. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,".
