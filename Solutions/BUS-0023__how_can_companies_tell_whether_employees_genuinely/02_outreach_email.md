**Subject:** Check one claim about employee agreement before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A company asks whether employees genuinely
> share its stated values or simply perform agreement. Its usual instrument is a survey. The
> claim is that the survey produces a reading, not a source. Three different internal processes
> can return the same "I live these values" answer: the person adopts the content because it is
> congruent with their own value system, because they want the reward or want to avoid the
> sanction, or because they want to preserve a relationship with the group. The reading records
> the answer and not which process produced it. So a high score cannot, by itself, discriminate
> genuine sharing from performance. What is owed is the shape of a discriminator, not a better
> wording of the same question.
>
> This is a shape, not a measurement. I am not claiming to know which employees in your
> organisation are genuine. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that engagement scores are worthless, that people who agree are
> lying, or that a single anonymous item would fix this. Surveys are instruments, and useful
> ones. The claim is that an instrument is not the thing it measures, and that two readings from
> the same source at the same moment are one witness, not two.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an instrument shown to map one agreement reading uniquely to one internal source, with
>    published discrimination error rates that hold across a change of conditions;
> 2. a self-report measure that does not move when you change anonymity, administrator or
>    stated stakes, and still predicts a costly, unobserved value-consistent choice;
> 3. a single reading that predicts such behaviour as well as the best low-overlap pair of
>    independent instruments, so independence buys nothing.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will
> retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you
> would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not
> my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project
> - The source-discrimination essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
