# Order and isomers in the protocell co-location claim

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question "how could the three functions be gathered in one compartment" admits at least three named historical stories in the world literature: genetics-first (an RNA world later enclosed), metabolism-first (a reaction network later enclosed), and compartment-first (vesicles or pores that later acquired polymers). These are not just different timelines. They commit to different dependency orders and different bond placements. Two questions are owed: (1) what is the dependency order the figures license, and (2) are the three stories isomers under Fig 12's definition, or something weaker?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is there one linear order, or a partial order inside a union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The co-location residual is a union, so there is no single chain that solves it.
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Within each unbundled loop the order matters; between loops there is no master sequence.
- Fig 13 :10: "near, far, between, contained". Containment is a state and is a precondition for retention. It comes before the other functions can act on one another.
- Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." A flow is a precondition for sustained catalysis, because Fig 12 :121 requires a cycle that re-enters as input and a chain cannot close by construction.
- ⇒ The licensed order is a **partial order**: containment is upstream of retention; a flow is upstream of a closed cycle; a heritable ordering is upstream of cumulative selection. But genetics-first, metabolism-first and compartment-first are three different linearisations of that partial order. The figures do not select among them.
- Fig 13 :88: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place." Each linearisation produces a different next residual, which is why the world literature has three traditions rather than one.
- **Falsifier:** show a total order derivable from the six terms that selects one of the three stories without importing a world fact. If that succeeds, the partial-order claim fails. Status: `general`, structural, single-source for the figure rule (Fig 9 and Fig 3 together).

### Q2 : Are the three stories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Operators table :59: "Symmetry decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."
- As the world literature writes them, the three stories share a broad atom set: a polymer, a boundary, a catalyst, an energy flow, and an environment. The same ingredients are arranged with different bonds and different upstream ordering. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers** at the level of the shape: same atoms, different arrangement, different behavioural consequence.
- Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." This is exactly the risk in origins-of-life writing: a story that says only "polymer plus vesicle plus gradient" has recorded half of itself.
- Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." The converse also holds: the same ingredient list under a different bond is a different substance. So the three stories cannot be collapsed by pointing at their shared ingredients.
- **Falsifier, written before this run:** if one story requires an atom that another does not (for example a mineral surface that the RNA-only story cannot use), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading below stands. Status: `specified`, ungrounded, triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 plus Fig 13), unmeasured for the chemistry.

### Q3 : Functional group, catalyst and cycle

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The shared substructure across all three stories is `containing state -> structure -> retained function`, one cross-kind crossing. That is the functional group. The qualifier (genetic, catalytic or energetic) says which member.
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- A vesicle that only grows is a chain. A vesicle that grows, takes up activated monomers, copies an internal template and divides is a cycle whose output re-enters as input. The figure's "sign of the loop" clause predicts that some loops close and some do not, so a growth-only system is not a protocell even if it resembles one in every other respect.
- World corroboration: Deamer 2024 describes a laboratory system in which activated monomers enter vesicles, a template is extended, and added lipids cause growth and division. This is a candidate closed cycle, not a settled one. It is an import with provenance.
- **Falsifier:** a system that satisfies the functional group and the catalyst definition yet has no loop that re-enters as input, and still persists through generations. Status: `specified`, single-source for the figure definitions.

### Q4 : What is the order after settlement?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom".
- Each of the three functions is already settled in the world: heredity is demonstrable in polymers, catalysis in surfaces and ribozymes, energy capture in gradients. Each can therefore serve as an atom at the protocell level.
- The co-located compound, once it persists, becomes an atom at the population level. Schrum, Zhu and Szostak 2010: "The integration of a dynamic fatty-acid compartment with robust, generalized genetic polymer replication would yield a laboratory model of a protocell with the potential for classical Darwinian biological evolution." Note "would yield": a prediction, not a report of settlement.
- ⇒ The recursion needs no new primitive. What changes between levels is the cast, the currency and the anchor, not the rules (Fig 4 :19-20).
- **Falsifier:** show that the compound cannot be composed over once settled, or that a seventh primitive is required at the population level. Status: `general`, structural, triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 4 after re-census).

## CONCLUSION

The three world stories are best read as **isomers of one shape** at the level of the figure vocabulary: same atoms, different bonds and different upstream ordering, so different substances with different behaviours. Their common substructure is a functional group (`containing state -> structure -> retained function`), and each story must still close a cycle to persist. The dependency order the figures license is partial, not total: containment precedes retention, a flow precedes a closed cycle, and a heritable ordering precedes cumulative selection. The figures do not select genetics-first, metabolism-first or compartment-first. Selecting one is a world import and must be provenanced and settled by an experiment.

This rescues what is checkable in the three traditions without pretending the figures decide a historical question they do not address.
