# Expert verification packet — prior knowledge and inference

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on prior knowledge, learning or decision making can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The effect of prior knowledge is not decided by its amount. It is decided by four relations. (1) What crossed the membrane: a reapplicable method, a head start, rather than a recording of one past success. (2) Whether the prior is bound as an ordinary term that predicts and can be refused, rather than an expected answer-shape that accrues no residual. (3) Whether it ranges against a ground that does not change its answer when the prior pushes on it. (4) Whether the residual is measured per case against that ground and checked by frames that do not share the same prior. Under those conditions the prior improves inference. When they fail, especially (2) and (3), the same prior locks the learner into a mistaken model: a once-successful method is replayed, a simpler current solution is missed, and the drift is coherent and invisible from inside. Correction adds a refusal; it does not delete the prior. The numeric tipping point is an honest gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 5

- **Statement:** a prior helps as a method and locks in as a recording; a prior held as an invisible preference cannot be corrected by the loop.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 — *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."* Fig 5 :62 — *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."*
- **Refuter:** a learner improves its per-case residual while holding the prior as an unbound expectation that predicts nothing and cannot be refused; or a recording replayed unchanged on a novel case reliably produces a new success rather than a repetition.

### C2 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 8

- **Statement:** a prior that changes what counts as the ground produces coherent drift; agreement among frames that share the prior carries no evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33 — *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34 — *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Fig 2 :40 — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 8 :48 — *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
- **What to check:** is the ground in the experimental design genuinely immovable by the prior, or has the prior been allowed to define the scoring rule?
- **Refuter:** a learner whose prior is allowed to change what counts as the ground still tracks an independent measurement and self-corrects; or two high-overlap frames produce a new correction traceable to their difference, and their agreement is shown to carry evidence.

### C3 — empirical; checkable by a researcher against their own data

- **Statement:** the signal is per case, and an average can hide the lock-in; correction is a refusal added to the record, not a deletion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 6 :42 — *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."*
- **What to check:** does the ablation show a per-case residual vector whose sign differs across cases, and does the corrected learner still carry the old prior plus its refusal?
- **Refuter:** a single global residual predicts per-case correction as well as the per-case vector; or a correction that deletes the prior from the record, with no refusing term, still prevents recurrence.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a number `w*`. The figures return a comparison shape, not a magnitude; the numeric threshold is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that priors are harmful in general, or that more prior is worse. Transfer as a method is the help condition.
- Not that more data or more compute removes lock-in. Closure is idempotent: *"no search, however long, adds an atom"* (Fig 8 :32).
- Not that the mistaken prior should be deleted. A refusal is added; the record only grows (Fig 6 :42).
- Not that the receiving researcher should agree. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment, and this packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Ablation with a fixed anchor:** with and without the prior, per-case residual, anchor held fixed.
2. **Refusability probe:** can a falsifying observation for the prior be stated and bound as a term?
3. **Method versus recording:** a novel case whose solution the prior cannot supply; does the learner search or replay?
4. **Independence:** do the checkers draw on a different evidence pool?
