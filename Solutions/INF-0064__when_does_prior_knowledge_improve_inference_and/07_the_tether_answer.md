# The Tether's answer: when prior knowledge helps and when it locks a learner in

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A learner's belief `b` is *"formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w"* (Symbols table), and `w` is itself learnable from outcomes. The question is not how much prior a learner has, but what the prior is bonded to and what is allowed to check it. The residual is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot, and the two outcomes can coexist on different slots of the same learner (Fig 1 :19). This is a union compound, so it is unbundled and the verdicts are never averaged (Fig 9 :54-58).

## 2. The shape, per domain

**Domain 1, the learner's inference loop.** Prior knowledge improves inference when it is bound as an ordinary term that predicts and can be refused. Held as a preference instead, it is invisible to the checking machinery:

> Fig 5 :62 **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."**

What persists and does not trend down is the signal to refuse (Fig 5 :58). Correction adds a refusal; it does not delete the prior, and the reach falls because something was added (Fig 6 :42). The live signal is per slot, never a global mean (Fig 1 :22).

**Domain 2, the membrane.** What crosses decides the outcome:

> Fig 4 :22 **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> Fig 4 :25 **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

A prior helps as a reapplicable method that makes the next search cheaper. It locks in as a recording replayed on a novel case. The round-trip gap `R_T` must be measured, not assumed (Fig 4 :30).

**Domain 3, the frames that check.** The anchor must not move:

> Fig 2 :33 **"The anchor must not update."**
> Fig 2 :34 **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

When many learners share the same prior, their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), and the next checker is selected against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :36).

**The one-line answer.** A prior improves inference when it crosses as a method, is bound as a refusable term with a live per-slot residual, ranges against a ground that does not update on it, and is checked by frames whose closures differ. It locks a learner in when it is a replayed recording, an invisible preference, a frame that has captured its own ground, a signal averaged into a global score, or a claim checked only by people who already share it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a number. There is no `w*` returned by the figures; the numeric threshold is a `gap` until an ablation measures it (Fig 9 :32).
- Not "priors are bad." Transfer as a method is the help condition; replay as a recording is the lock condition.
- Not "more data fixes it." Closure is idempotent: *"no search, however long, adds an atom"* (Fig 8 :32). The remedy is a different frame or a new axis, not more of the same prior.
- Not "delete the wrong belief." A refusal is added; nothing is removed from the record (Fig 6 :42).
- Not `grounded`. No ablation, no instrument, no measurement was run in this derivation.
- Not a claim about which specific prior is bad. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here the corresponding checks are:

1. **Ablation with a fixed anchor.** Run the learner with and without the prior, hold the ground fixed, and measure `R_s` per slot. Positive transfer shows up as a worse residual without the prior; negative transfer as a better one. A single global accuracy number cannot discriminate the two mechanisms.
2. **A refusability probe.** Bind the prior as a predicting term and ask what observation would make it false. If no such observation can be stated, the prior is held as a preference (F1) and the lock-in prediction is confirmed.
3. **A method-versus-recording test.** Put the learner on a novel slot whose solution the prior cannot supply. A generator produces a new search; a recording repeats the last success or declares the problem impossible. The Luchins extinction problem is the canonical design.
4. **An independence check.** Compare the verdicts of two frames that were not trained on the same prior. If they share the pool, their agreement is *"one frame wearing two names"* (Fig 8 :48-49).
5. **The standing falsifiers.** A learner that improves while holding an unrefusable preference; a prior allowed to move the ground that still self-corrects; an atom derived from inside the prior's closure; a recording that succeeds on a novel case; a global residual that predicts per-slot correction. Any one fires the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in number.** The figures give the conditions, not the magnitude. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the framework *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it"* (Fig 13 :135). The stop is at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, with the lineage recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The four world literatures corroborate the shape from low-overlap fields; they do not settle it (Fig 9 :60).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that prior knowledge is good or bad, and it gives no number for when one turns into the other. It says that the outcome is decided by the relation the prior holds: a prior helps when it crosses as a reapplicable method rather than a recording of one success, when it is bound as an ordinary term that can be refused rather than an invisible expectation that accrues no error, when it ranges against a ground that does not change when the prior pushes on it, when the error is measured case by case instead of averaged, and when the check comes from someone who did not learn the same prior. When those conditions fail, the same prior locks the learner in: a familiar method is replayed on a new problem, the simpler current solution is missed, and because the prior has quietly become the standard of correctness, the drift is coherent and invisible from inside. Correction adds a refusal rather than deleting the belief. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the ablation that would close it. The numeric tipping point stays open until that ground settles it.
