# Two learners with the same prior: isomers, bonds, and the order of repair

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Two learners can hold the identical prior and diverge: one improves, one locks. The prior's *contents* do not distinguish them. Something about the arrangement does. Two questions are owed: (1) are the two learners **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) in what **order** must the repair of a locked learner run?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the prior's terms (whatever the field supplies), plus the framework's own `b`, `w`, `Γ`, `R_s`, the ground, and the refusal operator `¬`. Bonds held: the seven binary bonds plus the unary negation (Operators table; Fig 12 :49, :134-136). No new primitive is available without import. The two learners are a **union**, not a partition: the helpful and locked conditions can coexist on different slots (Fig 1 :19; Fig 9 :54-58).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Same prior, different learner: isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Both learners hold the same prior atoms. The helpful learner bonds the prior as a **production** (⇒, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :76) into a head start, and as a **sequence** (→, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :71) into the next search. The locked learner bonds the same atoms as a **comparison** (⋛, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :86) against its own expectation, and never as a **negation** (¬, *"this does not hold"*, Operators table :54), so the prior cannot be refused.
- ⇒ Two learners with identical contents and different bonds are **isomers** in the strict figure sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a list of what they know and decisive in what they do.
- The weaker, always-available reading: they share the **functional group** `belief = mix(history, prior; w)` (Symbols table). The group says what kind of learner; the bond and the presence or absence of `¬` say which member and what outcome. As with GR and QM in the exemplar, this is the claim that survives without any extra structure.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if both learners can be written with the same bond set and the same refusability, and still diverge, the isomer claim fails and the difference must be located in the ground or the habitat instead (Fig 11 :39).

### Q2 — What order does the repair run in?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Step 1, bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65). Until the expectation is a predicting term it accrues no residual and cannot be refused.
- Step 2, refuse before mint (Formula step 2; Fig 5 :50-56). A locked learner owes a **negation** of the held term, not a new explanation. Fig 12 :112: *"A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- Step 3, describe the residual in effect terms, then look for the frame whose closure predicts its shape (Fig 9 :27-28; Fig 8 :53). This is the OUTWARD track. It is opposed to INWARD sharpening and must not be read against it (Fig 6 :31).
- Step 4, import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-14; Fig 8 :50-52), debited against the new frame's independence.
- Step 5, settle against the ground, not against agreement (Fig 1 :23; Fig 2 :45).

Running these out of order is the named failure: adding a new term before refusing the wrong one leaves the wrong term's standing intact.

### Q3 — Is the tipping point a comparison?

- The Operators table defines ⋛ as **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- The question "when does the prior flip from help to lock" is a **threshold** question, so its answer-shape is a comparison. That is why the framework can return the *shape* of the tipping point, a comparison between the prior's reach and the ground's pressure (Fig 7 :37-39: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place: the bracket's fidelity across the handoff, the ground's pressure on the room"*), while refusing to return the numeric `w*`.
- Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** A comparison shape is a kind of thing. A number is not derivable here.
- **Falsifier:** if the two quantities in the comparison cannot be independently measured (prior reach and ground pressure collapse into one reading), the comparison shape is vacuous and must be withdrawn.

### Q4 — The two currencies are not additive

- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**
- Fig 12 :127: **"and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."**
- A prior can be very cheap to state (a few words, a compressed rule, a prestigious name) and very expensive to settle (a full ablation experiment). *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* states the same asymmetry from the transmitting side: *"When knowledge gets packed into a few words, the work that built it stays behind: 'Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish'."* A learner that prices only bits will propose priors it cannot afford to refute. That is a structural account of how lock-in becomes cheap.
- **Falsifier:** show a case where the description price and the settlement price are the same quantity under one unit and no shortfall is possible.

## CONCLUSION

The helpful and locked learners are **isomers** when they hold the same prior atoms under different bonds (production and sequence versus comparison without negation); the weaker, always-available claim is a shared **functional group** with a different qualifier. The repair order is fixed by dependency: bind the shape, refuse before mint, describe before looking, import with gates, settle against the ground. The tipping point's answer-shape is a **comparison** between prior reach and ground pressure, which is why a number is a `gap` and the shape is not. Two currencies, bits and actions, must be read separately or the cost of refuting a prior disappears from the accounting.
