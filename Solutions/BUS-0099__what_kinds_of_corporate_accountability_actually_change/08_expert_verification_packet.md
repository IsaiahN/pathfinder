# Expert verification packet - the Tether on corporate accountability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of corporate accountability can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No per-slot measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The five candidates are not alternatives in a contest. They are habitat actors whose value is relative to the decision at issue. An accountability mechanism changes a decision only when four conditions hold: (C1) its reading does not move when the firm pushes on it; (C2) the firm cannot produce or reconstruct the check; (C3) the reading reaches a named decision-maker who can impose a consequence, at the level of the individual slot rather than a firm-wide average; (C4) the mechanism brings a closure that differs, so a new atom can enter. These conditions form a dependency chain, not a scorecard. Which actor satisfies them for a given residual is a `gap`, because the framework returns a shape, not a name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and ground-facing; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the anchor must not update when pushed, and a check the firm can reconstruct is not a check.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
- **What to check:** is a non-updating ground actually necessary for a mechanism to change a decision, or can a self-set, self-scored reading do the same work? Consider an issuer-paid ESG rating against an enforced court finding.
- **Refuter:** a documented case in which a mechanism whose reading demonstrably moves when the firm pushes on it changes a decision at the ground as reliably as an enforced external anchor. If observed, C1 and C2 are not necessary.

### C2 - verification-arrangement facing; checkable against Figures 10 and 7 and the Seat Map

- **Statement:** a channel can decay while the ground does not, and repetitive oversight compounds agreement rather than evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Seat Map :285: *"Every instrument points inward unless one is built to point out."*
- **What to check:** does the mechanism deliver a per-slot reading to someone who can act, or does it stop at a publication, a score or an annual review? Is the review the same room as last year's? A stream with a writer and no consumer is the named failure genus in the Seat Map (:271).
- **Refuter:** a report that reaches no decision-maker and carries no possible consequence, shown to change a firm's action at the ground; or an oversight chain that keeps the same closure across cycles and still produces new, ground-verified readings without any import.

### C3 - habitat facing; checkable against Figure 11 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** the five mechanisms form a functional group, not a ranking, because valence is relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **What to check:** find a decision slot where consumer pressure changes the decision and law does not, and another where the reverse holds. If no such reversal can be found, valence is not relative and a ranking may be possible.
- **Refuter:** a prospective, per-slot study in which a single mechanism restores ground-settled contact across habitat substitutions and leaves no categorically different residue. That collapses the union into a partition and breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a league table. No mechanism is named as the winner.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement.
- Not that law always wins, or that transparency, voice, investors or buyers never matter.
- Not that more disclosure, ratings, policies or pledges change behaviour. That is library growth, not reach growth (Fig 5 :72).
- Not that reputation or corporate culture is a ground. Both are instruments inside a frame.
- Not that this derivation is a ground reading. It is a simulated check against the figures and cannot settle the business question.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Non-updating test:** does the mechanism's reading change when scope, metric or expected answer changes?
2. **Non-reconstructibility test:** can the firm author and grade the check itself?
3. **Dead-channel test:** does a consequence-bearing decision-maker read the per-slot signal?
4. **Import test:** did any atom enter the firm's reach from a frame whose closure differs?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :33; Fig 10 :24, :26, :42; Fig 11 :49-51; Fig 9 :32. Six short passages.
2. Name one real accountability case you know well. State which of C1 to C4 fails first in that case, and why.
3. Try to produce a counterexample to valence relativity: a mechanism that works the same way across two very different decision slots and habitats.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate a changed decision from a changed description in that case, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
