## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not which mechanism sounds strongest, but which mechanism changes the firm's action so that the gap between what the firm predicts and what the world returns becomes smaller at a ground that does not move when the firm pushes on it.

In the loop's terms: `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per slot `s` (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here `Γ` is the firm's library of routines, reporting systems and policies; `b` is what it believes about what counts; `a` is what it decides and does; `o'` is what the world actually returns (a harm reduced or not, an incident, an emission, a worker outcome, a court verdict, a purchase, a capital allocation). `s` is the decision slot, never the firm averaged over all its activities.

- Option A, "one of these five mechanisms is the answer": leads to the prediction that a single lever changes decisions across slots and habitats.
- Option B, "the five are habitat actors whose valence is relative to the residual": leads to the prediction that a mechanism changes decisions only when it satisfies four conditions, and that a universal ranking is a category error (Fig 11 :49, Fig 9 :54).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** stating the conditions an accountability mechanism must satisfy to change a decision, plus an instrument that would discriminate a mechanism that changes contact from one that only changes a description. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The question as posed is a **union**, not a partition, so the honest answer is a shape with conditions and a `gap` on the specific ranking, not a winner among the five.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of corporate accountability.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). This simulated second seat shares the primary's evidence pool, so it is one witness wearing two names, not corroboration.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
