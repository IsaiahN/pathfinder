# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 are stated cleanly. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output on corporate accountability before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about corporate accountability. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which accountability mechanism changes decisions: law, investor oversight, employee voice, public transparency, or consumer pressure?" is not one question with five answers. It is a union of four conditions. From the framework's own lines: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* (Fig 2 :29), and *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"* (Fig 10 :42), and *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24).
>
> So an accountability mechanism changes a decision only if all four hold:
> 1. its reading does not move when the firm pushes on it;
> 2. the firm cannot produce the check itself;
> 3. the reading reaches someone who can impose a consequence, at the level of the individual decision, not as a firm-wide average;
> 4. it brings in something the firm's own library did not already contain.
>
> Law, oversight, voice, transparency and pressure are habitat actors whose value depends on the decision at issue, not a rank order. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so I am not claiming a winner.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that more disclosure, more ratings or more policies change behaviour. Those can grow the description while leaving contact with the ground untouched. *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows"* (Fig 5 :72). I am also not claiming that this derivation is a measurement. It is a simulated check against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. a mechanism whose reading moves when the firm pushes on it changes a decision as reliably as an enforced external anchor;
> 2. a check the firm can fully author and grade is as effective as one it cannot;
> 3. a report that reaches no decision-maker and carries no consequence changes a firm's action;
> 4. a single universal ranking of the five mechanisms holds up prospectively across changes of habitat.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, verbatim, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - The source-discrimination essay, on why a reading does not determine its source: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an accountability case you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
