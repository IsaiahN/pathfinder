## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which of five accountability mechanisms (law, investor oversight, employee voice, public transparency, consumer pressure) actually change corporate decisions. Option A: treat them as five alternatives and rank them, so the answer names a winner. Option B: treat them as habitat actors in a union of conditions, so the answer is a shape stating when any mechanism changes contact with the ground. What each leads to: A produces a league table that the figures do not license and that a frame cannot check against a ground per slot; B produces four conditions, a dependency order, stated refuters and a `gap` on the specific ranking. The verdict recorded here is B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** All five mechanisms reliably produce a change in the firm's description (`Δdescription`: a policy, a report, a rating, a pledge, a campaign). They do not reliably produce a change in the firm's action at a ground (`Δcontact`: a verified harm, incident, wage, emission, court finding, purchase, capital allocation). Arity: each mechanism is a three-term relation (read the firm, report a value, consequence follows or not), carrying an ordered `→` bond. Symmetry: asymmetric, regulator and firm are not interchangeable; "bite" is a `⋛` threshold relation the firm must not be able to set. Scale: one decision slot, the habitat around the firm, and the channel across reporting cycles. Fig 1 :19 indexes R per slot, never per firm average.
- **Domain 1, the decision loop.** Actors: managers, routines, the firm's Γ. Currency: actions and residual reduction. Anchor: the realized outcome on the named slot. Question type: what changes `a` so `R` shrinks.
- **Domain 2, the accountability habitat.** Actors: regulators, courts, investors, employees, buyers, NGOs, media, standard setters. Currency: sanctions, capital, wages, purchases, attention, disclosure. Anchor: the ground each channel reports against (a counter, a market, a patient outcome, Fig 2 :29). Question type: which actor can range against which ground.
- **Domain 3, the verification arrangement.** Actors: boards, auditors, raters, reporting systems, complaint channels. Currency: agreement, provenance, independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, Fig 7 :35). Question type: whether the check is independent or reconstructible.
- **Domain 4, the temporal channel.** Actors: metrics, disclosures, audits, ratings, warnings. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground does not decay, the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Question type: whether the signal reaches a decision point.
- **Excluded candidates.** Corporate culture as self-grounding accountability: no anchor independent of the firm's own reading, so not a domain yet. Reputation as the ground: it moves when the firm pushes on it via PR, so it is an instrument inside Domains 3 and 4, not a ground.
- **Partition or union.** D2 overlaps D3 (oversight actors are habitat actors), D3 overlaps D4 (the reporting channel is the verifier's instrument), D1 overlaps D4 (the firm reads through the same channel). Union, not partition. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54-57).
- **Order by dependency.** C1 non-updating ground, then C2 non-reconstructible check, then C3 live per-slot channel with consequence, then C4 differing closure. Each later condition presupposes the earlier one (Fig 3 :20).

Strain test applied: an early attempt to map the six physical terms of Fig 13 onto stakeholder groups only fit by calling a regulator a kind of energy. That is a strain, so Fig 13 does not govern stakeholder groups. It governs the honest stop only. No strain remains after re-census (see `01` sections 2 and 6).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the ranking does not. An accountability mechanism changes a decision only when (C1) it ranges against a ground that does not move when the firm pushes on it, (C2) the firm cannot produce or reconstruct the check, (C3) the reading reaches a decision-maker who can impose a consequence at the level of the named slot, and (C4) it brings a closure that differs. These are a dependency chain, not a scorecard. Law, investor oversight, employee voice, public transparency and consumer pressure are habitat actors whose valence is relative to the residual, so no standing winner exists. More disclosure, ratings and policies grow the description, not the reach. Which actor satisfies C1 to C4 for a given slot is a `gap`: the framework returns a shape, not a name. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a league table instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the four conditions and their refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A published ranking can be superseded by the corrected shape without withdrawing any of the four conditions or any figure. No primitive has to be retracted, and the five mechanism names remain as provenanced bindings from the business frame.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A prospective per-slot study showing that a mechanism whose reading moves when pushed changes decisions as reliably as an enforced anchor would break C1; one showing a reconstructible check to be equally effective would break C2; one showing a dead report changing action would break C3; one validating a single ranking across habitat substitutions would break the union and valence verdicts. A figure re-census that admits the five as Fig 13 physical kinds, or that clears the `⋛` operand strain at the firm level, would also change the derivation. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
