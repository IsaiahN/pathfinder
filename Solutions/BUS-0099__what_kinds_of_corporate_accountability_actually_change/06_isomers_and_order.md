### Q2 - Are they isomers or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As the question writes them, the five share almost no atoms: a regulator, an investor, an employee, a buyer and a reader of public disclosures are different actors with different currencies (sanctions, capital, wages, purchases, attention). By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are members of a **functional group**: each is a `ground-reading → consequence` molecule in which the kind is the same (a channel from a firm action to a ground and back to a decision) and the qualifier is which actor occupies the slot.
- The stronger isomer version would hold only if all five were written over a shared atom set, for example `{firm action, ground reading, consequence, consequence-bearing actor}`, differing only in the bond arrangement and the actor. That is a testable structural claim, not the question as posed.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any two mechanisms can be shown to use the same atom set under the same bond and still produce different ground-settled outcomes, the functional-group reading fails and the isomer reading must be re-opened.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order of the four conditions?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** The order, checked against the figures:

1. **C1, non-updating ground** (Fig 2 :29, :33). Without an anchor there is nothing to range against; the other three conditions assume an answer to "what settles this?"
2. **C2, non-reconstructible check** (Fig 10 :42, :43). Given an anchor, the checker must be outside the firm's ability to produce the reading.
3. **C3, live per-slot channel with consequence** (Fig 10 :24, :26; Fig 1 :19). Given an independent reading, it must arrive at a decision point and remain per slot.
4. **C4, differing closure** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45). Given a live channel, a new atom can enter only from a frame whose closure differs.

This is a dependency chain, not a ranking of mechanisms. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Mandating more disclosure (a C3 move) cannot rescue a mechanism whose reading the firm can set (a C1 failure). Most reform effort lands on disclosure because that is where measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).

- **Falsifier:** a documented case in which strengthening C3 alone restores ground-settled contact in a mechanism known to fail C1. That would break the dependency order, though not the anchor rule itself.

### Q5 - Union or partition, and what order to unbundle

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Law creates disclosures; transparency feeds investors and buyers; voice supplies the fact that makes a disclosure verifiable. The five are overlapping, so a lever applied to one leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56). The unbundling order is C1 to C4 above, because each later condition presupposes the earlier one. That is the answer to "which kind of accountability": not one of the five, but which condition is failing at the named slot.

## CONCLUSION

The five mechanisms are a **functional group**, not isomers: same kind of channel, different actor in the slot. The four conditions are a **dependency chain**, not a ranking, ordered C1 to C4. Which actor satisfies them for a given residual is a `gap`: the framework returns a shape, not a name. This framing rescues what is checkable in the question (which condition fails, and what instrument would close it) without producing the universal winner the figures do not license. Two falsifiers are stated above (Q2 and Q3); a third is in Q4.
