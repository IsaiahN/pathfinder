# Outreach draft : check one claim before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two domains below are kept separate in whatever you write. If the claim collapses into "reliability is possible" or "it is not", the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about communication without a shared code: what would refute it?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. A one-line failure is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** "Can sender and receiver communicate reliably without a shared code or common context?" is two questions with different anchors, not one. A fact-bearing question (did the signal get the world right?) has an external reference. A meaning-bearing question (did the signal mean the same thing to both sides?) has no external reference until the two build one. The figure-grounded result is:
>
> 1. A codebook is a convention, not an anchor. It can be revised by the participants, so it cannot settle a fact about the world.
> 2. Repeating a signal, adding redundancy, or slowing the rate cannot close a meaning residual when the receiver lacks the term. Those moves change the channel, not contact.
> 3. What can cross between two frames that share no code is a description of effects that both can check against something outside themselves, plus a two-way round trip in which the receiver shows what it took the message to mean. Meaning is rebuilt through contact, not transmitted as a codeword.
> 4. The agreement that results is a convention, which is a legitimate product, but it is not a truth verdict. Only a reference neither side can move decides facts.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that no reliable system is possible; that Shannon's coding theorem is wrong; that there is a universal code; that meaning is irrelevant; that any particular protocol, alphabet or grammar is the answer. Shannon's result governs the case where the code and channel statistics are fixed in advance. That is a different domain from the one this claim is about. This is a shape and a scope boundary, not a measurement or a protocol.
>
> **Written down before this email, the refuters are:**
> 1. Two frames with disjoint vocabularies derive a shared mapping for a novel domain from a one-way signal alone, with no round trip and no external reference.
> 2. Repeating a signal inside a fixed code closes a meaning residual that was open at the first transmission, with no change in the receiver's library.
> 3. A convention both participants can revise nonetheless settles a fact about the world with no external reference.
> 4. A pair that only talks to itself, with no separate experience, builds a shared vocabulary that still correctly predicts a measurement neither had seen.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. What would you count as a failure here?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, verbatim text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Public figures folder: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - The engineering scope boundary: Claude Shannon, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication", 1948: https://spinchange.com/the-mathematical-theory-of-communication/
> - The convention side: Vanderschraaf and Sillari, "Common Knowledge", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 2023: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2023/entries/common-knowledge/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
