# Are the two uncertainties isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the ordering claim, `general` on the structural analogy.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit concluded that reception uncertainty and meaning uncertainty are two failure genera with opposed remedies. Two supporting questions are owed:

1. Are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition (same atoms, different bond, different substance), or are they something else?
2. What **order** binds them, and is that order a fact about the world or about this particular residual?

A third question is whether the low-overlap analogues (Shannon's source/channel split, vervet alarm calls, metamerism) are instances, parallels or borrowed machinery. The answer changes how much weight they get.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six terms (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures), the seven bonds plus negation, `R` per slot `s`, the ground, a frame, a convention. No new primitive is available without import. The two uncertainties are not atoms in Γ; they are **sorts of residual** at two different links of the dependency chain (Fig 3 :4-13).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are they isomers? CORRECTION TO A TEMPTING READING

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- A naive reading: both uncertainties are "a residual on a slot", so they share atoms and differ only in bond. That would make them isomers.
- **Re-census refutes it.** They do not share the same atoms. U_reception is composed from `R`, slot `s`, the ground and the channel; U_meaning is composed from two frames, a reading, a bond/arrangement and a convention (Fig 10 :13, :46). The channel is not an operand of the seam composition, and the convention is not an operand of the channel composition. Different atom sets cannot be isomers.
- The correct relation is Fig 3's **dependency order**: perception (link 1) then vocabulary (link 2). They are two links of one chain, not two arrangements of one set.
- **I nearly recorded them as isomers.** Retracted before it entered the primary audit. Triangulated across Fig 12 (definition) and Fig 3 (chain order), two figures with different subject matter. An analogue of isomerism remains useful in a weaker sense: the two uncertainties are different in behaviour while looking similar in the aggregate residual, which is the phenomenon Fig 12 :117 describes. But the word "isomer" itself is declined.

### Q2: Is the order a fact about the world or about this residual?

- Fig 3 :18: "The break is drawn between three and four as an illustration. Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."
- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- So the order is **structural for any chain that has both links**, but the location of the break is a reading. In this case the break is drawn at reception because a reading below a missing input is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). That is a fact about this residual, not a universal ranking of perception above meaning.
- Fig 13 :4-5: "The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other." No privilege is being claimed for the channel as a kind of thing. It is upstream **for this chain**, in the same sense that a step is upstream of the step that consumes its output.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a chain can read meaning while its reception link returns nothing, the dependency order is false and the two uncertainties may be independent rather than ordered. That is refuter R3 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

### Q3: What do the figures say about the crossing between them?

- Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- In this case, what may cross from the channel test to the seam work is a **characterised residual** (Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source"), not the framework interior and not a verdict about the world. A known-positive fixture result is a head start for the meaning work; it is not a replay of a previous interpretation.
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Any compression of the channel result into a single confidence score must carry its round-trip gap, or the meaning work downstream is dead reckoning.

### Q4: Do the low-overlap analogues carry the verdict?

- **Shannon's source/channel split (mathematics and engineering).** The channel theorem governs reproducing a message at a point from a signal sent at another; the source coding theorem governs recovering symbols and notes that "the decoder knows the source" is an assumption that often fails. This is a **parallel**: it has the same shape but a different substrate and a different ground (bits and entropy, not frames and the ground). It corroborates, it does not decide.
- **Vervet alarm calls (animal behaviour).** Acoustically distinct calls make reception measurable; responses track semantics and habituation to unreliable signalers shows reliability tracked apart from detection. This is an **instance** in the architecture article's sense: a case the framework describes, arrived at independently by someone else. Lowest overlap of all the sources, so the strongest external check, but still only corroboration.
- **Metamerism / source discrimination (perception).** The green reading does not determine the source; a spectrometer resolved it. This is a **parallel with a shared author**: it is the framework's own essay, so it has high overlap with the figures and weak independent weight. It supplies the shape of the missing discriminator.
- Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." All three are recorded with category and provenance here; none is allowed to hand the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

### Q5: Is the union a partition or a compound?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- The two uncertainties can co-occur on one slot. A single lever (more bandwidth) clears the region where only U_reception holds and leaves the intersection (live channel, underdetermined reading, plus a dead second channel) standing. So the correct move is to unbundle: run the per-slot known-positive check, then treat the remaining seam as its own compound with its own ground.
- Seat Map :211: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Recorded as a constraint on the procedure, not as a new claim about information theory.

## CONCLUSION

The two uncertainties are **not isomers**. They are two links of a dependency-ordered chain, with different atom sets, different outputs and opposed remedies. The order is structural for any chain with both links, while the location of the break is a reading specific to this residual (Fig 3 :18, :20). The membrane between them carries a characterised residual and a head start, never a source or a replay (Fig 4 :21-25; Fig 10 :52). The low-overlap analogues (Shannon, vervet calls) are a parallel and an instance respectively; the framework-authored metamerism essay is a high-overlap parallel that supplies the shape of a missing instrument without filling the slot. The union case must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54-58).

This framing rescues what was defensible in the naive "syntax versus semantics" shorthand (two different things that look alike in the aggregate) without the part that failed: it does not merge them into one scalar, does not import a semantic theory to name the slot, and does not promote a convention to a verdict.
