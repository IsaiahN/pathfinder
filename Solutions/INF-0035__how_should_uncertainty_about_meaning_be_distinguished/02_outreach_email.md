# Outreach draft: check the two-test split before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 is still standing after you have read the packet. The first contact carries the residual and the failure condition, never the framework interiors.

---

**Subject:** Two uncertainties that look alike: would you count this split as a failure?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a receiver is unsure, two failures can register on the same object slot. One is that no reading is arriving (the channel to the ground has failed). The other is that a reading is arriving but what it is about is underdetermined (two frames have no shared reading). The claim is not that these are two ends of one uncertainty scale. They are two different failure genera with different outputs and opposed remedies.
>
> The figure this comes from has two separate panels, one for the seam (meaning) and one for the channel (reception). On the channel side it says "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24) and lists the ways a channel dies: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" (Fig 10 :25-26). The output there is not a verdict about the world but a verdict on the connection: contact has been lost, and the remedy is to restore the reading. On the seam side it says "a declared meaning is not a grounded one" (Fig 10 :18). The output there is a convention that makes a previously unstatable disagreement statable, not a verdict about the signal.
>
> **The operational shape:** (1) test contact per slot with a signal you know is there, because "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" (Fig 1 :19); (2) only then ask whether the arrangement that carries the reading is recoverable, since "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not" and "nothing signals that anything was lost" (Fig 12 :14, :16); (3) if both failures are present, unbundle them before searching. Meaning work done over a dead channel is not weak meaning work, it is "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :23).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not a new measure of semantic information; not that meaning reduces to channel statistics; not that a convention settles what a message was about; not that more bandwidth resolves an interpretive disagreement; and not that any of this is `grounded`. No per-slot channel measurement was taken here. This is a shape and a route, and it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it (written before sending):**
> 1. A case where meaning is fully determined by reception probability, so the seam panel is unnecessary.
> 2. A case where declaring a meaning grounds it, or a convention can decide an outcome and remain a convention.
> 3. A case where a reading taken while no signal arrives still discriminates between meanings, or where refining the same already-returning instrument resolves a pure seam disagreement.
> 4. A single scalar uncertainty in which more reception always reduces meaning uncertainty, so the compound is one quantity.
>
> If any holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
