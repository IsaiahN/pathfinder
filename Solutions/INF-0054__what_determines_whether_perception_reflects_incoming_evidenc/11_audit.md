## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :11, :19, Fig 2 :29-33, Fig 10 :24-29 and the Formula closure.
2. **Abstraction (`T_A`):** the shorthand in `07`: a per-object gap with three determinant conditions, an internal indiscernibility limit, and no single knob.
3. **Concretisation (`T_E`):** the tempting laboratory claim, "one precision parameter controls the evidence versus prior balance", which is how the shorthand is usually heard by a prediction researcher.

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Four constraints held by the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- `w` is belief-source composition, not observation-versus-belief (Symbols table :20, :24-25);
- channel decay and anchor capture are different conditions with different remedies (Fig 10 versus Fig 2);
- the residual must be per object, so a global fit is not a permissible concretisation (Fig 1 :13, :19);
- a perfect prediction and a closed channel are indiscernible from inside, so no internal scalar can certify contact (Formula closure).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a per-object gap between prediction and observation with three separately named determinant conditions. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09` now states.
