## 3. Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :40** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not pool stage-2 agreement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only if curious, the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 4. Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded first and never pooled later.
3. **Stage 3 sends a method**, which is permitted by Fig 4 :22. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
4. **Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. That is why the five refuters are in the first message, before any theory.
