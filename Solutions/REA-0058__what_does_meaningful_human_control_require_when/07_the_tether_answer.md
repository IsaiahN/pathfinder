# The Tether's answer: what meaningful human control requires at machine speed and scale

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive shape, `general` on the structural claims. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system acting faster or at larger scale than its supervisor does not create a shortage of human goodwill. It creates a decaying channel between the seat and the ground. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. The seat's output stops being a verdict and becomes *"you have lost touch"* (Fig 10 :27-28), while the arrangement still looks like oversight. The residual is indexed per slot: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"* (Fig 1 :13). This is a union compound of speed and scale, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*).

## 2. The shape

1. **Control is a property of the arrangement, not of the person.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Training the operator without changing instruments, authority or contact changes nothing (Fig 11 :40). The figure's seat has three offices: be outside the slice being graded, have no gain from a particular reading, and see what agreement hides.

2. **The anchor must not update, and the seat must be independent of the frame it grades.** Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A supervisor fed only the system's own output is a second spelling of the first. Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*

3. **Per-slot contact at the system's speed.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* A green dashboard with one live slot is the named failure. The honest diagnostic is Fig 3 :29-30: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

4. **The vocabulary must be able to compare and refuse.** Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* A seat with only ingredients and a sequence can say what happened and cannot say it was wrong. Operators table :57: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Comparison must precede refusal: you cannot subtract what you have not compared.

5. **Only methods cross the speed/scale membrane, and the round trip must be measured.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* A protocol that worked for ten agents is a recording at ten thousand agents until R_T is measured.

6. **The stake must be declared and the lineage kept.** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :65-66: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

7. **"Meaningful" must be an ordinary term with a refuter written before the run.** Fig 5 :66: *"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."* Fig 5 :67: *"a frame cannot certify its own completeness"*. The shape is bound in `01` §0 and the four refuters in `01` §4 precede the conclusion here.

8. **The number is a declared gap.** No figure line states a latency, an attention share, or an operator-to-agent ratio. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* What is missing is an instrument that discriminates a live slot from a pooled aggregate at the system's speed and shows whether a refusal changed contact with the ground.

The world sources are consistent with this shape but do not decide it. Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven (2018) supply tracking and tracing as necessary conditions, not a number; Bainbridge (1983) supplies the speed requirement that the computer act "at a rate, which the operator can follow"; automation-bias research supplies the mechanism by which the channel decays. These are corroboration from different fields, not verdicts.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a latency, a ratio, or a staffing number. Those are `gap`, not refusal.
- Not "the human should always make the final decision." Fig 2 :36-38 limits the human's role as anchor to questions constitutively about people; on other axes human judgment is *"only an instrument"* and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure.
- Not "more training" or "more vigilance." Fig 11 :40: an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing.
- Not that any published account of meaningful human control is false. The figure verdict is about what any such account must include, not about the standing of a particular school.
- Not a derivation of one field's theory from another.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument reading settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Channel test.** At the system's speed and scale, does a refusal change contact with the ground? An instrument that shows the live slot before and after the refusal would close F1.
- **Independence test.** Are the seat's readings drawn from evidence different from the system's? A design that forces two separate instruments would close F2.
- **Comparison-before-refusal test.** Can the arrangement state what counts as acceptable before it vetoes? An instrument that records the comparison and the negation as separate events would close F8.
- **Round trip test.** Does an oversight method moved from one scale to another retain its ground-validated refusal rate, and what does the moved description lose? This measures R_T (Fig 4 :30).
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §4:** the channel test, the mirror test, the vocabulary test and the contact test, each stated so it can fire.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is figure-derived and checkable. The number is not. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost of being wrong here is high enough to buy a second, independent frame; the cost of inventing a number before an instrument exists is that a clean-looking control arrangement becomes a mirror nobody can correct. The answer stops at what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for meaningful human control, and it does not claim that a human in the loop is enough. It says that speed and scale break the channel between the supervisor and the ground, not the supervisor's intentions, so the first requirement is a seat that keeps a non-updating anchor reachable and reads the live case per slot rather than through a green aggregate. The seat must be an independent frame, not a mirror of the system, and it must have a vocabulary that can compare what counts as acceptable and then refuse what is not. An oversight method moved across scales carries only as a method, with a measured round-trip gap. The specific latency or operator-to-agent ratio is a declared gap, owed to an instrument that can show a refusal changing contact with the ground. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
