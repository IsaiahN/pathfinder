## 0. The claim under audit

The question is put in effect terms before any figure is chosen.

> **R, stated as an effect.** R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s. The supervisor is a seat whose office is to keep the ground reachable: the anchor is whatever settles *"was this action acceptable here, under the reasons that apply?"* and does not move because the system or the operator thinks otherwise. When the system's cycle time and its number of object slots exceed what the supervisor can resolve, the channel decays. The figures name the decay: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* (Fig 10 :25-26). The seat's output stops being a verdict and becomes *"you have lost touch"* (Fig 10 :27-28). The appearance of control survives when the substance does not: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :12-13).
>
> **Option A (keep the human formally in the loop).** Presence is treated as sufficient. A button exists, a dashboard exists, a human signs off. This yields a mirror chain: agreement compounds, the live slot residual is averaged away, and the ground is never consulted. It also spends the independence of the person who was supposed to check.
>
> **Option B (require the structural conditions of a seat).** The shape of control is stated before the number; the seat keeps a non-updating anchor, an independent frame, per-slot contact at the system's speed, a vocabulary that can refuse, and a measured round trip. The specific latency or ratio stays open until an instrument settles it. This yields a checkable claim with a refuter written in advance.
>
> **What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** An answer is a set of necessary conditions on the *control arrangement*, not on the human's good intentions and not on a single number. The sought-for shape is therefore bound as: **MHC = a seat that (i) ranges against a non-updating ground, (ii) is independent of the frame it grades, (iii) resolves the live residual per slot at the system's speed and scale, (iv) can state a goal as a comparison and a refusal as a negation, and (v) carries a measured round trip across the speed/scale membrane.** Written down, it can accrue a residual like anything else. Fig 5 :66: *"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."*

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question: speed and scale are two overlapping conditions with a compound at their intersection (Fig 9 :55-57). A single lever that clears the speed-only region leaves the scale-only region and the intersection standing. Keep the verbatim line: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57).

## 1. Domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** A relation among at least three terms: the supervisor seat, the system frame, and a ground that is not a frame. A two-place reading (human, machine) is a mis-census, because it has no place for the thing that settles the question.
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric. The ground must not update on the frames: *"The anchor must not update."* (Fig 2 :33). The relation is "range against", not "negotiate with": *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* (Fig 2 :45). The supervisor also stands outside the slice it grades, so the system cannot range against the supervisor as its ground.
- **Scale.** Two membranes. Speed (cycle time of action versus cycle time of perception and correction) and count (number of object slots and agents). The kernel is invariant: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* (Fig 4 :19-20). What changes with size is the anchor's reachability, not the loop.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Supervisory seat / ground maintenance** | human supervisor, action stream, the ground | contact and residual reduction | the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29, :33) | admitted |
| **D2 System loop** | system Γ, action a, observation o′, object slots s | per-slot residual; actions | the same ground, read at a different cycle time; closure may differ from the seat's | admitted |
| **D3 Speed and scale membrane** | one-agent loop, many-agent population, instruments, head starts | methods up, head starts down, round trip gap R_T | the kernel (unchanged with size) and the anchor (must be kept reachable) | admitted |
| **D4 Verification and independence** | supervisor frame, system frame, outside frame (verifier) | agreement versus evidence; overlap | independence, that is low overlap (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :48) | admitted |
| **X1 "Meaningful" as a free-floating property of presence or intent** | the operator's good will | none nameable | none until the sought-for shape is written down as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65-66) | not a domain yet |
| **X2 "Control = a fixed intervention latency T"** | clock, button | seconds | absorbed into D3 and D4; the number is a `gap`, not a hidden term | absorbed |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. X1 becomes a domain only when the shape is bound, which is done in §0 and audited in F8.

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D4 form the verification compound (a seat that is also independent). D2 and D3 form the membrane compound (a system whose loop runs across the speed/scale boundary). The two compounds compose at the seat. Treating them as a list of separate requirements is the bundled-problem error: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* (Fig 9 :55). The intersection case, "fast and many at once", is categorically different from either condition alone (Fig 9 :57).

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception.** The seat can resolve what is in front of it and tell it apart from what was there before: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before"* (Fig 3 :5).
2. **Vocabulary.** Terms for what the system's states afford, including a way to say "stop" and "this is wrong": a vocabulary without bonds *"can name a state and cannot name a route to one"* (Fig 12 :111); *"A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* (Fig 12 :112).
3. **Objective.** The seat can say what would count as progress in its own terms: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"* (Fig 3 :10). This is the middle link where chains usually break, because it has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests (Fig 3 :24-27).
4. **Planning.** A route to intervene under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify, carry to the next problem. Across scales only a method crosses: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22).

Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a link below it: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (Fig 3 :19). A reading taken when the perception link is broken is *"a reading of nothing"* (Fig 3 :23).

## 3. Findings

### F1 (D1): Meaningful control is a property of the seat's contact, not of human presence

Verbatim:

> Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**
> Fig 10 :29: **"keep the anchor reachable"**
> Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

The failure named by the question is not that the human is slow. It is that the channel by which the ground reaches the seat has been cut while the seat still looks like a seat. The ground did not go anywhere. Saturated metrics and pooled readings are the same failure as a dashboard whose global number is near zero while a slot is live. The prescription is structural, not exhortatory: keep the anchor reachable, and keep it non-updating. A human who ratifies whatever the system emits is not maintaining a ground, they are a frame that has joined the system.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 10 on channel decay and Fig 2 on the anchor rule, low overlap between them). **Refuter:** a seat whose metrics are demonstrably saturated and whose readings are pooled still produces ground-validated refusals of the system's actions at the system's own speed and scale, with the live slot named. If that is shown, this finding fails.

### F1b (D1): Capability is not located in the supervisor alone

Verbatim:

> Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Training the operator harder, without changing the instruments, the interface, the authority to refuse, or the contact with the system's per-slot state, does not change control. The habitat of the seat includes the dashboard, the latency of the feed, the rule that lets a refusal stick, and the other frames the seat can import from. This is a consequence of the census, not a slogan: speed and scale change the habitat, and the kernel is unchanged (Fig 4 :19-20).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 on capability and Fig 4 on the invariant kernel). **Refuter:** an operator-only intervention that changes no instrument, no authority and no contact still moves the ground-validated refusal rate at the system's speed and scale.

### F2 (D4): The supervisor must be an independent frame, not a mirror

Verbatim:

> Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**
> Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
> Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

A supervisor fed only the system's own output is not a second frame; it is a second spelling of the first. The human-in-the-loop arrangement degrades into a mirror precisely when the loop's speed forces the human to rely on the system's summary. The system and the seat must draw on different evidence. Their union is where the surplus lives: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* (Fig 8 :45). Where the closures differ, the seat can import what the system cannot derive: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* (Fig 6 :23).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 on independence, Fig 8 on union surplus, Fig 7 on the mirror chain; three low-overlap routes). **Refuter:** a supervisor whose readings are generated entirely by the system's own output still passes an independence test, that is, still counts as a second frame whose agreement with the system carries evidence.

### F3 (D2 and D3): At scale, aggregate oversight hides the live slot

Verbatim:

> Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
> Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step."**
> Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**

This is the figure line that bears most directly on the "larger scale" half of the question. At scale the natural oversight instrument is a global metric: an accuracy, an alert count, a risk score. The figures say that such a metric is the exact instrument that loses the signal. A global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state, so the dashboard can be green while the thing that mattered is on fire. Meaningful control at scale therefore requires the seat to see the per-object residual, not a summary of it.

**Mode:** `specified`, `general` for the indexing rule itself. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 on per-slot indexing and Fig 4 :19-20 on what changes with size). **Refuter:** a single aggregate metric, with no per-slot access, still lets the seat locate and refuse the live slot at scale, measured on a real ground.

### F4 (D3): The dependency chain must be intact at the speed the system runs

Verbatim:

> Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
> Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
> Fig 3 :22: **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."**
> Fig 3 :29-30: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

Speed moves the break point. A seat that can perceive and veto at a human cadence may stop at link 1 when the system acts in milliseconds. The override button is then not a weak control; by Fig 3 :23 a reading taken below a break is *"a reading of nothing."* The middle link, having terms for what the situation offers and being able to say what counts as progress, has the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :24-27), which is exactly where "meaningful" lives. The honest question is not "is the human in control?" but "which link does the arrangement currently stop at, and was that measured?"

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 3 on the chain and Fig 10 :25-26 on dropped transients as the speed failure). **Refuter:** an arrangement whose perception link demonstrably does not resolve individual actions still yields a valid, ground-checked control verdict from its override button.

### F5 (D3): Only methods cross the speed/scale membrane, and the round trip must be measured

Verbatim:

> Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
> Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
> Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
> Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

A control procedure that worked at one scale is a method only if it can be reapplied; copied as a recording it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. At larger scale the transform loses information, and that is the point: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* (Fig 4 :27). The lossy step must be bracketed by a round trip gap R_T. Treating a small-team oversight protocol as identical to a fleet-level one sets R_T to zero by assumption, which is dead reckoning. `11_audit.md` runs the round trip on this solution itself.

**Mode:** `specified`, `general` for the membrane rule. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 4 on the membrane and Fig 12 on level-relative irreducibility). **Refuter:** an oversight procedure transferred from one scale to another as a fixed recording, with no measured R_T, retains its ground-validated refusal rate at the new scale.

### F6 (D4): The seat must see what agreement hides and declare its stake

Verbatim:

> Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
> Fig 10 :62: **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."**
> Fig 10 :65-66: **"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."**
> Fig 9 :51: **"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"**

Every oversight arrangement has a stake: the operator may want the deployment to succeed, the reviewer may want the framework to be right. The figures do not demand a stake-free seat, which does not exist. They demand that the stake be declared and that it not enter the reading. A clean record does not make a refusal correct; only the ground does. This is why the verification packet in `08` carries provenance but does not ask anyone to trust it, and why evidence about the maker is withheld from the verifier.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 10 on the seat and Fig 9 on what the verifier may see). **Refuter:** a supervisor with an undeclared stake in the deployment still qualifies as a seat because its audit trail is clean.

### F7: The numeric threshold is a `gap`, not a hidden answer

After a genuine re-census, no figure line governs a specific intervention latency, attention share, or operator-to-agent ratio. The figures govern the shape of the control arrangement and the conditions under which a number would be meaningful. They do not state the number.

> Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
> Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
> Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument in effect terms: an instrument that, at the system's speed and scale, distinguishes a live slot residual from a pooled aggregate, and shows whether a refusal actually changes contact with the ground. Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven (2018) make the same point in the field's own vocabulary: *"policy-makers and technical designers lack a detailed theory of what 'meaningful human control' exactly means."* The field supplies two necessary conditions (tracking and tracing), not the number. The Holy See's conditions reported in the Wikipedia LAWS article are a concrete shape, not a universal latency.

**Mode:** `specified` for the gap declaration, `general` for the honesty rule. **Status:** `gap`. **What would close it:** a ground measurement that settles a threshold under a declared instrument, or a figure line found after re-census that states one. Until then the honest report is *"unreached, not unreachable."*

### F8: "Meaningful" must be an ordinary term with a pre-registered refuter

Verbatim:

> Fig 5 :65: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"**
> Fig 5 :66: **"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."**
> Fig 5 :67: **"a frame cannot certify its own completeness"**

The Formula's "Direction" section states the limit: the loop can enumerate its bound terms and rank them by residual, but it cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape it seeks is not a term it holds. So the shape is bound in §0, and the refuter is written in §4 before any conclusion. A control claim whose "meaningful" is never bound cannot accrue a residual and cannot be refuted. That is the output-layer defect named in the exemplar's Fig 5 reading, and it is the defect that most outreach in this field commits when it asks for agreement with the word "meaningful" rather than a test of a stated shape.

**Mode:** `general` for the discipline, `specified` for its application here. **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 5 and the Formula's Direction section). **Refuter:** a control claim that survives its pre-registered refuter unchanged and with no residual, and whose "meaningful" was never bound, still counts as checkable.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, following the exemplar's discipline that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. Written here before the recommendation and before `07`/`09` were drafted. No figure line states this; it is a discipline carried from the checked exemplar package, not a figure quote.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Channel test.** A seat whose metrics are demonstrably saturated and whose readings are pooled still produces ground-validated refusals of the system's actions at the system's own speed and scale, with the live slot named (breaks F1, F3).
2. **Mirror test.** A supervisor whose readings are generated entirely by the system's own output still passes an independence test, that is, its agreement with the system still carries evidence about the ground (breaks F2).
3. **Vocabulary test.** An arrangement with no way to state a goal as a comparison and no way to negate a claim still produces refusable refusals that the ground validates (breaks F8 and the role of Fig 12 :111-112).
4. **Contact test.** An operator-only improvement that changes no instrument, no authority and no contact still moves the ground-validated refusal rate (breaks F1b).

A fifth item is recorded but is **not** a refuter of the shape: finding a figure line that states a numeric threshold, or a ground measurement that settles one, would close F7 without touching F1 to F4. Keeping it separate prevents the gap from being smuggled in as a verdict.

If any of 1 to 4 fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and the refuter, not with a number. The field's own founding paper says the theory is missing (Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven, 2018), so offering a number first would answer a question the figures do not pose.
2. Audit the arrangement, not the operator. Ask which link in Fig 3 the arrangement stops at, whether the anchor is non-updating, and whether the seat is a mirror. This is Fig 3 :29-30 applied literally.
3. Index the residual per slot at the system's speed. A global risk score is the instrument Fig 1 :22 warns about.
4. Bracket the speed and scale transforms with a measured R_T. A protocol that worked for ten agents is a recording at ten thousand agents until the round trip is run (Fig 4 :30).
5. Bind "meaningful" as an ordinary term and write the refuter before the run (Fig 5 :65-66). Declare the stake (Fig 10 :59).
6. Use the public, opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, and ask for a refutation rather than belief. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, not the conclusion.

### Strain test applied to two apparent conflicts

1. **Fig 4 :19-20 (kernel unchanged with size) versus Fig 10 :24 (channel decays).** Not strained after re-census. Fig 4 governs what crosses the membrane (methods and head starts); Fig 10 governs the ground maintainer's channel. Different quantities. A kernel can be invariant while its channel decays.
2. **Fig 2 :33 (anchor must not update) versus the field's "tracking" condition** (a system should respond to human reasons, Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven 2018). Not strained after re-census. Fig 2 :36-38 says an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Human reasons are the legitimate anchor for "acceptable to people"; for "does the system implement its spec" the ground is an instrument. The two sources govern different axes. World sources corroborate but do not overrule.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives. Meaningful control requires a maintained seat with a non-updating anchor, an independent and non-mirroring frame, per-slot contact at the system's speed, a vocabulary with comparison and negation, and a measured round trip across speed and scale. The specific number does not survive as a figure-derived claim: it is a `gap` that needs a ground instrument. Strains after re-census: **none hidden; two dissolved, one gap declared.**
