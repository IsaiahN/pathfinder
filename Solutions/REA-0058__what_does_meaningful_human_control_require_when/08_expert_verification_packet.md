# Expert verification packet: meaningful human control at machine speed and scale

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** No instrument reading is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Speed and scale break the channel between a supervisor and the ground that would settle whether an action was acceptable; they do not primarily break the supervisor's intentions. Meaningful human control therefore requires a set of conditions on the *control arrangement*: the seat keeps a non-updating anchor reachable and draws on evidence different from the system's own; it resolves the live residual per slot at the system's speed; it has a vocabulary with a comparison and a negation so it can state a goal and refuse a case; and a control method moved across scales carries only as a method, with a measured round-trip gap. The seat's stake is declared, and its provenance is kept without being mistaken for a verdict. The framework does not give a latency or an operator-to-agent ratio; that number is a declared gap owed to an instrument. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal and human-factors facing; checkable by reading Fig 10 and Fig 1

- **Statement:** the failure is a decaying channel to a non-updating ground, and at scale the live case is hidden by aggregation. Control must be specified per slot, at the system's speed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :29: *"keep the anchor reachable"*; Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
- **Refuter:** a seat whose metrics are demonstrably saturated and whose readings are pooled still produces ground-validated refusals of the system's actions at the system's own speed and scale, with the live case named. Or: a single aggregate metric, with no per-slot access, still lets the seat locate and refuse the live case on a real ground.

### C2: cross-field; checkable by anyone who has supervised a fast or many-agent system

- **Statement:** the supervisor must be an independent frame, not a mirror; and the arrangement must be able to compare what counts as acceptable before it refuses a case.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*; Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*; Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*; Operators table :57: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.
- **What to check:** in a real oversight arrangement, are the seat's readings drawn from instruments different from the system's, or are they the system's own summary? Is the "acceptable" threshold stated before the veto, or is the veto a reflex to a saturated alert?
- **Refuter:** a supervisor whose readings are generated entirely by the system's own output still passes an independence test; or an arrangement with no comparison and no negation still produces refusable refusals that the ground validates.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with an engineering consequence

- **Statement:** a control method moved across the speed/scale membrane carries only as a method, and the round trip gap must be measured; the capabilities of the arrangement are not located in the operator alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*; Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*; Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **Refuter:** an oversight procedure copied from one scale to another as a fixed recording retains its ground-validated refusal rate with no measured R_T; or an operator-only improvement that changes no instrument, no authority and no contact still moves that rate.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a latency, an attention share, a staffing ratio, or any other number. The number is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that the human is always the anchor. Fig 2 :36-38 allows human judgment as the right anchor only where the question is constitutively about people; elsewhere it is *"only an instrument"*, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure.
- Not that more training or vigilance restores control. That is the agent-only improvement Fig 11 :40 refuses.
- Not that any published account of meaningful human control is false; this is a statement of necessary conditions on an arrangement, not a school-level verdict.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an instrument.
- Not that a clean provenance trail makes a ruling correct. Fig 10 :65-66: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument, exactly as the spectrometer settled the metamerism case in *The Hard Problem is About Source*.

1. **Channel:** at the system's speed and scale, does a refusal change contact with the ground? An instrument showing the live slot before and after the refusal would close C1.
2. **Independence and order:** are the seat's readings drawn from evidence different from the system's, and is the comparison recorded before the negation? Two separate instrument traces would close C2.
3. **Round trip:** does a method moved across scale retain its ground-validated refusal rate, and what is lost in the moved description? This measures R_T and closes C3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :24, :25-26, :29; Fig 1 :13, :19; Fig 2 :33, :35; Fig 8 :48; Fig 12 :112; Fig 4 :22, :30; Fig 11 :40: eleven short passages.
2. Check C1 and C2 against one system you know: name the instrument that feeds the seat and say whether it is independent of the system's own summary.
3. Name one instrument that would show a refusal changing contact with the ground at that system's speed and scale, or say why none is possible. This is the gap.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not send agreement as corroboration; the stage-1 verdict is recorded before the theory is shown.
