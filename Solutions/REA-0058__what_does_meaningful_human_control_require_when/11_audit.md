## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as **"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."** Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured. I ran the trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 10 :24-29, Fig 1 :13/:19, Fig 2 :33/:35, Fig 3 :27, Fig 4 :22/:30, Fig 12 :112.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: "keep a human meaningfully in control at machine speed and scale".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete arrangement with an instrument, a comparison, a refusal rule, a declared stake and a measured scale transfer.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip down:

- **per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19) collapses into "human oversight";
- **the non-updating anchor** (Fig 2 :33) collapses into "the operator's judgment";
- **independence** (Fig 2 :35, Fig 8 :48) collapses into "a human is present";
- **comparison before refusal** (Fig 12 :112) collapses into "a veto button".

One thing survives cleanly: at speed and scale the control arrangement needs a maintained channel to something that does not move when the frames think otherwise. That is the residue worth sending. R_T is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints, not numerically, because no ground reading exists. Treating "human in the loop" as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error.
