# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the frame

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been read alongside this draft. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about human control at machine speed, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A system that acts faster or at larger scale than its supervisor does not primarily create a "human attention" problem. It creates a **channel** problem. The thing that would settle whether an action was acceptable (call it the ground) does not move, but the supervisor's contact with it does: metrics saturate, transients get dropped, readings get pooled. So the natural oversight instruments fail exactly where they are most needed. A global risk score can be near zero while one live case is unresolved.
>
> From that, the figures give a shape that any answer must have. The supervisor has to stay a genuine seat: a frame that ranges against a non-updating ground, draws on evidence different from the system's own, resolves the live case per slot at the speed the system runs, has a vocabulary that can compare and refuse, and carries a measured round trip when a control procedure is moved from one scale to another. Training the operator harder changes nothing unless it changes contact.
>
> **What this does not say.** It does not give a number. No figure line states an intervention latency, an attention share, or an operator-to-agent ratio. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The specific number is owed to an instrument, and until one exists I record it as a gap rather than filling it. I am also not claiming that any published account of meaningful human control is the right one, and I am not asking you to trust mine.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. A seat whose metrics are demonstrably saturated and whose readings are pooled still produces ground-validated refusals of the system's actions at the system's own speed and scale, with the live case named.
> 2. A supervisor whose readings are generated entirely by the system's own output still passes an independence test, that is, its agreement with the system still carries evidence about the ground.
> 3. An arrangement with no way to state a goal as a comparison and no way to negate a claim still produces refusable refusals that the ground validates.
> 4. An operator-only improvement that changes no instrument, no authority and no contact still moves the ground-validated refusal rate.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step formula and its mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven, "Meaningful Human Control over Autonomous Systems: A Philosophical Account", Frontiers in Robotics and AI 5:15, 2018: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00015/full
> - Bainbridge, "Ironies of Automation", Automatica 19(6), 1983, read via the notes at: https://ferd.ca/notes/paper-ironies-of-automation.html
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local file above. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two practical notes

1. The first two links are local framework files. If the recipient has no access to this folder, offer the public figures repository instead and keep the figure text as the citation form. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
2. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
