## Three findings that bear directly on the question

1. **The transmission literature names the same asymmetry as Fig 4.** The tacit-knowledge essay writes that what passes down is ways of doing things and head starts, never the experience itself, and quotes Fig 4 :22 ("Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."). That is the central mechanism of this solution. Cite it when explaining why digitising an artifact is not preservation.
2. **The crafts themselves are described as ground-driven, not theory-driven.** Vernacular architecture is *"learned by trial and error over generations of building construction, often existing long before the scientific theories which explain why they work"*; TEK is *"based on long-term observation, cultural methods, and experiential learning"*. Both are exactly Fig 11's maintained habitat and Fig 2's anchor, arrived at independently of the framework. Overlap: these are separate hosts and fields from the framework, so they are genuine low-overlap corroboration.
3. **Fidelity of transmission is a measured quantity in cultural learning**, where mechanisms *"differ in what is learned from others and in the fidelity with which behavioural forms are transmitted"*. That is a natural instrument for Fig 7's selection-between-rooms claim, and it is the finding most likely to refute this solution if fidelity alone turns out to be sufficient.
