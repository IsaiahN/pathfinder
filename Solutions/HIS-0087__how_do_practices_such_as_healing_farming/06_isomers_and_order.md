## DERIVATION

### Q1 — The functional group

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Write each practice at first order:

```
healing      practitioner – method – patient(outcome)
farming      practitioner – method – field(outcome)
navigation   navigator   – method – landfall(outcome)
building     builder     – method – wall(outcome)
```

The shared substructure is `actor – method – ground(outcome)`. That is the functional group: it fixes the kind (a practice that persists by re-derivation against a ground). The qualifier fixes the member (patient, field, landfall, wall). Under the bare atom sets the four share only the structure `actor-method-outcome` and differ in every medium, so **they are not isomers as written**. They are four members of one functional group.

### Q2 — The stronger isomer claim

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Now write all four over the same full atom set `{actor, medium, instrument, ground, outcome, correction, habitat}` and vary only the bonds:

- healing: `actor + instrument → medium(correction ⋛ outcome)` with the habitat a clinic and a pharmacology.
- farming: `actor + instrument → medium(correction ⇒ outcome)` with the habitat a field and a season.
- navigation: `actor + instrument → medium(correction ⋛ outcome)` with the habitat a sea and a star field.
- building: `actor + medium ⇒ outcome − failure` with the habitat a site and a material supply.

Same atoms, different bonds, different substances. That **is** the Fig 12 isomer claim, and it is checkable: does the difference in bond (sequence ⇒, comparison ⋛, subtraction −) actually predict the difference in failure mode? For navigation the decisive bond is comparison (is the swell change larger than noise?); for building it is subtraction (the wall with the failed joint removed); for healing it is comparison again (does the patient cross a threshold?). A healer and a navigator may then be isomers over the same atom set with different bonds, while a builder differs by bond type.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two practices written over the shared atom set require the same bond for the same decision at the same ground, the isomer claim collapses and the functional-group reading stands. Likewise, if any practice requires an atom the others do not (for example a navigation atom with no analogue in healing), the shared-set claim fails.

### Q3 — Order and the bond that cannot be read off the operands

Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** (sequence, ordered). Fig 12 :46: **"both hold at once"** (conjunction, symmetric). Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*.

The order that matters here is not a list of steps. It is the dependency order of `01` §2.4: habitat → settled method → membrane transfer → selection between variants. Effort spent at selection before the method is settled arrives at a step whose input is missing (Fig 3 :20-23).

The bond that cannot be read off the operands is the one the tacit-knowledge essay calls the "twisting stretch": the visible ingredients are stretching and practice, the decisive bond is a twist that only appears under correction. Fig 12 :46-47: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** This is why an ingredients list is not the practice.

### Q4 — Cycle, catalyst and the lineage

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A method is a catalyst-like part: it survives its own use and is available for the next case. The loop closes because the correction re-enters as the next perception. Fig 13 :142: **"The seed was always the seedline."** Four structures are present, and each names something the flat form cannot write:

### Q5 — Round-trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`: what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed.

1. **Sent up (x):** the four practices with their habitats, corrections and lineages (the world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv`).
2. **Abstraction (`T_A`):** the shorthand in `01`: generator crosses, head start descends, ground settles, habitat is maintained, variants selected.
3. **Concretisation (`T_E`):** a preservation programme built from that shorthand: fund an apprentice, a master, a ground check and a habitat.

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three things in the shorthand do not survive the return:

- the **pretext** each practitioner brings, which the written effect-description cannot carry (tacit-knowledge essay);
- the **specific bond** that decides each case (comparison versus production versus subtraction), which the word "method" flattens into one name;
- the **particular habitat actors** the practice depends on, which a generic "habitat" label drops.

One thing does survive cleanly: the requirement that a generator crosses and a recording does not. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints; it was not measured numerically because no ground reading exists. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## CONCLUSION

As written, the four practices are a **functional group**, not isomers: they share `actor-method-ground` and differ in medium. Under the stronger reading, written over a shared atom set with different bonds, they become a real **isomer claim with a stated falsifier**. The order of the whole is dependency order, not a step list; the decisive bond is not recoverable from the operand list, which is why apprenticeship and contact are structural necessities rather than nice additions. The round trip loses pretext, bond type and habitat detail; the residue that survives is the directed membrane itself.
