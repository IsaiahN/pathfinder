## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32 says **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can fill it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- **Shape:** a method crosses the generation boundary, a head start comes down, each slot is ranged against a ground that does not update, the habitat is maintained, and variants are selected.
- **Name:** the specific medicinal plant, the specific star sequence, the specific twist of the wrist, the specific soil treatment.

The shape is checkable without knowing any name. That is the whole point of stopping at it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

## 3. What licenses a name

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For a traditional practice, a name is licensed when the outcome pays in a specific place at a specific time, with the record of which slot it paid on.

## 4. Why this question's answer is a shape and not a recipe

If the framework named a recipe that "preserves knowledge across generations", it would be committing the flat-form error of Fig 12 :6-17: an ingredient list with the arrangement omitted. *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself"* (Fig 12 :47). *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the reason in transmission terms:

> *"Writing carries the context well and the subtext partly, but it almost never carries the pretext, the 'you had to be there' part. The understanding has to be rebuilt by the learner, and the way it gets rebuilt is contact: trying, being corrected, and trying again, in both directions."*

And the sentence that most directly answers this question:

> *"An apprentice isn't receiving messages; they're slowly building the history that makes the messages make sense."*

Polanyi's formulation, quoted in that essay: *"we can know more than we can tell."* A shape is what can be told. The rest is built by contact, which is why the answer cannot be a recipe even in principle.

> *"Teaching makes learning faster and easier, but the learner still has to do part of the work. The same limit holds between generations: what passes down is ways of doing things and head starts, never the experience itself. 'Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down' (Fig 4 :22)."*

## 6. Drift: why a record alone cannot hold the shape

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* reports Bartlett's retellings: a story passed person to person lost what did not fit and became *"more familiar forms"*, until each report mirrored the listener's own culture. Nobody lied. The errors compounded in one direction. That is Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*, observed in a laboratory rather than asserted from a figure.

The remedy the essay gives is the same as the figure's: *"Two people who also meet the world apart keep enough difference to catch each other. The apprentice needs time alone at the bench as well as time beside the master."* (Fig 10 :80, separate experience; Fig 2 :45, range against the ground.)

The exemplar records this rule from *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, which I did not re-open this session: prestige can fund an action and give it a budget to be tried, but it cannot settle an action; only the ground can.
