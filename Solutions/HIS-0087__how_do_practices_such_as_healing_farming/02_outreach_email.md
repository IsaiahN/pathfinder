# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly on your own account. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how craft knowledge survives a generation boundary

> Hi [name],
>
> I am [your name]. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about [healing / farming / navigation / building / traditional knowledge]. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a practice does not preserve its knowledge by copying a record faithfully. It preserves knowledge by carrying a reapplicable method and a head start across the generation boundary, while each new practitioner re-runs the loop against an outcome that does not move because anyone agrees: the patient recovers or does not, the harvest comes or does not, the landfall is made or not, the wall stands or falls. The chain persists by selection between variants, not by fidelity of copying. The knowledge is also the habitat it depends on, and the lineage of attempts and corrections that produced it.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a name for any particular plant, star, gesture or joint detail. I am not claiming the framework can tell you which one.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that any specific community fits this shape (that would need a ground reading, which I have not done); that writing is useless (a written effect-description can be a head start); that agreement among practitioners is evidence (it is not, if they share an evidence pool); or that this is settled science. It is a derivation from a set of figures, checked for internal consistency, by an AI. It is a simulated derivation, not a measurement, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A practice that kept reliable outcomes for generations by verbatim copying alone, with no ground re-check and no habitat maintenance.
> 2. A written record that, with no practice and no correction, reliably yields a correct *novel* outcome in a new practitioner.
> 3. A place-bound practice transplanted into a different habitat that keeps full reliability without reproducing the original actors and conditions.
> 4. A derivation from the figure set that names a specific recipe with its source gate and shadow test. If that is possible, the shape limit is wrong.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this workspace
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a result with no pre-registered refuter is not checkable. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]
