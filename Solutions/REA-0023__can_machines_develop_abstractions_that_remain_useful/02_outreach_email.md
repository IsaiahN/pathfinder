**Subject:** Check one output about cross-modal transfer before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about cross-modal or transfer learning. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the application, not `grounded`):** an abstraction that stays useful across a change of sensory input, language or task is not a stored context-free object. What persists is a reapplicable generator with its binding structure and its provenance, carried into the new frame and re-settled against a ground that does not update on the model's own report. The recording of one success does not persist: the new level remakes whatever lands in it. The residual has to be read per slot, because pooling across modalities is how a live failure in one channel disappears into an average.
>
> In the framework's words, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* and *"a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."*
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a frame that adds a genuinely new atom by internal composition alone, with no contact with a differing closure, and pays for it at the ground;
> 2. a recording of one occasion that crosses into a new modality unchanged and yields a novel correct result there without re-derivation;
> 3. an agent-only improvement that changes contact with the new habitat's ground and pays there while habitat and channel are held fixed;
> 4. a pooled cross-modal residual near zero while a per-modality slot stays live.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a particular embedding, architecture or latent space is *the* transferable abstraction. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that convergence between independently trained models is corroboration: if they share a corpus or a benchmark, that is one evidence pool talking, and the record is what separates convergence from an import. A synthetic cross-modal benchmark tests wiring, not capability in a habitat.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Canonical figure text used for this check: local file `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder framework
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state its mode. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
