## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether a network can score well on several benchmarks. It is whether a term φ in a library Γ, once settled against a ground in one frame (a sensorium, a language, a task), keeps predicting when the observation channel o′ changes. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per slot, never averaged across modalities (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "an abstraction is a stored context-free object": leads to the prediction that the same representation pays in every frame without re-derivation.
- Option B, "an abstraction is a reapplicable generator": leads to the prediction that what crosses is a method and a head start, that the new frame remakes what lands in it, and that the term must be re-settled against a non-updating ground in each habitat (Fig 4 :22, Fig 10 :68, Fig 11 :39).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* stating what must be invariant for R to stay small across a change of frame, plus an instrument that would discriminate a transferred generator from a replayed recording. Until it is bound, that shape predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged, with the concrete representation declared a `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the machine-abstraction question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of abstraction.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* and Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names. The machine-learning sources in this folder largely share one evidence pool and are counted as one witness.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
