## 0. The claim under audit

> Can machines develop abstractions that remain useful across different sensory inputs, languages, and tasks?

Stated in effect terms: a term φ is settled against a ground in frame A (a sensorium, a language, a task). When the observation channel changes to frame B, does φ keep paying? The residual is R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|, indexed per slot. The phrase "remain useful" is the sought-for shape, so it is bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): *a term that still reduces a per-slot residual after the channel changes, without being a replay of one occasion.* Until that binding exists, the question's direction is a preference no internal check can see (Fig 5 :62-63).

Two readings are in play and are kept separate:

- **A, the stored-object reading.** An abstraction is a context-free representation stored once and reused unchanged. This predicts invariance of content across frames.
- **B, the generator reading.** An abstraction is a reapplicable method with its typed bonds and its provenance, carried across the membrane and re-settled in each new habitat. This predicts invariance of the kernel, not of the recording (Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 10 :68).

## 1. Residual and domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22, :27-28)

- **Arity:** a relation among a term φ, a bet/action (b,a), an observation o′ from a named channel, and a transform between two frames. At least three operands plus a directed transform. It carries an ordered bond (transfer has a direction, from frame A to frame B) and a comparison bond (whether R stays small, Fig 12 :87). A two-place reading (representation, benchmark score) loses the channel and the transform, so it is rejected.
- **Symmetry:** the modalities are not interchangeable. Vision, touch, text and speech have different actors, currencies and grounds. Treating them as interchangeable is the common-cause collapse (Fig 2 :18-21), where agreement is the pool talking rather than two frames confirming each other. "Useful across" is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric conjunction.
- **Scale:** the slot (one input event), the loop (one frame's Γ), the membrane (transfer between frames with differing closures), and the lineage (a method carried across tasks). The residual is intrinsically cross-scale.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Single-frame loop | the model, its Γ, the training channel in one modality | residual reduction per slot | the ground readings in that modality (labels, reward, outcome) | admitted |
| D2 The membrane between frames | two frames with differing closures, the transform between them | what crosses: method and head start versus recording | a shared non-updating task ground both frames range against | admitted |
| D3 Habitat and task ground | tasks, environments, users, data distributions | contact: does the term still pay | the ground that does not decay | admitted |
| D4 Record and provenance | atoms, bonds, origin stamps and sequence numbers | reach and |φ| | settlement by the ground | admitted |
| X1 A modality-free context-free abstraction held as a timeless object | none nameable | none | none: it cannot range against any ground independent of a habitat | not a domain yet |
| X2 One universal language or task as the sole ground for every modality | one frame pretending to be an anchor | structural rents of that frame | already inside D2 and D3; if it updates on the frames' own reports it is the mutual-update collapse | absorbed |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. X1 is refused on that rule, not on preference.

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D4 (the library within one frame) and D2 and D3 (the membrane and the habitat) form a compound. A term that transfers must both be a reapplicable method with an intact bond structure and keep contact with a non-updating ground in the new habitat. Their overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-58). One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

perception (a reading exists per modality) → vocabulary (terms for what the input affords) → objective (what counts as progress, stated in effect terms shared across modalities) → planning (a route under incomplete information) → learn and carry (name what worked, verify it, carry it as a method).

The chain usually breaks between the second and third links. Fig 3 :25-27: *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Most cross-modal work builds a shared embedding (vocabulary) and a benchmark (perception) and never states what would count as progress in the new frame.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - D1: more training inside one modality cannot add an atom (decisive for the closure question)

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

A single-modality frame can enumerate arrangements of what it already holds without end (Fig 13 :116-118: *"So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."*). A new sensorium, language or task is the contact with a differing closure that can move the wall. This does not say scale alone is sufficient: contact must be with a closure that actually differs at the evidence level (Fig 2 :31-32).

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 8 definitional + Fig 6 wall rule, low overlap between the idempotence argument and the import operator).
- Mode: `general`.
- Refuter: a frame that adds a genuinely new atom by internal composition alone, with no contact with a differing closure, and pays for it at the ground.

### F2 - D2: what crosses is a generator, not an instance, and the level remakes it (decisive for the transfer question)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**

Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

Fig 10 :68: **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."**

Fig 4 :34-35: **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

So the answer to "remain useful" is not invariance of content. A seed is useful in a new soil because it is a generator, not because it contains a small tree. The abstraction that survives a change of modality is the reapplicable loop plus its naming rule, with a head start sent down; the replayed success is a description of one occasion and cannot produce a new one. This is why a fixed stored object (X1) has no anchor: it cannot range against a ground that the new habitat actually pays.

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 4 membrane rule + Fig 10 level rule, different figures with different quantities).
- Mode: `general` for the crossing rule, `specified` for the machine-abstraction application.
- Refuter: a recording of one occasion, carried unchanged into a new modality, that yields a novel correct result there without any re-derivation or re-settling against that modality's ground.

### F3 - D2: two instruments, inward and outward, never read against each other

Fig 6 :29: **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**

Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

Fig 6 :31: **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**

Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

Applied here: **SEARCHED** first (is the shared term already in the closure and unfound, more search and independent searchers), then **INWARD** (is our representation adequate to hold the cross-modal residual, sharpen the instrument) and **OUTWARD** (does another frame, for example animal cross-modal cognition or human crossmodal correspondence, already hold a frame that predicts the shape). A benchmark result cannot answer both. Read one against the other and you mistake a representation limit for an absence, or a budget limit for an underivability (Fig 8 :40-43).

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 6 two instruments + Fig 9 effect-description rule).
- Mode: `general`.
- Refuter: a single instrument that returns both whether the representation can hold the residual and whether another frame already holds the term, with the two answers distinguishable.

### F4 - D2: an import needs a source gate and a shadow test, and convergence is not adopted import

Fig 6 :12: **"source gate: provenance"**

Fig 6 :13: **"shadow test: explains a gap"**

Fig 6 :24: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**

Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

The machine-learning literature's convergence results (Huh et al. 2024) are exactly the case this line separates: models trained on different modalities arrive at similar representations. If they arrived independently, that is convergent derivation at high independence (Fig 8 :50). If they share a corpus or a benchmark, it is the common-cause collapse (Fig 2 :18-21) and their agreement is one evidence pool talking. The contents cannot decide it; the record must.

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 6 gates + Fig 8 provenance distinction).
- Mode: `specified` on the machine-learning case, `general` on the gate rule.
- Refuter: an import whose provenance is indistinguishable from convergent derivation and whose surplus persists even though the two frames draw on the same evidence.

### F5 - D3: capability is agent-and-habitat, and a harness proves wiring not capability

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

A model that scores well when image and text are drawn from the same curated distribution has been tested in a substituted habitat. What you failed to reproduce (the habitat's ground) is invisible until the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced (the harness's shortcuts) is invisible until it acts (Fig 11 :68-69). Cross-modal generalization claimed from such a harness is a claim about wiring.

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 11 habitat rule + Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*).
- Mode: `specified`.
- Refuter: an agent-only change that alters contact with the new modality's ground and pays there, with the habitat and channel held fixed and measured.

### F6 - D3: the residual stays per slot, and valence flips with it

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 11 :49-51: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

A pooled score across modalities is how a live failure in one channel disappears. A shared representation that helps a visual task can harm a tactile one when the residual changes; it is not a good actor carrying a fixed valence. Dynamic sensory reweighting in electric fish (Schumacher et al. 2016) is a low-overlap world analogue of the same structure.

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 1 indexing + Fig 11 valence relativity).
- Mode: `specified`.
- Refuter: a benchmark in which the pooled residual is near zero while a per-modality slot carries an obviously live residual that the shared term cannot reduce.

### F7 - D4: the bond carries what the atom list loses

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Fig 13 :129: **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."**

Transfer is not the movement of a feature list. The same atoms under a different bond are a different substance (Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance."*). A system that copies the shared vocabulary but not the typed relations has copied a state and not a route, which is why a fine-tuned head can look like transfer while the generator did not cross.

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 12 bond pricing + Fig 13 lineage identity).
- Mode: `specified`.
- Refuter: a transfer in which the atom list is preserved, the bond structure is lost, and the new frame still obtains the same result under a changed residual.

### F8 - D4: settling is necessary and not sufficient, and the reach can fall

Fig 5 :69-70: **"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one."**

Fig 6 :42-43: **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."**

A representation can be settled in every source modality and still be unusable for composition in the target modality. When a shared term is refuted, nothing is deleted; the record keeps it and its standing to be composed over is removed, so the reach falls by addition. This is why a growing library is not a growing reach (Fig 5 :72: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."*).

- Status: **triangulated** (Fig 5 sufficiency amendment + Fig 6 reach amendment).
- Mode: `general`.
- Refuter: a settled term that cannot be composed over, with no corresponding fall in the derivable reach.

### F9 - the gap: no figure line names the concrete representation

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 13 :135: **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

After re-census, no figure line selects a particular shared embedding, architecture, latent space, or language of thought as the thing that must persist. The figures fix the shape: a generator crosses, a head start descends, an import has provenance and a shadow test, the residual is per slot, and the round trip has a measured gap. The name is owed to an instrument and a ground, not to a derivation. Record as `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not as an invitation to invent a number.

- Status: **gap**, explicitly triangulated as a limit (Fig 9 + Fig 13 + Fig 8).
- Mode: `general`.
- Refuter: a figure line that names a specific representation as the transferable primitive.

### F10 - strain: the frame transform F carries a pose requirement, but only as a shape

The Symbols table (line 81) defines **F**: *"The map between allocentric and egocentric at one scale. Distinct from T_A and T_E, which run between scales. Cannot be run without a pose."*

Cross-modal translation is plausibly a transform between frames at one scale, which would make "cannot be run without a pose" relevant: an alignment between the frames is required. But F is defined for the allocentric/egocentric case, and no figure extends it to sensory modalities. Applying it directly to vision-versus-touch would be a strain.

**Re-census:** F is removed as a verdict witness. What survives is the shape it points at: a translation between frames needs an alignment that both frames can range against, which is already governed by Fig 2 :29 (the anchor) and Fig 6 :30 (cast it in effect terms). Recorded as a **live strain, single-source, not used in the verdict.** A world analogue is Spence's crossmodal correspondence review, where the binding problem is solved by correspondences that act as shared constraints rather than by a literal spatial pose.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device" (Seat Map :287).

The generator reading (Option B) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A frame adds a genuinely new atom by internal composition alone, with no contact with a differing closure, and pays for it at the ground (breaks F1).
2. A recording of one occasion crosses into a new modality unchanged and yields a novel correct result there without re-derivation or re-settling (breaks F2).
3. An agent-only change alters contact with the new habitat's ground and pays there while habitat and channel are held fixed (breaks F5).
4. A pooled cross-modal residual is near zero while a per-modality slot stays live (breaks F6).
5. The atom list is preserved, the bond structure is lost, and the target frame still obtains the same result (breaks F7).
6. A figure line names the concrete transferable representation (breaks F9 and turns the gap into a derivation).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F2 and F5: the question "remain useful" is answered by a generator crossing and a habitat paying, not by a stored object being invariance-tested.
2. State the mode on every claim (F1 to F9). No benchmark score is a settlement.
3. Put the per-slot residual before the pooled leaderboard (F6); a room is not an anchor (Fig 7 :35).
4. Separate convergent derivation from adopted import with a dated record (F4). Report where each representation came from.
5. Run inward and outward as distinct tracks (F3), and ask SEARCHED first (Fig 6 :49-50: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change."*).
6. Treat every synthetic cross-modal benchmark as a harness, hence a wiring test (F5), and owe a real habitat.
7. Stop at the gap. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is an abstraction a stored object?** No at the level that matters: the kernel is invariant and the recording is not (Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 10 :68).
2. **What crosses?** A method upward and a head start downward, with its bonds; not a replay (Fig 4 :22-25).
3. **Can one modality derive the other's atom?** No by closure idempotence (Fig 8 :32), yes through contact with a differing closure (Fig 6 :23).
4. **How do you find the frame that holds it?** Characterise R in effect terms first, then look up the frame (Fig 8 :53, Fig 9 :27-28).
5. **Is convergence evidence?** Only with provenance; contents alone cannot separate convergence from import (Fig 8 :50-52, Fig 2 :18-21).
6. **What settles it?** The ground in the new habitat, per slot (Fig 11 :39, Fig 1 :19), not a pooled benchmark (Fig 7 :35).
7. **What is owed?** A discriminator between generator and replay, a provenance record, and a measured round trip R_T. No figure names the representation (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: **what remains useful across sensory inputs, languages and tasks is a reapplicable generator carried across a membrane and re-settled against a non-updating ground in each new habitat, with its typed bonds and provenance intact; the stored instance does not remain useful, because the level it lands on remakes it.** Everything specific about which representation does this is a `gap`. Strains after re-census: one live (F10, the F transform), named and excluded from the verdict. No world source flipped the verdict; the one genuine conflict (Ciernik et al. 2025 versus a naive convergence reading) was returned to the census and resolved by F4.
