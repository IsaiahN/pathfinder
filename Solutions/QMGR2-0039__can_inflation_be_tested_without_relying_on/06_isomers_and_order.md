### Q3 : Which frame moves the wall?

- Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
- Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"

The inflationary model space is a closure: with a set of potentials and a set of initial-condition prescriptions, the space of predictions is generated by composition. Adding potentials enumerates arrangements. The only low-overlap frame available without new instrumentation is mathematical consistency: the Borde, Guth and Vilenkin theorem is a kinematic result requiring no energy condition, and it says an inflating spacetime is past-incomplete and needs physics other than inflation at the boundary (`10_source_ledger.csv`). Its provenance is a proof and its shadow test is that it explains a gap: why refining the potential cannot remove the boundary question. It therefore qualifies as an import under Fig 6 :23.

The instrument frame is the other low-overlap frame, but it is already in contact with the model frame through the likelihood; adding Planck data is not an import of a new atom, it is a sharper reading on the same axis. B modes and non-Gaussianity would add new axes, but they are not yet settled readings. Fig 6 :34 "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." There is a proto-instrument here: gravitational-wave B modes are already returning something that fails to resolve, so there is an edge to extend from. That is different from inventing a sensor for something that has never registered.

- **Falsifier:** produce a past-eternal model satisfying the BGV conditions, or show the theorem is silent on the boundary question. Then this import does not move the wall.

### Q4 : Are inflationary model classes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

Two models are **isomers** only if they share the same atom set and differ in the bond arrangement in a way that changes behaviour. Models that differ only by a reparametrisation of the potential, giving identical n_s and r at the same precision, are not isomers: they are the same spelling of one term under a symmetric relabelling, and charging for each as a separate candidate overcharges the space. Fig 12 :92-93 "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed." (This is a compound of two lines; the operative claim is the figure's own symmetry rule.)

Models that share the observables but differ in boundary condition or reheating history are isomers in the figure's sense: same atoms at the level of the spectrum, different arrangement, different behaviour at the boundary. That is the only isomer claim worth testing here, and it is testable only with an observable that reaches the boundary, which is exactly what is missing.

The two currencies matter: a model can be cheap in bits (one parameter) and expensive in actions (requires a cosmic-variance-limited B-mode survey), or expensive in bits and cheap to refute. Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Summing them into one testability number is the named error.

- **Falsifier:** show that two models with identical n_s and r but different reheating histories are observationally identical at every accessible axis. Then they are not isomers and the boundary distinction collapses.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union** of four overlapping conditions, to be unbundled before search. Its dependency chain stops at the objective link, where "assumption cost" must be bound as an ordinary term. The only import that moves the model frame's wall without new data is the mathematical consistency theorem; the instrument frame is a sharper reading on an existing axis, not a new atom. Model classes that differ only by reparametrisation are spellings, not isomers; those that differ in boundary structure are isomers but are not currently distinguishable. The result is a shape and a route, not a verdict on which model is true.
