# The Tether's answer: testing inflation without hard-to-verify assumptions

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A sky reading becomes a claim about the early universe only by passing through a potential, initial conditions, a reheating history, a prior and a measure. The instrument returns none of those. The residual is the gap between the ground reading and the claim, carried by auxiliaries that are not themselves grounded. Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does".

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

**Domain 1, fit of individual models to data: YES, with declared priors.** A specified model with a stated potential, a stated prior and a stated measure yields predictions that data can disfavour. Planck disfavours V(phi) proportional to phi squared and natural inflation relative to plateau models, and the joint BICEP/Keck and Planck analysis found no tensor signal while constraining r. That is a real anchor contact: Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 2 anchor rule plus Fig 10 channel rule, low overlap), `specified`, ungrounded.

**Domain 2, the paradigm as an assumption-free class: NO.** The model-to-claim map requires auxiliaries that no instrument settles, and where a measure or prior decides which model is favoured, a convention has become a verdict. Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Adding more models cannot help: Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 10 convention rule plus Fig 8 closure rule, low overlap), `specified`, ungrounded.

**Domain 3, the union as a whole: UNBUNDLED, not a single yes or no.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The four conditions overlap, so a single lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56 "intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."). Verdict: `single-source` for the structural rule (Fig 9), `general`.

**Domain 4, the boundary: a PARTIAL, ASSUMPTION-LIGHT TEST EXISTS.** The Borde, Guth and Vilenkin theorem is kinematic and needs no energy condition; it says an inflating spacetime is past-incomplete and needs physics other than inflation at the boundary. That constrains the paradigm without choosing a potential, an initial condition or a measure. It is an import from a closure that differs: Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Verdict: `single-source` for the figure governance (Fig 6), `specified`; the physical conclusion depends on a world source and is therefore not figure-grounded.

**Domain 5, which model (if any) is true: GAP for the figures.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". The figures return the shape an assumption-light test must have. They do not name a model. Verdict: `gap`. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

## 3. So, directly answering the question

**No, not for inflation as a whole. Yes, for specified models with declared priors and for structural constraints that do not need initial conditions.**

More precisely: you cannot test "inflation" without assumptions that are difficult to verify, because there is no instrument that reads the measure, the prior or the boundary condition, and the model class can absorb contrary data by adjusting them. You can test a *particular* inflationary model without *additional* unverified assumptions, provided the ones it uses are written down and the conclusion is reported conditional on them. And you can constrain the paradigm in an assumption-light way with a theorem whose premises are kinematic. Those three statements are not one averaged verdict; they are the four domain verdicts above and they are deliberately kept apart.

The shape of an assumption-light test, bound as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65 "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."), is:

1. ranges against an anchor that does not update;
2. requires no auxiliary unsettled by a ground;
3. comes from a closure that differs from the model frame;
4. carries a refuter written before the run, so the preference is refusable by the same test as everything else (Fig 5 :66 "Then the preference is refusable by the same test as everything else.").

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that inflation is false, and not that it is true.
- Not that inflation is unfalsifiable in one word. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that a non-detection of tensor modes proves anything about the paradigm; it is a bound on r.
- Not that any particular prior or measure is the correct one. Where a convention decides the outcome, the finding is a failure, not a recommendation.
- Not that the six-term set is nature's own list. Declaring the primitives makes a choice visible, not neutral.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation. Fig 8 :34 "A frame cannot certify its own limit."

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument.

1. **A tensor detection or a tighter bound with a declared foreground model** would settle which potentials remain, not whether inflation as a class is true.
2. **A non-Gaussianity measurement at the level predicted by single-field slow roll** would be an internal consistency test of the model (Maldacena's three-point computation), not an independent ground reading of the paradigm.
3. **A measure that is itself measured**, or a demonstration that the posterior is invariant across admissible measures, would clear the convention finding.
4. **A past-eternal inflationary model satisfying the BGV conditions** would remove the import.
5. **An instrument returning an assumption-cost observable** would rebuild the broken dependency link (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

Standing refuters from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 apply. If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to inflation as a whole, because that question is a bundle of four overlapping conditions and one lever cannot clear them. Specific models can be tested against the CMB once their priors and measures are declared, and Planck and BICEP/Keck have already ruled out some of them. The paradigm as an assumption-free class cannot be tested, because the auxiliaries that carry a sky reading to an early-universe claim are not returned by any instrument, and where a measure decides the result a convention has stopped being a convention. A partial assumption-light test does exist in the kinematic theorem that inflating spacetimes are past-incomplete, which needs no initial condition. The framework returns that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it; it does not name a model. The physical truth stays open until the ground settles it.
