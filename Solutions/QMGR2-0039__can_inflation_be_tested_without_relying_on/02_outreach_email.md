# Outreach draft: check the separator before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading. Say so in the first paragraph. If the stage-1 shape claim does not survive an expert's check, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about inflation testing before you decide whether the framework matters

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific claim to check, and I am not asking you to read the theory first or to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. I should also say plainly: what I am sending is a simulated derivation checked against a set of figures, not a measurement, and it does not take a position on whether inflation happened.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "can inflation be tested without unverifiable assumptions" is not one question. It bundles at least four conditions: a model makes a definite prediction; an instrument reads that observable against something that does not move when an assumption is pushed; the foreground and measure terms the test leans on are settled or have their provenance recorded; and a verdict on the whole paradigm is not just an average of the model verdicts. The figure set I am using says the residual must be scored per model and per observable, never aggregated, and that a term the test leans on has to be settled against a ground or imported from a frame with a different closure.
>
> So the answer I get is a separator, not a yes or no. A specific model can be tested without unverifiable load-bearing assumptions, provided each load-bearing term is settled or provenanced. The inflationary paradigm as a whole cannot currently be tested in that sense: its flexibility excludes no outcome, and the probability measure its likelihoods need has no instrument today. Individual models are genuinely tested, and some are genuinely ruled out. Both halves of the published debate are right about different objects.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that inflation did or did not happen; that any particular model is correct; that inflation is unscientific; that the BICEP2 dust result refuted inflation; or that a large number of experts agreeing would settle any of this. I am not asking you to trust the framework.
>
> **What would refute the separator (written before this message):**
> 1. one observation that discriminates the paradigm as such, robustly under every flexibility move;
> 2. a paradigm-level likelihood anchored to a ground that does not update on the number of models;
> 3. a test with no unsettled load-bearing term, or a demonstration that varying all such terms among settled alternatives leaves the verdict unchanged;
> 4. an aggregate paradigm score carrying a live per-model residual that all per-model tests miss;
> 5. a test whose verdict moves when an assumption is pushed while the sky reading is fixed;
> 6. a proto-instrument for the measure returning a reading at some resolution that fails to resolve it.
>
> If any holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Figure text with line numbers, canonical extract (local framework file, path supplied on request)
> - Ijjas, Steinhardt and Loeb, "Cosmic Inflation Theory Faces Challenges", Scientific American 2017: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/cosmic-inflation-theory-faces-challenges/
> - Dawid and McCoy, "Testability and viability: is inflationary cosmology Scientific?", European Journal for Philosophy of Science 2023: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13194-023-00556-3
> - BICEP2/Keck and Planck joint analysis, PRL 2015: https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00612
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and the formula are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for reading this far.

## Stage 2, only if the reader asks

Send the three source links and the canonical figure extract. Do not send the whole framework architecture before their stage-1 verdict is recorded, because a recipient who has absorbed the interior has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42). Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). If they are curious about the method itself, stage 3 offers the formula with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. The European Journal for Philosophy of Science requires LLM use to be documented and does not accept LLMs as authors, so any write-up must carry that disclosure.
