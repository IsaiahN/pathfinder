## 0. The residual, stated as an effect

**R:** A CMB or B-mode instrument returns numbers. An inflationary theorist turns those numbers into a claim about the early universe. The turn passes through a model: a potential V(phi), a choice of initial conditions, a reheating history, a prior over models, and in eternal models a measure. None of those is returned by the instrument. The residual is the gap between the ground reading and the claim, where the gap is carried by auxiliaries that are themselves not grounded.

- **Arity:** at least four distinct conditions, not one (see §2, F5).
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. The model frame needs the instrument; the instrument does not need the model frame to return a number. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Scale:** first order for a single model against data; second order for the paradigm as a class, where the comparison is between a closure of models and an instrument.
- **What an answer would look like:** bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65 "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."). An assumption-light test is one whose prediction (i) ranges against an anchor that does not update, (ii) requires no auxiliary that has not itself been settled by a ground, (iii) comes from a frame whose closure differs from the model frame, and (iv) carries a refuter written before the run. Until that term is bound, the question is a preference, and Fig 5 :61-62 "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term." applies.

## 3. Findings

### F1 : The question is a union compound, not one hard question (decisive for the route)

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

The question asks for a single yes or no about inflation as a whole. That is a search over a bundled region. Four conditions overlap: data fit, channel integrity, auxiliary settlement and frame independence. A tighter tensor bound (Planck 2018: r_0.002 < 0.056, see `10_source_ledger.csv`) clears only the first. It does not clear the third, because the bound is interpreted through a model with a prior. So the correct move is to unbundle and answer per condition, not to average the answers.

**Refuter:** show that the four conditions are independent (a partition), so one lever clears all of them; or show that no single condition binds in any region, so the bundle is really one question.

### F2 : The anchor is real, but it is reached only through a channel (decisive for the shape)

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :25 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
Fig 10 :27-28 "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"

The CMB sky is a proper anchor: Planck and BICEP/Keck readings do not change because a theorist prefers a potential. But the channel from the sky to the claim about inflation runs through foreground separation, a likelihood, a model family and a prior. BICEP2's original tensor claim is the standing example: the joint analysis found dust and no tensor signal. Fig 2 :37-38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" names the failure precisely. The instrument measures temperature and polarization spectra; it does not measure the measure.

**Refuter:** exhibit a model-independent observable that maps to a claim about the early universe with no auxiliary at all, and show it on the sky. Then the channel is not decayed and F2 fails. Barring that, what follows is the channel test, not the anchor test.

### F3 : The objective link has no instrument, so the question is held as a preference

Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 5 :61-62 "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."
Fig 5 :65 "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."
Fig 5 :66 "Then the preference is refusable by the same test as everything else."

Nobody has bound "assumption that is difficult to verify" as a term. There is no slot in the standard pipeline where it accrues a residual. So the search for a test without such assumptions cannot check its own direction: every check runs on the mechanism (the likelihood) and none on the direction (what would count as assumption-free). The remedy is the figure's own discipline: write down the shape as an ordinary term, with four conditions as in §0, and let it be refusable. This audit does that. On that binding, the answer is conditional: yes for conditions (i) and (iii), no for (ii) at the paradigm level, and `gap` for the physical truth of any specific model.

**Refuter:** show an existing, used instrument that measures assumption cost directly, so the objective link already works and no discipline is owed.

### F4 : More models do not supply the missing anchor

Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."
Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

The Encyclopaedia Inflationaris implements 118 single-field models, and the published edition extends beyond that. Adding models to a closure enumerates arrangements; it does not add an atom or an anchor. Within one closure, "most models fit" is variation, not a verdict. Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A model that accommodates any n_s and r by choosing a potential is not corroborated by accommodating them.

**Refuter:** exhibit two inflationary models whose predictions differ on an observable already measured, where both are held in the same closure and the data select one without importing a new auxiliary. Then the closure itself has discriminating power and F4 weakens (it does not fail: closure idempotence still holds, only the practical complaint about absorbing outcomes weakens).

### F5 : A convention that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention

Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 9 :51 "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel"
Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

In a Bayesian comparison of inflationary models, the prior over models and the measure over initial conditions are conventions. If the answer to "which model is favoured" changes when the measure is changed while the data are fixed, the convention is deciding the outcome. That is the named failure. It is not a defect of any particular prior; it is a defect of letting a frame-authored convention stand where the ground should be. The correct move is to rank cuts as reversible (Fig 9 :41 "so keep every cut ranked and reversible") and to report which conclusions survive a change of measure.

**Refuter:** show that for the conclusion at issue, posterior odds are invariant across all admissible measures and priors, or that the measure itself is measured by an instrument. Then the convention is not deciding the outcome.

### F6 : The comparison the question needs must be with a different closure

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"
Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."

Testing "inflation" against more inflationary models is a union of high-overlap frames: same atoms, different names. Fig 8 :50 "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." The low-overlap frame here is not another potential; it is the mathematical consistency frame. The Borde, Guth and Vilenkin theorem is a kinematic result requiring no energy condition: an inflating spacetime is incomplete in past directions and requires physics other than inflation at the boundary (`10_source_ledger.csv`). That is an import with provenance (a proof) and a shadow test (it explains why the initial-condition gap is structural rather than a matter of refining the potential). It moves the wall.

**Refuter:** exhibit a past-eternal inflationary model satisfying the theorem's conditions, or show that the theorem's conclusion is independent of the boundary question. Then this import does not move the wall and the frame-independence condition stays open.

### F7 : Underivable is not the same as unreachable at this budget

Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 8 :41 "Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."
Fig 6 :34 "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

No tensor mode has been detected; r is bounded, not measured. It would be a category error to conclude from that absence that inflation is untestable, or from a future detection that it is proved. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The two instruments must be kept apart: inward refinement of the existing bound (Fig 6 :29 "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms.") and outward search for a different frame (Fig 6 :30 "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."). Never read one against the other.

**Refuter:** show that a non-detection at the current budget entails underivability in principle for the quantity in question, so the two instruments do collapse into one.

### F8 : The status claim must not be a verdict

Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
Fig 9 :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"
Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

A tier, a bound or an evidence ratio is a filter for effort. None of them decides truth. The figures return a shape: an assumption-light test must have an ungullible anchor, a settled auxiliary set, a different closure and a pre-registered refuter. They do not return the name of the correct model, and Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," applies where the auxiliaries cannot be settled. So the honest answer to the original question is a conditional shape plus an explicit `gap` for the physical conclusion.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that directly decides the truth of a specific inflationary model. Re-census; if no such line exists after the census, the shape-only verdict stands.

### F9 : Two currencies, not one

Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A model can be cheap to state and expensive to test (a tiny region of parameter space requiring a huge number of observations), or expensive to state and cheap to refute (a long chain of parameters that one observable kills). The two prices are in different units. Summing them into one "testability score" is the error the figure names, not the fix. Any ranking of models by testability must keep the description cost and the action cost as separate columns.

**Refuter:** exhibit a conversion that maps bits and actions into one quantity without loss on both directions, and show it preserves both orderings.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An instrument returns a model-independent early-universe observable with no auxiliary about initial conditions, measure, potential or reheating (breaks F2).
2. The four conditions are shown to form a partition, so one lever clears all of them (breaks F1).
3. Posterior odds for the conclusion are invariant across admissible measures, or the measure is itself measured (breaks F5).
4. A past-eternal inflationary model satisfying the BGV conditions is produced, or the theorem is shown not to bear on the boundary question (breaks F6).
5. A figure line is found that directly decides the truth of a specific inflationary model (breaks F8).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run and a ground settles it, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Canonical usage check and strains

The canonical usage rule (`FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`) says the figures outrank the code, the docs and this audit, that the quotes are the authority, and that a reading which makes figures contradict each other has misidentified its domain. Applied here:

- **Apparent strain, Fig 8 :32 versus Fig 6 :23.** Fig 8 says search inside a closure adds no atom; Fig 6 says import moves the wall. Not strained. Fig 8 governs composition inside a fixed closure; Fig 6 governs contact with a different closure. Different objects. Re-census, not a softer reading.
- **Apparent strain, Fig 10 :24 versus Fig 2 :29.** The ground does not decay, but the channel does; the anchor is still legitimate. Not strained. The anchor is the sky; the channel is the likelihood-and-prior path. This is exactly why F2 is a channel finding and not an anchor finding.
- **Apparent strain, Fig 9 :32 (shape, not a name) versus the world sources naming models.** Not strained. The figures return a shape; the world sources name specific models and bounds, which are ground readings by other frames and are admitted only as secondary corroboration.
- **Live strain, Fig 12 two currencies.** How to price the action cost of a measure sweep in bits is not settled by the figures. Named, not hidden. See `11_audit.md` for the round-trip treatment.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Unbundle before answering. Report four verdicts, not one (F1).
2. Bind "assumption-light test" as an ordinary term with the four conditions in §0, so it can be refuted (F3).
3. Keep the anchor and the channel in separate columns. Report which conclusions survive a change of prior or measure (F2, F5).
4. Stop treating model proliferation as evidence. Seek frames whose closures differ (F4, F6).
5. Report non-detections as bounds, not absence proofs (F7).
6. Keep bits and actions as separate prices (F9).
7. Send the generator (the shape, the refuter and the enumeration of auxiliaries), not a recording of which model is fashionable. Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
