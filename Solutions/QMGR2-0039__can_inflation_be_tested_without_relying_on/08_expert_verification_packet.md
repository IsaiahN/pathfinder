# Expert verification packet: testing inflation without hard-to-verify assumptions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist, CMB experimentalist or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken by this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question whether inflation can be tested without hard-to-verify assumptions is a union of four overlapping conditions, not one question. Specific models can be tested once their priors and measures are declared, and real data have already disfavoured some. The paradigm as an assumption-free class cannot be tested this way: the auxiliaries that carry a sky reading to a claim about the early universe are not returned by any instrument, and where a measure or prior decides the outcome, a convention has stopped being a convention. A partial assumption-light constraint does exist in the kinematic result that inflating spacetimes are past-incomplete, which needs no initial condition. Which model (if any) is true is a `gap` for the figures and is for the ground to decide.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 10

- **Statement:** the question is a union, and a convention that decides an outcome stops being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Also Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".
- **What to check:** are data fit, channel integrity, auxiliary settlement and frame independence actually overlapping, so a tighter bound on r leaves the other three binding? Is there a conclusion in a model-comparison paper whose answer changes when the measure is changed with the data fixed?
- **Refuter:** show the four conditions form a partition (one lever clears all), or show posterior odds for the conclusion are invariant across admissible measures, or that the measure is itself measured.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable by a cosmologist against the observational record

- **Statement:** the sky is a genuine anchor but the model-to-claim channel can decay, so a non-detection is a bound and not an absence proof.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Also Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" and Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** Planck 2018 reports r_0.002 < 0.056 with BICEP2/Keck BK15 and n_s = 0.9649 +/- 0.0042 (`10_source_ledger.csv`). The joint BICEP2/Keck and Planck analysis found strong evidence for dust and no statistically significant tensor modes. Is that a channel correction, as claimed, or does it license a verdict on inflation as a class?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a model-independent early-universe observable returned by an instrument with no auxiliary about initial conditions, measure, potential or reheating. Then the channel is not decayed and C2 fails.

### C3 - cross-frame; checkable by a relativist or a philosopher of science

- **Statement:** the only available import that moves the model frame's wall without new data is a low-overlap consistency frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Also Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall".
- **What to check:** the Borde, Guth and Vilenkin result is kinematic, requires no energy condition, and concludes that inflationary models require physics other than inflation at the past boundary (`10_source_ledger.csv`). Does it bear on the boundary question in the way claimed, and are its premises weaker than the initial-condition assumption it is being used to replace? The Popper entry in the ledger is a low-overlap corroboration on the point that refutation criteria must be laid down beforehand.
- **Refuter:** produce a past-eternal inflationary model satisfying the theorem's conditions, or show the theorem is silent on the boundary question.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that inflation is false, and not that it is true.
- Not that inflation is unfalsifiable in one word. The claim is conditional and per-domain.
- Not that a non-detection of tensor modes proves anything about the paradigm.
- Not that any particular prior, measure or potential is the right one.
- Not that adding models supplies evidence. Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that the figures name a model. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

## 5. What would settle it

1. A model-independent observable with no auxiliary assumption (settles C2 in the opposite direction).
2. Measure-invariance of the posterior, or a measured measure (settles C1).
3. A past-eternal model satisfying the BGV conditions, or a proof that the theorem is irrelevant to the boundary (settles C3).
4. A discriminator reaching the boundary: B modes, non-Gaussianity, or a consistency-relation test with enough significance to separate projections.
5. An instrument returning assumption cost directly, rebuilding the broken dependency link (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-56, Fig 10 :24 and :47, Fig 6 :23, and Fig 8 :39. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against the Planck 2018 and BICEP/Keck abstracts: is the dust correction a channel event rather than a verdict on the paradigm?
3. Check C3 against the BGV abstract: are the premises kinematic, and does the conclusion bear on the boundary without an initial condition?
4. Name one observable that would distinguish a prior-driven model ranking from a ground-driven one, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
