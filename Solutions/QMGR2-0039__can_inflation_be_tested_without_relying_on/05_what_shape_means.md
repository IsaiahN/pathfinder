# What "shape" means for this question

**Sources.** The eight essays by Isaiah N are mapped in the imported exemplar (`Solutions/QMGR-0037.../05_what_shape_means.md`) and were not re-fetched in this session; their URLs are recorded there. The governing source read directly here is the canonical figures README at `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, together with the verbatim figure extract `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. The canonical README states that the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)", that "Indiscernibility is not identity", and that the quotes, not any summary, are the authority. This file follows that rule: wherever a figure bears, the verbatim line is quoted.

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :138-139 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."
Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

From *The Library of Babel*, a search can be cut short when the returned set is small and descriptive enough to provide a general shape of the answer. So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill.

For this question, the shape is not "inflation happened" or "inflation is unfalsifiable". It is a four-condition constraint on what a test would have to be:

1. the prediction ranges against an anchor that does not update (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks.");
2. every auxiliary in the model-to-data map is itself settled by something other than a frame-authored convention (Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one");
3. the comparison is made with a frame whose closure differs from the model frame (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs.");
4. the sought-for property is bound as an ordinary term with a pre-registered refuter (Fig 5 :65 "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM.").

That is a shape. "Use the Starobinsky model" or "adopt the local measure" would be names, and the figures do not return them.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- **Describe the effect, not the cause.** Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So instead of asking for an assumption-free test in the abstract, describe the effect: an observable whose value does not change when the prior is changed.
- **Residual first, frame second.** Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second." The residual here is the channel gap between a sky reading and an early-universe claim. Only after describing it do we choose the instrument frame (Planck, BICEP/Keck) and the mathematical frame (BGV). Choosing a favourite model first is the inversion that makes any model seem to fit.
- **Enumerate the habitat, never compose it.** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." The auxiliaries in contact with the residual are enumerated: potential, initial conditions, reheating, prior, measure, foreground model, beam and calibration. One does not compose a list of desirable assumptions and call it a test.
- **Keep the round trip.** Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Condensing the shape into a phrase like "inflation is testable" throws away three of the four conditions. That loss is the round-trip gap R_T, measured in `11_audit.md`.

From *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, when contact is not possible the best that can be sent is a plain description of what one would expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world. That is precisely why `02_outreach_email.md` sends the four conditions and their refuters, not the framework.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** A measurement or a proof returns a value, and the term is admitted only if it pays. Fig 9 :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides".
2. **A provenanced import from a closure that differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50 "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."). The BGV theorem qualifies structurally: provenance is a published proof, and the shadow test is that it explains why the initial-condition gap is not removable by refining a potential.

The Planck results name numbers: n_s = 0.9649 +/- 0.0042, r_0.002 < 0.056. Those are ground readings from a different frame, not names produced by the figure set. They are admitted as secondary corroboration and recorded with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 4. The metamerism warning applied to this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism: an instrument that reads green cannot comment on the source; yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK and unimagined schemes all give the same reading. If several paths create the same behavior, behavior is a projection with lossy information. What resolved it was a spectrometer, not an argument.

The same error here is reading a near-scale-invariant, adiabatic, Gaussian spectrum and naming its source as a particular inflationary potential. The spectrum is a projection. Multiple potentials, and non-inflationary alternatives, can project to it. Fig 2 :37-38 "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" names the limit: the instrument measures the spectrum, not the cause. What is owed is a discriminator (a B-mode detection, a non-Gaussianity measurement, a consistency-relation test) or an honest `gap`, not a name.

This is also why the BICEP2 episode matters. It was not a refutation of inflation; it was a discovery that the instrument had been reading dust on an axis it did not intend to measure. The correction restored contact with the ground. Fig 10 :27-28 "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch" describes the symptom, and Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" describes the cure.

## 5. Q2 for this question: what licenses a particular auxiliary?

**Answer: nothing in the framework.** A particular potential, prior or measure is licensed only by the ground settling a term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until then, the choice is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does the literature record commit the forbidden error?** Where a paper reports that one model is favoured, the claim is a ground reading from a likelihood, and it is legitimate only with its prior and measure declared. Where a claim is made about inflation "as such" from a fit to a model family, the source is named from a projection, which is the metamerism error. Both forms are distinguished in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F5 and F10.

## 6. Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote that the shape-not-a-name rule forbids naming any concrete model. That overstates it. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the library. Concrete models and bounds are ground readings from the instrument frame, and importing them with provenance is permitted. What remains forbidden is presenting a ground reading as if the framework had derived the name, and claiming that adding a 119th model moves the wall (Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."). Both the original claim and this correction are recorded so neither is silently lost.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** A list of famous names agreeing that inflation is testable is a convention, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give the shape a budget to be tried: ask for a refutation, let the ground settle it. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** LLM output is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on. Fig 8 :34 "A frame cannot certify its own limit." Any verdict in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` is therefore `candidate` until an instrument or a proof settles it.
