## Restatement in effect terms

The decision is between two ways of treating a harm produced by many hands. Option A spreads responsibility across all contributors, or assigns it to no one because each contributor had only a partial role. It leads to a live signal disappearing into an average (Fig 1 :22), an anchor that drifts because the parties negotiate it (Fig 2 :34), and a chain of handoffs where agreement compounds but evidence does not (Fig 7 :13). Option B indexes responsibility per contributor and per institution, ranges each against a ground that does not update, traces the decision lineage, and puts the assigning seat outside the slice it grades. It leads to a checkable, falsifiable attribution with an open record.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a **procedure** that (1) enumerates the hands in contact with the harm, (2) indexes each as a separate slot, (3) tests what each could have known and controlled given what it held, (4) reads the lineage rather than the roster, (5) fixes a ground the parties cannot move, and (6) assigns from a seat with no stake in a particular reading. That procedure is the sought-for shape, written down so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. **A room does not supply an anchor.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A list chosen for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that would make its agreement worthless (Fig 2 :40).
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). This entire simulated run draws on one evidence pool, so its internal agreement is not corroboration.
3. **Asking for trust before a check spends independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional submission routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
