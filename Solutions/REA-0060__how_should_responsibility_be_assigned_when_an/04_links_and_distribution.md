## Wider literature, opened and directly relevant

These are secondary corroboration only. They are read, not cited from memory. Full provenance and quotes are in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

1. **Matthias, "The responsibility gap: Ascribing responsibility for the actions of learning automata", Ethics and Information Technology 6, 175-183, 2004.** The abstract says a learning machine's manufacturer or operator is *"in principle not capable of predicting the future machine behaviour any more, and thus cannot be held morally responsible or liable for it."* This is the original statement of the gap. It bears directly on the decidability question in F7: if nothing in the hand's library could predict the route, the control condition fails.
2. **Santoni de Sio and Mecacci, "Four Responsibility Gaps with Artificial Intelligence", Philosophy and Technology 34, 1057-1084, 2021, open access.** *"The responsibility gap... is not one problem but a set of at least four interconnected problems."* It explicitly names *"the problem of many hands"* as an existing source of gaps in bureaucracies and corporations, and proposes *"meaningful human control"* as systems aligned with relevant human reasons and capacities. This is independent corroboration for F10 (union) and F7 (control).
3. **Gbouri and Gburi, "Who must answer? Answerability, moral remainder, and the limits of the responsibility-gap debate in AI-mediated decision-making", AI and Ethics 6, article 683, 2026.** The abstract distinguishes addressee-standing from reason-rendering and concludes *"An answerability gap can therefore persist where no attribution gap exists."* It corroborates F6: the lineage is the proof, and a named addressee without grounds does not close the question.
4. **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Moral Responsibility", first published 2019, revised 2024.** *"Moral responsibility should also be distinguished from causal responsibility... we cannot always infer moral responsibility from an assignment of causal responsibility."* Corroborates F7.
5. **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Collective Responsibility", first published 2005, revised 2022.** Methodological individualists *"challenge the very possibility of associating moral agency with groups"* while defenders argue groups can be moral agents with *"well-ordered decision-making procedures."* This is the live philosophical dispute behind the retraction in `01` section 5: the figures do not settle it, and they do not need to, because per-slot indexing works whether or not a group is also a slot.
6. **Diffusion of responsibility / Bystander effect, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23.** Darley and Latané 1968: intervention fell from 85% when alone to 31% with four other bystanders. *"The group size effect was inhibited when the victim specifically asked a specific person for help."* This is the social-psychological analogue of Fig 1 :22: address a specific slot and the signal survives.
7. **Responsibility assignment matrix (RACI), Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23.** *"there must be only one accountable stakeholder specified for each task or deliverable."* Corroborates per-slot indexing at the convention level. It is a filter, not a witness (F11).
8. **Swiss cheese model, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23.** Reason 1990. An accident occurs when holes in several defensive layers align; the model distinguishes active from latent failures and notes that *"medical error can be the result of 'system flaws, not character flaws'."* Corroborates F3: the habitat, not a single character flaw, is the object of enumeration.

**Overlap warning.** Sources 6, 7 and 8 are Wikipedia articles that cross-link one another; sources 1 to 5 are philosophy and AI-ethics literature that cite each other heavily (the 2026 article cites Matthias, Santoni de Sio and Mecacci, Bovens, and van de Poel). Agreement among them is partly a shared evidence pool, not many independent witnesses. This is recorded per row in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 9 :60:** *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."* Do not rank recipients by likely agreement. That is a filter deciding who to spend time on, not evidence about the claim.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A later endorsement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what would refute it. Record the answer before showing any theory (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the figure text, the formula, and the canonical README. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator. Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. The pre-registered refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is the test. If it fires, withdraw the claim before sending anything.
