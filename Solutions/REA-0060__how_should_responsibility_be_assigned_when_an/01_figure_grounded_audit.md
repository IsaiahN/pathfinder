## 0. The residual in effect terms

A harm occurred. Its causal history runs through many hands: designers of the model and data, operators who deployed and monitored it, and institutions that set incentives, rules, and budgets. The question asks how answerability should be assigned across them.

Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, diffuse or shared responsibility.** Treat the contributors as one pool and spread the verdict. It leads to the live signal disappearing into an average (Fig 1 :22), the anchor becoming another frame through mutual update (Fig 2 :34), a chain of handoffs that produces motion without progress (Fig 7 :13), and an answerability gap where nobody can render the grounds (world source, Gbouri and Gburi 2026).
- **Option B, indexed responsibility.** Enumerate every hand in contact with the harm, index each as a separate slot, test what each could have known and controlled given what it held, read the decision lineage rather than the roster, fix a ground the parties cannot move, and assign from a seat outside the slice graded. It leads to a checkable shape with written refuters, and an open slot until the ground settles the specific party.

**Bound shape (Direction).** Before running the search, write down what an answer would look like: a six-part procedure, listed in the README. It is an ordinary term here, so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: k hands, k greater than one, plus the harm. Symmetry: this is the decisive structural fact. Under a symmetric bond, *"k operands have k! spellings and one term"* (Fig 12 :92). If hands are treated as interchangeable, the arrangement is lost and a roster of names is a flat formula. Scale: at least three strata, individual, team or organization, and deployed system (Fig 4 :20, *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*). Arity is not reducible to a count, because the bond between hands carries information the hands do not.

**One line per domain, with actors, currency, anchor.** A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none is nameable, it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Attribution, framework internal | hands as slots, bonds between contributions, the residual harm | residual mass per slot, cost of a term or refusal | the harm record, which does not move when parties reinterpret it | yes, the recorded harm |
| D2 Socio-technical habitat | designers, operators, deployers, data, institutions, conditions, relations | contact with the residual, causal contribution | the harm, plus the enumerated habitat | yes, the harm |
| D3 The assigning seat | the checker, the parties, the forum | evidence versus agreement, provenance | the ground, never negotiation | yes, the ground |

**Partition or union.** Two different answers at two different levels, and they must not be confused:

- At the level of hands, the residual is a **partition**: each hand is a separate slot, and *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19).
- At the level of question types, the residual is a **union compound**: attribution, capability (what could each have known and done), and assignment (who decides with what standing) are three conditions. Fig 9 :57 says *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The many-hands problem is therefore not one harder responsibility problem. It is three loops with their own grounds.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance: *"Each link consumes what the one above produces"* (Fig 3 :20). Applied here:

1. Perception: record the harm and tell it apart from what was there before (Fig 3 :4-5). No attribution is possible without a grounded residual.
2. Vocabulary: terms for what each hand could afford, *"what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"* (Fig 3 :7). This is the control and foreseeability condition.
3. Objective: say what would count as progress in one's own terms, *"what would count as progress here"* (Fig 3 :10). This is the middle link, and it has no instrument (Fig 3 :27). It is where responsibility frameworks stall.
4. Planning: a route to assignment under incomplete information (Fig 3 :13).
5. Learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, and carry it with provenance (Fig 3 :15; Fig 13 :130).

A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). Most effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). In this question the unmeasured middle link is "what counts as adequate assignment."

**Strain test applied before conclusion.** An earlier draft tried to map the six-term substrate of Fig 13 (spectrum, gradient, network) onto three kinds of responsibility. That mapping needs a strain: the six terms govern physical composition, while this residual governs attribution. It governs another quantity, so it was dropped. After re-census, no figure line is forced. One genuine strain remains and is recorded in section 6: an institutional ground (a court or regulator) can be moved by framing, which breaks the anchor test unless the ground is narrowed to the recorded harm.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — Index per hand, never average across hands (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

A hand here is a slot: a designer, an operator, an institution. A global verdict such as "the organization was responsible" is a global R near zero with one or more live slot residuals underneath, which Fig 1 :13 explicitly permits as a legal state. Averaging removes the only signal that says where to look. The social-psychology sources corroborate this from a different field: responsibility to help falls as the number of bystanders rises, and the group-size effect is inhibited when a specific person is addressed (Bystander effect, Wikipedia 2026; Diffusion of responsibility, Wikipedia 2026). One source pool, one direction, so this corroboration is weak but low overlap with the figure text.

**Mode:** `general`, structural. **Refuter:** a measured case where aggregating across hands preserves, for every hand, the discriminating signal that identifies which hand's change would have altered the harm. Until such a case is shown, averaging is a failure of indexing, not a legitimate verdict.

### F2 — The roster is not the answer; the bond is (triangulated)

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**

Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart"**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

A list of everyone who touched the system is a flat formula. Responsibility attaches to the typed relation: who specified the threshold, who supplied the data, who authorized deployment, who could have refused, and in what order. Two projects with the same roster can have different responsibility structures because the bonds differ. The reverse also holds: a change in the bond can change the substance without changing the roster.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a case where two different arrangements of the same hands receive the same verdict at the ground, with a measurement showing the distinction makes no difference to repair or prevention. Note the limit: under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so if the hands really are interchangeable with respect to the harm, the roster is sufficient. That is a finding about that case, not a refutation of the indexing rule.

### F3 — Enumerate the habitat, and do not confuse enumeration with diffusion (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

The behavior belongs to the system and its habitat, not to the model alone and not to one hand in isolation. So neither "the system did it" nor "this engineer did it" is complete. The correct first move is enumeration: everyone in contact with the harm, then everyone in contact with them, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :52, *"And contact cascades."*). Safety engineering corroborates this from a different field: Reason's Swiss cheese model treats an accident as holes in several layers aligning, and treats error as *"system flaws, not character flaws"* (Swiss cheese model, Wikipedia 2026).

But enumeration is not diffusion. Enumerate to find the slots, then index each slot. Valence is relative to the residual: the same hand is a benefactor for one harm and a malfactor for another, so there is no permanent "responsible role" (Fig 11 :50).

**Mode:** `general` for enumeration, `specified` for the claim that a given hand is in the habitat. **Refuter:** a hand shown to be in contact whose removal leaves the residual unchanged at the ground. That hand drops out of D2 and the verdict narrows. The framework expects this: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim"* (Fig 11 :73), and the converse is available here.

### F4 — The anchor must not update (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :34: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :47: **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."**

If responsibility is settled by negotiation among the parties, the anchor becomes a participant and drifts coherently, which is exactly the failure nobody notices (Fig 2 :5-6, *"collapse 1: mutual update (coherent drift)"*). The anchor here is the recorded harm, which does not change because the designers reinterpret it. Independence matters separately: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"* (Fig 2 :31). Two internal reviews that read the same log are one frame wearing two names.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a documented procedure in which the harm record is altered by the parties' agreement and still settles responsibility correctly at a later measurement. That would show the anchor can update without drift. None was found. The strain on institutional anchors is recorded in section 6 and is not hidden here.

### F5 — The assigning seat is structured, not selected for agreement (triangulated)

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Fig 10 :86: **"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."**

Fig 10 :88: **"No gain from any particular reading."**

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**

Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."**

Fig 10 :62: **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."**

Fig 10 :63-64: **"The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."**

The body assigning responsibility must be outside the slice it grades. A team grading its own design is panel 1 of Fig 2, not a check. Where a stake cannot be removed (a regulator who also promoted the deployment, a board that set the bonus), declare it. The ruling must carry the residual it answered, the reasoning, and the date, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :63-64). This is a design rule, not a claim about a particular person. The RACI literature corroborates from business practice that one role should be *"ultimately answerable"* (Responsibility assignment matrix, Wikipedia 2026), but RACI is a convention, not a ground; it is handled as a filter in F11.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a checker that can author or reconstruct the very atom it checks, yet whose verification is sound under a formal zero-knowledge style test. That would break the non-access requirement. It needs a proof, not an assertion.

### F6 — Lineage is the proof, not the roster (triangulated)

Fig 13 :127: **"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."**

Fig 13 :129: **"It is recoverable from the lineage."**

Fig 13 :130: **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."**

Fig 13 :132: **"The first is provenance: how did this come to be, and from what."**

The behavior artifact does not disclose the responsible route. A logged decision trail does. This is why an explainability report produced after the fact is weaker than a decision record kept at the time: the first is a description written from the artifact, the second is the lineage. The 2026 responsibility-gap literature corroborates from AI ethics: an *"answerability gap can therefore persist where no attribution gap exists"* (Gbouri and Gburi, AI and Ethics 2026), because grounds can be withheld from the person who receives the demand or never formulated at all.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a case where the behavior uniquely determines the responsible hand with no lineage record available, demonstrated at the ground. That would narrow this finding to complex systems only. It is likely in simple cases and is recorded as a boundary, not a loophole.

### F7 — Decidability as the control and foresight condition (triangulated)

Fig 13 :133: **"given the library as it stands, could this have been reached at all."**

Fig 3 :7: **"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"**

Fig 13 :140: **"not only what was concluded, but what could have been concluded, and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ."**

A hand is answerable to the extent that, given what it held (its library, its data, its authority, its instructions), the harmful route was reachable and it could have refused or minted differently. This is the framework's decidability question applied to agency. It matches the philosophical condition independently: moral responsibility is *"distinguished from causal responsibility"* and we *"cannot always infer moral responsibility from an assignment of causal responsibility"* (Moral Responsibility, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy 2019, revised 2024). Santoni de Sio and Mecacci 2021 make the same point as *"meaningful human control"*, systems aligned with relevant human reasons and capacities.

Three sub-questions per slot, each checkable: what did this hand hold, was the harmful route reachable from it, and did it have a refusal available? A hand for whom the route was unreachable has no control condition and drops out, even if causally adjacent.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a hand held responsible at the ground for whom the harmful route was demonstrably unreachable in its closure, with the library and authority recorded. If that happens, the decidability condition fails and must be withdrawn.

### F8 — Buck-passing is a mirror chain (triangulated)

Fig 7 :13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 7 :42: **"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."**

Passing responsibility from designer to operator to institution without changing the closure is a mirror chain. Everyone agrees that the next party should answer, and evidence does not increase. A genuine handoff changes the room: it brings a different closure, a different instrument, or a new reading of the residual. The question to ask at each handoff is what new evidence arrived, not how many hands agreed to the transfer.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a handoff that introduced a new instrument or new evidence and moved the ground. That is a genuine new room, not buck-passing, and the finding does not apply to it.

### F9 — The framework returns a procedure, not a name (single-source for the limit)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134-135: **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Fig 13 :138: **"the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."**

The figures yield the shape of a correct assignment procedure. They do not yield "Engineer X is liable" for any actual case. Naming a liable party requires a ground reading: an investigation, a court, an instrument that discriminates among candidate routes. Until one fires, the status is `candidate`. A framework that names a standing responsible role for all cases would also break Fig 11 :51, since no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a figure-derived rule that names a specific role as always responsible, and a measurement showing it survives changes of residual. No such line was found; the claim is single-source on the canonical usage rule plus Fig 9, and is declared as such.

### F10 — Unbundle before searching: the many-hands problem is a union (triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 9 :56: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Three loops are bundled in the question: who contributed (attribution), what each could have known and controlled (capability), and who decides with what standing (assignment). Santoni de Sio and Mecacci 2021 corroborate from the field with a directly parallel split: *"The responsibility gap, it is argued, is not one problem but a set of at least four interconnected problems"* (culpability, moral accountability, public accountability, active responsibility). Different sources reach the same union shape, with low overlap between a metatheory text and an open-access philosophy of technology paper.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a case where the three conditions are inseparable at the ground, so that no instrument can address one without the others. Then it is one compound and needs a different loop; the unbundling instruction would be wrong for that case.

### F11 — Filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict (triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

Org charts, RACI matrices, job titles, and counts of contributors are filters. They decide where to spend attention. They are not evidence that a particular hand is answerable. The witnesses are the residual, the enumerated habitat, the lineage, and a ground that does not update. Where the cost of being wrong is high (a death, a lost benefit, a revoked license), buy independence by getting a second frame with a different evidence pool, not by getting a second opinion from the same report (Fig 8 :48).

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a filter entry that settles a responsibility verdict at the ground with no residual or lineage evidence behind it. That would show a filter can hand down a verdict, contrary to Fig 9 :60.

### F12 — The round-trip gap of "assign an accountable owner" (triangulated)

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

The common shorthand "assign one accountable owner" is a lossy condensation of the six-part procedure. Sent up, it carries the Useful part: one ultimately answerable role. Brought back down, it drops the per-slot indexing, the bond information, the decidability test, the non-updating ground, and the seat constraint. The gap `R_T` is nonzero and is enumerated in `11_audit.md`. Treating the shorthand as identical to the procedure is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a case where "one accountable owner" reconstructs all six constraints at the ground without additional record. That would set `R_T` to zero for that case.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before the world search was used to support any finding. Per the framework's discipline, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A documented case where aggregating across hands preserves, for every hand, the discriminating signal that identifies which hand's change would have altered the harm (breaks F1).
2. A measurement showing two different arrangements of the same hands receive the same verdict at the ground, with no difference to repair or prevention (breaks F2).
3. A hand held responsible at the ground whose harmful route was unreachable in its recorded closure (breaks F7).
4. A procedure whose anchor changes when the parties push on it and still settles responsibility correctly at a later measurement (breaks F4).
5. A figure line that names a standing responsible role and survives changes of residual (breaks F9).

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, nothing is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Enumerate the habitat first (Fig 11 :43-44), before choosing any frame. Describe the residual before going looking (Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).
2. Index per hand and per institution (Fig 1 :19, :22). Do not report a single global verdict while live slot residuals remain.
3. Record bonds, not only names: role, decision, authority, order, and refusal opportunity (Fig 12 :5, :92).
4. Apply the decidability test per slot (Fig 13 :133). Drop hands whose route was unreachable.
5. Keep the harm record as the non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :29-30). Declare any institutional stake (Fig 10 :60) and put the assigning seat outside the slice graded (Fig 10 :86).
6. Unbundle attribution, capability, and assignment before searching for a culprit (Fig 9 :54, :57).
7. Send the procedure and the refuter, not the conclusion (Fig 4 :22). Ask what would refute it.

### Strains

1. **Institutional ground.** A court or regulator is a social instrument that can be moved by framing, unlike a physical measurement. Fig 2 :29-30 requires an anchor that does not change with what frames think. Re-census narrows the anchor to the recorded harm (what happened, to whom, with what loss), which does not update; the sanction is a convention (Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*). The strain is named, not corrected into silence.
2. **Symmetry of hands.** If two hands are genuinely interchangeable with respect to the harm, the bond carries no distinguishing information and the roster is sufficient. This is a boundary on F2, not a refutation.
3. **External ground not reached.** No investigation, court record, or instrument was opened for a real case. All verdicts remain `candidate`.

## 6. Updated verdict

The shape survives: enumerate the habitat, index per hand, record the bonds, test decidability per slot, keep a non-updating harm ground, unbundle the three loops, and assign from a seat outside the slice with a declared stake and a provenanced ruling. The framework does not name a liable party. It returns a procedure and its refuter. Specific assignments are owed to a ground reading, not to this simulation. Status `specified`, `candidate`, not `grounded`. Strains after re-census: one live (institutional ground), one bounded (symmetric hands), none hidden.
