# The Tether's answer on responsibility with many hands

**Mode: `specified` on the procedure, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a verdict on any real case. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A harm has many causal contributors. Answerability is obscure because each contributor held only part of the control, the knowledge, and the authority. The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*): attribution, capability, and assignment each need their own loop and their own ground. At the level of hands it is a partition: Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

## 2. The shape, in six moves

1. **Enumerate the habitat before choosing a frame.** List everything in contact with the harm, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-44). Capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39), so neither the model alone nor one hand alone is the whole object. Describe the effect before naming a cause (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. **Index every hand as a separate slot.** Do not average (Fig 1 :19, :22). A global verdict can sit near zero while live slot residuals remain; that is a legal state in the framework, not a closed question.
3. **Record bonds, not only names.** A roster is a flat formula (Fig 12 :5). *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing"* (Fig 12 :92): if swapping two hands changes nothing, they are one term; otherwise they are isomers and the difference is decisive in the behaviour (Fig 12 :117). Record role, decision, authority, order, and refusal opportunity.
4. **Test decidability per slot.** Ask what the hand held, whether the harmful route was reachable from it, and whether a refusal was available (Fig 13 :133, *"given the library as it stands, could this have been reached at all."*). Drop hands for whom the route was unreachable. Causal contribution alone is not enough: SEP, *"Moral responsibility should also be distinguished from causal responsibility."*
5. **Fix a ground that does not update.** The recorded harm is the anchor (Fig 2 :29-30). If the parties negotiate the anchor, it becomes a frame and drifts coherently (Fig 2 :34, :47). *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35).
6. **Seat the checker outside the slice graded.** A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42). Declare any stake that cannot be removed (Fig 10 :60), and leave a ruling with the residual it answered, the reasoning, and the date (Fig 10 :62-64). Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

Two further constraints: unbundle the union before searching for a culprit (Fig 9 :54), and treat handoffs as progress only when the new room differs and matches the residual it surfaced (Fig 7 :35-36). Otherwise buck-passing is a mirror chain in which agreement compounds and evidence does not (Fig 7 :13).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a name. No engineer, operator, or institution is identified as liable in any actual case. That requires an investigation or a court.
- Not "collective responsibility is impossible." A group can be a slot in D1. What is refused is averaging away live slot residuals and letting the anchor update.
- Not "explainability closes the gap." An addressee can stand ready to answer while reason-rendering is defeated; the answerability gap can persist where no attribution gap exists (Gbouri and Gburi 2026).
- Not a legal doctrine. Legal liability regimes are imports from a frame with a different closure and need a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not grounded. No real harm record, investigation, or instrument was opened. Status `candidate`.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A recorded case, not a further argument. The instruments to bring:

1. **A decision lineage kept at the time**, not a post hoc explanation (Fig 13 :127, :130). Does it show which route produced the harm and which refusals were available?
2. **A per-slot reachability check.** For each hand, given what it held, was the harmful route reachable? A hand for whom it was not drops out.
3. **A non-updating harm record.** Did the fixed point change when the parties pushed on it? If yes, the anchor was captured.
4. **A checker outside the graded slice**, with declared stake and a provenanced ruling.

Standing refuters from `01` section 3: a case where averaging preserves the discriminating signal for every hand; a hand held responsible whose route was unreachable; a procedure whose anchor changes and still settles correctly; a figure line naming a standing responsible role that survives changes of residual.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The enumeration is never complete: contact cascades, and what cannot yet be perceived is residual (Fig 11 :48). The framework returns the shape any answer must have and stops where the instruments stop, recording the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134). One live strain remains: a court or regulator is a movable social ground, so the anchor is narrowed to the recorded harm while the sanction is treated as a convention (Fig 10 :46). Prestige or agreement is not an instrument (Fig 2 :42).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a culprit. It gives the shape a responsibility assignment must have when many hands are involved: enumerate everyone in contact with the harm; index each as a separate slot instead of averaging them into one verdict; record the bonds between them, since two teams with the same roster can be different substances; ask what each hand held, whether the harmful route was reachable from it, and whether refusal was available; keep the harm record as the fixed point the parties cannot negotiate; and put the person or body assigning responsibility outside the slice being graded, with any stake declared and the ruling dated. The many-hands problem is not one hard question but a union of attribution, capability, and assignment, so it must be unbundled before a search. Handing the question along a chain of institutions is progress only when the next room has different evidence, not merely a different name. What the framework offers is that procedure, its written falsifiers, and the instruments that would close it. The actual party stays open until a ground settles it.
