## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When a harm comes from many designers, operators, and institutions, a correct responsibility assignment must be indexed rather than averaged: enumerate every hand in contact with the harm, treat each as a separate slot, record the typed bonds between them rather than only a roster, test per slot what the hand held and whether the harmful route was reachable and refusable, keep the harm record as an anchor that does not change when the parties reinterpret it, and place the assigning seat outside the slice it grades with any unremovable stake declared and a dated, provenanced ruling. The many-hands case is a partition across hands and a union across three question types (attribution, capability, assignment), so it must be unbundled before a search. The framework returns this procedure, not the name of a liable party.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 12

- **Statement:** averaging across hands destroys the discriminating signal. A roster of names is not the responsibility structure; the bond is.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22, **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 12 :5, **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :92, **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**
- **What to check:** is the hand actually a slot with a nonzero residual, and is the relation between hands carrying information the roster does not? If two hands are symmetric with respect to the harm, the bond carries nothing and the roster suffices.
- **Refuter:** a measured case where averaging across hands preserves, for every hand, the signal that identifies which hand's change would have altered the harm; or a case where two different bond arrangements receive the same verdict at the ground and repair does not differ.

### C2 — Domain-facing; checkable against a real automated system

- **Statement:** for each hand, answerability depends on what it held, whether the harmful route was reachable from that, and whether it had a refusal available. Causal proximity alone is not sufficient.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :133, **"given the library as it stands, could this have been reached at all."** Fig 3 :7, **"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"** Fig 11 :49, **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
- **What to check:** take one hand in a system you know. Write down its data, authority, and instructions. Could it have foreseen and refused the harmful route? Is the same hand a benefactor for a different residual? The philosophical literature makes the same distinction: moral responsibility *"should also be distinguished from causal responsibility"* (SEP, Moral Responsibility, 2019, revised 2024).
- **Refuter:** a hand held responsible at the ground for whom the harmful route was unreachable in its recorded closure, or a hand whose removal leaves the residual unchanged. That hand drops out.

### C3 — Institutional; checkable against any review or assignment process

- **Statement:** the anchor must not update, and the checker must be outside the slice graded. Otherwise the process is a mirror chain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35, **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :47, **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."** Fig 10 :42, **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Fig 10 :60, **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."** Fig 7 :13, **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
- **What to check:** identify the fixed point in your process. Did it change when the parties pushed on it? Who grades whom, and does the grader gain from a particular reading? Does each handoff bring new evidence or only agreement?
- **Refuter:** a documented process whose fixed point changes under pressure and still settles responsibility correctly at a later measurement; or a checker that authors the atom it checks and whose verification is sound under a formal non-access test.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a name. No engineer, operator, or firm is asserted to be liable in a real case.
- Not that collective responsibility is impossible. A group can be a slot. Averaging away live slot residuals is what is refused.
- Not a legal liability rule. Legal doctrine is an import from another closure and needs a source gate and a shadow test.
- Not that explainability closes the gap. Answerability has two parts, addressee-standing and reason-rendering; the second can fail while the first holds (Gbouri and Gburi 2026).
- Not `grounded`. No investigation, court record, or instrument was opened. Status `candidate`.
- Not a complete enumeration. Contact cascades, and what cannot yet be perceived is residual (Fig 11 :48).

## 5. What would settle it

A recorded case with a decision lineage kept at the time, a per-slot reachability check, a non-updating harm record, and a checker outside the graded slice with a declared stake and a dated ruling. A further argument does not settle it. Prestige or agreement is not an instrument: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* (Fig 2 :42).
