# Isomers of responsibility, and the order of the handoffs

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after the main audit. (1) If two projects have the same set of hands, are they the same responsibility structure, or different substances with the same ingredients? (2) In what order must the checking chain run, and where does it usually break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the enumerated hands (designer, data provider, operator, deployer, institution) and the harm. Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus negation, with `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, and `¬` refusal. No new role is available without importing it from an institution. Currency: residual per slot, cost of a refusal. Anchor: the recorded harm.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are two projects with the same hands the same responsibility case?

Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart"**

Two automated-driving projects may share the same roster: a model team, a data team, an operator, a manufacturer, a regulator. If in project A the operator could refuse deployment and did not, while in project B the operator had no refusal authority because the institution set the threshold centrally, the two have the same atoms and different bonds. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not variants of one case, and the difference is *"decisive in the behaviour"*: the repair and the answerability differ.

The boundary matters. If the hands really are symmetric with respect to the harm (swapping any two changes nothing), then k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92). In that special case the roster is sufficient and the isomer claim does not bite. This is a measured boundary, not an escape clause: it requires showing that swapping hands leaves the harm and the refusal set unchanged.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a case where two different bond arrangements over the same hands receive the same verdict at the ground, and where repair and prevention do not differ. If that is shown, the isomer claim fails and the flat roster reading stands for that class of cases.

### Q2 — Is this a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

At the level of hands it is a partition: Fig 1 :19, **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** At the level of questions it is a union of at least three conditions: attribution, capability, and assignment. The field corroborates independently: Santoni de Sio and Mecacci 2021 split the responsibility gap into four kinds, and Matthias 2004 identified a capacity and foreseeability gap. Both are lower overlap with the figure text than the essays are.

A single lever clears a region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56), which is why the shorthand of naming one accountable owner works in simple cases and fails in the compound. The residue after a lever is *"categorically different"*: a case in two conditions is a specific compound, not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57-58).

### Q3 — What is the order of the chain?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The chain for responsibility:

1. **Perception.** Record the harm, tell it apart from the prior state. The ground enters as the only metric (Fig 1 :23).
2. **Vocabulary.** Have terms for what each hand affords: what it could move, open, join, block (Fig 3 :7). Without these terms the slot cannot be assessed.
3. **Objective.** Say what would count as progress in the system's own terms: repair, prevention, redress (Fig 3 :10).
4. **Planning.** Choose a route to assignment under incomplete information (Fig 3 :13).
5. **Learn and carry.** Verify what worked and carry it with provenance (Fig 3 :15; Fig 10 :62).

The break usually lands between 3 and 4: Fig 3 :25-26 says the link between having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress has *"neither"* obvious failures nor obvious tests, so *"it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Applied here: most organizations can list their roles (link 2) and name a sign-off (link 4), but cannot say what would count as an adequate assignment (link 3). Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The pre-registered refuter in `01` section 3 is an attempt to supply that instrument.

### Q4 — Is a passing chain progress or motion?

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

A handoff from designer to operator to institution is a room change only if the new room has a different closure and matches the residual the last one surfaced. If every room says "the next party is responsible", the chain is one long room. Fig 7 :42: **"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."** The test of a handoff is what new evidence arrived with it.

### Q5 — Analogues and where they stop

- **Chemistry, isomerism.** Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826, and Berzelius 1830, as cited in Fig 12 :98 and Fig 13 :122. Same composition, different properties, forced structure into the notation. It maps to Q1 because the bond, not the roster, decides the substance. It stops where molecules have fixed bonds and institutions do not.
- **Percolation and layered defence.** Reason's Swiss cheese model, formalized by Frosch as percolation on a Bethe lattice (Swiss cheese model, Wikipedia 2026). Holes in several layers align to let a hazard through. It maps to the enumeration rule in F3: first-order enumeration misses the second-order effect. It stops where the model is a warning about defences, not an attribution rule.
- **Social group size.** Darley and Latané 1968, as recorded in the Bystander effect article: 85% intervention alone, 62% with one other, 31% with four others; and the group-size effect disappeared when a specific person was named. It maps to Fig 1 :22: indexing a specific slot defeats the averaging. It stops at a lab emergency, not a board decision.
- **RACI.** *"one accountable stakeholder specified for each task"* (Responsibility assignment matrix, Wikipedia 2026). It maps to the single-slot rule as a convention. It stops at Fig 9 :60: a filter cannot hand down a verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Two projects with the same hands are responsibility isomers when the bonds differ, and the difference is decisive in repair and answerability. The question is a partition across hands and a union across question types. The chain is ordered by dependency and usually breaks at the middle link, where no instrument exists for what counts as adequate assignment. Handoffs are progress only when the new room has a different closure and matches the residual. The falsifier is stated in Q1.
