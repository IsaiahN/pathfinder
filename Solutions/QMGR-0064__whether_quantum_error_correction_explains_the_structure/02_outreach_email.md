**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 F1 and F3 is stated exactly as written there. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one structural claim about spacetime and QEC before you decide whether the idea is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with composition running across kinds and never within one. From that rule it follows that space and time cannot bond directly. If they compose at all, a structure must sit between them, so "spacetime" is a derivative, not a primitive. A continuum was removed as a seventh member for failing the indivisibility test. Smoothness and continuity are what a composition leaves behind.
>
> The question "does quantum error correction explain the structure of spacetime" is therefore a question about what occupies that middle slot. The framework does not name the occupant. It returns a shape. My own reading is that a QEC construction is a candidate binding onto a held atom, most plausibly the network structure, since that atom is defined as "that entities relate, and influence travels." It could instead be a functional group over a code subspace. I cannot at present discriminate those, and I do not claim the framework can.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that spacetime *is* a quantum error correcting code as a final theory; that QEC adds a new primitive; that the metric tensor or the area law *is* a specific framework atom; that this settles quantum gravity. Even a successful QEC account would be a second-order composition over already settled terms, not a last room. Agreement from people who already like the idea is not evidence here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. deriving one of the six terms from the other five;
> 2. showing the framework must admit a state-state or structure-structure composition it cannot refuse, so no structure slot is needed;
> 3. naming an instrument or proof that shows no QEC construction can occupy the slot, or that forces a different occupant;
> 4. showing the QEC construction explains no gap the geometric account leaves open;
> 5. deriving one theory from the other with no residual left, which would break the no-last-room rule.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary, is the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public entry point for the framework: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
