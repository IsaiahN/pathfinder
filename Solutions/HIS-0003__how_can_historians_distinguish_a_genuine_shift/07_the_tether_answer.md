# The Tether's answer: standards shift versus record survival

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on any case verdict. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No archive was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A historian observes that the surviving records of period A differ from those of period B. Two sources can produce that same observation: the community's standard of evidence changed, or the set of records that survived to the present changed. The record is a lossy projection; it cannot comment on its source. This is a union compound, not one hard question with two opposite answers (Fig 9 :55-58). The answer owed is a shape of a discriminator, plus the refuter and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. The shape

1. **Index the difference per slot.** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Ask which claims, genres, citations and subsets changed, not whether "standards rose" overall. Where different rules keep working on different subsets, the problem should be split rather than searched (Fig 9 :4-5).

2. **Separate a frame change from a channel failure.** Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does." A survival change is a channel problem; the honest output is "contact has been lost" plus a restoration of the reading, not a verdict about the past. A standards change is a change to the frame's own ruler, and Fig 10 :47 says a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one: that is how E1 leaves a trace, in the boundary of what was admitted as evidence. Run the two tracks separately (Fig 6 :31).

3. **Buy two low-overlap readings.** Fig 2 :31-32, independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence. One reading comes from the internal argument structure and explicit methodological statements of the documents. The other comes from material and preservation facts: dating, provenance, stratigraphy, archive history, known destructions. Historians who read the same archive and agree are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49). Only readings from different pools make agreement a verdict (Fig 2 :35).

4. **Use a non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :29, if it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor. A radiocarbon determination or a documented destruction does not change because the historian prefers a standards narrative. An interpretation of the record does. Do not put the record itself in the anchor's seat (Fig 2 :47).

5. **Import instruments with provenance.** Fig 6 :23, the bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. The Signor-Lipps confidence intervals, taphonomic reference classes and comparative preservation studies come from palaeontology and archaeology with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52). Fig 6 :32: an instrument is improved from a worse one already returning something, not specified from a description.

6. **Refuse the naive identification before minting a cause.** "The record shows a genuine standards shift" is a false identification of source, not a missing explanation. A refusal is a negation and is not a bond (Operators :58). The record of the refusal stays; the reach falls because something was added.

7. **Return a shape, not a case verdict.** Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." No figure line says which condition produced a named period's record. The framework reports "unreached, instrument owed," which is a verdict under Fig 13 :134, not a silence.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular historical shift was genuine or spurious. That is a ground reading nobody has taken here.
- Not a universal checklist of historical method. Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not that standards do not change. The historiography sources confirm that they do. The question is how to tell, not whether.
- Not that more reading of the same record settles it. Fig 8 :32 makes that definitional.
- Not that agreement among experts is evidence. Fig 2 :40 makes it disqualifying for a verdict when the pool is shared.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an archive.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the reading "cannot comment on the source," and "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Concretely:

1. For a given period, does a preservation model built from material facts predict the observed distribution of record types better than a model built from a changed admission rule? If the preservation model absorbs the pattern, E2 wins for that slot.
2. Does the internal argument structure of the documents show a change in what counted as a reason, in periods where survival conditions are independently known to have been stable? If yes, E1 wins for that slot.
3. Is there a collection from the same period preserved under demonstrably different conditions (wet versus dry, central versus peripheral archive)? Comparing them is the historian's version of the well-preserved versus poorly-preserved comparison that archaeology already uses.
4. Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5: a single reading determines the source; both explanations resolve from one pool; the anchor moves on interpretation; a standards change leaves no trace; the preservation model fails to bite; the per-slot residual is flat everywhere.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (the line is in the passage about reporting what the library as it stands cannot do). The discriminator's shape is available now. The case verdict needs instruments that were not run here. The record of what was imported, what was refused and what would reverse it is kept in `01`, `09` and `11`, so the stop is checkable. The canonical figures README puts the limit plainly: "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you whether a particular shift in the historical record was real. It tells you what an answer would have to look like. The surviving record is a lossy reading, and a change in evidential standards and a change in what survived can produce the same reading, so the record alone cannot settle its own source. A valid discrimination indexes the difference per document and subset, keeps a standards track and a survival track separate, uses two readings that draw on different evidence (argument structure on one side, material provenance and preservation on the other), ranges both against a fact that does not move when the historian changes their mind, and records where every imported instrument came from. It refuses the sentence "the record shows a standards shift" until that work is done. Standards really do have a history, and preservation really does distort what reaches us; the framework's contribution is the shape of the test that tells them apart, not a verdict about any named period. The case stays open until the ground pays.
