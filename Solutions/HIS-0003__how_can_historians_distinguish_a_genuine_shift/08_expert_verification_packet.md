# Expert verification packet: the Tether on standards shift versus record survival

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science or an archive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the case verdict. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The surviving record is a lossy projection, and a genuine change in evidential standards and a change in what survived can produce the same reading. Reading the record more closely cannot settle which source produced it. A valid discrimination has a specific shape: index the difference per document, genre and subset; keep a standards track and a survival track separate and never read one against the other; use two readings that draw on different evidence, the internal argument structure of the documents and the material or preservation record; range both against a fact that does not move when the historian changes their interpretation; record the provenance of every imported instrument; and refuse the naive identification before minting a cause. The framework returns that shape and a refuter, not a verdict about any named period. The case verdict is an explicit `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the observation underdetermines the source, so a single reading of the record cannot discriminate E1 from E2.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32, "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 9 :44, "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does."
- **Refuter:** show that a single reading of the surviving record determines its source without any independent instrument, or that the material anchor moves when the interpretation changes.

### C2: historiography-facing; checkable by a historian of science against the literature

- **Statement:** a standard in active use is not a mere convention; changing it changes what is admitted as evidence, and that leaves a detectable trace distinct from the trace of record loss. The historiography literature confirms that standards of evidence have a history, but does not itself supply the discriminator.
- **Source, verbatim figure line:** Fig 10 :47, "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :48, "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."
- **What to check:** in a case you know well, is there an observable change in the admission rule (what counted as testimony, measurement, citation, or competent witness) that is independent of the mere disappearance of a record type? If the only observable is disappearance, this sub-claim is unsupported for that case.
- **Refuter:** a real change in evidential standards leaves no trace in what was admitted or in argument structure. Then E1 has no legible signature and the separator fails.

### C3: archive and preservation-facing; checkable by an archivist or taphonomist

- **Statement:** the survival track needs an external reference class and a non-updating anchor. More surviving documents are not evidence of more activity without one.
- **Source, verbatim figure lines:** Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :31-32, "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."
- **World check:** Wang and Marshall 2016 report that "owing to the incompleteness of the fossil record, the appearance of a gradual extinction would be expected even if the extinctions were truly simultaneous." Surovell et al. 2009 conclude that "archaeological abundance in and of itself is meaningless without reference to the geology in which archaeological finds occur." Both are low-overlap fields and both predict that a count needs a preservation reference class.
- **Refuter:** apply a preservation model to a case where survival conditions demonstrably changed and show that the inferred pattern does not change. The taphonomic corrective then does not bite for that case.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict on any named period, movement or archive.
- Not a universal method checklist. Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- Not that standards do not change. They do; the question is discrimination, not existence.
- Not that more expert agreement is evidence. Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not that the imported preservation instruments move the framework's wall. Fig 6 :21, "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." They are provenanced bindings from other frames.
- Not `grounded`. No archive was measured in producing this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A preservation model built from material facts that predicts the observed distribution better than a model built from a changed admission rule, for a named period and slot.
2. A period where survival conditions are independently known to have been stable, and the internal argument structure nevertheless changes in what it treats as a reason.
3. Two collections from the same period preserved under demonstrably different conditions, compared directly.
4. Any of the six falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :32, Fig 9 :44 and Fig 10 :24. Three short passages.
2. Say whether, in a case you know, the admission rule changed in a way that is visible independently of record loss (C2).
3. Name one material anchor for that case that would not move if you changed your interpretation of the documents (C3).
4. Name one preservation reference class available for that archive, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
