## 0. The claim under audit

> How can historians distinguish a genuine shift in evidential standards from a change in the kinds of records that survived?

Stated as an effect: a historian observes that the documentary record of period A differs from that of period B. Two candidate sources are offered.

- **E1, standards shift.** What counted as adequate evidence changed inside the community. The ruler moved.
- **E2, survival change.** What was produced did not change in the relevant way; what persisted to the present did. The channel lost material.

Both write the same observation `o'`: the surviving record as we now read it. The question asks for the discriminator. It does not ask which one happened in a named period; that case verdict is a `gap` (F10).

**Direction bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** What an answer would look like, written down before the run: a procedure that returns, for a given observed difference, which of the two conditions is present, using evidence that would come out differently under the other condition, with a refuter stated in advance. If no such procedure exists for a case, the answer is "unreached, instrument owed," not a guess. Binding this shape makes it refusable (Fig 5 :65, "Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else.").

## 1. Residual, arity, symmetry, scale

- **Residual R.** The observed difference between two periods' surviving records, indexed per slot (which claim, which genre, which archive, which subset). Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global statement such as "standards rose" throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :28).
- **Arity.** At least two conditions, E1 and E2, and in real cases more (research interest, classification standard, digitization, language of publication). So a **union compound**, not a two-way partition (Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.").
- **Symmetry.** The observation is symmetric with respect to its two candidate sources: E1 and E2 can each produce it. That is metamerism in the source, equifinality in systems terms. The causal chain is ordered (standard → production → burial or discard → survival → present reading), so a correlation between the record and either candidate cannot choose the arrow. Operators table :63 on the priced bond applies: the relation is not recoverable from the operands.
- **Scale.** E1 is a frame-wide or subfield-wide convention change. E2 is a material channel process acting per object and per site over centuries. Different scales, so they need different instruments and must not be read against one another (Fig 6 :31, "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other.").

## 2. Domain census

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 past standards frame | inquirers, their genres, their explicit methodological rules | what counted as a good reason or proof | the standard actually in force then, read only from internal argument structure and explicit statements; not the historian's present preference | frame/convention change |
| D2 survival channel | media, archives, weather, war, curators, digitization | what persisted to be read | material facts that do not move when the historian interprets them: dating, provenance, stratigraphy, known destruction events | channel decay, a habitat selection process |
| D3 present research frame | historians, their questions and vocabularies | agreement and residuals | the ground (past practice), never agreement; in this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool | verification and outreach |

**Partition or union.** Union, with overlap. A standards change can alter what gets kept (a new genre is copied more), and a survival change can look like a standards change (a genre disappears). Conditions bind together in the intersections (Fig 9 :57-58, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."). Unbundle before searching; one lever clears only the region where one condition holds.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); index it per slot before aggregating; separate channel from frame before adjudicating (Fig 10 :24); refuse the identification "the record shows a shift in standards" before minting a cause (Formula step 2, a refusal is a negation, Operators :58); only then compare readings.

**Strain test applied up front.** "The ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24) appears to conflict with the obvious fact that documents decay. Re-census assigns objects: in Fig 10 the ground is the past reality, and the surviving record is the channel to it. The channel decays; the past does not. No contradiction after re-census. This strain is kept visible in section 8 and in `11_audit.md`.

## 4. Findings

### F1: Both candidates write the same observation, so the record cannot settle the source (decisive for the framing)

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."
Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

If the historian only compares the surviving documents of A and B, the closure is the record. E1 and E2 both compose inside it. No amount of further reading of the same documents adds the atom that distinguishes them, because that atom is outside the closure: a preservation model, a reference class, an independent dating or provenance reading. This is the source-discrimination limit, and it is structural rather than a complaint about effort. **Triangulated** (Fig 8 and Fig 9, low overlap between the idempotence proof and the sealed-room claim).

### F2: The question is a union compound, not a two-option choice

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

A standards shift can change what survives, and a survival change can imitate a standards shift. The intersection is a compound with its own loop. A single lever, for example "we found more records", clears only the region where E2 alone holds and leaves the intersection standing. Verdict: unbundle, derive per condition, then examine the compound. **Triangulated** with the world source on equifinality (low overlap, systems theory).

### F3: A survival change is a channel failure, not a change in the past

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"

This is the cleanest figure-derived separator in the question. E2 is a channel problem. The right output of a channel diagnosis is "contact has been lost" plus a restoration of the reading, not a verdict about what the past was like. E1 is a change to the frame's own ruler. Mixing them is Fig 2's mutual-update collapse: the historian adjusts both stories to fit one another and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). **Triangulated** across Fig 10 and Fig 2.

### F4: Independence is the parameter, and shared archives are one witness

Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not"

Two historians who read the same surviving archive and agree that "standards rose" are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49). They must range against separate readings: the internal argument structure of the documents on one side, and the material and preservation record on the other. Only if those two low-overlap readings point the same way is the agreement a verdict. **Triangulated** (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 8).

### F5: The anchor must not move when the historian pushes on it

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :46: "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."

A material fact is a candidate anchor: a radiocarbon determination, a stratigraphic position, an archive provenance, a documented destruction. An interpretation of the record is not an anchor, because it updates on the historian's own prior. Choosing "what the record says" as the anchor is Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame." **Single-source on the figure side for the general rule**, corroborated in the world by the palaeontology sources (F7).

### F6: A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"

An evidential standard is a shared convention about what counts. When it is used to decide which claims are admitted, it functions as a verdict, not as a convention. That is how E1 leaves a trace: the boundary of what got admitted moved. The historian can look for a change in the admission rule (what kinds of testimony, measurement, or citation were accepted) rather than only for a change in the surviving output. This is a different observable from the mere disappearance of a record type. **Triangulated** with the historiography of science world sources (F8): evidence and rules of evidence have a history.

### F7: World corroboration from low-overlap fields confirms the source-discrimination shape

- Florida Museum research news, Kowalewski and Nawrot 2018 (palaeontology): "We know these species are still living in the Adriatic Sea, so we can be sure that their disappearance from the fossil record does not represent a true extinction." - Beven and Hong 2026, earthArXiv (hydrology, read via search extraction, reduced weight): "observed outcomes may constrain behaviours without uniquely determining underlying causes or trajectories." Predicts underdetermination from the outcome alone. Corroborates F1. Overlap: low.

### F8: The historiography literature confirms that standards have a history, without deciding this case

- Sciortino, interview with Lorraine Daston, *British Journal for the History of Science* 58(3), 2025: "these organizing concepts – as we may call them – come into being through specific historical and social processes, and change and get their meaning from the uses we make of them in a certain period of history." Affirms E1 as a real historical phenomenon. It does not tell us how to discriminate E1 from E2 in a case, so it corroborates the census, not the separator.
- Chandler, Davidson and Harootunian, eds., *Questions of Evidence* (University of Chicago Press, 1994, publisher page): "the way 'rules of evidence' are themselves products of historical developments." Same corroboration.
- Daston, "The History of Science and the History of Knowledge," *KNOW* 1(1), 2017 (search extraction, direct fetch 403, reduced weight): the history of science risks a "hall of mirrors." Corroborates Fig 2's mirror and Fig 7's chain of mirrors as a live hazard in the field itself.

**Overlap declared.** These three are one in-field evidence pool, so they count as one witness for the E1 phenomenon, not three. They do not share the figures' evidence pool, so that witness is genuinely outside the framework.

### F9: No standing checklist of historical method is returned

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

A universal checklist would be a standing list of good methods. It would fail when the residual changes, which is exactly when the historian needs it. What survives is the *shape* of a discriminator: per-slot description, independent readings, a non-updating anchor, a channel/frame split, recorded lineage, a refuter. Naming the actual instruments for a case (dating, codicology, stratigraphy, a preservation model, a quantified comparison of well-preserved against poorly preserved traditions) is a provenanced binding from other fields, not a seventh principle. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 and Fig 9).

### F10: The case verdict is a declared gap, not silence

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

No figure line selects which condition produced a named period's record. That needs a ground reading: actual dating, actual provenance, an actual preservation model for that archive. None was taken. The honest report is "unreached, instrument owed," which is a verdict under Fig 13 :134. It is the shape-not-a-name limit of the framework, not a failure of the derivation. **Triangulated** across Fig 13 and Fig 9.

### F11: A refusal is owed before a cause is minted

Formula step 2 routes a residual that is wrong, not merely missing, to a refusal. Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." The ordinary sentence "the surviving record shows a genuine shift in evidential standards" is not a gap to be filled by a cause; it is a false identification of the source if survival is ignored. The refusal must be written before the mint. Outward import of preservation science then follows, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), and is debited against independence.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before outreach, per the Seat Map's failure genera: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single reading of the surviving record can be shown to determine its source without any independent instrument (metamerism fails for that case).
2. Both candidate explanations are resolved from the same evidence pool and the resolution still stands (independence is not required).
3. The chosen material anchor can be shown to update on the historian's interpretation of the record (it was a frame, not a ground).
4. A genuine change in evidential standards leaves no trace in what got admitted as evidence or in the internal structure of argument (E1 has no detectable signature).
5. A preservation model, applied to a case where survival conditions demonstrably changed, fails to change the inferred pattern (the taphonomic corrective does not bite).
6. The per-slot residual, once indexed, is genuinely flat everywhere (there is no local signal to discriminate, and F1 has nothing to work on).

If any fires, the separator as stated is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of evidence (Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
2. Describe the observed difference in effect terms before reaching for E1 or E2 (Fig 9 :27-28 and :30-31), or any frame will seem to fit.
3. Index the difference per slot. Reject pooled "more rigorous" scores (Fig 1 :19, :22, :28).
4. Split the union. Run the frame-change track and the channel-change track separately and do not read one against the other (Fig 6 :31).
5. Buy two low-overlap readings and a non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :31-35, Fig 2 :42).
6. Record provenance for every imported instrument and every ruling (Fig 8 :50-52; Fig 10 :62, "A ruling carries provenance like an atom.").
7. Report the case verdict as `gap` until a ground reading exists (Fig 13 :134). "Unreached" is a verdict, not an apology.
8. Send the generator and the refuter, not a recorded conclusion (Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
