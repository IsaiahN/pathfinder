# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive a check by someone who knows the archive. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would you count as a failure of this source-discrimination test?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape claim I would like you to check against what you already know about historical evidence. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A historian sees that the surviving records of period A differ from those of period B. Two sources can write that same observation: a change in what counted as adequate evidence inside the community, or a change in what happened to survive. Reading the surviving record more closely cannot, by itself, tell you which source produced it. The observation is a lossy projection; several causes can give the same reading.
>
> **The shape claim.** A valid discrimination needs four things, kept separate:
> 1. the difference indexed per document, genre and subset, not pooled into a single "standards rose" score;
> 2. two readings that do not draw on the same evidence: the internal argument structure of the documents on one side, and material and preservation facts on the other;
> 3. an anchor that cannot move when the interpretation changes, for example a date, a provenance, a stratigraphic position or a documented destruction;
> 4. the frame-change question and the channel-change question run as separate tracks and never read against one another.
>
> The output is a shape of a discriminator, not a verdict about any named period. I am explicitly not claiming that any particular shift in the historical record was a standards change. That case verdict is open until someone takes a ground reading.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this returns a universal method checklist; that a standards change leaves no trace (it does leave one in what gets admitted as evidence); that agreement among historians who read the same archive counts as corroboration (it does not); or that the framework settles which condition actually occurred in a given case. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a single reading of the surviving record determines its source with no independent instrument;
> 2. both explanations are resolved from the same evidence pool and the resolution still holds;
> 3. the material anchor can be shown to move when the historian's interpretation changes;
> 4. a real change in evidential standards leaves no trace in what was admitted as evidence or in argument structure;
> 5. a preservation model, where survival conditions demonstrably changed, leaves the inferred pattern unchanged.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
