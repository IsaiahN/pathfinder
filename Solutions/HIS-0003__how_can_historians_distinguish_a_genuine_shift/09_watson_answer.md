## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a historian can count on to distinguish a genuine shift in evidential standards (E1) from a change in the kinds of records that survived (E2), given that both produce the same surviving reading. Option A: treat the surviving record as sufficient evidence and infer the source from its content. Option B: treat the observation as a lossy projection and require a discriminator with two low-overlap readings, a non-updating anchor and a recorded lineage. What each leads to: A confuses the reading with the source and produces a confident narrative that cannot be checked from outside; B returns a shape, a refuter and an honest `gap` for the case until an instrument pays.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** The difference between two periods' surviving records. Arity: two or more conditions (E1, E2, plus research interest, classification, digitization). Symmetry: the observation is symmetric across its candidate sources, so a reading cannot choose the source; the causal chain is ordered (standard → production → discard or burial → survival → present reading). Scale: per document, genre and subset, and the two explanations act at different scales (frame-wide convention versus material channel). Fig 1 :19 indexes R per slot; Fig 9 :55-58 makes it a union compound.
- **Domain 1, past standards frame.** Actors: inquirers, genres, explicit methodological rules. Currency: what counted as a good reason or proof. Anchor: the standard actually in force, read from internal argument structure and admission rules, never from the historian's present preference. Question type: frame or convention change. Verdict `specified`, triangulated.
- **Domain 2, survival channel.** Actors: media, archives, weather, war, curators. Currency: what persisted. Anchor: material facts that do not update on interpretation, dating, provenance, stratigraphy, documented destruction. Question type: channel decay and habitat selection. Verdict `specified`, triangulated.
- **Domain 3, present research frame.** Actors: historians, their questions and vocabularies. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so its independence is zero. Verdict `general` structural, single-source on the figure side.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); index per slot before aggregating; refuse the naive identification before minting a cause (Formula step 2); run the two tracks separately (Fig 6 :31); import instruments from a differing closure (Fig 6 :23); range both readings against the anchor (Fig 2 :29, :46); record the lineage (Fig 13 :127-130).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union compound. The surviving record is a lossy reading, and a standards change and a survival change can write the same reading, so the record cannot settle its own source. What survives is the shape of a discriminator: per-slot description; a frame track and a channel track kept separate; two low-overlap readings of argument structure and material preservation; an anchor that does not update on interpretation; provenance for every imported instrument; a refusal of the naive identification before any cause is minted; and an explicit `gap` for the case verdict. Standards do have a history, and record loss really does distort, but the framework returns the test that distinguishes them, not a verdict about any named period. Mode `specified` on the separator and per-domain claims, `general` on the structure, `gap` on the case, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes its refuter before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a finished historical narrative instead of a discriminator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The separator can be narrowed or withdrawn condition by condition without touching the figure derivation. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and the declared `gap` stays a gap rather than a retracted verdict.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument for a named period, a demonstrated move of the chosen anchor under interpretation, the absence of any trace left by a real standards change, a preservation model that fails to bite where survival demonstrably changed, or a flat per-slot residual everywhere would move or break the verdict. Until one of these fires, the result stands as `candidate`.
