# Union, metamerism and order in the standards-versus-survival question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Given an observed difference between two periods' surviving records, is the underlying change a rule change (E1) or a material survival change (E2)? Three questions are owed: (1) is the split a partition or a union; (2) are the two periods, read from the same surviving record, isomers or something else; (3) in what order must the discriminating operations run?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- Atoms held for the descriptive side: the observed record elements (document, genre, citation practice, explicit method statement, material support, archive context). Bonds held: sequence (→) for the production-to-survival chain, comparison (⋛) for the threshold between two candidate sources, negation (¬) for the refusal of the naive identification.
- No new atom is available without import. The preservation model, the reference class and the independent dating come from outside this closure, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).
- R by arity, symmetry and scale: two or more conditions, observation symmetric across candidate sources, causal chain ordered, scale per document and per period.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

E1 and E2 are not mutually exclusive. A change in what counts as evidence can change what gets copied and kept; a change in survival can make a period look as though its standards changed. The intersection is a compound. A partition diagram with two clean boxes is therefore wrong. The correct structure is a union with a named overlap, and the overlap needs its own loop and its own ground.

**Falsifier (written before the run):** if a case can be found in which the two conditions are demonstrably independent and never co-occur, the union reading narrows to a partition for that case and the compound loop is not owed there.

### Q2: Same record, different source: isomer or metamer?

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

A period's surviving record is a molecule whose atoms are the documents and genres we can still read. Under E1, the bond between those atoms is a changed admission rule; under E2, it is a changed survival filter. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is the **isomer** shape, Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

But there is a second, sharper name for it. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source." That is **metamerism**: several underlying sources give the same reading. Isomer and metamer are the same structural fact at two descriptions. Isomer says the formula hides the arrangement; metamer says the reading hides the source. Both are triangulated here (Fig 12 plus the essay, though the essay shares the framework author's pool, so the figure carries the verdict).

**Falsifier:** if the bond type were recoverable from the surviving atoms alone, the isomer and metamer readings would both fail, and F1 of `01` would be refuted. This is the first condition in the pre-registered refuter.

### Q3: Functional group versus isomer

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two periods that both show a surviving record after a loss event share a **functional group**: material transmission through a lossy channel. The qualifier (which standard, which survival regime) says which member. Calling them isomers is justified only if the atom set is held fixed and the bond differs. Calling them a functional group is the weaker, safer claim when the atom sets themselves differ, as they usually do when whole genres are lost. The solution keeps both readings and does not promote the stronger one without a ground check.

### Q4: Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 3 :29: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."

The discriminating chain, in dependency order:

1. **Describe the residual in effect terms** (Fig 9 :27-28). Without this, any frame will seem to fit. This is the link most often missing in historical arguments, and it has no standard instrument in most of the literature.
2. **Index per slot** (Fig 1 :19, :28). Aggregate "more rigorous" scores destroy the signal.
3. **Refuse the naive identification** (Formula step 2; Operators :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"). "The record shows a standards shift" is refused as a source claim, not explained.
4. **Run the two tracks separately** (Fig 6 :31): internal argument structure for the standards track; material provenance and preservation for the survival track.
5. **Bring the outside instruments** (Fig 6 :32, "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."). Dating, stratigraphy and preservation curves already return something that fails to resolve; they are extended, not designed from scratch.
6. **Range both readings against a non-updating anchor** (Fig 2 :29, :45, :46). Only then is agreement a verdict.
7. **Record the lineage** (Fig 13 :127-130). The order is not recoverable from the conclusion; it is recoverable from the record.

A reader who starts at step 5 because that is where the impressive instruments are, has spent effort above a break at step 1. Fig 3 :23: "A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

### Q5: The two currencies do not add

Fig 12 :125-127: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them."

In this question, the descriptive currency is the compression of the observed difference into a claim ("standards rose", "record decayed"); the action currency is the work of obtaining a date, a provenance, a preservation curve, an independent collection. A historian who counts only descriptions sees a tidy story and misses the shortfall. A historian who counts only actions mistakes excavation volume for a verdict. Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies "coverage, and compression achieved" and "predicates minted, and credibility accrued" as frame-internal quantities. Both readings are required, and summing them is the named error.

### Q6: What crosses the membrane

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

The taphonomic reference class and the Signor-Lipps confidence interval cross into this question as **methods**, not as recordings of a particular extinction or a particular archive. What must not cross is the conclusion "this period's shift was real" copied from another field's case. The round trip carries a gap R_T (Fig 4 :30): what the imported method can hold at its coarser level is not what the local archive contains, and the gap must be measured, not assumed zero.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. The same surviving record under two different bonds is an isomer, and under two different sources it is a metamer; both names mark the same limit, that the reading does not determine the source. The weaker functional-group claim applies where the atom sets differ. The chain runs describe, index, refuse, separate tracks, import instruments, range against an anchor, record lineage, in that dependency order. The two currencies, description and action, are held apart. No case verdict is returned.
