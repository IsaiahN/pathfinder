## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of a claim you can state before you can settle it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, "a discriminator must use at least two low-overlap readings and a non-updating anchor" is a shape. "The shift happened in 1680 because of the Royal Society" is a name, and the framework cannot return it.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place." The paradox is that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it."

Applied here: describe the observed difference between periods in effect terms (which genres, which claims, which subsets), before reaching for either "standards changed" or "records were lost." Otherwise, Fig 9 :27-28, any frame you pick will seem to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

For this question, importing the Signor-Lipps confidence-interval method and the taphonomic reference class from palaeontology and archaeology is legitimate only with the record of where they came from and a shadow test: do they explain a gap that the historian's existing method could not? They do, because they predict a pattern the documentary reading alone cannot.

## 4. The governing essay here is *The Hard Problem is About Source*

The question is a source-discrimination question in exactly the essay's sense. An instrument reads green but "cannot comment on the source." Metamerism: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The essay's conclusion: "This is why the True Hard Problem is about determining the Source." And: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Map it directly: the surviving record is the reading. "Standards changed" and "survival changed" are two sources that give the same reading. The historian cannot settle the source by staring harder at the reading. An instrument that discriminates sources is owed. "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

Fig 6 :34: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." Fortunately something is already returning a failing reading: dating, provenance, stratigraphy, preservation curves, and the comparison of well-preserved against poorly preserved collections. These are the edges to extend from, and the world sources in `01` section F7 confirm that such instruments have worked in neighbouring fields.

## 5. Why "the record itself tells us" is the specific forbidden error

The essay's metamerism point and Fig 8 :50-52 combine:

- The reading does not determine the source.
- "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

A historian who says "the documents became more rigorous, therefore standards rose" has named the source from the reading. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. The correct form stays as a shape until a source-discriminating instrument returns.

## 6. Correction to my own §3 and §5 (kept visible)

I overstated the "shape, not a name" rule in sections 3 and 5. The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." It governs a primitive not held in the library. Dating, provenance, stratigraphy, preservation models and comparative collections are not missing primitives invented for this question; they are already available bindings from palaeontology, archaeology and source criticism. Naming them is a **provenanced import**, not the forbidden invention of a new primitive and not a wall-moving atom (Fig 6 :21, "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"). What the framework cannot do is name which source actually produced a given period's record. That remains a `gap`. Both the corrected reading and the overstatement are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A claim that a famous historian endorsed the standards-shift reading is a budget to try it, not a settlement. The proper ask is for an independent check of the separator.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." LLM output is a simulacrum. So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the historical question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 8. Effect-description discipline for outreach

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* supplies the rule for first contact: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." So the outreach file sends the shape claim and its refuter, and asks what the recipient would expect to see under each candidate source, before showing the framework. It also notes the drift hazard: two people who only check against each other drift together and nobody notices, which is Fig 2 :34 and the Daston "hall of mirrors" warning from the field itself.
