# Expert verification packet - strong-gravity tests of GR

**What this is:** a claim in a form a gravitational-physics researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the design claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle whether a departure exists. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Near-black-hole observations can serve as an independent anchor for testing GR, because the measurement does not change its answer when GR's prediction changes. The prediction-observation gap must be read per observable slot (inspiral phasing, ringdown modes, shadow geometry, polarisation, propagation speed), never as a single average, because a global near-zero residual can hide one live slot. A departure from GR would be a refusal of a held term, not another fitted parameter, and the refuter must be written before the run. Published null results are corroboration at a given sensitivity, not proof of GR. The framework does not name a departure or decide whether one exists; that is a ground question left open.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 1

- **Statement:** the anchor is the measurement, not GR; the residual is per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **What to check:** is the near-black-hole observable genuinely independent of GR's predicted waveform, and do the opened GWTC-3 tests actually report per-observable results rather than one global goodness-of-fit?
- **Refuter:** show that a candidate observable shifts when the GR prediction is changed (it is a frame, not an anchor); or exhibit a field practice in which an averaged statistic, not a per-slot residual, is the published verdict on a departure.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable by a researcher against the two instruments and the catalogs

- **Statement:** the strong-gravity observables are isomers over a shared Kerr atom set, so the arrangement of evidence carries information; null results are careful non-detections, not proofs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** can ringdown, shadow and X-ray observables (all built on the Kerr atom set) respond differently to the same departure? Do the opened papers phrase their results as "no evidence for violations" and "no evidence in support of physics beyond GR"? (GW150914, GWTC-3; EHT M87 abstract: *"Overall, the observed image is consistent with expectations for the shadow of a Kerr black hole as predicted by general relativity."*)
- **Refuter:** show that every strong-gravity observable responds identically to every departure, so arrangement carries no information; or show that a null result is legitimately a proof of GR's correctness rather than corroboration at a given sensitivity.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** a departure is a negation of a held term, and the refusal is written before the mint.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 - *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: *"REFUTED - a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*
- **What to check:** can a strong-gravity analysis register a departure without first stating which GR term would be refused, or does it merely fit another parameter and accumulate?
- **Refuter:** show a case where a genuine departure was identified with no pre-stated refusal criterion and no post-hoc criterion was introduced; or show that negation is not required because accumulation suffices.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that GR is correct, and not that it is wrong. No ground reading is claimed.
- Not that a departure has been detected. No slot residual was measured by this agent.
- Not that a null result proves GR. Fig 8 :39 and the Popper entry (SEP) agree.
- Not that any named modified-gravity theory is the answer. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). A specific model is a provenanced import, not a derivation.
- Not that all strong-gravity observables are the same test. They are isomers over a shared atom set, with the falsifier in C2.
- Not grounded. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

1. A single slot returns a persistent, non-trending residual under a low-overlap independent instrument. That fires the refusal and reopens the stop.
2. Two observables sharing the Kerr atom set are shown to respond differently to the same departure. That settles the isomer claim at the test level.
3. A combination of polarisation, dispersion and echo measurements breaks the degeneracy among theories giving the same QNM reading. That is the spectrometer analogue.
4. Any of the four standing refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 fires.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30 and :35, Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 8 :39, Fig 12 :117, Fig 5 :53 and Operators :58. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: do the opened papers say "no evidence," and can ringdown and shadow respond differently to one departure?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate among theories giving the same QNM reading, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
