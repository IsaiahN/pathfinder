## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: strong gravity can test GR if you measure per slot against an anchor that does not move, write the refuter first, and treat null results as corroboration.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete test proposal, for example: combine GW ringdown QNM deviations with EHT shadow diameter and an X-ray polarisation measurement, then fit a specific modified-gravity parameter.

**`R_T` is nonzero.** At least four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **refusal requirement**: a fitted modified-gravity parameter accumulates rather than refuses (Fig 5 :53, Operators :58), so a concrete test can silently drop the negation;
- the **no-proof-of-absence limit**: a concrete bound is easily reported as "GR confirmed" (Fig 8 :39);
- the **independence arithmetic**: a combined fit can pool GWTC data with an EHT prior and count the result as two instruments when it is partly one pool (Fig 2 :40);
- the **D3 gap**: a named modified-gravity theory is an import with a source gate and shadow test, not something the shorthand derives (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the anchor condition and the per-slot residual requirement. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
