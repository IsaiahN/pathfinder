# Union, order and analogues in the strong-gravity test

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles several conditions: several observable slots, several instruments and several possible departure types. Is this one question or several? If several, what is the order of testing, and what analogues govern "same reading, different source"?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms plus the operational terms of a strong-gravity test (residual, slot, anchor, instrument, threshold, refusal, ground). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without an import from the physics frame. Partition or union is decided below, not assumed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is this one question or a bundle?

*The Library of Babel*, read this session, names the failure directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

Fig 9 :54-55: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* And :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The strong-gravity question is a **union**, not a single hard question and not a clean partition. A departure could appear in the ringdown slot, the shadow slot or both; a single instrument clearing one slot leaves the intersection (a departure visible to two observables) standing. So the residual must be unbundled into per-slot loops before any verdict is stated.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single statistic existed whose vanishing was both necessary and sufficient for the absence of any departure in all slots, the union reading would be wrong. Berti et al's review, which catalogs distinct strong-field predictions and distinct bounds, is world corroboration against that. Triangulated for the union; single-source for the figure law.

### Q2 - What is the order of the test?

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Dependency order for each slot:

1. An instrument returns a reading (Fig 6 :33). No reading, no edge to extend (Fig 6 :34-35).
2. A vocabulary holds terms for the reading (templates, QNM basis, shadow model). Fig 3 :6-7.
3. An objective states what would count as a departure: the pre-registered refuter.
4. A plan chooses the comparison and the instrument.
5. The result is carried with provenance.

Formula step 2 adds a separate order constraint: *"BROKEN · mechanism / a mint is owed"* and *"REFUTED / a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."* A refusal comes before a mint. Applied here: before proposing a modified-gravity model (a mint), one must first state which held GR term would be refused if the residual persists. This is the order the outreach draft follows.

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The objective link (step 3) is the one most often assumed rather than measured in this field; that is the break to watch.

**Falsifier:** if a community can show that a departure was correctly identified without a pre-stated refusal criterion, and that the criterion was not smuggled in after the fact, the refusal-before-mint order is not necessary here.

### Q3 - Are the observables isomers, a functional group, or something else?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Consider three strong-gravity observables: GW ringdown quasi-normal modes, EHT shadow diameter, X-ray reflection features. All three range against the same Kerr atom set (mass, spin, metric structure). Under Fig 12's definition:

- They share the atoms and differ in the arrangement of evidence and instrument. That makes them **isomers** in the figure sense: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour under a departure. A departure that shifts QNMs but not the shadow is a different substance from one that shifts both.
- They also share a **functional group**: each is a strong-field read of the metric. The group says what kind of test; the qualifier (which observable) says which member.

So both readings apply at different levels, and they are not in conflict. The isomer claim is the stronger, checkable one: if two observables built from the same Kerr atoms can be shown to respond differently to the same departure, the figure's isomer definition is satisfied at the level of the tests. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* GR's Kerr solution is settled at the theory level and becomes an atom for the test level.

**Falsifier:** if every strong-gravity observable responds identically to every departure, so that the arrangement carries no information, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim remains. This is an empirical question for the ground, not for this simulation.

### Q4 - Which analogue governs a positive reading?

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this session, supplies the governing analogue. A color instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* The same reading can come from several sources, and *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: a measured QNM frequency is a projection. It cannot, by itself, name the theory that produced it. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (for example, combining polarisation content, dispersion and echo searches so that several paths no longer give the same reading). This is Fig 6 :29-30 applied: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it."* Refining one observable forever cannot discriminate sources; adding a second kind of observable can.

This analogue is world corroboration, not a figure line. It has low overlap with the physics papers (philosophy of perception versus gravitational-wave data analysis), so its agreement with F5 and F8 of the audit is weak corroboration at best, and it is recorded as such in `09_watson_answer.md`.

## CONCLUSION

The strong-gravity question is a **union** of overlapping per-slot conditions and must be unbundled before search. Each slot's test has a dependency order: reading, vocabulary, objective, plan, carried result, with refusal before mint. The observables stand to one another as Fig 12 **isomers** over the shared Kerr atom set and as members of one **functional group** at the level above. A positive reading is a projection whose source needs a discriminating instrument, not a name supplied by argument. All of this is `specified`, ungrounded, and falsifiable as stated.
