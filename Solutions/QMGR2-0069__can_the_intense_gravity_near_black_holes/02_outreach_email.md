# Outreach draft: check the test design first, then decide about the claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive the reader's own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold.

---

**Subject:** One checkable point about testing GR near black holes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** near-black-hole observations can serve as an independent anchor for testing general relativity, because the measurement does not change its answer when GR's prediction changes. That is not the same as saying GR is correct. The gap between prediction and observation has to be read **per observable slot**, never averaged: inspiral phasing, ringdown quasi-normal modes, shadow geometry, polarisation, propagation speed are separate slots. A global "GR passes" can hide one live slot. And a departure would be a **refusal** of a term GR holds, not another fitted parameter.
>
> **What the figures do not give us:** which departure is right, or whether one exists. That is a ground question, and only a measurement settles it. Published null results (GW150914, GWTC-3, M87 imaging) are corroboration at a given sensitivity, not proof of GR. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this message:**
> 1. a candidate observable is shown to move when GR's predicted waveform is changed, so it is a frame, not an anchor;
> 2. a single slot returns a residual that persists and does not trend down under retesting with an independent instrument, in which case a departure is owed;
> 3. per-slot residuals are shown not to be required, so a global average is enough to locate a departure.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not asking:** I am not asking for trust, for endorsement, or for your name on anything. Agreement from people who share the same evidence pool is worthless as a check. If you work on a different instrument or a different theory, that is exactly the vantage I need.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Local figure text with line numbers (the primary source): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the move:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
