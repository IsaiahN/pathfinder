**Subject:** One checkable claim about "finite or infinite universe", before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the governance claims, `gap` on the physical value):**
>
> 1. "Does the universe continue forever, or is its total extent finite?" is two questions bundled into one. Temporal duration and spatial extent have different anchors. They must be split before either is searched.
> 2. The figures do not return a finite/infinite value. After a genuine re-census, no figure line governs the global extent of the physical universe. The honest verdict on that value is `gap`, not silence dressed as an answer.
> 3. What the figures do govern is the form of any admissible answer. A frame whose reach is bounded by a horizon cannot promote "no edge found" to "infinite". Three separate figure mechanisms say so ("So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence", Fig 8 :39; "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room", Fig 9 :44; "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same", Fig 6 :27).
> 4. "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions" (Fig 8 :40-41). A finite compact topology whose fundamental domain lies inside the horizon is searchable. A global value with no signature at any resolution is not. Merging the two produces a wrong experiment.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the universe is finite, that it is infinite, that the figures settle cosmology, or that a precision flatness bound decides topology. Geometry constrains but does not dictate topology. I am also not claiming the framework's "no top and no infinite regress" lines are about physical space; they are about levels of composition, and I record that as a refused reading.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. a verbatim figure line whose subject is the global physical extent of the universe rather than the reach of a frame;
> 2. a measurement inside the horizon that discriminates a finite compact topology from the infinite simply connected case, or that discriminates eternal expansion from recollapse;
> 3. a demonstration that spatial extent and temporal duration share one anchor, so the split was a mis-census;
> 4. a demonstration that the "no top / no last route" lines are about the physical metric, which would make the framework a direct answer to the question.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own warning is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and that outputs could be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
