## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on the pitch

1. **Flatness is not infinity.** Luminet 2016: the Planck data *"fit well the simplest model of a zero-curvature, infinite space model"* yet *"remain consistent with more complex shapes such as the spherical Poincare Dodecahedral Space, the flat hypertorus or the hyperbolic Picard horn."* This is the world-side version of F3: a good global fit with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13).
2. **A null search is not an absence proof in practice.** Mota, Makler and Reboucas 2006: repetitions *"could be unobservable"* because of the finite last-scattering surface. Souradeep 2006: geometry *"constrains, but does not dictate"* topology. These are the physical counterparts of Fig 6 :27 and Fig 8 :39. They are corroboration only, never the verdict.
3. **The theory itself leaves the question open.** Reboucas and Gomero 2004: GR *"leaves the global topology of the universe undetermined."* So a figure line that decided the value would be surprising on the physics side too. This makes the F4 strain easier to refuse: it would also conflict with the standard local-metric reading.

All these sources are independent of the framework's author. Their agreement with the figures is therefore weak corroboration of the *method*, not of the Tether's authority. None of them decides the finite/infinite value.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the split, the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the article.
- **Fig 2 :35** — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not select recipients for likely agreement.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A reply is a conversation, never corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Spend contact cost on people whose public work touches the residual, not on platform size.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the split and the shape claim, (2) the pre-registered refuter, (3) ask what they would count as a failure, (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
