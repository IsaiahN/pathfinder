# Isomers, products and order: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. Simonton's multiplicative product (Crossref abstract, opened 2026-11-23) is a low-overlap world corroborator for one sub-claim, not a settlement. See `05_what_shape_means.md` §7 for the simulacra caveat.

**Scope.** Files `01` and `05` establish the residual, the census and the shape. This file answers the three supporting questions the README assigns it: why a weighted sum and a guard product are different objects, which works are isomers over a fixed atom set, and what order the evaluation chain runs in. Each question carries its own falsifier.

## RESIDUAL

A committee has three readings of one work, originality N, usefulness U and cultural significance S. The readings can come apart. The question asks how to evaluate the work when they conflict. Option A converts each reading to a number and adds them with weights. Option B keeps three verdicts with different anchors, refuses the collapse where the units differ, and owns the residual decision.

This file does not re-argue the verdict. It asks what kind of object each option is, so that the verdict in `07` rests on structure rather than on preference.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (the atoms and bonds in play)

- **Atoms held:** the three criterion readings {N, U, S} and the decision D. These are ordinary vocabulary from the question, already bound in the evaluators' frame. They are not primitives the framework derived and not imports that move a wall (Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; see `05` §5b).
- **Bonds held:** the seven binary bonds, three symmetric (`+`, `∥`, `≡`) and four ordered (`→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛`), plus the unary negation `¬`. Which bond holds is not recoverable from the operands (Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*).
- **Currencies held:** description bits for N against a library, actions for U against a ground, contacts and cascades for S in a habitat (Fig 12 :125-126, :130). They are in different units and do not add.

No new atom is introduced below. Every claim is a composition over what the question and the figures already supply.

## Q1. Why a weighted sum and a product are different objects

### The weighted sum is a symmetric bond over three scores

A rubric of the form `w_N·N + w_U·U + w_S·S` is a conjunction `+` over three converted scores with authored coefficients. Under the figure's bond rule that is a symmetric join:

Fig 12 :92: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges."*

With three criteria there are six orderings and one term. Listing originality first or last carries no arithmetical information. The only things that differentiate one committee's score from another's are the coefficients, and those are contributed by the committee. The appearance of a rich measurement is the overcharge the figure names.

### The two currencies are not addable, so the sum is the named error

Fig 12 :125-126:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units"**

Fig 12 :130:

> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**

Novelty is priced in bits against a declared prior-art closure. Usefulness is priced in actions against a ground that does not update on the judge. Significance is priced in contacts and cascades in a habitat. A 1 to 10 scale makes them share a numeral system without sharing a unit. The resulting rank has no referent. This is structural, not a complaint about unpopularity of rubrics: the figure identifies the arithmetic as a category error when the terms are in different units.

### A guard product is a different kind of object

Fig 5 :33 and :41:

> **"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."**
> **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."**

A product is non-compensable. A zero in any factor yields zero, so no amount of the other factors buys the term. Applied to the evaluation question, that is the difference between saying a work is original but useless, which keeps both readings visible, and saying its high usefulness compensates for a failed novelty guard, which erases the failure.

**Strain, named and kept.** Fig 5's product runs over SUPPORT, REACHABILITY and NOVELTY, all of them on the mint side. It is not a statement that originality, usefulness and significance must be multiplied. Using non-compensation across the three criteria is an *analogue* drawn from the gate structure, and the amount it can bear is limited. Simonton 2026 does define personal creativity as *"the multiplicative product of originality, utility, and surprise"* (Crossref abstract, opened 2026-11-23), which is a field arriving at a product from a different direction, but one field's definition is not a cross-domain license. The claim carried forward is narrow: originality is a gate at zero (Fig 6 :22: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*), and a gate is not a weight. Nothing here licenses a universal multiplication coefficient for all three.

### The useful distinction

A weighted sum is a convention that manufactures a rank. A guard product is a structural fact about what can be composed at all. Conflating them is how a committee ends up scoring itself: Fig 1 :27 says *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

## Q2. Which works are isomers over a fixed atom set

### The definition

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fix the atom set to {N, U, S} as evaluated readings of a work. A work is then a molecule: the three readings plus the bonds between them. Two works with the same three scores are not variants of one thing unless the same bond holds too.

### Three works with identical scoreboards

Consider three works each scored 9 of 10 on originality, usefulness and cultural significance:

- **W1, production chain.** `N ⇒ U ⇒ S`. The novelty produces the use, the use produces the significance. Fig 12 :76-77 defines `⇒` as *"production"* / *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*.
- **W2, flat conjunction.** `N + U + S`. Three independent properties coexist. `+` is symmetric, so the six spellings are one term and the scoreboard cannot tell W2 from a reordered W2.
- **W3, threshold comparison.** `S ⋛ (N, U)`. Significance is a threshold relation over the other two readings. Fig 12 :86-87 defines `⋛` as *"comparison"* / *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.

Under Fig 12 :117 these are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their behaviour at the ground differs. W1 loses its significance if the novel mechanism is removed; W2 keeps its three unrelated strengths; W3 crosses or fails a threshold depending on how N and U are weighted together. A scoring sheet that records only (9, 9, 9) has recorded the ingredients and lost the substance.

### Why the loss is invisible

Fig 12 :14-16:

> **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"**
> **"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost"**

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

This is the concrete reason a checklist of three criteria records half of the evaluation. The half that is lost is the half a committee most often argues about, because arguments about whether originality *produces* usefulness or merely *accompanies* it are arguments about the bond, not about the scores.

### What is not an isomer claim

Two works that share only one of the three readings are not isomers. They are two members of a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The isomer claim requires the full shared atom set. A committee that says two works are "the same kind of creative" on the strength of one shared score is making the functional-group claim, not the isomer claim, and should say so.

## Q3. What order the evaluation chain runs in

### Order is dependency, not importance

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it. The order below is derived from what each step consumes, not from what a committee finds convenient.

### The chain

1. **Bind the sought-for shape before the run.** Formula step "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM, write down what an answer would look like, and let it accrue a residual. `01` §0 did this. This is why the refuter can be written before the verdict.
2. **Unbundle the union before scoring.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* You cannot diagnose a composite score before you have said which conditions hold together.
3. **Settle the novelty gate against a declared Γ.** State the prior-art set before searching. Fig 5 :24-25: *"is it actually new?"* / *"it is not already in the library"*. A zero at novelty stops the mint; it is not traded against usefulness (Fig 5 :41).
4. **Settle usefulness against an instrument already returning something.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A committee's impression of usefulness is downstream of the thing it is meant to measure.
5. **Enumerate the habitat for significance.** Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* / *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* First-order praise is not the cascade; the DDT case in Fig 11 :52-53 is the warning.
6. **Set the checking arrangement last.** Which frames check which readings, and what the verifier may see. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
7. **Carry a per-work verdict to a field-level policy only through a measured round trip.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap R_T is not zero by assumption.

### The broken link

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Most evaluation effort lands at step 7 (scoring) with step 2 (what would count as progress for this criterion here) assumed. Fig 3 :25-26 names why: *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing, so a more elaborate rubric cannot rescue a missing objective.

## CONCLUSION

The weighted sum and the guard product are different objects under Fig 12: one is a symmetric join over converted scores with authored coefficients, the other is a non-compensable structural gate; the figure names summing incomparable currencies as the error, not the fix. Works with the same three scores can be isomers over the fixed atom set {N, U, S}, distinguished by which bond holds, and a scoreboard that omits the bond records half the evaluation. The dependency order runs unbundle, gate, settle, enumerate, arrange, round trip, with the objective link before the scoring link. All three claims are `specified`, ungrounded, and none returns a weight vector.

## FALSIER (written before the run, per `01` §4)

1. **Sum refuter.** Exhibit a common unit in which novelty bits, settled-use actions and habitat cascade are already measured before weighting, with a conversion that pays for itself under the minimum-description-length bargain. Then Fig 12 :130 narrows from a ban on summing to a ban on naïve summing.
2. **Isomer refuter.** Show two works with identical ingredient scores and identical outcomes under the seven-bond test whose evaluations still differ at the ground with no new atom imported. Then the bond is not carrying the information Fig 12 assigns it.
3. **Order refuter.** Exhibit instrumented objectives for all three criteria already in routine use whose readings are not pooled with the evaluator's own preference. Then the break is elsewhere and the Q3 chain narrows to institutions that still lack those instruments.
4. **Gate refuter.** Show a work that fails the novelty guard against a declared corpus and still settles and composes as the same term with no import and no change of anchor. Then originality is a weight after all.

A reported failure of any of these is more useful than an endorsement.
