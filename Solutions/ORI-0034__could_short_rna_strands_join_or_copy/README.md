**Question:** Could short RNA strands join or copy one another often enough to support evolution?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 47

**Section:** 4. RNA-first and genetic information

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the figure-derived constraints, `gap` on the literal frequency question (how often is "often enough"). Nothing here is `grounded`: the figures narrow the shape an answer must have and cannot measure a chemical rate.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The residual in effect terms

What fails to resolve is the step from "a particular RNA reaction happens in a flask" to "a sustained evolutionary system." The question names three coupled effects: strands joining into longer strands, strands copying one another, and both happening at a rate and fidelity where heritable variation persists and selection has purchase. Option A treats the strand as the whole answer. Option B treats the strand plus its habitat as the answer. What each leads to: A asks a chemistry question and mistakes it for an evolution question; B asks the correct contact question and then reports an honest `gap` where the figures cannot supply a number. An answer would look like a shape with named constraints and a stated refuter, bound here as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The ISSOL contact page publishes them; this folder does not copy them. Institutional and opt-in channels are used instead.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement is not corroboration.
2. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
3. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The two-stage offer records the outside verdict before showing any theory.

Legally, unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
