## 0. The claim under audit and the residual

The question: could short RNA strands join or copy one another often enough to support evolution?

Stated as an effect, not a topic: three conditions must hold together for the answer to be yes. (1) Joining: short strands must compose into longer strands (ligation). (2) Copying: a strand must direct the formation of a complement, and the complement must direct the formation of a copy, so the product re-enters as input. (3) Rate and fidelity: the copying must be accurate often enough for heritable variation to persist and selective differences to accumulate. "Often enough" is not decoration; it is the quantity that decides the answer.

The source file itself names the gap one line below the question (origins of life.txt line 55): the gap between showing that particular RNA reactions can occur in the lab and establishing that a complete, sustained RNA-based evolutionary system could arise under plausible environmental conditions. That is the residual in effect terms.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a shape with three named constraints, a clear subject (strand alone versus strand plus habitat), a per-domain verdict, an honest `gap` where the figures cannot give a number, and a written refuter. That bound shape is the thing refutable by the pre-registered test in §4.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: three conditions (joining, copying, rate/fidelity plus selection), which are overlapping rather than independent. Symmetry: copying is ordered and directional (template to complement to copy), not a symmetric join of two equals; spatial localization breaks the symmetry of who copies whom. Scale: molecular chemistry at the bottom, population and generational turnover at the top, with the bridge between them being the thing in question.

**Partition or union.** This is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Joining without copying gives chemistry without heredity; copying without a rate gives a one-off event; neither alone is a harder case of the other. The compound needs its own loop.

Every domain has a nameable anchor, so each is a domain. A domain without one would be refused here, not named.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order is D1 feedstock availability (import, not search) then D2 joining into chains then D2 copying with fidelity and strand separation then D3 variation plus selection. The link most likely to be assumed rather than measured is the D2 to D3 bridge: from strands that copy to a system that registers progress.

**Strain test policy.** If a figure line fits only by forcing it, it governs another quantity and the census is redone. Two strains are named and resolved in §3 F2 and F4; neither is smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one hard question (decisive for method)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

*The Library of Babel* (essay opened 2026-11-23) says the same thing in search terms: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

The residual unbundles into D1 joining, D2 copying and D3 evolution. A single lever (for example, a faster ligation reaction) clears the region where only D1 binds and leaves the D2 and D3 intersections standing. The "often enough" clause is the intersection, not a footnote. Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the Babel essay, low overlap between a figure about unions and an essay about search vocabulary). Refuter: show the three conditions are independent so that a partition, not a union, is the correct structure.

### F2 - Joining makes a chain; a chain cannot close a replication cycle by itself

Fig 12 :48: "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A linear RNA backbone is exactly Fig 12's degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. If the only available relation is the phosphodiester backbone, the product of copying cannot re-enter as input in the way a cycle requires. The cycle closes only because a **second** bond type exists: Watson-Crick base pairing lets a template recruit a complement, and the complement becomes a template in turn. So the minimal replication loop is a two-strand cycle, not a single strand replicating itself.

**Strain test and re-census.** Reading the cycle line as a refutation of RNA replication strains the figure, because real RNA does copy with base pairing. Re-census: the chain line governs the single molecule (one backbone bond type); the cycle line governs the template/complement process (a two-operand relation with a second bond type). Different quantities, no contradiction. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated after re-census (Fig 12 chain plus cycle; corroborated at the ground by Robertson and Joyce's account of template-directed synthesis and the self-structure problem, which is a world source and cannot decide the figure reading). Refuter: exhibit a single linear strand, with no complement and no second bond type, whose output re-enters as input to copy itself. That would break the figure reading and the claim goes with it.

### F3 - A replicator must be a catalyst that survives its own use

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." The figure adds that spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses and that nothing records which a term is.

A strand consumed in the act of copying cannot found a lineage. The evolving unit must be the regenerating kind. World corroboration (not a figure line): Robertson and Joyce describe net rate of production as error-free copies minus decomposition of parents; Szostak's eightfold path lists strand separation and reactivation as two of the eight obstacles, because a copied strand locked in a duplex is a dead end. Verdict: `specified`, single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated by two low-overlap world sources. Refuter: a system in which the template is destroyed on each copy and evolution nevertheless continues for many generations without any regenerating part.

### F4 - "Often enough" is the product of two positive factors, and the figures give the shape, not the number

Fig 7 :26: "progress per handoff: R = h² × S". Fig 7 :38: "the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff". Fig 7 :39: "the ground’s pressure on the room". Fig 7 :33: "→ variation inside, selection between". Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Progress needs both a fidelity term and a ground-pressure term. Zero either and the product is zero: a copier with no fidelity produces noise, and a variant with no differential survival has no selection. This is the figure-shaped answer to "often enough": the rate question is really two questions, fidelity and pressure, and both must be positive and sustained.

**Strain test.** Fig 7 is about rooms and handoffs. Applying it to molecular generations is a cross-scale instance, not a literal fit. Re-census: the figure governs the structure of progress across any handed-off bracket; at the chemical scale the bracket is the copied strand and the pressure is differential survival. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." So the shape is `general`, the instance is `specified`, and the number is a `gap`. The error threshold number is a **world** result (Eigen, via Robertson and Joyce and the quasispecies literature), not a figure line: for fidelity q over n condensations, an error-free copy requires q to the power n to exceed the selective advantage s. Verdict: `general`/`specified`, triangulated for the two-factor shape (Fig 7 plus Fig 4's round-trip rule), `gap` for the literal frequency. Refuter: sustained evolution with one factor demonstrably zero, i.e. every copy random (h² = 0) or no differential survival (S = 0). If that happens, the two-factor shape is wrong.

### F5 - Evolution is agent-and-habitat, not agent alone

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* corroborates from a different frame: "Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground. When there is no one in the Ground Maintainer seat, the environment is the only selector."

So the question as written carries a category error if "strands" is read as "strands alone." A short strand is capable only together with a habitat that supplies activated monomers, energy, cycling conditions and spatial localization. Szostak's eightfold path makes the same point from chemistry: the emergence of Darwinian evolution required spatial localization so a useful RNA could accrue an advantage for itself. Verdict: `general` (structural), triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 7 plus the RSI essay, three low-overlap routes). Refuter: sustained evolution in a system whose contact with its habitat is held strictly constant while the strand is improved in isolation.

### F6 - More of the same strands does not add a new atom

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 13 :118: "still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."

More short strands drawn from the same four monomers explore more arrangements of the same atoms. That can help search (Formula step 7 asks SEARCHED first, cheapest), but it cannot manufacture a new monomer kind or a new bond type. Long-term evolvability, meaning longer and more functional sequences, needs an import with a source gate and a shadow test: a feedstock chemistry, a mineral catalyst, or a prebiotic activation pathway. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." World corroboration: Robertson and Joyce on montmorillonite and eutectic phases; Sutherland-style pathways reported in the RNA world survey. Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 8 plus Fig 13 plus Fig 6). Refuter: a closed monomer set that yields a new monomer kind by arrangement alone.

### F7 - The ground question is a measurement, not a further argument

Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

The literal "often enough" is decided by measurements of fidelity, rate and strand separation, not by any amount of figure reasoning. The figure run stops at an honest `gap` and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The world sources disagree with each other about how close chemistry is: Szostak reports average copying errors around 17 percent, reducible toward 10 percent or roughly 5 percent on GC-rich templates, while the threshold for a roughly 50-nucleotide functional RNA is around 2 percent per position. Zhou, Ding and Szostak end with "Whether chemical replication can attain sufficient accuracy remains to be determined." Verdict: `gap` on the number, `general` on the rule that only the ground closes it. Refuter: an experiment that measures a sustained multi-generation copying fidelity and rate under plausible conditions and reports the number. That would move the `gap` toward `grounded` for that specific system.

### F8 - Verification cannot ask for agreement

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Fig 5 :49: "Machinery firing is not the milestone. A running system can mint and still return nothing new, which is exactly what the third guard exists to record."

A public campaign that gathers endorsements for this shape claim would be a mirror chain, not a check. The two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md` sends the residual and the refuter first and records the verdict before any theory is shown. Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 10 plus Fig 5).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single linear strand with one bond type and no complementary partner is shown to copy itself with its output re-entering as input (breaks F2 and Fig 12 :48, :121).
2. Sustained heritable evolution is observed with the product R = h² × S equal to zero, meaning one factor is zero and the other cannot compensate (breaks F4 and Fig 7 :26).
3. Sustained evolution is observed with no change in contact with the habitat: identical feedstock, energy and conditions, strand improved in isolation only (breaks F5 and Fig 11 :40).
4. A repeated search with the same monomer set produces a new monomer kind by arrangement alone (breaks F6 and Fig 8 :32).
5. A domain above is found to have no nameable anchor, so it was not a domain and the census must be redone.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the answer is recomputed from the census. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Keep F1's unbundling; do not answer the question with a single ligation rate.
2. Report the subject correctly: strand plus habitat, never strand alone (F5).
3. Report the replication loop as a two-strand cycle closed by a second bond type, and the evolving unit as a catalyst that survives its use (F2, F3).
4. Report "often enough" as the two-factor shape fidelity times selection, and refuse to supply a number from the figures (F4, F7).
5. Treat feedstock and mineral or activation chemistry as imports with a source gate, not as more of the same search (F6).
6. Send the shape and the refuter, then the links only on request (F8).
