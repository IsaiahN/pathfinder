## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a yes or no does not. The question is a union of joining, copying and rate. Joining makes a chain with one bond type, and a chain cannot close a replication cycle by construction, so copying requires two complementary strands and a second bond type with a catalyst that survives its use. "Often enough" is two positive factors, fidelity and selection pressure, not one rate. The capable subject is strand plus habitat, never strand alone. The figures do not name RNA as the first polymer and do not supply a fidelity number; the literal frequency is an honest `gap`. This is a `general` and `specified` result, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, writes refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The category error (strands alone) and the unsupported number are removable without touching the shape constraints; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An experiment measuring sustained multi-generation copying fidelity and rate under plausible conditions would move the `gap` toward a number; a single one-bond-type chain copying itself with no complement would break the D2 constraint; sustained evolution with the product R = h² × S equal to zero would break the D3 two-factor shape; sustained evolution with habitat contact held constant would break the agent-and-habitat constraint. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
