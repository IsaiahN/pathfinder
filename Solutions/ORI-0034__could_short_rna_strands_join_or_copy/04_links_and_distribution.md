## Wider literature that bears on the residual

Read through the sources above rather than separately opened, and recorded as such:

1. **Eigen's error threshold** is the quantitative form of F4. Robertson and Joyce give it as q to the power n exceeding the superiority s; Szostak restates it as an error rate below the reciprocal of the number of functionally important bases. These are two statements of one result and count as one witness, not two.
2. **Template-directed ligation of short oligomers** is the chemical form of F2. It is attractive because it avoids copying a whole strand in one pass, but it faces substrate supply and fidelity obstacles (Robertson and Joyce). The virtual circular genome model is a concrete instance: short fragments grow by one nucleotide on average and a length-versus-abundance gradient does the rest.
3. **Selection has already been observed once copying exists.** Spiegelman's Monster is the ground case. It does not show that the first replicator arose prebiotically; it shows that after a copying system is supplied, differential replication shrinks and changes the sequence. That distinction matters for the answer's non-claims.
