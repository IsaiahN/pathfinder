# Expert verification packet - the Tether on short RNA strands and evolution

**What this is:** a claim in a form an RNA replication researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the derived constraints, `gap` on the literal frequency. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of three overlapping conditions, not one hard problem: strands joining, strands copying, and both at a rate where heritable variation persists and selection has purchase. Joining makes a chain with one bond type, and a chain cannot close a replication cycle by construction, so copying requires two complementary strands and a second bond type, with the template surviving its own use as a catalyst. "Often enough" is the product of two positive factors, copying fidelity and the ground's pressure on variants; zero either and there is no evolution. The capable subject is the strand plus its habitat, never the strand alone. The figures return that shape, state what would refute it, and stop before the number, which stays an honest `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - structural, checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** a single linear strand (one backbone bond type) cannot close a replication cycle; closure needs a complementary strand and a second bond type. The evolving carrier must be a catalyst that survives its use.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :48 - "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching." Fig 12 :121 - "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :119 - "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."
- **What to check:** is the re-census sound? The chain line governs the single molecule; the cycle line governs the two-strand template process. No contradiction remains after separating them.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single backbone-bonded chain, with no complementary strand and no second bond type, whose output re-enters as input to copy itself; or show a template consumed each round supporting many generations with no regenerating carrier.

### C2 - cross-scale, checkable against the copying and evolution literature

- **Statement:** "often enough" is two positive factors, not one rate. Fig 7 :26 - "progress per handoff: R = h² × S". Fig 7 :33 - "→ variation inside, selection between". The two factors are fidelity and ground pressure (selection).
- **What to check:** does the fidelity-times-pressure shape match what the copying and quasispecies literature actually measures? Robertson and Joyce give q to the power n exceeding s; Szostak gives an error rate below the reciprocal of functionally important bases; the quasispecies literature gives an error threshold beyond which the cloud disperses. These are three statements of one quantitative structure and count as one witness.
- **Refuter:** sustained heritable evolution with one factor demonstrably zero, meaning every copy random (fidelity zero) or no differential survival (pressure zero). If that is observed, the two-factor shape is wrong.

### C3 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 8 and 11

- **Statement:** more of the same strands does not add a new atom, and the capable subject is the strand plus its habitat. Fig 8 :32 - "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 11 :39 - "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 - "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** is feedstock, mineral catalysis and cycling correctly classified as an import with a source gate (Fig 6), rather than as more of the same search (Fig 8)? Is the habitat part of the answer rather than background?
- **Refuter:** a closed monomer set producing a new monomer kind by arrangement alone; or sustained evolution with habitat contact held strictly constant while the strand is improved in isolation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that short RNAs did it, and not that they did not. The figures return a shape, not a name, and a search that finds nothing proves nothing (Fig 9 :44).
- Not a number for the required fidelity or rate. The error threshold is a world result with provenance, not a figure line, and the literal frequency is `gap`.
- Not that RNA was the first genetic polymer, and not a claim about pre-RNA polymers.
- Not that joining plus copying in a flask equals evolution. Selection and localization are habitat conditions.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet.
- Not a claim that this derivation validates itself. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. An external ground is required.

## 5. What would settle it

An experiment, not an argument. Three instruments already returning something:

1. Does copying of arbitrary short sequences reach an error rate below the reciprocal of the functionally important bases? Reported values are around 17 percent average, toward 5 percent on GC-rich templates; the functional threshold is about 2 percent.
2. Can the product become a template repeatedly with strand separation under plausible conditions? The virtual circular genome model is a proposal, untested.
3. Does a copying advantage persist across serial transfers under plausible conditions? Spiegelman's Monster is an instance with supplied replicase, not a prebiotic demonstration.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :48, :119, :121 and Fig 8 :32, Fig 11 :39 to :40 - five short passages.
2. Check C1 against their own knowledge of template chemistry: is base pairing the second bond type that closes the loop, and is the template a catalyst in the figure's sense?
3. Name one measurement that would distinguish an adequate copying fidelity from an inadequate one for a 50-nucleotide functional RNA, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
