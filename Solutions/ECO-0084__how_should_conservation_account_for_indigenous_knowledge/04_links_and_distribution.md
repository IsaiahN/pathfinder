## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending a full extracted dataset before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source links.
- **Fig 2 :40** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is high, because a bad single-perspective rule can erase a live community signal. Independent frames are worth the extra contact cost.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) then the three framework links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground, before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** only if curious, read the theory that produced it. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded and is never pooled with later agreement.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter, declares their own stake and states their own mode.

This follows Fig 10 :52 ("Bring a characterised residual, never the source"), Fig 2 :45 ("Frames range against the ground"), Fig 4 :22 (methods cross up) and `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" (a frame cannot check the direction of its own question).
