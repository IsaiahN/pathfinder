# Union versus partition, independence versus identity, and the scale membrane

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how conservation can account for Indigenous knowledge without treating diverse communities as one perspective. Three supporting derivations are owed: (1) is the residual a partition or a union, and what follows for search; (2) is "different community" the same thing as "independent frame"; (3) what may cross the boundary between local observation and regional decision. Each is derived below with its own falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held for this derivation: slots (communities), residuals (per-slot signals), evidence pools, anchors (measurable outcomes), bonds of union, selection and provenance, and a membrane between scales. No new primitive is introduced. The operator that must be refused is the symmetric `+` that collapses distinct community slots into one term, because Fig 12 :59 gives its test as "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Swapping communities changes this residual.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :58: "It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- The communities in a conservation decision overlap in watershed, species and management history, but their residuals are not identical. That is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Unbundling yields a specific compound with its own loop (its own anchor, its own refuter).
- If it were a strict partition, one lever would clear each slot and no compound would remain. The question's premise (a single perspective being imposed) only makes sense in the union case, because a partition needs no imposed unity.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two communities whose residuals share no condition, no actor, no place and no outcome measure, so the union is empty and the partition reading is correct. If that holds, Q1's union claim fails and the rest of the derivation narrows to a bookkeeping problem.

### Q2 — Independence versus identity

- Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
- Fig 13 :38: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence"
- Fig 13 :39: "Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."
- Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents" (the record, not the label, separates them).
- Two communities with different names and cultures but the same agency survey as their evidence pool are one frame wearing two names. Two communities with disjoint observation histories that reach the same conclusion are corroboration at maximum independence. The measure is the overlap of evidence pools, not the number of identities in the room.
- **Correction to my own first reading:** I initially treated cultural distinctness as sufficient. It is not. This correction is kept visible in `01` section 5 and is part of this derivation, not a quiet fix.
- **Falsifier:** show that two frames drawing on the same report are independent for the residual, or that two frames with disjoint observation histories are not. Either result breaks the rule.

### Q3 — Which operator combines communities?

- Fig 12 :59 gives the symmetric bond test: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."
- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Combining communities under the symmetric `+` bond declares them exchangeable, so k communities have k! spellings and one term. That is exactly the single-perspective error: a bigger space of names, not a bigger vocabulary of evidence.
- The licensed combination is **selection between rooms** (Fig 7 :33: "variation inside, selection between") at a described residual, with provenance for each contribution. Where closures genuinely differ and the residual is described, the union has surplus (Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone"). Where closures overlap, the union adds nothing and the agreement is one pool talking.
- **Falsifier:** show a case where swapping two community contributions leaves the conservation prediction and its residual unchanged. Then `+` is the correct operator and the single-perspective move is not an error for that case.

### Q4 — The scale membrane

- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- A local observation record is a description of one occasion in one habitat. It cannot be carried unchanged to a regional decision. What crosses is the method of enumerating contact and keeping provenance. The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap `R_T` is measured, not assumed zero. `11_audit.md` performs that round trip qualitatively for this solution.
- **Falsifier:** show a local observation record that lifts unchanged to a regional decision with a measured round-trip gap of zero.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union of overlapping conditions and must be unbundled before search (Q1). Independence is measured by evidence-pool overlap, not cultural identity (Q2). The correct combination is selection between distinct rooms at a described residual, with provenance, not a symmetric `+` that collapses slots (Q3). Only methods cross the local-to-regional membrane, and the round-trip gap must be measured (Q4). Four falsifiers are stated, each capable of breaking its part of the derivation. After re-census, the one live strain is that Q3's "selection" and the world source Two-Eyed Seeing's "use both eyes together" sound opposed; re-census shows they are not, because Two-Eyed Seeing keeps two distinct eyes (low overlap) while Fig 7 rejects a room that matches the last one. Both require difference. They would conflict only if the two eyes were collapsed into one, which is the error being refused here.
