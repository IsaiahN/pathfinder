# Outreach draft — check the output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 are stated in the first message. The offer collapses if the framework is shown before a verdict is recorded.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about "the Indigenous perspective" in conservation

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in conservation method. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a conservation decision is a prediction about an ecological residual: what is changing, what is at risk, which intervention pays. The evidence about that residual is held by several distinct Indigenous and local communities, each with a place-specific observation record and its own account of a good outcome. Collapsing those communities into one input slot called "the Indigenous perspective" destroys the signal that says where to look. Fig 1 :19 puts the rule compactly: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 adds: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
>
> **What I am asking you to check:** is the one-slot move actually an averaging error in the decisions you know, or is there a case where pooling communities preserves a live per-community signal at equal cost? A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before I sent this:**
> 1. Pooling N communities into one knowledge input preserves a live per-community residual at equal or lower cost than N separate slots.
> 2. A blended single-perspective input selects actions against a measured ground as well as separately maintained independent community frames, with no loss of the novel signal.
> 3. Two communities drawing on the same evidence pool are independent for this residual.
> 4. A single community's observation record lifted unchanged to a regional decision has a measured round-trip gap of zero.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. None of this has been settled by a measurement, so I am labelling it `specified`, not `grounded`.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any community is a permanently good actor, that all communities share one worldview, that an Indigenous observation can be extracted into a model without recording who held it and under what authority, or that the figures name which community should be consulted. They return a shape, not a name. The shape is: keep each community as a separate slot with its own ground contact and declared stake, describe the residual before choosing whose knowledge to consult, keep every imported observation provenanced, and carry methods across scales rather than recordings.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is in the formula link. Run it on a decision process you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Why this order

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and refuter first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat map rule carried in the exemplar: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| No ranking of recipients by receptiveness | Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation. |

| No claim that all communities are one voice | Fig 7 :14, "blending: novelty swamped to the mean"; TEK source: "does not function as a universal set of principles." |
| Stake declared, not denied | Fig 10 :60, "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| No prestige ask, no trust ask | Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essay: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter, declares their own stake and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. That is what stage 1 is for. Do not skip it.
