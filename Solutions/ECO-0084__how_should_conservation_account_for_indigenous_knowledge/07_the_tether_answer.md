# The Tether's answer on Indigenous knowledge in conservation

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No community was consulted and no ecological measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A conservation decision is a prediction about an ecological residual. Evidence about that residual is held by several distinct Indigenous and local communities, each with a place-specific record and its own account of a good outcome. The named failure is collapsing those communities into one input slot called "the Indigenous perspective." In figure terms that is aggregation across slots (Fig 1), blending to a mean (Fig 7), and an unbundled union treated as one problem (Fig 9). The residual is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not one hard question, and it has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Refuse the aggregate slot.** "The Indigenous perspective" has no anchor. It is produced by the conservation frame's need for one input, and it moves when that frame is pushed. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The slot is not a domain; it is a placeholder.
2. **Keep each community as a separate live slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A settled global picture with one live community signal is a legal state, not an inert one.
3. **Enumerate the habitat from contact before composing a plan.** Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Include second- and third-order effects. Valence is relative to the residual: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51).
4. **Describe the residual before choosing whose knowledge to consult.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Residual first, frame second (Fig 8 :53).
5. **Use independence, not identity, as the combination rule.** Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Two communities reading the same agency survey are one frame wearing two names. Two communities with disjoint observation histories that converge are corroboration at maximum independence. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).
6. **Select between rooms; do not blend them.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :14: "blending: novelty swamped to the mean." A single Indigenous seat is one room, not many.
7. **Unbundle the union before search.** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The Library of Babel essay says the same: a bundle of several problems must be searched individually and then combined.
8. **Keep imports provenanced and under authority to control.** Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." The CARE principles add "Authority to Control." A datum labelled "TEK" without community, place, observer and authority is not evidence.
9. **Carry methods across scales, not recordings.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Measure the round-trip gap rather than assuming it is zero (Fig 4 :30).
10. **Declare the stake.** Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all communities share one worldview or one perspective. The TEK source says the opposite: it "does not function as a universal set of principles."
- Not that any community is a permanently good or benign actor. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51).
- Not that knowledge can be extracted into a model without provenance and authority to control.
- Not which community should be consulted, which policy is correct, or how many seats constitute representation. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is settled by a measurement or by a community verdict.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

- Does pooling N communities into one knowledge input preserve a live per-community residual at equal or lower cost? If yes, F1 and F2 fail.
- Do two communities drawing on the same evidence pool behave as independent frames for this residual? If yes, F3 fails.
- Does a local observation record lift unchanged to a regional decision with a measured round-trip gap of zero? If yes, F8 fails.
- Is the union actually a strict partition of non-overlapping residuals? If yes, the unbundling requirement narrows to bookkeeping.
- **The middle link:** does a working instrument already exist for what a given community's knowledge affords and for what counts as progress in that community's terms? Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." This is the instrument to build, and the communities themselves are the ground that would validate it.

An endorsement by a prestigious scholar or a large number of agreeing respondents is not such an instrument (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: "Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it.").

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The six-term substrate, the figures and this derivation are "a human's careful guess," not nature's own list. Another evidence pool need not agree. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical README). Three findings (F7, F8, F9) rest on single figure lines and are marked as such, not promoted by repetition.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the right conservation policy or the right community representative. It narrows the form a defensible answer must take. "The Indigenous perspective" has no anchor, so it is not a thing to consult: it is a slot the conservation frame authored because it wanted one input. Each community is a separate frame with its own ground contact, its own account of a good outcome and a declared stake, and the residual is indexed per community, not averaged across them. Describe the ecological residual before choosing whose knowledge to consult, enumerate the habitat outward from contact, keep every imported observation provenanced and under the originating community's authority to control, select between distinct frames rather than blending them into one room, and carry methods across scales rather than lifting recordings. Independence is measured by evidence-pool overlap, not by cultural difference. What would refute this is a case where pooling communities preserves the live signal at equal cost, or where a local record lifts unchanged to a regional decision with a measured round-trip gap of zero. Until one of those fires, the claim is `specified`, not `grounded`, and the communities and the ecological measurements are the ground that would settle it.
