# Expert verification packet — the Tether on Indigenous knowledge in conservation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation practitioner, ethnobiologist or Indigenous-data researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No community verdict and no ecological measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"The Indigenous perspective" is not a domain, because it has no anchor: it is one input slot authored by the conservation frame. A defensible procedure instead keeps each knowledge-holding community as a separate slot with its own ground contact, declared stake and authority to control; describes the ecological residual before choosing whose knowledge to consult; enumerates the habitat from contact outward, including cascades; uses evidence-pool independence rather than cultural identity to decide when agreement means anything; selects between distinct frames rather than blending them into one room; keeps every imported observation provenanced; and carries methods rather than recordings across scales. The framework returns this shape, not the name of a policy or a representative. Falsifiers were written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** the residual is per community slot, and pooling slots is an averaging error. A global picture can be settled while one community's slot carries a live signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Anchor rule, Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a conservation decision in which pooling N communities into one knowledge input preserves a live per-community residual at equal or lower cost than N separate slots, or produce an instrument that resolves the aggregate signal as cheaply as the per-slot signal.

### C2 — practice-facing; checkable by a conservation practitioner or co-management researcher

- **Statement:** the right combination rule is independence of evidence pools and selection between distinct rooms, not blending into one perspective. A single Indigenous seat is one room, not many.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :14 "blending: novelty swamped to the mean". Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **What to check:** in a decision you know, do the communities consulted draw on disjoint observation histories or on the same agency survey? If they share a source, their agreement is not corroboration. Does the process keep distinct rooms and select between them, or does it average them into one "community input"? The *Fisheries co-management* source (opened 2026-11-23) records the practical consequence: "Conflicting views and economic versus conservation issues appear to be some of the main limitations to a successful co-management process."
- **Refuter:** show that two frames drawing on the same report are independent for this residual, or that a blended single-perspective input selects actions against a measured ground as well as separately maintained independent frames with no loss of novel signal.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with an external governance check

- **Statement:** imports need provenance and authority to control, and only methods cross the local-to-regional membrane. Lifting a community's observation record unchanged into a regional policy carries a recording, not a generator.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents"; Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."; Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."; Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."; Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **External check:** the CARE principles (opened 2026-11-23) are "people and purpose-oriented" and include "Authority to Control"; the TEK source says TEK "does not function as a universal set of principles."
- **Refuter:** exhibit an IK datum entering a conservation closure with no provenance and no shadow test that demonstrably expands the reach, or show a local observation record that lifts unchanged to a regional decision with a measured round-trip gap of zero.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all communities share one worldview, or that any community is a permanently good actor (Fig 11 :49, :51).
- Not that knowledge may be extracted into a model without recording who held it and under what authority.
- Not which community should be consulted, how many seats count as representation, or which policy is correct. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that the union is unsearchable. Fig 8 :54: "the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an ecological measurement or a community verdict.
- Not corroborated by this packet's own concurrence. If you agree because the sources share an evidence pool, that is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement.

1. Per-slot test: does pooling communities into one input preserve a live per-community residual at equal cost? (C1)
2. Independence test: do the consulted communities draw on disjoint evidence pools, and does selection between their frames outperform blending against a measured outcome? (C2)
3. Scale test: measure the round trip for a local observation record lifted to a regional decision. Is the gap zero? (C3)
4. Instrument test: does a working instrument already exist for what a community's knowledge affords and for what counts as progress in that community's terms? Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

A published endorsement, a large respondent list or a prestigious reviewer is not any of these. "Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it" (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap, opened 2026-11-23).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 2 :31 and :40, Fig 7 :35, Fig 8 :50, Fig 4 :22 and :25. Eight short passages.
2. Name one decision you know where "the Indigenous perspective" was treated as one input. Say whether any community's residual went live afterwards.
3. Say whether the communities consulted drew on disjoint observation histories or the same agency report.
4. Name one instrument that would measure the round trip from a local observation to a regional rule, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
