## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, in WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the mapping from three inputs to learned priority. Arity: a relation between a system and its habitat with three overlapping inputs, so a union, not an integer. Symmetry: per slot, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :28). Scale: within one closure per run, crossing scales through a lossy membrane (Fig 4 :30).
- **Domain 1, data and habitat.** Actors: examples, corpus, labels, distribution. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground the data was drawn from; the corpus is not itself the ground. Question type: partition boundary.
- **Domain 2, objective.** Actors: loss, reward, comparison, threshold. Currency: what counts as progress. Anchor: none of its own; a frame-produced score fails Fig 2 :29 to :30. Question type: partition.
- **Domain 3, feedback channel.** Actors: reward signal, preference labels, graders. Currency: residual R returned. Anchor: the ground at the far end. Question type: union with D1 and D2.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: outside readers and this seat. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence here is zero: one evidence pool.

Order by dependency: data and vocabulary, then the objective comparison, then planning and feedback (Fig 3 :20). Refuse before mint (Formula step 2). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary started from Fig 11 (habitat) and Fig 3 (dependency), I started from Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 9 (union versus partition). Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes within the framework, so the agreement is weak but not circular at the line level. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:28, Fig 3 :20/:27, Fig 9 :55, Fig 10 :24 and Fig 11 :40/:70.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "training data, objectives and feedback shape learned priority", used in the README and in the outreach draft.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete system with a corpus, a loss, a reward model and a per-slot evaluation.

**R_T is nonzero.** Five constraints the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **slot index**, which the shorthand drops and which Fig 1 :19 makes load-bearing;
- the **dependency order** of Fig 3 :20, which the shorthand flattens into three parallel causes;
- the **anchor test** of Fig 2 :29 to :30, which the shorthand does not apply to the objective or the reward model;
- the **union structure** of Fig 9 :55 to :57, which the shorthand reads as a list of three separable causes;
- the **two currencies** of Fig 12 :125, which the shorthand invites summing into one score.

What survives cleanly: data acts through contact rather than as a recipe, the objective is a comparison, and feedback is a decaying channel. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists for this solution; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the shorthand is the derivation" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
