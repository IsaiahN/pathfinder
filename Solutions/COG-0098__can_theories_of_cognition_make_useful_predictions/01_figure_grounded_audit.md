## 0. The residual in effect terms

R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s. Fix a cognitive theory Γ, a researcher belief b, a prediction protocol a, and an observed practice o' in a society the theory was not built on. R is the gap between what Γ predicts for that practice and what the practice actually does. The question adds a constraint on how R may be closed: the practices must not be reduced to familiar categories. In effect terms that constraint is a **provenance and closure condition** on the term that closes R. A category of Γ may close R either because it was already reachable inside Γ and merely re-arranged, or because a category from the society's own frame had to cross into Γ. Reduction is the claim that the first always suffices and that the second never happened, or worse, that the second happened while being reported as the first.

Three options and what each leads to:

- **A, reduction.** Map the unfamiliar practice onto a familiar category. Leads to a residual that may shrink on the researcher's coding sheet while the society's distinction is erased. Fig 8 :51 says convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, so the erasure is invisible without a record.
- **B, import with provenance.** Describe R in effect terms, find the frame whose closure predicts its shape, bring the category across with a source gate and a shadow test. Leads to a checkable expansion of the theory's reach.
- **C, no prediction.** Leads to a `gap` declaration rather than a forced fit, which is legitimate where no instrument can name the missing axis.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (formula, "Direction"): an answer to this question would look like a three-part separator, (i) a residual that stays indexed per practice and society and settles against an anchor that does not update on the theory's own coding, (ii) an effect-term description written before any frame is chosen, and (iii) a provenance rule that distinguishes a re-arrangement of held atoms from a category imported from the society's frame. I bind this separator now so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else. If it fails any refuter in section 4, it is withdrawn.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least a four-place relation among Γ, b, a and o', indexed by slot s = (practice, society). A two-place reading, theory versus society, drops the prediction and the action and is rejected. Symmetry: not symmetric. The society's ground is an anchor and does not update on what the theory thinks (Fig 2 :25), and the constraint "without reducing" is ordered, since the society's distinction must survive the prediction rather than be dissolved into the predictor's vocabulary. Scale: the slot is a practice, but the anchor that settles it is at the population or ecological scale, so the residual crosses the membrane between a single cognitive step and a society's selection loop (Fig 4 :3-16).

**Partition or union.** Union. Prediction validity (D1, D3, D5), category preservation (D2, D4) and anchor independence (D3, D5) are three overlapping conditions. Their overlap is a compound with its own loop: a prediction whose category was imported and whose anchor is local is not a harder case of either condition, it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound (Fig 9 :54-57). A single lever, such as "use better coding", clears the region where only D3 holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20).** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The break is between vocabulary and objective: a researcher can record behaviour and name familiar categories, but has no term for what the unfamiliar practice affords in its own frame and no local objective for what counts as progress. Fig 3 :27 says a link with no instrument is not a link that works, it is a link nobody has looked at. Most prediction effort lands above the break, where measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1: The question's constraint is a provenance condition, not a general banning of familiar words

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

So a familiar category used as a binding onto an atom the theory already holds is not itself forbidden. What is forbidden is reporting an imported local category as if it had been derived from the theory, and reporting a re-arrangement as if a new atom had crossed. "Without reducing" means the distinction must survive and its route must be recorded. A self-correction is preserved in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5b, where I first overstated the "shape, not a name" rule into a ban on naming held atoms.

**Refuter:** show a case where a local category enters Γ with a source gate and shadow test and the resulting prediction is still correctly described as pure derivation from held atoms. Then the provenance requirement is too strong.

### F2: A theory's home anchor is an instrument here, not the right anchor

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." The question is constitutively about unfamiliar societies, so the right anchor is the local outcome or local judgment, not the home lab's benchmark. Fig 2 :25: "The anchor must not update." The local anchor must not move when the theory changes its category assignment.

This is where reduction usually hides. If "useful" is scored by how well the society's practice fits the theory's existing category scheme, then the anchor is the theory's own vocabulary, which updates whenever the researcher recodes. Fig 2 :33-34 says that makes panel 3 turn into panel 1, everything drifts together coherently, which is why nobody notices.

**Refuter:** exhibit a prediction whose residual trends down on held-out local slots while the only anchor consulted is the researcher's own category fit, and show that the anchor did not move. Then the mirror charge fails.

### F3: The residual must stay indexed per practice and society

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan 2010 report that "there is no obvious a priori grounds for claiming that a particular behavioral phenomenon is universal based on sampling from a single subpopulation." A pooled cross-cultural score can therefore hide the very slot where the theory fails. Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."

**Refuter:** a pooled score that reproduces every per-slot verdict on held-out societies, including the sign and the ranking. Then per-slot indexing is a needless constraint.

### F4: Closure is idempotent, so more home data cannot import the missing category

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." So the answer to "can more data fix this?" is no when the deficit is a genuine absence, and yes when it is depth. Fig 6 :27 says the two look the same from inside the frame, and Fig 8 :39 says a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence. The remedy is to characterise R in effect terms and go outside, not to keep sampling the same panel.

**Refuter:** a theory that, after more within-closure search, predicts a previously unreachable local distinction with a residual that settles against the local ground, with no new atom entering Γ. Then idempotence was misapplied to this case.

### F5: Reduction is a wrong import, priced above starting from primitives

Fig 6 :24: "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 6 :14 names import as "the only operator that moves the wall." If a local practice is forced into a familiar category and that category was wrong, the theory has both lost the local distinction and paid for an atom it did not need. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Sending home-population results upward as if they were methods is the same mistake at the population scale.

**Refuter:** a forced mapping whose residual settles on held-out local slots and whose category survives later residual changes (Fig 11 :49-51). Then the wrong-import charge is refuted and the mapping was a genuine functional group.

### F6: The legitimate alternative to import is isomer, not synonymy

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Two practices can therefore be predicted without any new atom, if they are the same cognitive atoms under a different bond. They are not the same practice. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."

So "without reducing" has two success modes. Either the practice is an isomer over the theory's held atoms, in which case the arrangement must be stated and priced (bonds carry information the atoms do not), or it needs an import, in which case provenance must be stated. Saying "it is just another instance of category X" is neither mode.

**Refuter:** exhibit an isomer pair where the bond is irrelevant to the behaviour and the atom list alone predicts every slot. Then the bond charge fails. The supporting derivation is in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

### F7: The measurement channel is the broken link, and it decays

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link here is having terms for what a practice affords in its own frame and saying what counts as progress in that frame. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26 names the failure: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." A coding scheme imported from the home population is a saturated metric and a pooled reading at once. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

World corroboration: the measurement-invariance literature asks whether "the same construct is being measured across some specified groups," and Welzel and colleagues are reported as criticising the invariance criteria for favouring "constructs with low between-group variance, while constructs with high between-group variance fail these tests." That conflict was returned to the census, not used to flip the verdict. It is remapped below in section 5.

**Refuter:** a coding instrument that preserves every local distinction on held-out slots without new contact with the local frame, and whose readings are corroborated by a second instrument with a different evidence pool.

### F8: Agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for this verdict

Fig 2 :39-40: correlation has valence, and "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41 says it is "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Two researchers using the same manual and the same WEIRD panel are one frame wearing two names. Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :36: "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

This is also why the outreach material leads with the refuter. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

**Refuter:** two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence pools, each ranging separately against the local ground, that fail to converge on the shape claim after a fair trial. Then the independence requirement produced no surplus here.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is F9 rows 1 to 4. It is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A prediction stays useful on a held-out unfamiliar society, with a residual that settles against a non-updating local anchor, while no atom, bond, or provenanced binding from that society's frame entered Γ. This falsifies the import-or-isomer requirement (Fig 6 :14, :22, :23).
2. Two frames with genuinely different evidence pools, each ranging separately against the local ground, fail to converge on the described shape after a fair trial. This falsifies the triangulation requirement (Fig 2 :35, Fig 8 :47-49).
3. A measurement shows the society's distinction is fully recovered by re-arranging atoms the theory already held, with the bond stated and priced, and the import claim cannot be sustained. This refutes the strong "must import" version; the shape claim narrows to the isomer branch (Fig 12 :117).
4. A held-out prediction's residual trends down when the only anchor is the researcher's own category agreement, and that anchor demonstrably moved when the theory changed. This shows the result was coherent drift, not prediction (Fig 2 :33-35).
5. The result disappears once residuals are indexed per practice and society, that is, the pooled score did all the work. This falsifies the per-slot requirement (Fig 1 :19, :22).
6. A standing list of familiar categories predicts every slot across changes of residual. This falsifies the valence-relative claim (Fig 11 :49-51).

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn or narrowed in this file with the refuter recorded, not silently repaired. Until one is actually run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the residual in effect terms first and write the effect description before naming any theory or any familiar category (Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 8 :53).
2. Score "useful" per practice and society against a local anchor that does not update on the coding scheme (Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :25, :29).
3. Require a route statement for every category that closes a residual: re-arrangement of held atoms, or provenanced import with a shadow test (Fig 8 :51, Fig 6 :14).
4. Refuse the pooled universal verdict and the standing list (Fig 1 :22, Fig 11 :51). Keep the four domain verdicts separate.
5. Report the missing discriminator as `gap`, not as a name (Fig 13 :134-135). The honest sentence is: the theory can predict a shape here, it cannot name the society's category, and here is the instrument that would.
6. Send the generator, not the recording: lead with the refuter, offer the figures only after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22, Fig 10 :52).

### Round trip and honest stop

Carry the four-domain shape up to one sentence: *a cognitive theory predicts an unfamiliar society when it keeps the residual local and per-slot, ranges it against a ground that does not move, and states whether the closing category was a re-arrangement or a provenanced import.* Bring that sentence back down to a specific society and four things are lost: the bond-level test that distinguishes isomer from import, the smile of the channel that may have pooled readings, the identity of the local anchor, and the fact that the missing discriminator is unreached rather than absent. That gap is R_T, measured qualitatively in `11_audit.md`. It is recorded, not eliminated (Fig 4 :31-32).

**Final status:** the shape survives, the category list does not exist yet, and no society was measured. `specified` on domains, `general` on structure, `gap` on the list and the discriminator. Candidate, not accepted.
