# Expert verification packet: the Tether on cross-society prediction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a theoretical psychologist or cross-cultural researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, `gap` on the category list and the discriminator. Not `grounded`.** No society was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle COG-0098.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A cognitive theory is a frame with a finite closure, and an unfamiliar society is a frame whose closure may differ. More data from the theory's home population cannot import a category the theory does not hold. A prediction can still close without reducing the society's practices to familiar categories in exactly two ways: the practices are isomers over a shared atom inventory with the changed bond written down and behaviourally decisive, or a category crosses from the local frame with its source and gap-explaining role recorded. Calling a practice "just another case of category X" is neither way. The prediction must be scored per practice and society against a local anchor that does not move when the researcher changes the coding scheme. The theory returns the shape of the missing category, not its name, and the instrument that would decide between the two success modes is declared as owed rather than invented.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: The closure and import rule

- **Statement:** search inside a theory's own library never adds a new primitive. If a prediction requires a distinction the theory does not hold, the only operator that moves the wall is import from a frame whose closure differs, and that import needs provenance and a shadow test.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall." Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- **Refuter, written before the run:** a prediction stays useful on a held-out society with no atom, bond, or provenanced binding from that society's frame entering the theory. Or a within-closure search imports nothing and still reaches a previously unreachable local distinction with a residual that settles against the local ground. Either fires against C1.

### C2: The two legitimate success modes, and the one forbidden one

- **Statement:** over a shared atom inventory, two practices can be isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Where inventories differ, the honest claim is a functional group with different members. Where no arrangement of the inventory can represent the local distinction and the ground pays for it, a new atom is owed. Reduction is none of these; it is an unrecorded renaming of the practice as a held atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Fig 6 :24: "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."
- **What to check:** for a pair of practices you know well, are the atom inventories identical? If so, can you name the changed typed bond and show a behavioural difference on a held-out slot? If the inventories differ, is the functional-group reading the one being made? If neither, is the missing distinction described in effect terms rather than named?
- **Refuter, written before the run:** the atom sets differ and the claimed isomer still predicts every held-out slot with all bonds spelled out (fires against the isomer branch), or a category declared unreachable is later composed from held atoms with the behavioural difference confirmed (fires against the import branch, as a depth error rather than an absence).

### C3: The per-slot and anchor requirement

- **Statement:** "Useful" is settled by a measurement that does not change when the theory's own categories change. Residuals stay indexed per practice and society. Agreement between two researchers using the same coding manual is one evidence pool, not two frames.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."
- **Refuter, written before the run:** a pooled cross-cultural score reproduces every per-slot verdict on held-out societies, including sign and ranking; or a prediction's residual trends down when the only anchor is the researcher's category fit and that anchor demonstrably moved when the theory recoded. Either fires against C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that cross-cultural prediction is impossible, and not that all practices are unique.
- Not a list of cognitive universals. No list is returned, by anyone in this packet.
- Not that a local category is a new biological primitive. An import adds an atom to a theory's library, not to the world (Fig 6 :25).
- Not that the local habitat is the ground. Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- Not that the WEIRD literature, the Müller-Lyer literature or the measurement-invariance literature settles the verdict. They are secondary and partially conflicting; their overlaps are recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not `grounded`. The three sub-claims are checkable structure, not measured results.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A held-out local prediction scored against an anchor that does not move when the coding scheme changes, with the per-slot residuals reported rather than pooled.
2. A route statement for each closing category: re-arrangement of held atoms with the bond named, or import with source and shadow test.
3. A source-discriminating instrument for the isomer versus new-atom decision. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Until one exists, that cell is `gap`.
4. Any of the six refuters in `01` section 4, any of the four falsifiers in `06`, or any of the three sub-claim refuters above.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :32, Fig 6 :14 and :23 (three short passages). Is C1 a fair statement of the closure rule?
2. Read Fig 12 :117 and :123 and Fig 13 :115. Does the three-way split in C2 match the figure's own distinction between isomer, functional group and a new kind?
3. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22 and Fig 2 :29 and :35. Is the anchor requirement in C3 consistent with how you would score a prediction in a society you know well?
4. Pick one practice you know in two societies. Apply the `06` Q1 three-part isomer test. Report whether it passes, and which refuter, if any, it fires.
5. Send the failure. A refutation is more useful than an endorsement, and this packet has no way to pool your agreement into evidence.
