# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have read `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 to 5 and you are willing to state the refuter in your own words. If the stage-1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to offer.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about predicting unfamiliar societies

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about cognition and culture. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A theory of cognition is a library of terms and rules for combining them. It predicts by comparing what those terms say should happen with what is observed, separately for each practice in each society. The question I am working on is whether such a theory can keep predicting accurately in a society it was not built on while the society's own distinctions survive the prediction. The failure mode I am testing for is quiet: the prediction looks better once the practice has been recoded into a familiar category, and nothing in the report records that the recoding happened.
>
> **The shape claim.** Two ways can close that gap without reduction. Either the unfamiliar practice and a familiar one are the same cognitive atoms in a different arrangement, in which case the arrangement has to be written down and checked, or the practice needs a category the theory does not yet hold, in which case that category has to come from contact with the local frame with its source and its gap-explaining role recorded. Calling the practice "just another case of category X" is neither way. A theory that only samples more people from its home population cannot import the missing category by searching harder.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That no cognitive theory can predict across societies. That all practices are culturally unique. That there is a universal list of mental mechanisms, or that a local category is a new biological primitive. There is no list here and no measurement of any society. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a prediction stays accurate on a held-out society while no atom, bond, or sourced binding from that society's frame entered the theory;
> 2. two researchers with genuinely different evidence pools, each checking against the local outcome, fail to converge on this shape;
> 3. a measurement shows the local distinction is fully recovered by re-arranging categories the theory already held, with no import needed;
> 4. the result only holds when accuracy is judged by the researcher's own category fit rather than by a local outcome.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I will also send the second stage only if you ask for it.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
