## The question as an effect

R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s. Γ is a cognitive theory's library of terms and bonds, b is the researcher's belief, a is the prediction protocol, and o' is the observed practice in an unfamiliar society. The decision is whether R can settle against a ground on unfamiliar slots while the society's own distinctions survive as distinctions, rather than being renamed as members of the theory's familiar atom set. Option A says yes by reduction, mapping unfamiliar practices onto familiar categories. Option B says yes by treating the society as a frame whose closure differs, describing the residual in effect terms, and importing the missing category with provenance and a shadow test. Option C says no, prediction requires reduction. What an answer would look like is bound in `01` section 1 as an ordinary term: a three-part separator with a per-slot residual, a non-updating local anchor, and a provenance rule on imports. The separator then accrues a residual like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The IACCP site and its news pages publish some, and they are deliberately excluded.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for agreement manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. Fig 2 :40: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool. Agreement between a theory and a researcher who both learned the same familiar categories is one pool talking.
3. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Sending the full architecture before asking for a verdict spends the only thing the outside frame contributes.
