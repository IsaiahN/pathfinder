# Expert verification packet: the Tether on research and unequal power

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science, a science-studies scholar or a public-health researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain verdicts, `grounded` nowhere.** This is a shape and a route, not a historical finding. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The sign of a research effect is not a property of the research. It is a relation to a named residual and to a ground that someone could try to move. Research challenges unequal power when it refuses a regularity the arrangement composes over, a reachable ground pays for the refusal, and a low-overlap frame checks it. Research strengthens unequal power when it accumulates inside an evidence pool shared with the interest it appears to check, the categories it produces rank people, and the anchor moves when that interest pushes it. The two halves are a union and must be kept in separate loops. The figures return that shape and stop; dated cases are secondary imports from world sources with provenance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** valence is relative to the residual, not carried by the actor. "Science" cannot be assigned a standing moral sign.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a stable property of scientific method (falsifiability, reproducibility, preregistration) that predicts the sign across all residuals regardless of the reachable ground. Then valence is carried by the actor and C1 fails.

### C2: framework-internal plus world-facing, checkable by a scholar of science and power

- **Statement:** the two signs use different operators. Challenge enters the REFUTED bin (a unary refusal); justification is bonded accumulation with no refusal admitted. They are therefore not isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 12 :112 "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** does the world record actually show a refusal in the challenge cases (a held claim is said to be false and paid for), and mere accumulation in the justification cases? The ledger supplies two open-access witnesses: Chowkwanyun 2023 on community-shaped research agendas, and Eisenberg-Guyot and Eisenberg-Guyot 2022 on group-difference theories that gained currency because they were useful.
- **Refuter:** show a single binary bond expressing both refusal and accumulation without a unary operator, or a challenge case that never enters the REFUTED bin. Then the arity distinction fails.

### C3: the mechanism of the justification half, checkable against records

- **Statement:** when the ground can be moved by the interest it is meant to check, peer agreement stops being triangulation and becomes the shared pool talking.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :20-21 "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Fig 10 :93 "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
- **What to check:** in a named case, were the funding, publication and enforcement records open to the people affected? A keeper that publishes its own audit, as Nature did in 2022, is evidence that the question is live, not that the anchor is now unmoved.
- **Refuter:** an open record showing a funded, published, enforced result survived a genuine attempt by the funding interest to move the ground. Then Fig 2 :33-34 and Fig 10 :93 do not govern that case.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a chronology, a list of "when this happened", or a verdict on any named institution. The figure level is `gap` on chronology (Fig 13 :135 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").
- Not that researchers' intentions decide the sign. Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."
- Not that more agreement, or more prestigious agreement, settles anything. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not that the two halves can be averaged. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.
- Not that the world sources agree independently of one another. Overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`; where they share an evidence pool their agreement is one witness.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Open records that let a reader test whether the anchor moved when the interest pushed it (fires the C3 refuter).
2. Two low-overlap frames, one in contact with the affected community, with the verdict recorded before theory is shown (tests C1 in practice).
3. A research effect that survives changing the funder and the venue.
4. A method property that predicts valence across all residuals (fires the C1 refuter and withdraws the shape).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 5 :53, Fig 12 :112 and Fig 2 :30-34. Six short passages.
2. Decide whether C1's refuter is plausible in their own field. If it is, say so plainly.
3. Name one case where the funding and venue records were open enough to test whether the anchor moved, or say why none exists in their area.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
