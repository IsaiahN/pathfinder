## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran it on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :49-51 (valence relative to residual), Fig 12 :112 and :140 (refusal versus accumulation), Fig 2 :30-34 (non-updating anchor), Fig 9 :54 (union split), Fig 7 :35 (agreement is not a verdict).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` §2: "the sign is not carried by the research; it is set by the residual and by whether the ground can be moved."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete historical claim, for example "the study that lowered lead exposure challenged power and the lead-industry-funded study strengthened it."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **operator difference**: a refusal is a unary operator, while accumulation is a bond, and the shorthand's "sign" does not carry that distinction;
- the **non-updating condition on the anchor**: the shorthand says "ground can be moved" but drops the requirement that the frame cannot move it, which is the whole of Fig 2 :34;
- the **low-overlap requirement**: the shorthand does not carry Fig 7 :35, so it can be read as if professional consensus were sufficient;
- the **union split**: the shorthand compresses two loops into one sentence, which is exactly the averaging Fig 1 :22 warns about.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: science is not carrying a standing moral sign, and the question must be answered by naming the residual and the ground. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the metalaws would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
