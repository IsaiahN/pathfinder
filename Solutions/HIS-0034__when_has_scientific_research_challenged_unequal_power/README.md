# Solution HIS-0034

**Question:** When has scientific research challenged unequal power, and when has it helped justify or strengthen it?

**Source:** history of science and knowledge.txt line 43

**Section:** 4. Knowledge, power, and exclusion

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the domain verdicts, `grounded` nowhere. No measurement in this folder settles a historical "when", so nothing here is claimed as settled.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Boundary and standing.** This folder was written only from the local framework files named below and the public web pages recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Nothing outside the folder was modified. This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. Per *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." It cannot settle the question it reports on. That limit is carried on every substantive claim.

**What the question decides, in effect terms.** The same actor, scientific research, appears with opposite valences: it reduces the gap an unequal-power arrangement produces, or it closes that gap down and stabilises the arrangement. The effect to be decided is therefore not "is science good or bad" but **what changes the valence of the same actor relative to a named residual and a reachable ground.** An answer is bound as an ordinary term as follows: *research acts as a refusal when a held regularity is contradicted and a reachable ground pays for the refusal; it acts as a stored regularity when the evidence pool is shared with the interest it appears to check and no refusal is admitted.* That binding is the shape the files test, not a conclusion smuggled in.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, no scraped list, no bulk-send plan.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting for receptiveness manufactures that pool.
- No claim that agreement among historians or among scientists settles a historical question. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
