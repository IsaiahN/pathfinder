## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for a "when": a condition, not a chronology. It names one actor, scientific research, and two opposite effects on one residual, unequal power. Stated as an effect:

> What changes between a research act that reduces the gap an unequal-power arrangement produces, and a research act of the same kind that closes that gap and stabilises the arrangement?

This is **not** one hard question. It is a union of two conditions (Fig 9 :54). Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run, per the formula's Direction rule:

> `CHALLENGE(research, R)` = a held regularity is refused (¬) and a reachable ground pays for the refusal.
> `JUSTIFY(research, R)` = the evidence pool is shared with the interest the research appears to check, and no refusal is admitted, so the stored regularity composes into the powerful frame.

That binding is a prediction to be refuted, not a finding. It is written here before the figure census so the direction of the question is refusable like any other term.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two valences of one actor, so a bivalent residual. It takes two arguments: a research act and a named ground.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Challenging is a **refusal** (¬, a unary operator), while justifying is **accumulation** (composition of a settled term). These are not mirror images and they are not points on one scale, so they must never be averaged into "science is a tool that can be used either way" without stating the operator difference. Fig 12 :112: "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- **Scale:** recursive across strata (individual researcher, institution or journal, society or policy). Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." What counts as a refusal at one level is an instance at another, so verdicts are kept per level and never summed.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1: the research act / claim frame | researchers, instruments, methods, claims, refuters | residuals and settlement at a ground | **yes**: measurement, experiment, patient outcome (Fig 2 :29) |
| D2: the institutional / power frame | funders, states, regulators, journals, patent holders, gatekeepers | budgets, access, standing, enforcement | **conditionally**: material consequences exist (funding decisions, laws, outcomes) but are exactly what can be moved by the actors in the frame (Fig 2 :33-34). The anchor is nameable but fails the non-updating test when captured. That failure is the finding, not a reason to drop the domain. |
| D3: the verification / reception frame | historians, affected communities, expert outside frames | agreement and residuals | **yes**: the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35). Independence is low when all frames read the same archive, so this domain's checks are weak unless a low-overlap frame is brought in. |

No domain is admitted on a vague anchor. D2 is admitted precisely because its anchor can be named *and can be pushed*, which is the test Fig 2 :30 supplies.

### 1.3 Partition or union, and dependency order

Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54, :57). The two valences overlap: the same laboratory can challenge one power and justify another. A single lever applied to a list of famous cases clears the single-condition cases and leaves the compounds standing.

**Dependency order (Fig 3):** describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle whether a fact is present before searching (Fig 9 :4-5); refuse a held term before minting a new one (formula step 2); range each frame against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45-46). The middle link is the objective, "what would count as progress for whom", and Fig 3 :27 warns: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

### F1: Valence is a relation to a residual, not a property of research (decisive, `general`)

Fig 11 :49-51, verbatim:

> "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
> "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
> "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

So "when" is answered by naming the residual and the ground for each case, not by sorting research into a good basket and a bad basket. A study that lowers lead exposure in one city and a study that naturalises inequality in another are not two kinds of science; they are one kind of actor at two different residuals with two different grounds. This is the governing line for the whole question. **Triangulated** across Fig 11 (habitat valence) and Fig 2 (anchor legitimacy), two figures with low conceptual overlap.

**Refuter:** show a stable property of scientific method (falsifiability, reproducibility, preregistration) that predicts the valence across all residuals regardless of which ground is reachable. Then valence is carried by the actor and F1 is refuted as applied here.

### F2: The two valences use different operators, so they cannot be averaged (`general`)

Fig 12 :112: "A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 12 :140: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."

The world literature states the bivalence plainly. Bautista (2025) writes that students can recognise "how science can be a tool to either oppress or push against injustice." That is a faithful description of the phenomenon but it treats valence as a property of a tool. The figures locate the difference in the **operator**: a challenge passes through the REFUTED bin and owes a refusal; a justification is a settled regularity that composes without any refusal being admitted. Formula step 2 names the fifth bin, which "no arrangement of the other four reaches."

**Strain noted, not hidden:** the world claim and the figure claim are not a conflict. They operate at different levels. The world claim reports the human reading of the two outcomes (`general`); the figure claim governs which quantity the valence attaches to and which operator produces it. A conflict would send us back to the census; here the re-census confirms two different objects. **Triangulated** across Fig 5, Fig 12 and the Operators table.

**Refuter:** show that a justification case can be fully described as a refusal of a held term, or that a challenge case never passes through the REFUTED bin. Then the operator distinction collapses and the two valences may be averaged.

### F3: When the ground moves, peer consensus becomes the pool talking (`general`, D2)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

Fig 2 :20-21: "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

This is the mechanism by which research strengthens unequal power while appearing neutral. When the instrument, the funding stream, the journal or the regulatory venue is moved by the interest it is meant to check, the frame stops ranging against a ground and starts ranging against itself. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Eisenberg-Guyot and Eisenberg-Guyot (2022) supply the corroborating account from public health: "False theories of group difference gain currency not because sound evidence supports them, but because they are useful in maintaining inequities." They add that "the social organization of power distorts Western science (scientists' intentions notwithstanding)." This is the common-cause collapse stated in a different field, so it is genuine low-overlap corroboration. **Triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 10.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a case where a funded, published, enforced research result survived a genuine attempt by the funding interest to move the ground, with the funding and enforcement records open. If the anchor demonstrably does not move, Fig 2 :33-34 and Fig 10 :93 do not govern that case.

### F4: The question is a union and must be unbundled before search (`general`)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :57: "It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

A list of famous episodes, eugenics and its forced-sterilisation legacy, the lead-paint studies used to blame culture rather than housing, the Flint water measurements, is a set of cases in two conditions. Treating the list as one question produces a list of names, which is a recording, not a generator. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." **Single-source** (Fig 9), triangulated only in method with Fig 4's recording rule.

**Refuter:** produce a single condition that predicts the valence in both halves without residual-specific grounding. Then the union reading is unnecessary.

### F5: Agreement inside the scientific room is not evidence of justice (`general`, D3)

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The relevant outside frame is the affected community or a closure that does not share the institution's training and incentives. Chowkwanyun (2023) documents exactly this in environmental justice research: EJ activists "point scientists to the very problems to be studied, including ones that experts might have missed; shape many aspects of study design; and question the adequacy of prevailing paradigms in research." He also records the power asymmetry inside the partnership: "it is the academic partner who typically holds funding and on whose goodwill EJ activists must ultimately rely." Fig 11 :61-62 governs that asymmetry: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." **Triangulated** across Fig 7, Fig 2 and Fig 8.

**Refuter:** show a verdict that was correctly settled by a single closed professional room with no low-overlap frame and no contact with affected communities, on a case where those communities had a material stake in the outcome.

### F6: The "when" is a middle-link question with a missing instrument (`general`)

Fig 3 :26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The question asks for the condition under which a research act counts as progress for someone. That is the objective link of Fig 3's chain, the link most likely to break and least likely to be measured. An answer that lists cases skips the break and reports on perception and planning, which are the links that already have tests. **Single-source** (Fig 3).

**Refuter:** name and apply an instrument that measures "progress for whom" directly in a historical case, and show the resulting condition is stable across residuals.

### F7: The honest figure-level verdict on the specific chronology is `gap`

The figures are metalaws. They constrain the shape of the answer and the route to it; they do not contain dates, names or archival evidence. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." On the specific question "which years, which labs, which controversies", the figure level returns `gap`, not a list. The world sources supply the chronology; their agreement with the figure shape is corroboration, not derivation. Saying this plainly is required by the master plan: a question may honestly end in `gap`, and that is a verdict.

**Refuter:** derive a dated historical sequence from the figure text alone. Since no such sequence is in the text, this refuter is structurally closed within this evidence pool and is recorded for completeness.

### F8: Stakes must be declared, including this author's (`general`)

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

The solution author has a stake in the figures being useful; the world sources have stakes in their own normative commitments (Dawson and Lock declare their own situatedness explicitly); the institutions named in the ledger have reputational stakes. None of these is removed by this audit. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is high, so the ledger records overlap per source and the second seat in `11_audit.md` declares that it shares the primary's evidence pool.

**Refuter:** identify a source in the ledger whose stake or evidence overlap was omitted. The ledger is checkable; a gap in it is a real failure of F8.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim in §0 is refuted if:

1. a stable property of scientific method predicts the valence across all residuals independent of the reachable ground (breaks F1 and the valence-relativity shape);
2. the anchor in a power-domain case is shown not to move when the interested party pushes it, with open records (breaks F3);
3. a single closed professional room settles a high-stakes case correctly without any low-overlap frame (breaks F5);
4. a single condition predicts both valences without residual-specific grounding (breaks F4).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Answer the two halves in two loops and keep the verdicts separate. Never average them into a single "science is a tool" statement (F2, F4).
2. For each case, name the residual and the ground, then ask whether the ground could be moved by the interest in question (F1, F3).
3. Treat a challenge as a refusal first, and check whether the refusal was paid for by a ground, not by an audience (F2, formula step 5).
4. Add a low-overlap frame before accepting a verdict, and record who held the funding (F5, Fig 11 :61-62).
5. Say `gap` on the chronology at the figure level. Use world sources as secondary witnesses, with provenance and overlap recorded (F7, F8).
6. Offer the generator, the derivation and the refuter, not the list of cases (Fig 4 :25).

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives: research is a valence-taking actor whose sign is set by the residual and the reachability of the ground, not by a standing moral label. Challenge is a refusal (- not a bond) paid for by a ground; justification is accumulation inside a shared evidence pool where the anchor has been moved. The two halves are a union and must be kept separate. At the figure level the specific chronology is `gap`; the world literature supplies dated cases, which are secondary witnesses with declared overlap. This is `general`/`specified`, ungrounded, candidate. After re-census, strains: **two named and none hidden** (the "tool" phrasing of the world source at a different level, and the D2 anchor that is nameable but movable).
