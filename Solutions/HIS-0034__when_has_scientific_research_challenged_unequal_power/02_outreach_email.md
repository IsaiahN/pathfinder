# Outreach draft: check one shape before deciding whether the account is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No request for agreement, no request for trust.

**Hard precondition:** the claim below must survive stage 1 on its own. If it does not, there is nothing to show in stage 2.

---

**Subject:** A claim about when research changes power. What would you count as a failure?

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific shape to ask you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read an argument first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** The same thing called scientific research appears with two opposite effects. Sometimes it lowers a measured burden that an unequal-power arrangement produces. Sometimes it supplies the categories and the measurements by which that arrangement justifies itself. The question I am asking is not "is science good or bad." It is: what changes the sign of the same actor?
>
> **The shape (`general`, not `grounded`).** The sign is not carried by the research. It is a relation to a named residual and to a ground that someone could try to move. A challenge is a refusal: a regularity people have been composing over is said to be wrong, and a reachable ground pays for saying so. A justification is accumulation: the evidence pool is shared with the interest the research appears to check, the anchor moves when that interest pushes it, and no refusal is admitted, so the existing regularity composes straight into the powerful frame. I am not claiming a chronology or naming a single decisive case. The figures return a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that any named institution is uniquely evil, and not that a researcher's intentions decide the sign. The anchor test is about what can be moved, not about motives. Not that every historical case fits; the two halves overlap and should be kept in separate loops. Not that agreement among historians, or among scientists, settles any of this. Agreement inside one room is variation, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape. I wrote these down before writing the account:**
> 1. a stable property of scientific method (falsifiability, reproducibility, preregistration) that predicts the sign across all residuals no matter which ground is reachable;
> 2. an open record showing that a funded, published, enforced result survived a real attempt by the funding interest to move the ground;
> 3. a high-stakes case correctly settled by one closed professional room with no outside frame and no contact with affected communities;
> 4. one condition that predicts both signs without referring to the specific residual.
>
> If any holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. Please send your verdict on this before reading anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public health case study, Eisenberg-Guyot and Eisenberg-Guyot, 2022: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9190263
> - Environmental justice and research partnerships, Chowkwanyun, 2023: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10962396/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step loop is in the local formula file. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> With thanks,
> [name]
