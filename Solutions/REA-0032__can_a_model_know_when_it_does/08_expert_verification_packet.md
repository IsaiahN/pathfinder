# Expert verification packet: the Tether on model self-knowledge

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in epistemic uncertainty can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question splits by what the model is ranged against. Inside one frame, on its own confidence, a model cannot establish that it does not know: a frame cannot certify its own completeness, and a score the frame produces cannot score the frame. The relevant signal is per slot, so an aggregate confidence score hides the plausible-but-unsupported case. Ranged against an external ground, the discrimination becomes a checkable contact relation, and calibration, selective prediction and conformal prediction are working instances. With a second frame it holds only under real independence; repeated samples from one model are one evidence pool. At the meta-level the direction of the question cannot be checked from inside, and the remedy is to bind the sought-for shape as a refusable term. "I don't know" is a refusal (a unary negation), not a low probability; the price of that operator is open.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 5 and 8

- **Statement:** a single frame cannot certify its own not-knowing, and a self-produced confidence is disqualified as a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :37 "A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity." Fig 5 :67 "a frame cannot certify its own completeness". Fig 1 :27 "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 1 :26 "predicates minted, and credibility accrued".
- **What to check:** are the cited theorems stated correctly, and does the frame/model distinction apply here? Does the disqualification of self-produced scores follow from the figure, independent of the empirical calibration literature?
- **Refuter:** a figure line permitting self-certification; a self-score shown not to be a quantity the frame also produces; the metaframe theorems refuted or shown inapplicable to models as frames.

### C2: field-facing; checkable by a calibration or uncertainty researcher

- **Statement:** the discrimination is a contact relation, not inspection. The live signal is per slot, and aggregate confidence loses it. External calibration, selective prediction and conformal prediction are instances because they add an anchor, not because the model inspects itself.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 8 :54 "the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable."
- **What to check:** is the per-slot claim faithful to how these methods behave? Guo et al. (2017) find modern networks "poorly calibrated"; Geifman and El-Yaniv (2017) give a risk "dial" that abstains at a calibrated error rate; Angelopoulos and Bates (2023) convert "any heuristic notion of uncertainty from any model" into rigorous sets using calibration data. Does each add an external anchor as the figure requires?
- **Refuter:** a method giving valid abstention with no external calibration data, no ground labels and no second frame; or a demonstration that the aggregate score carries the per-slot information without loss.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a field consequence

- **Statement:** "I don't know" is a refusal, which is negation, not a low probability; a vocabulary without it can only accumulate. Self-sampling is not a second frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Operators table :73-74: subtraction is "the absence of an operand", negation is "the refusal of a claim". Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room."
- **What to check:** is the negation/subtraction distinction coherent as a claim about abstention? Is the independence rule correctly applied to self-sampling? Kadavath et al. (2022) report that considering many of their own samples improves self-evaluation; does that buy a better diagnostic (yes) or an independent verdict (no, by Fig 2 :40)?
- **Refuter:** a refusal written as a bond; a demonstration that probability thresholding is logically identical to negation; or repeated samples of one model shown to draw on distinct evidence pools.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the answer is a bare no. Underivable from inside is not unknowable from contact (Fig 8 :54).
- Not that any model has privileged access to its own grounding. That is the metamerism error: a reading that "cannot comment on the source."
- Not that calibration is self-knowledge, or that self-consistency is triangulation.
- Not that any particular model reliably does this under distribution shift. P(IK) struggles with calibration on new tasks (Kadavath et al. 2022). No measurement was taken here.
- Not a derivation of a mechanism, and no claim about the price or implementation of negation. Operators table :78: "What a unary operator costs is open."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an experiment run for this solution.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. Does a per-slot signal track an external anchor out of distribution, not just in distribution?
2. Does abstention hold its promised error rate on the plausible-but-unsupported cases, with the promised coverage?
3. Does a genuinely independent second frame agree and reduce error, rather than share a pool?
4. Can a refusal be priced and composed over? The unary price is open in the Operators table.

Standing refuters are listed in C1 to C3 and in `01` §4. Any one firing withdraws the claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :13, :22, :27, Fig 8 :34, :37, :54 and Operators :58 (seven short passages).
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: do calibration, selective prediction and conformal prediction add an external anchor, and do they preserve per-slot information?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a grounded from an ungrounded "I am uncertain" without external labels, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
