## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a model can discriminate a grounded answer from a merely plausible but unsupported one, especially when the two produce the same fluent output. Option A: treat the model's own confidence as the discriminator. Option B: treat the discrimination as an external contact relation, keep the domain verdicts separate, and bind the sought-for shape as a refusable term. What each leads to: A launches a frame-internal score into the role of a verdict and spends the independence of anyone asked to check it; B yields a checkable shape, five written refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The same reading from two sources: grounded and plausible-only output look alike from inside. Arity: two conditions (detect a live residual, refuse the answer), so a union compound. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Symmetry: asymmetric and many-to-one, so supported maps to plausible-looking but not back. Scale: within one frame and across frames.
- **Domain 1, single model inside its own closure.** Actors: library Γ, belief b, action a, prediction Γ(b,a), confidence score. Currency: frame-internal scores (confidence, credibility, coverage, compression). Anchor: none reachable from inside. No nameable anchor, so this domain cannot settle anything; its verdict is a limit.
- **Domain 2, model ranged against a ground.** Actors: model output plus anchor (held-out data, measurement, experiment, human judgment where the question is about people). Currency: per-slot residual R against the anchor. Anchor: the ground, which does not move when the frame thinks.
- **Domain 3, model plus independent peer frame.** Actors: two or more frames with low evidence overlap. Currency: independence and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement.
- **Domain 4, direction of the question.** Actors: the sought-for shape as a preference. Currency: pre-registered prediction and refuter. Anchor: the ground plus a seat outside the frame.
- **Partition or union:** a union, not a partition. The same model appears in all four; what changes is what it is ranged against. Fig 9 :54: unbundle before searching again. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The chain breaks at link 3, the objective, because a verbalised "I am uncertain" has no instrument distinguishing a grounded one from a fluent one. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Strain test applied: Fig 8's metaframe theorems govern what a frame cannot do from inside; Fig 8 :54 governs what a union of frames can do from contact. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census. The price of negation is recorded as a live open item (Operators table :78), not smoothed over.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question does not admit one unqualified answer. Inside one frame, on its own confidence, no, and the reason is theorem-backed (Domain 1, `triangulated`). Against an external anchor, yes, as a checkable contact relation with calibration, selective prediction and conformal prediction as working instances (Domain 2, `triangulated`, `specified` on any particular model). With an independent peer frame, yes only under real evidence low-overlap (Domain 3, figure rule `triangulated`, ground application `single-source`). At the meta-level, the direction of the question cannot be checked from inside; binding the shape as a refusable term is the only remedy offered (Domain 4, `triangulated` for the limit). "I don't know" is a refusal, a unary negation whose price is open, not a low probability. The four verdicts are never averaged. The whole is `specified`, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** if a self-produced confidence is accepted as the discriminator, the plausible-but-unsupported answer is laundered into a verdict and the independence of outside readers is spent. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is high in medical, legal and safety uses, where a fluent unsupported answer is exactly the failure that matters. The cost is avoidable by leading with the per-slot signal and the refuter and buying a real anchor.
- **undoable:** yes. The remedies add contact (anchor, independent frame, calibration data, a refusal operator) without replacing the generator. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A defective claim can be superseded by the corrected packet and no primitive has to be withdrawn. Operators table :78 leaves the price of negation open, so no implementation is committed.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument showing that a per-slot signal tracks an external anchor out of distribution would move Domain 2 toward `grounded`; a method that abstains validly with no anchor and no second frame would break Domain 1 and 2; repeated samples from one model shown to draw on distinct evidence pools would break Domain 3; a figure line permitting self-certification, or a refutation of the metaframe theorems, would break the whole shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
