# What "shape" means for this question

Canonical reading from the figures and the eight essays. Sources read this session; those by the framework author are high-overlap corroboration, not independent witnesses.

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question, a **shape** is the form that any acceptable answer about model self-knowledge must have, stateable before we can name the mechanism that implements it. The shape is four-fold:

1. it is per-slot, not aggregate;
2. it is a contact relation to a ground the model cannot move, not a private sensation;
3. it requires a genuine second frame where the stakes are high;
4. it binds the sought-for shape as a refusable term before the run.

That is a constraint on answers, not an answer. It does not say which architecture, which calibration method or which number will do it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained here

The derive-first order was: state the residual in effect terms, census R by arity/symmetry/scale, census domains with actors/currency/anchor, then quote figure lines, then conclude. That is Fig 9 :27-28 applied to our own procedure: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."

The residual was described as "one reading, many possible sources; the reading does not comment on its source." Only after that were the fields (calibration, selective prediction, conformal prediction, metacognition) named. If the fields had been named first, any of them would have seemed to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." A calibration curve against held-out labels is a ground settlement for the claim "this score predicts correctness on this distribution." It is not a settlement of the claim "the model knows it does not know" in general.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Each world source in `10_source_ledger.csv` is such an import, with author, venue, year, accessed date, quote and declared overlap.

*They're Scanning Books* adds: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." The six-term substrate is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral.

### Correction to a possible over-reading (kept visible)

An earlier draft of this section said "the framework returns a shape, not a name, therefore no mechanism may be named." That is too strong. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. Here the relevant operators and terms (residual, anchor, negation, frame) are already in the framework. Naming an observed mechanism from a world frame is a binding, permitted with provenance. What is forbidden is claiming the framework *derives* the mechanism, or that naming it moves the wall (Fig 6 : "IMPORT adds an atom" adds none when the atom is already held). The narrower form stands; the over-stated form is retracted.

## 4. Why the question's own phrasing is the metamerism error

"The Hard Problem is About Source" gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source". One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

A fluent answer is exactly such a reading. Grounded knowledge and plausible fabrication can produce the same text. Asking the text (or the score attached to it) which source produced it is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument: held-out data, a retrieval trace with provenance, a conformal calibration set, an external check. Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

Seat Map :143 names the same failure in the arrangement vocabulary: the outside frame's undetectable failure is that "Its output looks identical whether it is reasoning or retrieving, and it cannot tell from inside."

## 5. What "know" means after the re-census

The verb "know" is not a single quantity in the framework. After the census it resolves into three different things, and conflating them is the source of the apparent paradox:

| sense of "know" | domain | verdict | figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| a private, privileged awareness of one's own grounding | A | unavailable in principle; the theorems bar self-certification | Fig 8 :34, :37 |
| a signal whose validity is established against an external ground | B | available, and implemented in engineering practice | Fig 2 :29; Fig 8 :54 |
| a correction from a frame with genuinely different evidence | C | available only with real independence | Fig 2 :31, :40 |

The ordinary-language question "can a model know when it does not know?" does not distinguish these. The framework does. The honest answer keeps them separate and refuses to average them.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A list of famous researchers who agree that models do or do not know themselves is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground, an instrument, a calibration set or an abstention experiment settle it. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. The world sources in `04` and `10` were read, but reading is not experimentation, and none of the results here are `grounded` on a measurement taken for this solution.
