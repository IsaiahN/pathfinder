## 0. The residual, stated first

### 0.1 The effect, not the theory

A binding increase in the wage floor injects a cost. The question asks which slot pays for it. Four candidate slots are named in the question itself: new hires, profit margins, prices, and turnover. The residual is the per-slot gap between what a frame predicts for each of those slots and what the ground returns.

`R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per slot `s`, where `s` is (firm type, worker group, region, margin), `a` is the floor change, and `o'` is the measured outcome in that slot.

### 0.2 Arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** four conditions (hiring, profit, price, turnover) over at least three margins (extensive, intensive, composition). Not a scalar.
- **Symmetry:** the floor is a threshold relation, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48, the `⋛` comparison bond). It is ordered, not a symmetric join. Raising a floor and lowering it are not inverse operations once firms have re-priced or re-staffed.
- **Scale:** firm and worker-group level first; aggregate employment rate second. The transform between them loses information (Fig 4 :27-30), so an aggregate estimate is a recording of one occasion, not the thing itself.

### 0.3 Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Labor market measurement | workers, jobs, firms, vacancies | employment counts, hours, job finding | payroll records, administrative data, surveys | partition of slots; which slot carries positive mass |
| D2 Firm cost absorption | firms, incumbent workers, consumers | margin, price, separation and recruitment cost, productivity | firm accounts, price indices, separation records | union of four overlapping channels over one cost atom |
| D3 Research and verification | studies, datasets, identification strategies, reviewers | estimates and residuals | the data, never agreement | triangulation; how much the frames share evidence |
| D4 Policy framing ("meaningfully", "absorb") | legislators, commissions, firms, workers | a target and a threshold | a declared decision rule | dependency link with no instrument yet |

**Partition or union.** D1 partitions into slots. D2 is a **union**, not a partition: a firm can pass cost through prices *and* reduce turnover *and* take a margin hit at once. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The question as posed bundles D1 through D4 into one lever, which Fig 9 :56 says *"clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing."*

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (can we see the slot?) before vocabulary (do we have terms for the four channels?) before objective (what counts as "meaningful"?) before planning (which data?) before learn and carry. D4 is the middle link and the one most likely to be broken: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27).

**Strain test applied before selection.** The six-term substrate (Fig 13) is tempting here: a wage floor looks like an ordering (spectrum), a cost differential like a spendable difference (gradient), and firms and workers like a network. That mapping only fits by forcing the figures to name empirical quantities they do not name, and Fig 13 governs primitive kinds, not this residual. It is recorded as a declined strain in section 6, not used as a finding.

### 0.4 The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term

Written down before the run so it can accrue a residual like anything else:

> **SHAPE:** A checkable answer to this question must (i) name the slot where the cost lands, (ii) state the margin on which the effect registers, (iii) distinguish the four absorption channels rather than averaging them, and (iv) identify an anchor that does not move when a model changes its assumptions.

An answer that reports one national employment elasticity and calls it settled fails this shape. That is a prediction about answers, written before the figure lines were applied, and it is refutable in section 3.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union, not one hard question (decisive for framing)

Fig 9 :54-56:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Raising the floor is the single lever. A firm may pass cost through prices, cut turnover, and absorb the rest in margin, while hiring falls only in the intersection where none of those channels is available. Asking "does hiring fall, or can firms absorb?" treats a union as a choice between two items. Fig 9 says the answer is a set of per-channel verdicts, not an either/or.

From *The Library of Babel* (read 2026-11-23): *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

**Refuter:** show that the four channels compose over a partition, that is, that at most one can fire for any firm in any period, so no intersection exists. Then the either/or framing is legitimate and this finding fails.

### F2 - The effect is indexed by slot; an average throws the signal away

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Here the slots are firm type, worker group, region, and margin. An economy-wide employment elasticity is exactly the average Fig 1 warns about. The Neumark and Wascher review (2006) reports that effects are *"stronger disemployment effects for these groups"* when the least-skilled are isolated; the Neumark and Shirley synthesis (2021) repeats the finding for teens, young adults, and the less-educated. Whether one agrees with their overall conclusion or not, the slot-dependence is their own reported result. So is the Low Pay Commission's geographic result: employment *"fell more in areas with the lowest youth coverage"*, which is the opposite of what a single national average would predict for the high-coverage slots.

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** Collapsing slots to a national number is the abstraction step; using that number to decide a local case is the concretisation, and the round trip gap `R_T` is unmeasured.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that the per-slot residuals are statistically indistinguishable across all firm types, worker groups, regions, and margins, so indexing by slot adds nothing and the average is sufficient.

### F3 - The anchor is external data, not a model's own estimate

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

For this question the anchors are payroll counts, hours, prices, profit statements, and separation records. A structural model's estimated employment effect is a frame: it updates when the identifying assumption changes. The Card and Krueger survey of 410 fast-food restaurants before and after the New Jersey increase is closer to an anchor because the payroll counts were collected rather than inferred. The Low Pay Commission's *"projected coverage"* of jobs is a frame reading: the same report says the projection moved because *"stronger than forecast wage growth in the second half of 2025"* changed the bite. Projection is not the anchor; the later payroll data are.

Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

**Refuter:** exhibit a measurement of employment or prices whose reported value changes when the researcher changes only the theoretical model, with no new data. Then it is a frame and cannot serve as the anchor for this question, and the claim that external data are the anchor is unsupported.

### F4 - Independence is the live failure mode in this field

Fig 2 :31-32: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."** Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Neumark and Shirley (2021) open their synthesis with the observation that *"the absence of agreement on what the research literature says"* is *"more puzzling"* than the disagreement among studies. Their abstract lists three incompatible summaries of the same literature: *"it is now well-established that higher minimum wages do not reduce employment"*, *"the evidence is very mixed with effects centered on zero"*, and *"most evidence points to adverse employment effects."* Three summaries, one pool. By Fig 2 :40 those are not three witnesses.

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 8 :49-50: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** The same quantity decides whether agreement means anything and whether a union of frames has surplus.

The genuinely low-overlap frames available here are: a US fast-food payroll survey (Card and Krueger), a synthesis of US subnational studies (Neumark and Wascher, Neumark and Shirley), and a UK institutional monitoring record (Low Pay Commission). They use different countries, different instruments, and different selection rules. They still share a broad evidence tradition, so this is moderate independence, not maximum.

**Refuter:** show that the US restaurant survey, the US literature synthesis, and the UK monitoring record draw on one dataset or one identifying assumption, so their agreement is one pool talking. Then the triangulation claim falls.

### F5 - The four absorption channels are isomers over one cost atom

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The cost atom is the same in all four cases: the incremental payroll obligation created by the higher floor. The bonds differ:

| channel | bond over the cost atom | who holds the cost | what the ground must measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| lower profit | absorbed in the margin | owner | firm-level margin and cash flow |
| higher price | passed to the buyer | consumer | product-level price, pass-through rate |
| lower turnover | recouped via retained workers | firm and incumbent worker | separation and recruitment cost |
| fewer hires | not absorbed | job seeker | extensive margin, vacancy duration |

To the question's own framing, these are the same substance by formula and different substances by bond. "Cost absorption" is the functional group; *"the qualifier says which member."* The figures do not select the member. That is a ground question, so the verdict on *which channel dominates* is `gap` after a genuine census, not a silence and not a figure verdict.

**Refuter:** show that any two of these channels compose over different atoms, for example that the turnover channel introduces a new primitive not present in the price channel, so the isomer reading is a false analogy. Then the functional-group reading stands but the isomer reading falls.

### F6 - Second-order contact can flip the first-order sign

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The first-order reading is: higher floor raises cost, so hiring falls. The cascade is: floor → retention and separation → recruitment cost and vacancy duration → hiring; and floor → product price → demand → hours and hiring. The residual that matters can be three steps out. The Low Pay Commission's own channel report says *"Businesses adapt to minimum wage increases in a combination of ways. Firms say that price increases are one of the main ways they deal with minimum wage increases"*, and separately that the aggregate inflation contribution was *"between 0.1-0.2 percentage points to inflation."* A firm-level channel and an aggregate effect are different slots, exactly as Fig 1 and Fig 11 require.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the second-order cascade is zero (no price response, no turnover response, no demand response) and the first-order employment effect still reverses sign. Then the contact-cascade explanation is unnecessary for that case and this finding is weakened to a scope condition.

### F7 - The empirical direction is imported, not derived

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No amount of re-reading the figures yields the sign of the employment effect at a given bite. That sign lives in data. Calling the question "unreachable" would be wrong: it is reachable, and the reach requires an import of empirical atoms (data), with provenance and a shadow test. Formula step 7 distinguishes *"underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget"*. This is the reachable kind. Reporting `gap` on channel dominance is an honest report of where this derivation stops, not a claim that the world cannot answer.

**Refuter:** derive the sign and magnitude of the employment effect for a specified bite from the figures alone, with no data import. If that succeeds, this finding is refuted.

### F8 - Describe the residual before choosing the frame

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 9 :53: **"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."**

The two standard frames here are the competitive model (floor above marginal product cuts employment) and the monopsony/efficiency-wage model (floor can raise employment). Picking one first and reading every study through it is the named apophenia. The residual was described in section 0 before either frame was named, in effect terms and by slot. The frames enter only in section 2 as candidate bonds, never as the selector.

**Refuter:** show that the section 0 description already entails one of the two frames, that is, that it uses a currency or an anchor available only inside that frame. Then the "frame second" order was cosmetic.

### F9 - "Meaningfully" is a dependency link with no instrument

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

"Meaningfully reduce hiring" needs a threshold: meaningful to whom, over what horizon, on which margin, against which counterfactual. The Low Pay Commission's remit supplies an anchor for the *rate* (two-thirds of median hourly earnings) but not for *"meaningful"*. Fig 3 :23 says a reading taken below the break is *"a reading of nothing"*. Until the threshold is declared as an ordinary term and measured, any verdict on "meaningfully" is a reading below the break.

**Refuter:** supply a declared, measurable threshold for "meaningful" that all four channels can be ranged against, then show this finding no longer applies.

### F10 - Filters are not witnesses

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Study tier, journal prestige, sample size, and methodology label are budget filters: they decide what gets read first. Only a ground reading settles a channel verdict. The most cited study in a pool is not the anchor; Fig 2 :35 says a mirror cannot correct you. This applies with special force to a field where the headline disagreement is partly about how to summarise the literature.

**Refuter:** identify a verdict in this folder that was produced by a filter alone. There is none; if one is found, this finding must be narrowed.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before outreach, per the framework's own discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in section 0.4 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single aggregate employment number is shown to be invariant across all firm types, worker groups, regions, and margins, so per-slot indexing adds nothing.
2. The four absorption channels are shown to compose over different atoms, so the isomer and functional-group reading fails.
3. An accepted anchor for this question is found to change when a frame changes only its assumptions, so the ground cannot settle it.
4. The union reading is refuted: one lever demonstrably clears all four conditions with no intersections left standing.
5. The sought-for shape itself is shown to be unbindable, that is, no answer to this question can name a slot, a margin, an unbundled channel, and an anchor at once.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2. The first thing a checker should see is that this is a union question and a per-slot residual, not a single elasticity.
2. State the mode on every claim. The figures decide the shape, not the sign.
3. Do not present any of the four channels as the figure's answer. They are candidate bonds; the ground selects. Say `gap` where the `gap` is.
4. Buy independence before precision: prefer two low-overlap anchors (different countries or different data systems) over three estimates drawn from one pool (Fig 2 :42).
5. Declare the threshold for "meaningful" as an ordinary term before measuring it (F9).
6. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Lead with the refuter and the shape; offer the links after the verdict on stage 1 is recorded.

## 6. Verdict of the audit

The shape survives; the question as posed does not. The figures licence four structural claims (union, per-slot indexing, external anchor, independence) and one structural analogy (four channels as isomers over one cost atom). They do not licence a verdict on the sign or size of the employment effect, nor on which absorption channel dominates at a given bite. Those are `gap` after a genuine census, and the gap is owed to the ground, not to another figure line. After re-census: **one declined strain (Fig 13 mapping), one retracted reading (catalyst), one retracted source classification (projection as anchor), one narrowed claim (F1), and one corrected mode word (F7).** Nothing was smoothed over.
