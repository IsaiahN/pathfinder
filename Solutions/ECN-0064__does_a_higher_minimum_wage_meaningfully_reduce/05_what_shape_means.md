## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The local canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, then stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**

*The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For ECN-0064 the shape is:

> an answer must name the slot where the cost lands, the margin on which the effect registers, the channel that absorbs it, and the measurement that would settle it.

That constrains. It does not say that hiring falls, that prices rise, or that turnover offsets the cost. Those are names, and names are owed to the ground.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: proximity and bundle detection

*Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*

Here the starting vocabulary was the question's own four candidates: hiring, profit, price, turnover. Proximity added the margins (extensive, intensive, composition) and the slots (firm type, worker group, region). Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The question's words ("absorb", "meaningfully") are borrowed; the effect terms (margin, price, separation, vacancy) are the ones that can be ranged against data.

The same essay gives the bundle test directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is the essay-level source for F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, and it is a structural claim, not an empirical finding about minimum wage.

## 3. What licenses a name: a source-discriminating instrument

*The Hard Problem is About Source* is the most directly relevant essay for this question. Its core claim: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and an instrument that reads the output *"cannot comment on the source."* A colorimeter reads green whether the source was one green light or a mixture. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

Applied here: an employment decline is a reading. It can be produced by a price channel (demand falls), a profit channel (the firm exits or cuts vacancies), a turnover channel (a vacancy opens less often because incumbents stay), or by no absorption at all. The employment number cannot comment on which source produced it. Naming "the employment effect" as if it named the source is the metamerism error. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources: matched firm-level price, margin, separation, and vacancy data, not a more careful argument about the employment reading.

Corroboration from the world sources, kept secondary: the Low Pay Commission reports both a firm-level price response and a small aggregate inflation contribution. Those are two readings of one wage change at different slots, and they do not determine one another.

## 4. Shape, union, and the limit of this derivation

Combining the canonical rule with Fig 9 :54-58: when the residual splits into a union, the shape an answer must have is a **set of per-channel verdicts with their own anchors**, not a single number. The framework can return that shape. It cannot return which channel fires, because channel selection is a name, and naming it requires the source-discriminating instrument of section 3.

So this question is not a `gap` in the sense of "nothing can be said". It is a `gap` on two specific fields: which channel dominates at a given bite, and whether the change is "meaningful" under a declared threshold. Both are reachable by data import. Section 7 of `THE_FORMULA.md` distinguishes *"underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget"*; this is the reachable kind.

## 5. Prestige is not a witness

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* More fully: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

In this field the prestige proxy is the most-cited study or the most authoritative literature summary. Neumark and Shirley (2021) document that even the one-line summaries of the literature disagree. By Fig 2 :40, shared-pool agreement is disqualifying for a verdict. Asking a well-known economist to endorse the shape claim would give it a budget to be tried; it would not settle it. The outreach file asks for a refutation instead.

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `06_isomers_and_order.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the minimum wage question. Nothing in this folder may be reported as settling the sign or size of an employment effect.

## 7. Correction to my own §1 and §3

I initially read *"a shape, not a name"* as a ban on naming any of the four channels. That overstates it. The canonical sentence governs a **missing thing**: a primitive not held in the library. The four channels are not missing primitives of a metatheory; they are empirical candidates in a labor market, and the figures do not hold or lack them.

The correct reading here is narrower and stronger: naming a channel as *the* answer is unsupported until an instrument discriminates among the sources of the employment reading (section 3), and the canonical rule's actual scope is missing primitives. Both statements are kept so neither is quietly lost. The `shape-not-a-name` phrase is used in this folder only as an analogy for channel selection, and is labelled as such wherever it appears.
