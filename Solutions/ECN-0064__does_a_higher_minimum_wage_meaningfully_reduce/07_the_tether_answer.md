# The Tether's answer on minimum wage and hiring

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-channel predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A binding increase in the wage floor injects a cost. The question asks which slot pays: new hires, incumbent margins, consumer prices, or separation costs. The residual is the per-slot gap between predicted and observed outcomes across those four channels and their margins. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The question as posed invites an either/or. The figures refuse it. A firm can raise prices, retain workers, take a smaller margin, and still cut vacancies in the intersection where those responses are insufficient (Fig 9 :56).

## 2. The shape

1. **Report the effect per slot, never only as an aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** The slots here are firm type, worker group, region, and margin. The world literature itself reports this: Neumark and Wascher (2006) find stronger effects for the least skilled; the Low Pay Commission (2026) finds youth employment fell more where youth coverage was lowest. Different signs, same slot-dependence.

2. **The anchor is external measurement, not a model estimate.** Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Payroll counts, prices, profit statements and separation records are anchors. A structural estimate is a frame; it moves when its identifying assumption moves.

3. **Agreement cannot decide a channel verdict.** Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."** Neumark and Shirley (2021) document that researchers disagree even about what the literature says, quoting three incompatible summaries of one pool. Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

4. **The four channels are isomers over one cost atom.** Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** "Cost absorption" is the functional group; Fig 12 :124: **"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The figures do not select the member.

5. **Second-order cascades can flip the first-order sign.** Fig 11 :52: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** The wage cost is first order; price and retention responses are further out; hiring sits beyond them. A study that measures only the direct wage-to-hiring step can miss the channel that reverses it.

6. **The floor itself is a threshold, not a cause of a particular channel.** Operators table :48: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* The comparison predicts that a cut exists; it says nothing about which side of the firm adjusts. Naming the bond as the answer is the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 :5 names.

7. **"Meaningfully" is a link with no instrument yet.** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Meaningful to whom, over what horizon, on which margin, against which counterfactual? Until that is declared and measured, any verdict on "meaningfully" is a reading below the break.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a higher minimum wage reduces hiring, and not that it does not. The figures do not return an empirical sign.
- Not that firms can absorb the cost through any particular channel. The four channels are candidate bonds; the ground selects among them.
- Not that the competitive model or the monopsony model is the right frame. The residual was described before either was named (Fig 9 :27-28).
- Not that the literature's disagreement is purely methodological. The disagreement is substantive and partly about the same shared pool (F4).
- Not that any cited study is definitive, and not that citation count or prestige is a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not `grounded`. No employment, price, profit, or turnover measurement was taken in this folder.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument that discriminates sources, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads the output *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The spectrometer resolved the colour case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **The discriminator test:** at the same wage change, do matched firm records separately report price, margin, separation, and vacancy adjustments? If yes, the isomer claim can be settled per firm. If the data show only an employment total, the source remains unnamed.
- **The slot test:** are the residuals distinguishable by worker group, firm type and region? If they are, the per-slot claim is settled in one direction; if a single number is invariant across slots, F2 is refuted.
- **The anchor test:** does the reported employment figure change when only the modelling assumption changes? If it does, it is a frame, not the anchor.
- **The threshold declaration:** state what "meaningful" means in units and horizon before measuring it, so the objective link of Fig 3 has an instrument.
- **Standing refuters from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3:** partition instead of union; no slot heterogeneity; a moving anchor; one lever clearing all conditions; an unbindable shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reachable, not yet reached.** The channel selection is not underivable in principle; it requires a data import with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :39). This derivation stops at the limit of what the figures can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Two fields are honestly `gap`: which channel dominates at a given bite, and whether the effect is "meaningful" under a declared threshold. *"Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence"* applies to the figures' own reach; here the reach is the world's, and the world can answer it. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether a higher minimum wage reduces hiring. It says the question as posed is the wrong shape. Hiring, profit, prices and turnover are four overlapping channels over the same cost, so a single employment elasticity cannot tell you which one fired; the answer has to be per firm, per worker group, per region and per margin. The anchor for each channel is external data that does not move when a model changes its assumptions, and agreement among studies drawing on one literature is one witness, not several. The four channels stand to each other as isomers: same cost, different arrangement, different behaviour. Which one dominates is a name the figures do not return; it is owed to matched price, margin, separation and vacancy data. That is the shape any answer must have, with five refuters written in advance. The empirical sign stays open until the ground settles it.
