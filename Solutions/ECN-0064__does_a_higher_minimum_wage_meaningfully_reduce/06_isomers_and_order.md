# Are the four absorption channels isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `general` on the structural analogy, `specified` on the per-channel predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The question names four ways a firm can deal with a higher wage floor: lower profit, higher prices, lower turnover, and fewer hires. Two questions are owed: (1) are those four different substances over one atom, or four different atoms, and (2) is there an order among them that the figures license?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in this derivation: an incremental payroll cost, a wage floor, a worker, a job, a firm, a buyer. Bonds held: the seven, including `⋛` comparison and `→` sequence, plus negation for refusals. No empirical atom is available without a data import (Fig 6 :23). The four channels are arrangements over the cost atom, not new atoms.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the channels isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Written as arrangements over the same incremental cost `C`:

```
C → (margin)      lower profit
C → (price)       pass to buyer
C → (retention)   lower turnover
C → (vacancy)     fewer hires
```

Same atom `C`, different bond, different substance. To the question's own framing these are isomers: invisibly different in a formula that records only "the cost of the increase", and decisive in behaviour, because a firm that passes the cost to buyers and a firm that cuts vacancies behave differently in the data.

**Caveat, named as a strain rather than hidden.** The price channel introduces a buyer who is not present in the other three. Re-census: the shared atom is the *cost*, not the cast of actors. Fig 12's definition turns on atoms and bonds, and the buyer is an operand of the price bond, not a new atom replacing the cost. The isomer reading survives the re-census with this scope condition.

### Q2 - What is the functional group?

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

"Cost absorption" is the group: it says what kind of response is under discussion. The qualifier (margin, price, retention, vacancy) says which member fires. This is the form in which the shape claim should be sent: a group with four named members and a refuter for the claim that they are one thing.

The important consequence: finding a firm that raised prices does **not** refute a finding that hiring fell in the intersection where price pass-through was insufficient. Fig 9 :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

### Q3 - Is there an order among the channels?

Two different orders must not be confused.

- **The order of the inquiry** is licensed by Fig 3 : dependency runs from perception (which slot is readable?) to vocabulary (which channel?) to objective (what counts as meaningful?) to planning (which data?). This order is derivable from the figures.
- **The causal order among channels** is not licensed by the figures. Which response fires first depends on contract length, menu costs, labour turnover, and market structure. Those are empirical quantities. Any ordering I would write here would be a name where the figures return a shape.

What the figures do license is a contact order (Fig 11 :52): *"first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Price and retention responses are not first-order neighbours of the floor; they sit one or two steps further out, and hiring sits again beyond them. A first-order-only study will miss them. This is a scope claim, not a timing claim.

**Falsifier for this part:** if a dataset shows all four channels responding in the same period with no lag structure, the contact-order claim adds nothing and should be dropped.

### Q4 - Correction to my own first reading: the catalyst

I first mapped lower turnover to Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* That was a strain. A catalyst is a part whose persistence across uses is the point. Turnover reduction is a bond over the cost atom; its persistence is not what the figure governs.

**Retracted as applied.** The functional-group and isomer readings (Q1, Q2) carry the finding. The catalyst sentence is kept here as evidence of the correction, not silently erased. Re-census followed the canonical rule that a line needing a strain governs another quantity.

### Q5 - The threshold bond is licensed, but it does not decide

The floor is `⋛` comparison, defined in the Operators table as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Applying it to the floor wage and a worker's productivity or a firm's willingness to pay is a legitimate structural reading.

It predicts only that a cut exists: some workers fall below the new floor and some do not. It does **not** predict which side of the firm adjusts, because the comparison bond carries no information about the absorbing channel. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Naming the bond as the answer would be the flat-formula collapse.

## CONCLUSION

The four channels are **isomers over one cost atom** under Fig 12's definition, with the buyer-as-operand scope condition recorded in Q1. "Cost absorption" is a **functional group** whose qualifier names the member (Q2). The figures license the dependency order of the inquiry and a contact order of effects, but not a causal timing among channels (Q3). The `⋛` reading of the floor is licensed but underdetermines the absorbing channel (Q5). The catalyst reading was retracted (Q4).

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without pretending the figures settle it: no new primitive, no import that moves a wall, and no answer to the empirical sign question. What remains open is which member fires at a given bite, and that is owed to a source-discriminating instrument (section 3 of `05_what_shape_means.md`).
