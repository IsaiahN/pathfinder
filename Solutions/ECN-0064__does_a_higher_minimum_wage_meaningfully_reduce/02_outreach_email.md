# Outreach draft - check the shape of the question before you decide about the answer

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter to a public submission route listed in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until section 3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is read and the five refuters are understood. The offer collapses on contact if the recipient cannot tell what would refute it.

---

**Subject:** One structural claim about minimum wage studies, checked before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, not `grounded` on the empirical direction):** "Does a higher minimum wage meaningfully reduce hiring, or can firms absorb it through lower profits, prices, or turnover?" is not one question. It is a union of at least four overlapping channels over the same cost: the margin, the product price, separation costs, and the hiring margin. A firm can use several at once. So a single employment elasticity does not answer it. An answer has to name the slot, the margin, the channel, and the measurement that would settle it.
>
> That is a claim about the **shape** of a legitimate answer. It is not a claim about the sign or size of any employment effect. I am deliberately not saying whether moderate increases reduce hiring, or which channel dominates. The figures I use return a shape, not a name, and the channel selection is owed to data.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the competitive model is right, that the monopsony model is right, that any particular study is definitive, or that the literature's disagreement is purely methodological. The literature's disagreement is real and partly substantive. What I am claiming is narrower: an answer that reports one aggregate number and calls it settled has dropped the signal that matters for the decision.
>
> **What would refute the claim. I wrote these down before this message:**
> 1. show that the four channels form a partition, so at most one can fire for a firm in a period and there are no intersections;
> 2. show that the residuals are indistinguishable across firm types, worker groups, regions, and margins, so indexing by slot adds nothing;
> 3. show an accepted employment or price measurement whose value changes when only the theoretical model changes, so the anchor is not external;
> 4. show that one lever clears all four conditions at once with no residual intersections;
> 5. show that no answer to this question can name a slot, a margin, an unbundled channel, and an anchor together.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you, in this order:** first, what would you count as a failure of the shape claim? Second, when a wage floor bites in your data or your model, which margin returns the first readable signal, and what instrument reads it? I will record that answer before showing you anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the third link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Shape claim before any theory | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient ranges the claim against their own ground first. |
| No request for agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Refuters written before the message | The framework's pre-registration discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| No sign, no magnitude, no named channel | Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 12 gives the isomer definition but does not select the member. |
| Ask which margin returns a readable signal | Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* |
| Do not pick the competitive or monopsony frame first | Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* |
| Links only after the stage-1 verdict | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |
| No prestige ask, no "well-known reviewer" shortcut | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Hand the formula over as a method | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |

## What to do with the reply

Record the stage-1 verdict in `11_audit.md` with provenance before showing any theory. If the person returns a refutation, it is copied in with its source and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: by Fig 2 :40, agreement within a shared evidence pool is the pool talking.
