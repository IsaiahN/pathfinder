## Restating the question as an effect

The observed residual is a persistent asymmetry: some methods are trusted, funded, taught, and their outputs are taken to settle disputes, while others stay marginal even when they make comparable claims. Option A explains the asymmetry as the victory of the better method (authority tracks ground contact). Option B explains it as a compound of ground contact, community verification, habitat, transmission, and closure, in which authority can be earned either by anchoring or by convention. What each leads to: A predicts that the authoritative set is truth-tracking and that more agreement means more evidence; B predicts two distinct kinds of authority, mirror chains that compound agreement without evidence, and a stop at the limit of what current instruments distinguish. The answer below is bound as the ordinary term *authority* so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.
