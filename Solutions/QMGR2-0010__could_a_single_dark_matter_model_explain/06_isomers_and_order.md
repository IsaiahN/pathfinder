# Are competing dark-matter models isomers, and what order do the slots compose in?

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer and order claims, `general` on the structural rule, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The single-model question leaves two supporting questions open:

1. If several dark-matter candidates are written over a shared set of primitives, are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or only members of a functional group? And if they are isomers, what exactly is the bond that differs?
2. In which **order** do the four slots compose, and does that order make a single closure more or less able to reach all four?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available in the physics frame's library: a gravitating component, an equation of state, a sound speed, an interaction cross section, a phase-space distribution (hot, warm, cold), a density profile. Bonds available: the seven typed relations plus negation. No new framework primitive is admitted; the six-term substrate of Fig 13 remains the loop's substrate, not a particle taxonomy (see S1 of `01`).

Slots: early universe, clusters, lensing, galaxies, ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20). The early universe sets the cold dark matter density and initial perturbations; the other three are downstream.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the isomer claim even well posed? CORRECTION TO MY OWN SECTION 3 of `05`

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- The candidates do not obviously share one atom set. Cold dark matter has a phase-space distribution and essentially no pressure or self-interaction; warm dark matter adds a mass and primordial velocity; self-interacting dark matter adds a cross section; a unified fluid adds an equation of state and sound speed.
- ⇒ The isomer claim is **conditional** on writing them over a shared atom set, for example `{gravitating component, equation of state, sound speed, interaction, phase space}`. Over that set they are different bonded arrangements: the difference is in which bond is active, not in an extra ingredient. This is a binding convention from the physics frame, recorded as such.
- **I overstated this earlier.** I first treated all candidates as automatically isomers because they all "are dark matter." They are only isomers under an explicit shared-atom convention. Retracted as unconditional; kept as conditional (S2 of `01`). Triangulated (Fig 12 plus the explicit atom listing).

### Q2 - What is the differing bond?

- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- Cold, collisionless dark matter is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching, which Fig 12 :48 calls a chain ("A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching").
- A unified dark fluid adds a pressure bond between the gravitating component and the expansion, so the ingredient list alone no longer determines the behaviour. El-Zant 2015 is the empirical demonstration: the same "single dark component" that behaves as pressureless matter at high density cannot support flat or falling rotation curves in galaxy outers once the pressure gradient is significant, and its cluster outer profile is incompatible with the inferred one.
- Self-interacting dark matter adds a scattering bond whose presence fixes the halo core outcome, which is Fig 12 :123's functional group: "a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two candidates can be shown to require atoms the other lacks, so no shared atom set exists, the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group claim stands. This is the live strain S2.

### Q3 - What is the order of the four slots?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Order by dependency, not by how intuitive the slot is:
  1. **Early universe**: fix Ω_c h² and the primordial perturbation spectrum. The Particle Data Group CMB review quotes Ω_c h² = 0.1200 ± 0.0012 in the six-parameter fit and calls the acoustic scale "the most precisely measured observable." This is the perception-plus-vocabulary link.
  2. **Clusters**: the largest collapsed structures, closest to the linear initial conditions. Lensing mass measurements here are geometry-direct.
  3. **Lensing** as a cross-cutting probe: not simply downstream of clusters, because it supplies the independent anchor that mass is displaced from gas (the Bullet Cluster result).
  4. **Galaxies**: smallest, most baryon-contaminated, furthest from clean initial conditions, and the slot where the small-scale tensions live.
- A model that fits slot 4 while disagreeing with slot 1 has a break at the middle link of Fig 3's chain. Work at the downstream end does not arrive where it is needed. The 2025 clustered unified dark sector result (CMB secondary anisotropies forcing the clustered fraction toward unity while late structure data prefer values away from it) is this inversion made empirical.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** demonstrate that the dependency order for dark matter runs galaxies-first, so the CMB peaks are a downstream consequence of galaxy-scale physics rather than an initial condition. Then this order claim fails (F3, refuter 5 in `01`).

### Q4 - Does the settled-molecule rule apply across the slots?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- The CMB molecule is settled (the standard model fits the data well, with shrinking error bars, per the PDG review). It can therefore serve as an atom for the structure-formation composition at the downstream slots. This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not a new import.
- Type caveat, still open: the structure-formation molecule includes baryons and feedback as additional atoms, so the composition is not the CMB atom alone. Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." The migration from an upstream settled result to a downstream prediction is lossy; Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **Falsifier:** if a downstream slot can be predicted from the CMB atom with no loss and no additional atoms, the gap is zero and this caveat is withdrawn. Given baryonic feedback (Bullock and Boylan-Kolchin 2017: "whether a better understanding of baryon physics, dark matter physics, or both is required"), that is unlikely, but it is an empirical question, not a figure ruling.

## CONCLUSION

The competing models are **conditional isomers**: over an explicit shared atom set they are same atoms with different active bonds, decisive at galaxy and cluster scales, with a functional-group fallback if no shared set exists. The four slots compose in a dependency order that runs early universe first and galaxies last, with lensing cross-cutting as an independent anchor. A single closure's reach must be assessed along that order, because a downstream tune cannot rescue an upstream break. The settled CMB molecule becomes an atom for structure formation, but the migration is lossy and the round-trip gap is owed.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the single-model question without buying the aggregation: the isomer test and the order claim need no new framework primitive and no naming of a seventh term. Which candidate occupies the shape remains a `gap` (F5 of `01`). The isomer claim carries one live strain (S2) and the order claim one falsifier (Q3), both written before this conclusion.
