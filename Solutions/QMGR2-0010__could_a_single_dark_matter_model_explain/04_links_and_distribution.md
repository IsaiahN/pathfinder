## Four things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **The asymmetry is real and measured.** Bullock and Boylan-Kolchin 2017 and the PNAS 2012 review agree that ΛCDM succeeds on large scales and meets persistent challenges below about a Mpc. This is why the single-model question must be indexed by slot (F1): an averaged answer would be "works" and would hide the live signal.
2. **The single-component idea has a precise failure mode.** El-Zant 2015 shows that a unified dark fluid's pressure gradient makes flat or falling rotation curves impossible for a broad class of equations of state and produces outer cluster profiles incompatible with the inferred ones. This is a bond-level difference, not a missing ingredient, and it is the canonical case for the field named in the contact brief.
3. **The dependency chain is empirically visible.** The 2025 clustered unified dark sector study finds CMB data forcing the clustered fraction toward one while late-universe structure data prefer values away from one. That is Fig 3's dependency inversion made observable: tuning the downstream slot does not rescue an upstream mismatch.
4. **Scale dependence is not a defect of one model alone.** Anderson 1972, from a completely different evidence pool (condensed matter and the hierarchy of sciences), argues that "at each level of complexity entirely new properties appear." This corroborates the per-slot shape without carrying any dark-matter conclusion. It is one low-overlap witness, not a second copy of the cosmology literature.
