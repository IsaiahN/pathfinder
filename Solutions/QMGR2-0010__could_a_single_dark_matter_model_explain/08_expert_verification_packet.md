## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "could a single dark-matter model explain galaxies, clusters, gravitational lensing, and the early universe?" bundles four differently anchored measurements into one predicate. The framework says a residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated, so the honest form is four indexed residuals on a dependency chain running early universe first and galaxies last. Whether one closure reaches all four is a reachability question, not a matter of trying harder; where a slot fails, an import from a frame whose closure differs is owed, with provenance and a shadow test. The unconditional all-explaining model is a last room and is refused, but a null at one slot is not a universal "no." The framework returns a shape any candidate must have, with four named anchors and a refuter per slot, and does not name a particle or a fluid. Which model is correct is a recorded gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and structural; checkable by reading Figures 1, 3 and 9

- **Statement:** the four phenomena must be treated as four indexed slots in a dependency order, not as one averaged residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 3 :20 - "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 9 :54 - "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** does the early universe genuinely sit upstream of galaxies for this question, in the sense that the CMB fixes the dark-matter density and the initial perturbations the other slots inherit? Is lensing a cross-cutting independent anchor rather than merely downstream of clusters?
- **Refuter:** show that the four slots share one atom and one anchor such that one global residual suffices (breaks the per-slot claim); or demonstrate that the dependency order runs galaxies-first so the CMB is downstream (breaks the order claim).

### C2 - reachability; checkable by a cosmologist against the candidate models

- **Statement:** whether one closure reaches all four slots is a reachability question; a single unified component struggles specifically at the galaxy and cluster slots and at the CMB-versus-structure junction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 - "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32 - "no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 7 :6 - "the limit: no last room."
- **What to check:** is the reach characterization faithful? El-Zant 2015 (MNRAS) reports that a unified dark fluid's pressure gradient makes flat or falling rotation curves impossible for a broad class of barotropic equations of state and gives cluster outer profiles incompatible with the inferred ones unless the sound speed is tuned to extreme values. A 2025 clustered unified dark sector study reports that CMB secondary anisotropies force the clustered fraction toward unity while late large-scale-structure data prefer values away from it. Do those results stand, and do they support a per-slot reach reading rather than a blanket verdict?
- **Refuter:** exhibit one closure that settles all four per-slot residuals at once against independent anchors with no imported atom unaccounted for. That would break the reachability framing and the union verdict.

### C3 - shape-not-a-name and isomer claim; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the framework returns a shape, not a model; competing candidates are conditional isomers over a shared atom set, with a functional-group fallback.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 12 :117 - "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :104 - "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- **What to check:** can cold, warm, self-interacting and unified-fluid models be written over a shared atom set (gravitating component, equation of state, sound speed, interaction, phase space)? If yes, the isomer claim is well posed; if no, the functional-group fallback applies. Does the settled CMB molecule legitimately become an atom for structure formation, with a lossy round trip that is owed?
- **Refuter:** show that two candidates require atoms the other lacks, so no shared atom set exists (kills the isomer claim, leaves the functional-group claim); or exhibit a figure line that selects a specific dark-matter model (kills the shape-not-a-name claim and the gap).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument at the failing slot, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. A per-slot closure test at each of the four slots against a non-updating anchor.
2. An upper-chain test: does the candidate reproduce Ω_c h² and the acoustic peak structure before being tuned at galaxy scales? The PDG review quotes Ω_c h² = 0.1200 ± 0.0012.
3. A single-fluid discriminator: do unified-fluid outer profiles match flat or falling rotation curves and NFW-like lensing profiles? El-Zant 2015 says no for a broad class of equations of state.
4. A cross-slot consistency test: do CMB secondary anisotropies and late structure pull the model's clustered fraction the same way? The 2025 study says they do not.
5. Any of the five pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4.
