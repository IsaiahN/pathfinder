# Expert verification packet: the Tether on early energy sources

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist, geochemist or astrobiologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the substrate claim. Not `grounded`.** No flow-through experiment is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Energy by itself cannot power a self-maintaining chemical system, because it is conserved, finite and directionless on its own. The spendable thing is a gradient, a difference with direction; energy plus a gradient composes a flow. So the earliest self-maintaining systems were powered by a sustained difference delivered as a flow through an open reaction cycle whose catalyst regenerated, at a site whose habitat kept renewing the difference. Spatial and temporal gradients are different terms under the figure rule, so a vent redox or pH difference and a UV or wet-dry flux are not spellings of one source. The framework does not name which gradient did it on Earth: that slot stays `gap` until an instrument discriminates among sources. What is offered is the shape, four refuters written in advance, and the experiment that would close the slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 13

- **Statement:** energy alone is not a candidate answer. The spendable term is a gradient, and the power term is a flow composed of energy plus a gradient. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :14 *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*; Fig 13 :22 *"a difference that can be spent"*; Fig 13 :71 *"no direction to drive the flow, and no remainder left after each step"*; Fig 13 :76 *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a self-maintaining chemical network in a closed, equilibrated system with no through-flowing difference and no remainder per step; or show a spatial and a temporal gradient are interchangeable at a named site with no change in which reactions run.

### C2: chemistry-facing; checkable by a prebiotic chemist against reaction networks

- **Statement:** "self-maintaining" requires a cycle whose output re-enters as input and a catalyst that is regenerated, not spent. A long reaction pathway is not a self-maintaining system.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** in the system you know best, does the proposed maintenance mechanism actually close a loop, and is the catalyst demonstrably regenerated across more turnovers than the initial pool could supply? Is the system open to through-flow?
- **Refuter:** a named prebiotic system in which the catalyst is spent on first use and turnover nevertheless continues; or a maintained network with no cycle.

### C3: geochemistry-facing; checkable against habitats and source valence

- **Statement:** capability belongs to the network and its habitat, and the valence of any particular source is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The habitat renews the difference but is not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*; Fig 11 :49 *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"*; Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual"*; Fig 11 :73 *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*
- **What to check:** does the same source (UV, a redox couple, a thermal gradient) change valence when the network or the residual changes? Rimmer and Shorttle 2019 report that UV is *"constructive for synthesizing and activating nucleotides and amino acids"* but *"destructive for more complex organic systems, such as, for example, DNA strands."* That is the figure claim observed in the literature.
- **Refuter:** a source whose measured valence is invariant across two habitats with reversed conditions; or a gradient renewed with no habitat contact; or a habitat whose renewal leaves the network's residual unchanged.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named source. Not UV, not serpentinization, not alkaline hydrothermal vents, not wet-dry cycling. Each is a binding, not a figure output.
- Not that the earliest system used only one source. The two dominant literature classes answer different residuals and form a union (Fig 9 :57).
- Not that the habitat is the ground. It updates; the ground settles (Fig 11 :73).
- Not an import that moves the wall. Compositions from the six add no atom (Fig 6 :21).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet pays for the shape at a named site.
- Not a theory of everything. There is no last room, and the earliest system is a first-order site.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating experiment, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Concretely:

1. A flow-through reactor at a named site whose gradient, cycle closure, catalyst regeneration and habitat renewal are each measured, with the residual reported per site and per gradient rather than averaged (Fig 1 :19).
2. A swap test: exchange two candidate sources at the same site and observe whether the reactions that run change. If they do not, the two are one term and the isomer claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` falls.
3. A closed equilibrated control that nevertheless maintains the network over many turnovers. If it works, the shape claim is refuted outright.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :14, :22, :71, :76 and Fig 12 :119, :121, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a network you know: is it a cycle with a regenerating catalyst, or only a pathway?
3. Name one measurement that would distinguish a spatial gradient source from a temporal flux source at a site you work on, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
