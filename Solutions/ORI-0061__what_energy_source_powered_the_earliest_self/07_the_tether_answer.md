# The Tether's answer on the earliest energy source

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the substrate claim. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the energy source of the earliest self-maintaining chemical systems. In effect terms, what failed to resolve is why a reaction network at a named site keeps predicting correctly (its reactions keep running, its catalyst keeps regenerating) while other networks at other sites drift to equilibrium. The phrase "energy source" bundles three conditions: a spendable difference supplied, a cycle that closes, and a habitat that renews the difference. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Here there are three, so it is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Energy alone cannot be the answer.** Fig 13 :14: it is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone."* What can be spent is a gradient, *"a difference that can be spent"* (Fig 13 :22), whose directedness is the thing that drives a flow (Fig 13 :71). The question's own wording, "energy source," is a category error if read literally.
2. **The power term is a flow, a composition across kinds.** Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."* Energy is a state, gradient is a structure, so this is a cross-kind composition (Fig 13 :74). It carries directedness that neither operand carries alone.
3. **Self-maintenance is a cycle with a regenerating catalyst, held open.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A long reaction pathway is not a self-maintaining system.
4. **Spatial and temporal gradients are different terms.** Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."* A pH or redox difference across a vent membrane and a periodic photon or wet-dry flux are not spellings of one source. Their difference has the structure of Fig 12 :117 isomerism.
5. **Capability belongs to the network and its habitat, not to the network alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The habitat renews the difference (Fig 11 :73), and gives an actor its valence (Fig 11 :49). The same UV flux is constructive for building blocks and destructive for complex strands.
6. **The particular source is not named.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework returns the shape any candidate answer must have. Naming a specific gradient is a binding from the geochemistry or photochemistry frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation. It carries zero evidentiary weight until a source gate and shadow test are supplied. After a genuine re-census this slot is `gap`, not silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "the answer is UV light," and not "the answer is hydrothermal vents." Both are bindings, not figure outputs.
- Not that the earliest system used only one source. The two dominant literature classes answer different residuals (feedstock synthesis versus sustained habitat), so they are a union compound (Fig 9 :57), not one term.
- Not that any source is "the ground." The habitat is not the ground (Fig 11 :73); a flow-through measurement is.
- Not that any named binding is an import that moves the wall. Compositions from the six add no atom (Fig 6 :21).
- Not `grounded`. No experiment in this package paid for `left(R,φ)` at a named site.
- Not a theory of everything, and no last room: the earliest system is a first-order site, and whatever composes over it is a second-order question.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating experiment, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does a measured gradient at a named site pay for the closed catalytic cycle, with the network's residual measured **per site and per gradient**, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22)?
- Does the cycle close by re-entering its output, and is the catalyst regenerated rather than spent across more turnovers than the initial pool allows (Fig 12 :119, :121)?
- Does the habitat renew the difference at the rate the cycle spends it (Fig 11 :73; Fig 10 :24)?
- **Discriminator:** can two candidate sources be swapped at the same site with no change in which reactions run? If yes, they are one term and the isomer claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` falls. If no, the instrument that distinguishes them has been found and a name becomes admissible.
- Standing falsifiers from the audit: a closed equilibrated system that maintains itself; a system with no cycle; a source whose valence is invariant across reversed habitats; a habitat whose renewal leaves the residual unchanged; a derivation of one of the six from the other five.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the energy source of the earliest self-maintaining chemical systems, and it does not claim to. Energy by itself is directionless, so it cannot be the answer. What can be spent is a gradient, and energy plus a gradient composes a flow. So the answer is a shape: a sustained, spendable difference delivered through an open reaction cycle whose catalyst regenerates, at a site whose habitat keeps renewing the difference. Spatial and temporal gradients are different terms, so a vent membrane and a photon flux are not spellings of one source. The framework then stops. Which particular gradient did it on Earth is a gap, because the instrument that would discriminate among sources is not held. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the experiment that would close the slot.
