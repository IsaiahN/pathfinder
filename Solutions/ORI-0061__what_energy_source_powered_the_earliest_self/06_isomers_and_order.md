# Which gradient, which order? Spatial versus temporal differences, cycles, and analogues

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit establishes the shape: a spendable difference bonded to a state, delivered as a flow through a closed catalytic cycle, in a habitat that renews the difference. It withholds the name. Two questions are still owed:

2. In what **order** must the conditions C1 (supply), C2 (closure) and C3 (habitat renewal) be satisfied?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is a spatial gradient a different term from a temporal one?

- Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
- Therefore a pH or redox difference across a vent membrane (gradient bonded to space) and a periodic photon or wet-dry flux (gradient bonded to time) are **not variants of one source**. They are different molecules under the figure's own rule. Which state the gradient bonds to is part of the term.
- This is the isomer case in Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Same atoms (energy plus gradient); different bond-to-state; different substance.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a spatial and a temporal gradient can be swapped at a named site with no change in which reactions run and no change in the network's residual, the isomer claim fails and the two are one term under a symmetric bond.

**Mode: `general` for the figure distinction, `specified` for the prebiotic isomer claim. Status: triangulated (Fig 13 and Fig 12, low overlap: substrate pairing versus bond definition).**

### Q2: Is "self-maintaining" a cycle or a catalyst, and what is the difference?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A catalyst that survives its use is a necessary part, but a part alone is not a loop. A self-maintaining system needs both: a cycle that re-enters its output and a part that is regenerated rather than spent.
- The sign of the loop matters. The figure says the sign decides whether it closes. A catalytic cycle that produces more of its own catalyst is a positive loop that closes; a chain of reactions ending in a spent product cannot close by construction. This is why "a long reaction pathway" is not "a self-maintaining system."
- World corroboration at low overlap: Ruiz-Mirazo and colleagues write that *"self-maintenance is based on far-from-equilibrium reaction cycles in which there is a continuous turnover of the molecular species involved."* That is Fig 12 :121 in the systems-chemistry vocabulary.
- **Falsifier:** a named prebiotic system in which the catalyst is demonstrably spent on first use and yet turnover continues over more cycles than the initial pool allows. That breaks the catalyst clause even if a cycle is drawn.

**Mode: `specified`. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and the systems-chemistry literature).**

### Q3: What order do the three conditions come in?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 12 :106 (via Formula step 5 and Fig 5): a term must settle before it can be composed over. Applied here, the supply must be demonstrated to pay before the cycle can be said to be maintained by it.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** Once a source-driven cycle is settled at one site, it becomes an atom for a next-level network. The order is: supply → closure → habitat renewal → next level.
- Dependency in reverse is the failure mode. Supply before closure is a gradient with no loop to spend it. Closure before supply is a cycle with nothing driving it. Habitat renewal before either is a flux with no network to receive it. All three can be present and still fail if they are not connected at the same site.
- **Falsifier:** a documented case in which a self-maintaining network is established at a site first, and only afterwards does a measured supply arrive, with the network persisting unchanged through the interval. That breaks the supply-before-closure dependency.

**Mode: `specified`. Status: single-source for the exact order (Fig 3 plus Fig 12 :104), with the world literature supplying only the general far-from-equilibrium requirement.**

### Q4: Are the two candidate source classes isomers, or a union of different questions?

- Rimmer and Shorttle describe *"two dominant and contrasting classes of origin of life scenarios"*: submarine hydrothermal (chemical disequilibrium) and subaerial (UV catalysis). Superficially this looks like Fig 12's isomer case: same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- Re-census says otherwise. The two classes answer different residuals. The photochemical class solves **feedstock synthesis**; the vent class solves **sustained habitat**. Rimmer and Shorttle state it directly: photochemistry *"successfully produces many of the building blocks for life, without, on its own, providing early life with a viable habitat; whereas vent-based scenarios succeed in linking early life to a viable habitat, but struggle to demonstrate how that life could have emerged from the ambient vent chemistry."*
- Under Fig 9 :57 (*"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), this is a **union**, not an isomer pair. Their union is a compound with its own loop, which is exactly why the surface-vent proposal exists as a bridge.
- **Falsifier:** a single experiment that produces feedstocks and maintains a self-maintaining network in one environment with one measured difference, with no residual left over. That would collapse the union into one term and make the isomer reading unnecessary.

**Mode: `specified`. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the two differing world frames).**

## CONCLUSION

Spatial and temporal gradients are different terms under Fig 13 :76, and the difference has the structure of Fig 12 isomerism at the level of the bond to a state. Self-maintenance requires a cycle and a regenerating catalyst, not a catalyst alone (Fig 12 :119, :121). The dependency order is supply, then closure, then habitat renewal, then promotion to the next level (Fig 3 :20; Fig 12 :104). The two dominant source classes in the literature are a **union of different residuals**, not an isomer pair, and their union is itself a compound. None of these results names a specific source; they constrain the shape any name must have.
