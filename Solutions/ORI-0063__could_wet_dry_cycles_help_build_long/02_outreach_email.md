**Subject:** A checkable separator for wet–dry polymerisation claims

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "can wet–dry cycles build long polymers" is a bundle of at least four conditions with different anchors, not one question with a yes or no answer:
>
> 1. Bond formation inside one dry phase. Condensation releases water, so removing water favours bonds. A chain is a bonded arrangement, and a chain with one bond type is the cheapest possible one.
> 2. Loop sign across cycles. The product of one cycle re-enters as the input to the next. Cycles accumulate length only if bonds formed exceed bonds hydrolysed, and only if the next cycle differs from the last. Identical cycles give motion, not progress.
> 3. Habitat. Water availability, humidity, salts, temperature and cycle period set the valence. The same cycle helps in one regime and destroys in another; valence belongs to the relation, not to the cycle.
> 4. Sequence and function. Cycling explores arrangements over a fixed set of monomers; it adds no new monomer kinds. Ingredient lists do not tell you which sequence is functional. Which sequences acquire function is an open question here.
>
> **What this predicts.** Wet–dry cycling is a real, ground-visible contributor to conditions 1 and 2 under the right habitat, and it is not a sufficient mechanism for condition 4. I am not claiming it is the origin of life, that it is necessary, or that any particular sequence became functional because of it.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:** (a) a matched control showing no gain in net bond formation or mean length from cycling at equal integrated time and temperature; (b) a length distribution showing no accumulation when hydrolysis is short, or accumulation when hydrolysis dominates; (c) a cycle whose yield increases with water activity for every monomer set with no trade-off; (d) a new monomer species appearing from a closed set under mass balance; (e) a re-census showing a chain is not a bonded arrangement. Any one of these makes me withdraw the affected claim.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figure text and the formula are public at the links below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. The laboratory papers I read are also listed there so you can check the chemistry independently.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** run the separator on a problem you can check against a ground, state your own mode (`general`, `specified`, `grounded`), and write your own refuter before you run it. This output is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
