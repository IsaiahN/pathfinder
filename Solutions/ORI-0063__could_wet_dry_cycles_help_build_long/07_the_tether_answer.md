# The Tether's answer on ORI-0063

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which sequences acquire function. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Between a prebiotic inventory of small molecules and long, sequence-specific polymers there is a gap that is routinely described as one question. It is not. It is a union of at least four overlapping conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."):

- bond formation in one dry phase,
- loop sign across repeated cycles,
- habitat conditions that set the cycle's valence,
- sequence arrangement and function.

A single "yes, wet–dry cycles help" verdict would average across slots and flatten the union (Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :54). The answer is therefore per-domain.

## 2. The verdict, domain by domain (never averaged)

**D1, chemical composition: `specified`, triangulated.** Polymerisation is composition by typed bonds. Fig 12 :48: "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching." Homopolymer chains are the cheapest molecules available; removing water favours condensation. Wet–dry cycling is structurally appropriate to this condition. It cannot supply monomers that were not present (Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom").

**D2, cycle dynamics: `specified`, triangulated, conditional.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Cycling helps only when the wet phase is short relative to hydrolysis and when something varies between cycles (Fig 7 :36: "The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."). Identical closed cycles give "one long room: motion, no progress" (Fig 7 :32). So the honest claim is conditional: **cycling helps when the loop sign is positive and the rooms differ**, not "cycling helps".

**D3, habitat: `specified`, triangulated.** Fig 11 :46: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Water is not intrinsically helpful. Regulated rehydration can raise yields by an order of magnitude; overhydration flips valence to destructive because hydrolysis dominates. The habitat is enumerated (Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world"), and in the prebiotic case there is no maintainer: "Nothing was intelligent; selection ran" (Fig 11 :36).

**D4, sequence and function: structural claim `specified`, outcome `gap`.** Cycling generates many arrangements over a fixed alphabet, including sequence isomers with identical mass and different order. Ingredient lists cannot tell which are functional: "The arrangement is the substance" (Fig 12 :94). Which sequences acquire function is unreturned by the figures. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The gap is not an admission of failure; it is the honest stop.

**D5, verification: `general`.** This derivation and the laboratory papers share an evidence pool in this folder. The anchor is a length-distribution measurement and a function assay, not agreement (Fig 2 :29, :40).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that wet–dry cycling is *the* origin of life, nor that it is necessary. Other habitats and imports are not excluded.
- Not that a higher bulk yield closes the question. Yield is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a particular monomer, salt or sequence is the answer. Those are provenanced bindings from the chemistry frame, not figure derivations. The narrowed "shape, not a name" correction is in `05` section 5b.
- Not that chemical polymerisation is governed by Fig 13's six physical terms. That mapping was retracted as a strain in `01` section 6; chemical composition is governed by Fig 12, with Fig 8 for closure.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, and what is owed

The ground is an instrument, not a further argument. Three questions a refuter can run:

1. **Loop-sign test.** Measure mean length after N cycles with a short wet phase and a matched control with a long wet phase. Does length track the sign? Fig 12 :121.
2. **Regulated-hydration test.** Compare deliquescent, self-regulating rehydration with uncontrolled added water at equal integrated time and temperature. Does the valence flip? Fig 11 :49, Fig 10 :24.
3. **Sequence-discriminating test.** Use a method that separates sequence isomers and assay function per named chain. Does bulk length predict function, or is a new discriminator owed? Fig 12 :94/:117; cf. the spectrometer example in `05` section 4.

Standing refuters from the audit: a matched static control with no cycling advantage; no accumulation with short hydrolysis; accumulation with dominant hydrolysis; residual-independent valence; a new monomer from a closed set; or a figure re-census that breaks the chain/isomer/cycle definitions.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions have separate anchors and cannot be collapsed into one verdict (Fig 9 :54-57). The functional-sequence list is a `gap`, reported rather than filled. This solution stops at the limit of the instruments it has and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical README). Prestige or volume of agreement is not an instrument: "Only the ground can settle it" (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes, wet–dry cycles build life's polymers". It splits the question into four conditions with different anchors. Polymerisation is composition by typed bonds, so cycling can only rearrange the monomers already present, it cannot invent new ones. A cycle is a loop whose output re-enters as input, and the sign of that loop decides whether length accumulates; identical repeated cycles give motion, not progress. Water's effect is residual- and habitat-relative: regulated rehydration helps, uncontrolled overhydration destroys by hydrolysis. And length is not function, because the bond arrangement carries information the ingredient list does not. Wet–dry cycling is therefore a real, checkable contributor to bond formation and length accumulation under the right habitat, and it is not a sufficient account of functional polymers. Which sequences become functional is a declared gap, not an omission. The shape, the refuters written in advance and the instruments that would close it are what this offers; the ground, not agreement, decides.
