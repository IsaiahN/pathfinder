## QUESTION

Decide in effect terms what wet–dry cycling does to the gap between small molecules and long polymers. Option A: cycles help, because drying removes water and concentrates reactants. Option B: cycles do not help, because rewetting hydrolyses what drying built. What each leads to: A and B both flatten a union of four conditions with different anchors into one verdict, so each can be right for one subset and wrong for the rest. The figure-derived third option is to split the union, keep per-domain verdicts separate, and declare a `gap` where no instrument returns a name.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Wet–dry cycling is a real, ground-visible contributor to polymer formation in two domains and is not a sufficient account in the others. Bond formation is composition by typed bonds, so cycles can only rearrange a fixed monomer set; the homogeneity of a homopolymer chain makes it the cheapest possible molecule. A wet–dry cycle is a signed loop, so length accumulates only when formation exceeds hydrolysis and the rooms differ; otherwise repeated cycling is motion without progress. Water's valence is residual- and habitat-relative: regulated rehydration helps and overhydration destroys. Cycling generates sequence isomers, but which sequences become functional cannot be read from a bulk yield, so that is a declared `gap`, reported as a verdict rather than silence. Verdicts by domain: D1 `specified` triangulated, D2 `specified` triangulated and conditional, D3 `specified` triangulated, D4 structural `specified` with functional outcome `gap`, D5 `general`. Nothing here is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Each domain verdict carries its own refuter and anchor, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger cost is structural: sending a global "yes" or a framework summary before a reader has described the residual would spend their independence, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the four-condition separator and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The conditional claims are retractable one domain at a time without touching the others. Refuting the loop-sign claim leaves the bond-composition claim standing. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and no laboratory result is overturned by this derivation.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A matched static control erasing the cycling advantage would break D1; a length distribution that ignores the sign of the wet phase would break D2; a cycle whose yield rose with water activity for every monomer set would break D3; a sequence-discriminating assay showing bulk length predicts function would close the D4 gap; and a figure re-census breaking the chain or cycle definitions would reopen the derivation. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
