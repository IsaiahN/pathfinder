## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the combined effect of five conditions on major crops. The contact failure is not one hard question with five causes. It is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55-58): heat raises water demand and binds to drought; drought changes pest susceptibility; soil water storage changes the effect of both drought and flood; flood after drought is a sequence, not a coexistence. A case in two conditions is a specific compound, not a harder case of either. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into one global percentage.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not one global yield percentage for "major crops." That would aggregate away the live slot.
- Not a ranking of which of the five is always worst. Fig 11 :51 refuses the standing list.
- Not that additivity is false everywhere. Meijer et al. (2026) found 58.1% additive legume observations; the claim is that additivity is a hypothesis, not the composition rule.

## 4. What would settle it â€” the ground

A factorial, stage-resolved field instrument, not another argument. From Li et al. (2025): *"understanding the impacts of CDHW stress on crops necessitates comprehensive and specialized data that cannot be derived from single-stress experiments."* The discriminating tests are:

- **Bond test.** Does a full factorial over five conditions, at flowering and at grain filling, show an interaction term that survives across sites? If all terms vanish, `+` stands and the ordered-bond claim fails.
- **Union test.** Does a single lever clear every intersection, or do second conditions still bind after the first is removed?
- **Cascade test.** Is there residual yield variation at step 3 (crop state to pest susceptibility) after soil water and temperature are controlled?
- **Valence test.** Does the sign of a condition flip when the residual changes, for example soil organic matter under moderate versus extreme water supply?
- **Channel test.** Does the measurement channel stay reachable as the calibration climate is exceeded, or has the model gone stale while the ground stayed put (Fig 10 :24)?

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a single yield number for major crops, and it does not rank heat, drought, floods, pests and soil change from worst to best. It says the effect has to be read per crop, region and growth stage; that the five are a union of overlapping conditions, so fixing one stressor leaves the intersections standing; and that how they combine is a bond-type question, with additivity a hypothesis to test rather than a default. Same five conditions in a different order or at a different severity are a different substance. The path runs through soil water, crop state and pest susceptibility, so the residual that matters can be three steps out, and no condition carries a standing good or bad valence: soil organic matter helps under moderate water and not under extreme drought. What the framework returns is that shape, with five refuters written in advance and a factorial field measurement named as the instrument that would close it. The coefficient for a named crop stays open until the ground pays for it.
