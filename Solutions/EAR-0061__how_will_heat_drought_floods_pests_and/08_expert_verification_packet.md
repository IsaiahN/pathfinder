## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The combined effect of heat, drought, floods, pests and soil change on major crops is not one number and not a ranking of five causes. It is a per-slot residual: a **union of overlapping conditions** whose intersections are specific compounds, joined by a **typed bond** in which timing and severity change the substance, propagated through a **contact cascade** in which the pest link can be three steps out, with **valence relative to the residual** rather than standing on each condition. The settling anchor is a factorial field measurement with a channel that stays reachable as the climate moves. The framework returns this shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it; it does not return a yield coefficient for a named crop.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a yield coefficient for maize, wheat, rice or any other crop. The five-way term for a named crop is a declared gap (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From Li et al. 2025: *"understanding the impacts of CDHW stress on crops necessitates comprehensive and specialized data that cannot be derived from single-stress experiments."*

1. **Bond test:** does a full factorial over five conditions, at flowering and grain filling, return an interaction term that survives across sites?
2. **Union test:** after one condition is removed, do the second conditions still bind in the intersections?
3. **Cascade test:** is there residual yield variation at the crop-state-to-pest step after soil water and temperature are controlled?
4. **Valence test:** does the sign flip when the residual changes, as with soil organic matter under moderate versus extreme water supply?
5. **Channel test:** is the measurement channel still reachable as the climate leaves the calibration range (Fig 10 :24)?
