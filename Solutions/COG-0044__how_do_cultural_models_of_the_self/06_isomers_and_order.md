# Structure and order: union, refusal, instrument direction, feedback

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading

## RESIDUAL

`01` established that the self-model is a weighted belief, that the three effects are a union, and that
the binary is refused. Three ordering questions remain: (1) is the union a partition or a compound
with its own loop; (2) in what order do refusal, mint and instrument refinement come; (3) is the
culture-to-self-to-effect path a chain or a cycle? Each has a falsifier below.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Partition or compound?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57-58: "A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 9 :59: "Which is the second-order value of a big lever, and usually the larger one: not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."

Motivation (objective set), decision (action under incomplete information) and responsibility
(authorship judgment) are three overlapping conditions. Unbundling leaves three compounds, each with
its own instrument and its own ground. The residue is *describable* after the split: objective,
choice, seat. Before the split it is only "culture affects self."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** one instrument that predicts all three slots with residuals
that trend down together, no slot-specific residue, and no loss of description after rebundling. Then
the union claim fails and the three effects really are one partition.

### Q2 : Order of operators: refuse before mint

- Formula step 2 (perceive, route, then mint): a REFUTED term owes "a refusal, not an explanation," and
  the first four bins sort by what is missing while the fifth sorts by what is wrong.
- Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
- Fig 5 :54-55: "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."
- Operators table :70-74: negation acts on one operand, joins nothing, and is not a seventh bond.

The independent/interdependent binary is held and predicting, and its residual persists and does not
trend down (Vignoles et al. 2016; Matsumoto 1999). So it is the term to point the refusal at. The
seven-dimensional model is the refinement that comes *after* the refusal. Minting the 7D model first
and presenting it as a better theory would leave the false binary's standing intact and would commit
the order error named in the Formula: refusal is owed before a mint.

**Falsifier:** the binary's residual trends down once the 7D dimensions are admitted, so no standing
needs to be removed; or the 7D dimensions cannot be composed from held terms and require a genuine
new primitive, in which case an import with provenance is owed instead of a refusal.

### Q3 : INWARD or OUTWARD?

- Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."

The 7D result is INWARD: a better instrument for a representation the frame already holds. It is not
OUTWARD, because no new atom crossed a wall; adding a "new dimension of self" as if it were an imported
primitive would price a wrong atom above starting from primitives (Fig 8 :42-43). The priming work
(Kühnen 2009) is a separate INWARD move on the same construct: it sharpens access to self-knowledge
by making it temporarily available. Neither tells us whether the *structural* agent-and-habitat claim
is right; that is a figure question and needs a ground reading (Fig 6 :32-35 on instruments improved
from worse instruments).

**Falsifier:** show that the 7D model imports a primitive the self-construal frame did not hold, or that
the priming result requires a new kind of entity rather than a change in accessibility. Then the
INWARD reading is wrong and an import is owed.

### Q4 : Chain or cycle?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."
- Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

At the scale of one life the path is a chain: transmission -> belief -> objective -> action ->
authorship judgment (Fig 3). Across generations it closes into a cycle: the actions of the enculturated
person become part of the habitat that enculturates the next person, and Fig 11 :43 says the habitat is
enumerated, never composed. The sign of the loop is the question: does the next generation's contact
with the ground correct the model, or does agreement inside the room carry it unchanged (Fig 7 :12-13,
"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not")? This is where the
two-scale answer lives, and it is why a single "culture affects self" claim cannot close it.

**Falsifier:** a case where a transmitted self-model is reproduced across generations with no action
of the previous generation entering the next generation's habitat. Then the cycle reading fails and
the chain alone suffices.

### Q5 : What is the order of the *answer*?

Fig 12 :111-113: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan
is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A list of three cultural effects is
a state description. The answer owed here is a route: refuse the binary, refine the instrument, keep
three slots separate, state the authorship claim, then let the ground settle each slot. Fig 10 :52:
"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

## CONCLUSION

The union splits into three describable compounds. The correct operator order is refusal of the binary
before any replacement mint; the seven-dimensional model and the priming results are INWARD instrument
refinements, not imports. The path is a chain within a life and a cycle across generations, with the
sign of the loop decided by whether the ground or agreement governs the handoff. Each claim has a
falsifier above. Nothing here names a culture's self-model or measures an effect size.
