## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a cultural model of the self and its three
downstream effects. Option A: treat "culture shapes self, self shapes motivation, decision and
responsibility" as one empirical regularity with a single averaged effect. Option B: treat the
self-model as a weighted, frame-internal belief, unbundle the three effects into a union with separate
grounds, refuse the independent/interdependent binary in favour of a finer instrument, and hold the
agent-and-habitat authorship claim as structural until a ground settles each slot. What each leads to:
A hides which slot is live, mistakes self-report for a ground reading and keeps a refused term in
composition; B sends a checkable shape, four refuters written in advance and an open slot per effect.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or
union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The dependence of three effects on a transmitted self-belief. Arity: one cause
  and three effect slots, plus one upstream transmission slot. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by
  object slot, not aggregated." Symmetry: asymmetric within a run; culture conditions the belief,
  the belief conditions the effects; responsibility is a seat relation, not the same quantity as
  effort or choice. Scale: one person's frame within a run, cultural transmission across scales,
  authorship across the actor-observer seam.
- **Domain 1, transmission and self-model.** Actors: carriers of the model, the person's library, the
  membrane, belief `b`, weighting `w`. Currency: inherited prior mixed with private history, a
  prediction residual. Anchor: behaviour under manipulation, not a self-report. Question type:
  upstream; has a nameable anchor, so it is a domain.
- **Domain 2, motivation.** Actors: person, objective, gradient. Currency: a spendable difference,
  what counts as progress. Anchor: measured effort, persistence, goal choice. Separate loop.
- **Domain 3, decision-making.** Actors: person, action, plan. Currency: prediction against
  observation under incomplete information. Anchor: observed choice, not reported intention. Separate
  loop.
- **Domain 4, personal responsibility.** Actors: author of the action, observer, seat, habitat.
  Currency: authorship, provenance, standing to be composed over. Anchor: the structural claim in
  Fig 11 :39 and empirical attribution experiments. Separate loop.
- **Order by dependency.** transmit then hold the belief then set the objective then choose the action
  then judge authorship (Fig 3). Refuse before minting (Formula step 2). Describe each effect before
  choosing an instrument (Fig 9 :27-28).

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :55-56 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping
conditions, not a list of separate ones." Three grounds, three verdicts, no averaging.

**Strain test.** Mapping "the self" onto one of the six substrate terms strains, because Fig 13 :4-5
puts the six in two kinds in lockstep and none is a self. Re-census: the self-model is a belief term in
Γ (Symbols). No seventh member is minted. After re-census, one live strain remains: the binary is
refused by a figure rule while the world literature is itself unsettled. That is not a figure
contradiction; it is a ground question left open, recorded honestly.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only; where they share the self-construal evidence pool, overlap is declared.

- **Cultural psychology, foundational.** Hazel Rose Markus and Shinobu Kitayama, "Culture and the Self:
  Implications for Cognition, Emotion, and Motivation", Psychological Review 98(2), 224-253, 1991,
  accessed 2026-11-22. Quote: "The independent view of the self, represented in Western culture, is
  contrasted with the interdependent view in many other cultures." Predicts that a self-model changes
  cognition, emotion and motivation; corroborates the shape of Domain 1 and 2. Overlap: high, it is the
  theory most other sources cite.
- **Social cognition / attribution.** Ulrich Kühnen (with Hannover and colleagues), "Culture,
  Self-construal and Social Cognition: Evidence from Cross-Cultural and Priming Studies", chapter,
  2009, accessed 2026-11-22. Quote: "Westerners see agency as a property of the individual, whereas
  for Easterners, it is the group or one's social surroundings that is the origin of agency."
  Predicts that priming self-knowledge changes attribution of responsibility; corroborates the
  responsibility slot and the frame-internal status of the self-model. Overlap: lower than the reviews,
  different method (priming experiments), but shares the self-construal construct.
- **Cross-cultural psychology / measurement.** Vivian Vignoles et al., "Beyond the 'East-West'
  dichotomy: Global variation in cultural models of selfhood", Journal of Experimental Psychology:
  General 145(8), 966-1000, 2016, accessed 2026-11-22. Quote: "a simple contrast between independence
  and interdependence does not adequately capture the diverse models of selfhood that prevail in
  different world regions." Predicts a refusal of the binary and an INWARD instrument refinement;
  corroborates F7. Overlap: low, a 7,279-adult, 55-group survey with a new instrument.
- **Cultural psychology / critique.** David Matsumoto, "Culture and self: An empirical assessment of
  Markus and Kitayama's theory of independent and interdependent self-construals", Asian Journal of
  Social Psychology 2(3), 289-310, 1999, accessed 2026-11-22. Quote: "the evidence severely challenges
  the validity of their theoretical framework for explaining observed national differences in
  psychological phenomena." Predicts the same refusal; corroborates F7. Overlap: low in method, shares
  the primary theory under test.
- **Cultural psychology review.** Steven J. Heine, "Cultural psychology", WIREs Cognitive Science,
  2010, accessed 2026-11-22. Quote (abstract): "cultural differences in how people perceive the self,
  and the behavioral consequences that follow from these differences, in the domains of internal and
  external attribution styles, motivations for self-enhancement, approach/avoidance, primary and
  secondary control, as well as motivations for distinctiveness and conformity." Predicts effects in
  the motivation and attribution slots; corroborates weakly. Overlap: medium, a review of the same
  primary pool.
- **Reference summary.** Wikipedia contributors, "Construal" (self construal redirects here),
  Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-22. Quote: "those with an interdependent self-construal define themselves
  in terms of their relationships with others." Predicts the standard distinction and records the
  Vignoles critique; corroborates the setup, not the verdict. Overlap: high, a derivative summary.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The apparent conflict between the 1991 binary and
the 2016 refusal was handled by returning to the census, not by flipping the verdict: the re-census

talking, not independent corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the names do not. A cultural model of the self is a weighted, frame-internal
belief. Its three effects are a union: it sets the objective that governs motivation, it biases the
prediction that yields observed choice, and it bears on an authorship judgment that depends on the
habitat enumerated. Capability is agent-and-habitat, a structural claim of the framework that does not
settle how any population attributes responsibility. The independent/interdependent binary owes a
refusal because its residual persists; the seven-dimensional model and the priming work are INWARD
instrument refinements, not imports. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so it does not say
which model a culture holds or how large any effect is. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result,
candidate until each slot is settled at the ground.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, three checkable sub-claims and
  refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is
  spending the independence of outside readers by sending a framework before a residual, or by
  presenting a self-report score as a ground reading. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the
  cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The refused binary is removable without touching the domain census; `06` and `07`
  already do this. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be
  withdrawn. The agent-and-habitat claim is a figure line and remains until a figure re-census
  changes it, while the empirical attribution question stays open to experiment.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument that measures motivation
  independently of self-report and shows effort tracking the objective would close the motivation
  slot; observed choices diverging as the weighted belief predicts would close the decision slot; an
  attribution task that discriminates actor-only from agent-and-habitat authorship would test C3; a
  derivation of capability from the agent alone, or an isolated agent changing the ground with no
  habitat, would break the authorship claim; a binary residual that trends down after the
  seven-dimensional instrument would reverse the refusal. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands
  as `candidate`.
