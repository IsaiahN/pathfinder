# Outreach draft: check the evidence shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the recipient's own terms. If the recipient cannot say what would fail, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about inflation evidence, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the inflation evidence question I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "did inflation happen" is a contact question between a family of early-universe models and a set of instrument readings. It does not close as one yes or no. It closes per slot: the scalar tilt `n_s`, the tensor ratio `r`, curvature, Gaussianity, and the foreground channel that must be removed before any of them can be read. An average over those slots destroys the only signal that says where to look next.
>
> Three consequences follow, and all three are checkable without my theory:
> 1. The published numbers are upper limits and constraint bands, not a detection of a unique cause. `r_0.05 < 0.036` at 95% confidence is the BICEP/Keck XIII result, and the same collaboration calls it a constraint. A bound is not a proof of absence and not a proof of presence.
> 2. "Consistent with inflation" is a comparison, not an identity. A class of models is reported as compatible with any value of `r` all the way down to zero, so one dataset underdetermines which generator produced it. NASA's own cosmology page says the CMB properties are consistent with inflation while the physical process remains unknown.
> 3. The 2014 BICEP2 B-mode claim was withdrawn after a joint Planck analysis attributed most of the signal to Milky Way dust. The instrument moved; the paradigm did not. That is the cleanest example I know of a ground correcting a frame.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that inflation did or did not happen; that I have a new measurement; that any particular inflaton field is the driver; or that a model count is evidence. I am offering the shape any answer must have, not the answer.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. An instrument returns a primordial B-mode spectrum with `r` inconsistent with zero and with all named alternatives at high confidence, with foregrounds independently discriminated at several frequencies. That closes the slot and removes the underdetermination point for it.
> 2. A demonstration that no instrument at any resolution could ever return a source-discriminating reading for that epoch. That makes the question genuinely unreachable rather than merely unreached at this budget.
> 3. Two apparently independent analyses are shown to draw on one fully shared calibration and foreground pool, and their agreement nevertheless survives a proper effective-sample-size correction.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources and the derivation are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Planck 2018 constraints on inflation: https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06211
> - BICEP/Keck XIII, the `r_0.05 < 0.036` result: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00483
> - The 2015 retraction of the BICEP2 claim, in NASA's own words: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/gravitational-waves-from-early-universe-remain-elusive/
> - Figure text and the derivation: local files in this solution folder, listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the method is in `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
