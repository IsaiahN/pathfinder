## Three findings in the wider literature that bear on the residual

1. **The constraint is not a detection.** BICEP/Keck XIII reports an upper limit and states that foregrounds, especially dust, are the limiting factor to detection. The 2014 episode shows what happens when a single-frequency reading is treated as cosmological before the foreground habitat is enumerated. This is the empirical counterpart of Fig 8 :39 and Fig 11 :43 to 44.
2. **One observation, many generators.** Kallosh and Linde (2021) show that attractor models can fit any `r` down to zero, and the Planck paper reports no evidence for dynamics beyond slow roll. "Consistent with inflation" therefore cannot name a source. This is the empirical counterpart of Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :135.
3. **A null is still information.** NASA (2016) says a non-detection would mean a different early-universe model is needed, and JPL (2015) says the question remains open. The counterpart of Fig 8 :41 to 42: unreachable at this budget is not underivable in principle.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full derivation before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27 to 28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :42**: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Prefer recipients whose public work touches the instrument or the foreground model, not people selected for agreement with a conclusion.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (full line opens with "disqualifying for a verdict:"). A list of people who already agree about inflation is a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2.** The residual and the refuter go first. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior (Fig 10 :52; Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage 1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown and never pooled later as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user still needs a ground and a self-written refuter (`THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction note).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage 1 output survives. The shape claim here is deliberately smaller than a conclusion about inflation, so it can survive even if every specific inflaton model fails.

## Distribution boundary

`03_public_contact_points.csv` lists institutional, opt-in or public submission venues only. No personal emails, no purchased lists, no bulk send, no ranking by likelihood of agreement. If a recipient returns a refutation, it is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
