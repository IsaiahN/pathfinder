# Are competing early-universe mechanisms isomers? Bond structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No new observation is claimed here.

## RESIDUAL

The opened sources show one dataset compatible with many generators. Planck (2020) finds no evidence for dynamics beyond slow roll; Kallosh and Linde (2021) report attractor models compatible with `r` all the way down to zero. The residual is therefore not "which model fits" but: **what is the bond structure between a set of observables and a set of mechanisms, and in what order can that structure be investigated without picking a favourite frame first?** Three questions are owed:

1. Are two mechanisms that give the same CMB readings isomers under Fig 12, or merely members of one functional group?
2. What is the correct order of enumeration, description and frame selection?
3. Which analogues actually govern here, and which are decorations?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are two mechanisms with the same readings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Let the observable set `{n_s, r, Ω_K, f_NL, B-mode spectrum}` play the role of the atoms. Two mechanisms that reproduce **all** of it, yet differ in which structure is bonded to which state, are isomers in the figure's sense: the same composition read off the sky, different substance in the early universe, and the difference invisible in the composition. This is the formal version of the canonical statement *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*
- If they share only a subset (for example flatness and a nearly scale-invariant tilt, while differing in `r` or `f_NL`), they are **not** isomers. They are members of one functional group, the `state -> structure -> state` shape with different qualifiers.
- The Planck and BICEP/Keck results presently leave the tensor channel open, so the stronger isomer claim is **not yet checkable**. The functional-group reading is the one that survives the data as opened.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a source-discriminating instrument closes the tensor or non-Gaussianity slot and excludes all but one mechanism for that observable set, the isomer claim for that pair fails; if two mechanisms are shown not to share the full observable set, the isomer definition is not met and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 — What is the order of investigation?

- Fig 9 :27 to 28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
- Fig 9 :30 to 31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- Fig 11 :43 to 44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Correct order: (1) enumerate the observable and foreground habitat; (2) describe the residual per slot in effect terms, what would be seen if a mechanism were present; (3) state what would count as progress, namely discriminating sources; (4) choose the instrument or frequency combination; (5) carry the bound to the next season. The 2014 BICEP2 sequence inverted steps 1 and 2: a favoured interpretation was mapped onto a single-frequency reading, and the foreground actor was discovered only afterwards. NASA (2016) records the inversion in its own words: *"BICEP2 didn't have enough information."*
- **Falsifier:** a case where the residual was described in effect terms first, the habitat enumerated, and the conclusion still depended on which frame was chosen. Then the describe-before-looking rule does not govern here.

### Q3 — Which analogues govern?

1. **Metamerism / source discrimination.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."* The green reading cannot comment on the source. The CMB power spectrum cannot comment on the generator. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* This analogue governs directly; it is why multi-frequency foreground removal and non-Gaussianity are the decisive channels, not further model building.
2. **Chemical isomerism.** Fig 12 :97 to 98: *"Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation. Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; named isomerism by Berzelius, 1830."* The CMB composition is the formula; the generator is the structure the formula does not record. This analogue governs the Q1 derivation and is not a decoration.
3. **Instrument lineage.** Fig 6 :32 to 33: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The move from one BICEP frequency to 95/150/220 GHz plus WMAP and Planck 23 to 353 GHz is this, not a deduction from a theory of dust. Governs Q2.
4. **A analogue that does NOT govern:** treating `n_s` and `r` as a space-time pair analogous to the GR/QM two-kind composition in the exemplar `QMGR-0037`. Re-census: here both are observables in the same frame, not a state and a structure crossing kinds. Forcing that analogy would be a strain, so it is refused (Fig 8 :53: residual first, frame second).

## CONCLUSION

The competing mechanisms are **not yet isomers** on the opened evidence; they are members of a functional group sharing a subset of observables, and the stronger isomer claim becomes checkable only when a source-discriminating instrument closes the tensor or non-Gaussianity slot. The correct order is enumerate, describe in effect terms, then select the frame or instrument, and the governing analogues are metamerism, chemical isomerism and instrument lineage. A forcing analogue from the GR/QM composition is refused after re-census. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. Falsifiers are stated before the run above.
