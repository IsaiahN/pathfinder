# Expert verification packet: the Tether on inflation evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist or CMB instrumentalist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural rules, `specified` on the inflation application. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the early-universe epoch is claimed by this packet. It is a shape and a route, not a result, and it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "did inflation happen" does not close as one yes or no. It closes per observable slot, and the sky settles each slot, not agreement among models or people. The published tensor ratio limits are upper bounds, not detections, so a null is unreached rather than false. "Consistent with inflation" is a threshold comparison between a prediction and a reading, not an identity: one CMB reading is compatible with many generators, and the framework returns a shape rather than a source name. Foregrounds must be enumerated before any slot is read, and analyses that share a sky and a dust model are one witness, not two. The framework offers the shape any answer must have, the order of investigation, refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the open slot. It does not answer the factual question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed by observable slot and must never be averaged into one verdict. `n_s`, `r`, curvature, non-Gaussianity and the foreground channel are separate slots with separate instruments.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :6 *"the verdict: is φ* in here at all?"*
- **Refuter:** produce a single scalar evidence score that claims to settle the question while at least one of those slots remains live, and show the framework admits it as a verdict. Then C1 fails.
- **Physics check:** confirm that the Planck and BICEP/Keck numbers are reported as per-parameter constraints with their own confidence levels, not as a single "inflation confirmed" statistic.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable by a cosmologist against the opened sources

- **Statement:** "consistent with the predictions of inflation" is a ⋛ threshold comparison, not ≡ identity. The data constrain a shape and underdetermine the generator.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and ≡ as *"two names, one referent"* with the test *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*
- **What to check:** Kallosh and Linde (2021) report attractor models *"compatible with any value of the tensor to scalar ratio r, all the way down to r = 0"*; NASA (2025) says CMB properties are *"consistent with the predictions of inflation"* while *"the physical process behind inflation remains unknown."* Is there any observation in the opened sources that selects a unique generator? If yes, C2 fails for that observation.
- **Refuter:** an instrument returning a source-discriminating signature that closes a slot uniquely against all named alternatives at high confidence, with foregrounds independently separated. That moves the slot to `grounded` and narrows C2 honestly.

### C3 — framework-internal plus instrument history; checkable by a CMB instrumentalist

- **Statement:** a non-detection is unreached, not false, and the foreground habitat must be enumerated before any slot is read; correlated analyses are one witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 8 :41 to 42 *"Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 2 :44 *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."*
- **What to check:** the 2014 BICEP2 excess was attributed to Milky Way dust after a joint Planck analysis (JPL, 2015; NASA, 2016); BICEP/Keck XIII (2021) then added 220 GHz and 95 GHz channels and WMAP and Planck maps from 23 to 353 GHz. Is there any foreground actor still omitted from the list? If one is later shown to be in contact with the `r` slot, C3's enumeration was incomplete.
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated underivation in principle, showing no instrument at any resolution could return a source-discriminating reading for that epoch; or two analyses with independent instruments and independent foreground models whose agreement survives an effective-sample-size correction (then the shared-pool half of C3 does not apply to that pair).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that inflation did or did not happen. No ground reading was taken by this packet.
- Not a driver, a potential, an energy scale, or a model selection. Those are names where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that `r_0.05 < 0.036` is a detection. It is an upper limit reported as such by the collaboration.
- Not that a null result proves the paradigm false. Fig 8 :39 forbids that inference.
- Not that agreement among experts, reviewers or collaborations settles anything. Fig 2 :35 and :40 apply.
- Not `grounded`. Every substantive claim is `general` or `specified`.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here:

1. Does the tensor channel return a primordial B-mode spectrum with `r` inconsistent at high confidence, with foregrounds separated at multiple frequencies?
2. Does non-Gaussianity discriminate among mechanisms sharing `n_s` and `r`?
3. Do the analyses remain independent after an effective-sample-size correction?
