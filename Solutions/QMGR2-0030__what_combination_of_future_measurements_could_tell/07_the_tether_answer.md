# The Tether's answer on physical discrepancy versus analysis problem

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a decision order, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No cosmological measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A single H0 reading is compatible with at least two sources: the ground differs from the model (physical discrepancy) or the channel carrying the reading is faulty (analysis problem). The question asks for the combination of future measurements that would tell them apart. The residual is a union compound (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."), so it needs its own loop and its own ground. It is not a call for more precision.

## 2. The shape

1. **The combination must be low-overlap.** Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." A second team on the same catalog is not a second frame.
2. **It must contain a statistic that does not contain the disputed quantity.** Fig 10 :18-19: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one" and "→ install what can be violated". The published instance is an H0-independent ratio of strong-lensing time-delay distances to standard-siren luminosity distances, predicted early and measured late. H0 cancels, so no ladder calibration can move it.
3. **It must range against an anchor that does not move.** Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The measured distances, times and redshifts are the anchor. The inference is not.
4. **Inward and outward tracks stay separate.** Fig 6 :29-31: the inward track sharpens existing ladder and CMB analyses until a systematics floor appears; the outward track imports independent closures, sirens, lensing, distance duality, chronometers, BAO. "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."
5. **Analysis problems are refusals of held terms, not new physics terms.** Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." The candidate to refuse is the term whose residual persists and does not trend down (Fig 5 :59-60). TDCOSMO's mass-sheet prior is a concrete case.
6. **Report per method, with reversible cuts.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :41: "so keep every cut ranked and reversible". One averaged H0 with one error bar is the aggregation error that hides the live slot (Fig 1 :22).
7. **The outcome table has three readable cells, plus a compound.** Null consistent and H0 estimates disagree along an analysis choice, then analysis problem. Null inconsistent and H0 estimates agree across independent frames, then physical discrepancy in the expansion history. Both consistent, then no discrepancy at current sensitivity, which is Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Both failing, then the union must be split again, not read as a verdict.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the discrepancy is real. The figures decide the shape of the test, not its outcome.
- Not that it is an analysis problem. The refuter in `01` section 4 is designed to fire in that direction, but it has not been run.
- Not a named instrument. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Survey names in `10_source_ledger.csv` are imports with provenance, not framework verdicts.
- Not grounded. No measurement settled any of this. The lensed-siren version of the null test is still forthcoming.
- Not a majority vote. Fig 7 :35: "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; it was "the arrival of a better instrument" that resolved the case. Here the corresponding checks are:

- Does the H0-independent lensing-to-siren ratio, predicted from early-universe data, match the late-time measured value? A mismatch with agreeing independent H0 estimates points to the expansion history.
- Does a single blind assumption swap (lens mass profile, Cepheid crowding, TRGB metallicity, LMC anchor) move H0 by the size of the tension while leaving the null statistic unchanged? That points to the channel.
- Do two supposedly independent frames actually share a calibration, a data release, or a prior? If yes, their agreement is one witness and the triangulation collapses. This is the cheapest check and should be run first.
- Standing falsifiers: the four in `01` section 4. Any one firing changes the answer, not just the wording.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The specific null test exists in paper form but awaits lensed gravitational-wave standard sirens. The canonical README says the framework stops "at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest." That is where this answer stops. It records the shape, the refuters, and the instrument gap. "Indiscernibility is not identity." The answer does not claim that no future instrument can settle the question; it claims that no present one has.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether the Hubble tension is new physics or a mistake. It says what a measurement set would have to look like to tell them apart. The problem is a bundle of overlapping conditions, so more of the same measurement cannot close it. What is owed is a low-overlap combination containing a statistic from which the disputed quantity cancels, so no calibration error can move it, together with blind single-assumption swaps and per-method residuals. Inward refinement of existing ladders and outward imports of independent methods are two tracks and must never be read against each other. An analysis problem is a refusal of a held assumption, not a new particle; a physical discrepancy is what remains when independent frames agree and the source-discriminating statistic still fails. The framework returns that shape, its falsifiers and its instrument gap, not the name of a telescope and not a verdict on the tension.
