# Outreach draft: check the discriminant before you decide about the tension

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 to 5 has survived the four pre-registered refuters. The output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Would this consistency test separate new physics from an analysis problem?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific question I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the question does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an H0 reading is compatible with at least two different sources. The ground could genuinely differ from the model, or the channel carrying the reading could be wrong (calibration, crowding, stellar populations, a lens mass-profile prior, a pipeline choice). More of the same measurement cannot separate those, because a closed set of methods does not add a new observable however long you search inside it.
>
> **The shape claim I want checked:** a discriminating set must have four properties. (1) Low evidence overlap, so agreement means something. (2) At least one statistic that does not contain the disputed quantity, so no ladder calibration can move it. The instance I have in mind is a dimensionless ratio of strong-lensing time-delay distances to gravitational-wave standard-siren luminosity distances, predicted from early-universe data and measured at late times. (3) Blind single-assumption swaps, with the H0 shift recorded; time-delay work already shows a shift of several km/s/Mpc from the mass-profile prior alone. (4) Per-method residuals with reversible cuts, never one averaged tension.
>
> **What is explicitly not claimed:** that I know which source is real. That a named telescope or survey is the answer. That this derivation is a measurement. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. every candidate "independent" method turns out to share a calibration, data release, or prior, so the combination is one witness;
> 2. a proposed statistic returns the same value under a specified new-physics model and a specified systematics model of the size of the tension;
> 3. a blind reanalysis inside one existing family removes the offset under all analysis choices with no import from another closure.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Kouniatalis and Saridakis, "A null test of the Hubble tension", 2026: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22043
> - Di Valentino et al, "In the Realm of the Hubble tension: a Review of Solutions", 2021: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01183
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and formula are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
