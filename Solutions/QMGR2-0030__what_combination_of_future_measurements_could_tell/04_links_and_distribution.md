## Three findings in the wider literature that bear on this question

1. **The discriminant already exists in paper form.** Kouniatalis and Saridakis (2026) construct an H0-independent ratio from lensing time-delay distances and standard-siren luminosity distances. This is a SEARCHED result: we did not need to invent a new measurement, we needed to find one already in the closure. It is a forecast, not a delivered result, which is why it corroborates the shape and not the verdict.
2. **An analysis problem can be large enough to mimic the tension.** Birrer et al (2020) show a same-data H0 shift from 73.3 to 67.4 under a different mass-profile prior. That is the concrete answer to "could the discrepancy be an analysis problem?" in one frame, and it is why blind assumption swaps belong in the combination.
3. **The two candidate sources are stated in the literature's own words.** Alfano et al (2025/2026): deviations "may result from systematic errors in distance measurements or, more interestingly, hint at new physics." That is the residual of this question almost verbatim, which is a useful check that the derive-first census did not invent it.

Note on evidence pools: sources 2 through 7 above draw heavily on the same public data sets (CMB, SH0ES, Pantheon, DESI). Their agreement with each other is one pool talking, not independent corroboration (Fig 2 :40). The two essays share an author with the figures. Corroboration in this folder is therefore weak in the independence sense, and is recorded as such in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
