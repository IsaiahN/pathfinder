## Wider literature that bears on the question (found through the pages above, not claimed as fully read here unless listed in the ledger)

1. Merton 1968, "The Matthew Effect in Science", *Science* 159: 56 to 63, and Merton and Zuckerman 1988, *Isis* 79: 606 to 623. The original cumulative-advantage papers behind the Wikipedia entry.
2. Thorndike 1920, "A Constant Error in Psychological Ratings". The original halo study behind the Wikipedia entry.
3. Milgram 1963, "Behavioral study of obedience", *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology* 67: 371 to 378; Bickman 1974, "The Social Power of a Uniform", *Journal of Applied Social Psychology* 4: 47 to 61; Hofling hospital experiment. Authority markers and compliance.
4. Wynne 1996, "Misunderstood Misunderstandings", in Irwin and Wynne (eds), *Misunderstanding Science?*, Cambridge University Press. The contextualist case that grounds the "contact, not reputation" finding.
5. Nisbet 2018, "Divided Expectations", *Skeptical Inquirer* 42: 18 to 19, and the Pew 20-country survey reported by Branch 2021. Trust in science varies by population and framing.
6. Kunda 1990, "The Case for Motivated Reasoning", *Psychological Bulletin* 108: 480 to 498; Ballantyne and Dunning 2022, *Reason, Bias, and Inquiry*, Oxford University Press. Trust in experts judged by character rather than reliability.
7. Nadelson et al. 2014, "I Just Don't Trust Them", *School Science and Mathematics* 114: 76 to 86. The 21-item Trust in Science and Scientists Inventory.
8. Song, Markowitz and Taylor 2022, "Trusting on the shoulders of open giants? Open science increases trust in science for the public and academics", *Journal of Communication* 72: 497 to 510. Open practices as a possible source-discriminating instrument.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the whole architecture before anyone has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and the refuter.
- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The two-stage order sends a checkable method first; the theory is for an opt-in second stage.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
- **Fig 7 :35** "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A pile of endorsements is not a result.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Once a recipient has absorbed the source their agreement stops being evidence. Record the stage-1 verdict first.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1: send the residual, the shape claim and the refuter. Ask for a failure condition, not agreement. Stage 2, opt-in only: offer the source links. Stage 3: offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A mass send selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration. Track B style direct contacts, if any, carry zero verdict weight and are one to one with an opt-out line. Nothing in this folder is sent by this agent.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4 and `08` section 2 are the test.
