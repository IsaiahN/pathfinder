## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a public trust reading toward an expert claim. Option A: the reading is produced by careful evaluation, meaning observation independently ranged against a non-updating ground. Option B: the reading is produced mainly by institutional reputation, meaning an inherited prior manufactured inside the same frames that use it as evidence. What each leads to: A yields a per-claim, refutable signal whose independence can be bought and audited; B yields a self-confirming loop in which agreement compounds, evidence does not, and the ground is never consulted. The Symbols table rejects the pure dichotomy by giving a learnable weighting w, so the real question is which conditions push the weighting which way.

- **D2, expert knowledge production.** Actors: expert, instruments, data, peer frames. Currency: predictions that pay. Anchor: measurement, experiment, counter, patient outcome. Verdict: credibility accrued is frame-internal, so it cannot score the claim. `general`, triangulated.
- **D3, institutional reputation and credentialing.** Actors: institutions, credential bodies, citation and prize systems. Currency: standing, accumulated advantage. Anchor: none that does not update on the frame's own output. This is a mirror or common-cause collapse, not yet a domain with a ground. Verdict `specified`, triangulated for the self-confirming shape.
- **D4, dissemination and the room chain.** Actors: rooms, media, chains of agreeing frames. Currency: agreement and influence. Anchor: none unless the next room is selected against the surfaced residual. Verdict `general`, triangulated.
- **D5, the verification arrangement.** Actors: ground-maintainer function, outside frames, records. Currency: independence, refusal, provenance. Anchor: the ground, never the agreement. Verdict `general`, triangulated.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only. Most are secondary summaries whose primary papers are named where possible; overlap with each other and with the framework is declared rather than hidden.

- **Sociology of science.** Robert K. Merton and Harriet Zuckerman, Matthew effect, via "Matthew effect" (Wikipedia, page fetched 2026-11-22; originals *Science* 1968 and *Isis* 1988). Quote: "eminent scientists will often get more credit than a comparatively unknown researcher, even if their work is similar; it also means that credit will usually be given to researchers who are already famous." Prediction: recognition flows to existing standing rather than to the specific work. Corroborates F2 and F8. Overlap: genuinely low with the framework; the primary papers are one pool with the Wikipedia summary.
- **Cognitive psychology.** Edward Thorndike, halo effect, via "Halo effect" (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22; original 1920). Quote: "the tendency for positive impressions of one aspect of a person or entity to encourage a positive perspective on that subject's other aspects." Prediction: a global impression of an institution colours judgment of a specific claim from it. Corroborates F2. Overlap: low.
- **Social cognition.** Stanley Milgram and the authority-bias literature, via "Authority bias" (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22; Milgram 1963, Bickman 1974, Hofling hospital experiment). Quote: "the tendency to attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of an authority figure (unrelated to its content) and be more influenced by that opinion." The Hofling result, 21 of 22 nurses, predicts that an authority cue overrides written guidance. Corroborates F3 and F8. Overlap: low.
- **Science studies, the field source.** Brian Wynne 1996 and Matthew Nisbet 2018, via "Public awareness of science" (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22). Quote: "Because of this shortcoming from the side of the scientists, local farmers lost their trust in them" and "scientific success can create either trust or distrust in experts in different populations". Prediction: trust follows ground contact and local fit, not just credentials. Corroborates F7 and F9. Overlap: low with the framework; this is the closest thing to a field witness.
- **Political psychology.** Ziva Kunda 1990 and Ballantyne and Dunning 2022, via "Motivated reasoning" (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22). Quote: "People tend to trust experts whom they believe share their perspectives, distrusting experts that do not" and "Expertise is judged based upon the character, not reliability." Prediction: selecting experts by alignment manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Corroborates F4. Overlap: low.
- **Metatheory, framework essay.** Isaiah N, "The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap" (adventuresinml Substack, 2026-11-19, opened 2026-11-22). Quote: "The prestige loop confirms itself. The high-prestige person is believed because they are high-prestige." Prediction: reputation-driven trust is self-confirming; prestige can fund a try but cannot settle. Corroborates F2, F3 and F8. Overlap: maximum, same author as the figures, so no independent witness weight.
- **Perception and instrumentation, framework essay.** Isaiah N, "The Hard Problem is About Source" (adventuresinml Substack, 2026-08-28, opened 2026-11-22). Quote: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." ## VERDICT (from the figures)

Public trust reflects careful evaluation when the reading is indexed per claim (Fig 1 :19), the weighting leans on observations ranged against a non-updating ground (Fig 2 :29), at least two low-overlap frames are corrected separately by it (Fig 2 :31, :40), a source-discriminating instrument already returns something (Fig 6 :32), the verifier cannot reconstruct the claim (Fig 10 :42), and a trusted claim can be refused without deleting the record (Fig 6 :42-43). It relies mainly on institutional reputation when the reference updates on the frames (Fig 2 :34), the frames share one pool (Fig 2 :40), the status signal is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27), standing survives outcomes (Fig 10 :61), a room supplies the verdict (Fig 7 :35), and the object is a standing class (Fig 11 :51). The two readings are isomers over a fixed atom set (Fig 12 :117) and members of one functional group otherwise. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The numerical tipping point and the named trustworthy expert are a declared `gap`, not silence. Mode `specified` on the separator, `general` on structure, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the framework before their stage-1 verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A second cost is mistaking a shared repetition for corroboration, which Fig 2 :40 disqualifies.
- **undoable:** yes. The separator is deliverable without committing to any institution or expert, and a wrong condition can be removed by adding a refusal while keeping the record (Fig 6 :42-43). A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and no credibility has to be erased.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that separates observation from inherited prior at the slot would name the weighting; a demonstration that shared-pool agreement predicts the ground as well as independent frames would reverse Fig 2 :40; an institutional signal that does not update on its own holders' standing and whose keeper has no interest in the reading would make it an anchor; a standing list of experts invariant across residuals would reverse Fig 11 :51; a derivation of a numerical tipping point from the figures would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
