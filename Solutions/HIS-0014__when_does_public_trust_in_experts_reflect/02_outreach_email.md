# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. If the stage-1 output fails, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about public trust in experts, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question and would value a check, not agreement. I am not asking you to trust a framework, and I am not asking you to read it first. If the output below fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Public trust in an expert is a belief about a particular claim, not a stock of confidence in a class of people. The claim I want checked is a separator of conditions:
>
> 1. Trust reflects careful evaluation when the trust reading is indexed per claim, when the belief leans on observations that can be ranged against a ground that does not change its answer because anyone thinks it should, when at least two frames with little shared evidence are corrected separately by that ground, and when some instrument already returns a reading that distinguishes the source of the signal from the signal itself.
> 2. Trust relies mainly on institutional reputation when the reference updates on the frames (standing moves when the institution is praised), when the frames share one evidence pool so agreement is the pool talking, when the reputation signal is a quantity the same frames produce and then use to grade themselves, and when standing survives the outcomes it was earned on.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. The framework does not return a percentage and does not name which expert is trustworthy. Prestige can get a claim a hearing; it cannot settle the claim.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That all institutional trust is bad, that credentials never correlate with competence, that scepticism is itself evidence, or that there is a numerical point at which trust flips. Those are bindings from the world onto held terms, and until a ground settles them they carry no evidentiary weight in this claim.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these before sending this:
> 1. a public trust reading that tracks independently observed ground contact reliably with no anchor and no independent frame;
> 2. an institutional signal that does not update on its own holders' standing, whose keeper has no stake in the answer, and that settles claims as well as a ground reading;
> 3. agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool that predicts ground outcomes as well as low-overlap frames, across several domains;
> 4. a standing list of trusted experts that survives a change of residual with contact varied independently;
> 5. a checker that reconstructs the claim from the same frame and still corrects it independently, repeatedly;
> 6. a reputation-only signal that separates true from false expert claims without ground contact.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Prestige and the ground, essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-recursive-self-improvement-trap
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Recording the verdict before theory

Stage 1 ends when the recipient names what they would count as a failure and gives a verdict on the separator. Record that verdict, with date and provenance, before sending any link. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they have read the source and joined the frame (Fig 2 :40, Seat Map :163). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
