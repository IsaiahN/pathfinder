## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

We observe a trust reading t(s) directed at an expert or an expert claim on slot s. We do not observe its cause. Two producers are on offer.

- **Producer A, careful evaluation.** The public's belief b leans on observations o' that are ranged against a ground which does not change its answer because any frame thinks it should. The trust reading carries residual that can rise or fall with the ground. Frames are independent, so agreement is triangulation.
- **Producer B, institutional reputation.** The belief leans on an inherited prior: credentials, affiliation, title, citation count, prestige, standing. That prior is a quantity the same frames manufacture and then use as evidence. The trust reading confirms itself; agreement is the pool talking.

The question is written as an either/or, but the Symbols table gives the mechanism as a weighting, so the residual is better stated as: **which conditions make the weighting lean on observation-against-ground, and which make it lean on inherited prior?** Prediction: if A governs, t(s) should move with independently observed ground contact while institutional signals are held fixed; if B governs, t(s) should move with standing signals and with others' trust while the ground is never consulted.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): a **separator**, a finite list of conditions with a nameable anchor each, such that a trust reading can be classified per slot. Binding the separator lets it accrue a residual and be refused. It is bound in section 3 and used in `06`, `07`, `08` and `09`.

A warning carried from the exemplar and confirmed in `05`: "shape, not a name" governs a *missing* primitive. It does not forbid naming world quantities such as "credential" or "reputation" that are already in the habitat. The forbidden move is promoting one of them into a primitive or an anchor.

## 2. Domain census

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** At least two conditions: an observation channel to a ground and a reputation signal. Possibly more, because the habitat enumeration in Fig 11 adds medium, stake and room structure. The Symbols table gives w_A + w_B = 1, so the two named conditions are a mixture, not exclusive alternatives. This is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Symmetry.** Belief update through a shared prior is common-cause and symmetric in the frames: they converge on whoever had the most influence. Triangulation is asymmetric: each frame is corrected separately by something that cannot be moved. Correlation has valence (Fig 2 :39), so the symmetry difference is structural, not cosmetic.
- **Scale.** Per expert-slot and per claim, never as one global "trust in experts" number (Fig 1 :19, :22). The population level is a different scale with a lossy transform (Fig 4), and the round trip gap must be measured, not assumed.

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**D1, public belief formation (human cognition).** Actors: members of the public as bounded frames, each with a belief b and a weighting w. Currency: prediction against observation, R = |Γ(b,a) − o'| per slot. Anchor: the expert's ground-contact outcome o', when independently observable. Verdict: careful evaluation is possible here only when that anchor is reachable and the weighting leans on private observation. Where no anchor registers, the frame has no edge to extend from (Fig 6 :34-35), and the belief leans on the prior.

**D2, expert knowledge production (the ground side).** Actors: the expert, instruments, data, peer frames. Currency: predictions that pay; settled terms. Anchor: measurement, experiment, counter, patient outcome (Fig 2 :29). Verdict: credibility accrued is frame-internal (Fig 1 :26-27), so it cannot score the claim. A trust reading that only sees confidence and credentials is an instrument that cannot comment on the source.

**D3, institutional reputation and credentialing (the prestige loop).** Actors: institutions, credential bodies, citation and prize systems, media prestige signals. Currency: standing, credibility accrued, accumulated advantage. Anchor: **none that does not update on the frame's own output**. Verdict: this is not yet a domain with a ground; it is a mirror or common-cause collapse (Fig 2 :4-7, :13-15, :18-21). It can fund a try, it cannot settle a claim. Its keeper has an interest in the reading (Fig 10 :59, :93).

**D4, dissemination and the room chain (social transmission).** Actors: rooms, media, chains of agreeing frames. Currency: agreement, compounding, influence. Anchor: none, unless the next room is selected against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :35-36). Verdict: a chain of mirrors is one long room (Fig 7 :36). Popularity is variation, not a verdict.

**D5, the verification arrangement (how a public could check).** Actors: a ground-maintainer function, outside frames, records. Currency: independence, refusal, provenance. Anchor: the ground, never the agreement. Verdict: soundness requires the checker not to be able to reconstruct what it checks (Fig 10 :42); agreement drawn from one pool is disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40).

### 2.3 Partition or union

Union. A single trust reading can carry both a carefully evaluated component and a reputation component; the weighting w is continuous. This matters practically: a lever that only removes prestige signals leaves the unanchored instrument condition standing, and a lever that only adds open data leaves the shared-pool condition standing (Fig 9 :55-57). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a single "trust is mostly X" number.

### 2.4 Dependency order (Fig 3 :20)

1. Is there reachable ground contact for this claim at all? (D2, anchor test, Fig 2 :29-30.)
2. Is there an instrument, already returning something, that the evaluator can range against it? (D1, Fig 6 :32-34.)
3. Are there two or more frames with low overlap that can be corrected separately? (D5 and D4, Fig 2 :31-32.)
4. Only then ask how much the weighting leans on the prior (D3). Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Reputation engineering at step 4 cannot rescue a missing ground at step 1.

Ordering is by dependency, not by importance. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

## 3. Findings F1 to F13

Each finding states a claim, a verbatim figure line cited before the conclusion, a mode, and a refuter written down before the run.

### F1: Trust must be indexed per expert and per claim, not aggregated

**Verbatim first.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

A single index of "public trust in experts" averages over slots with different anchors. The live signal, the slot where trust moved with the ground and the slot where it did not, disappears. The question as posed invites that average; the figures refuse it.

**Mode:** general (structural), `specified` when applied to a named domain. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 13's per-turn residual. **Refuter:** show a single aggregate trust index that reliably predicts per-claim trust corrections against an independent ground better than the per-slot readings. If it does, per-slot indexing is not load bearing here.

### F2: Credibility accrued is frame-internal and cannot score the claim

**Verbatim first.** Fig 1 :24-27: "coverage, and compression achieved" and "predicates minted, and credibility accrued" and "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

Reputation, credentials and citation counts are quantities the expert frame produces and also uses to grade itself. Fig 5 :48: "Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing." The prestige loop is the same arithmetic one level up: the high-prestige person is believed because they are high-prestige, and the ground is never consulted.

**Mode:** general. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1, Fig 5, and the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essay, which has maximal overlap with the framework). **Refuter:** produce a reputation signal that does not update on its own holders' standing and that independently settles a claim at least as well as a ground reading. That would make the signal an anchor, and F2 would fail.

### F3: Careful evaluation requires a non-updating anchor; reputation-driven trust is mutual update or common cause

**Verbatim first.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :13: "no anchor; each updates on the others". Fig 2 :18-21: "one evidence pool", "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other". Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

Producer A is panel 3 of Fig 2: two frames range against a pinned anchor that does not update on them. Producer B is panels 1 and 2: either the reference updates on the frames (institutional standing moves when the institution is praised) or the frames never talk but share one pool (the same report, the same prestige signal). The drift is coherent, which is why it feels like careful evaluation from inside.

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." For "is this expert's claim true", the legitimate anchor is the claim's ground contact, not the expert's standing.

**Mode:** specified. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 panels plus Fig 10 channel rule). **Refuter:** demonstrate a stable trust correction that occurs with no anchor and no shared evidence, so that neither mutual update nor common cause is present. Then the anchor condition is not necessary and F3 fails.

### F4: Independence decides whether agreement means anything

**Verbatim first.** Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 8 :47-49: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

A public that trusts an expert because many independent-seeming sources repeat the same institutional signal is one frame wearing many names. The number of sources is a contact-cost estimate, not a witness count. Fig 8 :50: "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Only the record of where each came from separates convergence from adoption (Fig 8 :51-52).

**Mode:** general. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 8). **Refuter:** show agreement between frames sharing an evidence pool that reliably predicts ground outcomes at least as well as independent frames, across several domains. Then the disqualifying-valence rule does not govern this quantity and F4 fails.

### F5: A room, a consensus or a chain does not upgrade the trust signal into a verdict

**Verbatim first.** Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Seat Map :283: "A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." Broad public consensus about an expert is a room's agreement. It selects nothing unless the next room differs and matches the surfaced residual.

**Mode:** general. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 7, Seat Map, Fig 2). **Refuter:** produce a case where adding agreeing frames, with the evidence pool fixed, reliably moves a trust judgment closer to the ground. Then agreement is adding evidence and F5 fails.

### F6: The literal mechanism is the weighting w, not a binary switch

**Verbatim first (Symbols table).** Symbols :19-21: "What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation." Symbols :23-26: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."

This is the closest thing the figures give to a direct answer to the question's shape. Public trust is a belief formed from private evaluation and inherited prior, mixed by w. The weighting is learnable from outcomes, so it is not a fixed personality trait; it can be recalibrated by what happens. Two publics with identical information diverge because their histories and their w diverge. The question's "when A, when B" becomes "under what conditions does w lean on observation against a reachable anchor, and under what conditions does it lean on the inherited prior?"

**Mode:** general (the mechanism), `specified` when applied to a public. **Status:** single-source for the symbol definition (Symbols table), triangulated with Fig 2's anchor and independence rules for the conditions. **Refuter:** show a trust belief that is not a mixture of private history and inherited prior, or a w that is not learnable from outcomes. Then the symbol reading fails and the question must be re-censused.

### F7: Careful evaluation needs a source-discriminating instrument

**Verbatim first.** Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay gives the mechanism: an instrument that detects green "cannot comment on the source"; the reading could come from one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK or a scheme not yet discovered. Its words are: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

A public reading expert confidence, credentials or institutional affiliation is reading a projection. Those signals cannot comment on the source, which is whether the claim has met its ground. Where nothing at any resolution is returning a source-discriminating reading, this is `unreached`, not a licence to invent a trust score.

**Mode:** specified. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 3, and the source essay, which shares the author's evidence pool). **Refuter:** exhibit a reputation-only signal that reliably separates true from false expert claims without any ground contact, across domains whose ground differs. Then the instrument condition is not necessary and F7 fails.

### F8: A reputation that decides an outcome has stopped being a reputation, and standing is a disqualifying stake

**Verbatim first.** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :61: "And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :26: "saturated metrics, pooled readings".

When an institution's standing is used as the thing that settles whether its expert is right, the standing has become a verdict. Its keeper benefits from the answer, so the keeper is disqualified from maintaining the ground. The channel, not the ground, decays: stale track records, saturated citation metrics and pooled readings make the signal look healthy after contact is lost. Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

**Mode:** general (rule), specified (institutional application). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10, Seat Map :171, essay). **Refuter:** show an institutional signal that settles a claim without deciding its outcome by convention, whose keeper has no interest in the reading, and whose channel does not decay with use. Then the disqualification does not govern here.

### F9: No standing list of trusted experts survives a change of residual

**Verbatim first.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :39: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

"Experts" is not a standing trustworthy class. The same expert is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so trust is a relation, not a property carried by a person or a title. Improving credentials in isolation changes nothing if contact is unchanged. This dissolves the question's implied standing class: the answer is per residual, per slot.

**Mode:** specified. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 1 per-slot). **Refuter:** exhibit a standing class of experts whose trustworthiness is invariant across residuals and habitats, with contact held and varied independently. Then valence is carried, not relative, and F9 fails.

### F10: Verifier non-access is what makes careful evaluation sound

**Verbatim first.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Seat Map :149: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it. The checker must be unable to produce what it is checking."

A member of the public who has absorbed the institution's whole framing of the expert is no longer outside the frame they were to check. This is why outreach must carry the residual and the refuter first and the source only on opt-in. It is also why the question "when does public trust reflect careful evaluation" cannot be answered by giving the public more of the institution's own material.

**Mode:** general. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10, Seat Map, zero-knowledge analogy in Fig 10 :84). **Refuter:** show a verifier that reconstructs the claim from the same frame and still independently corrects it against the ground over repeated trials. Then non-access is not load bearing and F10 fails.

### F11: The question is a union, so one lever leaves the intersections standing

**Verbatim first.** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

"Distrust all institutions" clears the region where only reputation binds, and leaves the case where the public has no source-discriminating instrument standing. "Publish all the data" clears the instrument region and leaves the shared-evidence-pool case standing. The honest output is a separator of conditions, not a single attitude.

**Mode:** general. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9, Symbols w). **Refuter:** show that the conditions are exclusive, so that every trust reading is produced by exactly one of evaluation and reputation with no overlap. Then the union reading is wrong and a partition is owed.

### F12: No figure line returns a tipping point or a named trusted expert

**Verbatim first.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

No line gives the numerical value of w at which trust flips from evaluation to reputation, and no line names an expert or an institution as trustworthy. Those are `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreached rather than silence. An instrument is owed (Fig 6 :32), not an invented threshold.

**Mode:** general. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9, Fig 13, Fig 8). **Refuter:** derive a numerical tipping point from the figures alone, or exhibit a figure line that names a standing trusted actor. Then the gap closes.

### F13: Refusal is a negation, so a trust claim can be refused without deleting a reputation

**Verbatim first.** Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Fig 6 :42-43: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."

When a trusted expert's claim is refuted, the reputation record is not deleted. What falls is the standing of that claim to be composed over. This is the only safe way to revise public trust without rewriting history: keep the record, add the refusal. The reach falls because something was added.

**Mode:** general. **Status:** single-source for the operator type (Operators table plus Fig 6 amendment), triangulated with the Formula note on negation. **Refuter:** show a correction to public trust that requires erasing a prior reputation record rather than adding a refusal. Then the monotone-record rule does not govern here.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The separator claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1, anchor not necessary.** A public trust reading tracks independently observed ground contact reliably, under varied residuals, with no anchor and no independent frame, so neither mutual update nor common cause is present.
2. **R2, reputation can be an anchor.** An institutional signal does not update on its own holders' standing, has a keeper with no interest in the reading, and settles claims at least as well as a ground reading.
3. **R3, correlation not disqualifying.** Agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool predicts ground outcomes at least as well as low-overlap frames, across several domains.
4. **R4, standing valence.** A standing list of trusted experts remains valid across residuals and habitats with contact varied independently.
5. **R5, reconstructible verifier.** A checker that reconstructs the claim from the same frame still corrects it independently against the ground over repeated trials.
6. **R6, source-discriminating reputation.** A reputation-only signal separates true from false expert claims without ground contact, across domains with different grounds.
7. **R7, partition.** The two producers turn out to be exclusive, so the union reading is wrong.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the separator is rebuilt. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Publish the **separator**, not an attitude toward expertise. Keep verdicts per claim and per domain, never averaged into an index (F1, F11).
2. Treat reputation as a **budget**, never a verdict: it can fund a hearing, it cannot settle a claim (F2, F8).
3. Build the **instrument** before arguing about trust: open methods, raw records, public track records keyed to claims, adversarial review with a refusal path (F7, F13).
4. Keep frames **independent**: cite provenance of each reading, do not pool repetitions of one signal as witnesses (F4, F5).
5. **Declare standing** wherever a keeper benefits from the answer, and route verification through a seat that cannot see the interiors it grades (F8, F10).
6. Record the **weighting w** as a learnable parameter in its own right, with the data source and outcome history attached (F6).
7. Say `gap` for the numerical tipping point and for any named trusted class. Do not invent a threshold (F12).

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the separator and the refuter, not a conclusion about who to trust.

## 7. Strains, re-census and the honest stop

- **S1, Fig 2 anchor versus Fig 11 valence.** Dissolved by re-census: anchor is what settles; habitat is where actors come from and it updates. No contradiction.
- **S2, Fig 8 surplus versus Fig 2 disqualifying correlation.** Dissolved: same independence parameter, two quantities. High overlap gives no surplus and disqualifies the verdict; low overlap gives both.
- **S3, Symbols w continuous versus the question's binary either/or.** Re-censused as a union (F11), not a strain. The binary is the question's framing, not the framework's.
- **S4, "shape, not a name" applied to credentials.** Narrowed and preserved in section 6: it governs missing primitives, not bindings in the habitat.
- **S5, living with an unmeasured tipping point.** Live and named. No figure gives a numerical w. The honest form is a separator plus a refuter plus an owed discriminator, not a number.

After re-census: one live strain (S5), recorded, none hidden.

**Stop.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The separator is reachable from the figures. The numerical boundary and the named expert are not. The answer stops there and records what would close it.

## 8. Verdict summary (detail in `07` and `09`)

Public trust reflects careful evaluation when the trust reading is indexed per claim, the weighting leans on observations independently ranged against a non-updating ground, at least two low-overlap frames are corrected separately by that ground, and a source-discriminating instrument exists. It relies mainly on institutional reputation when the anchor is absent or updates on the frames, the frames share one evidence pool, the reputation signal is frame-internal and self-confirming, and standing survives the outcomes it was earned on. This is `specified` on the separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical tipping point and on any standing list of trusted experts, and not `grounded`. No verdict is averaged across domains.
