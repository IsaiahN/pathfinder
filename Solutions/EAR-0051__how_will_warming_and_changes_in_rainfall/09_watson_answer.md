## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the effect of warming and changes in rainfall on soil carbon storage. Option A: answer with a single global number or a single dominant mechanism. Option B: answer with a shape, a slot-indexed conditional map with a threshold region and a pre-registered refuter, and refuse the magnitude until the ground settles it. What each leads to: A aggregates away the live slots and treats a union compound as one problem, so a single-factor lever appears to answer it; B keeps the two conditions separate, keeps the interaction as its own operand, and states what would falsify the claim before any theory is shown.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between predicted and observed net soil-carbon flux under a temperature and water combination. Arity: three, warming, rainfall and their interaction. Symmetry: not symmetric, warming is an energy condition and rainfall a water and gradient condition, and the flux comparison is ordered. Scale: first order at the plot and profile slot, second order at landscape, third order through cascade. Fig 9 names a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, habitat conditions.** Actors: temperature, water, season, terrain as conditions. Currency: contact with the soil system. Anchor: the figure text itself, which lists water and temperature as habitat conditions. Question type: enumeration, not composition.
- **Domain 2, stock and flow.** Actors: SOC fractions, roots, microbial residues, mineral surfaces. Currency: carbon mass, residence time, input flow versus output flow. Anchor: the operators' comparison and the figures' six-term set. Question type: a threshold comparison of flows, with isomeric fractions.
- **Domain 3, actor valence.** Actors: decomposers, mycorrhizae, fauna, crops. Currency: benefit or harm relative to SOC storage. Anchor: the valence rule in Fig 11. Question type: relational, not a standing list of good or bad organisms.
- **Domain 4, compound and cascade.** Actors: warming, rainfall, erosion, fire, thaw, snow-cover change. Currency: second and third-order effects and the intersection term. Anchor: the union rule in Fig 9 and the cascade rule in Fig 11. Question type: union.
- **Domain 5, verification.** Actors: field warming plus precipitation experiments, long-term monitoring, remote sensing, process models. Currency: independent contact with the ground. Anchor: measurement and experiment, Fig 2. Question type: triangulation, not negotiation.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate conditions first, describe the residual before choosing a frame, index by slot before composing flows, resolve fractions before comparing flows, then verify with independent frames.

Strain test applied: Fig 13 governs what composes with what; Fig 9 governs how the residual splits into conditions; Fig 12 governs the arrangement of fractions. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census. The one live limit is that the figures license the comparison shape but not the numerical location of a threshold; that location is a world claim, declared as such.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the magnitude is refused. Warming and rainfall are two enumerated conditions whose bond is a flow, the combined question is a union compound whose intersection a single-factor lever cannot clear, the net flux is a threshold comparison between input and output flows, fractions are isomers whose different bonds make bulk totals misleading, actor valence is relative to carbon storage, indirect cascade terms can dominate direct ones, and the carbon loop's sign can flip. The answer is a slot-indexed, fraction-resolved conditional map with a refuter written in advance, not a global PgC number. This is a `specified`, `candidate`, ungrounded result. Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." So the refuter leads and the framework follows only by opt-in.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, keeps verdicts per domain and carries four falsifiers, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a global number or a theory before the residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The bindings of temperature to energy and moisture to gradient are removable without touching the derivation, and neither is asserted as a figure output. The magnitude, when it arrives, can supersede the shape without withdrawing it. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A fraction-resolved, multi-level warming-by-precipitation experiment across several slots that locates the sign flip would supply the magnitude; a single-factor response that predicts the combined result within error, a linear-additive flux with no threshold, a global mean that predicts each slot, or indirect effects strictly smaller than direct would refute the shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
