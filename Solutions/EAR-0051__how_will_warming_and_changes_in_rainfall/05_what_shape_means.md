# What "shape" means for this question

**Mode: `general` on the reading of the figures, `specified` on the shape claimed for soil carbon.**

**Reading discipline.** The only essay opened this session is *The Library of Babel: Locating The Book of Books* (Isaiah N, adventuresinml Substack, 2026-10-02, https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-library-of-babel-locating-the, accessed 2026-11-22). Other essays listed in the exemplar were not opened this session and are therefore not cited here. Provenance is in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule (`FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, local copy) states that the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and that "Indiscernibility is not identity". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From the essay actually read: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." Also: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer that can be stated before it can be filled in. It constrains and it does not fill. For this question:

- **Not a shape:** "global soil carbon will fall by X PgC by 2100". That is a number with no slot index, no fraction resolution and no threshold.
- **A shape:** "the net flux is a slot-indexed comparison between input and output flows under two enumerated conditions, with a sign flip near a threshold in the interaction; indirect cascade terms dominate direct terms; the sign of the global loop can flip."

The shape is checkable before any magnitude is supplied. That is its whole value.

## 2. How the shape is obtained here

- **Enumerate, do not compose.** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." The conditions are read off the world, not invented: Fig 11 :46 "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain".
- **Describe the residual before choosing a frame.** Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The Library of Babel essay calls this the vocabulary problem: use the terms you already have that are closest, then "with each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary".
- **Keep the synonyms honest.** The essay warns that new terms "overlap, correlate, or are even used interchangeably with your known terms". "Soil carbon", "soil organic matter", "SOC stock" and "soil respiration" are not synonyms. The first two name a stock, the third a measurement of a stock, the fourth an output flow. The shape requires the distinction.
- **Split before searching.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The essay's bundle passage is the same rule in search language.

## 3. What licenses a magnitude or a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Here: a fraction-resolved, multi-level warming-plus-rainfall field measurement, or a long-term monitoring record.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The critical-transitions frame (Bury et al. 2020, Scheffer et al. 2009) is such an import, used with a source gate and a shadow test: it explains the threshold shape the soil frame leaves undescribed. It is debited against independence, so its agreement is not a second witness.

The essay states the stopping rule: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." For this question we are not done. The literature itself says data on individual and combined direct effects are very limited. Honest stop: shape reported, magnitude owed.

## 4. Why the global-average answer is the specific forbidden error

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A global SOC percentage averages permafrost, peatland, tropical mineral soil and temperate cropland. IPCC AR6 reports carbon loss in the tropics and accumulation at high latitudes under climate change alone, with permafrost release as a separate high-confidence process. Averaging those is exactly how the live slots disappear. The shape therefore requires slot indexing before any number is offered, and it requires that the refuter include a test of whether the global mean predicts each slot (it should not).

This is not a metamerism claim about instruments reading one source from several paths, because no such instrument dispute is in evidence here. It is a slot-index claim, and it is figure-derived.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming the mechanism?

**No, with a bound.** The rule governs a missing primitive, one not held in the library. "Energy", "gradient" and "comparison" are already in the six-term set and the operator set (Fig 13, operators table). Naming the mechanism as a binding onto held terms is permitted if the binding is provenanced as ours and not presented as a figure output. What is forbidden is presenting the binding as something the framework derived, or using it to smuggle in a seventh primitive.

For this question: "energy plus gradient compose a flow" is a figure line; "temperature and soil moisture are that energy and gradient in this soil" is a binding from the earth-system frame, recorded as such. "Net flux is a comparison" is a figure line; "the threshold is at 3 °C in an alpine meadow under +50% precipitation" is a world measurement from Chen et al. 2026, `specified`, not a figure output.

## 6. Two consequences for this folder

**Prestige is not a validator.** A large list of distinguished signatories cannot settle a soil-carbon claim. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." The relevant ask is for a refutation from someone whose evidence pool differs, bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** The output is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
