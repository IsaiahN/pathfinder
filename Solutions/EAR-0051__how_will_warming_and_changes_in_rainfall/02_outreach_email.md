**Subject:** Check one shape claim about soil carbon under warming and rainfall

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** warming and rainfall change are two conditions in the soil habitat, not one "climate" variable. The figures list water and temperature separately as habitat conditions and require that the habitat be enumerated rather than composed. When the question is a case in two conditions, the two-condition case is categorically different from either single-condition case: a warming-only lever clears the region where water is not limiting and leaves the intersection standing. So the answer cannot be a single global soil-carbon number. It has to be a conditional map, per soil slot and per carbon fraction, of where the net flux is a sink, where it is a source, and where the sign flips.
>
> The mechanism shape is a comparison, not a sum. Input and output flows are weighed against each other, so the response should be nonlinear near the switch. Temperature and moisture bond into a flow rather than adding: a gradient over time and a gradient over space are different terms. The sign of the larger carbon loop can flip, which is why a chain-of-causes account cannot represent the feedback.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving you a PgC number for 2100. I am not claiming the framework returns the magnitude, the date of a threshold, or which soil slot is largest. Those are world claims that need measurements. I am not claiming that models agreeing with one another corroborate anything, and I am not asking for agreement with the framework.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single-factor response function that predicts the combined, fraction-resolved result within error at every slot, with no interaction term;
> 2. a net soil-carbon flux that is linear and additive in temperature and water, with no threshold and no fraction dependence across the measured range;
> 3. a global mean that predicts each slot's sign and magnitude;
> 4. indirect effects (erosion, fire, thaw, snow-cover change) shown to be strictly smaller than direct effects everywhere.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Global syntheses and field tests on the residual itself: see `04_links_and_distribution.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
