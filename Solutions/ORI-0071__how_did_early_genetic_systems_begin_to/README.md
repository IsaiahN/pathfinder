# Solution ORI-0071

**Question:** How did early genetic systems begin to produce peptides or proteins?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 100

**Section:** 8. From chemical evolution to cells

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive shape claims, `general` on the structural grammar. Nothing here is `grounded`: the historical route is not directly measurable at this budget, so no claim is asserted as settled by an instrument.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual, in effect terms.** Early genetic polymers could copy themselves but had no directed route from a heritable sequence to an amino-acid chain. The question asks for the shape of the transition by which that route appeared. Two options are compared: (A) treat it as one hard problem and search harder inside the RNA world frame; (B) describe the residual, split it into its constituent conditions, and derive the shape of the crossing from the figures. Each leads somewhere different: A spends budget inside a closure that cannot add the missing atom; B records what had to be imported, what bond did the coupling, when the loop closed, and what a ground reading would have to show.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"):** an answer is a *route description* with four parts: (1) the two frames that had to touch, (2) the atom that crossed, (3) the bond that coupled a sequence to a peptide, and (4) the feedback that made the arrangement settle. This shape was written down before the figure census and is treated as a refusable term, not as a preference.
