**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been checked against the verbatim figure text by the recipient. If the stage-1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** Check one claim about the RNA to protein transition before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in origins research: how an early heritable polymer came to direct the formation of peptides. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is not a single "which came first" problem. It splits into at least two conditions, a heritable carrier and a catalytic output, plus a coupling and a feedback. Fig 9 puts it: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." From that split, three things follow.
>
> 1. A replicating polymer cannot reach coded peptide synthesis by search alone. Fig 6: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." So amino-acid chemistry, present in the habitat, had to cross into the genetic closure.
> 2. The crossing is a bond, not a list of ingredients. Fig 12: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." The adapter slot is doing the work, not the monomer inventory.
> 3. The beginning is a cycle, not an invention. Fig 12: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Once peptides helped the carrier that encoded them persist, the loop closed. Where nothing maintains anything, selection ran; no foresight is required.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the historical route. The framework returns a shape, not a route name. RNA world, pre-RNA world, peptide-RNA world and the three origin hypotheses for the code remain live candidates. Fig 13: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" So the slot for "which polymer actually did it first" stays open, and I would like to know what would close it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. a single closure, with no differing chemistry contacting it, producing a heritable sequence-to-peptide mapping;
> 2. a reconstruction showing the first peptidyl transferase activity was a fully formed apparatus with no selectable precursor;
> 3. a heritable mapping with no typed adapter relation;
> 4. a high-fidelity code that persists with no peptide feeding back on carrier replication;
> 5. translation requiring a primitive outside the six held terms.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
