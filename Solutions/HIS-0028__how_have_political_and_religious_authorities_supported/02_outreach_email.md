**Subject:** Check one distinction before you decide whether the claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about science and authority. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** political and religious authorities do not have a fixed sign in the history of science. What an authority does to inquiry depends on which of two operations it performs, not on whether the authority is "enlightened" or "repressive".
>
> 1. **Support is an introduction that changes contact.** When an authority funds an instrument, imports a method from a different community, founds an academy, or sponsors a translation, it changes what an inquiring community can actually read from the world. The currency is changed contact, not money spent.
> 2. **Limit is the substitution of the authority for the ground.** When a decree, a doctrine, or a reputation-protecting convention becomes the thing that settles the dispute, the measurement can no longer correct the frame. The authority has taken the anchor chair. The figures warn that once the anchor updates on what people think, everything drifts together and nobody notices, because from inside it looks like agreement.
>
> The claim is that these are two relations to a residual, not two kinds of actor, so the same institution can perform both, and a support can turn into a limit when the question changes. I am **not** claiming which institution was the bad one, and I am not offering a list of centuries. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an authority-supported program whose readings do not change, with no new instrument, no import and no new contact;
> 2. a decree that constrained inquiry while the ground's verdict remained correctable and was actually used to reverse the decree;
> 3. an authority whose valence stays the same across a documented change of the question;
> 4. an institution whose support and limit cannot be separated into two operations with different instruments.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1:** send the distinction and its four refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record the verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** if curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a reusable method. Each user writes their own refuter, names their own ground and states their own mode.

A stage-2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
