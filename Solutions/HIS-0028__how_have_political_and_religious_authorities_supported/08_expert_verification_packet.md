# Expert verification packet: the Tether on science and authority

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science or an STS scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No historical verdict is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An authority's effect on inquiry is not a fixed property of the authority. It depends on which of two operations it performs. **Support** is an introduction that changes contact with a ground that can settle the question: an instrument, an imported method, a new office, a head start. **Limit** is the installation of the authority where the ground should be, so a decree becomes what settles the dispute and the measurement can no longer correct the frame. Under a fixed core of authority, inquirers and ground, the two are different bond arrangements, so the same institution can perform both and a support can turn into a limit when the question changes. The framework names no institution as the answer; which case is which is left to the archive.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 2

- **Statement:** an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. A decree that becomes the settlement is a frame occupying the anchor chair.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 2 :34 - *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **Refuter:** a documented authority whose measured valence is invariant across a change of the question that the case itself documents; or a decree that leaves the ground's verdict correctable and actually used to reverse the decree in the ordinary course of the institution.

### C2 - history-facing; checkable by a historian against known cases

- **Statement:** support shows up as a change in what can be read, not as expenditure alone. Limit shows up as an institution certifying its own prior reading. The two are separable with different instruments.
- **What to check:** in a case you know well, did the authority's intervention add an instrument, an imported method or a head start that produced a new reading? Separately, did a decree or a convention become the thing that settled the dispute? Are those two observations about the same slot, or about different slots?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **Refuter:** an authority-supported program with no change in ground contact and no import, measured prospectively; or an institution whose support and limit cannot be separated into two operations with different instruments.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a structural fork

- **Statement:** under a fixed three-atom core (authority, inquirers, ground), support and limit are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. If the mediator and the decree must be counted as different atoms, the claim degrades to a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **Refuter:** show that either operation requires an atom the other does not, so no common three-atom core can be held fixed; or show an institution whose method remains a regenerating catalyst across two generations after its residual changed, with that persistence measured.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No archive was adjudicated and no new measurement was taken.
- Not that any named institution was wholly good or wholly bad, and not a ranking of centuries.
- Not that the conflict thesis is true or false as a historical generalisation. That requires the archive.
- Not that a single patron causes bad science. The figure quantity is independence of evidence (Fig 2 :31), not the number of funders.
- Not that the framework settles which authority dominated in a given case. That is a `gap`, not a silence.
- Not that the eight essays or the world sources are independent corroboration. They share authors and evidence pools with the figures and with each other.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Contact test:** did a new reading become available after the intervention, with a new instrument or an import from a differing closure?
2. **Anchor test:** after a decree, was the ground still able to reverse it through ordinary institutional procedure?
3. **Catalyst test:** did the institution keep generating new readings after its original question changed, or did it reproduce past success (Fig 12 :119)?
4. **Discriminator:** an instrument that distinguishes an introduction from a substitution in the same archive would move part of the claim from `specified` toward `grounded`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51, Fig 2 :33-34, Fig 10 :47 and Fig 9 :57: four short passages.
2. Pick one case you know well. Ask the contact test and the anchor test separately, and write down the answer to each before comparing them.
3. Say which of C1 to C3 fails, if any, and name the document you would use to decide.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
