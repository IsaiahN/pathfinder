## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that political and religious authorities both support and limit scientific inquiry. Option A: treat authorities as fixed kinds with fixed moral signs, so the answer is a ranking of institutions. Option B: treat support and limit as two relations of an authority to a residual, so the answer is a shape with a discriminator. What each leads to: A produces a list of good and bad institutions, invites a prestige-ranked verdict, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B produces a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open slot until an archival instrument settles which operation dominated in a given case.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The valence inversion: an authority's action sometimes reduces the gap between what an inquiring community predicts and what its ground returns, and sometimes replaces that ground with a decree so the gap cannot be measured. Arity: three-place (authority, inquiry slot, ground), indexed by time. Symmetry: not symmetric; patron and inquirer are not interchangeable, though the loop is recursive. Scale: single inquiry loop, population habitat of patrons and journals, multi-century channel. Fig 9 names it a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the inquiry loop.** Actors: inquirers, instruments, Γ, per-slot R. Currency: residual reduction and settlement. Anchor: the returning measurement. Question type: the loop being acted on.
- **Domain 2, authority as habitat actor (support).** Actors: patrons, academies, churches, states, universities, observatories, translation bureaus. Currency: introductions, imports from differing closures, head starts. Anchor: did contact with the ground increase? Question type: introduction, available but not subtractable.
- **Domain 3, authority as ground substitute (limit).** Actors: censors, inquisitors, party ideologues, reputation-protecting keepers. Currency: conventions that decide outcomes. Anchor: the ground, which cannot be moved. Question type: habitat substitution, in which a decree takes the anchor chair.
- **Domain 4, the historical record and verification frame.** Actors: archives, published sources, expert readers. Currency: provenance and residual description. Anchor: the documentary record (updates) and the ground (does not). In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so agreement here is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Excluded before figure selection:** "authority is good or bad by nature" (no anchor independent of the slot's ground), and "science versus religion as two fixed blocks" (no independent anchor; already D2 and D3 merged, so it is a union reported as a partition).
- **Order by dependency.** Settle perception before vocabulary, vocabulary before objective, objective before planning (Fig 3 :20); refuse a false identity before minting a new term (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: an early attempt to apply Fig 13's six physical terms to institutions fit only by renaming an academy a kind of energy. Re-census sent Fig 13 to the honest-stop role. No unresolved strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the historical distribution does not. An authority supports inquiry when its action changes the inquiring community's contact with a ground that can settle the slot, through introduction, instruments and imports from a differing closure. It limits inquiry when the authority installs itself where the ground should be, turning a convention into a verdict and a habitat into a substitution. Valence is relative to the residual, so one institution can do both and a support can become a limit when the question changes. Under a fixed three-atom core the two operations are a Fig 12 isomer claim with a stated falsifier. Which institution, in which century, performed which operation is a `gap`: the archival discriminator that would settle it is not held in this simulated run. Mode `specified`; no part is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The corrected packet states its mode, lists four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a moral ranking instead of a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the two operations and the refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The moral-ranking reading is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no atom has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An archival instrument that discriminates an introduction from a substitution in the same case, showing that a claimed support changed no contact or that a decree was routinely reversed by the ground, would fire the refuter. A documented authority with invariant valence across a changed residual would break Fig 11 :49-51. A demonstration that the two operations need different atoms would collapse the isomer claim to a functional group. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
