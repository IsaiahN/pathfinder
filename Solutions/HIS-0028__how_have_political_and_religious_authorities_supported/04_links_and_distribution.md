## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The two-valence observation is not a figure invention.** The open encyclopedia entry on science and the Catholic Church says the relationship includes *"both extensive institutional patronage and support as well as periods of censorship and sharp conflict."* It also records that the Church founded Europe's first universities and that *"Copernicus, Brahe, Galileo, Kepler, and Newton all studied at universities founded during the Middle Ages."* That is the support operation in institutional form. The same entry records the Roman Inquisition and the Index as the limit operation. It is a tertiary source, so it corroborates the shape without settling any case.
2. **Support can be measured by what it lets people read.** The Galileo entry records that Jesuits at the Collegio Romano *"confirmed its results and threw a celebratory banquet"*, that Galileo obtained a chair *"with the help of Clavius and del Monte"*, and that he used the Starry Messenger to *"solicit patronage in his native Tuscany, naming the moons of Jupiter he had found the 'Medicean' stars."* Those are introductions with downstream effects on contact, which is what F2 predicts. The same entry records the limit: admonition, abjuration, house arrest, and the later *"1992 rehabilitation of Galileo"*, which shows an authority can also reverse its own limit when the ground eventually pays.
3. **A single patron changes the closure picture, not the morality picture.** Gordin reports Krementsov's *"one and only one patron for science, the party-state itself"*, and notes that in the pluralist case *"philanthropies, universities, and businesses all vied with the state to support particular sciences."* Read with Fig 2 :31, the relevant quantity is how little the competing frames draw on the same evidence. Many patrons sharing one evidence pool is one witness wearing many names.
4. **The limit is an authority problem, not a piety problem.** The SEP entry on Authority notes that *"a theoretical authority's judgment ... is a reason to adopt the belief expressed by the judgment"*, while *"a practical authority issues directives that are reasons for action rather than belief"*, and that authoritative directives are *"content-independent reasons for their subjects"* which can *"exclude and replace competing reasons"*. That is the mechanism in F3: a decree that preempts the ground reason is a frame occupying the anchor chair.
5. **The cognitive literature corroborates at a different stratum.** Authority bias is defined as *"the tendency to attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of an authority figure (unrelated to its content)"*. It predicts that a community will treat a decree as a better reading without new evidence, which is the individual-level version of Fig 10 :47. It is single-source for the institutional claim and is not used as a witness for it.
6. **The prestige rule matters for how this is sent.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* The outreach gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not gather names to endorse it.

No world source was allowed to flip a figure verdict. The two apparent conflicts were returned to the census and re-mapped (see `01` section 6, R1 and R2).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the whole framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the two operations and the refuters; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape were true before showing the article.
- **Fig 11 :51** - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Do not send a list of enlightened and benighted institutions and ask which one the reader likes. That manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two-operation shape and its four refuters; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**(1)** Validate the shape against an archive the reader knows. **(2)** Only if curious, read the theory that produced it. **(3)** Use the formula recursively on other cases.

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt in by construction.** Once a reader has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded first and their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per case.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 lists the four refuters that must be checked first.
