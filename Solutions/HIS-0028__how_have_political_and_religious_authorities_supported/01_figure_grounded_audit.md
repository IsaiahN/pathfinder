## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is what an inquiring community takes to be the case, formed from its own observations and what it inherited.
- **a** is the inquiry: observe, build an instrument, compute a calendar, run a trial, publish, teach an apprentice.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the community's library of methods and models predicts will happen.
- **o′** is what returns from the ground: a measurement, a harvest, an eclipse, a patient outcome, a reproducible reading.
- **s** is the inquiry slot, indexed per question and per community. R is never averaged into a single "science versus authority" number.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is a **valence inversion**: an authority that reduces R in one slot increases R in another, and often does both in the same institution in the same decade. State the residual as: *why does an authority's action sometimes extend a community's contact with what settles its questions, and sometimes replace that settlement with the authority's own decree, and why do the two effects co-occur?* This is an effect description. It names no theory, no "enlightenment", no "conflict thesis", no "patronage system". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a pair of operations whose difference is checkable, plus an instrument that discriminates a contact-changing intervention from a ground-substituting one. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 5 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names candidates: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* A decree, a consensus, a prestige ranking and an official doctrine all move when pushed. They are instruments or frames at best. Viable anchors here: the returning measurement and the archival record of what was actually done, kept strictly apart (Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** The residual is a three-place relation: authority A, inquiry slot s, ground reading o′, indexed by time. It is an ordered sequence, so it carries the **sequence** bond (`→`, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) and a **comparison** bond for "supported versus limited" (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87). It is not symmetric: patron and inquirer are not interchangeable, though the loop is recursive so an inquirer can later become an authority.
- **Symmetry.** The valence is relative to the slot, not absolute (Fig 11 :49-51). "Support" and "limit" are not properties of an institution; they are relations to a particular residual at a particular stratum.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single inquiry loop (one observer, one instrument), the population of inquiries sharing a habitat of patrons, journals, academies and churches (Fig 4 :5), and the multi-century channel through which a rule outlives the question that produced it (Fig 10 :24). The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured, not assumed zero (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| **D2 Authority as habitat actor, support** | patrons, academies, churches, states, universities, observatories, translation bureaus | introductions: instruments, funding, office, imports from a differing closure | yes: did contact with the ground increase? (Fig 11 :37, :40) | admitted |
| **D3 Authority as ground substitute, limit** | censors, inquisitors, party ideologues, dogmatic boards, reputation-protecting keepers | conventions that decide outcomes, credibility, enforcement | yes: the ground, which cannot be moved (Fig 2 :33-34) | admitted |
| **D4 The historical record and verification frame** | archives, published sources, expert readers | provenance, corroboration, residual description | yes: the documentary record, which updates, and the ground, which does not | admitted, secondary |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that no institution has ever acted consistently. X2 is the familiar "conflict thesis" framing; it is not a second domain but a premature merge of D2 and D3, and it is unbundled in section 1.3.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D2 and D3 overlap. The same actor can fund an observatory and proscribe a book. The overlap is a compound with its own loop, so the whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: **(S)** an intervention that changes contact with a ground, and **(L)** an intervention that substitutes the authority for the ground. A case of support only, a case of limit only, and a case of both are categorically different compounds and need different instruments. Fig 11 :66 names the second mechanism in the figure's own terms: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)
> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** (Fig 3 :29)

Order for this question: 1 perception (what did the instrument actually return?) → 2 vocabulary (does the community have terms for what the authority affords, and can it say what would count as progress under that action?) → 3 objective (is the objective more ground contact or more compliance?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link is the one that usually breaks: a community can describe a patron's money and a censor's rule, but it often has no instrument for saying what either does to contact. Most historical effort lands above the break because that is where the documents already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable claim about authorities and inquiry, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: Valence is a relation to the residual, not a property of the authority

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 2, low overlap: habitat relations versus anchor structure).**

> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)

This is the direct figure answer to the "while also limiting it" in the question. A political or religious authority is not a fixed kind with a fixed sign. It is an actor whose sign flips when the residual changes. The world records this at every scale: a papacy that sponsors astronomy and later proscribes a cosmology, a state that builds genetics institutes and later abolishes them, a court that funds an instrument and then owns the answer it returns.

**Refuter:** a documented authority whose measured valence is invariant across a change of the residual that the case itself documents, with both side-effects measured against the same ground. If no such case can be found because authorities always flip, F1 weakens toward `general`. If one is found where valence is invariant, F1 as applied here is false.

**World bearing (secondary only):** the open-access encyclopedia entry on science and the Catholic Church opens with *"The relationship between science and the Catholic Church has included both extensive institutional patronage and support as well as periods of censorship and sharp conflict."* That is the same two-valence observation in the historian's vocabulary. It corroborates the shape but cannot settle it, and it shares no evidence pool with the figures.

### F2: Support is introduction, and its currency is changed contact

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 6 and Fig 4, three figures with distinct roles: habitat operation, import, membrane).**

> **"enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."** (Fig 11 :37)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"The only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14, on IMPORT)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** (Fig 4 :22)

Support is not money in the abstract. In the figures it is an introduction into the habitat: an instrument, an imported method, a new actor, a head start that makes the next search cheaper. An authority supports inquiry when its action increases contact with what settles the slot. Patronage that buys an office but changes no reading is a prestige transfer, not support. The SEP entry on Galileo records the concrete forms: Jesuit confirmation of telescopic results, appointment to a chair *"with the help of Clavius and del Monte"*, naming the Jovian moons the *"Medicean"* stars to secure a court position, and a supportive pope who *"was elected ... Galileo felt empowered to begin work"*. Each is an introduction with a measurable downstream effect on what could be read.

Fig 11 :61 gives the asymmetry: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* An authority can add an actor; it cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult it. This is why support is always permanent work rather than a finished state (Fig 11 :63).

**Refuter:** an authority-supported program whose readings do not change, with no new instrument, no import from a differing closure, and no increase in ground contact after the support, measured prospectively. If that is the normal case, F2 is false.

### F3: Limit is the substitution of the authority for the ground

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 10 and Fig 7, low overlap: anchor, maintainer, room chain).**

> **"The anchor must not update."** (Fig 2 :33)
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."** (Fig 2 :34)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** (Fig 10 :49)
> **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)

The limit operation is not censorship as such. It is the installation of the authority in the anchor position: what the authority says becomes what settles the question, so the ground is no longer able to correct the frame. Fig 2 :34 names the failure exactly; once the anchor updates on what frames think, the arrangement becomes coherent drift with nobody noticing, because from inside it looks like agreement. Fig 10 :47 names the institutional version: a convention (what counts as a respectable answer, what counts as a heresy, what counts as a publishable result) that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and has become a verdict.

The Lysenko case is the cleanest world instance. Borinskaya, Ermolaev and Kolchinsky (2019, *Genetics*) record that at the August 1948 VASKhNIL session *"State authorities directly interfered with the scientific arguments of biologists and prohibited research, in this case in genetics"*, and that *"Geneticists had to pit scientific arguments against the political accusations that Lysenko and his adherents published in central newspapers"*. The party endorsement turned a contested claim into the thing that settled the contest. The same source records the repair when the ground was restored: after Lysenko's protection was withdrawn, *"events happened quickly"* and the Institute of Genetics was reconstituted.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which an authority's decree constrained inquiry while the ground's verdict remained unchanged, publicly correctable, and actually used to reverse the decree within the ordinary operation of the institution. If that is the normal case, F3 is false.

### F4: The same institution can do both, because the two operations bind differently

**Mode: `specified`. Domain union D2-and-D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 union rule and Fig 11 valence, low overlap).**

> **"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57, in the fuller form: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** (Fig 11 :66)

A church that founds universities and sponsors observatories can also proscribe a cosmology. These are not contradictions in its character; they are different bindings to different slots. The support binding is `state → structure → state` in effect terms: the authority supplies a structure (instrument, office, method) between an inquiring community and what it wants to read. The limit binding installs the authority itself at the anchor slot, so the structure that should mediate is replaced by a decree. Fig 11 :66 says the limit is a substitution of habitat, not a removal: the inquirers are still working, but on a field composed by the authority.

The BBC-derived summary in the public encyclopedia of the history of science puts the empirics plainly: the Papacy and Jesuits *"initially promoted the observations and studies of Galileo Galilei but the Roman Inquisition later forced him to recant his views and then sentenced him to house arrest."* Same institution, two operations, different slots, different decades.

**Refuter:** a documented institution whose support and limit are not separable into two operations with different instruments, such that every intervention changes contact and the ground simultaneously and no residue is left over. That would collapse the union into one condition.

### F5: Reputation at the anchor is the failure trigger

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 2, two distinct figures).**

> **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** (Fig 10 :93)
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"** (Fig 10 :59)
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."** (Fig 10 :60)
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** (Fig 2 :42)

When the institution that certifies a claim is also the institution whose standing depends on the claim, it has acquired a stake in the reading. The figures do not require a saint to occupy the seat; they require that the stake be declared. The Galileo SEP entry says the trial *"transpired in a fraught political context"*, that Galileo was *"a creature of the powerful Medici and a personal friend of Pope Urban VIII, connections that significantly modulated developments"*, and that the doctrine's status was *"cloudy"* because *"the Church’s first public pronouncement that Copernicanism is a heresy appears in Galileo’s condemnation."* That is an institution protecting a reading while claiming to be the ground that settles it.

**Refuter:** a documented case where an authority with a declared reputation stake produced a reversal of its own prior reading using only the ordinary ground instrument, with the stake recorded in the same document. If that is impossible in practice, F5 is a structural idealisation, not a description (Seat Map :54: *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."*).

### F6: Stated ignorance is load-bearing in the support claim

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` methodological consequence. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 6 and the Library of Babel essay, one framework evidence pool).**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** (Fig 9 :27-28)
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** (Fig 2 :42)

The shape this solution returns is *an operation that changes contact* and *an operation that substitutes the authority for the ground*. It does not return a list of which centuries were good and which were bad, and it does not name a single institution as the answer. *The Library of Babel* states the stop: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* The specific historical attributions are a `gap` here, not a silence: the archival instrument that would discriminate them is not held in this simulated run.

**Refuter:** an instrument, applied to the archive, that names which operation dominated in a given case and predicts a new case correctly. That would move part of this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

### F7: The question is a union and must be unbundled before a lever is chosen

**Mode: `specified`. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel bundle passage, one evidence pool).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 are quoted in section 1.3. The two conditions have different instruments:

- A support failure shows up as no new instrument, no import from a differing closure, a library that grows while reach does not (Fig 6 :21, Fig 7 :12).
- A limit failure shows up as a decree that settles a dispute, a convention that decides an outcome, a ground whose keeper has a reputation to protect (Fig 10 :47, :93; Fig 2 :34).

A single lever, for example "more funding", clears the region where only the support condition holds and leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :55). F7 is why the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores both conditions in all failure types with no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### F8: A support can change valence when the residual changes

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations versus composition structures).**

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** (Fig 12 :121)

An academy that begins as an importer of methods can become a room that hands itself the same closure. Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* And Fig 7 :42: *"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."* A journal, a society or a church office that once opened a field can later be the mechanism by which the field stops travelling. Nothing in its charter records which it has become.

**Refuter:** an institution whose method remains a regenerating catalyst across a documented change of residual and across two full generations of practitioners, with that persistence measured rather than asserted.

### F9: Two genuine conflicts with the world literature, and how they were resolved

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed.**

1. **The conflict thesis.** The popular framing treats science and authority as two fixed blocks. Fig 11 :50-51 says valence is relative to the residual and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual. A conflict between world framing and the figures sends the derivation back to the census; it never flips the verdict. Re-census R1 in section 7 resolves it: the two blocks are D2 and D3 prematurely merged, so the framing is a union reported as a partition. Verdict unchanged.
2. **The "one patron" reading.** Gordin's encyclopedia entry reports Krementsov's account that in the Soviet Union *"there was one and only one patron for science, the party-state itself"*, unlike the *"pluralist market for science in the developed capitalist states, where philanthropies, universities, and businesses all vied with the state to support particular sciences"*. Read carelessly, this says single patronage causes bad science. Fig 2 :31-32 makes independence a quantity, not a moral category: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Re-census R2 in section 7 resolves the strain: the figure governs the number of independent closures in contact with the inquiry, not the number of funders. A single patron that raises independent closures can still support; many patrons that share one evidence pool are one witness wearing many names. Verdict unchanged.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule in the Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. An authority's material support produces no change in ground contact: no new instrument, no import from a differing closure, no new reading (breaks F2, Fig 11 :40).
2. An authority's limit leaves the ground's verdict correctable and actually used to reverse the decree in the ordinary course of the institution (breaks F3, Fig 2 :33-34).
3. An authority's valence is invariant across a documented change of residual (breaks F1, Fig 11 :49-51).
4. Support and limit cannot be separated into two operations with different instruments (breaks F7, Fig 9 :57).
5. An institution's method stays a regenerating catalyst across two generations after its residual changed (breaks F8, Fig 12 :119).
6. Fig 2's anchor rule fails: a pushed anchor does not move and yet correction stops anyway, so the rule is not describing this domain (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a ground reading has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of institutions (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep the two operations separate in every report. A blended "authorities helped and hindered" sentence is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Ask at each handoff which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
4. Treat any list of "enlightened" or "repressive" institutions as Γ, that is, as a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27).
5. Use the history of science literature as one corroborating witness and as a budget filter, never as the verdict. The Lysenko sources share an evidence pool with each other; the Galileo sources share one with the church (Fig 2 :31, :40).
6. Declare the stake of every keeper who certifies a result (Fig 10 :60). An undeclared interest is modelled anyway.
7. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
8. Stop at the shape. Do not name which institution is the answer. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
