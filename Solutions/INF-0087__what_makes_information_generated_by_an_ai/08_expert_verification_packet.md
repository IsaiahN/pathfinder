# Expert verification packet: the Tether on opaque AI output

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the sub-claims, not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

What makes an opaque AI output trustworthy is not the legibility of its internal route. Trust is a property of contact between the claim and an unmoving anchor, checked by someone who cannot reconstruct the claim, corroborated by a frame with a different evidence pool, and recorded as a lineage so an error can be located. Legibility is an instrument for finding errors; it is neither necessary nor sufficient. Fluency, confidence and a clean audit trail each fail one of the four conditions. The framework returns this as a shape, not a name, and offers four refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2, 5 and 10

- **Statement:** trust is settled by an anchor that does not update, not by a frame-internal signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 5 :70 *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it."* Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **Refuter:** a claim never ranged against an independent anchor is as reliable as one that was, with only route legibility differing; or a frame-internal confidence signal predicts ground correctness as well as an external check with no answer leakage.

### C2 - soundness; checkable by a researcher who designs verification protocols

- **Statement:** a checker that can reconstruct the claim loses soundness. Opacity is compatible with sound checking, not a guarantee of it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43 *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 9 :51 *"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*
- **What to check:** does the verification protocol let the checker see only the claim and its ground reading, or does it let the checker rebuild the claim from the maker's internals? Does agreement rise while ground accuracy stays flat when reconstruction is allowed?
- **Refuter:** a verifier that can reproduce the claim from the model's internals is demonstrably sound with no independent ground access. That breaks Fig 10 :42 and :43.

### C3 - corroboration; checkable against any multi-reviewer or multi-model study

- **Statement:** agreement carries verdict weight only at low overlap; provenance makes an output auditable but does not make it true.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*; Fig 8 :50 *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*; Fig 13 :127 *"The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."*; Fig 10 :65 *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
- **Refuter:** agreement between frames with measurably shared evidence carries the same verdict weight as agreement between frames with no shared evidence; or two outputs with different lineages and identical ground contact are reliably separated by lineage alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that opacity is desirable, or that interpretable systems are untrustworthy. Opacity is roughly neutral once C1 to C3 hold.
- Not that reasoning traces are false, or that explanation research is useless. They are instruments for locating errors.
- Not that a clean lineage establishes truth. Legitimacy and rightness are different quantities.
- Not that a larger reviewer panel helps when the panel shares an evidence pool.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim about a particular model, vendor, benchmark or regulation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground experiment, not agreement. Four tests are specified in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4: legibility swap, reconstruction, independence, lineage. Predictions are stated there before any run. A result that fires any of the four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 retracts the corresponding claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :23 and :27, Fig 2 :29, :33, :35, :40, Fig 10 :42, :43, :65, Fig 13 :127. Nine short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own verification practice: does allowing the checker to reconstruct the claim change correctness, or only agreement?
3. Name one instrument that would separate ground contact from route legibility in a deployed system, or say why none exists.
4. Check C3 against a study you know: were the agreeing frames independent, or did they share a corpus?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
