## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity, symmetry, scale).** A checker holds an emitted claim and must decide whether to rely on it without reading the maker's route. Arity: two conditions, ground contact and route legibility, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Symmetry: trust is not symmetric, the checker must be outside the claim (Fig 10 :43). Scale: per claim; an aggregate trust score is exactly the average that hides the live slot (Fig 1 :22).
- **Domain 1, output and ground.** Actors: AI frame, claim, ground. Currency: residual R per claim, settled when the ground pays. Anchor: the ground, which must not update. Question type: which compositions reach settlement.
- **Domain 2, verification.** Actors: checker, maker, peer frames. Currency: admissible evidence on the residual; independence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. The checker is denied maker-only evidence (Fig 9 :51).
- **Domain 3, interpretability as instrument.** Actors: instruments reading internal states, the lineage record. Currency: legibility, description length. Anchor: none of its own; it is an instrument (Fig 2 :37), so it cannot be the domain that decides trust.
- **Order by dependency.** State what would count as the claim being wrong, then arrange checkers who cannot rebuild it, then sharpen instruments (Fig 3 :19). Do not read the inward instrument track against the ground track (Fig 6 :31).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 1 and Fig 10, I started from Fig 3's dependency order and Fig 12's bond distinction (legibility as `output → route`, trust as `output ⋛ ground`, different substances over shared atoms). Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :23/:27, Fig 2 :29/:33/:35/:40, Fig 10 :42/:43/:65, Fig 13 :127/:128.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` §6 and `09` VERDICT: trust is ground contact, not interpretability; legibility is an instrument; provenance is auditable, not true.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a hypothetical vendor claim, "our model is trustworthy because its chain of thought is readable and its audit log is complete."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **non-reconstructibility condition** (Fig 10 :42/:43), which a readable chain of thought violates;
- the **independence condition** (Fig 2 :40), which a single complete audit log from one maker does not supply;
- the **legitimacy versus rightness limit** (Fig 10 :65), which a complete log breaches by implication.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a claim must be ranged against something that does not move. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground experiment exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with an enumerated gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
