# Are interpretability and trust isomers? Structure, order and a falsifier

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question treats "internal reasoning is difficult to interpret" as the obstacle and "trustworthy information" as the goal, as though one were a degraded version of the other. Two derivations are owed: (1) are those two things the same substance arranged differently, or different substances, and (2) in what order do their checks have to run?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available without import: an **output** `phi`, a **checker**, a **ground**, a **route** through the maker's library, and a **lineage** record. Bonds held: `→` sequence (ordered), `⋛` comparison (ordered), `≡` identity (symmetric), `+` conjunction (symmetric), `¬` negation (unary, not a bond). No new primitive is needed for either derivation.

Question type: a partition for the substance question (which bond is being used), and a dependency chain for the order question (Fig 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Same atoms, different bond, different substance

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :96: **"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."**
- Interpretability arranges the atoms as `output → route → maker`: a sequence running backwards into the frame that produced the claim. Trust arranges them as `output ⋛ ground`: a comparison against something that does not move. Both use the output atom; one uses the ordered bond `→`, the other the ordered bond `⋛`.
- Under Fig 12 the arrangement is the substance. So they are **isomers**, not two readings of one quantity and not a continuum from less to more of the same thing. Improving one is not improving the other.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a route description that perfectly reconstructs the generating process also reliably predicts ground correctness with no independent check, the two substances collapse into one and the isomer claim fails.

### Q2 - The weaker, functional-group claim

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- If the claim is weakened to "both interpretability work and trust work are checks applied to an output", then they share a functional group: `output → some check`. The qualifier, which check (route or ground), decides the member. This is the defensible weaker form if Q1's stronger isomer claim is refused.
- Fig 12 :101: **"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."** A trust workflow that records ingredients (output, confidence, source name) without recording which bond joins them has recorded half of itself.
- Status: the isomer reading is triangulated for the figure definition, single-source for its application here. The falsifier in Q1 is unrun.

### Q3 - The order is dependency, not importance

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- The chain for this question is: (1) perception, can the checker see the claim at all; (2) vocabulary, does it have terms for what the claim affords; (3) objective, can it say what would count as the claim being wrong; (4) planning, a route to that objective under incomplete information; (5) learn and carry, name what worked and carry it to the next claim.
- Interpretability work is link 2 to 4 work: it enriches the vocabulary and can supply a route. Ground contact is link 3: it states the objective. A route that cannot say what would count as wrong is effort spent above the break. So the order is ground first, then route instrument, then corroboration, not the reverse.

### Q4 - A vocabulary with only routes cannot state the verdict

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
- Operators table: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."**
- A system that can only describe routes (the `→` bond) can say what the model did and what came next. It cannot state a **goal** (a comparison, `⋛`), and it cannot **refuse** a claim (negation, `¬`). A trust check that cannot refuse can only accumulate explanations. This is why a negative result is more informative than a positive one in the outreach packet.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a purely sequential trace vocabulary is shown to state a ground comparison and a refutation without adding `⋛` or `¬`, this derivation fails.

### Q5 - Analogues that correspond, and the one that does not

- The gemstone case in *How Intelligence Actually Works*: a projection that no instrument distinguishes from the thing is still a projection, *"which is why the lineage has to be kept even when the copy is perfect."* This is the provenance analogue, and it corresponds to F5.
- The spectrometer case in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading cannot comment on its source; a better instrument resolved it. This is the instrument analogue, and it corresponds to F6.
- The **non-correspondence**: a room full of agreeable reviewers is sometimes offered as an analogue of triangulation. It is not. Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** A room shares a pool; triangulation needs separate bearings on a fixed landmark. Recorded as a refused analogue, not smoothed into one.

## CONCLUSION

Interpretability and ground contact are **isomers** over the atoms output, checker, ground, route and lineage: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The weaker functional-group claim stands if the stronger one is refused. Their checks run in dependency order, ground objective first, route instrument second, corroboration third, because a link whose objective never arrives cannot be diagnosed. A vocabulary holding only route descriptions cannot state the comparison or the refusal that a trust check needs. No new primitive, no import and no naming of an anchor is required for any of this. Every claim remains `candidate` until a ground settles it.
