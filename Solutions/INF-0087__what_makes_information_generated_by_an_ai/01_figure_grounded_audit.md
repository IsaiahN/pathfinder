## 0. The residual in effect terms

> What makes information generated by an AI system trustworthy when its internal reasoning is difficult to interpret?

Restated as an effect: a checker (a person, a downstream system, an auditor) holds a claim `phi` emitted by an opaque AI frame and must decide whether to rely on it. `R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` per slot: the gap between what the library predicted and what the ground returns. Two things are being asked at once.

- **Lever G (ground contact):** does the claim range against something that settles it and does not move when the checker pushes on it?
- **Lever L (route legibility):** can the checker read the steps by which the system arrived at the claim?

"Internal reasoning difficult to interpret" is a fact about lever L. The question asks which lever confers trust. The figures answer: G, and L only as an instrument that can locate errors, never as the anchor.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** A trustworthy output is one for which (a) an anchor that does not update has been ranged against it, (b) the checker that ranged it could not itself have produced it, (c) at least one other frame with a different closure corroborates at low overlap, and (d) the lineage is recorded so an error is locatable. Anything that substitutes legibility for these four conditions is a filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60). This binding is written down before the run so it can accrue a residual and be refused like any other term.

**Question type.** A union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-56): a single lever that clears L leaves G standing. Unbundle before searching.

## 1. Domain census

Done before choosing any figure line. R by arity, symmetry and scale; then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

- **R.** Arity: two conditions (G and L), so a union, and it must be unbundled before search. Symmetry: trust is not symmetric between maker and checker, the checker must be outside the claim (Fig 10 :43). Scale: per claim, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22); a global a global one-place verdict of trustworthiness average throws away the only signal that says where to look.
- **Domain 1, output and ground.** Actors: the AI frame with library Gamma, the emitted claim, the ground. Currency: residual R per claim, settled only when the ground pays. Anchor: the ground, which must not move. This is the domain that decides trust.
- **Domain 2, verification.** Actors: checker, maker, independent peer frames. Currency: admissible evidence bearing on the residual; independence between frames. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. The verifier must be denied evidence bearing only on the maker (Fig 9 :51).
- **Domain 3, interpretability as instrument.** Actors: instruments that read internal states, the lineage record. Currency: legibility and description length. Anchor: none of its own. It is an instrument (Fig 2 :37), not the thing that settles. Without a nameable anchor this domain cannot decide trust; it can only extend what can be represented (Fig 6 :32).
- **Partition or union.** Domains 1 and 2 are a partition: trust is decided by ground contact and independent checking. Domain 3 is a different quantity bonded to the output, not a third way to decide (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** First establish a ground and say what would count as the claim being wrong (link 3, the objective). Then arrange independent checkers. Then, only if the first two exist, sharpen instruments. *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not"* (Fig 3 :19). Making reasoning legible while no ground exists spends effort above the break.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Trust is ground contact, not route legibility (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*

Fig 5 :70: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it."*

A claim becomes trustworthy by contact with an anchor that does not move when the claim or its maker is pushed on. Whether the route inside the generator can be read is a different property. If the system's own account of its reasoning is used as the metric, the frame scores itself with a quantity it also produces.

**Refuter:** show a case where a claim, never ranged against any independent anchor, remains reliable at the same rate as one that was, and where the only difference is the legibility of the generating route. That would break Fig 1 :23.

### F2 - The checker must not be able to reconstruct the claim (triangulated)

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

This is the finding that reverses the question's framing. "Internal reasoning difficult to interpret" is not a defect that trust must overcome. It is close to the zero-knowledge condition that makes a check sound: the checker must not be able to rebuild what it checks. What matters is not that the checker sees the route, but that it can range the output against a fixed landmark separately (Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

**Refuter:** demonstrate a verifier that can reproduce the claim from the model's internals and is nevertheless sound, with no independent ground access. That breaks Fig 10 :42 and :43.

### F3 - Confidence and internal coherence are frame-internal, disqualified as the metric (triangulated)

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*

Symbols table: R is *"The gap between prediction and observation,"* not the gap between the output and the system's account of the output.

A fluency or confidence signal, or a well-formed chain of reasoning, is produced by the same frame whose output is in question. It cannot be the anchor. The world sources corroborate from independent fields: Guo et al. report that modern networks are poorly calibrated, so reported confidence is not the true correctness likelihood; Adebayo et al. show some saliency explanations are independent of both model and data. Those two are separate witnesses from the ML literature, not the framework's author.

**Refuter:** an internal confidence measure that predicts ground correctness at the same rate as an external check, across a published held-out set, with no leakage of the answer into the confidence signal. That breaks Fig 1 :27.

### F4 - Agreement from a shared pool is disqualifying; independence is the currency (triangulated)

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Fig 8 :50: *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*

Many reviewers who share the model's training pool, or the same prompt, are one frame wearing many names. Corroboration requires low overlap: two instruments aimed at the same ground, or two frames that arrived separately. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A high aggregate trust score is a filter for where to look, not a witness.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that agreement between frames with measurably shared evidence carries the same verdict weight as agreement between frames with no shared evidence. That breaks Fig 2 :40.

### F5 - Provenance, not introspection, makes an output auditable (single-source for the figure claim, triangulated with the seat guide)

Fig 13 :127: *"The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."*

Fig 13 :128: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."*

Fig 13 :130: *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."*

Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

This is the strongest positive thing the figures say about opacity. When the route is not legible, what makes the output checkable is the recorded lineage: sources, tool calls, actions, stamps of origin and sequence. But the fifth quote is the limit: a clean lineage proves legitimacy, not truth. A source-discriminating instrument is still owed. The essays sharpen this with metamerism: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"* (*The Hard Problem is About Source*).

**Refuter:** two outputs with different lineages but identical ground contact, where the lineage alone reliably predicts which one is correct without any ground reading. That breaks Fig 10 :65.

### F6 - Interpretability is an inward instrument, not an anchor or an import (single-source, with a stated strain)

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

Fig 2 :37: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

Opening the black box is INWARD work: extend the instrument from one already returning something. It is not an import, because no new primitive crosses a wall; it is a finer reading of an existing frame. A report that no explanation was found is a statement about the current instrument, not a verdict about the claim. The reverse is also true: a beautifully legible route is a reading, and readings can be wrong on axes they do not measure. The Nature Machine Intelligence editorial puts this independently: reasoning tokens are *"not accurate explanations of LLMs' internal computations, raising challenges for monitorability."*

**Refuter:** an instrument built from a pure description of the internals, with no prior instrument returning anything, that reliably discriminates correct from incorrect outputs. That breaks Fig 6 :32.

### F7 - The question is a union compound; unbundle before search (single-source, structural)

Fig 9 :51: *"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*

Fig 9 :54-56 (described, not quoted): when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before searching again; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.

A program that improves only legibility clears the region where L alone binds and leaves G standing. The residue is smaller and categorically different: an opaque but grounded output is not a harder case of an ungrounded but legible one. They are different compounds and need separate loops.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that simultaneously increases route legibility and ground contact, measured on independent axes, with no mediation through one another. That would collapse the union and break this finding.

### F8 - A trust score is a specification, not a verdict (single-source, framework rule)

Seat Map :281: *"An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."*

`THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate."*

A number called "trustworthiness" that has not been settled by a ground is a specification of what to measure. Stating it as settled is the output-layer failure genus the seat guide names. This applies to this folder too: nothing here is `grounded`.

**Refuter:** a published operationalisation of trust whose value is settled by a named, unmoving anchor and whose mode can honestly be labelled `grounded`. That would not break this finding; it would instantiate it. What would break it is a settled score that needs no anchor at all.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. A claim never ranged against an independent, unmoving anchor is as reliable as one that was, with only route legibility differing (breaks F1, Fig 1 :23).
2. A verifier that can reconstruct the claim is demonstrably sound with no ground access (breaks F2, Fig 10 :42).
3. A frame-internal quantity (confidence, fluency, chain-of-thought coherence) predicts ground correctness as well as an external check, with no answer leakage (breaks F3, Fig 1 :27).
4. Agreement between frames with measurably shared evidence carries full verdict weight (breaks F4, Fig 2 :40).

Until one is run, every claim in this folder remains `candidate`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the residual before choosing a frame. Lead with: trust is a ground-contact question, not an interpretability question (F1, F2).
2. Ask what would count as the claim being wrong before showing the framework. That is the objective link in Fig 3, and it is the stage-1 question in `02_outreach_email.md`.
3. Offer the lineage of an output, not merely the output. Keep the limit visible: provenance does not settle truth (F5).
4. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. Two low-overlap frames beat ten reviewers who share a pool (F4).
5. Treat interpretability work as instrument work. It can make a residual visible; it cannot become the anchor (F6).
6. Label every number `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A trust score without a ground is a specification (F8).

Read after §§0 to 5, as the figures README requires: the quotes are the authority, not the summary, and a reading that makes two figures contradict has misidentified its domain.

- `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: *"They outrank everything else in the repo: the code, the rulings and the docs answer to them... the quotes are the authority, not this summary."* The framework reports *"a shape, not a name"* and stops where instruments cannot distinguish, recording the lineage.

### Retractions and confirmations of my own readings

| earlier reading in this audit | status after the canonical check |
|---|---|
| F2 first written as "opacity is the soundness condition" | **Corrected, not erased.** Opacity is not sufficient and does not confer trust. It only removes one failure mode (the checker joining the maker's frame). A system can be opaque and wrong. The surviving claim is the narrower one: a checker that can reconstruct the claim loses soundness; opacity is compatible with soundness, not a guarantee of it. |
| F5 first read as "provenance makes an output trustworthy" | **Retracted at that strength.** Fig 10 :65 says provenance shows legitimacy, never rightness. Provenance makes an output auditable; the ground still decides. Both statements are now kept. |
| F6 treated interpretability as if it might be an import | **Retracted.** Fig 6 says import adds an atom from a frame whose closure differs; reading internals more finely adds no atom. It is INWARD instrument work. |
| F3 assumed confidence and chain-of-thought coherence are the same quantity | **Split.** Confidence is a scalar produced by the frame; a reasoning trace is a route description. Both are frame-internal and both are disqualified as the metric, but they are different objects and need different instruments. |
| Fig 2 :37 versus Fig 1 :23 apparent strain | **Not strained after re-census.** Fig 2 :37 says human judgment is the right anchor when the question is constitutively about people, and only an instrument otherwise. "Is this answer good for a person" is a people question; "is this code correct" is not. Different question types, different domains. The verdict in F1 governs claims about the world, where the ground is the anchor. |
| "difficult to interpret" assumed to be a defect | **Reversed.** The figures make non-reconstructibility close to a soundness condition. The question's premise is not what the figures support. |

### Round-trip note

Condensing the figures into a one-line rule *"trust comes from ground contact, not interpretability"* is lossy. Fig 4 :30 requires the gap to be measured: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* What the shorthand drops is F2 (non-reconstructibility), F4 (independence), F5 (provenance is not truth) and F6 (instrument versus anchor). The honest form is a lossy condensation with an enumerated gap, recorded in `11_audit.md`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives and is stronger than the question's premise: trust is a property of an output's contact with an unmoving ground, checked by someone who cannot rebuild it, corroborated at low overlap, with lineage recorded and mode stated. Route legibility is an instrument, not an anchor, and is neither necessary nor sufficient for trust. Strains after re-census: none hidden; the Fig 2 :37 people-question case is recorded as a domain difference, not smoothed.
