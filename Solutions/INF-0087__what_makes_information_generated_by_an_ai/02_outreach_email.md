# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives a refuter you wrote before sending. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about opaque AI output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific, checkable claim I would like you to test against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. A one-line failure is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** When an AI system's internal reasoning is hard to interpret, what makes its information trustworthy is not how legible the reasoning is. It is four other things: the claim has been ranged against an anchor that does not move when anyone pushes on it; the person or instrument checking it could not have produced the claim itself; at least one independent check draws on a different evidence pool; and the lineage of the claim is recorded so an error can be located. Legibility is an instrument for finding errors, not the thing that settles truth.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. It says what an answer would have to look like; it does not name a particular system or score.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That a fluent chain of thought is trustworthy, that a high confidence number is a correctness probability, that a clean audit trail makes a claim true, or that many reviewers of the same output corroborate it. Those are frame-internal quantities or shared pools. I am not claiming this derivation is a ground reading; it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute it, written before sending this:**
> 1. a claim never ranged against any independent anchor is as reliable as one that was, with only route legibility differing;
> 2. a verifier that can reconstruct the claim is demonstrably sound with no ground access;
> 3. an internal confidence or coherence signal predicts ground correctness as well as an external check, with no answer leakage;
> 4. agreement between frames with measurably shared evidence carries full verdict weight.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. I wrote these down before sending.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures folder and formula (public): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks (independent field check): https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]

## Why this order

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Shape and refuter first, framework second | Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Do not ask for trust | Seat Map :171: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Asking for trust recruits a stakeholder, not a witness. |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Legibility withheld as the verdict | Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* |
| Provenance offered with its limit | Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right."* |
| Links only after stage 1 | Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Giving away a generator does not give away an answer. |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

## The two stages

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer three links. Their stage-1 verdict is never pooled as corroboration later (Fig 2 :40), and later agreement is not a second witness.

One-to-one only. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line in every message. Public institutional or opt-in channels only, per `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
