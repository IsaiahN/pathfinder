# The Tether's answer: trustworthy output with opaque reasoning

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the four checkable sub-claims, nothing `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An AI system emits a claim whose route from inputs and library to output a checker cannot read. The question asks what makes that claim trustworthy. It bundles two conditions that need separate loops: contact of the claim with a ground (G), and legibility of the route (L). A single lever that clears L leaves G standing, so this is a union compound (Fig 9 :54-56), not one hard question.

## 2. The shape

1. **Trust is ground contact, not legibility.** Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A claim is trustworthy to the degree that an unmoving anchor has been ranged against it. How readable the internal chain is does not enter that quantity.
2. **The checker must not be able to rebuild the claim.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."* The question's premise is therefore reversed: opacity is not the defect; non-reconstructibility is close to the condition that makes a check sound. What is needed is a fixed landmark, not a window into the maker (Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).
3. **Self-reports are frame-internal and disqualified.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Confidence, fluency and a well-formed chain of thought are outputs of the same frame. The independent literature agrees: calibration is often poor (Guo et al.) and some saliency explanations are independent of the model (Adebayo et al.).
4. **Corroboration needs low overlap, not numbers.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Ten reviewers who share the model's training pool are one frame with ten names. Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
5. **Lineage makes an output auditable, not true.** Fig 13 :127: *"The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."* Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."* Record sources, tool calls, actions and stamps of origin. Keep the limit visible.
6. **Interpretability is an instrument, not an anchor.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 2 :37 allows that instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Opening the box is INWARD work: it can make a residual visible, then it must be ranged against the ground. It never substitutes for the ground.
7. **Mode discipline.** Seat Map :281: *"An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."* A number called trustworthiness that has not been settled by a ground is a specification. Every claim above stays `candidate`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that opacity is good, or that interpretable systems are untrustworthy. Opacity is neither necessary nor sufficient; it is roughly neutral once the four conditions hold.
- Not that a reasoning trace is false. A trace can be a useful instrument for finding errors. The claim is only that it cannot be the anchor.
- Not that an audit trail settles truth. Legitimacy and rightness are different quantities.
- Not that more reviewers help. Reviewers who share an evidence pool add spelling, not evidence (Fig 6 :53-54).
- Not `grounded`. No experiment here measures the trust of any real system.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An experiment, not a further argument, with an unmoving anchor and independent checkers:

1. **Legibility swap test.** Hold ground contact fixed and vary only route legibility, with checkers who cannot reproduce the claim. Prediction: no reliable change in ground-contact accuracy.
2. **Reconstruction test.** Compare checkers who can reconstruct the claim with those who cannot, both with the same ground. Prediction: the reconstruction group's agreement rises while its ground accuracy does not.
3. **Independence test.** Compare agreement among frames with measured shared evidence against agreement among frames with separate evidence. Prediction: only the latter carries verdict weight.
4. **Lineage test.** Two outputs with different lineages and identical ground contact. Prediction: lineage alone does not reliably pick the correct one.
5. Standing refuters from `01` §4: any of the four that fires retracts the corresponding finding.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure text gives the shape but no instrument that settles trust in a deployed system. *They're Scanning Books* warns that *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* and *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* A trust metric is such a choice. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* This derivation cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> When an AI system's reasoning is hard to read, the thing that makes its output trustworthy is not how readable the reasoning is. The figures say trust is ground contact: the claim must have been ranged against something that does not move when anyone pushes on it, by a checker who could not have produced the claim, with corroboration from a frame drawing on different evidence, and with the lineage recorded so an error can be found. A confident tone, a fluent chain of thought and a clean audit trail each fail one of those tests, and a crowd of reviewers who share the model's training pool is one opinion said many times. Readable reasoning is an instrument for finding errors, not the thing that settles truth. What this offers is the shape any checkable answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the experiments that would close it. The claim is `specified`, not `grounded`, and this derivation is a simulation, not a measurement.
