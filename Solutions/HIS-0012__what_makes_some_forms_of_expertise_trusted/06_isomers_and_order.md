# Isomers and order: trusted versus rejected expertise over a fixed atom set

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the order claim, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question's residual is the flip in trust status of a form of expertise across periods. Two questions are owed for the supporting derivation: (1) when the atoms are held fixed, what kind of relation holds between the trusted arrangement and the rejected arrangement, and (2) what is the dependency order among the conditions, so that a lever is not applied to a link whose input never arrives.

Atoms held, as figure terms applied to a historical domain: claim, instrument, observation, practitioner, institution, text, credential (states in the broad sense of what a system is at a moment), plus method, convention, channel and relation (structures in the broad sense of how quantities are arranged). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without an import that needs a source gate (Fig 6 :12-14, Fig 8 :51-52).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are trusted and rejected expertise isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- The trusted arrangement in period P1: practitioner bonded to ground through a discriminating instrument, institution bonded to question through a recomposed room, credential bonded to renewed contact.
- The rejected arrangement in period P2: the same atoms, with the instrument-to-ground bond replaced by a convention-to-agreement bond, the room bonded to the old question, and the credential bonded to a standing that survives the outcome (Fig 10 :61).
- Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour in the world (accepted versus refused). By Fig 12 :117 that is an **isomer**, not a different substance in the ingredient sense.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either arrangement requires an atom absent from the other, the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import or subtraction (Fig 6 :23). Specifically, if the rejected period requires a new instrument or a new institutional form that was not in the trusted period's set, the atom set changed and isomerism is the wrong description.

**Answer:** yes, conditionally on a fixed atom set. The condition is part of the claim, not a footnote.

### Q2 - Are they a functional group instead?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- "Expertise" is the functional group: a claim-producing frame with a method that can be checked. The qualifier (trusted, rejected, drifted) is the member, fixed by the bond to the ground and the room.
- So there are two true descriptions at different resolutions: over a fixed atom set the two arrangements are isomers; as members of the expertise functional group they differ only in the qualifier. Fig 12 :124's `The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.` is the finer reading when the atom set is not fixed across periods.
- **Falsifier:** show a case where the qualifier is fixed by the atom list rather than by the bond (for example, a form of expertise that is trusted in every period in which it exists, regardless of ground contact). That would break the functional-group reading.

### Q3 - Credential as catalyst or spent part

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :107-110: **"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."** Where the arrangement is not recorded, `the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again.
- A credential that continues to pay because its holder keeps returning to the ground is a catalyst: it survives its own use. A credential that paid once and is replayed as a standing is a spent part: it looks identical in the ingredient list, and Fig 12 :119 says nothing records which it is.
- The historian's discriminator (needed in F11 of `01`) is exactly the missing record that would distinguish catalyst from spent part. Until that record exists, a period's prestige is a compressed description that cannot comment on its source.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a credential whose continued payment at the ground is independent of renewed contact under a repeated measurement. Then the catalyst/spent-part distinction does not govern this case.

### Q4 - What is the dependency order, and which link actually breaks?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Ordered chain for this question:
  1. **Perception:** is there a fact here, and is the residual indexed per slot? (Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 1 :19)
  2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what an expertise affords, what it can settle, what it can be refused by? (Fig 3 :7)
  3. **Objective:** can the checking community say what would count as a change in trust status in its own terms? (Fig 3 :10)
  4. **Planning:** can a room be assembled from the residual it surfaced? (Fig 7 :40)
  5. **Learn and carry:** can the method cross periods as a method, not as a recording of who was believed? (Fig 4 :22-25)
- Fig 3 :25-27: **"The middle link is where chains usually break."** Having terms for what a situation affords `was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar.` For expertise debates the middle link is the objective: most literature can name the actors (experts, credentials, institutions) but has no instrument for what would count as a change in the *groundedness* of trust. So reform effort lands above the break, on credentials and reviewer lists, and cannot use the missing objective.
- Fig 3 :22-23: **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."** A claim that "the room was biased" is a reading below the break if no one has first said what the room would have had to detect.
- **Falsifier:** show an expertise debate with a working instrument for grounded versus conventional trust, in which reform still fails for lack of credentials or reviewer diversity. That would move the break and change the recommended lever.

### Q5 - Two currencies: describing the flip is not the same as testing it

- Fig 12 :125-127: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**
- A historian can describe the four-condition union cheaply in bits. Testing which condition bound in a particular case may cost an archive year, a replication, or an instrument. Fig 12 :129-130: **"A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."** Neither figure predicts the other, so both must be read.
- Consequence for distribution: the separator is cheap to state and therefore easy to over-claim. Its mode must be stated and its refuter attached before it is sent.

### Q6 - Order of operations inside one loop (Formula step 7)

- **SEARCHED first:** is the named-expertise list already in the historical literature and merely unfound? The answer is yes, and the framework declines to return it because Fig 9 :32 says it returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
- **INWARD second:** sharpen the separator in our own terms. This is `01` sections 3 and 4 and the discriminator in F11.
- **OUTWARD third:** import a case-specific atom (a particular archive, instrument or institution) from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and shadow test. Debit it against independence (Fig 6 :24).
- Fig 6 :67-68: `INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed; never read one against the other.` A historian who keeps sharpening the separator instead of importing the archive is refining forever on an axis that cannot carry the answer. A historian who imports the archive before describing the residual will find that any frame seems to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, a trusted expertise and the same expertise rejected are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117): same atoms, different bond, different substance. More generally, "expertise" is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123) and the trust status is the qualifier. A credential is a catalyst only when it survives its own use; otherwise it is a spent part that looks identical in the ingredient list. The dependency chain breaks at the objective link, where a term for what would count as grounded trust is missing, so reform above the break cannot arrive. Description in bits and settlement in actions are two currencies and do not add. The falsifiers above are written before the run and are repeated in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.
