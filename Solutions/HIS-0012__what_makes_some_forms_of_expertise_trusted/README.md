# Solution HIS-0012: what makes some forms of expertise trusted in one period and rejected in another

**Question:** What makes some forms of expertise trusted in one historical period and rejected in another?

**Source:** `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 17

**Section:** 2. Trust, authority, and expertise

**Field for public contact points:** history and sociology of expertise research (history of science, science and technology studies, sociology of scientific knowledge)

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. Every substantive file states that this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the historical question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** done

## The question restated as an effect

A form of expertise E is trusted in period P1 and rejected in period P2. The question asks what changed. Two readings are possible. Reading A: the change is a property of E itself, so a standing list of trustworthy and untrustworthy expertise forms could in principle be written. Reading B: the change is a relation between E and (i) a ground that does not update on what anyone thinks, (ii) the independence of the frames checking it, (iii) the composition of the room that settles it, and (iv) the habitat of actors and conditions that give the expertise its valence. Reading A leads to a credential list. Reading B leads to a discriminator and a refuter. The figures decide B (Fig 11 :51: `No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.`).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a **separator** that returns, per expertise-domain and per period, whether trust was grounded, conventional, or drifted, and what would have to be true for the verdict to differ. A universal list of trusted expertise forms is explicitly not the sought object.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named historians or sociologists, and no scraped contact lists.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan. Selection for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40, Seat Map :163 as carried in the exemplar). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## Authority order used here

The 13 figures and the Operators and Symbols tables are primary and decide. `THE_FORMULA.md` supplies the mode rule and the eight-step loop. World sources (Collins and Evans 2002, Kuhn via the Stanford Encyclopedia, the Columbia Accident Investigation Board, Chimento et al. 2024, Thompson 2021, Kumkale and Albarracin 2004) are secondary corroboration only; none overrules a figure, and a conflict sends the derivation back to the census. Every quoted figure string was checked character by character against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and cited as `Fig N :line`.
