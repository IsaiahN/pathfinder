# Expert verification packet: the Tether on trust in expertise

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or sociologist of expertise can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No historical measurement was taken. This is a shape and a separator, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A form of expertise is not trusted because it is intrinsically credible. Credibility accrued is a frame-internal quantity and cannot settle a claim (Fig 1 :26-27). Trust is legitimate only when a claim-producing frame ranges against a ground that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29). It becomes conventional or drifted when the reference is the community's own agreement (Fig 2 :35). A form of expertise loses trust when the expert room stops following the question (Fig 10 :71-72), when the channel to the ground decays while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24), when checking frames share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40), or when the habitat changes the valence of the same actors (Fig 11 :49-51). The four conditions form a union, so verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Over a fixed atom set the trusted and rejected arrangements are isomers (Fig 12 :117). The framework returns a separator, not a list of named expertise forms.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** credibility accrued cannot be the ground of a verdict; a non-updating anchor is required.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :26-27: *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"* and *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **What to check:** is the anchor definition satisfied in the case you know? Name the thing that did not move when the community believed the expertise. If there is none, the trust was not triangulated.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a credential or reputation that changes the truth status of a claim by itself, with no bearing on a ground, under a repeated measurement. Also: show a case where trust was settled by agreement among frames known to share one evidence pool, with no separate anchor, and where the later rejection tracked the world rather than a change of personnel.

### C2 - history-facing; checkable against a case the reader knows

- **Statement:** when the question changes and the expert room does not, a former verdict becomes a convention; when the channel decays while the ground stays fixed, the same flip occurs for a different reason.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :71-72: *"The room composition is a function of the question."* and *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** in a flip you know well, did the problem list, instruments or standards change? Did the set of gatekeepers change with them? Was there an instrument that stopped discriminating, a metric that saturated, or readings that were pooled? These are different discriminators and they predict different archival traces.
- **Refuter:** find a period where the problem list, instruments and standards changed, the expert room did not, and the room still produced ground-tracked verdicts at the same rate as a recomposed room. Or show that all four conditions collapse into one measured variable, with that variable named and measured.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, with a historical analogue

- **Statement:** over a fixed atom set the trusted and rejected arrangements are isomers; the same actors and instruments are differently bonded, and the trust status is the qualifier of a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** is the atom set actually fixed across the two periods? If a new instrument, institution or actor was required, the relation is import (Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*), not isomerism.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a standing list of expertise forms whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual, with the residual stated in effect terms. That breaks Fig 11 :51 and the isomer reading with it.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of trusted and rejected expertise forms, and not a date for the next flip (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not "credentials are irrelevant." They are a budget filter (Fig 9 :60) and may be catalysts when they survive their own use (Fig 12 :119).
- Not "all trust is drift." Triangulation is possible (Fig 2 :35).
- Not a single cause. The conditions form a union (Fig 9 :54-57); one lever leaves the intersections standing.
- Not that Kuhnian changing standards contradict a non-updating anchor. Different objects; see `01` section 8.
- Not `grounded`. This is `specified` per domain and `general` on the shape, a simulation checked for internal consistency.

## 6. What would settle it

A discriminator, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green `cannot comment on the source`; the case was settled `not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument.` Here:

1. **Anchor test:** name the thing that did not move when the community believed the expertise. If none exists, the trust was not triangulated.
2. **Room test:** did the problem list, instruments or standards change while the gatekeepers stayed fixed?
3. **Channel test:** did an instrument stop discriminating, a metric saturate, or readings get pooled, while the ground stayed fixed?
4. **Import test:** did the new standing require an atom absent from the old period? If yes, record provenance and run the shadow test before calling it an isomer flip.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read the three quoted passages in C1 and the two in C2 (five short passages).
2. Pick one flip you know and apply the four tests in section 6. Name which one separates the grounded reading from the conventional reading.
3. Try R1 from `01` section 5: can you produce a standing list of expertise forms whose valence did not change with the residual? If yes, the claim is refuted.
4. Send the failure or the discriminator. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
