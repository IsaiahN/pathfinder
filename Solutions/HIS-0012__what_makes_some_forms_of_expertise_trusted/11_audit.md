## 1. Independent domain census (written from the residual line alone)

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity, symmetry, scale).** The residual is the variation in trust status of a claim-producing frame across periods. Arity: four continuous conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54: `And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.`), not a two-item partition and not one dial. Symmetry: trust is not symmetric with correctness; Fig 2 :39-41 gives agreement a valence that flips depending on whether the agreeing frames share a pool. Scale: one practitioner is a slot (Fig 1 :19), a period is a room, and the cross-period handoff is a chain.
- **Domain 1, claim and ground.** Actors: claim, instrument, observation, ground. Currency: residual and settlement. Anchor: a referent that does not change its answer when a frame thinks differently. Question type: a partition boundary, since a frame either ranges against such a referent or ranges against other frames.
- **Domain 2, credibility.** Actors: credentials, reputation, affiliation. Currency: credibility accrued. Anchor: none. Not yet a verdict domain; only a question about where the hearing budget goes.
- **Domain 3, room and institution.** Actors: gatekeepers, journals, academies. Currency: agreement and conventions. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: partition by question-match, with union where several conditions bind.
- **Domain 4, habitat and valence.** Actors: practitioners, instruments, conditions, relations. Currency: contact, introduction, capability. Anchor: the ground settles; the habitat updates. Union.
- **Domain 5, transmission.** Actors: master, apprentice, public, text. Currency: contact and correction. Anchor: a checkable effect description. Union of context, subtext and pretext.

Order by dependency: establish Domain 1 before enumerating Domain 4; draw Domain 3's room from the residual surfaced in Domain 4; transmit Domain 5 last; hold Domain 2 outside as a firewall. SEARCHED first, INWARD second, OUTWARD third (Formula step 7).

**Comparison with the primary census.** My five domains match the primary's five. I reached them from different starting figures: the primary began at Fig 11 :51 (no standing list) and Fig 10 :71-72 (room follows question), while I began at Fig 9 :54 (union) and Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual). Different starting figures, same boundaries: low overlap between the two routes within the framework, so the agreement is weak but not circular in its starting move. It is still one evidence pool. The one difference in emphasis is that I treat Domain 5 as a genuine fifth domain rather than a transmission consequence; the primary does the same in its table, so there is no substantive disagreement.

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :26-27 (frame-internal credibility), Fig 2 :29/:35/:40 (anchor, mirror, shared pool), Fig 10 :71-72 (room follows question), Fig 11 :49-51 (valence and no standing list), Fig 12 :117 (isomer).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried across the folder: expertise is a relation, not a property; four overlapping conditions; a separator instead of a list; isomers over a fixed atom set.
3. **Concretization (T_E):** the checkable historical claim: a specific specialty lost trust because one of the four conditions bound in its period, with a discriminator that would show which one.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **The identity of the ground in a given case.** The shorthand says "a non-updating anchor"; the historical case requires an instrument, record or outcome that actually stayed fixed. Fig 2 :29 says an anchor is whatever settles the question, so naming which thing did so is a binding that needs evidence.
- **Which of the four conditions bound.** The union reading forbids averaging, but the shorthand does not say how to assign a case to one condition or to an intersection. That is the discriminator owed in F11.
- **The fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim.** The shorthand says "same actors, different bond"; a case may require a new instrument, which turns the relation into import (Fig 6 :23) and needs a source gate and shadow test.
- **The distinction between catalyst and spent part.** Fig 12 :119 says nothing records which a credential is. The shorthand's "credentials are a budget filter" does not carry the record that would decide a particular credential.

Conversely, several things survive cleanly: no standing list of expertise forms; credibility accrued cannot settle a claim; a room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; valence is relative to the residual. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no historical measurement was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is a condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
