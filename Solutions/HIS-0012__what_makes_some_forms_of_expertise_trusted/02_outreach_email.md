# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four per-domain verdicts in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are stated plainly and the refuter is attached. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why expertise loses trust, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about the history and sociology of expertise. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the common answer to "why was this expertise trusted then and rejected now" names a property of the expertise: better evidence, better credentials, better methods. The figures refuse that form. Fig 11 :51 says `No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.` Fig 11 :49 says `An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries.` So the thing that flips is not the expertise. It is the relation between the expertise and four conditions: a ground that does not move when people admire it, the independence of the people checking it, the composition of the expert room, and the habitat of actors and instruments around the claim.
>
> Three consequences follow, and they are the part I want checked:
>
> 1. Credibility accrued cannot be the explanation. Fig 1 :26-27 lists it among frame-internal quantities and adds `They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.` Credentials can fund a hearing. They cannot settle a claim.
> 2. A period's expert room is a function of the question. Fig 10 :71-72 says `When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom.` A trusted expertise becomes a convention when the room keeps the old correlation after the question has moved.
> 3. The ground does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24). A specialty can lose trust because its instrument stopped discriminating, not because the world changed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this produces a list of which expertise will lose trust next. Fig 9 :32 says `you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself.` It produces a separator: per case and per period, was the trust grounded, conventional, or drifted, and what would change that verdict. The four verdicts are kept separate, because the question is a union of overlapping conditions, not one dial (Fig 9 :54-57).
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. A standing list of expertise forms whose valence stays the same across a documented change of residual, with the residual stated in effect terms.
> 2. A case where trust was settled by agreement among people known to share one evidence pool, with no separate bearing on a non-updating anchor, and where the later rejection tracked the world rather than a change of personnel.
> 3. A period where the problem list and instruments changed, the expert room did not, and the room still tracked the ground as well as a recomposed room.
> 4. A credential that changes the truth status of a claim by itself, under a repeated measurement with a named ground.
>
> If any of these holds, I retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I would also value one case from your own reading that the separator handles badly.
>
> - Figure text and line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Collins and Evans, `The Third Wave of Science Studies: Studies of Expertise and Experience` (2002): https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/78065/
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, `The Incommensurability of Scientific Theories`: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/incommensurability/
> - Columbia Accident Investigation Board, section 8.2, normalization of deviance: http://reportsthatmatter.org/reports/columbia-accident/82-failures-of-foresight-two-decision-histories-and-the-normalization-of-devianc
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the preference has to become an ordinary term first. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output, then refuter, then theory | Fig 10 :52: `Bring a characterised residual, never the source.` The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: `Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.` |
| Pre-registered refuter before the conclusion | The seat-reasoning rule carried from the exemplar: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: `Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which.` |
| Credibility withheld as a witness | Fig 1 :26-27 (frame-internal) and Fig 9 :60 (filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict). |
| No list of expertise forms | Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence. |
| Verdicts kept separate | Fig 9 :54-57, union not partition. Averaging would erase the finding. |
| Links only after the verdict is recorded | Fig 4 :22-25: methods and head starts cross, recordings do not. A reader who has absorbed the theory before judging the output has joined the frame being checked. |
| No request for trust or prestige | The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: `Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it.` |
| Offer the formula as a method, with its own refuter rule | Fig 4 :22 and Formula step "Direction": a frame cannot check what it is looking for unless the sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term. |
