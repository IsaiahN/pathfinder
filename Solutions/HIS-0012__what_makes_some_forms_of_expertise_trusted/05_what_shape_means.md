## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The *Library of Babel* essay (opened) gives the canonical sentence: `You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question.` It also gives the mechanism: `With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary.`

For HIS-0012 the question asks for the cause of a flip in trust. A **shape** answer says what any cause must look like: it must change contact with a ground, or change the independence of checking frames, or change the room relative to the question, or change the habitat's valence. It does not say which expertise flips next or when. `The separator, not the list` is the shape. `Phrenology lost trust in the 1840s because of X` is a name with a date, and is a binding from the historical record, not a figure output.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrap vocabulary by proximity. Use the terms you already have that are closest to the thing, then recurse on the new terms that overlap with them. The essay's warning applies here: a researcher who first reads the list of candidate expertise forms and then searches for a cause will find a cause that fits whichever form they started with.
- Fig 6 :30: `The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms.` Fig 9 :30-31: `your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause.` So "trust" is not taken as an ordinary object. It is restated as the effect: a claim-producing frame is accepted or refused by a checking community in a given period.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened): `When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world.` A historian checking this account needs an effect description, for example: "if room composition is the binding condition, then a change in the problem list without a change in reviewers should leave the old expertise’s standing intact longer than a recomposed room would."
- Fig 9 :27-28: `describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit.` In this question the tempting frames are prestige, credentialism, institutional capture and paradigm shift. Picking one before describing the residual is the named inversion.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things only, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: `the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground.` For HIS-0012, a discriminator in the historical record that distinguishes a ground-tracked flip from a convention-tracked flip would settle a case.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Collins and Evans's contributory/interactional distinction is such an import from the sociology of expertise; it arrives with its own venue and date and is recorded as such.

The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay (opened) gives the discriminator requirement in instrument terms: `An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it.` It says the color case `was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument.` Historical disputes about why an expertise lost trust are usually argument without a discriminator; that is the honest `unreached` state, not an answer.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (carried from the exemplar mapping) sharpens the import point: `The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space.` Importing a new institutional actor into the account is only an import if it adds a dimension, not a synonym for "authority".

## 4. Why the ordinary answer is the specific forbidden error

The ordinary answer is "expertise is trusted because it has accumulated credibility and credentials." Fig 1 :26-27 names `credibility accrued` as frame-internal and adds `They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.` That is exactly a field scoring itself with a quantity the field produces.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened) gives the mechanism and the refusal: `Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly.` and `Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige.` A credential can get an expert a hearing. It cannot make the expert right, and it cannot explain why a period stopped listening except by describing the period's own self-image.

Fig 10 :61: `And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on.` The credential holder has a stake in the old reading. Fig 10 :59: `an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying.` So the ordinary answer is not merely shallow; it is structurally disqualified as a verdict.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a historical expertise or a condition?

**Answered. No, with a distinction.** The README's phrase is `what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)`. The subject is a missing primitive, one not held in the library. In this solution the conditions named (ground, independence, room, habitat) are not new primitives invented for the occasion: they are figure terms already held, applied to the historical domain. Naming them is a **binding** from the historical frame onto held figure atoms. Naming a particular expertise (phrenology) or a particular century is likewise a binding from the historical record. What is forbidden is claiming that the framework derived the name, or that the binding moves the wall. Fig 6 :23: `No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs.` Fig 6 :25: `An import adds no atom to the world.`

**Correction to my own first pass.** I initially wrote that the framework forbids naming any condition in a historical explanation. That overstates the rule. The rule governs missing primitives; it does not govern selecting among held atoms or binding an external case. The corrected statement is kept here and in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 8 rather than erasing the first reading. Triangulated (README + Fig 6 + Fig 13).

## 6. The source/provenance consequence for this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened) gives metamerism: an instrument reads green and `cannot comment on the source`; yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK or an undiscovered scheme can all produce the same reading. The essay concludes that several paths to one behavior make behavior `a projection with lossy information`. Applied here: "the experts were trusted" is a reading that does not determine its source. It could be ground contact, shared pool drift, a room matched to the question, a habitat that made the expertise useful, or several at once. Fig 8 :51-52: `Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them.` What is owed is a discriminator, not a preferred narrative.

## 7. Consequences for outreach and for what this output can claim

**Prestige is not action.** Already quoted in section 4. A campaign that gathers prestigious historians to agree with the separator is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try it: one checkable output, a refuter, and a request for a failure.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened) says LLMs `can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output` but `the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself`. So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the historical claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

**Mode of the shape itself.** `general` on the structural claim (any trust flip must pass through one or more of the four conditions), `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, never `grounded`. Accepted would mean the ground settled it; no historical measurement was taken here, so the status is `candidate`.
