## 0. The residual in effect terms

A form of expertise E is trusted in period P1 and rejected in period P2. The residual R is the **variation in trust status of a claim-producing frame across periods and social worlds**. It is not the truth or falsity of E's claims; that is a different residual with a different anchor.

- **Arity.** At least four conditions are in contact with R: ground contact, independence of checking frames, room composition, and habitat. There may be more. Fig 9 :54 says a union must be unbundled before searching: `And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.`
- **Symmetry.** Trust is not symmetric with correctness. Fig 2 :39-41 gives the asymmetry: `Correlation has valence.` Agreement is `disqualifying for a verdict` when frames share an evidence pool, yet `efficient for a convention` when agreement about a shared meaning is the product. So a trusted expertise can be a convention, and a rejected expertise can be right.
- **Scale.** The individual practitioner is one slot (Fig 1 :19: `The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.`). The room is the period-level checking arrangement. The handoff between periods is the chain (Fig 7). A global claim such as "expertise was trusted in the nineteenth century" aggregates away the only signal that says where to look.

**What an answer would look like, bound before the run (Formula step "Direction"):** a separator that returns, per expertise-domain and per period, whether the trust was grounded, conventional, or drifted, plus the instrument that would change that verdict. A standing list of trusted expertise forms is explicitly not the object sought. Binding this in advance makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else (Fig 5 :65-66: `make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else.`).

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

### F1 - Trust is triangulation or drift, never negotiation (governs D1 and D3)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :45:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

An expertise is trusted for a good reason when its claim is corrected by something that does not move when the community admires it: a measurement, an outcome, a board that pays or does not. It is trusted for a bad reason when the reference is the community's own agreement. Fig 2 :33-34 names the failure: `If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices.` That is the figure-level account of an expertise that is trusted throughout a period and then suddenly rejected: the drift was invisible from inside because everyone was using the same moving reference.

**Refuter:** show a historical case in which trust was settled by agreement among frames that demonstrably shared one evidence pool, with no separate bearing on a non-updating anchor, and where the later rejection tracked a fact of the world rather than a change of personnel. If that case exists, F1 is narrowed to a requirement on descriptions, not on verdicts.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor, mirror, ranging), Fig 8 :48-49 (`Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names.`) and Fig 10 :24 (`the ground does not decay, the channel does`). Low overlap between the alignment figure and the habitat figure.

### F2 - Credibility accrued is frame-internal; prestige can fund a trial but cannot settle it (governs D2)

Fig 1 :26-27:

> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

This is the single most decisive line for the question as usually asked. The ordinary answer, "expertise is trusted because it has accumulated credibility, credentials, and institutional endorsement," names a quantity the field produces about itself. By Fig 1 that quantity cannot be the metric. Fig 9 :60: `Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.` Credibility determines whether a claim gets a hearing (a budget), not whether it is right (a verdict).

The essay opens 2026-11-23 makes the same separation in different vocabulary: `Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly.` and `Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige.`

Fig 10 :61 adds the historical sting: `And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on.` A reputation earned in P1 is a stake in P2, so the person or institution holding it has an interest in the old reading remaining authoritative. Fig 10 :59: `an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying.` The stake must be declared, not used as evidence.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where a credential or reputation, by itself and with no bearing on a ground, changed the truth status of an expertise claim rather than only its funding or hearing. None was found. If one is produced, F2 fails.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 (frame-internal), Fig 9 :60 (filters vs witnesses), Fig 10 :61 (standing is a stake) and the low-overlap essay (prestige/action separation). The essay shares an author with the framework, so its corroboration is weak; the figure line stands alone if the essay is discounted.

### F3 - A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure (governs D3, decisive for period change)

Fig 10 :71-72:

> **"The room composition is a function of the question."**
> **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

This is the figure that answers the question in effect terms. A historical period is a room: a set of institutions, journals, instruments and credentialing paths that settles which claims count. When the question changes (a new instrument, a new anomaly, a new problem list) the room must be recomposed from the residual it surfaced. If it is not, the same expertise that was trusted because it answered the old question is now rejected, not because the expertise changed but because the room kept the correlation and lost the question.

Fig 7 :36: `A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced.` Fig 7 :35: `A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes.` So a period's consensus is variation inside a room; the rejection in the next period is the ground voting through a different room. Fig 10 :47: `a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.` When a credentialing convention starts determining which claims are true, it has become a verdict and is refused.

World corroboration with low overlap: the Stanford Encyclopedia entry on incommensurability reports that Kuhn found `proponents of incommensurable theories have different conceptions of their discipline and different views about what counts as good science; and that these differences arise because of changes in the list of problems that a theory must resolve and a corresponding change in the standards for the admissibility of proposed solutions`. That is the room-follows-question claim stated from the history of science. The entry also notes that Newton's theory `was initially widely rejected because it did not explain the attractive forces between matter`, an instance of trust status flipping with the problem list.

**Refuter:** find a period in which the problem list, instruments and standards changed, the expert room did not change, and the room still produced ground-tracked verdicts at the same rate as a recomposed room. If that holds, F3's dependency claim is wrong.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 7 and the low-overlap Kuhn source. The overlap between the figures and Kuhn is conceptual, not textual; counted as one weak corroborator, not two witnesses.

### F4 - No standing list of good expertise survives a change of residual (governs D4)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

This refuses Reading A. There is no intrinsic "trustworthy expertise" that keeps its standing across periods, because valence is a relation to the residual. Fig 11 :39: `capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone.` Fig 11 :40: `An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves.` A form of expertise that sharpens its internal consistency without changing its contact with the ground does not become more trustworthy; it becomes more eloquent.

Fig 11 :66-69 names what happens when a practitioner or institution moves between periods: `Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another.` and the two silent failures, `what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails` and `what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a standing list of expertise forms whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual, with the residual specified in effect terms. This is the pre-registered refuter that would kill the derivation; it is repeated in `08` and `09`.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 (valence, capability, substitution) and Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual). Low overlap between the habitat figure and the agent figure.

### F5 - The same expertise, trusted then rejected, is an isomer over a fixed atom set

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Consider the same set of atoms in two periods: practitioners, instruments, claims, institutions, texts. What changes is not the atom list but the bond to the ground and to the room. Under Fig 12 :117 the two arrangements are different substances. Fig 12 :94: `The arrangement is the substance.` Fig 12 :123: `The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.` So "expertise" is a functional group (a claim-producing frame with a method), and the qualifier (trusted or rejected) is the member, decided by the bond, not by the ingredient list. The full derivation is in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Refuter:** show the flip requires an atom absent from the first period's set, in which case the relation is import (Fig 6 :23), not isomerism, and this finding narrows to that case.

**Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 12 definition), with the atom-set condition named as a live constraint. Not upgraded by world sources.

### F6 - A search for the expertise that will flip next returns a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: `you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself.` Fig 13 :134: `Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence` (the extract continues with a comma; the clause is the quoted unit). Fig 8 :39: `So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.`

No figure line returns a list of expertise forms by century, a date for a flip, or the identity of the next rejected specialty. The framework returns a shape: the flip happens when the ground channel decays, when frames share a pool, when the room stops following the question, or when the habitat substitutes. Naming a concrete expertise (phrenology, astrology, whatever) is a binding from the historical record onto that shape, provenanced as such, not a derivation.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that selects a named expertise or a date. Until one is shown, this is a declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, reported as a verdict, not silence.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and the canonical README (`a shape, not a name`), all sharing the framework pool, so agreement here is internal consistency.

### F7 - The ground does not decay; the channel does

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :25-28 name the mechanisms: `stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings` and `the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch`. A period can lose trust in an expertise because the channel to its ground saturated (a metric stopped discriminating, readings were pooled, the instrument was replaced by a reputation). The ground did not change. Fig 10 :29: `keep the anchor reachable`. This is distinct from F3: F3 is the question moving; F7 is the channel decaying while the question stays.

The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay, opened 2026-11-23, gives the instrument-level form: `An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it.` The essay says the color case `was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument.` That is the discriminator owed whenever a historical dispute about expertise cannot be settled by more argument.

**Refuter:** show a case where a claim lost its ground status because the ground itself changed its answer under the same measurement, with the measurement held fixed. That would break the anchor definition.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 and the low-overlap-in-content essay (same author, so weak independent weight).

### F8 - Importing a new expert frame needs a source gate and a shadow test; a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :51-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 6 :24: `Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives.` A period that imports an expertise wholesale (a foreign credential, a fashionable method, an external authority) moves its wall only if the new atom is real and the provenance is recorded. Otherwise it has added a wrong atom and paid above the cost of deriving from primitives. Fig 8 :48-49 gives the independence price: two frames drawing on the same evidence are one frame with two names, so their agreement carries no surplus.

**Refuter:** exhibit a closure that expanded without contact with a differing frame, or a case where an unprovenanced import moved the wall and later paid for itself at the ground with no debit. Neither was found.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (different figures, different objects: wall movement vs surplus location).

### F9 - A credential is a catalyst only if it survives its own use; most are spent parts

Fig 12 :119: `catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is.` A credential that keeps paying because the bearer keeps returning to the ground is a catalyst. A credential that paid once and is now replayed is a spent part. Fig 12 :107-110: `Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish.` Where the arrangement is not recorded as a method, `the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again.` This is why a period's prestige can look like durable expertise while being only the compressed description of an old settlement.

**Refuter:** show a credential that pays at the ground without any renewed contact, with the ground specified and the measurement repeated. If such a thing exists, the catalyst/spent-part distinction collapses for this case.

**Status:** single-source on the figure side, with the world corroboration from Collins and Evans 2002 (contributory vs interactional expertise) kept as a low-weight analogue, not a witness for the figure claim.

### F10 - The question is a union, so one lever leaves the intersections standing

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

So "reform credentials" only clears the region where prestige is the binding condition. Where the channel to the ground is also dead, credential reform leaves the intersection standing. Where the room is fixed while the question moves, replacing reviewers leaves that intersection standing too. Fig 9 :5: `Split it rather than search.` This is the methodological consequence of the census, and the reason four per-domain verdicts are reported instead of one averaged answer.

**Refuter:** show that all four conditions collapse to one measurable variable in the historical record, with that variable named and measured. Then the union reading is unnecessary.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 (union/partition) and Fig 3 (dependency order), low overlap between them.

### F11 - What would settle it is a discriminator, not more agreement

Fig 2 :42: `Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.` Fig 10 :42: `A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.` Fig 9 :30-31: `your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause.`

A historian or sociologist can settle whether a particular expertise flip was grounded by finding the discriminator: an instrument, dataset, experiment, or record that distinguishes "the room changed because the question changed" from "the room changed because influence changed." Without that, competing historical narratives are one pool talking (Fig 2 :40: `disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool`).

**Refuter:** specify a discriminator and run it on a case; if it fails to separate the readings, F11 narrows to that case.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 9, Fig 10 and the low-overlap CAIB example below (a real case of a channel decaying while the ground did not).

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. The exemplar's Seat Map note is carried forward: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not an honesty device. The shape claim is refuted, narrowed, or strained if any of the following holds:

1. **R1:** A standing list of expertise forms is exhibited whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual, with the residual stated in effect terms. This breaks Fig 11 :51.
2. **R2:** A historical case is shown in which trust was settled by agreement among frames known to share one evidence pool, with no separate bearing on a non-updating anchor, and where the later rejection tracked the world rather than a change of personnel. This breaks Fig 2 :35 and :40.
3. **R3:** A period is found in which the problem list, instruments and standards changed, the expert room did not, and the room still produced ground-tracked verdicts at the same rate as a recomposed room. This breaks Fig 10 :71-72.
4. **R4:** A credential or reputation is shown to change the truth status of a claim by itself, with no bearing on a ground, under a repeated measurement. This breaks Fig 1 :26-27 and Fig 9 :60.
5. **R5:** The four conditions of the union are collapsed into one measured variable with that variable named and its measurements shown. This breaks Fig 9 :54-57.
6. **Instrument condition:** a discriminator is named and run on a flip case and fails to separate the grounded, conventional and drifted readings. This does not break the shape; it shows the verdict for that case is `unreached`, not `grounded`.

If R1 to R5 fires, the derivation is withdrawn or narrowed as indicated. If only R6 fires, the status of that case is downgraded to `unreached`. Until one is run, nothing may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F4 and F3: no standing list, and a room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure. Those two lines answer the question in effect terms.
2. State the mode on every claim (F1 to F11 all `specified` or `general`, none `grounded`).
3. Keep the four per-domain verdicts separate. Do not average them into a factor list (F10).
4. Treat reputation as a budget filter, never as a witness (F2). In outreach, ask what would refute the separator; do not ask anyone to endorse it.
5. Send the generator (the residual, the separator, the refuter), not a recording of which experts were trusted in which century (Fig 4 :25: `A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion.`).

## 10. Closing verdict of the audit

The shape survives every re-census: trust in a form of expertise is a relation, not a property; it flips when the channel to a non-updating ground decays, when checking frames share one evidence pool, when the room stops following the question, or when the habitat changes the residual. The standing-list answer is refused, credibility accrued is disqualified as a witness, and the union of conditions is unbundled into four per-domain verdicts. The specific name of the expertise that flips next, and the date, are a declared `gap`, not a silence. Strains after re-census: one live (F5's fixed-atom condition), one visible tension (Kuhn's "standard" versus the anchor), one narrowed (sleeper effect), one downgraded to analogue (animal behaviour). Mode: `specified` per domain, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`, and a simulation throughout.
