## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the answer

The canonical README gives the limit: the framework says what a missing thing would have to look like, *"a shape, not a name"*, and then stops at the limit of what the instruments can distinguish, recording the lineage. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*, exactly: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for EAR-0070 a **shape** is the form that any acceptable preparation plan must have, statable now, before anyone can say which crop, which technology or which policy wins. It constrains; it does not fill. "A per-slot, habitat-enumerated, imported-hazard, sequenced and reversible plan with signposts" is a shape. "Plant drought-tolerant maize and build three grain stores" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained here

- *Library of Babel*: use the terms already held that are closest to the thing, then let new terms narrow the space. Verbatim: *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- The same essay names the failure mode that matters for this question: if the final search does not answer it, *"perhaps you are asking the wrong question altogether"* and the problem may be *"a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one"*. That is exactly the census finding in `01`, F3: rarity and regime change are a union compound, not one hard statistics question.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Describe the residual in effect terms first; only then pick paleoclimate, physics or practice as a frame.
- Fig 6 :30, quoted inside *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* The essay adds: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is why the outreach in `02` asks a checker what they would expect to see before showing any framework material.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For a future rare extreme, that means the event actually arrives and the plan is measured against what happened.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8). A paleo-proxy or a long control run can supply a hazard the short record lacks, but only if its provenance and its shadow test (does it explain a gap the record cannot?) are recorded.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism that forbids naming from a reading. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Verbatim: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was *"proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: a yield time series is a reading. It cannot comment on the source of the risk (a trend, a tail, a supply-chain coupling, a pest shift). Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a confident name. This is why the shape claim in `01` refuses to name a winning adaptation.

## 4. Why the aggregate answer is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* shows how a projection loses the distinction among several sources. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A national food-security index is a projection: many different component failures can produce the same green reading. Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."*

The two failures are the same failure at different scales: a reading treated as if it determined its source, and an average treated as if it determined the state of every component. The remedy in both cases is a better instrument, not a better argument. For food systems that means measuring the live slots (crops, regions, supply nodes, seasons), not smoothing them.

## 5. What the question itself is, in Library of Babel terms

The canonical question "how should food systems prepare for extremes too rare to estimate from historical records?" contains the word "estimate" in a role it cannot discharge. The essay's test is to ask whether this is a malformed bundle: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* The census in `01` answers yes: estimation from a short record and preparation under a changed climate are two conditions with a compound at their overlap. Splitting them is progress; searching harder on the bundle is motion without progress (Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*).

## 6. Two consequences for this folder

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, verbatim: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous institutions that agree with the shape claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the plan and a refutation of the shape, with the ground settling it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay, verbatim: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* So `01`, `06`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the question and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 7. Correction carried forward (kept visible)

I initially wrote this section as if the shape-not-a-name rule forbade naming any specific adaptation. That overstates it. The canonical wording governs *"what a missing thing would have to look like"*, a primitive not held in Γ. Drought-tolerant seed, irrigation and stockpiles are not missing primitives; they are bindings from the agronomy frame onto capabilities the food-system habitat already has. Naming one is not forbidden, but it is a **binding with provenance**, not a derivation, and it carries zero evidentiary weight until the ground settles it. The earlier, stronger statement is retracted here rather than erased. The metamerism point in §3 stands unchanged: the reason not to read a source off a projection is the missing discriminator, not a ban on names.
