## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how a food system should prepare for climatic extremes too rare to estimate confidently from historical records. Option A: calibrate the plan on a return period estimated from the record, treat that number as the design basis, and pool the agreement of record-trained models as confirmation. Option B: refuse the number as a design basis, derive the shape a plan must have when the record lacks the needed quantity, and state what would refute that shape. What each leads to: A produces a precise but understated hazard and an ensemble with one evidence pool; B produces a per-slot, habitat-enumerated, import-based, sequenced and reversible plan with signposts, an explicit refuter, and an honest open slot where no instrument returns a signal.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two conditions (rarity and regime change) plus a decision that consumes their result, so a union compound, not one hard question: Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Symmetry: not exchangeable. Observed centre and unobserved tail are not two reads of one event; the historical climate and the future climate are not two samples of one distribution. Scale: harvest, season, supply network, multi-decadal lineage of plans; the residual crosses scales, so the membrane rule applies.

**Domains (actors / currency / anchor).**

- **D1, record and estimation.** Actors: observations, return-period fits, risk models. Currency: estimated frequency and confidence. Anchor: the event that actually arrives. The record is a frame, not the anchor.
- **D2, hazard import.** Actors: climate physics, paleoclimate proxies, long control runs, physical bounds. Currency: physical plausibility and constraint. Anchor: physical measurement and process understanding, a different closure.
- **D3, food-system habitat.** Actors: crops, soil, water, pollinators, pests, labour, processing, transport, trade, markets, institutions. Currency: continued function under contact. Anchor: availability, access, utilisation and stability after the extreme.
- **D4, verification.** Actors: researchers, practitioners, affected communities. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so D4's independence is zero.

**Partition or union.** Union. D1 and D2 overlap on "what hazards are possible" but their closures differ; the overlap is a specific compound, a rare event in a changed climate, with its own loop. D3 is a network union of contact relations, not a sector list. D4 is a seam between the others (Fig 10 :13-14).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Verdict first (is the atom in the record closure?), then import, then habitat enumeration, then sequencing with thresholds, then settlement against the ground, then carry the method up. Describe the residual before selecting a frame.

**Strain test.** A line about "more search" governs unreachable-at-budget, not underivable. Applying it to a non-stationary record strains it, so the record is re-censused as the wrong closure. No strain remains after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The number and the name do not survive; the shape does. A preparation plan cannot be calibrated on a quantity the historical record does not contain. Its required form is: verdict first (the atom is not in the record closure); import hazard knowledge from a differing closure with provenance and a shadow test; enumerate the food-system habitat and its cascade per slot; change contact, not just the agent; sequence actions with adaptation thresholds and signposts, keep cuts reversible, and treat the work as permanent; buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong; keep description cost and action cost in separate columns; and carry the pre-registered refuter as part of the output. A stress test locates failure and does not prove capability. The figures do not name a winning crop, technology or policy; such a name is a binding from another frame and carries zero evidentiary weight until the ground settles it. The verdict is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** high and asymmetric, since the object is food security. The avoidable part is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a recording (a finished plan with a number) instead of a generator (the shape and its refuter). Fig 2 :42 - "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cheaper error is to ask for a refutation first and record it before offering any framework material.
- **undoable:** mostly yes. Sequencing, signposts and reversible cuts are designed to be revised, and DAPP's adaptation tipping points exist for exactly that. Not fully undoable: an introduced actor whose cascade was not enumerated (Fig 11 :65) and a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66) cannot be recalled by decision. That is why those moves require enumeration before introduction and why cuts stay ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A procedure that derives the unobserved tail from the record alone under a verified regime change would break D1; a demonstration that record and future hazard share one closure would reduce the problem to budget; an aggregate indicator that fires before every component slot would break D3; an agent-only improvement that changes the ground outcome with contact unchanged would break the habitat claim; a synthetic test shown sufficient for real capability would break the harness limit. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate. Prestige and volume of agreement are not such evidence: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."
