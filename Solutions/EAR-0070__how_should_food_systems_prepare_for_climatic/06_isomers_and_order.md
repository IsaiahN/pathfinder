# Union, order and isomers: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit derives the shape of a preparation plan. Two supporting questions remain: (1) is the question one problem or a compound, and where does its dependency chain stop; (2) if two plans contain the same ingredients, are they the same plan, and what decides the order? Without answers, the recommendation in `01` could be read as a checklist rather than as a structure.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (recap, see `01` §1)

R: two conditions (rarity, regime change) plus a decision; not symmetric; scale from harvest to lineage. Domains: D1 record estimation, D2 hazard import, D3 food-system habitat, D4 verification. Union, not partition. Dependency order: verdict, import, enumerate, sequence, settle, carry.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

Fig 9 :55: *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

- Condition A: the record is too short. The remedy is more or longer data, which is a budget question (Fig 8 :42: *"the remedy is more search, not another atom"*).
- Condition B: the record is from a changed climate. The remedy is a different closure, which is an import question (Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*).
- The overlap A ∩ B is a **specific compound**: a rare event in a changed climate. It is not "A plus B", and a single lever (collect more data) clears only region A. What remains is smaller and categorically different: it stops being a mixture and becomes a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :57-59).

**Ruling:** union. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 9 union rule + Fig 8/Fig 6 budget-versus-closure split; low overlap between the search and import figures).

### Q2 - Where does the dependency chain stop?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

The chain for this question:

```
1 perception      is the rare hazard returning anything to any instrument?
2 vocabulary      do we have terms for what the hazard affords the food system?
3 objective       what counts as "prepared" (a threshold, in the Operators' sense)?
4 planning        which actions in which order, with which signposts?
5 learn and carry name what worked, verify it, carry the method to the next hazard
```

The break is between 1 and 2. The historical record returns nothing for an event that has not occurred, so there is no edge to extend there. Fig 6 :33: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Paleoclimate proxies, long control runs and process-based hazard physics are already returning something, so they are the instruments to extend. Fig 6 :34: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Buying a better plan (links 3 to 5) before fixing perception is effort that arrives at a step which cannot use it. The IPCC's own emphasis on early warning and monitoring, and DAPP's monitoring system with signposts, are world-side recognitions of the same link.

**Ruling:** the chain stops at perception in the record alone; it is re-opened by import. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 3 + Fig 6). **Strain check:** Fig 8 :39 says *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"*, which might seem to forbid concluding anything from a silent record. It does not: it forbids concluding absence. We conclude *unreachable from this closure*, which is a different claim (Fig 8 :40-43). No strain after re-census.

### Q3 - Are two plans with the same ingredients the same plan?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Consider two plans built from the same four ingredients: a seed bank, on-farm water storage, diversified supply routes and an early-warning service.

```
Plan P:  early warning → (threshold ⋛) → water storage → (sequence →) → seed bank → diversified routes
Plan Q:  diversified routes → (threshold ⋛) → early warning → water storage + seed bank (conjunction +)
```

Same ingredients, different bonds. By Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not variants of one plan: the arrangement is the substance, and nothing in an ingredient list can tell them apart. P fails if the warning lead time is shorter than the time needed to release seed; Q fails if trade routes are already correlated before the hazard arrives. The difference is decisive in behaviour and invisible in the budget line.

**Ruling:** ingredient lists are not plans. Verdict `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the operational corroboration from DAPP's sequencing and adaptation tipping points. Under Fig 12 :124, plans built from the same functional pattern (`sense → threshold → act → carry`) are members of one **functional group**: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The functional group is the transferable method; the particular isomer is local.

### Q4 - What order is the plan, and in which currencies?

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and states *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. A preparation plan therefore needs:

- **→ sequence**: an action's input must actually arrive before the next one fires (the dependency order of Q2);
- **⋛ comparison**: an adaptation tipping point, the condition under which the current action stops meeting its objective (DAPP's "sell-by year");
- **− subtraction**: a progress measure against the goal, not a count of actions taken;
- **¬ negation**: the pre-registered refuter, which is a refusal of the shape claim, not another ingredient. The Operators table: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* A cheap hazard map (bits) does not make the field test cheap (actions), and summing the two hides the shortfall. Keep two columns in any plan budget; do not merge them.

**Ruling:** order is structural, not optional, and the two currencies stay apart. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 12 + Operators + DAPP practice).

### Q5 - Analogue test: is the food system a Fig 11 habitat?

The living-system test maps **relations**, not looks. Fig 11 :45-47 gives the mapping directly for agriculture:

- actors: *"what acts on it, and what it acts on"*, plus conditions *"light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* and relations *"pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*.
- currency: continued function under contact (yield, continuity, nutrition, access).
- anchor: what the ground returns when the extreme arrives.
- Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The top-level rule is enumeration: list everything in contact with the residual and outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :52 gives DDT to the eagles, three steps out).

This is not a pretty analogy. The figure's own examples are agricultural, and the world literature returned the same relational structure: cascades cross "sectoral, spatial or governance boundaries" (Burkitt et al. 2026) and compound with non-climatic drivers (IPCC AR6 SYR SPM B.2.3). Where the analogy **strained** on first reading was the word "seat": a food system has no single maintainer. Re-census resolves it: the seat in D4 is the planning institution, not the ecosystem, and Fig 11 :40 applies to the system as a whole: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

**Ruling:** the analogue holds as a habitat, with the seat located in D4. Verdict `triangulated` (Fig 11 + two independent world fields). Strain after re-census: none hidden.

## FALSIFIERS (written before the conclusion)

1. Show that rarity and regime change are a partition, not a union: one lever closes both, and no compound remains. That breaks Q1 and the unbundling step in `01`.
2. Show that perception is not the broken link: an instrument already returns a resolvable signal for the rare extreme from the historical record alone, with error bars that survive a regime change. That breaks Q2 and removes the need for import.
3. Show that two plans with identical ingredients but different bonds behave identically under multiple hazards, so the arrangement carries nothing. That breaks Q3 and the claim that a plan is a molecule.
4. Show that the food-system analogue is only superficial: contact structure can change without altering ground outcomes, or the cascade stops at first order in more than one documented hazard. That breaks Q5 and Fig 11 :40.
5. Show that description cost and action cost can be summed in one currency without hiding a shortfall. That breaks Q4 and Fig 12 :125.

If any fires, the corresponding ruling is withdrawn. None has been run; this is a simulated derivation.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound whose dependency chain stops at perception; import re-opens it. Plans are molecules, so identical ingredients under different bonds are different plans, and the transferable object is the functional group (`sense → threshold → act → carry`), not the particular ingredient list. Order is structural (sequence, comparison, subtraction, negation) and the two currencies do not add. The food-system habitat is a genuine Fig 11 domain, with the planning institution in the seat, and the analogue maps relations rather than looks.
