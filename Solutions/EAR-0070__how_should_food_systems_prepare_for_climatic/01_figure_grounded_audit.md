## 0. The residual, in effect terms

**Effect, not cause.** A planner must choose actions now. The quantity that would tell them what to prepare for, the frequency and magnitude of extremes under a changed climate, cannot be estimated confidently from the historical record. Two distinct effects are bundled in the question:

1. **Rarity.** The extremes are too rare to appear often enough in a short record. The estimator is unstable and the worst cases are absent from the sample.
2. **Regime change.** Under a warming climate the record was drawn from a different closure. It is not merely a short sample of the current hazard; it is a sample of a hazard that no longer holds.

**What each option leads to.**

- **Option A, "design to the historical return period."** Leads to a plan calibrated on events that did happen, with the tail systematically understated, and to the institutional habit of treating absence from the record as evidence of low probability. It also makes every additional model fed the same record look like independent confirmation.
- **Option B, "derive the shape of a plan that does not require the missing quantity."** Leads to a per-slot, habitat-enumerated, import-based, sequenced and reversible plan with signposts, and to an explicit statement of what would refute it. It does not promise a number or name a winning technology.

**Sought-for shape, bound before the run (Formula "Direction").** *A preparation plan is a per-slot, habitat-enumerated, imported-hazard, sequenced set of reversible actions with signposts, checked against the ground by frames that do not share one evidence pool.* This is an ordinary term here, not a preference, so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22)

- **Arity:** two conditions, rarity and non-stationarity, plus a decision that consumes their result. That is a union compound, not one hard question: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). It has its own loop.
- **Symmetry:** not exchangeable. The observed centre and the unobserved tail are not two readings of one event, and the historical climate and the future climate are not two samples of one distribution. A symmetric treatment of past and future observations is exactly the error.
- **Scale:** step (one harvest), cycle (a season), level (a region or a supply network), lineage (a multi-decadal series of plans and revisions). The residual crosses all four, so the membrane rule (Fig 4) applies.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the record/estimation frame | observations, return-period fits, risk models | estimated frequency and confidence | the events that actually occur. The record is a frame, not the anchor. Yes: the arriving event. |
| D2, the hazard-import frame | climate physics, paleoclimate proxies, process models, long control runs, physical bounds | physical plausibility and constraint | physical measurement and process understanding, a different closure. Yes. |
| D3, the food-system habitat | crops, soils, water, pollinators, pests, labour, processing, transport, markets, institutions | continued function through contact | whether food is still available, safe and affordable after the extreme. Yes. |
| D4, the verification frame | researchers, practitioners, reviewers, affected communities | residuals and agreement | the ground, never agreement. Yes. |

A domain with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain. All four have one. Absent D2, the question is closed and unanswerable; that absence is itself the finding.

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. D1 and D2 overlap on "what hazards are possible" but their closures differ: D1 contains only what was observed, D2 contains what physics permits. The overlap is a specific compound, "rare events in a changed climate", with its own loop and its own ground. D3 is a network union of contact relations, not a list of sectors; a failure in one node is a different thing from a failure of the same node in isolation. D4 is a seam between the other three (Fig 10 :13-14), and in this simulated run all four draw on one evidence pool, so D4's independence is zero.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. State the verdict first: is the needed quantity in the record closure at all? (Fig 9 :6, Fig 6).
2. Import hazard knowledge from a differing closure before setting objectives (Fig 6 :23).
3. Enumerate the habitat and its cascade before choosing interventions (Fig 11 :44).
4. Sequence actions with signposts, settle each against the ground, then carry the method up (Fig 12 :104, Fig 4 :22).
5. Describe the residual before selecting any outside frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied during census: a line about "more search" governs *unreachable-at-budget*, not *underivable*. Applying it to a non-stationary record would be a strain, so the record is re-censused as the wrong closure, not as a short one (F1, F2). Nothing is smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The record closure cannot supply the tail (decisive, `general` structural claim)

Fig 8 :32: *"no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* And Fig 6 :14 names import as *"the only operator that moves the wall."*

The historical record is a closed set of observations. No amount of re-analysis, finer binning or longer fitting adds an event that never occurred. Patel and Schneider 2026 (statistics) found that short series "generally lead to substantial underestimation" of return levels and that "ensembles composed of shorter segments retain substantial bias", even when total data volume is large. That is the idempotence of closure measured in a climate model.

**Verdict:** searching the record harder is the wrong instrument. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 8 + Fig 6, low overlap between idempotence and the import rule; statistics corroborates). **Refuter:** exhibit a procedure that derives an unobserved return level from the record alone with bounded error under a changing climate, or show that the record and the future hazard share one closure.

### F2 - Absence is not proof of absence; the estimate is a frame, not the anchor

Fig 8 :39: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

A return-period estimate is a frame-internal quantity. It updates when the data or the model changes. The anchor is the event that actually arrives. Treating "no such event in 100 years" as "such an event is a 1-in-1000-year event" promotes a frame to the anchor, which is the collapse Fig 2 names.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 8 + Fig 9 + Fig 2). **Refuter:** show a case where absence from a complete and stationary record does license a probability bound, with the completeness and stationarity established independently of the record.

### F3 - It is a union compound, not one hard problem

Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Rarity (D1 is short) and regime change (D1 is the wrong closure) bind together. Patel and Schneider show that ignoring a trend can bias a return level more than finite-sample bias does, and the IPCC AR6 SYR SPM (2023) states that *"Climatic and non-climatic risks will increasingly interact, creating compound and cascading risks that are more complex and difficult to manage"*.

A single lever aimed at "better estimation" clears the region where only rarity holds and leaves the regime-change region standing. The residue is not a harder statistics problem; it becomes a specific compound with its own loop.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 + world statistics and assessment). **Refuter:** demonstrate that record length alone, with stationarity established, closes the residual; then the two conditions were a partition and one lever suffices.

### F4 - Per slot, never aggregated

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* And Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state."*

A global or national yield average can be near normal while one crop, one region or one supply node is failing. Burkitt et al. 2026 (food-system ecology) found that import reliance and optimised supply chains propagate shocks and that "fragmented governance" and "lack of systems thinking" leave interventions disconnected. The average is where the live signal goes to hide.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 + Fig 11 + food-system literature). **Refuter:** show an aggregate food-security indicator that fires before any component slot does, consistently across regions and hazards.

### F5 - Enumerate the habitat and its cascade

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* The figure's own actor list includes agriculture: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* (Fig 11 :46) and *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"* (Fig 11 :47).

Practical reading: the preparation unit is not the farm in isolation. It is the cascade from hazard through production, processing, labour, transport, storage, trade and price to access and nutrition. Burkitt et al. define cascading effects as sequences where an initial hazard triggers "secondary and tertiary effects that propagate across interconnected components of the food system, often crossing sectoral, spatial or governance boundaries." The IPCC AR6 SYR SPM gives the same shape as a verdict: *"Climate-driven food insecurity and supply instability... interacting with non-climatic risk drivers such as competition for land between urban expansion and food production, pandemics and conflict"* (B.2.3).

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 + two independent world fields). **Refuter:** exhibit a food-system risk that is fully captured by direct first-order exposure, with no second-order or third-order contact, across more than one hazard.

### F6 - Capability lives in agent and habitat, so interventions must change contact

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A drought-tolerant seed, a better forecast or a larger stockpile is an agent-side improvement. It matters only if it changes the system's contact with the extreme: what water the soil holds, how many independent supply routes exist, which pollinators remain, whether a failed node can be bypassed. Diversity and redundancy are not slogans here; they are changes in the contact graph.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 + Fig 4's "only methods cross up" for the carry method). **Refuter:** show an agent-only improvement that changed the ground outcome while leaving the habitat's contact structure identical, measured on both.

### F7 - Asymmetry, permanence and maladaptation

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* And Fig 11 :65 warns: *"Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

A planner can add irrigation, a crop, a store or a trade route. They cannot subtract the climate's contact. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Moving a crop to a new region, or relocating a supply chain, inherits actors that were not chosen, and *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* (Fig 11 :68). Haasnoot et al. 2013 / Deltares put the operational form: DAPP *"enables initial decisions to be made that do not lock in investments, thus leaving flexibility to change path and avoid maladaptation"*, and adaptation should start rather than wait for *"certainty which will always come too late or perhaps never at all"*. The IPCC records increasing evidence of maladaptation (AR6 SYR SPM A.3.4).

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 + engineering practice + assessment). **Refuter:** exhibit an irreversible food-system intervention whose cascade was fully enumerated in advance and whose new habitat's unwanted actors were absent or harmless, with the outcome measured.

### F8 - Independence is bought, not assumed

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

A twenty-model crop ensemble driven by the same reanalyses is one room. Paleo-proxies, long control runs, process-based hazard physics, practitioner records and affected-community observation are different rooms only if they do not draw on the same evidence. Food security is high cost, so the figure says to pay for that independence.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 + Fig 7). **Refuter:** show that two purportedly independent hazard frames draw on disjoint evidence and are still selected by the same outcome, or that agreement among record-trained models predicted a rare extreme before it arrived.

### F9 - Bonds, two currencies, and the round trip

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* The Operators table states *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

A hazard map names a state; it is not a plan. A plan needs → sequence (do A then B), ⋛ comparison (a threshold at which the current action fails), and − subtraction (how far from the goal). A cheap description of the risk is not a cheap intervention; the two prices are in different units and cannot be summed. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 + Operators + Fig 4). **Refuter:** show a preparation plan with no threshold, no sequence and no progress measure that still passes a ground test across multiple hazards, or a single currency that correctly prices both description and action.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by seat reasoning: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim in §0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A procedure derives an unobserved rare-extreme return level from the historical record alone with bounded error under a verified non-stationary hazard (breaks F1 and F2).
2. The historical record and the future hazard are shown to share one closure, so rarity is only a budget problem and the compound dissolves (breaks F3).
3. A single aggregate food-security indicator fires before every component slot, consistently across regions and hazards (breaks F4).
4. An agent-only improvement changes the ground outcome while the habitat's contact structure is unchanged and measured (breaks F6).
5. A stress test or synthetic scenario is shown to be sufficient for real-world capability, not merely for wiring (breaks F7 and Fig 11 :70).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or rewritten. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`. The refuter is sent in `02`, `04` and `08` before any theory is offered.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Stop treating the historical return period as the design basis. State the verdict first (Fig 9 :6): the needed atom is not in the record closure.
2. Import hazard knowledge from frames whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23): climate physics and physical bounds, paleoclimate and long control runs, with provenance and a shadow test. Check that each import is an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :34).
3. Enumerate the food-system habitat outward through the cascade (Fig 11 :44), per slot (Fig 1 :19), including conditions and relations the figure itself names.
4. Sequence actions with signposts and adaptation thresholds (Fig 12 :112, Operators :52), keep cuts ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41), and state that this is permanent work (Fig 11 :63).
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). Never pool agreement from one evidence pool as corroboration.
6. Carry the method, not a recording (Fig 4 :22), and measure the round-trip gap rather than assuming the formula is the figures (Fig 4 :30).
7. Prestige is not action: *"Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it"* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*). The ask is for a refutation, not an endorsement.

### Round trip gap `R_T`

`R_T` is the gap between what was sent up and what came back down (Symbols table: `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`). Sent up: the verbatim figure set on closure, import, habitat and bonds. Abstracted (`T_A`): "a closed record cannot supply an absent tail; enumerate the habitat; sequence reversible actions; buy independence." Concretised (`T_E`) into a funded food-system plan: the gap is **nonzero**. Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip without explicit work:

- which hazards are imported, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 requires a source gate, not just "use physics");
- which slots are live, since aggregation hides them (Fig 1);
- what counts as changed contact, since an agent-only improvement can look like progress and change nothing (Fig 11 :40).

`R_T` was not measured numerically; no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

**After re-census: no hidden strains.** One live tension is named rather than smoothed: Fig 6 :32-35 requires an import to come from an instrument already returning something, while the rarest extremes may have no such instrument in some regions. That is a gap for an instrument builder, not a licence to let the world decide (Fig 8 :39).

## 7. Updated verdict

**The shape survives; the number and the name do not.** A preparation plan cannot be calibrated on a quantity the historical record does not contain. Its required form is: verdict first, import from a differing closure, enumerate the habitat and its cascade, act per slot, sequence with signposts and thresholds, keep cuts reversible, buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and treat the work as permanent. The figures do not say which crop, which technology or which policy wins. That is a binding from another frame and carries zero evidentiary weight until the ground settles it. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Strengths: F1, F4, F5 and F8 are triangulated across figures and independent world fields. Weaknesses: the instrument question at the very tail is open, and this second seat shares the primary's evidence pool (see `11_audit.md`).
