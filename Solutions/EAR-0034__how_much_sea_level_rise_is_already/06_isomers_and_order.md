## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Partition, isomer, or functional group?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The five slow contributors do not share all atoms: thermal expansion is density over a temperature gradient, Antarctic marine loss is mass over a grounding-line gradient with a threshold, Greenland loss is mass over a surface-mass-balance gradient with a size-linked irreversibility, glaciers are mass over a response-time gradient, land water is mass redistribution. As the record would write them they share the functional group "state to structure to changed state" but not the full atom set. Under Fig 12 :123 they are a **functional group**, not isomers: the group says "slow committed contributor", the qualifier says which one.

The stronger form is available but not licensed by the figures alone: if each contributor is written over the full shared atom set (mass, density, gradient over space, gradient over time, threshold), differing only in arrangement, they become a real isomer claim. What would have to be shown is that the same atoms can be arranged to produce each contributor with different behaviour. The world source gives a hint: IPCC AR6 Ch9 reports a near-field relative sea-level fall and a far-field rise from the same mass source, which is an arrangement effect, not an ingredient effect. But that is a world observation, not a figure derivation, so the isomer form is `specified` and single-source on the figure side.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any contributor can be written without an atom another requires, or if a single arrangement law generates all five with the same behaviour, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 : What is the dependency order of the slow responses?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." (Fig 3 :20-21)
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." (the full line continues "So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom")
- Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."

The order is not a ranking of size. It is a dependency chain:

1. **Bind the definition first** (horizon, forcing definition, contributor). Without this, link 3 of Fig 3 is broken and nothing downstream can be diagnosed. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
2. **Observe the per-slot residual** against altimetry, gravimetry, tide gauges and paleo. This is link 1 to 2 of Fig 3.
3. **Settle the fast-ish slots before the slow ones.** Glaciers have a response time of years to a few hundred years (IPCC Ch9 Box 9.1); Greenland surface mass balance responds on century scale; the Antarctic marine sector and the deep ocean carry the multi-millennial tail. Each settled contributor becomes an operand for the next level (Fig 12 :104), so the union can be composed only after the components are separately settled.
4. **Compose the union at second order**, as a threshold comparison over settled contributors, not as a fresh primitive. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

This order matters because a first-order reading of "ice sheets respond slowly" treats all five contributors as one lag. That loses the fact that the deep-ocean thermal slot and the Antarctic marine slot are the ones that keep the commitment open after emissions stop, while the glacier slot saturates. Levermann et al. (2013, opened 2026-11-22) report exactly this: glacier contribution saturates, Antarctic thermal and ice-sheet contributions are quasi-linear, and Greenland's nonlinear response overcompensates the saturation. That is a world-side statement of the dependency order, not a figure line.

### Q3 : Which analogues actually govern?

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." The ice sheets are persisting parts: the commitment is not a one-off melt but a part that keeps acting across uses and across centuries. This analogue governs the slowness directly, at low overlap with the Fig 9 union reading. Triangulated.
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." This governs **irreversibility**. Ice loss reduces ice volume, which changes surface elevation and melt rate, which changes ice loss again; the sign of that loop decides whether the system returns. The world source reports a size-linked threshold (about 3 to 3.5 m Greenland SLE lost, beyond which regrowth fails; about 2 m irreversible), which is a binding onto the cycle-sign structure, not a new primitive. Single-source for the figure definition; world corroboration for the existence of the threshold.
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Governs Q1. Triangulated with Fig 9 :54.
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Governs Q2 step 4. Triangulated with Fig 3 :20.

The analogues that do **not** govern and were declined in the draft: (a) mapping the ocean onto Spectrum and ice onto Gradient one to one, which Fig 11 :43 forbids because the habitat is enumerated, not composed; (b) treating the committed amount as a seventh primitive, which Fig 13 :115 forbids; (c) treating model agreement as a catalyst, since a catalyst survives its own use and an ensemble agreement does not act on the ground at all. These are kept visible here as declined readings rather than silently dropped.

### Q4 : What is the object at the threshold?

The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities" and gives the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Operators table :51. In this question the threshold comparison is between a committed amount and a domain-specific limit (an irreversibility size, a regional flood frequency, a coastal defence design level). At first order the operands are quantities, so the type is clean. At second order, after settled contributors become atoms (Fig 12 :104), the operands are molecules-now-atoms. Fig 13 :115 permits the demotion; whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. This is the live strain recorded in `01` section 7 and `11_audit.md`. It is named, not smoothed over.

## CONCLUSION

The five slow contributors form a **functional group**, not an isomer set as currently written; the stronger isomer claim is checkable and carries a stated falsifier. Their dependency order is definition first, then per-slot observation, then fast-to-slow settling, then a second-order union composition. Three Fig 12 analogues govern with low overlap: catalyst for persistence, cycle for irreversibility, functional group for the class; a fourth, settled-molecule-becomes-atom, governs the second-order composition. Three draft analogues were declined, with reasons kept visible. The threshold operand type at second order remains an open strain.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without importing a seventh primitive or presenting a world magnitude as a figure derivation. The number stays with the instruments.
