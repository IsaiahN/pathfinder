## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **The commitment is horizon- and definition-dependent, not one number.** The 0.7 to 1.1 m figure is a historical-emissions zero-emissions commitment to 2300 (IPCC AR6 Ch9, section 9.6.3.5). The 2 to 6 m figure is a 2000-year commitment conditional on 2 °C peak warming (SPM B.5.4). The 2 to 3 m and 6 to 7 m figures from Palmer (2024) are 2000- and 10,000-year horizons at 1.5 °C. These are different objects. Reporting one as "the answer" is the flat-form error the figures name in Fig 12 :5, "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
2. **The observed signal is per slot and per coast.** Greenland and Antarctica have different mass-loss histories (IPCC Ch9: 4890 Gt and 2670 Gt respectively over 1992 to 2020), and the regional relative sea level can fall near a mass source and rise above the global mean far away (Box 9.1). NOAA's distinction between global and relative sea level makes the same point in public-facing form. This is the world-side corroboration of Fig 1 :19 and :22.
3. **Agreement is not a new witness.** The IPCC numbers, the Levermann slope, the Palmer commentary and the Strauss commentary draw on the same assessment and model pool. Strauss (2013) itself notes it omits the regional gravitational deviations. Four outlets quoting one pool are one witness. The low-overlap witness is the paleo archive, which is why Levermann et al. (2013) pair it with process models rather than with another model ensemble.
