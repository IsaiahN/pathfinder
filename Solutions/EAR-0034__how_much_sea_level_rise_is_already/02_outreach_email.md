**Subject:** Check one output about committed sea-level rise before you decide whether the shape is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the gap between what ice-sheet and sea-level projection frames predict per contributor and per coast, and what the altimeter, gravimeter, tide gauge and paleo record actually return. That residual is indexed per slot, not aggregated. Fig 1 :19 puts it plainly: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 adds: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single global number for "how much is already committed" collapses the Greenland slot, the Antarctic marine slot, the thermal slot, the glacier slot and the regional fingerprint into one scalar that cannot say which one is live.
>
> **The shape claim I am asking you to check:** "how much is committed" is a union of overlapping slow responses, not one hard question, so it must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). The amount is not even defined until a horizon and a forcing definition are bound: zero emissions versus constant present warming, 2100 versus 2300 versus 2000 years. The framework returns the form of the answer, not the metres. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
>
> **What the claim does not say:** it does not give a committed value in metres. It does not say the ice sheets will vanish by a particular date. It does not treat model agreement as evidence: the models share forcing and ancestry, so their agreement is one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). The published magnitudes I have seen, about 2.3 m per degree over 2000 years and about 0.7 to 1.1 m from historical emissions to 2300, are world imports with authors and dates, not outputs of the loop. I am not asking you to accept them on this email.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single global committed-sea-level scalar that predicts per-contributor and per-region residual trends with no loss of sign or rank;
> 2. a demonstration that thermal expansion, glaciers, Greenland and Antarctic dynamics reduce to one variable under one monotonic law across all horizons and forcing definitions;
> 3. a committed amount that is invariant to horizon and forcing definition, with a stated instrument that would move it;
> 4. a model ensemble that moves the measurement instead of ranging against it;
> 5. a first-order-only contact list that predicts the observed regional relative sea-level and extreme-flooding pattern.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** the residual, the shape claim and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the four links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement from a recipient is never pooled as corroboration. By Fig 2 :40 it would be agreement inside one evidence pool, and by Fig 10 :42 ("A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.") a reader who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has spent the independence that made the verdict useful.
