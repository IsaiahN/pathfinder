# Expert verification packet - the Tether on stated values versus revealed choice

**What this is:** a claim in a form a consumer researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No purchase data is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A customer's answer to "do you value sustainability, quality, or privacy" and their choice at the till are two readings of the same slot by two instruments with two different anchors. The avowal is the legitimate anchor for "what was said"; the market is the legitimate anchor for "what was chosen". An avowal is only an instrument for the choice question, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. The word-reading costs bits, the market-reading costs actions, and summing them into one preference strength is the error the figures name. What results is a union of two conditions, an instrument-and-anchor condition and a contact condition, rather than one preference with a willpower problem. The customer's capability to choose well belongs to customer and habitat together, the attribute changes valence when the habitat stops paying for it, and the specific driver stays an open slot until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 2 and 11

- **Statement:** the two readings have different anchors and the survey is a substituted habitat; it proves wiring, never capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37-38 - *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 11 :70 - *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 2 :29 names the market: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **What to check:** is the avowal an instrument for the choice question rather than its ground? Does a survey remove the price of the action in the way a test harness removes the habitat?
- **Refuter:** a preregistered field test in which changing only the survey response, with price, availability, habitat, and contact held constant, changes realized purchase above instrument error. That breaks C1.

### C2 - framework-internal with a world-facing check, checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the two readings are isomers, not different strengths of one preference. Words price the description; the purchase prices finding out whether it holds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126 - *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **What to check:** is a common, lossless unit for stated value and actual payment available on the same slot? The world literature predicts it is not: Penn and Hu (2018) find hypothetical bias in stated-preference methods and show it is mitigated by incentive-compatible designs. That is corroboration from economics, not a derivation from the figures.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a common unit in which stated willingness to pay and actual payment are commensurable without loss on the same slot, validated against the ground. That breaks C2.

### C3 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the divergence is a union of an instrument condition and a contact condition, and the customer's capability belongs to customer-and-habitat. One lever clears only the region where one condition holds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades."*
- **What to check:** does a single intervention (more information, a nudge, a price cut) restore both conditions with no categorically different residue? If it does, the union reading is wrong.
- **Refuter:** one intervention restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue, or a case in which purchase is fully predicted by immediate attitude once habitat terms are measured, with no residual three steps out. That breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that customers lie or are hypocritical. "Hypocrisy" has no nameable anchor independent of the market and is not admitted as a domain.
- Not that price, habit, distrust of the label, or income is *the* driver. Each is a binding from a world frame, and none has a source-discriminating instrument here. The driver is a declared `gap`.
- Not that information campaigns or nudges close the gap. Effort lands above the dependency break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30); that is a budget filter, not a verdict.
- Not a single-preference theory with a strength parameter, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a purchase.
- Not that the framework is the only true account of consumer choice.
- Not a claim that agreement with this packet is evidence. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness wearing many names (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating design, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a colour instrument reads green and *"it cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the metamerism. Here:

1. **Two-reading test:** avowal and realized choice on the same slot, with habitat held constant. Does the word-reading predict the anchor above instrument error?
2. **Two-currency test:** is a lossless common unit for stated value and payment available on the same slot?
3. **Contact test:** does stated value vanish while contact measures fresh and unpooled?
4. **Habitat substitution:** does the attribute keep invariant positive valence after payment or availability is removed at the point of choice?
5. **Union test:** does one intervention restore both conditions with no categorically different residue?

All five are registered in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 before this packet was written.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :36-38, Fig 11 :70, Fig 12 :125-126, :130, Fig 9 :57 (six short passages).
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of stated-preference methods: is the two-currency distinction real in your instruments, or can you produce the lossless common unit?
3. Name one design that would discriminate a word-reading failure from a contact failure on the same customer and occasion, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
