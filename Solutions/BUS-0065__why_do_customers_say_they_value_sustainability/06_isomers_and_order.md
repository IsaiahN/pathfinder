# Supporting derivation: are the word-reading and the market-reading isomers?

Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what order, if any, connects them, and what does that order license?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two readings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Write the word-reading as `person - words - product(attribute)` and the market-reading as `person - payment - product(attribute)`. The person, product, and attribute are shared. The bond differs: words cost bits, payment costs actions (Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*).
- By Fig 12's definition the two are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible if one writes only "customer values sustainability" as a formula with one undifferentiated valuation term. It becomes decisive in behaviour, because one composition can be executed in a survey and the other only at the till.
- The weaker claim also holds: both readings share the functional group "valuation of an attribute" (Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*). The qualifier, words or payment, says which member. So even if the isomer claim failed, the two would still be different members of one kind, not different strengths of one member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single instrument can state a valuation and pay the price of the action in one composition, with no separability between bits and actions, the isomer claim fails and the two readings are one substance. Equivalently, a demonstrated common currency that is lossless on the same slot kills the claim.

### Q2 - What order connects them, and does the order make them one thing?

- `→` is sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). In the common framing, the word-reading comes first and the market-reading second: say, then choose. That ordering is real at the level of the occasion.
- But Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* A statement is settled (the person did say it) and can become an atom for the next composition. It does not follow that the second composition is a playback of the first. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The survey gives a head start, never a recording of the purchase.
- The order therefore licenses a chain, not a merge. Treating "say" as stage 1 of a single preference machinery and "choose" as its weakened stage 2 is a flat-formula error: it records the ingredients and not the bond (Fig 12 :14: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*).
- **Falsifier:** a case in which the word-reading and the market-reading are demonstrably the same composition at the same level, separable only by time. Then the sequence reading is correct and the isomer reading is overstated.

### Q3 - Are the analogues at other strata the same isomer?

- Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- **Privacy analogue.** Norberg, Horne and Horne (2007) define the privacy paradox as *"the relationship between individuals' intentions to disclose personal information and their actual personal information disclosure behaviors."* Same structure, different attribute. Actors: person and data field. Currency: disclosure. Anchor: the data actually disclosed.
- **Health analogue.** Rhodes and de Bruijn (2013) measure an intention-behaviour gap of 46% for physical activity; Feil, Fritsch and Rhodes (2023) find that *"Successful translation of a positive intention into behaviour is nearly at chance."* Same structure again. Actors: person and activity. Currency: sessions performed. Anchor: observed activity.
- **Green analogue.** ElHaffar, Durif and Dubé (2020) define the gap as the discrepancy between what consumers say and what they truly do; Blas (2022) calls it *"the conundrum between positive attitude and apparent lack of corresponding behavior."*
- Under Fig 4 the kernel transfers across these strata and the anchor must be renamed at each. The corroboration is of the **shape**, not of a customer-level cause. The three literatures also share the "intention-behaviour gap" label at the word level, so at that label they are one pool, not three independent witnesses (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :50-52). Overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- **Falsifier:** a low-overlap field in which a stated intention predicts the anchor as well as an action-based reading does at the same stratum. That would break the kernel transfer.

### Q4 - Which bond does the comparison carry?

- The phrase "cheaper or less responsible" is a ⋛ comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87; Operators table :48). It is ordered, not symmetric: swapping price and responsibility changes the thing being said.
- Fig 12 :130 warns that the price in bits and the price in actions are two readings, held apart. A comparison that ranks them on one scale is the named error, not the fix.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the comparison is symmetric, that is, that "cheaper than responsible" and "more responsible than cheap" are the same term. They are not, so the ordered reading stands.

## CONCLUSION

The two readings are **isomers** under Fig 12: same person, product, and attribute, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in a flat valuation formula and decisive in behaviour. The weaker functional-group claim survives even if the isomer claim fails. Their order is a real sequence at the occasion level, but it licenses a chain of handoffs, not a merge of one preference machinery. The privacy, health, and green literatures carry the same kernel at different strata with renamed anchors, which corroborates the shape while remaining one pool at the shared label. The isomer claim has a stated falsifier: a single lossless composition that states and pays at once.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the common "gap" framing without the part that fails. What fails is treating the word-reading as the ground for the choice question (Fig 2 :37-38) and summing two currencies into one preference strength (Fig 12 :130). What survives is a checkable structural claim that needs no new primitive and no imported driver.
