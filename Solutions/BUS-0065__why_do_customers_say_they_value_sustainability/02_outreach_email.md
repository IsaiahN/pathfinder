# Outreach draft: check the two-reading claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 survive the reader's own check. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about stated versus revealed value before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a customer's answer to "do you value sustainability, quality, or privacy" and their choice at the till are two readings of the same slot by two different instruments, with two different anchors. The avowal is the legitimate anchor for the question "what did this person say". The market is the legitimate anchor for the question "what did this person choose". An avowal is only an instrument for the choice question, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. Summing these two readings into one preference and reporting the difference is the error the figures name.
>
> What follows is a union of two conditions, not one preference with a willpower problem: (1) an instrument condition, where the word-reading is promoted to an anchor for a question it cannot settle; (2) a contact condition, where the habitat at the point of choice does not pay for the attribute, or the customer's contact with that fact has degraded in the channel. A customer failing only one of these and a customer failing both are different compounds, not different degrees of one failure, and they need different instruments.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that customers lie, that price is the only driver, that a nudge closes the gap, that this names which segment will buy, or that the figures settle the marketing question. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I make no `grounded` claim and I am not claiming the framework is the only true account of consumer choice.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. changing only the survey response, with habitat and contact held constant, changes realized purchase above instrument error;
> 2. a common unit makes stated and revealed value commensurable without loss on the same slot;
> 3. stated value disappears while contact with price and consequence measures fresh and unpooled;
> 4. the attribute keeps an invariant positive valence across a habitat substitution that removes payment or availability;
> 5. purchase is fully predicted by immediate attitude with no habitat term three steps out, or one intervention restores both conditions with no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Crossref record for the green-gap review, ElHaffar, Durif and Dubé 2020: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2020.122556
> - Crossref record for the hypothetical-bias meta-analysis, Penn and Hu 2018: https://doi.org/10.1093/ajae/aay021
> - Crossref record for the intention-behaviour meta-analysis, Rhodes and de Bruijn 2013: https://doi.org/10.1111/bjhp.12032
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link in the packet. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]
