# Outreach draft: check the discriminator shape before you decide about the origin claim

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated plainly and the pre-registered refuter has been written. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would actually distinguish an arrival origin from an Earth origin?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** Two routes, life originating elsewhere and arriving here, and life originating on Earth, converge on the same present reading: a biosphere with shared biochemistry, a tree rooted in a common ancestor, and a genetic code. The question is what could tell those routes apart. The difficulty is that the current artefact is symmetric under the exchange of the two routes, so its contents carry no information about which one happened. In measurement terms this is a source problem: an instrument that reads green cannot comment on the source, and several sources can produce the same green.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the prediction, not `grounded`).** A source question cannot be settled by reading the artefact's contents. It needs either a source-discriminating character, a reading whose value would flip if the two origin routes were exchanged, or a recovered lineage record. The framework puts it this way: nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it; the order is recoverable from the lineage, not the artefact. A transport step is a substitution of one habitat for another, so the trace classes to look for are what the arriving lineage failed to reproduce in the new habitat and what it brought with it that the destination did not choose.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that panspermia happened, that it did not, or that any named signature (an isotopic ratio, a chirality bias, an implanted message) settles it. Those are bindings from the literature onto the shape, with provenance, not results the framework derived. I am not counting the survival of spores in ejection or transit as evidence for arrival: that raises the plausibility of the route, but it does not discriminate the route in a given case. And I am not treating a failure to find a discriminator as evidence for an Earth origin. A null is unreached at this instrument and budget, not proof of absence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a present-day character whose value differs between the two routes without any lineage record;
> 2. a demonstration that the habitat substitution law does not apply to microbial transport, or that neither failure class leaves a readable residue;
> 3. a result showing unmaintained and maintained arrival make the same predictions;
> 4. an instrument shown to draw on the same evidence pool as the artefact reading, so it is a mirror rather than an anchor;
> 5. a sealed-room proof that no route-dependent character can exist.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Full architecture with the habitat and ground figures (20 min): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - Source discrimination, the spectrometer argument: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
> - Panspermia evidence overview for the field context: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panspermia
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the architecture link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
