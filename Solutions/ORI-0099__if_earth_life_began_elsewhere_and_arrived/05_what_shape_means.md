## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134-135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From the Library of Babel essay: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

Here the shape is not a molecule, a ratio, or a message. It is the form of an admissible answer: **a reading whose value flips under exchange of the two origin routes, readable by an instrument already returning something, with a stated failure condition.** That constrains the search without filling the slot. "Same biochemistry" and "same genetic code" fail the constraint because their values do not flip; they are metamers.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." The effect here is route-dependent reading, not "panspermia" or "abiogenesis" as causes.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." That is why the discriminator is described functionally before any named test is championed.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." A route is not in contact with us now, so the checkable description is of the trace it would leave.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line "will also be the starting place," and the paradox is that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The README: the framework stops "at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

Applied here: naming isotopic ratios, chirality, or an implanted genome message as *the* discriminator would be a binding from the panspermia literature onto the shape. It is permitted as a binding with provenance. It is not permitted as a figure derivation. The folder records those names as candidates from world sources and gives each a refuter, which is the difference between a binding and a claim to have named the missing primitive.

## 4. The source problem is the specific shape of this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* is the essay that governs this case directly. Its statement of the problem: "You can have several underlying causes that create identical readings but the definition of the output would be read as the same." Its metamerism example: an instrument reads green, "it cannot comment on the source," and the source could be one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or "a completely novel color scheme that humans have not yet discovered." Crucially: "The full set of ways to produce a reading is not known to be closed or determined."

The parallel to origin routes is exact. The biosphere is the green reading; in-situ origin and arrival plus evolution are two of the sources that can produce it; the set of possible sources is not known to be closed. The essay gives the only resolution it accepts: the green case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument," and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." So the answer shape is an instrument or a lineage record, not a further argument about which source is more elegant.

## 5. What the shape permits us to say now

From the received material:

- We can say contents do not decide route (Fig 13 :128-129, Fig 12 :111).
- We can say arrival is habitat substitution with two trace classes (Fig 11 :66-70).
- We can say the null is unreached, not absence (Fig 8 :39, Fig 13 :134).
- We cannot say which named trace will work. *Arrival of the Fittest* records the framework's own epistemic limit: LLM-assisted outputs are "simulacra," and a derivation "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."

That last point is why this whole folder is labelled a simulation. The shape is derived; its application to the real biosphere is `specified` and awaiting a ground.

## 5b. Correction to my own first reading

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any candidate discriminator in the outreach material. That was an overstatement. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive. Candidate traces such as an isotopic residue or a genome message are not missing primitives; they are bindings from the world literature onto terms already discussed. What remains forbidden is inventing a new primitive, or presenting a binding as a derivation that moves the wall (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."). Both readings are recorded so the narrowing is visible rather than silent.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." Gathering famous names to agree that panspermia is plausible is the named collapse, not corroboration.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They cannot settle the origin question they report on.

## 7. Source discipline for the eight essays

All eight essays share one author with the figures. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight; Fig 2 :40 applies to them as much as to any other pool. The genuinely low-overlap corroboration for this question comes from the panspermia and astrochemistry literature fetched this session (Wikipedia Panspermia, Wikipedia Pseudo-panspermia, Berera 2017), which does not share the framework's examples. Those sources corroborate the shape (contents versus route, null unreached) but do not derive it.
