# Solution ORI-0099

**Question:** If Earth life began elsewhere and arrived here, what evidence could distinguish that from an Earth-based origin?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 134

**Section:** 10. Life beyond Earth and broader implications

**Field for public contact points:** panspermia evidence research

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape (the current artefact does not carry its own route; a source question needs a source-discriminating instrument), `specified` on the predicted form of any discriminating evidence. Nothing here is `grounded`: no source-discriminating measurement or recovered lineage record was opened in this run.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this folder.** Every substantive file is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. Per *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It states the shape a discriminating answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the honest stop at the limit of the instruments.

## Restating the question in effect terms

Two origin routes are on the table. Option A: life originated elsewhere and arrived here (panspermia, in its several transport forms). Option B: life originated on Earth (in situ abiogenesis). Both routes, if they happened, end in the same present artefact: a biosphere with shared biochemistry, a phylogenetic tree rooted in a last universal common ancestor, and a genetic code. The question asks what could tell the routes apart given that the artefact is the same reading under either hypothesis.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **route-discriminating character**, a quantity or record whose value would flip when A and B are exchanged, readable by an instrument that is already returning something. A merely interesting fact about life, a feasibility result for transport, or a similarity score between Earth life and a candidate space organism is not such a character. This binding is written down so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else.

## Why the answer has the shape it has

The current biosphere is a reading; the origin is a source. An instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source" (*The Hard Problem is About Source*), and several paths can create the same reading. Fig 13 :128-129: "the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage." So contents and current readings have zero standalone verdict weight: "same biochemistry" or "same genetic code" is a metamer, not a discriminator. Fig 11 :66 places the transport step as a habitat substitution with two silent failure modes, Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." These failures, plus a recovered lineage, are where a discriminator could live. A null is `unreached at this instrument and budget`, never a second origin or a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39, Fig 13 :134).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reasons, in the figures' own words: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict" (Fig 7 :35); "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40); "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it" (Fig 10 :42). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless, and asking them to trust before they check spends the only thing they contribute. The contacts in `03` are public institutional or opt-in venues. Nothing is sent by this agent.
