## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about distinguishing two origin routes: A, life originated elsewhere and arrived on Earth; B, life originated on Earth. Both produce one present artefact, a biosphere with shared biochemistry, a rooted tree and a genetic code. Option 1: treat a reading of the artefact (shared code, shared chemistry, similarity to a candidate space organism, transport feasibility) as the discriminator. Option 2: treat the discriminator as a route-dependent character or recovered lineage, indexed per slot, with a pre-registered refuter and a null held as unreached. What each leads to: Option 1 spends the search on a metamer and promotes a filter to a witness, so a null is misread as an Earth origin; Option 2 returns a shape any answer must have, keeps the two arrival modes separate, and leaves the slot open until an instrument or a lineage record settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The contact failure between two origin routes and one artefact reading. Arity: two conditions bearing on one observation, so a union compound; "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57). Symmetry: the reading is invariant under exchange of A and B, so it carries no route information. Scale: origin is first order, artefact is settled output, discriminator is second order (Fig 12 :104).
- **D1 Arrival and habitat.** Actors: spores, ejecta, comets, source habitat, target habitat, a seat if arrival is directed. Currency: survival through ejection, transit and entry; introduction of actors. Anchor: the ground, what survives and what the new habitat does to it (Fig 11 :22). Union of two modes, unmaintained and maintained, kept separate.
- **D2 In-situ origin frame.** Actors: prebiotic chemistry, gradients, networks, local minerals. Currency: chemical to biological transitions. Anchor: experiments reproducing a transition. Competing route ending in the same artefact, not a verdict to average with D1.
- **D3 Instrument and reach.** Actors: spectrometers, sequencers, isotope-ratio instruments, sample return. Currency: readings and discriminating power. Anchor: the ground via a source-discriminating instrument (Fig 2 :29). This is the anchor domain.
- **D4 Lineage and provenance.** Actors: phylogenetic characters, isotopic ratios, chirality, genetic-code contingencies, records. Currency: traces of route, arbitrary and historical. Anchor: the ground paying for a lineage claim (Fig 13 :126-130). Partition of contents versus route.
- **D5 Verification and interpretation.** Actors: expert readers, journals, societies. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so D5 independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Read the artefact per slot, state what counts as a route-dependent reading, enumerate the habitat from the world, split arrival modes, read trace classes, ground the character, only then compose a verdict. The likely break is the objective and the arrival-mode split, where there are fewest instruments (Fig 3 :20, :27).

Strain test: Fig 11 :56-59 appears to say both "nothing selects" and "the ground still decides." Re-census resolves it: two modes with different seats, not one process (Fig 11 :55). No strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the named discriminator does not. A source question is answered by a route-dependent character or a recovered lineage, never by the artefact's contents, because "the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage" (Fig 13 :129). Arrival is a habitat substitution with two trace classes, what failed to reproduce and what was brought, and the unmaintained and maintained modes make different predictions. Evidence is read per character slot, feasibility is a filter, agreement is not an anchor, and a null is `unreached at this instrument and budget`, not an Earth origin. The named discriminator is a declared `gap`. This is `general` / `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Which route actually obtained is not decided here.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is sending a metamer as a witness and letting a null be promoted to an answer, which spends the search and misreads the field. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The candidate traces are bindings from the literature, not primitives; they can be withdrawn or replaced without touching the derivation. A null can be re-opened when an instrument improves. No atom has to be withdrawn from the six-term set.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A present-day character whose value differs between the two routes without a lineage record (breaks F1); a demonstration that the substitution law fails for microbial transport or that neither failure class leaves a residue (breaks F2); a result collapsing the two arrival modes (breaks F3); a sealed-room proof that no route-dependent character can exist (breaks F4); an instrument shown to share the artefact's evidence pool (breaks F5); or a figure re-census showing contents can carry route. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
