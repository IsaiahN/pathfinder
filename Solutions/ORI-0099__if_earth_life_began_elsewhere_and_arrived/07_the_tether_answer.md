# The Tether's answer: what could distinguish an arrival origin from an Earth origin

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the predicted form of evidence. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two origin routes, life arriving from elsewhere (A) and life originating on Earth (B), converge on one present artefact: a biosphere with shared biochemistry, a tree rooted in a common ancestor, and a genetic code. The question asks for evidence that could tell the routes apart. The difficulty is that the artefact is symmetric under the exchange of A and B, so its contents are the same green under either source. This is a source-discrimination problem at the level of the whole biosphere, and a union compound (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), not one hard problem with a single answer.

## 2. The shape

1. **Contents do not decide route.** Fig 13 :128-129: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Shared biochemistry, a common genetic code, and a phylogenetic tree all name the state, not the route. They are metamers.

2. **The discriminator is a kind of reading, not a molecule.** A route-discriminating character is a quantity whose value flips when A and B are exchanged, readable by an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32-35), with a failure condition stated in advance. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The framework does not name which molecule, ratio, or message.

3. **Arrival is a habitat substitution with two trace classes.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." So the place to look is what the arriving lineage could not reproduce in the new habitat and what it brought that the destination did not choose. A second adjacent instance of life is a "what you brought" trace at planetary scale.

4. **Unmaintained and maintained arrival make different predictions.** Ordinary panspermia is unmaintained: "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept" (Fig 11 :15). Directed panspermia is maintained: "arrival worked on" (Fig 11 :7), with a seat that introduces. These are two loops inside D1, kept separate and never averaged.

5. **Evidence is read per character slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." There is no "total evidence for panspermia" score. One character with positive mass can carry the whole signal while the global score sits near zero.

6. **Feasibility is a filter, not a witness.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Spore survival, ejection and transit results, and the well-supported extraterrestrial origin of organic molecules raise the plausibility of a route; they do not discriminate the route in a given case.

7. **A null is unreached, not absence.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Failure to find a discriminator today is `unreached at this instrument and budget`, not evidence for an Earth origin and not a second genesis.

8. **The anchor is a source-discriminating instrument or a recovered lineage.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Ten readers of one meteorite are one witness, not ten.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that panspermia happened, and not that it did not. The verdict is about the shape of discriminating evidence, not about which route obtained.
- Not that a named signature (an isotopic ratio, a chirality bias, a genome message, adjacent biosignatures) settles the question. Each is a binding from the world literature with provenance, not a derivation. The specific named discriminator is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that transport feasibility is evidence for arrival. It is a budget filter.
- Not that a null is evidence for an Earth origin. It is unreached.
- Not `grounded`. No source-discriminating measurement was taken.
- Not that the two routes are isomers in Fig 12's strict sense. They are two routes to one substance (Fig 13 :121); see `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument or a recovered lineage, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, the green case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." The checkable questions are:

- Is there a present-day character whose value differs between A and B without requiring a lineage record? That would break F1.
- Does the habitat substitution law fail for microbial transport, or do neither silent failure classes leave a readable residue? That would break F2.
- Do unmaintained and maintained arrival make the same predictions? That would break F3.
- Is a candidate instrument drawing on the same evidence pool as the artefact reading? Then it is a mirror, not an anchor (F5).
- Does a character exist whose route dependence can be read without an aggregate? If not, F7 fails.

Standing falsifiers from `01`: a sealed-room proof that no route-dependent character can exist; a ground measurement assigning the artefact to one route; a figure re-census showing contents can carry route.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The stop is at the limit of the available instruments and is recorded so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical figures README): not being able to distinguish the routes now does not mean the routes are the same, nor that one did not happen. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" Then Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The lineage of this derivation is in `01` and `11`, so a later reader can locate an error rather than only feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether life came from elsewhere or began on Earth. It says what any answer to that question would have to look like. The biosphere we can read today is the same green under either story, so its contents, shared biochemistry, a common genetic code, a tree of life, cannot tell the two routes apart. A source question needs a source-discriminating reading: a character whose value would change if the two routes were exchanged, or a recovered lineage that the artefact itself does not carry. Transport is a move from one habitat to another, so the traces to look for are what the arriving lineage failed to reproduce here and what it brought with it that this habitat did not choose. Ordinary arrival and directed arrival make different predictions and must be kept apart. Feasibility results are filters, not witnesses, and a failure to find a discriminator is unreached at this instrument and budget, not proof of an Earth origin. The framework returns that shape, its refuters written in advance, and an open slot until an instrument or a lineage record settles it.
