# Outreach draft: check one shape before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Precondition before sending:** the three capacity claims must be stated with their modes and refuters attached (C1 to C3 in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`), the single-source status of the assessment claim must be visible, and the live strain on premise encoding must not be smoothed over. If any of those is stripped for brevity, do not send.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape about schooling and reasoning, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on each part, `general` on the structure, `gap` on magnitude):** access to formal education does not install three new reasoning faculties. The capacities are already there. What changes is what crosses the classroom membrane and which checking instruments the learner has. A re-applicable method sends down a head start and extends what a person can reach. A replay of one success grows what they can recite without growing what they can reach. From that one distinction, three different verdicts follow, and they have different currencies and anchors, so I keep them separate:
>
> - Abstraction changes when the round trip from an abstraction back to a specific situation is taught and its loss is measured. Abstraction itself is universal.
> - Statistical use changes when the representation format and the independence of the witnesses change, not simply with years of exposure. Aggregating across people can delete the very signal a test is meant to report.
> - Hypothetical reasoning changes when the vocabulary of routes, goals, comparisons and refusals is transmitted. The decisive term is the refusal: a vocabulary that cannot say a claim is false can only accumulate answers.
>
> Standardised tests can sever the channel to a ground while the ground itself stays intact. I am not claiming a number for any of this. The figures return a shape, not an effect size, so the magnitude stays open until a measurement settles it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that schooling creates abstraction, that more years means better statistical reasoning, that a mean test-score lift is a verdict on a capacity, or that this derivation settles anything. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. I am also not claiming the framework is the only one that could produce this shape.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I wrote the rest:**
> 1. a population with no formal schooling performs at the same rate as a schooled one on per-slot statistical discrimination, on treating correlated witnesses as one witness, and on counterfactual-premise reasoning, against a ground that does not update on the test;
> 2. a curriculum transmitting only replays extends reach to a new residual as well as one transmitting methods;
> 3. years of schooling predicts per-slot statistical reasoning after representation format and witness independence are held fixed;
> 4. no assessment can be built whose anchor does not update on the assessment and whose results transfer to an untrained ground task;
> 5. one of the framework's six base terms is derived from the other five, or a seventh is admitted.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in those files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [Your name]

## Handling a reply

Record the verdict on stage 1 *before* sending any framework material. If the recipient supplies a failure, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it in the auditor seat as if it came from any other source. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). A Track B style reply is a conversation, never corroboration, and carries zero verdict weight.
