## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three capacities isomers over a fixed atom set?

Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Two distinct comparisons must not be conflated:

1. **Between capacities.** Abstraction, statistics and hypothetical reasoning do **not** run over the same atom set, so they are not isomers of one another. Abstraction composes a state with a structure and returns across the membrane (Symbols table :70, :74). Statistics registers R per slot and compares independence across frames (Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :31). Hypothetical reasoning composes bonds over terms not currently observed (Fig 12 :111). Their atoms differ, so the figure's definition does not apply. They are three conditions of a union, not three arrangements of one substance (Fig 9 :54-55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.").
2. **Within a capacity, schooled versus unschooled.** Here the isomer claim is real. The atoms are the same (taxonomic and thematic categories, premises, countable frequencies, the gradient and spectrum primitives). What differs is the bond: thematic grouping uses context and practical sequence; taxonomic grouping uses a comparison bond over a decontextualised ordering; counterfactual acceptance composes over a premise that is not observed; empirical bias refuses that composition. Scribner and Cole 1973 report exactly this for categorisation (form and function versus colour) and for syllogisms. Kroupin et al. 2024 name the complement "analytic bias." Under Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference "invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a schooled and an unschooled performance difference requires an atom available to one group and not the other (a genuinely new primitive, not a different bond over the same categories), the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import. Kroupin et al. report that unschooled participants often fail to *encode* the premise structure at all (Scribner: "40% dropped the second premise entirely in their recall"), which could be read as a missing atom. I keep this as a live strain rather than resolve it by assertion: encoding failure could be a bond not being applied rather than an atom not being held. An instrument that discriminates "not held" from "held but not composed" would settle it. Until then, the isomer reading is `specified`, triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 plus the cross-cultural reports), strained on the encoding question.

### Q2 - What is the order of the union?

Two orders compose here, and neither may be read against the other.

1. **Dependency order across the learner (Fig 3).** Vocabulary first: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked" (Fig 3 :7). Objective second: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms" (Fig 3 :10). Method transfer third: "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once." (Fig 4 :24). Ground contact fourth: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." (Fig 2 :45). Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
2. **Round-trip order within abstraction (Symbols table, Fig 4).** T_A (abstraction) then T_E (concretisation), with R_T measured between: Symbols table :77 "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." An education that teaches only T_A, or that treats the abstract result as the answer, has a one-legged loop.

These are not the same order. The Fig 3 order is across links of a chain inside a learner; the T_A/T_E order is between scales. Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Reading one against the other is the error Fig 6 :31 warns about: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."

**Falsifier:** show that a learner can state what would count as progress on a ground-anchored transfer task (Fig 3 :10 satisfied) before acquiring terms for what situations afford (Fig 3 :7 not satisfied), or that an abstraction can be concretised with R_T = 0 on a new situation. Either breaks the order claim.

### Q3 - Analogues that carry the mechanism

Three analogues were checked. Only one is admitted as a corroborator rather than a name.

1. **Fong, Krantz and Nisbett 1986, "guided induction" (human statistical training).** This is the strongest low-overlap analogue for method transfer. Free recall of the abstract abstract rule and training on worked examples both transferred to "domains where the rule was not taught." That is Fig 4 :23 "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." admitted from cognitive psychology with provenance. It corroborates the mechanism; it does not derive it.
2. **Schulze and Hertwig 2022 / Till and Sproesser 2020, representation format (statistical reasoning).** Natural frequencies and sequential experience change performance without changing years of schooling. Structurally this is Fig 12 :117 again: same atoms (counts, base rates), different bond (normalised percentage versus nested frequency), different behaviour. It corroborates F4 of `01` and the isomer reading.
3. **Kroupin et al. 2024, "Small Games" (cross-cultural cognition).** Schooling is described as "an imposition of Small-Game structure on what was historically education in a rich social environment." This resembles Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another" and Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." I admit it as a **shape analogue only**, not as a derivation: the figure's habitat law is about survival and arrival across a ground, while the Small Game claim is about the context demands of a problem class. They rhyme, but they govern different objects. Marking this honestly keeps the analogy from becoming a false import.

**Falsifier for the analogue set:** if a representation-format change improves performance only because it adds a genuinely new atom to the learner's library (rather than rearranging held atoms), the isomer/format analogue collapses into import and Q1 must be re-censused.

### Q4 - Why the union cannot be cleared by one lever

Fig 9 :55-57 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Here the levers most often tried are "more years," "better tests" and "more content." Each clears one region:

- More years can raise exposure to a bond vocabulary without guaranteeing the round trip.
- Better tests can sharpen a metric without changing the channel to a non-updating ground (Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does").
- More content grows the library without growing the reach (Fig 5 :72 "and a library that grows is not a reach that grows.").

The residue where all three conditions bind is the learner who can compose a route, goal and refusal over a per-slot residual and check the round trip against a ground that did not train them. That residue is smaller and "categorically different" (Fig 9 :57-58). It is also the only residue worth sending to an outside checker.

## CONCLUSION

The three capacities are a union, not isomers of one another; schooled and unschooled performances *within* a capacity are isomers over a shared atom set, with one live strain about whether premise encoding is a missing bond or a missing atom. The union has two orders that must be kept apart: the dependency chain inside the learner (Fig 3) and the abstraction/concretisation round trip between scales (Symbols table, Fig 4). One analogue carries the mechanism (Fong et al. on transfer), one corroborates the isomer reading (format effects), and one is admitted only as a shape analogue (Small Games). No lever clears the union; the residue is per slot and must be checked against a ground that does not update on the instruction.
