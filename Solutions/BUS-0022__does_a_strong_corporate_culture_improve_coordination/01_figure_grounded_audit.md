## 0. The residual under audit

The question is posed as an either/or: strong culture either improves coordination or suppresses dissent and independent judgment. Stated as an effect, the residual is:

> With a widely shared and strongly held set of meanings in place, does the firm's contact with its ground improve (coordination), does its capacity to refuse a wrong term degrade (judgment), or are these two different quantities with two different anchors that cannot be returned as one verdict?

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." So "does culture help or hurt" is already the wrong granularity. The question must be answered per slot: which decision, which coordination problem, which judgment, against which anchor. Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The coordination literature and the dissent literature are two such subsets.

We bind the sought-for shape before deriving: an answer is acceptable only if it (a) names the quantity and anchor per domain, (b) identifies the condition under which sharing flips sign, (c) says what a single aggregate score hides, and (d) states a refuter. This is Formula "Direction": "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

Describe R by arity, symmetry, scale. Then one line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. Partition or union. Order by dependency (Fig 3).

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least two conditions (a coordination condition and a judgment condition), each indexed per decision slot. Symmetry: "coordination" is a comparison against a shared code (symmetric agreement, Fig 12 :59), while "improve" and "suppress" are ordered claims (Fig 12 :73 sequence, :87 comparison), so a single pooled correlation cannot choose the arrow. Scale: the individual member loop, the team or firm pool, and the channel across time; the transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27).

**Partition or union.** This is a **union**, not a partition. D1 and D2 overlap: the same shared code that makes coordination cheap is the same overlap that destroys independence for a verdict. Fig 9 :54-55: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." So one lever (for example, "build a stronger culture" or "encourage more dissent") clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The either/or is a union of two conditions with different anchors (decisive for the framing)

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"

The same high correlation between members is disqualifying in one domain and efficient in the other. There is no contradiction: Fig 2 :39 names valence as the rule. A convention is a shared meaning whose product is agreement itself; a verdict is a claim about a world that can refuse it. Strong culture is cheap in the first and costly in the second. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

**Mode:** `general` for the valence rule, `specified` for the firm application.
**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 :39-41, Fig 9 :54-57, and Fig 12 :59 (symmetric bond) versus :73 (ordered bond). Low overlap between the agreement rule and the union rule.
**Refuter:** show a shared meaning that lowers coordination cost and is also a verdict about a world that can refuse it, with no residual difference between the two. That would collapse the valence distinction.

This dissolves the question as posed. "Improve coordination, or suppress judgment" is not one dial with a right setting. It is two quantities with two anchors that happen to be driven by the same parameter, exactly as Fig 8 :47 says: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :48-49: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

### F2 - Coordination gain is real, but only in the convention domain

Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."
Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

A shared code is a symmetric bond (Fig 12 :59: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."). Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92), so the shared meaning removes spelling cost: members do not re-derive who does what, and a new member can be given a head start rather than a replay. Fig 4 :24: "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once." That is the legitimate coordination gain. It is a reduction in description cost, not evidence that the firm's verdicts are correct.

The gain fails when what crosses is a recording rather than a method. Fig 4 :25-26: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." A culture that transmits a ritual of one past win is not coordination capital; it is a replay.

**Mode:** `specified` (the coordination effect is real and checkable, but unmeasured here).
**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 :41, Fig 4 :22-26, Fig 12 :59/:92/:94, three figures with distinct roles.
**Refuter:** a measured coordination task in which a shared meaning that is demonstrably a convention (its product is agreement, not a claim about an independent world) fails to lower cost or transfer time.

### F3 - Judgment suppression is real, but it requires the anchor to update or be absent

Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."
Fig 2 :18-21: "one evidence pool", "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"
Fig 2 :33-34: "The anchor must not update.", "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 7 :35-36: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes.", "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

When everyone reads the same report and the reference updates on the organization's own reports, the firm is Fig 2's collapse 1 (mutual update, coherent drift) and collapse 2 (common cause, one evidence pool). Agreement then carries no information about the world. The dissenter is the frame that ranges against a non-updating anchor; remove that and "a mirror cannot correct you." This is why a strong culture can suppress independent judgment, but the figure-grounded condition is precise: it is high evidence overlap plus a lost or updating anchor, not strength by itself.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* states the same thing in the author's own words, independently of the figure notation: "You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."

**Mode:** `specified` (structural condition), not a measured claim about any firm.
**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 :31-35 (anchor/independence), Fig 7 :35-36 (mirror chain), Fig 8 :48-49 (one frame wearing two names), and the essay (low overlap with the figure notation but same author, so weak independent weight).
**Refuter:** an anchor that updates on the organization's own reports is shown to still settle the verdict correctly across a change of residual. Or: two frames with demonstrably near-total evidence overlap produce a genuine surplus in verdict accuracy where the ground pays. Either breaks this finding.

### F4 - The failure genus is a convention that decides an outcome

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"
Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"
Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

This is the exact shape of "culture suppresses judgment." A shared value that begins as a convention ("we decide quickly because we share terms") is promoted into a verdict ("this option is correct because it fits our values"). At that moment it stops being a convention and becomes an uncheckable constant. Declaring the value does not ground it. The ritual becomes a seat that authors the outcome it was meant only to coordinate. Fig 10 :72: "A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

**Mode:** `general` (this is a named failure genus), `specified` when applied to a corporate value.
**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 :47-49 and Fig 2 :43, with Fig 10 :72 as the temporal form.
**Refuter:** show a convention that decides an outcome and remains checkable and revisable at the ground, with no loss of its convention status. That would break Fig 10 :47.

### F5 - "Strong" as a single score aggregates away the live signal

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :21-22: "Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one." "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
Fig 10 :26: "saturated metrics, pooled readings"

A culture survey returns one number for the firm, yet the live signal is per decision slot. A global score near zero is a legal state with one failing slot, so "strong culture" can hide exactly the judgment failure the question asks about. Worse, the score is produced by the same frame it grades: employees answer a survey about their own agreement, and the agreement is then used to certify the agreement. That is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces.

**Mode:** `specified`.
**Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 :19/:21-22/:27 and Fig 10 :26.
**Refuter:** a single culture score shown to preserve, not hide, a documented per-slot live residual across a change of ground.

### F6 - Valence is relative to the residual, so no standing verdict survives

Fig 11 :49-51: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries.", "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds.", "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 11 :39-40: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone.", "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Strong culture is not an actor with a fixed sign. Under a coordination residual it is a benefactor; under a verdict residual with an updating anchor it is a malfactor. The literature mirrors this: Sorensen (2002) reports that strong culture firms have more reliable performance in stable environments while the reliability benefit disappears in volatile ones. That is valence flipping with the environment, which is the habitat half of Fig 11 :39.

**Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded (the Sorensen result is world corroboration, not a figure line and not a measurement taken here).
**Status:** triangulated for the structural claim (Fig 11 :49-51 plus Fig 2 :39 valence plus Sorensen as a low-overlap-in-field but same-broad-business witness).
**Refuter:** an invariant culture valence across a documented change of residual and habitat.

### F7 - Dissent is a seat, not a personality trait

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
Fig 10 :85: "Who holds the seat is not chosen. The position selects the occupant."
Operators table :53-58: negation is "this does not hold" and "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

The figure-grounded remedy for suppressed judgment is not "hire contrarians" (that would be selecting a standing list of good actors, refused by Fig 11 :51). It is to keep a seat that can refuse, outside the frame it grades, ranging against a non-updating ground. Asch's low-overlap result makes the same point in a different currency: in the conformity experiments, "the presence of a single dissenter dramatically reduced conformity" (from about one-third of responses to about five percent with a true partner). The dissenter's structural position, not their trait list, broke the unanimity. The Condorcet jury theorem supplies the mathematical form: the majority is correct only when "each voter has an independent probability p of voting for the correct decision," and correlated votes destroy the result. Independence is the variable.

**Mode:** `general` for the seat rule, `specified` for the organizational application.
**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 :42/:52/:85 and Operators :53-58, with Asch (human cognition) and Condorcet (mathematics) as genuinely low-overlap world witnesses.
**Refuter:** show a verdict corrected by a frame that can reconstruct the claim and shares all of the authoring frame's evidence, with no non-updating anchor in play. That would break the seat requirement.

**Open type question, not smoothed over:** the Operators table says "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate" and negation "acts on a single operand" (Operators :70-72). A corporate "dissent seat" is an office occupied by a person, which is not the same as the unary operator. Whether an institutionalized dissenter is an instance of negation or a binary bond between a checker and a verdict is left open, with a falsifier in `06`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Aldag's critique of groupthink is a live reminder that a comfortable story can survive with little evidence, so these are written down before the derivation's conclusion and before any contact.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A convention that decides no outcome can be shown, in a measured task, to fail to lower coordination cost or transfer time.
2. Two frames with demonstrably low evidence overlap, both drawing on differing closures but sharing the firm's code, produce no surplus in verdict accuracy where the anchor pays.
3. An anchor that updates on the organization's own reports is shown to still settle the verdict correctly across a documented change of residual.
4. A single culture score is shown to preserve, not hide, per-slot live residuals across a change of ground.
5. A figure line is produced that selects a numerical culture strength, a universal intervention, or a named slot filler. That would break the declared `gap` and force a re-census.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A failed prediction is more useful than an endorsement.

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is this one question?** No. It is a union of a convention condition and a verdict condition with different anchors (Fig 2 :39-41, Fig 9 :54-57). Split it.
2. **Does sharing help coordination?** Yes, as a symmetric bond that removes spelling cost and transfers as a head start, provided a method crosses and not a recording (Fig 2 :41, Fig 4 :22-26, Fig 12 :59/:92).
3. **Does sharing hurt judgment?** Only with high evidence overlap plus an updating or absent anchor, which produces coherent drift and a mirror chain (Fig 2 :31-35, Fig 7 :35-36).
4. **What is the exact failure?** A convention promoted into a verdict, which stops being a convention; declaring the value does not ground it (Fig 10 :47-49).
5. **What does the score hide?** Per-slot live residuals, and the self-scoring of the frame that produces the score (Fig 1 :19/:21-22/:27).
6. **Can one intervention fix both?** No. One lever leaves the intersection standing because this is a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :55-57). Independence is one quantity read twice (Fig 8 :47).
7. **What is owed?** A non-updating anchor, a refusing seat, and a discriminator that measures per-slot contact. Until those exist the answer is `candidate`, not `grounded`. The specific numerical strength is `gap`, reported as unreachable rather than silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).

## 8. Strains, conflicts and re-census

- **S1, Fig 2 :41 versus Fig 2 :40.** Apparent contradiction: agreement is both efficient and disqualifying. Re-census: :39 "Correlation has valence" is the governing rule; the two lines govern a convention and a verdict respectively. Not strained after census.
- **S2, world conflict from Aldag 2022.** The BPS article argues that groupthink lacks empirical support and that "there is no evidence either that the purported characteristics of groupthink 'hang together' as a group or that these characteristics are most evident in highly cohesive groups." It quotes Moorhead and Montanari 1986: cohesion had "a negative impact on self-censorship, a positive impact on dissent." This conflicts with the popular cohesion-suppresses-dissent story. Returned to the census: Fig 2 independence is a claim about *evidence overlap*, not about emotional cohesion. Aldag's result refutes the psychological mechanism, not the figure's correlation law. Verdict unchanged. Recorded as a genuine low-overlap conflict, not hidden.
- **S3, Fig 11 :49-51 versus Fig 2 :29.** Apparent conflict: valence is relative to the residual, yet the anchor must not update. Re-census: :49 governs actor valence; :29 governs the anchor's property. Different objects. Not strained.
- **S4, the word "strong."** A single score is an aggregate. Re-census assigns it to D4 as a frame-internal summary, not to the residual itself (Fig 1 :27).
- **S5, live type question.** Is an institutionalized dissenter an instance of unary negation (Operators :53-58, :70-72) or a binary relation between a checker and a verdict? Named, not hidden, in `06` and `08`.
- **After re-census: one live type question (S5), one recorded world conflict (S2), no hidden strain.**

## 9. Updated verdict

The shape survives, the popular framing does not. Strong culture is a union compound: it is cheap and productive for conventions (D1), and it is dangerous for verdicts when evidence overlap is high and the anchor updates or disappears (D2 and D3). A single score hides the per-slot signal (D4). The remedy is structural, not a trait list: keep a non-updating ground reachable and keep a seat able to refuse (D5). Culture's valence flips with the residual, so no standing pro-or-anti verdict survives (F6). The numerical strength and the universal intervention are a declared `gap`. The strongest figure-grounded lines are Fig 2 :39-41 (valence), Fig 2 :33-35 (anchor and mirror), Fig 10 :47 (convention promoted to verdict), and Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual).

Mode: `specified` on the five domain verdicts, `general` on the union and valence structure, `gap` on the number. Not `grounded`. This is internal consistency against the figures, not ground contact.
