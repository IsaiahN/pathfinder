## Restating the question as an effect

The question asks whether one object, "strong culture," has one sign or two. In effect terms: when members of a firm draw on a widely shared and strongly held set of meanings, does the firm's residual `R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` shrink (better coordination) while its capacity to refuse a wrong term stays intact, or does high overlap among the members' frames turn the firm into one evidence pool wearing many names, so that agreement about the world becomes worthless and nobody can correct it? The two halves name different quantities with different anchors, so the honest task is to keep them separate rather than average them.

## What an answer would look like (the sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term)

A candidate answer must specify, per domain, (1) which quantity a shared meaning is cheap for and which anchor settles it, (2) the condition under which that same sharing becomes disqualifying for a verdict, (3) which slot-level signal a single culture score hides, and (4) what would reverse each verdict. We bind that four-part shape here so it can accrue a residual like any other term (Formula "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ordinary term rather than an invisible preference).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Adding readers raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." A list ranked by likely agreement is a list selected to agree, which is a convention, not a verdict.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The outside frame must not hold the derivation's interior before returning a verdict.

Legally, harvesting personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
