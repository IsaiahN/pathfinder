**Subject:** One checkable claim about culture and coordination, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does a strong corporate culture improve coordination or suppress judgment" is a union of two conditions with different anchors, not one dial. A shared meaning is efficient for a convention, because agreement about a shared meaning is the product. Fig 2 calls this "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." The same sharing is disqualifying for a verdict about the world, because "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2). The rule that separates the two is "Correlation has valence" (Fig 2).
>
> So the prediction is not "culture is good" or "culture is bad." It is: where the anchor is a shared meaning, sharing lowers coordination cost; where the anchor is a world that can refuse the claim, high overlap plus an anchor that updates on the organization's own reports produces "coherent drift, which is why nobody notices" (Fig 2), and "a mirror cannot correct you" (Fig 2). Culture's sign flips with the residual. No standing pro-or-anti verdict survives.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the numerical culture strength that is best, that any particular firm has the right amount, or that "groupthink" is a settled causal mechanism. The empirical literature on groupthink is in real conflict, and I record that conflict rather than hiding it. The framework returns a shape and a separator, not a number.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a measured coordination task in which a true convention fails to lower cost or transfer time;
> 2. two low-overlap frames, sharing the firm's code, producing no accuracy surplus where the ground pays;
> 3. an anchor that updates on the organization's own reports still settling the verdict correctly across a change of residual;
> 4. a single culture score shown to preserve, not hide, per-slot live residuals;
> 5. a figure line that selects a numerical culture strength or a universal intervention.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the affected finding. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, verbatim with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures folder, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* the run, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question and outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
