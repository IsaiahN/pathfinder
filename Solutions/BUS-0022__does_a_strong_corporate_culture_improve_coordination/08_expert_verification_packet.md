# Expert verification packet - the Tether on culture, coordination and judgment

**What this is:** a claim in a form an organizational scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the five domain verdicts, `general` on the union and valence structure, `gap` on the numerical question. Not `grounded`.** No firm was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does a strong corporate culture improve coordination or suppress dissent and independent judgment?" is a union of two conditions with different anchors, not one dial. A shared meaning is efficient for a convention, because agreement about a shared meaning is the product; it transfers as a method or head start, and lowers coordination cost. The same sharing is disqualifying for a verdict about the world, because agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool; with an anchor that updates on the organization's own reports, everything drifts together coherently and a mirror cannot correct the group. The characteristic failure is a convention promoted into an outcome, which stops being a convention. A single culture score aggregates away the per-slot residual and lets the frame score itself. The remedy is structural: keep a non-updating ground reachable and a seat able to refuse. Culture's valence flips with the residual, so no standing pro-or-anti verdict survives, and no figure returns a numerical optimum.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 2

- **Statement:** correlation has valence. The same high agreement that is efficient for a convention is disqualifying for a verdict, and the question is therefore a union, not one question with opposite answers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" And Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Refuter:** show a shared meaning that lowers coordination cost and is also a verdict about a refutable world with no residual difference between the two roles; or show the two conditions to be a clean partition rather than a union (one lever would then clear the whole problem, which Fig 9 :55-57 forbids for a union).

### C2 - management-facing; checkable against coordination and culture research

- **Statement:** the coordination benefit is real but belongs to the convention domain and is conditional on a method crossing rather than a recording of one occasion. The judgment cost is real but conditional on high evidence overlap plus an updating or absent anchor. Culture's sign flips with the residual, so no standing verdict holds.
- **What to check:** is Sorensen 2002 faithful as read here, namely that "strong culture firms have more reliable or consistent performance in relatively stable environments. In volatile environments, however, the reliability benefits of strong cultures disappear"? Is the Aldag 2022 critique of groupthink correctly characterized, namely that "there is no evidence either that the purported characteristics of groupthink 'hang together' as a group or that these characteristics are most evident in highly cohesive groups"? Are the Asch partner effect and the Condorcet independence condition stated correctly?
- **Figure lines:** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **Refuter:** a measured convention that fails to lower coordination cost; an anchor that updates on the organization's own reports yet still settles the verdict correctly across a change of residual; or an invariant culture valence across a documented habitat change. Any of these breaks C2.

### C3 - measurement and design; checkable by an organization researcher

- **Statement:** a single culture score hides per-slot live residuals and lets the frame score itself; the structural remedy is a non-updating ground plus a seat able to refuse, not a standing list of dissenting personalities.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
- **Refuter:** a single culture score shown to preserve, not hide, a documented per-slot live residual across a change of ground; or a verdict corrected by a frame that can reconstruct the claim and shares all of the authoring frame's evidence, with no non-updating anchor in play.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a verdict that strong culture is good or bad. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that groupthink is a settled causal mechanism. The empirical conflict is recorded, not hidden, and the figure claim is about evidence overlap, not emotional cohesion.
- Not a numerical culture strength, an optimal score, or a universal intervention. This is a declared `gap`: Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" and Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.
- Not that a dissenter is a "good actor" on a standing list. The dissent role is a seat defined by what it can and cannot see (Fig 10 :85).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." Concretely:

1. **Convention test:** a measured coordination task in which a true convention lowers cost and transfer time, or fails to.
2. **Anchor test:** does the reference move when the frames push on it? Fig 2 :29-30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
3. **Independence test:** with two low-overlap frames sharing the code, is there an accuracy surplus where the ground pays? Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
4. **Per-slot test:** does one score preserve a live residual at a decision slot across a change of ground?
5. **Gap:** an instrument that distinguishes a grounded shared code from performed agreement. Owed, not supplied here.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :39-41 and Fig 10 :47. Two short passages carry the union and the failure genus.
2. Check C2 against your own literature: is the Sorensen result stated correctly, and is Aldag's critique a real conflict rather than a straw man?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a convention from a verdict at a decision slot, or say why none is possible. That is the gap.
4. Apply the five refuters in `01` section 4. Report the first one that fires. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
