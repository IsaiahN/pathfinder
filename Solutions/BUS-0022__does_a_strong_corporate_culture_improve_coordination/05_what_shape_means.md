## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** here is the narrowed form of an answer that can be stated before a firm is measured. It constrains; it does not fill. "The question is a union of two conditions with different anchors, with per-slot residuals and a refusing seat" is a shape. "Culture strength should be 4.2 out of 5" is a name, and no figure line licenses it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained for this question

- *Library of Babel* on proximity: "If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of." We started from "shared meaning," "coordination," and "dissent" and let the figure vocabulary (anchor, independence, correlation valence, convention, mirror) replace the near terms.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." That is why "strong culture" is restated as an effect on `R` and on independence, not as a cause.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." Hence the five refuters in `01`: effect descriptions a reader can check, not a theory to believe.
- *Library of Babel* on the bundled question: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually." That is Fig 9 :54-57 applied to the either/or.

## 3. The essay that maps this question most directly

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* is unusually on point because its subject is shared pretext, which is close to what the business literature calls culture:

- "Culture is a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge. Its members can hold very different mindsets; what they share isn't a set of beliefs but the backdrop they read each other against."
- "It also drifts. Communities and institutions that only check against themselves drift together and don't notice (Fig 2 :34)."
- "Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources (Fig 2 :40–41)."
- "High contact builds exactly the shared understanding that makes communication work, but that same factor makes the learner's agreement with the teacher worthless as a check. Two frames that overlap almost completely are 'one frame wearing two names' (Fig 8 :48–49)."

That is F1 and F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` in the author's own prose, derived from the figures rather than from the business literature. It also gives the remedy explicitly: "Three things stop it: A ground that both are corrected by, separately... Separate experience... Claims in effect terms." Those map to D5, to Fig 10 :80 ("Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained"), and to the refuter-first outreach in `02`.

## 4. What licenses a name here

Only two things:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." No firm measurement is in this folder, so nothing here is `grounded`.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Sorensen 2002, Asch, and Condorcet are such frames. They corroborate the shape; they do not name a firm's correct culture strength.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried quote): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." A culture survey declares a primitive; it does not make it neutral.

## 5. Why the word "strong" is the source-naming error

*The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." And: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

A survey score of "strong culture" is a reading, not a source. Several very different arrangements can produce the same score: a genuinely shared and grounded code; a set of rituals performed under pressure; a single dominant frame that everyone echoes; a well-designed measurement instrument. From the score alone the source is not distinguishable. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a better slogan. That is the declared `gap` in `01` section 8.

## 6. Correction to my own earlier reading (kept visible)

I initially wrote the "shape, not a name" rule as if it forbade naming any slot filler in this derivation, including a convention, a shared code, or a refusing seat. That was an overstatement. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Gamma. A convention, a shared meaning and a checker's office are already available: they are bindings or seats, not invented seventh terms, and naming them is provenance rather than an import that moves the wall. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." No atom was added by naming them.

The limit still bites where it should: no figure line names the numerical culture strength, the specific intervention, or the particular content of a shared value that would make a firm correct. Those remain `gap`. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Prestige is not action, and my own audit is a simulation

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*:

- "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."
- "The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."
- "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."

So a well-known culture researcher's endorsement is not evidence; it is a budget to be tried. And `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a test of the claim. It cannot settle anything it reports on. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." The essays share the figures' author and evidence pool completely; their agreement is expected and carries no independent witness weight.
