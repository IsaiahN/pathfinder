## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the amount of investment but **which group's expected return survives the trip from a present action to a future payoff**. In effect terms (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`):

- **b** is a group's belief about whether an investment will be repaid, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is the investment action: plant trees, build terraces, register a title, hold livestock, keep a child in school.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the group's library of routines predicts it will receive.
- **o′** is what actually arrives at harvest, at sale, or at the bank.
- **s** is the group. R is indexed by group and by plot, never averaged into a national investment rate (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

Two options, stated before the derivation:

- **Option A, "a title is a thing that causes investment":** leads to the prediction that investment is a function of legal form alone, and that the same form has the same effect in every habitat.
- **Option B, "investment is a property of investor-and-tenure-habitat":** leads to the prediction that investment depends on a maintained channel to a future return and a reach to a wider set of investments, both of which fail when ownership is precarious (Fig 11 :39, Fig 11 :40).

**What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): a pair of conditions whose conjunction keeps a group's `R` small across a horizon longer than one season, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of the other. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 5 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is **Option B**, kept per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified`. The specific magnitude of the effect, and which institutional arrangement works in which habitat, is a declared `gap`: the framework returns a shape, not a name.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that private title is always the answer.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). The development-economics literature cited here largely shares one evidence pool, so it is one witness, not seven (Fig 2 :31).
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
