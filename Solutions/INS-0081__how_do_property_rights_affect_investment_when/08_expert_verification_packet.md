# Expert verification packet - the Tether on property rights and investment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a property-rights or development economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No group-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Investment is a property of a group and its tenure habitat, not of the group alone. The question is a union of two conditions that must be kept separate: **C1, a maintained channel** from a present investment to a future return, and **C2, a renewed reach** to a wider set of investments. Precarious ownership degrades C1 and confines C2 to short-cycle, movable, quickly recovered assets; secure ownership permits long-horizon, immobile investment. A title or an enforcer is a relation whose valence changes with the residual, so the same institution can protect one group and expose another. Secure and precarious groups are isomers over a shared atom set when both are written fully, differing in the appropriation bond. The framework returns that shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument questions that would close it. It does not name which arrangement works where.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 10 and 7

- **Statement:** the question is a union of two conditions, and the loss under precarious tenure is a loss of channel fidelity, not a decay of the ground. The claim applies to long-horizon, immobile investment, not to effort in general.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
  - Fig 7 :37-39 - *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"*, *"the ground’s pressure on the room"*
- **What to check:** is the union reading faithful to the way tenure security and investment interact in the work you know? Does the scope restriction to long-horizon immobile investment match the cases where security is measured to matter (trees, terraces, founded firms rather than annual labour)?
- **Refuter:** one lever that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue; or a precarious group with both handoff factors measured high and non-declining that still refuses long-horizon immobile investment. Either breaks C1.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against the empirical record

- **Statement:** security of claim, and the power that makes it usable, track long-term investment. The sign is conditional, not universal. The observed post-titling rise in activity is a reading whose source is not identified by the title alone.
- **Sources actually read, with quote:**
  - Timothy Besley, *Property Rights and Investment Incentives: Theory and Evidence from Ghana*, Journal of Political Economy 103(5), 1995, abstract record: *"This paper examines the link between property rights and investment incentives. The author develops three theoretical arguments based on security of tenure, using land as collateral and obtaining gains from trade."*
  - Markus Goldstein and Christopher Udry, *The Profits of Power: Land Rights and Agricultural Investment in Ghana*, Journal of Political Economy 116(6), 2008, abstract record: *"individuals who hold powerful positions in a local political hierarchy have more secure tenure rights and that as a consequence they invest more in land fertility and have substantially higher output"*
  - Daniel Ayalew Ali, Stefan Dercon and Madhur Gautam, *Property rights in a very poor country: tenure insecurity and investment in Ethiopia*, Agricultural Economics 42(1), 2011, abstract record: *"limited perceive transfer rights, and the threat of expropriation, negatively affects the long-term investment in Ethiopian agriculture"*
  - Erica Field, *Entitled to Work: Urban Property Rights and Labor Supply in Peru*, Quarterly Journal of Economics 122(4), 2007, abstract record: *"titling results in a substantial increase in labor hours, a shift in labor supply away from work at home to work in the outside market, and substitution of adult for child labor"*
  - James Fenske, *Land tenure and investment incentives: Evidence from West Africa*, Journal of Development Economics 95(2), 2011, abstract record: *"Self-reported tenure security has been a poor predictor of investment outcomes"*
  - Abhijit Banerjee and Maitreesh Ghatak, *Eviction threats and investment incentives*, Journal of Development Economics 74(2), 2004, abstract record: *"the effect of eviction threats on unobservable investment effort can be positive"*
- **What to check:** are those characterisations faithful? In particular: does the 2008 result identify hierarchy position rather than title form, and does the 2004 result genuinely bound the sign claim?
- **Refuter:** a group whose investment response is invariant across a tenure-habitat substitution that removes the payment for what it invests in; or a title whose measured valence is invariant across a change of residual. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 6, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** a title is a binding onto a held relation, not a new atom; renewal of reach requires contact with a closure that differs; and secure versus precarious is a bond difference over a shared atom set, an isomer when both are written fully.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :25 - *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 12 :123 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** is the bond distinction recoverable from the record, or is it imposed by the derivation? Can a secure and a precarious group actually be written over the same atom set without adding an atom to one side?
- **Refuter:** a group that renews its reach purely by internal composition with no new atom from a differing closure and still passes the ground test across a habitat change; or a documented case where one group requires an atom the other does not hold, which collapses the isomer claim to the functional-group claim. The first breaks C3, the second narrows it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that formal title causes investment, and not that a title alone creates credit. The public record on the Peruvian programme records the counter-observation that *"Contrary to de Soto's claims, the land title project provided no change to the access of credit to poor Peruvians"* (Hernando de Soto, economist, Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23).
- Not that insecurity always lowers effort. The sign is conditional and the derived claim is scoped to long-horizon immobile investment.
- Not that private title is the only secure form. Ostrom's work is recorded as showing that communities can govern common-pool resources *"without either state intervention or markets with private property"* (Elinor Ostrom, Wikipedia, read 2026-11-23).
- Not a national average, not a cross-country rights index, not a `grounded` verdict.
- Not a claim that this derivation settles the question. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Channel test:** a plot-level panel with an independent tenure measure and an independent return measure, showing whether bracket fidelity falls when the claim is contested while ground pressure is held constant.
2. **Reach test:** whether the set of investments a group can compose expands only after contact with a differing closure (credit, court, market), rather than at the moment a document is issued.
3. **Isomer test:** whether a secure and a precarious group can be written over the identical atom set, differing only in the appropriation bond.
4. **Valence test:** whether the same enforcer or title changes sign when the residual changes.
5. **Standing falsifiers:** the six listed in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, in particular invariant investment response across a habitat substitution and a prospectively validated averaged statistic that predicts which named group disinvests.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55, Fig 10 :24 and Fig 7 :37-39 - four short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of the Ghana, Ethiopia, Peru and West Africa results: are those characterisations faithful, and is the hierarchy result really about power rather than form?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a channel failure from a reach failure at the group level - or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
