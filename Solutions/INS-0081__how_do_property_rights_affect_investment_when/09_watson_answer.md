# WATSON answer: property rights and investment under uneven ownership security

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

How do property rights affect investment when ownership is secure for some groups but precarious for others? Restated as an effect: why does `R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|` stay small across decades for some group-slots and widen into a single season for others, under the same technology and prices? `R` is indexed per group and per plot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). The anchor is the market in realized returns and output per plot (Fig 2 :29), not the title and not legal legitimacy. Option A: a title is a thing that causes investment. Option B: investment is a property of group-and-habitat, through two separable conditions. Each leads to a different instrument and a different falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a four-place relation (group Γ, tenure condition, action, return), indexed by slot and ordered by time; carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: "secure" is a threshold relative to a threat (Fig 12 :87). Scale: the single group loop, the population with a shared market, and the time channel across seasons and generations; the membrane is lossy (Fig 4).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict.** Investment responds to ownership security because security keeps the channel between a present action and a future return reachable and enlarges the reach of investments a group can compose. Precarious ownership degrades C1 and confines C2 to short-cycle, movable, quickly recovered assets. The effect is scoped to long-horizon, immobile investment, is indexed per group and plot, and is never averaged. The right and the enforcer are relations whose valence changes with the residual. Secure and precarious groups are isomers over a shared atom set if both are written fully. Which institutional arrangement works in a given habitat is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The world pool corroborates the two-condition shape after re-mapping the title to D2; the low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not the group-level fact.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected policy and misdirected diagnosis. A group failing C2 would be sold channel instruments, or a group failing C1 would be told to import more, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A plot-level instrument that measures bracket fidelity and ground pressure independently would name the channel source; a reach instrument would show whether composability expands only after contact with a differing closure; an isomer test showing one group needs an atom the other lacks would collapse the stronger claim to the functional-group claim; an averaged national statistic validated prospectively per named group would break the indexing rule. Any of the six pre-registered falsifiers would also reverse the verdict. Prestige or consensus is not such an instrument.
