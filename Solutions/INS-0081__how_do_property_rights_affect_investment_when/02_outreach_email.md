# Outreach draft - check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this email does not assert that formal title causes investment. If you add that sentence, the two-stage offer collapses on contact, because the first thing a reviewer will ask is which of two different conditions you actually measured.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on property rights and investment, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** When ownership is secure for one group and precarious for another under the same technology and prices, the framework predicts that the difference is not one variable but two conditions that have to be kept separate:
>
> 1. **A channel to a future return.** A long-horizon investment is a chain of handoffs from what you do now to what you get back later. If the holder cannot count on still owning the plot at harvest, the bracket carrying the expected return across that handoff loses fidelity even while the market keeps paying other people. The market has not stopped paying; the holder has lost touch with the payment.
> 2. **A reach to a wider set of investments.** Secure groups can compose investments that take years to pay and cannot be moved away: trees, terraces, founded firms. Precarious groups are confined to short-cycle, movable, quickly recovered assets. A legal title is a binding onto a right the habitat already recognises, not a new atom that appears when the paper is printed.
>
> The two conditions have different instruments. A failure of the first shows up in a bracket that no longer matches the return; a failure of the second shows up in a reach that stops moving while effort keeps rising. A single lever, for example "issue more titles", clears the region where only one condition fails and leaves the intersection standing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that formal title always causes investment; that insecurity always lowers effort (a credible model shows eviction threats can raise investment effort when investment buys tenure retention); that private title is the only form of security (communities can govern shared resources durably); or that this derivation is a measurement. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which arrangement works in which habitat is an open instrument question, and I mark it as a gap.
>
> **Indexing rule:** the residual is indexed by group and by plot, never averaged into a national investment rate. An average has already destroyed the signal that says which group is losing contact.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. investment response unchanged across a tenure-habitat substitution that removes the habitat's payment for what is invested in;
> 2. one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue;
> 3. a precarious group with bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measured high and non-declining, still refusing long-horizon immobile investment;
> 4. a group renewing its investment reach entirely inside its own closure with no new atom from a differing closure, still passing the ground test across a habitat change;
> 5. a title or enforcer whose measured valence never changes when the residual changes.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. The public figure text with line numbers is at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the local framework; the canonical figures README is at the public repository below.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
