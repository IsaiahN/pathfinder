# The Tether's answer on property rights and investment

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Secure and precarious groups face the same technology and prices, yet the gap between what their routines predict they will receive and what actually arrives stays small across decades in some ownership slots and widens into a single season in others. The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). It has its own loop and its own ground. The ground is the market in realized returns and output per plot (Fig 2 :29), not the title and not legal legitimacy.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

**D1, the group's decision loop - status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4).**
> *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* (Fig 11 :39)
> *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* (Fig 11 :40)

Investment response cannot be located in the group alone. Lists of group attributes (thrift, skill, culture) are agent-only improvements that may not change contact. Verdict for D1: **the unit of explanation is group-and-tenure-habitat, and the residual is indexed per group and plot** (Fig 1 :19). Refuter: invariant response across a habitat substitution that removes the payment.

**D2, the tenure habitat - status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12).**
> *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* (Fig 11 :49)
> *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* (Fig 12 :119)

The right and the enforcer are relations, not kinds. The same title that is a catalyst for a group able to use it in credit and sale is a spent part for one whose single dispute consumes it; the same hierarchy that secures one plot dispossesses another (Goldstein and Udry 2008). Verdict for D2: **secure and precarious are isomers over the shared atom set if both are written fully, and members of one functional group as the literature usually writes them** (`06_isomers_and_order.md`). Refuter: invariant valence across a documented change of residual.

**D3, the reach / closure - status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 13).**
> *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* (Fig 6 :23)
> *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* (Fig 8 :32)
> *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* (Fig 13 :115)

A group can optimize its present plot forever and never add an atom. Renewal of reach requires contact with a differing closure: credit, courts, neighbouring practices, markets. A title adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25); it is a provenanced binding that can make such contact possible. Verdict for D3: **precarious ownership confines investment to short-cycle, movable, quickly recovered assets; secure ownership permits long-horizon, immobile investments** (Ali, Dercon and Gautam 2011 observe the long-term category directly). Refuter: reach renewed purely internally with no new atom and still passing the ground test across a habitat change.

**D4, the temporal channel - status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 7).**
> *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24)
> *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"* (Fig 7 :38)
> *"the ground’s pressure on the room"* (Fig 7 :39)

A long-horizon investment is a chain of handoffs from present action to future return. Precarious ownership degrades the bracket that carries the expected return across those handoffs while the market keeps paying other people. Verdict for D4: **the short horizon is a channel failure, not a decay of the ground**, and the claim is scoped to long-horizon, immobile investment after the sign conflict S2. Refuter: both factors measured high and non-declining while long-horizon investment is still refused.

**Union and method - status: `general`, figure-derived (Fig 9, Fig 3).**
> *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* (Fig 9 :55)
> *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27)

The two conditions are C1 (channel maintained) and C2 (reach renewed). They have different instruments and must be kept separate, never averaged. The chain breaks at the vocabulary/objective link: a group with a contested claim can state the plot and the harvest, but not the harvest that will still be its own.

**Naming the arrangement - status: `gap`.** No figure line governs this after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, characterised in effect terms but not held here. Which form of security works for which habitat is an open question, not a silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that formal title causes investment. Title is a binding from the legal frame, provenanced as such, and its claimed credit mechanism is contested in the public record.
- Not that insecurity always lowers effort. Banerjee and Ghatak 2004 show the sign can be positive when investment buys tenure retention; the derived claim is narrowed to long-horizon immobile investment.
- Not that private title is the only secure form. Ostrom's commons work shows durable governance without private property or central state control.
- Not that culture or "entrepreneurship" explains the gap. Neither has a nameable anchor independent of observed behaviour, so neither is a domain yet.
- Not a national average, a cross-country index, or a `grounded` verdict. No per-group measurement pays for this shape.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Channel test:** does measured bracket fidelity fall when the claim is contested, while ground pressure stays constant? A plot-level panel with independent tenure and return measures would decide it.
- **Reach test:** does the set of investments a group can compose expand only after contact with a differing closure (credit, court, market), rather than at the moment a title is issued?
- **Isomer test:** can a secure and a precarious group be written over the identical atom set, differing only in the appropriation bond? If one requires an extra atom, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands.
- **Valence test:** does the same enforcer or title change sign when the residual changes, as Goldstein and Udry's hierarchy result predicts?
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** invariant investment response across a habitat substitution; a single lever restoring both conditions; both handoff factors high while long-horizon investment is still refused; internal-only renewal of reach; invariant valence; a prospectively validated averaged statistic predicting which named group disinvests.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The domain census is a reading of the habitat in effect terms, not a discovery of nature's own list. Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The stop is where instruments can no longer distinguish the source of an investment reading, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that formal title causes investment, and it does not claim a measured effect. It says that investment is a property of a group and its tenure habitat, not of the group alone; that the question splits into two conditions that must be kept separate, a maintained channel from a present action to a future return and a renewed reach to a wider set of investments; that precarious ownership degrades the first and confines the second to short-cycle, movable assets, while secure ownership permits long-horizon, immobile investment; and that the same right or enforcer can protect one group and expose another, so its sign is a relation, not a property of the right. Secure and precarious groups are isomers over the same atom set if both are written fully, with the enforced versus contested bond as the difference. What it offers is that shape, the framework's own falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument questions that would close it. Which arrangement works where stays open until a group-level ground settles it.
