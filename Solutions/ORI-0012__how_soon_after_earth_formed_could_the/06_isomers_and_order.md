# Order and isomers in the timing of first life

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `gap` on which setting wins and on the date. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after the main audit:

1. Is the interval `t0 -> t1` a genuine sequence, or is the origin a continuum in which the order bond is the wrong bond?
2. Are two proposed settings over the same early-Earth feedstocks **isomers** under Fig 12, or are they something else?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (before choosing a figure line)

- **R.** One interval, ordered in time, compound in conditions, crossing a scale membrane. Arity: one interval over several conditions. Symmetry: ordered, so `->` not `+`. Scale: planetary to molecular.
- **D1 order:** actors are the dependency links (crust, water, habitat, feedstocks, closure, signature); currency is prerequisite satisfaction; anchor is the rock record's U-Pb and Sm-Nd ages.
- **D2 arrangement:** actors are the feedstocks and conditions; currency is which bond composes them; anchor is the experiment or the specimen that pays.
- **D3 classification:** actors are the proposed settings; currency is same atoms versus different atoms; anchor is a figure definition plus a discriminator.
- **Order by dependency:** settle the sequence question first (it governs whether t1 is a point), then the arrangement question (it governs what t1 is made of), then the classification.

Partition or union: D1 is a chain (ordered partition of prerequisites), D2 and D3 are a union (overlapping conditions, Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again").

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is `->` the right bond for the interval?

- Operators table: sequence means "A then B; B needs A to have happened" with the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."
- Earth's formation precedes any environment on Earth, and a cooled crust precedes liquid water at a site. Swapping them yields a different physical claim. The interval is ordered.
- Fig 13 :12: time is "the ordered succession of differences." The interval between t0 and t1 is made of time, so ordering is in the substrate, not imported.
- **But:** Ansari 2026 states the transition from non-living chemistry to biology "was likely a gradual process rather than a discrete event," and the essay *Arrival of the Fittest* (carried mapping) treats selection as a loop rather than an instant. The *boundary* t1 may be fuzzy even though the *interval* is ordered.
- **Resolution:** the sequence bond governs the interval; the fuzziness governs the boundary term. Two different objects, so no contradiction. This is strain S2 in `01`, named not hidden.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a measurement showing that a lineage met its ground at a site before the habitat conditions at that site were present. That breaks the sequence and the answer is withdrawn.

### Q2: The chain has five links, not one

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19 names the links: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Read across to this question:

| link | in planetary terms | status |
|---|---|---|
| perception | the record returns a reading (zircon age, delta-13-C, stromatolite lamination) | present but sparse |
| vocabulary | terms for what a site affords (water, gradient, Concentration, protection) | partly present; no term for source discrimination in deep time |
| objective | what would count as the first lineage (a self-maintaining evolvable closure) | defined operationally but contested at the boundary (F2) |
| planning | a route: the habitability boundary to the biosignature boundary | Pearce et al. supply this bracket |
| learn and carry | the name and the lineage carried into the next problem | the rock record carries the signature but not the derivation |

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The broken link here is between vocabulary and objective: we can list what a site affords and state a definition, but there is no instrument that turns a carbon reading into a source assignment for a 4.1 Ga specimen. Effort spent improving the perception link (more microscope time on the same rocks) cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not").

### Q3: Are the settings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fix the atom set to the early-Earth conditions enumerated in Fig 11 :46: light, water, temperature, season, terrain, plus feedstocks and mineral surfaces held in the chemistry frame.
- A submarine alkaline vent and a volcanic pond over that fixed set use the same atoms and differ in which bond composes them: a sustained gradient and continuous throughput in one case, wet-dry cycling and concentration in the other. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list.
- Ansari 2026: "no single setting fully satisfies all requirements for life's emergence" and "complementary processes operating across interconnected and evolving environments." That corroborates the union, and it leaves the isomer claim as a structural reading rather than a winner.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one setting requires an atom the other cannot have (a genuinely different feedstock or substrate kind), the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism, and this claim fails (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). This is strain S3, live.

### Q4: Single event or continuum is itself an isomer pair

- Over the fixed set {chemistry, compartment, energy use, inheritance, variation, selection}, a single-event origin composes them with a sequence bond (`->`): one after another, one boundary. A continuum origin composes them with conjunction (`+`): "both hold at once," gradual integration.
- Operators table: conjunction is symmetric, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92). So the continuum reading collapses many apparent "orders of appearance" into one term, while the single-event reading charges for the sequence.
- Fig 12 :100: "The price of an arrangement is the choice it records, so it is logarithmic in the number of bonds available." The choice of `+` over `->` is a real recorded choice, not a cosmetic one: it decides whether "first life" is a point or a label on a lineage (Fig 11 :36).
- World status: Ansari 2026 supports the continuum reading; the firm fossil record supports a readable point by 3.5 Ga. Both can be true: the continuum precedes the readable point. That is a union, not a conflict (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").
- **Falsifier:** an instrument-independent, frame-independent sharp discontinuity at t1 would refute the continuum reading and restore the single-event term.

### Q5: What crosses the scale membrane?

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A method that can be reapplied (a source-discrimination protocol) crosses up from the specimen to the field; a head start (an age date, a delta-13-C value) comes down to the next search. A recording of one occasion carried upward "looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion" (Fig 4 :25). So a single famous zircon is a recording, not a method. The method is the protocol that would let 1000 such specimens speak (Bell, quoted in Scientific American 2015, opened).

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Once a closure settles against the ground, it becomes an atom for evolution. The origin is a level crossing: the chemistry that was an operand becomes an atom for the biology that composes over it. That is why "before life" and "after life" can both be about the same atoms at different levels, and why asking for a single atom that marks the boundary is the wrong request.

## CONCLUSION

The interval is ordered (sequence bond), the chain has five dependency links with a broken vocabulary-to-objective link, the proposed settings are isomers over a fixed early-Earth atom set with a live fixed-atom condition, and the single-event versus continuum pair is itself an isomer pair whose bond choice decides whether "first life" is a point or a label on a lineage. None of this returns a year. It returns the arrangement any answer must have, with four written falsifiers.
