# Expert verification packet: the Tether on the timing of first life

**What this is:** a claim in a form a planetary scientist, geochemist or origins researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain structural claims, `general` on the shape of the bound, `gap` on the date. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The interval between Earth's formation and the first life is a dependency-ordered union of conditions, not a single duration with one cause. The earliest possible life is bounded below by the slowest necessary link: a cooled crust, liquid water, an enumerated habitat (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), available feedstocks, and a self-maintaining evolvable closure. Habitability, chemistry, preservation and definition are kept as separate verdicts and never averaged into one year. The proposed settings are isomers over a fixed early-Earth atom set. "First life" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground, and the origin is a level crossing at which a settled closure becomes an atom for evolution. Because a carbon isotope reading cannot comment on its source, the date stays a shape until a source-discriminating instrument fires. The figures return that bracket and its written refuters, not a year.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 3 and 11

- **Statement:** the interval is dependency-ordered, and the habitat is enumerated; a reading of "when life began" taken before the habitability link is not assessable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :22 "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :46 "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"
- **What to check:** is the lower bound really a constraint from prerequisite structure, rather than a numerical coincidence in the literature? Does any proposed setting actually remove the water or cooled-crust requirement?
- **Refuter:** a measurement showing a self-maintaining, evolvable lineage at a site before liquid water and a cooled crust were present there; or a derivation of one of the six substrate terms from the other five.

### C2: geochemistry-facing; checkable against the two carbon studies

- **Statement:** the carbon isotope reading is a projection with lossy information; it cannot assign source for a Hadean or Eoarchean specimen without a discriminator, so the date stays a shape.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Framework essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."
- **What to check:** Bell et al. (2015, PNAS, PMC4664351) report a 4.10 Ga zircon graphite signature "consistent with a biogenic origin" and then list abiotic processes that could produce the same light carbon. Harding et al. (2025, Communications Earth & Environment) find sediment graphite averaging -20.6 per mil and vein graphite averaging -20.4 per mil in the same belt, interpreted as biogenic detritus and abiotic fluid precipitate respectively. Is the lossy-channel claim a fair reading of those two papers?
- **Refuter:** two low-overlap instruments assigning a specimen's carbon unambiguously to a biotic or abiotic source, fixing the local date within error bars. A single new isotope value from the same outcrop is not enough.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a world-facing check

- **Statement:** the settings are isomers over a fixed atom set, and the single-event versus continuum pair is itself an isomer pair; the choice of bond decides whether "first life" is a point or a label on a lineage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground."
- **What to check:** do the compared settings actually use the same feedstocks and conditions? Ansari 2026 (Discover Life) says "no single setting fully satisfies all requirements for life's emergence" and treats the transition as gradual; does that support the union and continuum reading, or does it require a different frame?
- **Refuter:** showing one setting requires an atom the other cannot have, which makes the relation composition plus import rather than isomerism; or an instrument-independent sharp discontinuity at t1, which restores the single-event term.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a year, not a preferred range as a verdict, and not an answer to "when did life actually appear." The 200 to 800 Myr interval (Pearce et al. 2018) is a budget filter.
- Not an early-origin or late-origin conclusion. Both are candidate readings of a sparse record.
- Not that the 4.1 Ga zircon carbon is biogenic, and not that it is abiotic. The reading does not decide the source.
- Not that LUCA (about 4.2 Ga) is the first life. It is a node in a family tree (Marshall 2025).
- Not a winning setting, and not a seventh substrate primitive. Life is a label on a lineage that met its ground.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. Concretely: a population of Hadean carbon specimens measured with two low-overlap protocols; a secular habitability record fixing the first link; a multiply confirmed fossil older than the 3.5 Ga Pilbara anchor; or one of the standing falsifiers in section 2 firing. Until one of these, the verdict stays `candidate`.
