# Solution ORI-0012: How soon after Earth formed could the first life have appeared?

**Question:** How soon after Earth formed could the first life have appeared?

**Source:** `Questions/origins of life.txt` line 19. Section 2, Early Earth: where and when?

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain structural claims, `general` on the shape of the bound, `gap` on the numerical date. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive file states that this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending (set by the registry owner, not by this folder).

## What this folder answers, in effect terms

The residual is an interval, not a date. It runs from Earth's formation (t0, about 4.54 Ga in the world literature) to the first self-maintaining, evolvable lineage (t1). The word "could" makes it a lower-bound question: which preconditions must be in place before t1 is even possible, and what instrument could move the bound from `candidate` to `grounded`. An answer would look like a bracket with a stated anchor and a stated refuter. That bracket is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual (Formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :65).

The figures return the shape of that interval (a dependency-ordered chain, a habitat that is enumerated rather than composed, an anchor that does not update, and a date that stays a shape until a source-discriminating instrument arrives). They return no number. The number is a declared `gap`, which is a verdict and not silence (Fig 13 :134).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. Public institutional and opt-in channels only.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that life appeared early. Selecting for agreement manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- No bulk-send plan. One-to-one only.
- No claim that this derivation settles when life actually appeared. It is a shape and a route to a ground, not a measurement.
