# The Tether's answer: how soon after Earth formed could the first life have appeared?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain structural claims, `general` on the shape of the bound, `gap` on the numerical date. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for an interval between Earth's formation and the first life, read as a lower bound. That interval is a **union compound**, not one scalar with one cause: habitability, prebiotic chemistry, preservation, and the definition of "first" are overlapping conditions with different anchors (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"; Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a single year.

## 2. Verdicts per domain

### D1, planetary habitability: `specified`, triangulated

The earliest possible life cannot precede the links it consumes. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." In the world literature the habitability boundary ranges from 4.5 Ga to 3.9 Ga, and surface waters were below the critical point within 100 million years of formation (Pearce et al. 2018; Lunine 2006). The lower bound is set by the slowest necessary link at a site, not by a single reaction.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a year, and not a narrow range presented as a verdict. The 200 to 800 Myr interval from Pearce et al. is a filter for the budget, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that life appeared early, and not that it appeared late. Both are candidate readings of a contested record.
- Not a winning setting. Vents, ponds, ice, and tidal flats are rooms in a union.
- Not that the carbon in the 4.1 Ga zircon is biogenic, and not that it is abiotic. The reading does not decide the source.
- Not that LUCA is the first life, and not that the six-term substrate is nature's own list. Declaring it makes a choice visible (Fig 11 :43, "The habitat is enumerated, never composed").
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Concretely:

1. **Specimens and protocol.** A population of Hadean carbon-bearing zircons or Eoarchean graphite occurrences measured with two low-overlap methods that assign the source (Bell's own remedy, quoted in Scientific American 2015: "we could start to see patterns in the data to make a much more solid case for Hadean life"). This would close a local gap.
2. **Habitability discriminator.** A secular record showing when a site held liquid water and a cooled crust within error bars, fixing the first link of the chain.
3. **Firm lower boundary.** A fossil or texture accepted by multiple lines of evidence at a date earlier than the current 3.5 Ga Pilbara anchor, which would move the biosignature boundary.
4. **Standing falsifiers.** A lineage before habitability; derivation of one of six substrate terms from the other five; failure of the habitat enumeration; collapse of the union to a single sufficient setting.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say when life first appeared, and it does not pick a winning setting. It says that the interval between Earth's formation and the first life is a dependency chain, so the earliest possible life is set by the slowest necessary link: a cooled crust, liquid water, an enumerated habitat, available feedstocks, and a self-maintaining closure. Habitability, chemistry, preservation, and the very definition of "first" are overlapping conditions, so they get separate verdicts, never an averaged year. The settings over the same early-Earth atoms are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, and the origin is a level crossing at which a settled closure becomes an atom for evolution. The date stays a shape because a carbon isotope reading cannot comment on its source: biogenic and abiotic processes give the same light carbon. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. Four falsifiers were written in advance. The bracket stops where the rocks and instruments stop, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.
