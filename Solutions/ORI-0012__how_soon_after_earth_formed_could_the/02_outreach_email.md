**Subject:** One checkable bracket on the timing of first life, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with an early date. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` structure, `general` shape, `gap` on the number):** the time between Earth forming and the first life is a dependency chain, not a single duration. A self-maintaining, evolvable lineage cannot precede the links it consumes: a cooled crust, liquid water, an enumerated habitat (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), available feedstocks, and a closure that persists. So the earliest possible life is set by the slowest necessary link, not by one lucky reaction. The figures put it plainly: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." And: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."
>
> What I am offering is a **bracket with an order**, not a year. The published literature already has two boundaries: a habitability boundary and a biosignature boundary, with a reported interval of "within 200 Myr or as long as 800 Myr" (Pearce, Tupper, Pudritz and Higgs, 2018). I treat that range as a filter for where to spend a search budget, never as a witness for when life began. The figures return "a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
>
> **The central difficulty, stated before any theory:** a carbon isotope reading cannot comment on its source. Bell and colleagues found graphite in a 4.1 Ga zircon whose signature is "consistent with a biogenic origin," and then listed abiotic processes that could produce the same light carbon. Harding and colleagues showed that within one 3.7 Ga belt the same average reading can come from biogenic detritus or abiotic fluid-precipitated graphite. This is a source-discrimination problem, not an argument. The chapter is: it was settled elsewhere "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."
>
> **What would refute the bracket:**
> 1. a self-maintaining, evolvable lineage shown to exist before liquid water and a cooled crust at that site;
> 2. two independent instruments assigning a Hadean carbon reading unambiguously to a biotic or abiotic source, fixing the date for that specimen;
> 3. a demonstration that the listed habitat conditions are not necessary, or that one of the six substrate terms is derived from the other five;
> 4. evidence that one setting satisfies all conditions and the others demonstrably cannot, which would turn the union into a partition.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the bracket is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
