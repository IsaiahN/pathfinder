## Two findings that bear directly on the question

1. **Continuity is not the same as absence of discrete symbols.** Maley's paper is the careful precedent: analog computation is to be characterised by a kind of representation, and discontinuous analogue computers exist. This is why the audit retracts the naive "no symbols means continuous" reading (strain B in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6).
2. **Finite precision is a real constraint at the ground, and it is not a compositional primitive.** The Diamantini et al. result says every physical computation runs with a finite number of bits. Read with Fig 13 :124-125, this is a constraint on what a measuring instrument can distinguish, not a new seventh term. It is kept as a named strain, so a reader can see where the figure verdict and the physics result touch without one silently flipping the other.

## Wider literature relevant to the field

- Hemery and Fages on continuous chemical reaction networks: continuous molecular species, no tape, no central controller. Corroborates F1 and F2 as a physical instance, not as a figure derivation.
- Cellular automata and continuous spatial automata: local rule applied to the whole grid simultaneously; no executive step. Corroborates F2's distribution claim.
- Swarm intelligence: explicitly *"decentralized, self-organized"*, with no centralized control structure. The strongest low-overlap corroboration for F2.
- Wikipedia "Computation" mapping and mechanistic accounts: the attribution depends on a mapping from physical states to computational states, which is the D3 attribution domain. Its warning about pancomputationalism corroborates F3's demand for an instrument.

None of these sources shares an evidence pool with the figures. Their agreement is therefore weak corroboration of the mechanism shape, not a second witness for the figure verdict.
