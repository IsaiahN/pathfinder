# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the shape claim and its refuter in stage 1. The theory, the figure links, and the analogues in `06_isomers_and_order.md` come later and only on request.

---

**Subject:** One shape claim about computation, with a failure test written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question:** can computation occur without discrete symbols and without a central processor? I treat that as two separate conditions, not one hard problem.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. Composition does not require discrete symbol atoms. Discreteness is described in the framework I am using as something a composition leaves behind, not as one of the primitives. So a process can have a route from input to output without discretised tokens.
> 2. The loop has no privileged executive. The framework forbids a top rung, a last room, and a bottom. A single central processor would be a top room. Control is distributed across frames, each ranging against a ground.
>
> That is a shape, not a claim about any particular physical system. I am not saying which substrate would do it, and I am not claiming a particular analogue computer or slime mould or chemical network is the answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that absence of discrete symbols means continuity. It does not. Analog computation can be discontinuous, and finite physical precision can still force discretisation at a measuring instrument without making discreteness a primitive. I am also not claiming that "computation" is a property read straight off a system. It is better described as a label applied afterwards to a process that met its ground, settled by an instrument rather than by agreement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written before this message):
> 1. Derive discreteness as a seventh primitive, or show no input/output route can be composed without a discrete token.
> 2. Exhibit a legitimate top room that issues verdicts to subordinate frames, or a computation whose mapping is definable only relative to one central state with no frame decomposition.
> 3. Show that "computation" has a nameable, non-instrument anchor, or that two independent frames must agree on the attribution with no ground reading.
> 4. Produce an instrument that measures computation arrival without importing a computational frame first.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the relevant half. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three short sources: the framework's figures and formula, a paper on analog computation and representation, and a physics result on the finite-bit bound for analog systems. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want the method:** I can send the eight-step loop. Please run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
