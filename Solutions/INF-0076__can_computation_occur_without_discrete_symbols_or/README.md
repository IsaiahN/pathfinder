# Solution INF-0076: figure-grounded package

**Question:** Can computation occur without discrete symbols or a central processor?
**Source:** Information Theory.txt line 93
**Section:** 8. Computation and its foundations
**Mode:** `specified` on the shape-level verdicts for both halves of the question; `general` on the structural no-top/no-bottom claim; `gap` on the physical existence claim. Nothing here is `grounded`.
**Status:** done
**Registry status:** pending

## What this package is

A derive-first, simulated WATSON derivation. The figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary corroboration only and never overrule a figure. This package is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on (see `05_what_shape_means.md`, section on simulation status).

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question: (condition 1) no discrete symbols and (condition 2) no central processor. Each condition has its own domain, its own loop, and its own ground. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

## Restating the question as an effect

Decide what an outside frame would count as "computation occurring" in a process P that has no discrete symbol tokens and no single controlling processor. The residual R is the gap between predicting P by its own dynamical rules and predicting P by the attribution "P computes f". R is indexed per process-slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). An answer would look like: a shape any candidate process must have for the attribution to be warranted, plus a stated instrument that would settle the attribution at the ground. That shape is bound as an ordinary term and carries its own refuter (formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reasons in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.

Take `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `02_outreach_email.md` to the public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Ask what would refute the shape claim. Record that verdict before offering any theory. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
