# Isomers and order: supporting derivation for INF-0076

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim has two halves. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are the candidate analogues (continuous chemical networks, cellular automata, swarms) **isomers** or merely members of one functional group, and (2) what does "order" mean in "no central processor", given that the substrate explicitly denies an order of rank.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: space, time, energy (states); spectrum, gradient, network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No seventh atom is available without import. Analogues are bindings of these atoms by different bonds, not new primitives.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are the analogues isomers?

Fig 12 :117, verbatim:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124, verbatim:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Reading the three world analogues over the shared atom set {space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network}:

- **Continuous chemical reaction network:** concentrations over a spatial region, gradient-driven flow, network of reactions. Atoms present: space, energy, gradient, network. The route is a `state → structure → state` composition with gradient and network as the intervening structures.
- **Cellular automaton:** a grid (space), discrete time steps (time), local rule (network of neighbourhood relations). The route is `space → network → time`. No executive cell.
- **Swarm:** agents in space, local interaction (network), directed movement (gradient), time. Route is `space → network → gradient → time`.

Under Fig 12 :117 they share atoms and differ in the bond arrangement, so they are **isomers at the structural level**: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. Their shared `state → structure → state` skeleton is Fig 12 :124's **functional group**: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* This matches the exemplar's handling of GR and QM: weaker claim is the functional group, stronger claim is isomerism over the full shared atom set.

**Falsifier:** show that one analogue requires an atom the others do not (for example, a cellular automaton with no time state, or a chemical network with no network structure). If shown, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands.

### Q2. What order does "no central processor" deny?

Fig 13 :4-5, verbatim:

> **"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."**

Fig 10 :68: *"That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not."* Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room"*

"Central processor" in this frame set is a **rank claim**: one member senior to the others, a top room that issues verdicts. What Fig 13 denies is rank, not sequence. The ordered bond `→` remains legal inside a route: Operators table :34 *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"* and Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

So the derivation distinguishes two different orders:

1. **Intra-route order** (`→`, sequence): required for a computation, because a computation is a route from input to output. A vocabulary with only `+` can name a state and cannot name a route (Fig 12 :111; Operators :16 *"the only bond in a flat formula, which is why a formula records ingredients and not arrangement"*).
2. **Inter-level rank** (a top rung): forbidden. There is no central state that stands above the frames and issues verdicts.

Collapsing these two is the error the question invites. A process can have a strictly ordered route and still have no central processor.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a figure-sanctioned rank ordering among frames, or show that an ordered bond requires an executive state to fire. If either holds, Q2 fails.

### Q3. Does a purely symmetric vocabulary suffice for computation?

Fig 12 :92, verbatim:

> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**

Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92-93). A conjunction-only vocabulary collapses every route into one term: it can say the input and the output both hold, and cannot say which came first. The Operators table :57-58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. Without negation and without ordered bonds, a system can accumulate states and cannot carry a correction or a route.

**Reading:** a minimum vocabulary for computation needs at least one ordered bond (sequence or production) and one unary refusal (negation). It does not need a discrete symbol atom (Fig 13 :124-125) and it does not need a top rung (Fig 10 :68).

**Falsifier:** a computational process fully described by a symmetric-only, negation-free vocabulary that still supports a route and a correction. If produced, Q3 fails.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate analogues are structural isomers over the shared six-term atom set, with a common functional group `state → structure → state`. "No central processor" denies **rank**, not **sequence**: ordered routes are required, a top rung is not. A minimum computational vocabulary needs an ordered bond and a refusal operator, not a discrete atom and not an executive state. This rescues what is checkable in the question without importing a seventh term.
