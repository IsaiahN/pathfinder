## What in the wider literature bears directly on this question

1. **The anchor is occupational, not clinical.** WHO defines burn-out in the occupational context. That supports treating it as a contact residual (Fig 11 :39-40) rather than a free-floating individual disposition. It does not tell us which intervention closes it.
2. **The field genuinely disagrees about level.** Panagioti (organization-directed larger) and Bes (workload-focused organizational interventions largest) point one way. Dreison (person-directed better for emotional exhaustion) and Kiratipaisarl (individual small, organizational not significant) point another. Four syntheses, one broad healthcare evidence pool, different slots and outcomes. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* The disagreement is returned to the domain census in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §F6; it does not flip the verdict.
3. **Measurement is the channel, not the ground.** Kiratipaisarl reports that all included studies were at high risk of bias, *"primarily stemmed from the fourth domain, concerning subjective participant-reported outcomes without blinding"*. Bes grades the overall evidence very low. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.
4. **JD-R gives the vocabulary of the bond, not the figure's verdict.** Bakker and de Vries describe job demands, job resources, recovery and self-regulation, and state that *"when job strain increases, employees are less likely to use adaptive self-regulation strategies, such as job stress recovery and job crafting"*. This corroborates the union-compound reading (Fig 9 :55-57) and the idea that resources and relations are part of the habitat (Fig 11 :43-48). It is a secondary source from a field that shares the question, not an independent witness for the framework's rule.
5. **Workload interventions appear in the organizational results.** Bes reports a reduction in exhaustion for workload-focused interventions. That is outside the question's constraint (workload held fixed), so it is recorded as a boundary of the evidence, not as an answer to this question. Combined interventions also did better than organizational ones. The question forbids the first operand change; the literature keeps finding it effective.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Stage-1 verdicts are recorded before any theory is shown and are never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and label the mode of what they get.
