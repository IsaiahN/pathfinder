## What is being decided, in effect terms

An organization observes burnout in some worker-slots: a gap between what the work system predicts it can sustain and what people actually sustain (exhaustion, cynicism, reduced efficacy). Two operands are declared fixed: the workload (the demand), and the underlying design of jobs (the formal arrangement of tasks). The question asks whether a third lever exists, one that lowers the residual while leaving both declared operands untouched. The answers diverge as follows. If the residual is generated by the contact between worker and job, an agent-only lever (resilience training, wellness apps, coping skills) cannot close it; only a change of contact can. If the lever changes the relation between the same operands while leaving their formal description unchanged, the change is real but it is a change of contact, so the "without changing job design" condition holds only as a notation claim. If the residual is concentrated in a worker-internal slot, the answer can be yes for that slot, and the per-slot census decides where.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words.

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies their agreement.
2. **A shared pool is one witness.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). The burnout meta-analyses read here largely re-analyse the same healthcare intervention studies.
3. **Do not hand the recipient the interiors.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and institutional submission routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
