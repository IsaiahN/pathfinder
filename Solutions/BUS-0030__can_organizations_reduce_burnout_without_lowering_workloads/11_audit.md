## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census from the question line before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the per-slot gap between predicted and observed sustainable effort, with burnout's three observed dimensions. Arity: at least three-place, worker plus demand plus resources and relations, indexed by worker, role, shift or team. Symmetry: none. Effort is spent in one direction, so swapping operands changes the thing. Scale: one worker loop, a team or organizational habitat, a lossy membrane between. Union, not partition: a demand condition, a design condition, and social and recovery relations (Fig 9 :55-57).
- **D1 Worker (agent).** Actors: capacity, coping, self-regulation, recovery. Currency: finite effort. Anchor: an outcome not produced by the intervention, such as turnover, sickness absence or functional capacity. Admitted.
- **D2 Job habitat (contact).** Actors: tasks, schedule, supervisor, colleagues, tools, feedback, autonomy in practice, meaning. Currency: demands and resources. Anchor: workload records and observed outcomes. Admitted.
- **D3 Measurement and verification.** Actors: HR, managers, survey instruments, meta-analysts. Currency: scores, averages, agreement. Anchor: the ground, which must not be a quantity this frame also produces. Admitted, usually unanchored.
- **X1 Free-floating individual disposition.** No nameable anchor once the occupational context is removed. Not a domain; WHO's definition excludes it.
- **X2 Wellness app or course as a fourth operand.** Already inside D1 and D3. Absorbed.

Dependency order: enumerate live slots, find a vocabulary for what the job affords, state a grounded objective, then choose a lever, then carry the method (Fig 3 :4-15). The break is usually at the objective, because the score is the same instrument the intervention changes (Fig 1 :27).

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires that it be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39-40 (agent and habitat), Fig 9 :55-57 (union), Fig 12 :5 and :94 (bond), Fig 1 :19 (per slot), Fig 2 :29 (anchor).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `01` and `07`: "improve the person, not the job, cannot work; change the relation around the same work and it can; one lever leaves a residue."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a practitioner's likely restatement: "send everyone to resilience training but keep workloads and job design as they are."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **contact clause**: if the relation change is real, contact changed, so the "not the job" part of the slogan is false in effect (Fig 11 :40);
- the **union clause**: a single relation change leaves an intersection where demand and design both bind (Fig 9 :56-57);
- the **per-slot clause**: an organization-wide average can move while a shift or role keeps a live residual (Fig 1 :19, :22).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: burnout is a contact residual, and an agent-only improvement does not close a contact-generated residual. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
