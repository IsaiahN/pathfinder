# Supporting derivation: union, bond and order for the burnout question

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the structural rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question forbids two changes (lower workload, redesign jobs) and asks whether a third lever closes the burnout residual. Three supporting questions are owed: (1) is the residual one condition or a union, (2) can the lever be a change of bond rather than a change of operand, and (3) in what dependency order must the levers be tried.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: worker capacity (finite energy), demand, resource, task structure, and relations of support, feedback, predictability, meaning and recovery. Bonds held: sequence (→), comparison (⋛), subtraction (−), conjunction (+). No new primitive is available without import, and none is claimed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is this a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- The conditions here are not separable. A high-demand job with high control and a high-demand job with low control produce different residuals at the same workload. Reducing demand (forbidden by the question) and changing control (partly forbidden, depending on the reading of "design") are different levers on different regions.
- ⇒ The residual is a **union compound**. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single lever clears the demand-and-control intersection with no categorically different residue, the union claim fails and the problem is a partition after all.

Status: triangulated for the rule (Fig 9 and Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*), `specified` for the application.

### Q2 — Is the lever a bond change or an operand change?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Holding W and D fixed while changing supervisor support, feedback, predictability, autonomy in practice, meaning or recovery changes the **arrangement**, not the declared operands. Under Fig 12 that is a different substance.
- But Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* If the bond change is real, contact changed. If contact did not change, the bond change was cosmetic and R does not move.
- ⇒ The narrow "yes" is an **isomer move**: same declared operands, different bond, different effect. It is not an agent-only improvement. Calling it "no change to job design" is a claim about the job description, not about contact.

**Analogue, checked not borrowed.** Chemistry's isomerism (silver fulminate and silver cyanate, Liebig and Wöhler 1824 to 1826; named by Berzelius 1830, cited in Fig 12 :97-98) is the case where the same ingredients under a different arrangement produce different behaviour. That is a structure match, not evidence about organizations. The source is in the figure, so it is part of the framework's own citation, not a world witness for the verdict.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if changing those relations leaves the worker-job bond identical in effect, the isomer move fails; if it moves R, then contact changed and the "without changing anything" part of the question is false in effect.

Status: triangulated (Fig 12 bond definition and Fig 11 habitat enumeration), `specified`.

### Q3 — What is the dependency order of the levers?

Fig 3 :4-15 names five links: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The chain is ordered by dependency, and Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Applied here:

1. **Perception:** which slot carries live positive residual? Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
2. **Vocabulary:** what does this job afford, in the worker's terms? This is Fig 3 :7's "what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". It is the link most often missing. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
3. **Objective:** what would count as less burnout, settled by an outcome anchor not produced by the intervention? Fig 2 :29.
4. **Planning:** which lever, tried on which slot?
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry the method outward, not the recording (Fig 4 :22).

The usual break is at link 3: the objective is a self-report the intervention also changes. Effort then lands at link 4 (another wellness programme) and cannot rescue the break. Fig 3 :29-30: *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."*

**Falsifier:** if an organization shows a verified objective anchor at link 3, enumerates live slots at link 1, and still fails after a correctly aimed lever at link 4, the dependency-order claim is not supported for that case and the break must be re-located.

Status: triangulated (Fig 3 dependency order and Fig 1 per-slot rule), `specified`.

### Q4 — Which function does each candidate relation have?

Fig 12 :119 defines a catalyst: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :123 defines a functional group: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- Recovery opportunities and sleep are **spendable and regenerating**: they behave like the gradient (Fig 13 :22, *"a difference that can be spent"*) and must be distinguished from the one-off perk. A "wellness Wednesday" is a recording; a protected recovery period that regenerates is a different term.
- Supervisor support and feedback are **functional groups**: their presence fixes whether a demand is survivable. The qualifier (which supervisor, which team) says which member.
- These are candidate classifications, `specified` and unmeasured. No figure line selects which relation is the catalyst and which is the functional group for a given slot. That is the recorded `gap` in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F7.

**Falsifier:** an outcome measurement showing that a candidate classified as regenerating is spent once, or that a candidate classified as a functional group does not fix the outcome across slots.

Status: single-source for the definitions (Fig 12), `gap` for the bindings.

### Q5 — Cross-field analogue: habitat and effective sample size

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

Ecology's habitat concept is an analogue, not a source for the verdict. The framework itself cites Carson (1962) for the cascade and Jones, Lawton and Shachak (1994) for ecosystem engineering (Fig 11 :54, :65). The relevant structure here: a second-order effect three steps out (a staffing change affecting supervisor load, affecting feedback, affecting exhaustion) is invisible to first-order enumeration. That is the Fig 11 :52 point, *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

Survey statistics supplies the second analogue. Fig 2 :44: *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."* Four burnout meta-analyses that re-use the same trials are not four witnesses. This is a filter on how much weight to give, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

**Falsifier:** a cascade measured to stop at first order with no second-order residue, or a set of syntheses shown to draw on genuinely disjoint trial pools.

Status: single-source per analogue, corroborating structure only.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union compound, so one lever leaves a categorically different intersection (Q1). The only lever compatible with holding the two declared operands fixed is a bond change, and a bond change is a contact change, so the "yes" is real but is not an agent-only fix (Q2). The levers must be tried in dependency order, with the objective anchor before the programme, or effort lands above the break (Q3). Candidate relations can be typed as spendable-regenerating or functional-group, but the figure set does not select which one closes which slot (Q4). Ecology and survey statistics corroborate the cascade and correlation structure, not the organizational fact (Q5).

Which specific bond closes which specific slot remains a `gap`. That verdict is stated, not filled.
