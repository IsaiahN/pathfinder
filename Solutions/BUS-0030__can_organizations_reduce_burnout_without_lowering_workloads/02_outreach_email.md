**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the wording below has survived the check in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. The output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one claim about burnout and job design before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know from occupational health and job design research. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Burnout is defined by WHO as an occupational phenomenon resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. Treating it as a residual, it is indexed per worker, role, shift or team, not per organization. The framework I am testing states that capability is a property of agent and habitat together, never of the agent alone, and that an improvement which does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves.
>
> Applied here: if the residual is generated by the contact between the worker and the job, then a lever that changes only the worker (resilience training, a meditation app, a wellness lecture) cannot close it while workload and the formal design of the job stay fixed. What can close it is a change in the relation around the same work: supervisor support, feedback, peer relation, predictability, autonomy in practice, meaning, recovery opportunity. That is a real change of contact, so it is not an agent-only fix. Calling it "the same job" is a statement about the job description, not about the worker's experience.
>
> I also expect burnout to be a union of conditions, not one condition. A single lever clears the cases where only one condition binds, and leaves the cases where demand and design bind together. That residue is categorically different, not a harder case of either.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that individual interventions have no measurable effect, nor that my effect sizes are right. I am not claiming to know which relation closes which slot: that is a `gap` in what I hold, and I record it as a verdict rather than fill it with a favourite programme. I am not claiming that the framework settles the empirical question. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. A controlled trial with no change in workload, formal job design, supervisor behaviour, colleagues, schedule, tools, feedback, recovery or meaning, which produces a sustained clinically meaningful fall in per-slot burnout against an outcome anchor not administered by the intervention team.
> 2. A per-slot census showing the live residual concentrated in a worker-internal slot untouched by the job habitat.
> 3. A reasoned case that supervisor support, feedback and autonomy in practice are part of "the underlying design of jobs" under the ordinary meaning of that phrase, so that holding design fixed forbids the lever.
> 4. A single lever that clears the demand-and-design intersection with no categorically different residue.
> 5. An organization-wide score that moves on an outcome anchor while no slot-level residual remains anywhere.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. Which of them would you expect to fire first?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The WHO definition of burn-out, for the anchor: https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Claim first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Refuter before conclusion | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. It also keeps the recipient from rebuilding the claim to pass its own test. |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`, *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Per-slot before average | Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* |
| No claim to know the lever | Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The `gap` is recorded, not filled. |
| Relation named as contact change, not agent fix | Fig 12 :94, *"The arrangement is the substance."* If the bond changes, contact changes. |
| Meta-analytic disagreement not cited as a verdict | Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* One healthcare evidence pool, several readings. |
| Outcome anchor requested | Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: prestige can fund a trial but cannot settle it. The ask gives the claim a budget to be checked; it does not ask for endorsement. |
| Stage 2 opt-in only | Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A verdict recorded after reading the theory is no longer an outside verdict. |
| Stage 3 sends a method | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, not an answer. |

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the claim and its five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the answer before showing anything else.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter, names their own ground and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition repeated:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. The five refuters are live. Sending a recording instead of a generator is the failure Fig 4 :25 names: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
