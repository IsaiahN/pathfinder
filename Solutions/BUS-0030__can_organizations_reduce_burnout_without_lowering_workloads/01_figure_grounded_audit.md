## 0. The residual under audit

> Can organizations reduce burnout without lowering workloads or changing the underlying design of jobs?

Stated in effect terms, not in the vocabulary of the question:

- **R, the residual.** For worker-slot s, R_s is the gap between what the work system predicts it can sustain (Γ(b,a) for a given belief and assignment) and what actually arrives (o'): exhaustion, mental distance or cynicism, reduced professional efficacy. WHO defines burn-out as *"a syndrome conceptualized as resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed"* and adds *"Burn-out refers specifically to phenomena in the occupational context and should not be applied to describe experiences in other areas of life."* That is a contact residual, not a free-floating disposition. The anchor is an outcome (sickness absence, turnover, functional capacity, a clinically interpreted instrument), never the organization's own sense that a programme worked.
- **The two fixed operands.** Workload W (the demand operand) and job design D (the formal arrangement operand). The question does not ask to lower W or to re-specify D. It asks whether a lever φ exists in closure(Γ) such that R falls while W and D stay as they are.
- **What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** An answer is a triple (lever, contact changed or not, slot on which R falls). Binding it makes the sought-for shape refusable. Three candidate shapes: (A) a pure agent lever, no contact change, R falls; (B) a relation lever, contact change, formal W and D unchanged, R falls; (C) no lever in closure(Γ) closes R under the constraints, so the answer is gap or unreachable at this budget. The refuter in §3 is written against these bindings before the conclusion.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

R is described by arity, symmetry and scale.

- **Arity.** At least three-place: worker, demand, and the resources and relations available to meet it. R is indexed by slot (worker, role, team, shift), never by organizational average (Fig 1 :19). The union of conditions is at least two: a demand condition and a design or resource condition; in practice a third, social and recovery relations (Fig 9 :55-57).
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. Effort is spent in one direction (energy is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*, Fig 13 :14; a gradient is *"a difference that can be spent"*, Fig 13 :22). The relation between worker and demand is ordered, so swapping the operands changes the thing. That rules out a symmetric-join reading of the lever.
- **Scale.** One worker loop, a team or organizational habitat with a shared library, and a lossy membrane between them (Fig 4 :19-20).

One line per domain, with actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not admitted.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The worker (agent) | capacity, coping, self-regulation, sleep and recovery | finite effort and energy | clinical or functional outcome, independent of the intervention's own survey | admitted |
| D2 The job habitat (contact) | tasks, schedule, supervisor, colleagues, tools, feedback, autonomy in practice, meaning | demands and resources | workload records, job-design instruments, observed turnover and absence | admitted |
| D3 The measurement and verification frame | HR, managers, survey instruments | agreement, averages, scores | the ground, which must not be a quantity this frame also produces | admitted, currently often unanchored |
| X1 Burnout as a free-floating individual disposition | the person alone | trait | none nameable once the occupational context is removed | not a domain, excluded by the WHO definition |
| X2 "Wellness solves it" as a fourth operand | app, course, perk | participation | already inside D1 and D3 | absorbed, no separate loop |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 and D2 overlap in the worker's recovery and self-regulation, which are in contact with the job; D2 and D3 overlap because the same managers who design the work often administer the survey. A single lever clears only the region where the other conditions do not hold (Fig 9 :55-57). Each overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a harder instance of one condition.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception of which slot is live, then vocabulary for what the job affords, then an objective stated in grounded terms, then a lever, then learning and carrying. The chain is ordered by dependency, and effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20-21). In this field the break is usually at the objective: "burnout went down" is read off the same survey the intervention also changes, so effort lands on the lever (another wellness programme) rather than on the objective. Fig 3 :29-30 names that exact bias: *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."*

**Strain test applied before concluding.** A first reading tried to make Fig 13 :74 govern the question (composition across two kinds). It strains: workload and job design are not the six substrate terms. Re-census sends that line to a different quantity (the substrate, not organizational levers) and leaves it out of the verdict. The lines that do not strain after re-census are Fig 11 (capability is agent and habitat), Fig 9 (union compound), Fig 12 (bond carries information the operands do not), Fig 1 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 and Fig 10 (anchor and verifier) and Fig 3 (dependency order).

## 3. Findings

### F1 — An agent-only lever cannot close a contact-generated residual (decisive for D1)

Fig 11 :39-40, verbatim:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

WHO's definition makes burnout constitutive of an occupational context. It is a property of worker-and-job, not of worker alone. A resilience course, a meditation app, or a wellness lecture that leaves tasks, schedule, supervisor, colleagues, tools, feedback and recovery opportunities exactly as they were is an agent-only improvement. By Fig 11 :40 it changes nothing in R, however much it improves the person on some other axis. Fig 4 :19-20 triangulates at a different scale: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change"* when size changes, so moving from the worker loop to the organizational habitat does not let the worker alone carry the capability.

**Status: single-source for the application** (Fig 11 governs the exact claim; Fig 4 governs scale and is not an independent witness for the same quantity). **Mode: `specified`.** Refuter: a controlled trial in which a pure agent intervention, with no contact change of any kind, produces a sustained clinically meaningful fall in R against an outcome anchor. If that fires, this finding is withdrawn.

**Important boundary.** This does not say individual interventions never help. It says that when they help, something about contact changed, or the live slot was worker-internal and in contact with the job (sleep, recovery). The world literature in §8 confirms the ambiguity rather than settling it.

### F2 — Burnout is a union compound, so one lever leaves the intersections standing (D1 x D2)

Fig 9 :55-57, verbatim:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The two declared-fixed operands are not two independent questions. High demand with high control is a different compound from high demand with low control; the same workload can be survivable in one relation and corrosive in another. A lever that acts only on the person clears the cases where the job conditions were not binding. The cases where both bind are not a harder version of either; they are a specific compound and need a specific loop.

Fig 3 :19 triangulates from a different figure and a different quantity: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Adding coping skills to a slot whose break is in the job's affordance or objective link is effort on a link that already works.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 union split and Fig 3 dependency order, low overlap between them). **Mode: `specified`.** Refuter: a single lever that clears both the demand-only and the demand-and-design intersection with no categorically different residue.

### F3 — The narrow "yes": change the bond, not the operands (D2)

Fig 12 :5 and :94, verbatim:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 11 :43-48 requires the habitat to be enumerated, not composed: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* Actors, conditions and relations are all in the habitat. Supervisor behaviour, peer support, feedback, predictability, autonomy in practice and the meaning of the task are relations between the same worker and the same formal job. Changing them changes the bond while leaving the formal description of W and D intact. That is a real lever.

But Fig 12 :117 refuses the comfort: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If the bond changes, the substance changes. Saying "the job design did not change" is then a notation claim about the formal job description, not an effect claim about the worker's contact. Fig 11 :40 still governs: if R falls, contact changed.

**Strain, named and sent back to census.** The phrase "underlying design of jobs" is ambiguous between (a) the formal task structure and (b) the full relation between worker and work. Under (a) the narrow yes is available. Under (b) it is not, because changing the bond is changing the design. Re-census under the rule that a strained reading governs another quantity yields: D2 contains both the formal arrangement and the relations; "design" in the question is read narrowly as the formal arrangement; the relations are part of the habitat and therefore fair game. The strain is definitional, not a figure conflict. It is recorded here and in `11_audit.md` rather than smoothed over.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 bond definition and Fig 11 habitat enumeration, distinct figures and quantities). **Mode: `specified`.** Refuter: show that supervisor support, feedback and autonomy in practice are not in contact with R, or that changing them leaves the worker-job bond identical in effect.

### F4 — The residual is per slot; the averaged score is the named error (D1, D3)

Fig 1 :19 and :22, verbatim:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

An organization-wide burnout score can improve while the overloaded team, the night shift, or a single role keeps a live positive residual. A single programme justified by the average is the exact failure Fig 1 names. The operational instruction is to enumerate slots first and only then choose a lever, which is also Fig 11 :43's *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*

**Status: single-source** (Fig 1 governs the slot rule directly; Fig 11 corroborates enumeration but at a different quantity). **Mode: `general` for the rule, `specified` for organizations.** Refuter: a case where the aggregate moves and no slot-level residual remains anywhere, measured on the same instrument.

### F5 — The anchor must not be a quantity the intervening frame also produces (D3)

Fig 2 :29 and Fig 1 :27, verbatim:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Most intervention studies in this field measure burnout with a self-report inventory administered by the same organization or research team that delivered the intervention. Kiratipaisarl et al. (2024) record that all included studies were at high risk of bias, *"primarily stemmed from the fourth domain, concerning subjective participant-reported outcomes without blinding"*. That is a channel failure, not a verdict: Fig 10 :24 says *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, and Fig 10 :26 lists *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. The output is then *"you have lost touch"*, not an outcome.

Fig 2 :37-38 adds the legitimate role of human judgment: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* A person's own account is the right anchor for "is this working for you"; a questionnaire is an instrument for reading it, and the instrument can be wrong on axes it does not measure.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 1 frame-internal rule, plus Fig 10 channel rule). **Mode: `specified`.** Refuter: an outcome that moves with the intervention and is demonstrably not produced or interpreted by the intervening frame.

### F6 — The world conflict returns to the census; it does not flip the verdict (D3)

Four syntheses read here disagree about which level works better. Panagioti et al. (2017) find organization-directed effects larger than physician-directed ones. Dreison et al. (2018) find person-directed interventions more effective on emotional exhaustion. Bes et al. (2023) find workload-focused organizational interventions most effective. Kiratipaisarl et al. (2024) find small effects for individual interventions and no significant organizational effects. All four are healthcare-burnout syntheses drawing on overlapping trial pools, different outcome versions and different inclusion rules.

The figures forbid treating this as four witnesses. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Conflict rule applied: return to the census. The re-census finds the disagreement is about **which slot and which outcome**, not about the agent-habitat shape. Panagioti and Bes aggregate across exhaustion with controlled designs; Dreison separates emotional exhaustion in mental health staff; Kiratipaisarl restricts to residents with high risk of bias. Different slots, different anchors, different compounds (Fig 9 :57). The figure verdict is unchanged. Agreement or disagreement among the syntheses carries zero verdict weight.

**Status: triangulated as a method rule** (Fig 7 and Fig 2). **Mode: `general`.** Refuter: a synthesis with a genuinely independent evidence pool and a non-self-report outcome that reverses the per-slot shape, not merely the pooled effect size.

### F7 — Which specific bond closes which slot is a `gap`, not a hidden answer (D2)

After a genuine re-census, no figure line names the intervention that closes a given slot. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument: a per-slot enumeration plus an outcome anchor, not another framework injunction. Six inward sharpenings of the same self-report cannot manufacture an atom (Fig 8 :32, *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*).

**Status: `gap` after re-census.** Mode: `general`. Refuter: a figure line that names a specific organizational lever for a specific slot, produced by re-census without straining.

### F8 — Only methods cross outward; the refuter travels, the framework does not

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* A first contact that sends the full framework before asking for a verdict converts an outside frame into an inside one. The legitimate offer is the shape, the refuter and the request for a failure (Fig 2 :35, *"A mirror cannot correct you"*).

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 4 membrane direction and Fig 10 verifier rule). Refuter: a case where showing the interior increases rather than spends the checker's independence, with a measurement that demonstrates it.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device.

The shape claim (agent-only cannot close a contact-generated residual; a bond change can, but is a contact change; one lever leaves union intersections standing) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Agent-only fires.** A randomized controlled trial with no change in workload, formal job design, supervisor behaviour, colleagues, schedule, tools, feedback, recovery opportunity or meaning produces a sustained, clinically meaningful fall in per-slot R against an outcome anchor not administered or interpreted by the intervention team. This directly refutes Fig 11 :40 as applied here.
2. **The live slot is non-contact.** A per-slot census shows the residual concentrated in a worker-internal slot that is demonstrably untouched by the job habitat, so improving the agent in isolation changes the very quantity that generates R. This would re-census D1 as the generating habitat and move the verdict.
3. **Bond and design are the same atom.** A re-census shows that supervisor support, feedback, autonomy in practice and recovery are part of "the underlying design of jobs" under the question's own terms. Then F3's narrow yes collapses and the answer becomes no.
4. **The union is a partition.** A single lever clears the demand-and-design intersection with no categorically different residue, refuting Fig 9 :55-57.
5. **The aggregate is not the averaging error.** The organization-wide score moves with no live per-slot residual anywhere and on an outcome anchor, refuting Fig 1 :22.
6. **An independent pool reverses the shape.** A synthesis drawing on trials with no overlap with the healthcare pool, using outcome anchors, finds that pure agent-only levers close contact-generated burnout. Agreement from the same pool does not count (Fig 2 :40).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed, and the retraction is recorded here rather than erased.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not sell agent-only wellbeing as burnout reduction while W and D are fixed. If a programme changes only the person, it is an improvement on another axis, not a closure of R (F1).
2. Enumerate slots before choosing a lever (F4). An average is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
3. If the constraint is "formal job description unchanged", name the real lever as a **bond change**: supervisor support, feedback, peer relation, predictability, autonomy in practice, meaning. State honestly that this changes contact, so it is not an agent-only fix (F3).
4. State the objective in grounded terms before the lever. Use outcome anchors and keep the measuring frame separate from the intervening frame (F5).
5. Do not quote the meta-analytic disagreement as if four teams independently found four facts. It is one pool read four ways (F6).
6. Record the `gap` for which bond closes which slot. Send the generator, the shape and the refuter, not a conclusion (F7, F8).

## 7. Verdict summary

- **D1 worker alone:** no, for a contact-generated residual. `specified`, single-source (Fig 11 :39-40).
- **D2 relation and habitat:** yes, but only as a contact change; "job design unchanged" survives only if design is read as the formal description. `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 :5, :94 and Fig 11 :43-48).
- **D3 measurement:** no verdict without an outcome anchor and a separate measuring frame. `gap` for which bond closes which slot.
- **Not averaged.** These three verdicts stay separate. The practical answer is that organizations can reduce burnout without lowering workload or formally redesigning jobs only by changing the relations around the work, and that change is a real change of contact, not an agent-only improvement.

No measurement was taken. This is a simulated derivation. It cannot settle the empirical question.
