**Subject:** Check one checkable claim about hybrid AI architecture before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about combining neural learning with symbolic reasoning, search, memory and tools. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is a union of overlapping conditions, not one architecture to be named. Fig 9 says *"Split it rather than search"* and *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The five parts are ingredients; the answer is the typed bonds among them. From the figures it follows that:
>
> 1. the ground is the only metric, so a symbolic self-check by the same system that produced the neural output is one frame grading itself, not a verdict;
> 2. the residual stays indexed per object slot, so one aggregate benchmark score has thrown away the signal that says where to look;
> 3. search visits more of the same closure and never adds an atom on its own, so independent searchers and an import are what move a wall;
> 4. a memory that grows is not a reach that grows, and a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate, so the symbolic part must be able to refuse;
> 5. tools are imports and instruments that extend contact, and capability is a property of agent and habitat, not of the agent alone.
>
> I am explicitly **not** claiming that any named architecture wins, that this is a theory of everything, or that the framework has settled which bond to put in which slot. It returns a shape, not a name. The slot stays open until a measurement settles it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or admit a same-kind bond that cannot be refused;
> 2. swap a bond type between two components and show every per-slot residual and probe outcome unchanged;
> 3. move the ground with an agent-only change while habitat and contact are held fixed;
> 4. grow a library and its reach with no new atom and no refusal, or add an atom by search inside a closed set;
> 5. restore all five conditions with one lever and no categorically different residue.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding finding. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1 is just this claim and these refuters. Please record what you would count as a failure before reading any theory.** Your verdict on this stage is kept separate and is never pooled as corroboration later.
>
> **If it survives, and only then, here are the sources.** The figures are the authority, not my summary of them:
> - The figures and formula, Figures 1 to 13 plus the Operators and Symbols tables (local extract with line numbers): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`

> - AlphaGo, Silver et al., Nature 529, 2016: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16961
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery, stage 3 is opt-in:** the eight-step formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. `THE_FORMULA.md` says a loop *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
