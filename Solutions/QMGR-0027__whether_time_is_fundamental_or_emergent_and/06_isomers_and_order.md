# Atoms, clocks and order: a supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves two questions open. (1) If time is a substrate atom, what exactly is a clock in framework terms? (2) Are different emergent-time programs (thermal clock, entanglement clock, discrete update step) the same kind of thing under the figures, or different kinds? This file derives the structure, the order and the analogues needed to answer those, and states a falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy as states; Gradient, Network, Spectrum as structures; plus the seven bonds and negation. No new primitive is available without import.

- **D1 substrate.** Actors: the six. Currency: composition and settlement. Anchor: Fig 13.
- **D2 clock constructions.** Actors: clock subsystem, observable algebra, correlation, thermal state, update step. Currency: a time-representation. Anchor: a comparison of clock readings against the ground (R4 in `01`).
- **D3 order of derivation.** Actors: links in the dependency chain. Currency: what each link consumes. Anchor: Fig 3.

Order by dependency: settle the substrate atom before classifying the clock; classify the clock before comparing clocks.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is the time state an atom or a molecule at D1?

Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it." Fig 13 :42: "Not hard to derive. There is no route. Ordering does not produce adjacency; duration does not produce conservation; a quantity does not produce"

**Result:** time is an atom at D1. It is not a molecule composed from space, energy, spectrum, gradient and network. Any program that claims to derive it from those five is attempting to compose a member from non-members, which Fig 13 :116-118 rules definitionally out of reach.

**Falsifier:** derive the ordered succession of differences from the other five, with no ordering in the premises.

### Q2 — What is a clock in framework terms?

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"

Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."

**Result:** a clock is a **second-order molecule**. First order, a state-structure-state composition (for example energy through a structure to a time-representation) settles against the ground. Second order, that settled molecule becomes an atom and can be composed over again. A clock therefore has the *functional group* of a first-order molecule: Fig 12 :124, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The group says "clock"; the qualifier says "thermal," "entanglement," or "discrete step."

This is the precise sense in which a clock is not the time state. The time state is the atom the first-order molecule bonds to; the clock is the settled molecule that becomes an atom at the next level. Conflating them is a level error, exactly the error Fig 12 :104 is written to prevent.

**Falsifier:** show that a clock construction produces the substrate atom rather than bonding to it, with the atom absent from the premises.

### Q3 — Are the emergent-time programs isomers, or instances of one functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 13 :76 (quoted above) licenses at least two distinct first-order molecules: a gradient over space and a gradient over time are different terms.

**Result:** if the thermal clock and the entanglement clock are written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network}` and differ only in which structure mediates which state pair, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance. As the programs are usually described, they share only the functional group (state-structure-state) and differ in the atoms they select, which is the weaker claim. Both readings are `specified` and unmeasured.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either program can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, a clock with no energy term, or one with no time-representation term), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q4 — What order is the union?

Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

**Result:** the dependency order is substrate atom, then first-order state-structure-state molecules, then clock constructions as second-order atoms. A dispute about clocks cannot be settled before the first-order molecules are settled, and a dispute about the substrate cannot be settled by tuning a clock. Note the notation: `→` is sequence ("A then B; B needs A to have happened," Operators table :29-33), and the exemplar's audit already recorded that Fig 13 writes the six-term relations with commas rather than arrows. So I do **not** write `Space→Time`; I write the relation as a composition with a structure in the slot, and I reserve `→` for the derivation order of this file, where order really is at issue.

**Falsifier:** show that a clock can be settled before the first-order molecule it is built from, or that the substrate relation carries an order the figure writes with commas.

### Q5 — Analogues: what a clock is not

The essay *They're Scanning Books When They Should* distinguishes outputs from generators: "The corpus contains the problems evolution already solved and someone wrote down. It does not contain the capacity to solve new ones, because that capacity lives in the living process, the mind generating, not in the text it generated."

Three analogues, each with a limit:

| analogue | what it gets right | where it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Thermometer and temperature | a thermometer reads temperature; it is not the temperature | temperature is intensive and a clock is an extended process; the analogy must not be read as identity |
| Ruler and space | a ruler measures spatial intervals and presupposes them | a clock is a dynamical system, not a static standard, so the analogy understates the composition |
| Reading and source (metamerism) | several sources give the same reading, so the reading does not name the source | the essay's spectrometer was an instrument that discriminated; R4 asks for the equivalent here |

The shared point: a clock reading is an **output** of a composition. Treating the output as the generator of the time state is the outputs-not-generators error under another name.

**Falsifier for the whole file:** an instrument that shows a clock construction generating the ordered succession of differences with no time-like primitive in its premises. That would collapse Q1 through Q5 at once and would fire R3 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## CONCLUSION

The time state is an atom at D1. A clock is a second-order molecule that settles first and then serves as an atom at the next level; it is an instance of the state-structure-state functional group, and thermal and entanglement clocks are isomers if written over the shared atom set, otherwise members of the same functional group. The derivation order is substrate, first-order molecules, clocks, and the order is not written into the substrate relation itself. None of this settles nature's question; it makes the level claim precise enough to be refuted.
