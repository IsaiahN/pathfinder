**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** Check one output on time before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds: states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network). Time is defined as "the ordered succession of differences" and is one of the states. The framework's rule is that composition runs across kinds and never within one, and that each member is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it. From this it follows that quantum evolution is a composition *over* time (energy generates change in that ordering), not a derivation *of* it. A clock built from correlations is a second-order composition that acts as a time-representation; it does not add a seventh substrate member. This is a claim about **levels**, not a measurement of nature.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that nature has settled this question, that the six terms are nature's own list, or that emergent-time research is wrong. The framework returns a shape, not a name. What it says is that "fundamental" and "emergent" answer different level-questions: at the substrate level time is an atom; at higher levels settled compositions become atoms. It does not refute clocks, thermal time, or relational time, and it forbids calling any of them a new primitive.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before sending this:**
> 1. deriving any one of the six from the other five, or deriving a law the table says needs time without time;
> 2. showing the framework must admit a state-state or structure-structure composition it cannot refuse;
> 3. deriving the ordered succession of differences from non-temporal primitives inside the closure, with the ordering not smuggled in;
> 4. an instrument that settles the ground question directly, for example one that discriminates a background time parameter from an internally generated clock where they predict differently.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Two-stage offer, explicitly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and R1 to R4. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Do not send to a list selected for receptiveness.** Fig 2 :40 disqualifies the resulting agreement, and the legal rules on unsolicited bulk mail add a practical failure on top.
