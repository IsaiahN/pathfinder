## Wider literature that bears on the question

These are the sources read for `09_watson_answer.md` and `10_source_ledger.csv`. All are secondary corroboration; none can overrule a figure line.

1. **Quantum foundations and quantum information.** Moreva et al. (2014) give an experimental illustration of the Page-Wootters mechanism: a clock subsystem correlated with the rest makes an internal observer see evolution while an external observer sees a static state. This is a **clock construction**, which the figures classify as a second-order composition, not a derivation of the substrate state. It corroborates the shape in F3 and F6.
2. **Mathematical physics / thermodynamics.** Martinetti and Rovelli (2003) define time by the flow a state induces on an observable algebra. This is a **binding onto held atoms** (state, spectrum/ordering), not a seventh member. It corroborates F6 but is not independent of the framework's own vocabulary, so its agreement carries little weight.
3. **Quantum optics / relationalism.** Menicucci, Olson and Milburn (2011) build local thermal clocks without positing a single global clock. Their Tolman-Ehrenfest consistency condition arises without assuming gravity or a metric. This is a second-order clock construction, corroborating F3's level distinction.
4. **Philosophy of physics.** Chua (2024) argues the thermal time hypothesis is circular: it needs dynamics, hence time, to get started. This is the one source that names a **conflict shape** in the world literature, and it corroborates the figure claim that quantum evolution presupposes the time state. It does not conflict with the figure verdict; it supports the same limit from a different field.
5. **Cognitive science and animal behaviour.** Ortega and Tishby (2016) model temporal distortions as a consequence of coding efficiency, with memory constraints renormalising perceived timescales. Jura (2019) ties temporal resolution to synaptic memory traces and ecological adaptation. These are D4 evidence, not D1 evidence: they show perceived duration is constructed, which is compatible with taking the time *state* as a substrate member and its *phenomenology* as a composition.

Overlap declaration: sources 2 and 3 share the thermal-time literature; sources 1 and 3 share the relational-clock literature. They are not four independent witnesses. Sources 4 and 5 are genuinely low-overlap fields and are the ones that carry what independent corroboration there is. All sources agree with the figure shape; the conflict rule was not triggered.
