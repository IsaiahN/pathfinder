# The Tether's answer on time: fundamental or emergent, and quantum evolution

**Mode: `specified` on the in-framework claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a level-indexed shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A physics claim uses time in two incompatible roles: as the parameter consumed by quantum evolution, and as the output that emergent-time programs derive from correlations, thermal states, or update rules. The contact is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-57: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). It has its own loop and its own ground. Unbundled, it becomes two level-questions, not one dilemma.

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

Fig 13 :35-36: "cannot: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction." Space and time are not two readings of one thing.

**D2, quantum evolution. Verdict: evolution composes over time; it does not generate the time state. `specified`, triangulated.** Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. Hamiltonian evolution is a composition of energy over time through a structure, so it consumes the slot. ## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a verdict on nature. The six terms are "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (*They're Scanning Books*); declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral.
- Not a refutation of emergent-time research. Clocks, thermal flows, relational time and discrete steps are compositions and are allowed; what is refused is promoting one to a seventh member.
- Not "quantum mechanics generates time." It presupposes the time state as one of the six the loop assumes.
- Not "gravity is not curvature" or any other overwrite of settled physics. The framework does not replace a physical theory; it classifies its terms.
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room."
- Not `grounded`. No instrument in this folder has settled R4.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

An instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **R1/R2 (framework):** derive one of the six from the other five, or show a state-state or structure-structure composition that cannot be refused. Either breaks the substrate claim.
- **R3 (derivation):** derive the ordered succession of differences from non-temporal primitives without the ordering in the premises. This would fire both F3 and F4.
- **R4 (ground):** an instrument that discriminates a background time parameter from an internally generated clock in a regime where they predict differently. That is the analogue of the spectrometer.
- **R5 (observer):** an actor or axis the six cannot represent, showing the set is not minimal for another observer.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in "time is fundamental or emergent," because it finds two level-questions inside that phrase. At the substrate level, time is one of six indivisible states, defined as the ordered succession of differences, and no arrangement of the other five yields it. Quantum evolution is a composition over that state, generated by energy and parameterised by time, so it consumes the slot rather than producing it. What emergent-time programs derive, successfully, is a derivative: a clock, a continuum, a thermal flow, an arrow. Those are legal compositions, and calling one of them a seventh primitive would be a category error. Because irreducibility is a fact about a level and not about the world, "fundamental" and "emergent" are both true of different levels, and the framework refuses to average them. What it offers is the level-indexed shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. The ground question stays open until that instrument runs.
