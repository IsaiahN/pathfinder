## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about time. Option A: time is fundamental, so quantum evolution is parameterised by a primitive. Option B: time is emergent, so it must be recovered from correlations, thermal states, or update rules, and quantum evolution is the source of time. What each leads to: A leaves the ordered succession of differences unanalysed but keeps the framework's minimal set intact; B owes a derivation that does not already presuppose ordering, succession or difference, and the philosophy literature names that debt as circularity. A third reading, developed in the census, is that the two options answer different level-questions. The task is to report which reading the figures license, with a stated refuter, without claiming to settle nature.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures do not return a single "fundamental" or "emergent." They return a level-indexed shape. At the substrate level, time is an indivisible member of the six-term set and no arrangement of the other five yields it. Quantum evolution is a composition over that state, so it consumes the time slot rather than producing it. Emergent-time programs derive derivatives, clocks, continua, thermal flows and arrows which are legal compositions and may not be promoted to primitives. Because irreducibility is a fact about a level and not about the world, the two words answer different level-questions and are never averaged. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Verdicts were kept separate by domain: D1 substrate member (triangulated), D2 evolution-over-time (triangulated), D3 derivative-not-primitive (single-source), D5 unreachable-as-derivation and reachable-as-clock (triangulated), D4 separate anchor (triangulated). The nature question is a gap: unreached, not answered.
