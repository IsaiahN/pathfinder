## Fetch failures and honest limits

- `https://www.mdpi.com/journal/life` returned 403 on direct fetch. The Omran et al. 2020 article was read through the NCBI PMC mirror instead, and that is the URL cited in the ledger.
- `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14716004/` returned only a vulnerability-disclosure page on direct fetch. The Ricardo et al. 2004 borate-stabilizes-ribose result is therefore cited through the formose Wikipedia entry and the reference list of the Omran et al. full text, both of which were read. It is marked as one witness, not two.
- `https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-astrobiology` loaded with cookie notices, but the journal scope, editor, open-access status, and submission route were readable and are reported.
- Web search integration returned no results for several queries on 2026-11-23. Sources were located by fetching known public URLs directly. This is a budget limit on breadth, not evidence of absence (Fig 8 :39).

## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on the residual

1. **Half-life is habitat-conditional, not intrinsic.** The RNA world entry reports the same nucleoside at 19 days at 100 degrees C and 17,000 years in freezing water. This is Fig 11 :49 in data form: valence is relative to the residual. Any "stable chemical" claim lacking a condition set is unbracketed.
2. **Stability and concentration are different quantities.** The Omran et al. full text says concentrating mechanisms are required, and the RNA world entry describes crystallization of ribose aminooxazoline as a purification step. A long-lived species can stay dilute; a short-lived one can be trapped. Fig 12 :125-126: the two currencies do not add.
3. **What the network ends up holding is not what one hoped to make.** Under alkaline hydrothermal conditions the formose product pool is dominated by carboxylic acids, with under one percent of formaldehyde remaining as sugars. Fig 12 :121 (cycle) and :119 (catalyst) describe the structure; the measurement supplies the binding. Do not present the name as a figure derivation.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Lead with the shape claim and its refuter. The full framework goes only after a stage-1 verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 2 :35**, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement, and do not select recipients for likely agreement (Fig 2 :40).

- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the architecture before anyone has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which habitat conditions they would hold fixed; (3) only then the figure text and the journal link, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, plus a third stage

1. Stage 1: validate the output against the ground. Send the residual, the shape, and the pre-registered refuter. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. Stage 2, opt-in only: if they are curious, offer the figure text and canonical README. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. Stage 3: offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits methods to cross. Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The output here is a shape, not a name list. If a recipient's first question is "but which chemicals", the correct answer is the gap in F6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, with the provenanced bindings and their conditions offered separately.
