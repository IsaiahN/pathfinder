## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the request for a list of chemicals stable enough to accumulate. Option A: return a standing list of named prebiotic molecules. Option B: return the shape an accumulating species must have, with per-habitat verdicts, provenanced bindings for any name, and a written refuter. What each leads to: A presents an import as a derivation, ignores that valence changes with the residual, and spends the independence of the people checking it; B reports a gap where no figure line governs, keeps the list open until instruments settle it, and gives a checkable shape instead.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what formed and what survived degradation long enough to become an operand for the next composition. Arity: a molecule is operands joined by a bond; the residual is indexed per species, not pooled. Symmetry: stability is not carried by an ingredient, it is a relation between an arrangement and a habitat. Scale: bond-level degradation and population-level concentration are different scales with a lossy transform between them. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Domain 1, composition and structure.** Actors: atoms, seven typed bonds, isomers, catalysts, cycles, functional groups. Currency: which bonded arrangement persists and can be composed over. Anchor: ground-measured persistence under stated conditions. Question type: partition boundary between ingredient lists and arrangements.
- **Domain 2, habitat and geochemistry.** Actors: light, water, temperature, season, terrain, mineral surfaces, borates, pH, UV flux, impacts. Currency: contact and persistence. Anchor: the actual early Earth environment, which does not update on what any frame thinks. Question type: enumeration of conditions, never composition.
- **Domain 3, accumulation and selection.** Actors: species, degradation channels, concentrating processes. Currency: concentration and survival. Anchor: the ground, since nothing maintained the habitat. Question type: selection between rooms, not agreement within one.
- **Domain 4, verification and settlement.** Actors: instruments and experimental chemists. Currency: ground contact, never agreement. Anchor: a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes on it. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Union, not partition.** Hydrolysis resistance, UV resistance, thermal survival and concentration overlap. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate habitat, then settle the bond and arrangement, then assign valence, then check concentration, then admit a provenanced name. Refuse before mint (formula step 2). Describe the effect before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test: Fig 13's six physical terms were refused as a grammar for molecular species and Fig 12 was used instead. Different objects, so no contradiction after re-census (S1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and the list does not. Stability is a relation between a bonded arrangement and a habitat, indexed per species and never averaged; the bond and the arrangement carry the difference, so same-formula isomers can have different fates; the species a network keeps are the ones a cycle or catalyst regenerates or a functional group protects; a settled molecule becomes an operand for the next composition; and persistence and concentration are two currencies whose threshold, not their sum, decides accumulation. No figure line yields a standing list, so that part of the question is `gap`: a verdict of unreachable at this level of description, not silence and not absence. Chemical names are admitted only as provenanced bindings with a stated habitat and a shadow test. Mode `specified` on the domain claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the list, nothing `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the shape and its refuter travel together. The real cost is presenting an experimental binding as a figure derivation, which imports a wrong atom priced above starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24), and spending the independence of outside readers by asking them to agree with a list rather than test a shape (Fig 2 :40, Fig 10 :42). Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Both costs are avoidable by leading with the residual, the condition set, and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. Bindings are removable without touching the shape, and a corrected packet supersedes a sent one. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Replacing a name with a better-provenanced one changes the habitat description, not the framework.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Discriminating instruments that measure disappearance rates per channel under a stated pH, temperature, UV flux and mineral assemblage, plus a concentration trajectory, would settle a name. An isomer pair with identical profiles under every channel would break Fig 12 :117; habitat-invariant persistence would break Fig 11 :49; a half-life that predicts concentration with no concentration term would break Fig 12 :125. A figure re-census that finds a line naming a chemical would close the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
