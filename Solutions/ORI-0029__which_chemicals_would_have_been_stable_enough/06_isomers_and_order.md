# Isomers, catalysts, and the order of the accumulation chain

**Mode: `specified` on the per-structure claims, `general` on the ordering rule, `gap` on any named species. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks which chemicals accumulated. Three supporting questions are owed before any name can be admitted: (1) what makes two chemicals with the same composition different in persistence; (2) which structural features make a species survive its own use; and (3) in what order must settlement, refusal, and composition run. A fourth quantity, concentration, must be kept in its own currency.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Same formula, different fate (the isomer test)

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :97: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"*, the case that forced structure into chemical notation (Liebig and Wöhler 1824 to 1826; named isomerism by Berzelius 1830).
- The Miller-Urey record reports glycine and beta-alanine as separately identified products of one discharge. The formose record reports glucose, xylose, and mannose from one alkaline mixture. Elemental inventories do not separate these; bonds do.
- Therefore a candidate answer that lists formulas has not named a chemical species at all. It has named an ingredient set. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a controlled comparison in which isomer pairs have the same degradation rate constants under hydrolysis, UV photolysis, and thermal stress, across the full range tested. If that holds, the bond is not decisive here and Q1 is retracted into the functional-group claim of Q3.

### Q2 — The structures that survive use

- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The formose reaction is the figure's chemistry instance made concrete: the introduction and the reaction-mechanism sections of the Wikipedia entry call it autocatalytic, with glycolaldehyde regenerating itself from formaldehyde. Omran et al. 2020 call it *"the only known abiotic autocatalytic cycle"* and report that at high pH and temperature the equilibrium pool is carboxylic acids rather than sugars.
- Borate mineral stabilization of ribose (Ricardo et al. 2004, as recorded in the formose entry) is the functional-group case: a substructure plus a mineral condition fixes which member persists.
- **Falsifier:** an accumulating species that is neither regenerated by a cycle, nor catalytic, nor protected by a functional group, while a sister species with those features decays faster under the same measured conditions. That would leave all three structures idle for this residual and send them back to a different domain.

### Q3 — The order: settle, refuse, compose, then name

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Settlement is the link that must work before composition.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* A species that persists becomes an operand at the next level.
- Formula step 2 routes a claim to REFUTED before MINT: *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."* A proposed name is a candidate until it pays.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Describe the persistence effect before selecting the chemical frame.
- The question as posed reverses this order: it asks for names before describing the residual. Doing so makes any famous prebiotic molecule seem to fit. The corrected order is: habitat, degradation channel, persistence rule, concentration channel, then a provenanced name.
- **Falsifier:** a case where a named molecule correctly predicts its own persistence in an undescribed habitat, repeatedly and without adjustment. That would show the name-first order is sufficient here.

### Q4 — Analogues that corroborate the structure, not the chemistry

These are low-overlap frames used only as corroboration; the figures decide.

- **Ecology, contact cascade.** Fig 11 :52-53: *"contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* In prebiotic chemistry the analogue is the mineral that both stabilizes a species and changes which species adsorbs and concentrates. A first-order stability claim misses the third-order accumulation effect.
- **Engineering, substituted habitat.** Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* A flask experiment that shows a molecule surviving in one buffered solution proves the wiring of that setup, not its persistence in a Hadean pond. Isolation substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66); it does not remove one.
- **Human cognition, source discrimination.** The metamerism case in *The Hard Problem is About Source* is the low-overlap witness for section 4 of `05_what_shape_means.md`: a reading cannot identify its source, and the better instrument, not the better argument, settles it.
- **Mathematics, no proof of absence.** Fig 8 :32 and :39: closure is idempotent and a search that finds nothing proves nothing. The absence of a measured half-life is not evidence that a species was unstable.

### Q5 — The threshold between two currencies

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

Here: persistence is a rate (bond and habitat); concentration is an abundance reached (trapping process and measurement). The Operators table defines comparison ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"* Accumulation is the comparison of a persistence timescale against a concentration timescale. A species accumulates where the first exceeds the second in a stated habitat.

- **Falsifier:** a measured half-life that predicts achieved concentration across species without any concentration term. That collapses the two currencies and breaks Q5.

## CONCLUSION

Same composition does not mean same persistence; the bond and the arrangement carry the difference. The structures that a network keeps are the ones that survive their own use (catalysts), re-enter as input (cycles), or are fixed by a substructure (functional groups). The order is habitat first, then persistence rule, then concentration channel, then a provenanced name; settling precedes composition and refusal precedes mint. Persistence and concentration are two currencies whose comparison, not their sum, is accumulation. Every named species stays a candidate until an instrument settles it in a stated habitat.
