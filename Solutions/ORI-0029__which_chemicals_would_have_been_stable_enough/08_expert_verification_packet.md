# Expert verification packet: the Tether on prebiotic stability and accumulation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist or astrobiologist can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural and per-domain claims, `general` on the cross-domain shape, `gap` on the literal list. Not `grounded`.** No persistence or concentration measurement was taken in this derivation. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Stable enough to accumulate" is not a property a molecule carries. It is a relation between a bonded arrangement and a habitat, indexed per species and never pooled into an inventory. The bond, not the ingredient list, carries the difference, so same-formula isomers can have different degradation profiles. The species a reaction network keeps are the ones a cycle or catalyst regenerates or a functional group protects. Once such a species is settled against the ground it becomes an operand for the next chemistry. Persistence and concentration are two currencies that do not add; accumulation is a threshold between them. The figures return this shape together with four refuters and leave the literal standing list as a declared gap. Specific chemical names enter as provenanced bindings from the chemistry frame, each tied to a stated habitat, never as figure derivations.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Persistence is habitat-relative, and the residual is per species

- **Statement:** a persistence claim without a stated condition set is unbracketed; verdicts are per species and per habitat and must never be averaged into "the stable organics".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :39, *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** does the reported persistence of a given species actually change with pH, temperature, UV flux, or mineral assemblage? The RNA world entry reports the same nucleoside at 19 days at 100 degrees C and 17,000 years in freezing water; the Miller-Urey entry reports amino acid yields rescued by carbonate buffering and an oxidation inhibitor. Are those habitat effects being conflated with an intrinsic property?
- **Refuter:** a species with invariant persistence across habitats that differ in those conditions. That breaks C1.

### C2 — The arrangement, not the formula, decides the fate; the structures that survive are identifiable

- **Statement:** a molecular formula is an ingredient list. Under a symmetric arrangement the operand order is invisible, and isomers have the same composition but different behaviour. The structural features that keep a species in play are catalyst, cycle, and functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :119, *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."* Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."*
- **What to check:** do the named products in a given experiment actually distinguish isomers, or only elemental compositions? Is there evidence that the accumulating species is regenerated by a cycle (formose glycolaldehyde) or stabilized by a substructure (borate and ribose)? Omran et al. 2020 report that under alkaline hydrothermal conditions the equilibrium pool is carboxylic acids, with under one percent of formaldehyde remaining as sugars.
- **Refuter:** two arrangements with the same ingredients and the same degradation profile under every channel tested, or an accumulating species that lacks all three structures while a sister species with them decays faster. Either breaks C2.

### C3 — Persistence and concentration are separate; the list is a gap

- **Statement:** a half-life alone does not predict an accumulated abundance. The claim is a threshold between two currencies, and no figure line returns a standing list of chemicals.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Operators table: comparison ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*.
- **What to check:** does the experiment report a concentration trajectory as well as a disappearance rate? If a species persists but is never concentrated, it has not accumulated. Does the paper's "stable" mean "we could detect it" rather than "it survived the habitat at a usable concentration"?
- **Refuter:** a measured half-life that predicts concentration across species without a concentration term, or a figure line that names a specific prebiotic chemical. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of stable chemicals. The figures refuse a standing list; the literal list is `gap`.
- Not that stability is intrinsic to a molecule, and not that a formula determines persistence.
- Not that meteoritic delivery or a particular atmosphere settles supply; those are imports with provenance.
- Not that a published half-life settles a name in a different habitat.
- Not that agreement among reviews corroborates the shape. Reviews that share an evidence pool are one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not grounded. Nothing in this packet is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that this derivation settles the origin-of-life question it reports on. It is internally consistent with the figures, and that is all.

## 5. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49, Fig 12 :117, Fig 12 :125 and Fig 11 :51. Four short passages.
2. Pick one named prebiotic species you know well. State the pH, temperature, UV flux, and mineral context under which you believe it persists. Have you actually measured a persistence rate there, or inferred it from a different habitat?
3. Look at one experiment you know. Did it report a concentration trajectory or only a detected product? Does its "stable" mean survived or accumulated?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate hydrolysis from photolysis for that species in that habitat, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not send agreement; agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness talking to itself (Fig 2 :40).
