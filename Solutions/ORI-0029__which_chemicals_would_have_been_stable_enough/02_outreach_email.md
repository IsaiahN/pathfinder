**Subject:** Check one claim about prebiotic persistence before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the structure, `general` on the shape, `gap` on the list):** "stable enough to accumulate" is not a property a molecule carries. It is a relation between a bonded arrangement and a habitat. The same species persists in one condition set and is destroyed in another, so a standing list of stable chemicals does not survive a change of residual. What the figures license is a shape: the arrangements that persist are the ones whose bonds survive the habitat's specific degradation channels, and the ones a cycle or a functional group keeps regenerating. Once such a molecule is settled against the ground it becomes an operand for the next composition. Persistence and concentration are two currencies that do not add, so a half-life never by itself predicts an accumulated abundance.
>
> I am deliberately **not** giving you a list of chemicals. The framework returns a kind of thing, not the answer itself. Specific names (a nucleobase stabilized by borate, an amino acid yield rescued by carbonate buffering, the carboxylic acids that formose chemistry drives toward at equilibrium) are bindings from the chemistry frame. I would record where each came from and the conditions it was measured under, and I would treat them as candidates until a non-updating instrument settles them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A species whose persistence is invariant across habitats that differ in pH, temperature, and UV flux.
> 2. Two arrangements with the same ingredients and the same degradation profile under every channel tested.
> 3. An accumulating species with no concentration or trapping process acting, so that half-life alone predicts abundance.
> 4. A figure line that names a specific prebiotic chemical. I do not think one exists, and I would rather report the gap than fill it.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - International Journal of Astrobiology, open-access submission route: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-astrobiology
> - ISSOL, the International Society for the Study of the Origin of Life: https://issol.org/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the write-up warns that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
