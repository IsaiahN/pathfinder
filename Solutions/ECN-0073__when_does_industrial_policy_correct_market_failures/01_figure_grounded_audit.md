## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question arrives as a two-way choice. Stated as an effect, the residual is this:

> An intervention `a` is applied at market slot `s` on a belief `b` that a failure occupies that slot. The prediction `Γ(b,a)` is that the failure closes, so `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` should fall when measured against an observation `o′` taken from a market counterfactual. The question asks when that happens and when the same action instead keeps an inefficient firm alive. The two outcomes have different anchors. "Correct" is a residual that falls against a market that does not move when the policy frame declares victory. "Protect" is a firm that survives because the reference against which it is judged was changed to the supported room's own report.

Three things follow before any figure line is chosen.

1. **The residual is per slot.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** So there is no national "industrial policy works, on average" verdict. There is a verdict per named sector or firm slot.
2. **A market is an anchor in the figures' own list.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** The counterfactual is the anchor, not the ministry's scorecard.
3. **The question is a union until the census proves otherwise.** A policy can correct one failure and protect one incumbent in the same sector at the same time. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** The two outcomes are not opposite ends of a dial.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound so it can be refused: a **separator** of checkable conditions, each with a named anchor and a written failure condition. The shape claim is that the separator has five parts (real measured support, an MDL-paying reachable instrument, a non-updating ground, a seat able to refuse, and a method rather than a recording), and that dropping any one moves the case from the "correct" column to the "protect" column or into the intersection. This is the ordinary term bound at Formula step "Direction", before the run.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least three place. `(policy action, market slot, counterfactual observation)`, indexed by slot and ordered by time. A two-place "policy causes outcome" query drops the counterfactual, which is the anchor, and is therefore under-specified.
- **Symmetry:** "correct" and "protect" are **not** symmetric inverses on one axis. Correcting is a comparison of a residual against a fixed ground (an ordered comparison bond, `⋛`). Protecting is the persistence of an actor whose exit the market would otherwise have forced (an absence, `−`, plus an introduction that cannot be un-made, Fig 11 :61). A pooled policy effect size is symmetric and cannot choose which arrow is meant; the same arithmetic that makes a correlation useless for direction applies here (Fig 2 :39-40). So no single scalar answer is licensed.
- **Scale:** three loops, with a lossy membrane between them (Fig 4 :30): the firm/sector loop, the policy-instrument loop, and the political-maintenance loop. A method that works at one scale is remade at the next (Fig 10 :68).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Market-failure frame** (firm and sector loop) | firms, consumers, entrants, externalities, knowledge spillovers, suppliers | prices, quantities, residual reduction at slot `s` | market counterfactual, which does not update on the ministry's report | admitted |
| **D2 Policy-instrument frame** (selector / mint) | policymakers, tariffs, subsidies, R&D credits, loan guarantees, procurement | description length `|φ|`, leftover residual `left(R,φ)`, reachability | settlement: does the ground pay for the instrument (Formula step 5) | admitted |

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception of a real failure at a slot (link 1), terms for what the instrument affords (link 2), a stated objective that can be measured (link 3), a route to it under incomplete information (link 4), and only then learning and carrying (link 5). Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Most industrial-policy effort lands at link 4 or 5, above a break at link 3 where no instrument for the externality exists. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 and D2 form the failure-correction compound; D3 and D4 form the protection compound; their overlap (an intervention that genuinely closes a failure and also entrenches an incumbent) is a specific compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either. Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Fig 13 :137: **"It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."** One lever, for example "impose a sunset clause", clears the region where only the maintenance condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

Each finding states mode, verbatim figure text, the per-domain verdict, and a refuter. Verdicts are not averaged across domains.

### F1: The question is a union of two conditions with different anchors, not one dial (decisive for the framing)

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the market application.**

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
Fig 13 :137: **"It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."**

D1/D2 (is the failure real and is the instrument worth it?) and D3/D4 (does the reference stay external and can support be withdrawn?) are different conditions. The world literature independently reaches the same union shape: Juhász, Lane and Rodrik group rationales into externalities, coordination failures and public inputs, while the Socio-Economic Review case study derives two first-order conditions (firm capabilities and institutional alignment) with the same union logic, *"industrial policy is certain to fail when both conditions are absent."* These are world corroborations only; the verdict comes from Fig 9.

**Verdict per domain:** D1/D2 answer "correct"; D3/D4 answer "protect"; the intersection needs a fourth, compound loop. No domain's verdict is averaged with another.
**Refuter:** a single measured variable that predicts both outcomes with no residual left in either domain, and a demonstration that the two conditions are mutually exclusive rather than overlapping. The cane-toad case below defeats this refuter: one introduction both failed its target and created a new cascade.
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 union rule plus Fig 13 split rule, low overlap between the leave/arrive figure and the substrate figure).

### F2: A policy is a mint and needs real measured support at a named slot

**Mode: `specified`.**

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 5 :20: **"is there anything to explain?"**
Fig 5 :21: **"the gap has real mass somewhere"**
Fig 5 :41: **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."**
Symbols :38: **"It is the aim, not only the error signal"**

The first guard is `|R⁺_s| > 0` at some slot before the search. A subsidy to a sector with no measured externality, no coordination failure and no public-input shortfall has zero support: the mint is inert and what remains is a transfer. The world literature agrees on the ordering: the IMF policy paper says *"IP can be considered if there is a well-identified market failure (e.g., under-provision of public inputs, negative externalities, coordination failures) that inhibits socially optimal outcomes"*, and the OECD pro-competitive report says *"Implement industrial policies where and when market failures have been clearly identified."* Agreement from a shared economics pool is one witness, not two.

**Verdict per domain:** D1 admits a case only when a failure is measured at `s`; D2 admits an instrument only when it is aimed at that same `s`. A policy justified by a national average fails this finding.
**Refuter:** a demonstration that the framework must admit a mint at a slot with `|R⁺_s| = 0` and still call it a correction rather than a transfer.
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 per-slot indexing plus Fig 5 support guard; different figures, different quantities).

### F3: The anchor must not update: when the success metric is set by the supported room, the drift is coherent and protection is invisible

**Mode: `specified`. This is the sharpest figure-grounded separator of "protect".**

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**
Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :26: **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

A programme whose success criterion is employment at the supported firm, export volume of the supported sector, or the number of firms that stay in the scheme measures the room, not the market. Fig 2 :34 is the protection mechanism in one sentence: once the reference updates on what the supported frame reports, every participant drifts together and the drift is coherent, which is exactly why the failure is not noticed from inside. The low-overlap world corroborator is measurement science: Strathern 1997, *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."* CNA 2022 adds the mechanism, *"when we use a measure to reward performance, we provide an incentive to manipulate the measure in order to receive the reward."* The Goodhart / Strathern field is not industrial economics, so this is a genuinely separate frame; its agreement corroborates the mechanism shape, not the economic fact.

**Verdict per domain:** D3 is the location of protection. D1 keeps its market anchor only if the counterfactual is external to the supported room. D4's job is to keep that channel from decaying (Fig 10 :24), not to lower the threshold.
**Refuter:** a case where the success metric is written by the supported firms, the market counterfactual is never observed, and the residual still falls against an independent anchor at the same slot. If that exists, this separator fails.
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor invariance plus Fig 10 channel decay plus the low-overlap measurement literature; the figure verdict does not depend on the world source).

### F4: The bargain: policy cost plus leftover must cost less than the failure, and the two currencies do not add

**Mode: `specified`.**

Fig 5 :42: **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."**
Fig 5 :26: **"and then: does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"**
Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A tariff or subsidy is a term φ. It pays only if `cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R)`. The two prices are in bits and in actions and cannot be summed: the administrative and distortion cost of a measure is not the same currency as the cost of discovering whether the failure is actually closed. An effect size from a meta-analysis is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. The IMF paper's *"the bar to get it right is high"* and the OECD's call for measures that are *"well-targeted, time-bound, cost-effective, transparent"* are world-level statements of the same bargain, not a substitute for it.

**Verdict per domain:** D2 accepts an instrument only on the bargain; D1 supplies the residual to be discounted; D3 and D4 supply the action cost of monitoring and withdrawal, which is charged separately.
**Refuter:** a policy whose full description and enforcement cost plus the residual it leaves exceeds the cost of the uncorrected failure, yet is still admitted as a correction by the framework.
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 5 MDL bargain plus Fig 12 two-currency rule; the accumulation of an unpriced bond is the flat-form failure).

### F5: Head start versus recording: a temporary measure must be a method that can be refused, or it becomes a permanent institution

**Mode: `specified`. This is the infant-industry finding.**

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
Fig 4 :26: **"Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**
Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**

The textbook infant-industry case is exactly a head start: the tariff is meant to be temporary, to let the industry move down its learning curve, and then to be withdrawn. The figures say what makes it work and what makes it fail. It works if what crosses to the firm is a reapplicable method, a head start that makes the firm's next search cheaper, and if the policy vocabulary contains a negation, a way to refuse and withdraw the term once the ground pays. It fails if what crosses is a recording of one occasion, or if the policy set has no operator for "this no longer holds." Without negation the intervention can only accumulate, and with introduction available and exclusion not, a managed industry is permanent work rather than a solved state. This is the figure-grounded form of time inconsistency. The world record is unusually direct: Juhász and Lane quote Mill's 1868 letter, *"industries artificially fortified, even though it be professedly for a time only, raise up private interests which combine, as they have done in the United States, but too effectually, to convert what was intended as a temporary expedient into a permanent institution"*; Taussig 1914 noted that *"professedly short-term industrial policies had a way of enduring"*; the EBRD working paper finds that *"Policies without a stated end date ('sunset clause') may be harder to discontinue since doing so requires a specific action relative to the status quo"* and that *"letting losers go may in fact be more difficult than picking winners."* Taiwan's Industrial Development Bureau withdrawing VCR protection when the industry failed to become competitive is the counterexample where the refusal actually fired.

**Verdict per domain:** D2 must be able to refuse its own term; D4 must hold the sunset and performance conditions; D3 is where the refusal is resisted. A policy without a written exit is not a head start, it is an introduction without exclusion.
**Refuter:** a policy with no withdrawal condition and no operator for refusal that nevertheless closes the measured residual and exits on its own at the market-determined time, with the counterfactual still external. That would show negation is not needed here.
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 4 method/head start rule, Fig 11 asymmetric power rule and Operators negation rule; three figures, three different quantities).

### F6: Valence is relative to the residual: the same instrument is benefactor or malfactor, so "good or bad industrial policy" is the wrong object

**Mode: `general` on the valence rule, `specified` on the instrument application.**

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

An export subsidy that closes a cost-discovery externality in one slot and keeps a non-competitive incumbent alive in another is not two different objects with two different moral properties. It is one object whose valence is set by the residual it meets. So no general answer of the form "industrial policy corrects" or "industrial policy protects" survives a change of residual; only a per-slot verdict does. The economics literature's polarized debate (OECD: proponents call intervention essential, opponents call it *"inefficient, if not wasteful, distorts markets and comes with rent seeking"*) is the ordinary-language version of trying to make a standing list. Fig 11 :51 refuses it structurally.

**Verdict per domain:** each of D1 to D4 is scored separately at slot `s`; verdicts are never averaged into a national "industrial policy works somewhat."
**Refuter:** an instrument whose valence is invariant across at least two documented changes of residual, measured against an external anchor in both.
**Status: triangulated for the rule** (Fig 11 :49/:50/:51 are one figure, so the valence rule is single-source on the figure side; its separation from Fig 12 isomer reading in `06` supplies the structural cross-check. Recorded honestly as **single-source** for the valence claim itself.)

### F7: Implementation is agent and habitat: capacity is contact, not a property of the ministry alone

**Mode: `specified`.**

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

This is why "good design" alone is not the separator. A policy instrument has to be in contact with the slot it targets and with a ground it can read. Juhász and Lane's NBER abstract states the same shape from political economy: *"government failure is not a necessary feature of industrial policy; rather, it is more likely to emerge when countries pursue industrial policies beyond their governance capacity constraints."* Rodrik and Stiglitz's five elements, *"embeddedness, coordination, monitoring, conditionality, and institutional development,"* are a world-level description of maintained contact, and their *"the ability to let the losers go"* is the refusal of F5. These corroborate the shape; they do not decide it.

**Verdict per domain:** D2's capacity is a relation between instrument and habitat, not a score of the agency; D4 is where contact is maintained or lost.
**Refuter:** a policy that closes the residual with an agency whose contact with the slot is zero and whose monitoring instrument is absent, with the counterfactual still external.
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 11 capability claim plus Fig 3 instrument link plus Fig 2 ranging rule).

### F8: Capture is a mirror chain: rent-seeking turns separate frames into one evidence pool

**Mode: `specified`.**

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**
Fig 8 :51: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

Rent-seeking is not just a moral failing; it is an independence failure. When the reviewer, the beneficiary and the ministry read the same report, their agreement is the pool talking, and the chain of approvals is one long room. Rodrik 2008 names the mechanism in world terms: *"industrial policy is an invitation to corruption and rent-seeking. Once the government is in the business of providing support to firms, it becomes easy for the private sector to demand and extract benefits that distort competition and transfer rents to politically connected entities."* OECD adds that broad measures *"avoid the pitfalls of targeted measures related to the ability to pick winners, capture and rent seeking."* Note the asymmetry: a low-overlap frame is needed for the verdict, but importing its atom is debited against independence (Fig 6 :24). Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), not gathered by adding agreeing reviewers.

**Verdict per domain:** D3's default reading is protection until independence is shown; D2's evidence must come from a frame whose closure differs from the supported room's.
**Refuter:** a captured room whose agreement still tracks an external counterfactual at the slot, with the shared pool shown to be irrelevant.
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 7 mirror chain plus Fig 2 evidence-pool rule plus Fig 8 independence-as-surplus, low overlap among the three figures).

### F9: Cascades: introduction is available, exclusion is not, and the residual that matters is often three steps out

**Mode: `specified`, single-source for the ecological instantiation.**

Fig 11 :52 onwards: **"And contact cascades."**
Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

Fig 11 names cane toads as the cautionary case for an introduction with an unenumerated cascade. The low-overlap world literature makes the same point with data: the cane-toad biological-control study found that *"any direct benefit of toads on agricultural production (via consumption of insect pests) likely was outweighed by negative effects that were mediated via the toads' impacts on other taxa"*, and the National Museum of Australia records that in 2010 the Australian Government stated, *"There is unlikely to ever be a broadscale method available to control cane toads across Australia."* That is Fig 11 :61 (introduction available, exclusion not) with an empirical anchor. An industrial intervention aimed at one externality can therefore generate second-order residuals three steps out (Fig 11 :52-53), and the ones that matter are often the ones the policy did not enumerate.

**Verdict per domain:** D4 must enumerate the habitat outward from the slot before crediting the intervention; D1 must keep the second-order residual in the same per-slot ledger.
**Refuter:** an introduced instrument whose full cascade is enumerated in advance and whose second-order residual is zero at the ground.
**Status: single-source on the figure side** (Fig 11 only for the ecological law), corroborated by a genuinely low-overlap ecology source. The agreement fixes the mechanism shape, not the economic verdict.

**Refuter:** a figure line that derives a numeric intervention level from the six-term set, or a ground reading that fixes one at a named slot.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat map rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Per-slot refuter.** A policy's correction status can be established from a national aggregate with no per-slot residual and no counterfactual, and the aggregate still predicts the ground at each slot (breaks Fig 1 :19/:22 and F2).
2. **Anchor refuter.** A success metric written by the supported room, with no external observation, still tracks an independently measured fall in the failure at the same slot (breaks Fig 2 :29/:33/:34 and F3).
3. **Exit refuter.** A policy with no withdrawal condition and no possibility of refusing its own term closes the residual and exits at the market-determined time with the counterfactual intact (breaks Fig 4 :23/:26, Fig 11 :61/:63 and F5).
4. **Valence refuter.** An instrument's sign is invariant across two documented changes of residual against external anchors (breaks Fig 11 :49-51 and F6).
5. **Union refuter.** One variable fully predicts both correction and protection with no leftover residual, and the two conditions are shown to be mutually exclusive (breaks Fig 9 :55/:57 and F1).
6. **Cascade refuter.** An intervention's second-order residuals are enumerable in advance and measured zero at the ground (breaks Fig 11 :61/:68/:69 and F9).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the separator is redrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
