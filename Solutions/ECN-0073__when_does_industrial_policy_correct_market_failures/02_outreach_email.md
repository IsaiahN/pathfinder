**Subject:** A five-condition separator for when industrial policy helps, with its refuter written first

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about industrial policy, with one specific output I would like checked against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does industrial policy correct market failures or protect inefficient firms?" is not one dial with two ends. It is a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, so the verdict has to be given per sector or firm slot and kept separate. From a figure-grounded derivation, a policy counts as correcting a failure only when five conditions hold at that slot:
>
> 1. a real failure is measured at the named slot, not inferred from a national average;
> 2. the instrument pays its own way, meaning its full cost plus the failure it leaves is less than the failure it removes;
> 3. success is judged against a counterfactual that does not move when the supported firms report success;
> 4. support can be refused and withdrawn, with a written exit and a performance test;
> 5. what crosses to the firm is a reapplicable head start, not a one-occasion support that has to be replayed forever.
>
> Where condition 3 fails, the success metric has become a target and stops measuring the failure. Where condition 4 fails, a temporary measure has a way of becoming a permanent institution. Where condition 5 fails, an introduction cannot be un-made. Those are the protection cases.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that industrial policy is always good or always bad; not that any particular sector should be picked; not that there is a universal subsidy rate or optimal duration. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the country-specific numbers are a declared gap, not a hidden answer. I am also not claiming this is settled by measurement. It is a `specified` separator derived from a framework and checked for internal consistency, not a result the framework measured.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:** (1) a correction established from a national aggregate with no per-slot residual and no counterfactual; (2) a metric written by the supported room that still tracks an independently measured fall in the failure; (3) a policy with no exit that closes the failure and exits on its own with the counterfactual intact; (4) an instrument whose sign never changes across two documented changes of residual; (5) one variable that fully predicts both outcomes with no leftover residual and shows the two conditions are mutually exclusive; (6) an intervention whose full second-order cascade is enumerable in advance and measured zero.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The New Economics of Industrial Policy, Juhász, Lane and Rodrik, Annual Review of Economics 2024: https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-economics-081023-024638
> - The Political Economy of Industrial Policy, Juhász and Lane, NBER WP 32507 / JEP 2024: https://www.nber.org/papers/w32507
> - Moving the goalposts, EBRD Working Paper 311, 2025: https://www.ebrd.com/publications
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and the formula are public. Run them on a policy you know well and can check against a counterfactual. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
