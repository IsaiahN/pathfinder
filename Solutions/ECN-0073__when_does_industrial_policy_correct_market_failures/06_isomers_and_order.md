# Are "correct" and "protect" isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary question treats "correct a market failure" and "protect an inefficient firm" as opposite outcomes of one intervention. Two structural questions are owed: (1) over a fixed set of policy instruments, are the two outcomes isomers under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what is the order of the bond that connects a temporary measure to a self-sustaining outcome, sequence or production? A third question follows from the answer to F5 of `01`: what kind of part is a subsidy that survives its own use?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the economics binding: tariff, production subsidy, R&D credit, loan guarantee, procurement rule, sunset clause, performance condition, monitoring instrument, counterfactual. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate. The two outcomes are not atoms; they are arrangements of the same instrument set under different bonds and different anchors. So this is a question about structure and order, not about which ingredients to add.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the two outcomes isomers over a fixed instrument set?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

Take the same atoms: a temporary production subsidy, a performance condition, a sunset clause, an external counterfactual. Arrangement A reads `counterfactual ⋛ measured failure → subsidy ⋛ performance condition → withdrawal`, where the subsidy is a head start bonded to a target and to its own exit. Arrangement B reads `firm report → subsidy → continued subsidy → continued firm report`, where the subsidy is bonded to the supported room and the withdrawal atom is absent. Same atoms, different bonds, different substance. In A the residual falls against an external comparator; in B the reference updates on the room and the measure becomes a target. Under Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers**, not different mixtures. This is the structural reason why a policy effect size cannot choose between them: the formula (the ingredient list) is identical, and only the bond differs, which is exactly what Fig 12 :5 says the flat formula loses.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a correction case requires an instrument that no protection case uses, or vice versa, the isomer claim fails and the two outcomes are different atom sets rather than different arrangements. The cane-toad case does not refute this: introduction is the shared atom, and the bond to an enumerated cascade versus an unenumerated one is the difference (Fig 11 :65).

### Q2: What order connects the measure to the outcome?

Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**
Operators :30: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** (the sequence definition)
Operators :37: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** (the production definition)

A temporary support written as `subsidy → competitiveness` is a **sequence**: competitiveness is already waiting, and the subsidy merely precedes it. That is the reading in which a policy can be extended indefinitely, because nothing about the bond requires the supported activity to be absent before the subsidy fires. The infant-industry theory requires **production** (`subsidy ⇒ competitiveness`): the competitive industry does not exist before the measure, and the measure brings it into being. The distinction is testable at the ground: does the sector exist, with its own counterfactual, after withdrawal? If yes, the bond was production and the withdrawal is legitimate. If the sector exists only while the measure fires, the bond was sequence and the measure is a transfer. Fig 12 :112 makes the mapping exact: a goal is a comparison, so the target must be `⋛`; progress is a subtraction, so the residual must be `−`; and the ability to end the support is `¬`, which is not a bond at all and must be supplied as a separate operator. A vocabulary with `→` and `−` but no `¬` can say what is missing and cannot say that the arrangement is wrong (Operators :73-74).

**Falsifier:** a case where withdrawal is followed by a self-sustaining competitive sector even though no production bond can be shown, meaning the sequence reading suffices. That would collapse the distinction and remove the need for a refusal operator in this domain.

### Q3: What kind of part is a subsidy that survives its own use?

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A head start is a spent part: it is consumed in making the firm's next search cheaper, and after that the firm carries the method. A permanent subsidy is a catalyst in the figure's sense: it survives its own use and regenerates across uses. The two behave differently across uses, and a policy ledger that records only the amount disbursed does not record which kind it is. The EBRD finding that *"letting losers go may in fact be more difficult than picking winners"* and the Mill passage about a temporary expedient becoming a permanent institution are the world-level descriptions of a part that regenerates. The test at the ground is the same as Q2: measure the part after its supposed use. If it is still there, it is a catalyst and the policy is in the protection column regardless of its stated intent.

**Falsifier:** a support that survives its own use and yet is observable as a spent part at the ground because the firm's own search now carries the method with no further payment. If both readings survive measurement, the catalyst test needs a finer instrument, which is recorded as a gap rather than resolved by assertion.

### Q4: Order by dependency, not by importance

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The dependency order is: perceive the failure at the slot, have terms for what the instrument affords, state a measurable objective, choose a route under incomplete information, then learn and carry. A country that adds a sophisticated monitoring instrument (link 5) while its externality is unmeasured (link 3) is optimizing above the break. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The world literature's repeated call for *"well-identified market failures"* (IMF) and *"Identifying the main causes of market and co-ordination failures is crucial"* (OECD) is the ordinary-language version of this dependency order.

**Falsifier:** a policy that closes the residual at a slot whose failure was never measured and whose objective was never stated, with the counterfactual still external. That would show the chain is not ordered by dependency in this domain.

### Q5: Is there a seventh policy term to mint?

Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**
Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

No. The instrument set is closed; the arrangements over it are not. What can be imported is a country-specific counterfactual or a new measurement from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test. Importing a novel-sounding policy label against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed, and Fig 6 :24 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives. The gap in `01` F10 is therefore a gap about parameter values and named bindings, not a missing seventh instrument.

**Falsifier:** a policy atom that is genuinely not composable from the held set and that the ground pays for, introduced with a source gate, a shadow test and a measured reduction of the residual.

## CONCLUSION

"Correct" and "protect" are **isomers over a fixed instrument set**: same atoms, different bonds, different anchors, and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list (Q1). The bond that matters is production, not sequence: a competitive sector that did not exist before the measure must be produced by it, and a sector that merely follows the measure is a transfer (Q2). A support that survives its own use is a catalyst and belongs in the protection column even if it was announced as temporary (Q3). The chain runs in dependency order, so monitoring cannot rescue an unmeasured failure (Q4), and no seventh policy atom is owed (Q5).

This framing rescues what was defensible in the ordinary debate without pretending a general answer exists: the type of the bond, not the amount of the instrument, is the variable. `metric that updates on the room = success` remains an unproven and refused binding; `temporary support and permanent support are isomers over the same instruments` is a checkable structural claim with four stated falsifiers.
