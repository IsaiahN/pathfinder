# The Tether's answer: comparing knowledge systems without forced categories

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is the shape and route of a comparison, not a result about any tradition. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No historian or practitioner was consulted as a ground.

## 1. The residual it answers

A historian faces several knowledge systems and must compare them. One option translates all of them into one vocabulary, which destroys the arrangement that makes each work and lets the historian's own frame decide the outcome. The other declares them incommensurable and stops, which produces an honest silence but no checkable claim. The residual is the third operation: comparison that preserves the difference that makes comparison informative.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **The anchor is the ground, not the category scheme.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** Each system is corrected separately by what the world returns to its practice: a harvest, a cure, a landfall, a building that stands. If the historian's taxonomy decides which system counts as knowledge, the taxonomy has become a frame sitting inside its own verification.
2. **The comparison is a conjunction of independent rangings, not a common measure.** Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Fig 8 :48: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."** The asset being compared is the difference of closures. A shared vocabulary spends that asset; it does not create it. Ordering the systems (folk to scientific) or applying a threshold ⋛ presupposes the metric in dispute.
3. **Describe effects, not categories.** Fig 6 :30: **"Cast it in effect terms."** Fig 9 :30: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."** Ask what the practice predicts and what comes back, not whether it is "really" science. Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**
4. **Agreement is a convention or one pool, never a witness.** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**; Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**; Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** Shared terms may be declared and used for coordination. They may not decide the verdict.
5. **The union is unbundled, and verdicts stay per slot.** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Never average several traditions into one "worldview." Where two overlap, give the overlap its own loop.
6. **Two systems over one habitat can be isomers.** Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Same plants, seasons and bodies; different arrangement; different knowledge. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` for the local condition and its falsifier.
7. **The stake is structural and declared.** Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."** Comparisons of knowledge systems sit inside histories of unequal power. An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying.
8. **The middle link is the missing instrument.** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Historians can describe practices and argue conclusions; what they lack are terms for what each situation affords in its own frame and for what counts as progress there. Building that vocabulary is the actual work.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all traditions are really science under other names. That is the forced-category error in reverse.
- Not that any tradition is more reliable in general. Such a ranking would need a single metric, which the derivation refuses.
- Not that incommensurability means no comparison. The SEP Kuhn entry says incommensurability *"did not mean non-comparability"*; comparison runs through effects and a shared ground.
- Not that religious knowledge can always be ranged against an external ground. Where no such anchor is nameable, the comparison is limited. The tradition is not judged. This is the live strain F7.
- Not a final taxonomy and not a new universal category scheme. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**
- Not `grounded`. No case study or measurement settles `left(R,φ)`.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A real comparative study, not a further argument:

1. Name an anchor that both frames are separately corrected by (an outcome neither can move).
2. Describe both frames in effect terms and record the provenance of every borrowed term.
3. **Case-study falsifier:** show that successful comparisons do *not* have the four properties in §2, or that a single shared category scheme outperforms effect-term description while the anchor is held fixed.
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a scheme that settles disagreement without moving; two overlapping frames producing a new ground-settled surplus; isomer status failing because the atom sets differ; a union cleared by one lever with no categorically different residue; a tradition with no external anchor yielding a ground-settled verdict.
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** one that distinguishes convergent derivation from adopted import where the contents look identical (Fig 8 :51). Until then, provenance is the only discriminator.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Which specific categories to use, whether a given practice is proto-science, and the final cross-frame term are declared `gap`s after a genuine re-census (F10). Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** and :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The answer stops where the available instruments cannot distinguish sources, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

One strain remains live and is not hidden: some traditions locate their anchor internally. The comparison is limited there; the tradition is not. Setting that strain to zero would make the pitch smoother and the work dishonest.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not supply a universal grid for comparing knowledge systems, and it does not declare them incommensurable. It says that comparison is possible without shared categories if each system is ranged separately against a ground neither can move, if each is described in what it predicts and what the world returns rather than in borrowed category names, if agreement between frames is treated as a convention or one evidence pool rather than as corroboration, and if verdicts are kept per system instead of averaged into a worldview. Where two systems address the same habitat under different arrangements they stand to each other as isomers, same atoms and different substance. The framework also names where it must stop: it will not pick the final taxonomy, rank the traditions, or pretend a tradition whose anchor is internal has been externally checked. What it offers is the shape a legitimate comparison must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the missing instrument, a vocabulary for what each situation affords in its own frame, that would close it.
