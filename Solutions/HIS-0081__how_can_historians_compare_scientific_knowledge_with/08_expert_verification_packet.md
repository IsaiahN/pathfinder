# Expert verification packet: the Tether on comparing knowledge systems

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian, anthropologist or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No historical case study or measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result about any tradition.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A comparison of scientific, practical, Indigenous, religious and craft knowledge is legitimate iff four conditions hold: (i) each system is ranged separately against a ground that neither system can move, the ground being whatever the world returns to the practice; (ii) each is described in effect terms, what it predicts and what comes back, rather than in borrowed category names; (iii) agreement between systems is treated as a declared convention or as one evidence pool, never as corroboration; and (iv) verdicts stay per system and are never averaged. Two systems over a shared habitat under different arrangements stand as isomers (same atoms, different substance), and the comparison is a symmetric conjunction of two rangings, not a common measure and not a ranking. The framework does not choose the final taxonomy; that is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the anchor is the ground, not the category scheme. A shared vocabulary is permitted only as a convention that does not decide the outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**; Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**
- **Refuter:** exhibit a comparison whose shared category scheme itself settles a cross-frame disagreement, and where the scheme does not move when the ground pushes on it. Or exhibit a declared convention that correctly decides an outcome with the anchor held fixed. Either breaks C1.

### C2: world-facing; checkable by a historian or anthropologist against a real case

- **Statement:** effect-term description plus independent ranging against a ground distinguishes a non-distorting comparison from forced categorization; category-label translation alone does not. The observed world does not decide which system is "really" science, and shared content does not establish convergent derivation.
- **What to check:** take one documented comparison (a folk taxonomy against a scientific taxonomy of the same flora, or a craft technique against its engineering account). Are the two frames separately corrected by an outcome neither can move? Are the borrowed terms declared? Does the comparison survive if the category labels are removed and only predictions and returns remain?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :30: **"Cast it in effect terms."** Fig 9 :30: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."** Fig 8 :51: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**
- **Refuter:** a comparison in translated category labels yields a ground-settled verdict that effect-term description cannot; or two frames drawing on identical evidence produce a new ground-settled surplus neither could reach alone. Either breaks C2.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a world-facing edge

- **Statement:** the compared systems form a union to be unbundled, not a partition to be averaged; where they share a habitat and a scale they are Fig 12 isomers; and where no external anchor is nameable for a tradition the comparison is limited, not the tradition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- **Refuter:** one category scheme clears every region of the comparison and leaves no categorically different residue (breaks the union claim); or one compared system needs an atom the other has no contact with (breaks the isomer claim for that pair); or a tradition with no external anchor still yields a ground-settled cross-frame verdict (breaks the limitation claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal grid, not "all traditions are really science," and not a ranking of traditions.
- Not that incommensurability means no comparison (SEP Kuhn: it *"did not mean non-comparability"*).
- Not that religious knowledge has been externally checked. F7 is a live strain: where the anchor is internal, the comparison stops.
- Not a final taxonomy or a seventh category. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a case study or measurement.
- Not that agreement among experts, prestige, or institutional authority is a witness. Fig 2 :40 applies to this packet too.

## 5. What would settle it

A real comparative study with an anchor both frames are separately corrected by, where the four conditions in §1 are tested against a case that the author did not choose to fit them. Specifically: (1) does removing category labels leave a checkable set of effects? (2) can each frame range against the anchor without the other frame moving it? (3) is the residue categorically different after one lever is applied, or was it a partition? (4) is a source-discriminating instrument available for convergent versus adopted content? A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :40-41, :45 and Fig 10 :47, :60 (six short passages). Check C1.
2. Take one comparative case you know well. Are the two frames separately corrected by an outcome neither can move, and do the borrowed terms get declared? Check C2.
3. Read Fig 9 :54 and Fig 12 :117. Is the case a union or a partition, and are the two systems isomers over a shared habitat? Check C3.
4. Name one tradition in your case whose anchor you cannot locate externally. Say whether the honest response is to limit the comparison or to judge the tradition. This is the live strain.
5. Send the refutation, or one line saying which condition failed. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
