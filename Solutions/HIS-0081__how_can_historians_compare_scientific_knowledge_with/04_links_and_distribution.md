## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **No common measure is not the same as no comparison.** The SEP Kuhn entry says incommensurability *"did not mean non-comparability."* This is the single most useful correction to the naive reading of "without forcing them into the same categories." It blocks Option B (total silence) while keeping the refusal of Option A (one vocabulary). Cite it when explaining why the answer is triangulation, not paralysis.
2. **External criteria distort; extracting only the testable parts dispossesses.** Mazzocchi (2006) gives both halves: *"Trying to analyse and validate traditional knowledge systems by using external (scientific) criteria carries the risk of distorting such systems in the process"* and *"we cannot extract just those parts of traditional knowledge that seem to measure up to scientific criteria and ignore the rest. This process of cognitive mining would atomize the overall system and threaten traditional knowledge with dispossession."* Together they are the world-facing statement of the figure verdict (Fig 2 :45, Fig 8 :53).
3. **Convergent content and adopted content look the same.** Feyerabend's observation that the same sentences make incompatible statements under different theories, plus the folk-biology evidence that some classifications converge across societies, shows why the *record of origin* matters. Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* Do not present a shared category as evidence of convergence until its provenance is shown.

**Overlap warning.** Mazzocchi cites Feyerabend; SEP Kuhn discusses Feyerabend; the TEK and Ethnoscience pages draw on the same anthropology literature. On the incommensurability claim they are one pool, not three independent witnesses. The source ledger records this. The two essays share an author with the figures and carry no independent weight.
