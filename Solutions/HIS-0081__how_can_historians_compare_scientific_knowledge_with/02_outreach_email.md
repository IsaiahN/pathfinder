# Outreach draft: check the shape of the comparison before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F7 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been read. The honest treatment of traditions whose anchor is internal is part of the claim, not a footnote.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about comparing knowledge systems, before any theory

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A historian wants to compare scientific knowledge with
> practical, Indigenous, religious and craft traditions without forcing them into the same
> categories. Two familiar options fail. Putting every system into one vocabulary destroys the
> arrangement that makes each one work. Declaring them incommensurable and stopping produces an
> honest silence, but no checkable comparison.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A comparison is legitimate iff four things hold:
> (1) each system is ranged separately against a ground that neither system can move, where the
> ground is whatever the world returns to the practice, a harvest, a cure, a landfall, a building
> that stands; (2) each system is described in effect terms, what it predicts and what comes back,
> rather than in borrowed category names; (3) agreement between systems is treated as a convention
> or as one evidence pool, never as corroboration; and (4) verdicts are kept per system and never
> averaged into a single "worldview." The comparison is a union of separate loops, not one
> categorization. This is a shape, not a measurement, and it names no final categories.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that all traditions are really science under other names. Not
> that one tradition is more reliable in general. Not that religious knowledge can be ranged
> against an external ground from outside; where I cannot name such an anchor, I say the comparison
> is limited rather than calling the tradition defective. Not that agreement among historians is a
> check. And not that the framework settles any of this: it returns the form a comparison must
> have, not the content of any tradition.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before writing this note:
> 1. a comparison in which the shared category scheme itself settles cross-frame disagreement,
>    and the scheme does not move when the ground pushes on it;
> 2. two systems drawing on identical evidence and experience produce a new ground-settled result
>    neither could reach alone;
> 3. a comparison in translated category labels yields a ground-settled verdict that effect-term
>    description cannot deliver;
> 4. one category scheme clears every region of the comparison and leaves no categorically
>    different residue standing;
> 5. a tradition with no nameable external anchor nonetheless yields a ground-settled cross-frame
>    verdict.
>
> The one that can really bite is a case study: a real comparative history with a declared anchor
> that both frames are separately corrected by, showing that successful comparisons do not have
> this shape. If any of these fires, I narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails
> because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is
> needed.
>
> **Stake, declared.** I have an interest in the outcome of this comparison; I have no interest in
> the reading. If you see a stake I have not named, tell me and I will record it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this repository
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a case you know
> well and can check against a ground. State your own anchor, write down what would refute your
> result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful
> to me than a reported success.

**Two-stage contract.** Stage 1 is the residual, shape and refuter above. Record the recipient's verdict **before** showing any theory. Stage 2, opt-in only, is the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because after reading the framework they share the author's evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
