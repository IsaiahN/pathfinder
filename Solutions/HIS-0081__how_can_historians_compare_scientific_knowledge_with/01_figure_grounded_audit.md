## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A historian holds several knowledge systems: modern science, practical and craft traditions, Indigenous knowledge systems, and religious traditions. The question asks for a comparison that does not *"forc[e] them into the same categories."*

- **Option A, shared categories.** Translate every system into one vocabulary, usually the scientific one. Effect: the arrangement that carries the system's knowledge is replaced by a flat ingredient list, the differences that made comparison informative are erased, and the verdict is really the historian's own frame talking. Mazzocchi (2006, EMBO Reports) names the failure: *"Trying to analyse and validate traditional knowledge systems by using external (scientific) criteria carries the risk of distorting such systems in the process."*
- **Option B, declared incommensurability.** Say there is no common measure and stop. Effect: an honest silence, but no checkable comparison. The question is abandoned rather than answered.
- **The residual R.** What operation permits comparison while preserving the difference that makes comparison worth doing? Equivalently: can two frames be compared without one becoming the measure of the other?

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run.** A comparison is legitimate iff (i) each frame is ranged separately against a ground that neither frame can move, (ii) the description of each frame is cast in effect terms rather than in borrowed category names, (iii) agreement between frames is treated as convention or as one evidence pool, never as corroboration, and (iv) verdicts are kept per frame and never averaged. This is the sought-for shape, written down so it can accrue a residual like anything else (Formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65).

**Anchor.** The world that returns outcomes to a practice: a harvest, a cured patient, a navigated landfall, a building that stands. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least three places, frame A, frame B, and the ground, with the historian occupying a separate checking seat. A two-place mapping between vocabularies is the wrong arity; it omits the anchor.
- **Symmetry:** the two knowledge systems are symmetric in the comparison. Neither is the measure of the other, and neither is the anchor. The comparison is a conjunction of two separate rangings (`+`, "both hold at once"), not a threshold relation (`⋛`) that would require one common quantity. The ordering relation `→` is not licensed either: Fig 4 :20 says *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- **Scale:** a tradition is a frame at one scale (a library plus a loop). The comparison is a second-order operation over frames. The transform between the practice scale and the comparison scale is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the scientific frame | researchers, instruments, theories, Γ_science | predictions and residuals against experiment | experiment and measurement | admitted |
| D2, the tradition frames (craft, practical, Indigenous, religious) | practitioners, apprentices, tools, place, transmitted texts or oral lines | reliability of practice, transmission, survival of the technique | outcomes in the world returned to the practice | admitted where a nameable anchor exists; see F7 and the strain |
| D3, the comparison seat (the historian) | historians, comparison conventions, descriptions that travel | residuals surfaced, conventions authored | the shared ground, never agreement and never the category scheme | admitted |
| D4, the ground | no actors; it is not a frame | what the world returns | itself | admitted |
| X1, a neutral universal category scheme | none nameable that is not somebody's frame | categorization | none that is not itself a frame | **not a domain yet** |
| X2, permanent incommensurability as a stance | none | silence | none | **not a domain; a refusal, recorded as a gap verdict** |

The engine room rule applies: no nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. X1 is the thing the question warns against. Pretending it is a domain is what "forcing into the same categories" means.

### Partition or union

D1 through D4 form a **union compound**, not a partition. Each tradition has its own loop, and the comparison is a new loop over the union. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Treating a union as a partition is the structural form of the forced-category error.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** what does the practice actually do, and what is in contact with it?
2. **Vocabulary:** what does the situation afford, *in that system's own terms*?
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress or settlement in that frame?
4. **Planning and comparison:** only now, how do the frames compare?

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The middle link is where the break usually is: Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Historians have instruments for describing practices and for arguing conclusions; the link between them, terms for what each practice affords and what counts as success in it, is where categories get imposed.

## 2. Findings

### F1: The anchor must be the ground, not the category scheme (structural, decisive)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**
Fig 2 :46: **"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."**

If the shared category scheme decides which tradition counts as knowledge, the scheme has become the anchor, and it is a frame listed as a party to its own verification. That is Fig 2's collapse 1 (mutual update): frames converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence. Historically the influence has been the scientific institution, which is exactly the asymmetry the question wants to avoid.

Fig 10 :47 reinforces it: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**

- **Mode:** `general` for the anchor rule, `specified` for the historian application.
- **Status:** triangulated, Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 10 (convention), low overlap between the ground figure and the seat figure.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a comparison whose shared category scheme itself settles a cross-frame disagreement, where the scheme does not move when the ground pushes on it. Under the figure that is impossible by definition, so the refuter would show the figure's anchor rule is wrong, not merely that a historian used a taxonomy.

### F2: Independence, not agreement in categories, is what makes comparison informative

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 8 :48: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**
Fig 8 :50: **"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**
Fig 13 :38: **"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything."**

The value of comparing a scientific frame with an Indigenous or craft frame is precisely that their closures differ. Translating both into one vocabulary spends that asset. Note the distinction in Fig 8 :50: shared content is not the signature of a bad comparison. Two frames can arrive at the same content separately, which is corroboration. The record, not the content, tells them apart (Fig 8 :51: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**).

- **Mode:** `general` for the independence quantity, `specified` for the comparison.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 8 and Fig 13, three distinct uses of the same quantity.
- **Refuter:** two knowledge systems demonstrably drawing on identical evidence and experience produce a new, ground-settled cross-frame result that neither could produce alone. If that happens, overlap is not the asset killer the figures say it is.

### F3: Describe the effect, not the category

Fig 6 :30: **"Cast it in effect terms."**
Fig 9 :30: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

Concretely: do not ask "is this healing practice science or religion?" (a category question that forces the practice into someone else's slots). Ask "what does this practice predict, and what does the world return when it is used?" (an effect question anyone can check). This is the bridge between F1 and the historian's actual method. The Decoding Tacit Knowledge essay (Isaiah N, 2026) puts the same rule for craft knowledge: **"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."**

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated, Fig 6 and Fig 9, with the essay as framework-internal corroboration only (shared author, no independent weight).
- **Refuter:** a comparison carried out purely in translated category labels yields a ground-settled cross-frame verdict that effect-term description cannot deliver. That would show effect description is not the necessary carrier.

**Strain recorded, not smoothed:** the SEP entry on Kuhn says **"Kuhn stressed that incommensurability did not mean non-comparability (just as the side and diagonal of a square are comparable in many respects)."** F3 must not be read as "no common measure means no comparison." It says the comparison runs through effects and a shared ground, not through a common category vocabulary. Re-census confirmed no conflict with the figures; the strain was in my first reading, not in the derivation.

### F4: It is a union to be unbundled, and verdicts must not be averaged

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Scientific, craft, Indigenous and religious knowledge are not four instances of one thing to be averaged into "how cultures know." Each is a slot with its own residual. Where two of them overlap, the overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a reason to merge them. The Library of Babel essay names the mirror error: **"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."**

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated, Fig 9 (union split) and Fig 1 (per-slot indexing).
- **Refuter:** one category scheme clears every region of the comparison and leaves no categorically different residue standing. Then it was a partition after all and the unbundling instruction was spent for nothing.

### F5: A shared vocabulary is allowed only as a convention

Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

Comparison needs some shared descriptive language. The figures do not forbid that. They forbid letting the language decide the outcome. The honest declaration is: "we use these terms as a convention for coordinating; they are not the ground and they do not rank the systems." Send the characterised residual first, the convention second.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated, Fig 2 and Fig 10.
- **Refuter:** a declared convention that decides a cross-frame outcome correctly, with the anchor held fixed. Then Fig 10 :47 is false as a ban.

### F6: Forced categorization is a REFUTED case, not a missing-term case

Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

The first four bins in the mint pipeline sort a gap by what is *missing*. The historian's universal category scheme is not missing; it is held, and it is false as a universal. What is owed is a refusal (negation, ¬), not a larger and more inclusive taxonomy. This matters operationally: the repair is to remove the scheme's standing to be composed over, not to build a grander scheme that finally contains every tradition. Fig 5 :51: *"The four kinds sort a gap by what is missing. None of them sorts a gap by what is wrong."*

- **Mode:** `specified`, framework-internal.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure mechanics (Fig 5 amendment); corroborated in effect by Mazzocchi (2006): **"Allowing science to be the final arbiter of the validity of knowledge, and to establish the threshold beyond which knowledge is not worthy of its name, would create the conditions whereby an astonishing cultural heritage is transformed into a monolithic structure."** Different field, same direction, but it is a world witness, not a second figure.
- **Refuter:** show the historian never held the universal scheme as universal, in which case there is no false term to refuse and F6 does not apply.

### F7: No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

This cuts both ways and must be applied honestly. For craft and practical traditions the anchor is usually easy to name: the tool works, the crop returns, the building stands. For religious traditions the anchor is often internal to the tradition (scripture, experienced insight, communal authority). If the only anchor is "the tradition says so," then by Fig 2 that is a frame-internal anchor, and the comparison cannot range that frame against the ground from outside. The honest report is not "this tradition is unscientific" but "I cannot name an external anchor here, so this comparison is limited." That is a `gap` in the historian's instrument, not a defect in the tradition.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** single-source (Fig 2 anchor rule), with the strain explicitly recorded.
- **Refuter:** identify a tradition with no nameable external anchor whose comparison nonetheless produces a ground-settled cross-frame verdict. Then the anchor requirement is too strong.
- **Strain:** the world source on TEK notes that TEK includes *"cosmology"* and *"culture and identity"* facets alongside *"factual observations."* A comparison that discards those facets to keep only the empirically testable ones is the *"cognitive mining"* Mazzocchi warns about. The strain is between the anchor requirement and the integrity of the tradition. It is named here, not resolved by fiat.

### F8: The stake is structural and must be declared

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."**

Comparisons of knowledge systems occur inside histories of colonization and unequal power. The TEK Wikipedia page records that *"The term TEK has been criticised as a form of intellectual appropriation that modifies traditional/indigenous knowledges to better fit a conventional Western modern science framework."* Fricker's hermeneutical injustice is the matching philosophical name: *"a gap in our collective interpretive resources unfairly disadvantages someone in terms of understanding their own specific social experiences"* (IEP, summarizing Fricker 2007). The historian's stake in the outcome cannot be removed; an interest in the reading must be declared so that it can be modelled by the person checking the work.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 and two low-overlap world fields (epistemology, Indigenous studies), but the world sources corroborate the *existence* of the stake, not the figure's verdict.
- **Refuter:** produce a comparison with a genuinely removable stake and show that declaration changes nothing. The figure says no such seat exists; a counterexample would narrow the rule.

### F9: The break is at the vocabulary and objective link

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Historians can describe a practice (perception) and can argue a conclusion (planning), but the middle link, terms for what a situation affords *in that frame* and what counts as progress *in that frame*, attracts the fewest instruments. Forcing categories is what happens when that link is assumed rather than measured. Mazzocchi (2006) corroborates from science studies: **"Western science and traditional knowledge systems have developed radically different strategies to create and transmit knowledge, and it is exceedingly difficult to analyse one form of knowledge using the criteria of another tradition."**

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated, Fig 3 plus the science-studies witness (different field, different author).
- **Refuter:** a comparison with no terms for what the practice affords in its own frame, but with a ground-settled verdict that survives contact. Then the middle link was not load-bearing.

### F10: What cannot be derived: a `gap`, stated plainly

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

No figure line governs: which specific categories a historian should finally use; whether a given practice is "really" a proto-science; which tradition is more reliable in general; or the final cross-frame term that would close the comparison. These are not silences. They are declared gaps. The framework returns the shape of a legitimate comparison (range each frame against the ground separately, in effect terms, with declared stakes and unbundled unions), not the name of the right taxonomy or a ranking of traditions.

- **Mode:** `general` for the limit, `specified` for its application here.
- **Status:** `gap` after a genuine re-census. Formula step 7 was run: SEARCHED (the figure set was re-grepped for a category-mapping line, none found), then INWARD (is the historian's representation adequate? it currently lacks terms for each frame's affordances, Fig 3), then OUTWARD (import a frame that already predicts the shape: comparative knowledge systems research supplies effect-term methods, but those are world sources, not a figure line that decides the verdict). The three remain distinct.
- **Refuter:** a figure line that directly governs the specific cross-frame category mapping after re-census. Until one appears, no naming is licensed.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in this folder is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A comparison whose shared category scheme is itself the thing that settles cross-frame disagreement, with a scheme that does not move when the ground pushes on it (breaks F1 and Fig 10 :47).
2. Two knowledge systems drawing on identical evidence and experience produce a new ground-settled cross-frame result neither could reach alone (breaks F2, Fig 2 :40 and Fig 8 :48).
3. A comparison carried purely in translated category labels yields a ground-settled verdict that effect-term description cannot (breaks F3).
4. One category scheme clears every region of the compound and leaves no categorically different residue standing (breaks F4).
5. A tradition with no nameable external anchor nonetheless yields a ground-settled cross-frame verdict (breaks F7).
6. A real comparative-history study, with an anchor both frames are separately corrected by, shows that the derived shape is not what distinguishes successful from distorting comparisons.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

**Guard on the refuter itself:** the refuter is stated so that the derivation could fail, not so that any result confirms it. Refuters 1 and 4 are nearly tautological against the figure text, which is a weakness, and it is recorded as such. Refuter 6 is the one that can genuinely bite, because it uses a ground outside the figures.

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Start from the residual, per tradition.** Write what failed to resolve in each system, indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19). Do not begin with a list of categories.
2. **Name the anchor for each frame, in effect terms.** Where none is nameable, say so and stop claiming a comparison (F7).
3. **Describe effects, not categories.** What does the practice predict, and what does the world return? (Fig 6 :30, Fig 9 :30).
4. **Range each frame separately against the ground.** Never let agreement between frames stand in for correction by the ground (Fig 2 :45, :46).
5. **Treat any shared vocabulary as a convention**, declared as such, and never let it decide an outcome (Fig 2 :41, Fig 10 :47).
6. **Unbundle the union before searching** (Fig 9 :54). Where two traditions overlap, give the overlap its own loop.
7. **Build the missing middle instrument**: terms for what each situation affords in each frame, and for what counts as progress there (Fig 3 :27). This is the work the question actually asks for.
8. **Declare the stake** (Fig 10 :60), and keep verdicts separate, never averaged (Fig 1 :22).
9. **Stop at the shape.** Name no final category and rank no tradition (Fig 9 :32). Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.
10. **Send the generator, not the recording.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** A historian given the method can rerun it on a case the author never saw; a historian given the author's taxonomy can only repeat it.

### Round-trip note

A condensation of the figures into the eight-step loop loses information (Fig 4 :30). The round-trip gap R_T here is nonzero: the shorthand "compare against the ground in effect terms" drops (i) the convention/verdict distinction, (ii) the union-before-partition requirement, (iii) the declared-stake rule, and (iv) the gap verdict for specific mappings. `11_audit.md` enumerates these and refuses to set R_T to zero.

### Verdict of this audit

The shape survives: a category-free comparison is triangulation of separate frames against a shared ground, described in effect terms, with declared stakes, unbundled unions, and per-slot verdicts. The forced-category option is refused as a false held term, and the total-silence option is refused as an unexamined gap. Which categories to finally use is not derived here; that is a declared `gap`. After re-census: **one live strain (F7, tradition anchors), no hidden contradictions.** Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
