# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have survived a first read by someone outside the framework. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about legal stability and change, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about legal institutions. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the demand that a legal institution be both stable and adaptable is a union of conditions, not one dial with an optimal setting. Three things have to hold at once, and they have different anchors. Predictability needs a reference the deciding seat cannot move; adaptation needs a refusal available before a new rule is minted; the transmission of a ruling from one case to the next needs a method, not a recording of one occasion. The reference stays unmoved while the channel to it is monitored for staleness. A rule that has stopped paying is first refused, and the refusal is recorded without deleting the old rule. Only then is a replacement minted. The social conditions are treated as habitat to be enumerated, not as a standing list of good actors and not as the ground itself.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a universal stability percentage, an amendment threshold, a standard of review, or that any particular court or test is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A specific institution is a binding from legal practice onto that shape, provenanced as such, and carries zero evidentiary weight until it is checked. Nor am I claiming that prestige or agreement settles a reform. Prestige can fund a trial of a change; it cannot settle it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A legal order in which the reference for expectation demonstrably changes when the deciding seat pushes on it, and an independent reviewer reports no drift and no stale channel.
> 2. A doctrine that transmits only one-occasion outcomes, with no method or reasoning bond, and still generates novel correct rulings under changed conditions.
> 3. A system that can only accumulate rules, has no way to record one as wrong, and shows no fall of reach or saturation.
> 4. A single lever that clears both the stability and the adaptation residual with one anchor and nothing left over.
>
> I wrote these down before putting the claim in front of anyone. If one holds, I will withdraw the affected part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - WATSON 60, the figures as metalaws (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it; the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and its refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the figures as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits method transfer; Fig 5 forbids treating one's own output as a measure of success.

No trust is requested, no agreement is requested, and no bulk send is planned.
