## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Wagner 2008 splits robustness by level.** Genotype robustness and evolvability are antagonistic, while phenotype robustness and phenotype evolvability are positively associated. Legal analogue, marked `specified` and unmeasured: freezing the text of each individual rule is genotype-style robustness and reduces the variety of responses available; making the maintaining arrangement robust (a stable reference, a monitored channel, a recorded refusal) is phenotype-style robustness and increases accessible variation. This corroborates the Fig 4 :22 method-not-recording result from a genuinely low-overlap field. It is an analogue, not evidence about courts.
2. **Hart's secondary rules make change internal to legality.** The rule of recognition, rules of change and rules of adjudication are social rules practiced by officials. This corroborates the claim that an institution can change its rules without losing validity, but it also produces the live strain S1 in `01`: the anchor of validity is itself a moving practice. The figure verdict is not flipped by this conflict; the census is returned to and the two questions, validity and payment against a social condition, are separated.

A third point is recorded as a strain rather than a corroboration: Ashby's good regulator theorem says the regulator must model its environment, which sounds opposed to Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Re-census dissolves it by seat (controller versus verifier), and the dissolution is recorded in `01` §8.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Lead with the shape claim and the refuter. Sending the whole architecture before a reader has described a residual sends the interior and spends the reader's independence.
- **Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Do not ask for agreement. Ask what would count as a failure.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".** Ask what the reader would expect to see if the shape claim were true before offering any theory.
- **Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** A mistaken legal reform is costly and often hard to reverse, so independence is worth paying for here, not optimizing away.
- **Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it".** Agreement about legal vocabulary is fine and useful. The prohibition is on treating agreement as a verdict about the world.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the four refuters; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures are the authority; (4) the figures as a method to reuse, with each user writing their own refuter and mode.

## The two-stage offer (third stage is opt-in)

Stage 1 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45, frames range against the ground separately. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement is no longer evidence (Fig 2 :40). Stage 3 sends a method, which Fig 4 :22 permits, but each user needs a ground and a pre-registered refuter of their own, because the Loop's "Direction" section says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The refuter is published with the claim so it can fail in public. Nothing here is sent by this agent.
