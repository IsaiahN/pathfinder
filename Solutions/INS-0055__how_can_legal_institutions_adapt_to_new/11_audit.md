## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Three conditions bind at once, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."), not a dial. Symmetry: "adapt" is ordered, "stable" is a threshold (Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"). Scale: case slot, doctrine, habitat, verification seat.
- **Domain 1, adjudication.** Actors: judge, parties, rule, case facts. Currency: a ruling that pays or fails. Anchor: the case record and evidence neither side can move. Union member, anchored.
- **Domain 2, doctrine and institution.** Actors: courts, legislature, precedent, doctrine. Currency: methods and head starts across the membrane. Anchor: the practiced rule of recognition plus the legal ground. Overlaps D1 and D3.
- **Domain 3, social habitat.** Actors: conditions, customs, technologies, markets, affected people. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground that settles whether the rule pays; the habitat itself updates (Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does.").
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: reviewing seat, legal community, constitutional text, public. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run its independence from the other three is zero.

Order by dependency: describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); settle a first-order ruling before composing doctrine (Fig 12 :104); keep the channel reachable (Fig 10 :24).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29 and :34, Fig 4 :22 and :25, Fig 5 :53, Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 10 :24 and :72, Fig 11 :43 and :73, Fig 12 :104, Operators :58.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand used in the folder, stability is an unmoved reference and a live channel; adaptation is refusal before mint; methods cross, recordings do not; the habitat is enumerated; imports need provenance.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete legal order, with a named court, a named review standard, a stability metric and a defined amendment threshold.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **The per-slot indexing of the residual.** A shorthand that says "the legal order is stable" has already aggregated away the case where the rule has stopped paying (Fig 1 :22).
- **The refusal operator.** A shorthand that says "the law updates" can describe addition and subtraction only. The unary negation that keeps the refused rule in the record while dropping its standing is lost unless it is carried explicitly (Operators :58).
- **The convention/verdict seam.** "A reliance interest" reads as a quantity to be optimized and becomes a verdict-producing standard. Fig 10 :47 forbids that promotion; the shorthand does not enforce it.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a reference that must not update when pushed, read through a channel that can go stale, and a method rather than a replay crossing between a case and the doctrine. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was not measured numerically, because no legal ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
