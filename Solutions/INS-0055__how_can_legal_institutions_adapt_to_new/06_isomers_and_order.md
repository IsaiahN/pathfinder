### Q2:What are the bonds?

- **"Stable" is not a stock, it is a threshold.** Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities". The comparison bond ⋛ has the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?" (Operators table). So "predictable enough" is ⋛ applied to expectation and outcome, and the threshold is set by the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **"Adapt" is an ordered response.** Fig 12 :71 "A then B; B needs A to have happened" describes →. Refusal before mint is a sequence, not a conjunction. Writing the shape as "anchor + channel + refusal + mint" with + would hide the order, and Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **A settled ruling produces an atom for the next case.** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." This is ⇒ production at one level and ≡ identity at the next: the ruling is not waiting to be produced again, it now serves as an operand. Whether the bond is ⇒ or ≡ depends on the level, which is Fig 12 :103 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."
- **A refusal is ¬, not −.** Operators :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A rule that is refused stays in the record; what falls is its standing to be composed over. A legal order with only addition and subtraction can say that an exception was carved out and cannot say that the rule is wrong. That is the structural defect F3 names.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a legal order can be shown to require a symmetric bond (where swapping operands changes nothing) for the relation between a rule and its refusal, the ordered/refusal reading fails; refusal would then be mere absence and the accumulation finding collapses.

### Q3:Are the two legal orders isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- As commonly written, "a stable legal order" and "an adaptable legal order" share **only a vocabulary word**, not an atom set. One is described with reference and threshold; the other with change and replacement. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two members of a **functional group**: Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Both are members of the group "institution that maintains an anchor under change."
- The stronger version: fix the atom set `{reference, channel, rule, refusal, method, habitat, provenance}` and let two legal orders differ only in which structure mediates which reference, for example one routes case rulings through a published-method structure and the other routes them through a personnel-loyalty structure. Then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because the two orders are indistinguishable at the level of their stated ingredients ("we value stability and fairness") and differ in the arrangement that carries the load.
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." A legal order's constitution lists ingredients. Its binding arrangement is what decides behaviour.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either order can be written without an atom the other requires (one has no channel, or no refusal, or no habitat enumeration), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q4:What is the order of the union, and what breaks if it is reversed?

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The dependency chain for this question:

1. perception: the residual is indexed per case slot (Fig 1 :19);
2. vocabulary: there are terms for what a changed situation affords (evidence, method, unworkability);
3. objective: what counts as progress is stated in the institution's own terms, through the threshold ⋛;
4. planning: refusal is routed before mint;
5. learn and carry: a settled ruling becomes an atom for doctrine, and its provenance travels with it.

A break between 3 and 4 is the characteristic failure: a legal institution can name what it values and say what would count as improvement, but has no refusal operator and therefore no route to a changed rule. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link attracts the least attention because it has neither the obvious failures of perception nor the obvious tests of planning.

**Falsifier:** a legal order shown to have a working refusal route while its objective link (what counts as progress) is unmeasured. Then the break is elsewhere and this ordering is wrong.

### Q5:Analogues, and their limits

Three structural analogues were opened and each is graded as analogue only.

- **Wagner 2008** (biology): "In sum, a highly robust RNA genotype has low evolvability. In contrast, a highly robust phenotype has high evolvability." Shape analogue: freezing each rule reduces available variation; making the maintaining arrangement robust increases it. This corroborates the method-not-recording shape, not any fact about courts.
- **Ashby, via Variety (cybernetics):** "only variety can destroy variety". Shape analogue: a legal regulator needs enough distinguishable responses to match the variety of the habitat it faces. The associated good regulator theorem strains against Fig 10 :42 and was dissolved by seat re-census in `01` §8.
- **Hart, via SEP Legal Positivism:** the rule of recognition, rules of change and rules of adjudication are practiced social rules. Shape analogue: change is internal to legality. This one also generates the live strain S1, because the validity-anchor is a moving practice.

**Falsifier for all three:** a legal system measured to show that freezing individual rules *increases* the variety of available responses at the maintaining level, or that a low-variety regulator tracks a high-variety habitat without loss. Either would break the analogy and send the census back.

## CONCLUSION

The five-part separator is not a list of ingredients. It is a typed arrangement: a threshold on expectation, an ordered refusal before mint, a production/identity relation between a settled ruling and the next case, a unary negation for refused rules, and a one-way introduction rule over the habitat. Two legal orders over the same atoms but with different mediating structures are isomers (Fig 12 :117); as ordinarily described they are only members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123). The dependency chain breaks at the objective/refusal link when the vocabulary can name what the institution wants but has no term for a rule being wrong. Specific institutions remain a `gap`: Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
