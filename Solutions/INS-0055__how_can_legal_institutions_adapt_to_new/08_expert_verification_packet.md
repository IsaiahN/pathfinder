# Expert verification packet: the Tether on legal adaptation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a legal scholar or institutional designer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the concrete institution. Not `grounded`.** Nothing here is asserted as settled by a court, a dataset or an experiment.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The demand that a legal institution be stable and adaptable is a union of conditions, not one dial. Stability is a non-updating reference read through a channel that can go stale, not frozen text. Adaptation is a refusal before a mint: a rule whose residual persists and does not trend down is refused, kept in the record, and only then replaced. What crosses from a case into doctrine is the method and its reasoning bond, not the one-occasion outcome. Social conditions are habitat to be enumerated, not the ground. Borrowed doctrine needs provenance; agreement and prestige can fund a reform but cannot settle it; conventions must not be promoted into verdicts; and the checking room should change when the question changes. The framework returns that shape and written refuters, not a court design or a stability percentage.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1:Ground and channel; checkable by reading Fig 2 and Fig 10
- **Statement:** predictability is a non-updating reference plus a reachable channel. A legal reference that moves when the deciding seat changes its mind becomes another frame, and the resulting drift is coherent and invisible from inside.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 2 :34 "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
- **Refuter:** show a legal order whose reference demonstrably changes when the seat pushes on it, where an independent reviewer with separate evidence reports no drift and no stale channel. Then the anchor test fails at the ground.
- **World check:** Cornell LII's public statement of the predictability rationale for stare decisis, and the framework essay's line "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."

### C2:Refusal before mint; checkable by reading Fig 5 and the Operators table
- **Statement:** a rule that has stopped paying owes a refusal first. The refusal is a negation, so it stays in the record while the refused rule loses its standing to be composed over. A legal order with only addition and subtraction can record an exception and cannot record that the rule is wrong.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Operators :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 5 :59 "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at".
- **What to check:** does the institution have a public mechanism for recording that a rule is wrong, as distinct from carving out an exception or adding a newer rule on top? Is the persistent-and-flat residual test a recognizable description of how unworkable precedents are actually identified? The U.S. Supreme Court's own "stare decisis is not an 'inexorable command'" with departure for decisions "unworkable or are badly reasoned" (Cornell LII) is the world's version of this bin.
- **Refuter:** a legal order shown to accumulate rules with no refusal operator, no fall of reachable doctrine and no channel saturation. Or a demonstration that the persistent-and-flat residual test selects the wrong rule in a real line of cases.

### C3:Method, not recording; checkable by reading Fig 4 and Fig 12
- **Statement:** a ruling settles at first order and can then serve as an atom for doctrine. What crosses the membrane is the reapplicable method and its bond, not a replay of the result. A doctrine that transmits only outcomes will repeat past successes and fail on novel cases.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- **What to check:** when a precedent is extended to a case it did not cover, what is transmitted, the reasoning structure or the outcome? Does the written record carry the bond, and does a later reader have to re-derive it?
- **Refuter:** a doctrine shown to transmit only one-occasion outcomes, with no method or reasoning bond, that still generates novel correct rulings across multiple changed case slots.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not an optimal stability percentage, amendment threshold, standard of review or term limit. No figure returns those; it is a declared `gap`.
- Not that any particular court, constitution or test is *the* answer. Specific institutions are bindings from the legal frame, provenanced candidates, not derivations.
- Not that "stability" is a stock to be maximized by freezing text.
- Not that the social condition is the ground. It is the habitat; the ground is what settles whether the rule still pays.
- Not that agreement among experts or the prestige of a court settles a reform. Those can fund a trial; they cannot settle it.
- Not that every convention is bad. Agreement about shared legal meaning is the product. The failure is a convention that decides an outcome.
- Not `grounded`. No legal outcome was measured in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved whether a green reading came from one source or several.

1. Independent-seat test: does the reference stay unmoved when pushed, as measured by a reviewer drawing on separate evidence?
2. Recording test: is the refused rule retained in the record and removed from the reachable doctrine?
3. Novel-case test: does the transmitted object carry the reasoning bond, so a case the precedent never covered can be decided?
4. Habitat test: does the enumeration reach second-order contact, or stop at the direct actors?
5. Provenance test: can independent reviewers tell convergent derivation from adopted import by the record, not by the contents?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :34, Fig 10 :24 and Fig 5 :53, plus the Operators entry for negation. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against a jurisdiction you know: is there a public way to record that a rule is wrong, and is "unworkable" actually used as a refusal condition?
3. Check C3 against one precedent extension you remember: was the reasoning bond transmitted or only the result?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish an unmoved reference from coherent drift in that jurisdiction, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
