**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about quantum critical scaling and holography

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Near a quantum critical point, microscopically different materials return the same critical exponents and the same scaling functions. Something in the world is producing the same numbers in more than one description. Two readings are on offer: the exponents are the shape of a renormalization-group fixed point inside the material, or they are read off a bulk gravitational geometry. I want to know which parts of the claim survive, and where each description earns its keep.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The two readings are not one question with opposite answers. They are a union of two conditions with different grounds. The exponent values are a shape generated inside the material's own closure, indexed per universality class; the gravitational frame does not add a primitive that the fixed-point reading lacks. Its genuine surplus is at finite temperature and real time, where the boundary calculation is hard and the bulk geometry makes a prediction. "Deeper" is not a rank in a hierarchy of rooms, it is a second closure with a lossy transform between; the loss has to be measured, not assumed to be zero. No figure line, and no result I have, names a particular bulk geometry for a particular material, so that slot stays open.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That a specific gravitational dual has been identified for a real quantum critical material. That a power law by itself establishes criticality. That a bulk geometry is unique given a set of exponents. That "gravity explains quantum criticality" has been measured. Those are bindings from one frame onto another, and until they carry provenance and a source-discriminating measurement they have no evidentiary weight. I am also not asking you to trust a framework. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before this run:
> 1. a measured exponent that requires two irreconcilable fixed points with no discriminator between them;
> 2. a bulk prediction that exactly duplicates what the boundary frame already computes, so no gap is closed and the import is a recording;
> 3. a measured pair of critical exponents for a material claimed to have a weakly coupled bulk dual that lies outside every consistency region the bulk allows;
> 4. one of the six baseline terms of the framework derived from the other five, or a direct state-to-state bond admitted where the framework forbids one.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. Please record your verdict on the claim above before opening them, because a reader who has absorbed the framework first is no longer an outside check.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the loop file. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
