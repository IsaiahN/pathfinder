# Universality classes, order and the RG route

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Two descriptions of one QCP are on the table: a boundary fixed point and a bulk geometry. Two supporting questions are owed. (1) If the atom set is held fixed, are different universality classes **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or merely members of a functional group? (2) What is the **order** of the route that connects a tuning parameter to a scaling function, and does the bulk add an atom to that route?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) plus Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. Parameters already in the physics frame: the exponents ν, z, θ, η. No new primitive is available without import. The exponents are derived quantities (Fig 13 :124-125), not a seventh member.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the bulk radial direction a seventh primitive? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST READING

- Fig 13 :43-44: "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."
- Sachdev (2012), opened 2026-11-23: "The gravity theory on AdS_{D+1} maintains the complete 'history' of the renormalization group flow in the structure of the metric as a function of u." The radial coordinate u is the RG scale. It is a **parameter of a composition**, not a new kind.
- Scale invariance, power laws, smoothness and a continuum are what a composition *leaves behind* (Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member").
- ⇒ **I was wrong on the first pass** to read the bulk radial direction as a seventh term. **Retracted.** Kept here as a record of the wrong census. Triangulated (Fig 13 :43-44 plus Fig 13 :124-125 plus the world source).

### Q2 - Are two universality classes isomers over a fixed atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."
- Write two critical descriptions over the same six atoms. Class A: a relativistic fixed point, `Space – Spectrum – Time` with z = 1. Class B: a Lifshitz, hyperscaling-violating fixed point, `Space – Gradient – Time` with z > 1 and θ ≠ 0. Same atoms, different bond and different binding of the exponent parameters, different substance (different exponents, different scaling functions). Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**.
- The weaker reading: if the two classes are written with different atom sets (one needs a structure the other does not), they share only a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."). The group here is `state – structure – state`, one cross-kind crossing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either class can be written without an atom the other requires (for example a QCP that needs no structure between space and time, or one that requires a primitive outside the six), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands instead. A direct state-to-state bond would fail the stronger and the weaker claim both.

### Q3 - What is the order of the RG route?

- Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." And: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- The route to a scaling function has typed order:
  1. **Sequence** (→): tune g toward gc; the flow is A then B, and B needs A to have happened. Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened."
  2. **Comparison** (⋛): test whether the flow has reached a fixed point; this is a threshold relation between quantities, the definition of the fixed-point condition.
  3. **Subtraction** (−): distance from the fixed point, i.e. |g − gc|, which is what makes an operator relevant or irrelevant.
  4. **Negation** (¬): refuse the claim that the exponents come from a direct state-state bond, if that claim is offered.
- A vocabulary holding only the exponents (the ingredients) could name the end state but could not name this route. The figures' bond set is what makes the route statable. Imada et al. (2010) report a quantum tricritical point with "the concomitant divergence of an order parameter and uniform fluctuations, in contrast to the conventional quantum critical point," which is exactly a different bond arrangement over a similar atom set, not a different list of ingredients.
- **Falsifier:** if the flow to the QCP can be fully specified by the exponent values alone, with no typed ordering, comparison and subtraction, then the bond analysis adds nothing and this claim is void.

### Q4 - Does the bulk cross as a generator or as a replay?

- Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not."
- Fig 4 :22-23: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- Sachdev (2012): the bulk computes finite-temperature real-time correlators that conventional field theory and numerical methods struggle with. That is a **generator crossing**: a method produces new predictions.
- A claim of the form "this material's exponents equal those of geometry Y" is an **instance replay**: a recording of one matching occasion. It crosses only if the geometry predicts something further that is then checked.
- Dong et al. (2012) illustrate the honest form: "the metric (1.3) will only give a good description of the dual theory in a certain range of r." A window of validity, with the loss named, is a generator report. An unconditional identification is a replay.
- **Falsifier:** show a bulk-to-boundary mapping that predicts no observable not already predicted by the boundary fixed point, at any temperature. Then the crossing is a replay and the word "deeper" has no content.

### Q5 - What order is the import itself?

- Fig 9 :43: "The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."
- Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
- Applied here: (1) verdict, the boundary closure cannot currently close the finite-temperature real-time gap; (2) lookup, the frame whose closure predicts the shape of that gap is the gravitational/bulk frame; (3) arrival, the allowed (z, θ) region is used to narrow candidates and the ground (a measured scaling function) decides. Source gate and shadow test attach at step 2 (Fig 6 :12-13).
- The ⋛ type question remains live. The Operators table defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities." At second order the operands are scaling functions and exponent regions, which count as quantities; the bounds from the null energy condition are comparisons between them. Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom at the next level. Whether an exponent region is a quantity at that level is a type question that needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

Two universality classes over the fixed six-term atom set are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition when their atom sets coincide and their bonds differ; where the atom sets differ they are members of a **functional group** instead. Both readings have pre-registered falsifiers. The RG route is a typed sequence with a comparison, a subtraction and a refusal, so an exponent list alone cannot express it. The bulk frame crosses legitimately only as a generator of new finite-temperature predictions; as a one-occasion identification it is a recording and cannot support the word "deeper." Naming the radial coordinate a seventh primitive was my first-pass error and is retracted above.
