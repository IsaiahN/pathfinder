## 1. The residual it answers

Near a quantum critical point (QCP) a material shows power-law response functions and data collapse with exponents shared by microscopically different materials. The question asks whether those exponents "reflect a deeper gravitational structure."

Stated as an effect: **the same numbers arrive in different closures.** A neutron-scattering measurement on one material, a specific-heat measurement on another and a transport measurement on a third return exponent values that one low-energy description can organise. Two readings are on offer. Option A, the fixed-point reading, says the exponents are the shape of a renormalization-group (RG) fixed point inside the material, with measured exponents and scaling functions as ground; no gravitational atom is needed. Option B, the gravitational reading, says the exponents are read off a bulk geometry (AdS, Lifshitz or hyperscaling-violating spacetime) claimed to be deeper than the material, with bulk consistency plus reproduction of boundary correlators as ground. A yields verdicts per universality class and stops where the RG cannot compute finite-temperature real-time dynamics. B yields constraints on the allowed exponent region and a working method for those finite-temperature questions, but owes a source gate and a shadow test before any of it is called an explanation of a measured exponent.

The ordinary term bound before the run (Formula "Direction", Fig 5 :65: "Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else") was: *a shape for the scaling plus a statement of which closure supplies which part of that shape*. It is written down so it can be refused rather than smuggled in as a preference.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a specific gravitational dual has been identified for a real quantum critical material. No measurement is taken and no dual is named in this folder.
- Not that a power law by itself establishes criticality. Beggs (2022) notes that "Power laws, in contrast, are typically described by only one number, their slope" and that several non-critical processes produce power laws. The residual is a universal scaling function with interlocking exponents, indexed per class.
- Not that a bulk geometry is unique given a set of exponents. Several constructions can give the same (z, θ); the reading does not determine the source.
- Not that gravity explains quantum criticality has been measured, and not a derivation of one closure from the other or a new primitive. Scale invariance is what a composition leaves behind, not a seventh term: Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."
