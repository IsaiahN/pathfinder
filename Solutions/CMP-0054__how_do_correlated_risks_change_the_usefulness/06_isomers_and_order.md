# Diversification as a union, correlation as a collapse: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the network analogues.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit in `01` established that diversification usefulness tracks independent slots, not named slots, and that correlation is a hidden shared source. Two structural questions remain:

1. What kind of composition is a diversified collection, and what does correlation do to it?
2. Are a low-correlation network and a high-correlation network over the same parts different *things*, or just different spellings of one thing?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (atoms and bonds held)

Atoms held: slots (holdings, species, components), losses, a ground (realised outcomes), a hidden shared source, evidence pools. Bonds held: the seven. Operators: negation. No new primitive is available without an import. The Operators table gives the tests; the bond is the part of the arrangement the operands do not carry. Fig 12: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information".

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What composition is diversification at rest?

- Fig 12 :23: "this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"
- Operators table :15: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." (the test for `+`, conjunction)
- Fig 12 :92: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"

A diversified collection at rest is a **symmetric conjunction** of `k` slots: `A + B + C + ...`. Swap two holdings, two species or two components and the collection is the same term. There is no order among them. The `k!` spellings are one term, and a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by `log2(k!)`. This is why counting arrangements of the same slots cannot be a measure of diversification.

**Falsifier (Q1):** exhibit a diversified collection whose behaviour changes when two otherwise identical slots are exchanged. Then the symmetric-conjunction reading is wrong.

### Q2: What does correlation do to the composition?

- Operators table :27: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." (the test for `≡`, identity)
- Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."
- Fig 13 :39: "Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."
- Fig 2 :6-7: "collapse 2: common cause", "(hidden shared source)"

Correlation is **not a new bond**. It is a fact about the provenance of the operands that changes which bond is load-bearing. Under full overlap, the `k` operands are no longer `k` independent terms. They are one referent with `k` names, which is the identity collapse `≡`. The conjunction `A + B + C` does not become a different composition; it becomes `A + A + A`, and the surplus of the union goes to zero (Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus").

**This is a strain, named rather than hidden.** The figures' bond grammar relates two operands by a typed relation; correlation is not one of the seven typed relations. It is closer to a shared-source fact about the operands, which the grammar records in provenance rather than in the bond slot. Fig 12 :90-91: "A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet." Correlation is not a claim that has not been made; it is made in Fig 2 as a collapse genus. So it belongs to the failure taxonomy, not to the bond table. Re-census sends it to Fig 2, and the composition reading above is kept as an analogue, not as a derived bond.

**Falsifier (Q2):** show two slots that share an instrumented source yet remain distinct terms under the identity test (substituting one for the other loses something). Then the collapse reading fails for that case.

### Q3: Are the networks isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two networks over the same slot set, one with low coupling and one with high coupling, have the same atoms. Their behaviour under stress is decisive and invisible in a listing of parts. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, provided the difference can be recorded as a bond difference. Because correlation is not a bond (Q2), the strict isomer claim is **strained**. The claim that survives re-census is the weaker one: the two networks share a **functional group**, "many partially independent slots combined", and the coupling regime is the qualifier that says which member. This is checkable: hold the slot set fixed, vary only the coupling, and see whether loss under stress differs.

**Falsifier (Q3, written before the run):** if two networks with the same slot set and different measured coupling structures suffer the same loss under the same stress, the qualifier is empty and the functional-group distinction collapses. Then F1 and F4 of `01` need re-census.

### Q4: What order is the failure?

- Fig 12 :70-73: "sequence", "A then B; B needs A to have happened"
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

At rest diversification is symmetric (`+`). In failure it becomes **ordered** (`→`): one slot fails, its load moves to another, and that one fails. The cascading-failure source fetched for `04` describes exactly this: "the failure of one or few parts leads to the failure of other parts, growing progressively as a result of positive feedback". Where the load returns to the start, the ordered chain closes into a cycle, and the sign of the feedback decides whether it closes. A chain cannot close by construction, so a model that only permits chains cannot represent reignition.

So the full structural claim is: **diversification is symmetric at rest and ordered in collapse.** A risk model that uses only a symmetric conjunction (a static variance) cannot represent the order; a model that uses only a sequence cannot represent the diversification. Both readings are needed and they are not additively combined (Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add").

**Falsifier (Q4):** exhibit a cascade with no order of propagation (all slots fail simultaneously with no load transfer), or a diversified collection at rest whose slots have a required order. Then the symmetry/order split is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

Diversification is a symmetric conjunction of slots. Correlation is an identity collapse driven by a hidden shared source, not a new bond, and it drives the union surplus to zero as overlap approaches one. Networks with the same parts under different coupling regimes share a functional group, with the coupling as qualifier; the stricter isomer claim is strained on the bond type and is named as such. At rest the system is symmetric; in collapse it is ordered and can close into a cycle. Four falsifiers were written before the run, one per question.
