# Expert verification packet: correlated risk and the usefulness of diversification

**What this is:** a claim in a form a systemic-risk or correlation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on each domain application, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed by this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Diversification usefulness is a function of independent slots, not named slots. When holdings, species or components share a hidden source, they are one bet with many names and the surplus of the union goes to zero. Correlated risk is not a new kind of risk but the common-cause collapse in which parts that never interact fail together. This effect has a valence: it is disqualifying for a verdict about independent exposure and productive for a convention that lives on shared meaning, so an unqualified claim that correlation always destroys diversification is wrong. The accounting is in effective bets via the standard correction for correlated observations; the cost of being wrong sets the independence budget; and the source that matters is often three steps out. The identity of the source in any particular system stays open until an instrument names it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** a diversified collection's benefit is decided by how little its members draw on one evidence pool; at heavy overlap the collection is one frame with many names and its union surplus is zero.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :48 "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 13 :39 "Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."
- **Refuter:** show a collection whose realised loss reduction does not fall as instrumented overlap rises with other terms held fixed, or a zero-overlap collection with zero surplus where the rule predicts one.

### C2: domain-facing; checkable by a researcher in finance, ecology or infrastructure

- **Statement:** correlated risk is the hidden-common-cause collapse, not a direct interaction among the parts; a name count is therefore not an effective-bet count.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :6 "collapse 2: common cause", Fig 2 :7 "(hidden shared source)", Fig 2 :19 "they never talk, and still agree", Fig 2 :21 "not two frames confirming each other"; Fig 2 :44 "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** in a system you know, are the co-failing parts actually independent conditional on an instrumented source, or do they share one? Does the correlation-adjusted effective count predict the realised joint loss under stress better than the name count?
- **Refuter:** instrument the candidate source in one domain and show the parts' losses remain independent conditional on it; or show that the effective-count accounting fails to predict realised joint loss under stress.

### C3: cross-cutting; checkable by anyone who has seen a connectivity tradeoff

- **Statement:** the effect has a valence. Rising correlation is disqualifying for a verdict about independent exposure and productive for a convention about shared meaning. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and that price is a budget filter, never a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a convention-function made worse by rising agreement, or a verdict-function made better by correlation approaching one; or show the budget rule being used to declare a specific holding or species good.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numerical correlation threshold, and not a functional form for usefulness against correlation.
- Not a name for the correlating source in any specific portfolio, grassland or grid. The slot stays `???`.
- Not that all connection is bad. Middle ranges of connectivity can be shock-absorbing; the valence rule is conditional.
- Not that adding names never helps. It helps until the covariance floor; the floor is a ground quantity.
- Not a separate theory for each of the three networks. One law, three casts; verdicts kept separate and never averaged.
- Not `grounded`. This packet records a shape, not a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating measurement under stress, not a further argument:

1. **Source test:** instrument the candidate common cause and re-measure joint losses conditional on it.
2. **Effective-count test:** compare realised loss reduction against the correlation-adjusted effective number of slots.
3. **Valence test:** run the same coupling regime against a verdict-function and a convention-function of the same network.
4. **Reach test:** compare a first-order correlation audit with a habitat enumeration three steps out.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :31, :39-42, :44 and Fig 8 :48-49. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against a system you know: name a source that would make two apparently independent parts fail together, or say why none exists.
3. Say which function your system is serving, verdict about exposure or convention about shared meaning, and whether rising agreement helps or hurts it.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
