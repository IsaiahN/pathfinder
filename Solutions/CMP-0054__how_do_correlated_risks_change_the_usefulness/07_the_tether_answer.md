# The Tether's answer: correlated risk and the usefulness of diversification

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on each domain application, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Diversification is the claim that combining many slots (holdings, species, components) reduces aggregate loss because a loss in one slot is not mirrored in another. The residual appears when the slots fail together. The gap is between the promise (independent losses average down) and the observation (a hidden source moves them together). Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The single aggregate number used to declare diversification useful is already the average in which the localising signal is lost.

This is a **union compound**, not three separate questions. The three named networks differ only in actors, currency and anchor; one law governs all three, and their verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Usefulness is a function of independent slots, not named slots.** Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fifty holdings sharing one factor are one bet with fifty names.

2. **Correlated risk is the common-cause collapse.** Fig 2 :6-7 names "collapse 2: common cause" and "(hidden shared source)"; Fig 2 :19-21: "they never talk, and still agree", "so the agreement is the pool talking,", "not two frames confirming each other". The parts need not influence one another to fail together. In finance the source may be a shared factor or common leverage; in ecology a shared stressor; in infrastructure a shared supply or control system. Which one, in a given system, is not yet named.

3. **The accounting is in effective bets.** Fig 2 :44: "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Adding names stops reducing variance when what remains is the average covariance. The arithmetic is a provenanced import, not a figure derivation.

4. **The effect has a valence.** Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." For a verdict about independent exposure, high correlation destroys usefulness. For a convention that needs shared meaning or common protocol, correlation is the product. "Correlation always destroys diversification" is therefore wrong as an unqualified sentence.

5. **Independence is purchased against the cost of being wrong.** Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A household portfolio and a continental grid do not owe the same redundancy budget. The price sets the budget; it does not declare an asset good.

6. **The loss is often third order.** Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A correlation audit of direct neighbours can miss the shared source that matters.

7. **At rest symmetric, in failure ordered.** `06_isomers_and_order.md` derives that a diversified collection is a symmetric conjunction of slots, while a cascade is an ordered sequence that can close into a cycle. A static variance cannot represent the order; a cascade model cannot represent the diversification.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- No number for the correlation at which usefulness flips, and no functional form for usefulness against correlation. That is a `gap`, not an omission. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- No name for the correlating source in any specific portfolio, grassland or grid. The slot stays `???`.
- Not that all connection is bad. Point 4 is the part most often stripped; a middle range of connectivity can be shock-absorbing.
- Not that adding names never helps. It helps until the covariance floor is reached; the figures give the direction, not the floor.
- Not a new principle per domain. Only the cast changes.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating measurement under stress, not a further argument:

- **Source test:** instrument the candidate common cause (factor, stressor, shared dependency) and re-measure joint losses conditional on it. If losses become independent, F2 of the audit is confirmed in that domain.
- **Effective-count test:** compare realised loss reduction against the correlation-adjusted effective number of slots, not the name count (F3).
- **Valence test:** check the same coupling regime against a verdict-function and a convention-function of the same network. If both move the same way, the valence split is wrong (F4).
- **Reach test:** compare a first-order correlation audit with a full habitat enumeration three steps out (F6).
- **Standing falsifiers:** the five pre-registered conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5. Any one fires, the matching finding is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, quantities unreached.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The absence of a figure-derived threshold is not evidence that no threshold exists. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The answer stops at the limit of what the figures distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. A ground reading, not agreement, would move it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a number for how much diversification is left when risks are correlated. It gives the shape. Usefulness tracks the number of *independent* bets, not the number of names: when holdings, species or components share a hidden source, they are one bet wearing many names and the union surplus goes to zero. Correlation is not a new kind of risk but the common-cause collapse, in which parts that never talk still fail together. This has a valence. It is disqualifying for a verdict about independent exposure, and productive for a convention that lives on shared meaning, so "correlation always destroys diversification" is wrong as an unqualified claim. The effective-sample-size correction supplies the accounting, the cost of being wrong sets how much independence to buy, and the loss that matters is often three steps out. The source in any particular system stays open until an instrument names it. What is offered is the shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the tests that would close it.
