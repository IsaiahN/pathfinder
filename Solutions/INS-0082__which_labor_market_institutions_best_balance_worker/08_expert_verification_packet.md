# Expert verification packet — the Tether on balancing security, adaptation and jobs

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labor economist or labour-market researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the literal ranking, not `grounded`.** No labor-market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The residual in "which institutions best balance security, adaptation and opportunity" lives at the worker and firm slot, not at the country level. The three goods are a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, so one lever clears the single-condition regions and leaves the intersections standing. An institution's valence is relative to the residual and the habitat, so a standing ranking of "best" institutions is not licensed. "Balance" is a flat-formula compression of several typed bonds, not a scalar quantity. The opened literatures stop at the objective link, where adaptation has no instrument that reads it independently of the policy being evaluated. What is owed is a discriminator, not a weight vector. No figure line returns a ranking, so the ranking is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot, the three goods form a union, and there is no standing best actor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a scalar measure that reads all three goods at the worker slot, preserves the live residual under aggregation, and does not move when the institution being graded changes; or show the three conditions form a partition, disjoint and singly anchored, so one lever closes all intersections; or show a named institution keeping invariant valence across a change of shock and habitat with a ground that pays the same way.

### C2 — measurement-facing; checkable by a labor economist against the evaluation literature

- **Statement:** the chain stops at the objective link. Security and employment have instruments (registers, surveys, employment and job-finding rates); adaptation at the worker slot, and the weights across the three goods, do not have an instrument independent of the institution being evaluated.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
- **What to check:** name one source in the evaluation literature that reads adaptation at the worker slot without using the policy as the reading instrument. The EPL literature says the net effect on employment and unemployment is not identifiable a priori and that economists disagree (Blanchard and Portugal versus Lazear versus Bertola and Bentolila, as summarised in the opened Wikipedia page). The ALMP literature reports heterogeneous program effects and "a lack of agreement on whether ALMPs are effective". Kreiner and Svarer 2022 reports that flexicurity in itself is insufficient without compulsory active programs. Do these three statements support the union reading, or is there a fourth instrument that closes it?
- **Refuter:** produce that instrument plus a declared weighting rule that stays fixed when the evaluated institution changes. If it exists, link 3 exists and packages can be ranked end to end.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** "balance" is a compound of typed bonds, not one quantity or one operator, and candidate packages over a fixed atom set are isomers in the figure's sense.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Operators table: ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities", ⇒ means "A produces B; B did not exist before".
- **Refuter:** show that the three goods compose under a single operator with a determinate weight, so two packages with the same score always behave the same at every slot; or show a candidate package requires an atom another lacks, which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing. The live type question is whether ⋛ can take three operands with different currencies; it is named, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that institutions do not matter. That is the same wrong verdict in the opposite direction.
- Not that any named institution is the winner. Flexicurity, employment protection, activation and collective bargaining are bindings from the labor frame, with provenance, not figure-derived winners.
- Not a universal weight vector, country score, or optimal level of protection.
- Not a derivation of labor-market entities from the six-term substrate.
- Not `grounded`. No labor measurement was taken, and no result is asserted as settled.
- Not that adaptation could never be measured. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not another review:

1. A slot-level instrument for adaptation that does not update on the institution being graded.
2. Independent readings of the three goods from different evidence pools, each against the same outcome.
3. A bond discriminator that distinguishes two packages with the same three scores by their internal arrangement.
4. Any of the five standing refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 9 :55 and Fig 11 :51 (three short passages). Does the union reading fit the EPL and ALMP findings you know?
2. Check C2 against your own field: can you name a worker-slot adaptation instrument independent of the policy? If yes, C2 fails and the ranking question reopens.
3. Read Fig 12 :5, :117 and :125. Do the three scores of two known packages fail to predict their different behaviour? If they do predict it, the bond claim fails.
4. Name what you would count as a failure of the separator. Send that, not an endorsement. A refutation is more useful than agreement.
