## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity, symmetry, scale).** The residual is a rule that protects one worker's job while excluding another's application. Arity: three conditions, so a union compound. Symmetry: not symmetric, because the three goods have different currencies and Fig 12 :125 says two currencies do not add. Scale: the question is national, the residual is per worker and firm slot.
- **Domain 1, measured outcomes.** Actors: workers, firms, jobs, spells. Currency: tenure, income, job-finding rates. Anchor: a separate outcome that does not update. This is a real domain, so the census stops before any gap claim.
- **Domain 2, institutions.** Actors: protection, insurance, activation, coordination. Currency: rules, transfers, incentives. Anchor: domain 1 outcomes. Question type: boundary within one closure, which condition each lever binds.
- **Domain 3, habitat and adaptation.** Actors: technology, demand, entry and exit, skills. Currency: reallocation. Anchor: the same ground over time plus a shock the policy frame did not author.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: labor economists and institutions. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence here is zero in this simulation, since all four domains draw on one pool.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry (Fig 3 :20). Break at link 3: the objective has no independent instrument. Refuse the scalar before minting, and describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53).

My census matches the primary's four domains, its union reading and its dependency break. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :5/:117/:125.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: per slot, union, relative valence, bond compound, ranking gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete question as a policy would use it: pick the institution with the best balance score.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** is lost when a national score is reported;
- the **union structure** is flattened into a weighted sum, so the intersections disappear;
- the **bond information** between the three goods is dropped, so two packages with equal scores read as equivalent;
- the **gap itself** is erased when the shorthand is heard as "no answer", which is the opposite verdict.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a rule cannot be ranked as best across slots and conditions without a discriminator. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
