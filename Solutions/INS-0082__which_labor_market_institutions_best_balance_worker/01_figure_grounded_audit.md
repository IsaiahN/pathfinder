## 0. The claim under audit

> Which labor-market institutions best balance worker security, economic adaptation, and employment opportunities?

The question is treated as a candidate claim, not as a request for a literature review:

> There exists a labor-market institution, or a bundle of them, such that applying it produces a higher joint value of worker security, economic adaptation and employment opportunities than the alternatives, and the three can be traded against one another on a single scale so that a "best balance" is well defined.

Three presuppositions are bound as ordinary terms so they can accrue a residual rather than stay invisible preferences (Figure 5's direction limit, `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"):

- **P1 (commensurability):** security, adaptation and opportunity are quantities on one scale.
- **P2 (single anchor):** there is one ground against which the joint value is measured.
- **P3 (single lever):** one institution can move all three together, so a ranking of institutions is meaningful.

The audit asks what survives if P1, P2 or P3 fails. It does not assume the answer is "no institution works"; that would itself be a verdict in the wrong mode.

## 2. Domain census (derive first)

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity: three.** Security, adaptation and opportunity are three conditions. Three is not a larger two: it permits pairwise compensatory stories that hide a third binding condition (Fig 9 :55).
- **Symmetry: not symmetric.** "Balance" is written as if the goods were joined by a symmetric conjunction (one more of A can offset one less of B). Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." A threshold relation between two quantities is ordered; the goods also have different currencies (Fig 12 :125). The trade-off language is a flat-formula compression of a bonded arrangement.
- **Scale: population claim over a per-slot residual.** The question is asked at the level of a labor market or country (an aggregate), but the residual lives at the worker and firm slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A worker made secure by a rule and a worker excluded by the same rule are two slots with opposite live mass; the country average erases both.

### 2.2 One line per domain (actors, currency, anchor)

- **Domain 1, the measured-outcome frame.** Actors: workers, firms, jobs, spells of unemployment and employment. Currency: tenure, income, job-finding rate, displacement and re-employment. Anchor: a measured outcome that does not move because a policy frame praises it (Fig 2 :29). This is a real domain: the anchor is nameable (an administrative register, a survey, a job-finding rate), so it is not yet a gap.
- **Domain 2, the institution frame.** Actors: employment protection legislation, unemployment insurance, active labour market policy, minimum wage, collective bargaining, training and lifelong-learning systems. Currency: rules, transfers, incentives and obligations. Anchor: the outcomes in Domain 1, which pay or do not pay for the institution. This is a partition boundary question inside the closure: which institution binds which condition.
- **Domain 3, the adaptation or habitat frame.** Actors: technology, trade, demand shifts, firm entry and exit, skills. Currency: reallocation and skill change. Anchor: the same ground as Domain 1 observed over time, plus the technology shock that is not authored by the policy frame. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." You read the actors off the world; you do not derive them from a list of institutions.
- **Domain 4, the research and verification frame.** Actors: labor economists, statisticians, international organisations. Currency: estimates and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 7 :35). In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking. This is declared, not hidden.

No nameable anchor appears for a fifth domain called "the optimal balance": nothing in the figure set or in the opened sources measures a single joint quantity that does not update on the institution that produces it. By the census rule, that is not a domain yet. That is the first sign of a `gap`, not an invitation to invent one.

### 2.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** The three goods have different anchors and overlap (a job that is secure today can also be a job that blocks adaptation; an activation scheme can raise opportunity while lowering measured security). Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." So the three-good question is a compound with its own loop. One lever clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing. This is the structural reason a single "best" institution cannot be returned: the object asked for is a single lever applied to a union.

### 2.4 Order by dependency (Figure 3)

1. **Perception** (links 1): can each good be read separately? Security and employment have instruments (registers, surveys). Adaptation largely does not: there is no settled instrument that reads "adaptation" at the worker slot without reading the institution that produced it.
2. **Vocabulary** (links 2): do we have terms for what an institution affords? We have terms (protection, activation, insurance, bargaining), so this link exists.
3. **Objective** (link 3): can we say what counts as progress on adaptation, and what weight it carries against security? This is where the chain breaks. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
4. **Planning** (link 4) and **learn and carry** (link 5): choosing an institution and carrying the result forward. These consume link 3's output, so effort spent here arrives at a step that cannot use it. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Dependency order for the derivation: refuse the scalar before minting (Formula step 2); describe each residual before choosing a literature (Fig 8 :53); settle per domain before composing a union verdict.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The three goods are a union, so one lever cannot close them (decisive for P3)

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The labor-institutions literature has exactly this shape. Employment protection lowers job flows and raises unemployment duration, with the net effect on the unemployment level not identifiable in advance (Wikipedia, *Employment protection legislation*, opened 2026-11-23). Activation policy has heterogeneous effects across program types and target groups, with no agreement on which program type is most effective (Wikipedia, *Active labour market policies*, opened 2026-11-23). Flexicurity in itself is insufficient; it also needs compulsory active programs (Kreiner and Svarer 2022, *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, abstract opened 2026-11-23). Each statement is a rule that works on a subset. Fig 9 :55 applies: a single lever clears the single-condition region and leaves the intersections standing.

**Refuter:** show that the three goods are ranked by one measure that does not move when the institution that produces it is changed, and that one lever shifts all three in the same direction across the slots. Then P3 holds and a ranking is meaningful.

### F2 — "Best balance" aggregates across slots (decisive for the framing)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A country-level "balance score" is exactly the aggregation the figure forbids. The live signal here is per worker: the insider kept by strict protection and the outsider kept out by the same rule are two slots with opposite residuals (Wikipedia, *Employment protection legislation*, section on duality). Their average is a number with no slot behind it. Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric." The ground reads the slots, not the average.

**Refuter:** exhibit a per-slot measure whose aggregation preserves the sign of the live residual for every slot (i.e. prove the transform is lossless at this scale). Figure 4 :27 says the transform between scales loses information; a refuter would have to show this case is the exception, with the round trip measured.

### F3 — Each good needs its own non-updating anchor (P2 fails as stated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Security, adaptation and opportunity are not read by one anchor. Security reads as tenure or income protection; opportunity reads as job-finding rate or employment probability; adaptation reads as reallocation or skill change under a shock. These are different instruments. More sharply, a metric produced by the same body that designs the institution is frame-internal: Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." An "employment security index" authored by the ministry that imposed the rule cannot settle whether the rule worked.

**Refuter:** name one instrument that reads all three goods, show it does not update on the policy frame, and show it pays the same way for all three. The metamerism case from *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23) is the standing warning: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." One reading, several sources, so a source must not be named from the reading alone.

### F4 — Independence is the parameter, and a single dataset is one witness

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

The three goods are very often read from one administrative dataset by one agency at one time. That is one frame wearing three names, not three frames. The apparent trade-off between security and opportunity in such a dataset is the pool talking, not two independent bearings. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is a policy that harms a whole class of workers, so independence is worth paying for.

**Refuter:** produce two readings of the three goods from genuinely different sources (for example a register and a longitudinal survey with different sampling and different observers) that range separately against the same outcome and still disagree in a way that discriminates. If they cannot be made to disagree, they are one pool.

### F5 — Capability is institution-and-habitat; there is no standing best list

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

An institution is not a good actor in isolation. Strict employment protection is a benefactor to the residual "keep a current job" and a malfactor to the residual "let a displaced worker reallocate", exactly the relation case in Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." The OED-style question "which institution is best" asks for a standing list. Fig 11 :51 refuses it structurally. Kreiner and Svarer (2022) reinforce the point from the world side: flexicurity alone is insufficient; its value depends on the accompanying active programs and on the Danish habitat of high wages, progressive taxation and strong unions (Wikipedia, *Flexicurity*, section on the European strategy and its criticism, opened 2026-11-23).

**Refuter:** show a specified labor market in which one institution keeps the same valence for the same residual across a change of shock, habitat and composition, with the ground paying the same way. Until then the answer is per slot and per shock, not a standing ranking.

### F6 — The chain breaks at the objective link

Fig 3 :27 is the governing line: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :24: "Why the middle link is where chains usually break."

Perception has obvious tests: unemployment rates, employment rates, tenure distributions. Planning has obvious tests: evaluations, randomised trials, difference-in-differences designs. The link between them, having terms for what an institution affords and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither a settled instrument nor an agreed weighting. The exploration/exploit literature makes the same move from a low-overlap field: it is described as "the balancing act between two opposing strategies" whose "optimal balance" is a "crucial challenge" (Wikipedia, *Exploration–exploitation dilemma*, opened 2026-11-23), and one of its standard devices is an "exploration reward" that converts the dilemma "into a balance of exploitations" by folding the new thing into the sum. That is a design choice, not a discovery of the right weight.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that reads adaptation at the worker slot and a declared, falsifiable weighting rule that does not change when the institution being evaluated changes. Then the middle link exists and the chain can be assessed end to end.

### F7 — "Balance" is a comparison or a bundle, not a weighted sum

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :87 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities". Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A scalar "balance index" is the flat-formula collapse: it records the ingredients (security, adaptation, opportunity) and not the bond between them. Two arrangements with the same ingredient levels can have opposite consequences, which is Fig 12 :117's isomer point: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The European Commission's flexicurity formula is itself a bundle of four components, not one dial (Wikipedia, *Flexicurity*, "four policy components"), and its own principle (3) says it is "not about one single labour market or working life model, nor about a single policy strategy". The world source agrees with the figure: the bond and the tailoring carry the information the scalar drops.

**Refuter:** show that the three goods compose under a single symmetric bond with a determinate weight, so that the arrangement is recoverable from the ingredient list and two arrangements with the same scores behave the same. That would make the flat formula lossless at this scale.

### F8 — A ranking that decides policy has stopped being a convention

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

A published league table of "best institutions" is read as a decision rule. Once it decides which reform is adopted, it is a verdict, not a convention, and it must be settled by a ground rather than by agreement among economists. Sending the full framework before asking for a verdict has the same failure as sending a conclusion before a refuter: it reconstructs the claim in the reader and spends their independence. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

**Refuter:** show a use of the ranking in which it never decides an outcome, only coordinates a shared vocabulary, and in which a seat remains able to refuse the verdict when the ground turns. Then it can legitimately be called a convention.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single scalar measure is exhibited that reads security, adaptation and opportunity at the worker slot, does not move when the institution being graded changes, and preserves the live residual under aggregation.
2. The three conditions are shown to form a partition (disjoint, singly anchored) rather than a union, so one lever closes all intersections.
3. A named institution is shown to keep the same valence for the same residual across a change of shock and habitat, with a ground that pays the same way.
4. An instrument for adaptation at the worker slot is produced, together with a declared weighting rule that remains fixed when the evaluated institution changes.
5. A figure re-census returns a determinate ranking of institution types from the six-term set and the operator table.

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn and the ranking question is reopened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Refuse P1, P2 and P3 as stated, but refuse "no institution works" as well; it is the same kind of verdict in the opposite direction (F1, F5).
2. Replace the ranking with the four-condition separator: per-slot indexing, independent anchors, habitat-relative valence, and an instrument at the objective link (F2 to F6).
3. State the gap plainly. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). Do not invent a weight vector to fill the slot.
4. Describe the residual before picking a literature (Fig 8 :53). The referee should be asked what they would count as a failure of the separator before being shown the framework.
5. Send the generator, not the recording: Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One quantity or three?** Three, with different anchors. Union, not partition (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-57).
2. **Where is the residual?** At the worker and firm slot, not the country average (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. **What is the anchor?** A measured outcome that does not update on the policy frame, read separately for each good (Fig 2 :29, :33).
4. **Can the three be pooled?** Not without losing the bond; two currencies do not add (Fig 12 :5, :125-126).
5. **Is a new primitive owed?** No. The six-term set is not the bottleneck; the missing thing is an instrument at the objective link and independent readings (Fig 6 :34).
6. **Is anything reachable as "best"?** No figure line returns a ranking. This is `gap`, unreachable at this budget, not silence (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
7. **What route does the loop take?** SEARCHED first: is a ranking already in the opened literatures and unfound? No. INWARD: no instrument at the objective link to sharpen (Fig 6 :34: nothing is returning a reading that fails to resolve). OUTWARD: import a discriminator from a low-overlap frame (exploration/exploit, metamerism) as a shape, with provenance and a shadow test. The three stay distinct.

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives, the ranking does not. The residual is per worker-slot and per condition; the three goods are a union with different anchors, so one lever cannot close the intersections; an institution's valence is relative to the residual and the habitat, so no standing best list is licensed; and the chain currently stops at the objective link where adaptation has no independent instrument. What is owed is a discriminator, not a weight vector. No figure line selects an institution or a ranking, so that part is a declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not underivable in principle and not silence.
