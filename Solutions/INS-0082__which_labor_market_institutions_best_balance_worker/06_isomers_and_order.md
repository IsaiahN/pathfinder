# Is "balance" one quantity, and what bond does the question actually use?

**Mode: `specified` on the bond claims, `general` on the structural split, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question imports the word "balance" as if it named a quantity that could be maximised. `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F7 showed that it is a bonded arrangement compressed into a scalar. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) which of the eight operators does the word actually use, and (2) are candidate institutional packages related as isomers over a fixed atom set, or do they differ by atoms and therefore by import? A third question follows: what order, if any, does the dependency chain impose?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available in the labor frame opened this session: income security, job protection, activation or training, wage coordination, job-finding opportunity, skill reallocation, income support. Bonds available: the seven binary ones plus negation (unary). No new atom is available without a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23). Note that these atoms are bindings from the labor frame, not new members of the six-term substrate; section 5 of `05_what_shape_means.md` states the scope condition.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Is "balance" a conjunction? NO: it is the flat-formula collapse

- Fig 12 :6-17 names "collapse 6: the flat formula". Three different physical changes share one written formula because "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not" and "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost".
- A score of (security, adaptation, opportunity) is exactly that formula. Two packages with the same three numbers can behave oppositely: one keeps insiders in jobs and closes the door to outsiders; the other opens the door and withdraws protection. Same ingredients, different substance.
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- ⇒ The word "balance" in the question is not a quantity. It is a name for a missing bond that the question never wrote down.

### Q2 — Which operator, then? SEVERAL, and that is the point

Fig 12 :111-113: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

Map the three goods onto the operator set:

| relation in the labor question | operator | verbatim test or definition |
|---|---|---|
| security versus flexibility at one slot | ⋛ comparison | "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87) |
| income support then participation | ⇒ production | "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77) |
| opportunity minus exclusion | − subtraction | "A with B removed or absent" (Fig 12 :82) |
| activation before re-employment | → sequence | "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72) |
| refusing a rule that decides an outcome | ¬ negation | "this does not hold" (Operators table :55) |

The ILO *What Works* project gives the production case directly: "Income support sustains workers' incomes, preventing them from falling into poverty and giving them the opportunity to take part in ALMPs." Participation does not exist before the support in that account, which is ⇒ rather than →. The Commission's four components are "interacting", i.e. a bundle with internal bonds, not a single + (Wikipedia, *Flexicurity*).

- Singular claim refused: ⋛ cannot take three operands with three currencies. The Operators table defines it over two quantities, and Fig 12 :125 says two currencies do not add. The three-way "balance" is a **compound** of pairwise comparisons plus a production and a subtraction, not one comparison.
- Live strain, recorded: whether a national package's internal bonds are → or ⇒ is an empirical question about whether the second component waits or is absent until the first fires. No opened source settles it. It stays named.

**Falsifier for Q1/Q2:** exhibit one operator and one scalar score such that two packages with the same score always behave the same at every worker slot, and such that the operator's own test applies to three operands. Then the flat formula is lossless here and the bond finding is refuted.

### Q3 — Are packages isomers over a fixed atom set? CONDITIONALLY YES

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take three packages over the same atom set {income security, job protection, activation, wage coordination, opportunity}:

- **A, the Danish-style package:** low job protection (easy hire and fire) + high income security + compulsory activation.
- **B, a protection-first package:** high job protection + moderate income security + weak activation.
- **C, a deregulated package:** low job protection + low income security + market-led training.

Over that fixed atom set they have the same ingredients and different bonds, so they are isomers in the figure's sense. Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

The world sources support the conditional form. Kreiner and Svarer (2022) say flexicurity in itself is insufficient and needs the active programs, so A's binding includes a bond that B omits. The EPL literature says strict protection reduces both job creation and job destruction with an unidentifiable net level effect, so B's bond produces a different distribution across slots. Wikipedia *Flexicurity* principle (3) says the Commission explicitly rejects one single model and requires national tailoring, which is the same structural point: the arrangement, not the ingredient list, decides.

**Falsifier for Q3, written before the conclusion:** if any package requires an atom that another does not (for example a training system that cannot be expressed without a universal skill-forecasting instrument, or a security regime that cannot be expressed without a separate wage-coordination atom), then they are not isomers; they differ by atoms and the relation is composition plus import. The isomer claim then fails and the functional-group reading (Fig 12 :124: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member") stands instead.

### Q4 — What does the dependency chain order?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order for this question is:

1. perception (can the good be read at the worker slot?);
2. vocabulary (terms for what an institution affords: protection, insurance, activation, coordination);
3. objective (what counts as progress on adaptation, and at what weight);
4. planning (which package to choose);
5. learn and carry (which result survives to the next reform).

The break is at link 3. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The opened sources have instruments for links 1 and 2 and evaluations for links 4 and 5; they do not have an independent instrument for adaptation at the worker slot. So the packages in Q3 cannot be ranked end to end. They can only be described as arrangements with different bonds, each with a stated valence relative to a stated residual (Fig 11 :49).

A second order is the scale order. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A package that works in one country crosses as a method with its bond structure intact, or it does not cross. It never crosses as a recording of one country's score. The Commission's "no size fits all" principle is the world-side statement of this membrane rule.

### Q5 — Catalyst and cycle analogues, offered and limited

Fig 12 :119 defines a catalyst: "A part that survives its own use." A high-trust coordination body can plausibly be a catalyst in the labor frame: it is spent and regenerating across bargaining rounds. But this is a world-side analogy with no measurement in the opened sources, so it is `single-source` at best and cannot decide anything. Fig 12 :121 defines a cycle: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Unemployment insurance funded by employment contributions is a candidate cycle, but again there is no figure line that selects it and no ground reading here. Both are recorded as analogues, not findings.

## CONCLUSION

"Balance" is not one quantity and not one bond. It is a flat-formula compression of a compound: pairwise comparisons, a production relation between support and participation, a subtraction of exclusion from opportunity, sequences within programs, and a refusal when a fixed rule decides an outcome. Over a fixed atom set, candidate packages are a real isomer claim with an explicit falsifier; where the atom set changes, the relation is composition plus import instead. The dependency chain stops at the objective link, so no package can be ranked end to end. What survives is the arrangement and the falsifier, not a score.
