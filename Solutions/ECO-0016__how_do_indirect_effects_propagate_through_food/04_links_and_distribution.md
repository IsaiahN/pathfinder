## 3. Wider literature that bears on the question

These are the substantive lines that matter, kept separate by field and overlap:

1. **Definition and routes.** Wootton 1994: *"Indirect effects occur when the impact of one species on another requires the presence of a third species."* Two routes, linked chains and interaction modification. Wootton 2002 repeats and expands this. These two are one author, so one witness.
2. **Magnitude.** Menge 1995: two analyses put indirect effects at about 40 percent of the change due to manipulation, range 24 to 61 percent, with the fraction roughly independent of web species richness. He also found that keystone predation (35 percent) and apparent competition (25 percent) were the most common types. This is the best direct empirical answer to "when do they outweigh" in the sources read: they do not dominate on average in these webs, but they are a large and roughly constant fraction, and individual target species can show indirect effects much larger than direct ones.
3. **Predictability.** Novak et al. 2011: network size and structure generate indeterminacy that overwhelms interaction-strength estimates once a modest number of links is present. This limits any universal dominance claim and supports the per-slot, named-perturbation reading.
4. **Node importance.** Zhao et al. 2016: indirect indices and direct indices rank species differently; secondary extinctions can occur more than one link away; material flow does not always translate to removal impact. Supports F5 and F6.
5. **Timing.** Menge 1997: indirect effects appeared simultaneously with or shortly after direct effects in intertidal experiments, contrary to the expectation that they are always slow. Recorded as a limit on any "indirect means delayed" claim.
6. **Cross-system cascade.** The Nature Scitable article's fish to dragonfly to pollinator to seed-set example shows an indirect effect crossing from a pond to adjacent terrestrial plants, a concrete three-step-plus case of Fig 11 :52.
7. **Low-overlap corroboration.** Watts 2002 from network science shows that cascade size and trigger identity depend on connectivity and threshold heterogeneity, and that a system can be robust for long periods then fragile in a large cascade. It corroborates the shape "cascade over a contact network with sign/threshold structure" without deciding any ecological magnitude question.

**Overlap warning.** Items 1 to 6 all sit in community ecology and cite one another (Wootton, Menge, Paine, Novak and Zhao share references and in several cases co-authorship). By Fig 2 :40 they are one evidence pool, not six independent witnesses. Item 7 is the only genuinely low-overlap frame, and it is from a different field, so it can only corroborate the structural shape. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was not triggered.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The first contact carries the per-slot residual, the scoping condition and the refuter. Sending the framework first converts a potential outside checker into a member of the frame being checked.
- **Fig 2 :35**, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement, and do not select recipients by likely agreement.
- **Fig 9 :27 to :28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the scoping claim were true before giving them the article or the figures.
- **Fig 12 :125**, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Do not collapse flow magnitude and removal impact into one ranked importance number in the cover note.
- **Fig 11 :61**, *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Once a claim is in a list or a mailbox it cannot be un-introduced; choose the venue before sending, not after.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual vector and the scoping claim; (2) the five refuters, with the request "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record their verdict on stage 1; (4) only then, if they are curious, offer the figure links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) stage 3 offers the formula as a method for their own problems, with each user writing their own refuter and stating their own mode.
