# The Tether's answer: indirect effects in food webs

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the three domain readings, `gap` on a universal dominance law. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## 1. The residual it answers

A direct-link model of a food web predicts each species' response from its direct edges. When an intervention on A changes C and the A to C edge is absent or already correct, the unexplained part at C is an indirect effect. The question has two parts: what carries it, and when it outweighs the direct part. The two are different predicates and keep separate verdicts below.

## 2. The shape

1. **The residual is per slot and per step.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A web-wide total cancels opposing responses and erases the effect.

2. **Propagation is contact outward through an enumerated habitat.** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 13 :24 names the carrying structure: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*; Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."* The list is read off the world, not composed from a vocabulary of interaction types (Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*).

3. **Dominance is a scoping condition, not a universal law.** Indirect effects outweigh direct ones when a first-order scope misses a residual that the ground shows sitting further out, and the relays do not damp it away. This is exactly the DDT case in Fig 11 :52 and the mechanism in Fig 11 :54: *"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."* Any claim of a universal percentage is refused.

4. **The sign is relative to the tracked residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* An intermediary that helps one species harms another in the same web (Fig 11 :50). A two-link chain of negative links reads as positive on the tracked species; a third link inverts it again. The multiplication rule is our binding, not a figure quote.

5. **Direct and indirect form a union compound.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Density-mediated chains and interaction modifications are two members of that union (Wootton 1994, 2002), not two readings of one quantity.

6. **Flow and impact are two currencies.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Biomass passing along a link and the abundance change caused by removing it are in different units; a single ranked "importance" number is the flat-formula error (Fig 12 :130).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that indirect effects always outweigh direct effects. The figure gives a scoping case, not a law.
- Not a universal percentage. Menge 1995's roughly 40 percent is a result in rocky intertidal webs, not a constant.
- Not that material flow is interaction strength. The two currencies do not add (Fig 12 :125).
- Not that an interaction type has fixed valence (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that network is a new primitive. It is one of the six (Fig 13 :24).
- Not that any of this is settled by an experiment run here. Mode is `specified`, not `grounded`.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

An intervention, not another review. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Concretely:

1. Choose a species pair A and C with no A to C edge, enumerate the habitat outward to step k, and measure the response of every species at every step to a controlled perturbation of A. The residual vector is the ground payload.
2. Fix the step index at which dominance is claimed before looking at the data. Read the sign of each link independently before computing the chain product.
3. Run the same perturbation on a second web with the same species set and permuted links (the isomer test, `06` Q3). If per-slot residuals are identical, the arrangement claim fails.
4. Separate flow and impact in two columns; do not rank them into one number.
5. The standing falsifiers from `01` section 4: pooled statistic that still resolves slots; indirect effect across a confirmed contact gap; first-order dominance in every species and perturbation after correct enumeration; sign not equal to the link product; a scalar that collapses flow and impact; an actor with fixed sign across two residuals.

## 6. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the magnitude.** The figures give the carrier, the indexing rule, the sign rule and the form of the dominance condition. They do not give a number, and this derivation holds no instrument that could produce one. The stop is recorded with its lineage so it is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the framework *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* What is owed is a per-slot perturbation experiment, not more agreement (Fig 2 :40).

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a universal number for when indirect effects beat direct ones. It gives the shape an answer must have. The response of a web to a perturbation has to be tracked per species and per contact step, because a web-wide total cancels the very signals that matter. Effects travel by contact outward through a habitat that is enumerated until the cascade stops mattering; the figures' own example is DDT reaching eagles three steps out after a first-order enumeration missed it. So the dominance condition is a scoping relation: indirect effects outweigh direct ones when a first-order model misses a residual that the ground shows sitting further out and the relays do not damp it. Direct and indirect form a union compound, not two kinds of interaction, and material flow and removal impact are two currencies that do not add. The sign of a chain is relative to the species whose response you are tracking. What is owed is a per-slot perturbation experiment, not another review. This is a `specified`, ungrounded shape with written falsifiers, not a settled ecological law.
