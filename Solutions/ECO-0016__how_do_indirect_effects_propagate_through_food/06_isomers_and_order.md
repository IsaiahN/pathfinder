# Chain, cycle, sign and isomer: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the sign and isomer readings, `general` on the order constraint.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit shows that indirect effects travel by contact outward (Fig 11 :52) and that direct plus indirect form a union compound (Fig 9 :54). Three supporting questions remain:

1. What is the **order** of an indirect chain, and what does the bond type add that the species list does not?
2. How does the **sign** of a chain behave, and what does Fig 12 :121's cycle line license?
3. Are two webs with the same species set but different links **isomers**, and does that distinction help the dominance question?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held: species as the ecological analogue of states, and the six substrate terms (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures) as the framework vocabulary. Bonds held: the seven, of which `→` sequence and `⇒` production are ordered, and `+` conjunction and `≡` identity are symmetric. No new primitive is introduced; network is already in the six (Fig 13 :24).

Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20): enumerate the actors, then name the bond, then compute the sign, then test against the ground. A sign computed before the actors are enumerated is a reading below the break.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What order is an indirect chain?

Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**
Operators table: **"sequence / A then B; B needs A to have happened"**; **"production / A produces B; B did not exist before"**.
Fig 12 :5: **"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

An indirect effect is an **ordered bond**, not a set. `{A, B, C}` as a species list does not say whether the chain is A then B then C or C then B then A. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term; under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms (Operators table :59 to :62). A food web whose links are recorded as an undirected adjacency matrix commits the flat-formula error: it records ingredients and not arrangement. Both Paine's interaction webs and Wootton's path models are ordered, which is why they can predict the direction of a response.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if an unordered adjacency matrix predicts the sign and magnitude of a per-slot response as well as an ordered interaction web under the same ground, the order constraint is wrong for this quantity.

### Q2 — What does the loop sign license?

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

A chain cannot close by construction; a web can. The figure says the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. Our binding onto ecology is:

- a two-link chain of two negative trophic links gives a positive indirect effect on the tracked species (the "enemy of my enemy" case);
- adding a third negative link inverts the sign again;
- a sign product around a closed loop determines whether the loop damps or amplifies the residual.

This arithmetic is **our binding**, `specified` and single-source on the figure side. The figure itself does not state a multiplication rule for ecological link signs. That is why the refuter in `01` section 4 asks for an independently measured chain whose sign is not the link-sign product.

Fig 13 :31 to :32 adds the scale caveat: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."** So a long signed chain is not just a longer version of a short one: a compounding remainder can change regime. This is a structural warning, not a claim that every long food chain is chaotic.

Wootton 2002 distinguishes density-mediated chains from interaction modifications. Under Fig 12 :121, a density-mediated chain is the ordered-chain case; an interaction modification is closer to a change in the bond itself, which Fig 12 :46 says is not recoverable from the operands. The distinction is structural, not a vocabulary preference.

**Falsifier:** a measured chain whose sign does not follow the link-sign product after links and residual are independently measured, or an interaction modification that leaves the bond unchanged and alters only density.

### Q3 — Are two webs with the same species set isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Case 1. Two webs with the same species list but different **trophic links** (who eats whom swapped among the same species). Same atoms, different arrangement. By Fig 12 :117 they are isomers: the species list cannot tell them apart, but their cascades differ. This is the `specified` isomer claim worth testing, and it is checkable with a removal experiment on both webs.

Case 2. Two webs that share only the motif "predator, competitor, resource" but have different species in those slots. By Fig 12 :123 they share a **functional group**: the motif says what kind of cascade to expect, the species say which web it is. This is the weaker claim and the one most ecology studies actually support (the "keystone predation" and "apparent competition" types in Menge 1995).

Case 3. A species whose effect persists across seasons or generations while its own abundance is spent and regenerating. Fig 12 :119 warns that a part surviving its own use behaves differently across uses. A web that records only current biomass cannot distinguish a catalyst-like species from a spent one. This is a live limitation, not a finding.

Zhao et al. 2016 report that direct and indirect indices give quite different rankings of node importance in the same network. That is the isomer distinction in ecological clothing: the same species list, two different ranking functions, two different substances. Watts 2002 adds a low-overlap structural warning: in sparse networks the most connected nodes are disproportionately likely to trigger cascades, but in dense networks they are not. So "hub" is not a standing valence either (Fig 11 :49).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two webs with identical species sets and permuted links produce the same per-slot residual vector under the same perturbation and ground, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q4 — Does the isomer distinction help the dominance question?

Yes, and only in a limited way. It says that "when do indirect effects outweigh direct ones" cannot be answered from a species list: two webs with the same list can differ in dominance because the arrangement differs (Fig 12 :117). It does not say which arrangement wins. That still needs a slot, a perturbation and an anchor (Fig 9 :27 to :28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**). The dominance condition in `01` F3 remains `specified` and ungrounded.

## CONCLUSION

An indirect chain is an **ordered molecule**, not a species set. Its sign is our `specified` binding onto Fig 12 :121's loop rule, and it is relative to the tracked residual (Fig 11 :49). Two webs over the same species set with different links are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117; webs sharing only a motif are members of one **functional group** under Fig 12 :123. A catalyst-like species is a live limitation because current biomass does not record whether a part survives its own use. None of this yields a universal dominance law; all of it yields a checkable shape with three written falsifiers.
