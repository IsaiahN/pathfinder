# Expert verification packet: indirect effects in food webs

**What this is:** a claim in a form a community ecologist or food-web researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three domain readings, `gap` on a universal dominance law. Not `grounded`.** No new field measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The response of a food web to a perturbation must be tracked as a residual per species and per contact step; pooled web-wide totals cancel the opposing responses that carry the indirect effect. Effects propagate by contact outward through an enumerated habitat, which is the DDT to eagles case in the figure: a first-order enumeration missed a residual that mattered three steps out. Dominance of indirect over direct is therefore a scoping condition, a first-order model missing a ground-visible further-out residual, and not a universal magnitude law. Direct and indirect form a union compound, material flow and removal impact are two currencies that do not add, and the sign of a chain is relative to the species whose response is tracked. The shape comes with written falsifiers and an experiment that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus ecology-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11

- **Statement:** propagation is contact outward; the residual is indexed per species and per step; dominance is a scoping condition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** does a first-order interaction web miss a response that sits two or three links out, and does that response remain after the direct links are correctly specified? Is the magnitude visible per species but not in a web total? The DDT case in Fig 11 :54 and the Stanford and EPA pages in `04` give the historically settled instance; the claim is about the form of the condition, not about DDT specifically.
- **Refuter:** a pooled web-wide statistic that still identifies which species needs a mediator and at what step; or an indirect response confirmed by measurement across a gap in the contact habitat with no intermediary at any step; or a web where, after correct outward enumeration, the dominant per-slot residual is first order in every species under every perturbation tested.

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable against a removal or perturbation experiment

- **Statement:** direct and indirect effects form a union compound, not two kinds of interaction, and material flow does not measure removal impact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** in a system you know, do density-mediated chains and interaction modifications require different manipulations to distinguish, as Wootton 1994 and 2002 describe? Does a link's biomass or carbon flow rank the same species as a removal experiment does? Zhao et al. 2016 report that it need not.
- **Refuter:** direct and indirect contributions to the same slot cannot be separated even in principle by any intervention or path model; or a scalar that ranks flow magnitude and removal impact exactly across several webs; or an actor with identical sign relative to two independently defined residuals in the same web.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 13

- **Statement:** an indirect chain is an ordered molecule, its sign is relative to the tracked residual, and two webs with the same species set but permuted links are isomers under Fig 12 :117.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 13 :24 *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*.
- **What to check:** the sign multiplication rule is **our binding**, not a verbatim figure line. Does a two-link chain of negative links read as positive on the tracked species, and does a third link invert it? Do two webs with identical species sets and permuted links give different per-slot residuals under the same perturbation?
- **Refuter:** a measured chain whose sign is not the link-sign product after links and residual are independently measured; or identical per-slot residuals from two webs with permuted links under the same perturbation and ground (which kills the isomer claim and leaves only the functional-group claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that indirect effects always outweigh direct effects. Fig 11 :52 is a scoping case, not a law.
- Not a universal percentage. The roughly 40 percent in Menge 1995 is a result for rocky intertidal webs and one residual definition.
- Not that material flow is interaction strength; the two currencies do not add.
- Not that a species or interaction type has fixed valence (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that network is a new primitive. It is one of the six (Fig 13 :24).
- Not that the framework can rank species by importance without a named residual (Fig 9 :28).
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement was taken here.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

1. Perturb A, enumerate outward to step k, and measure the abundance response of every species at every step, with the dominance step fixed before the data are read.
2. Run the same perturbation on a second web with the same species set and permuted links (the isomer test).
3. Measure each link's sign independently before computing the chain product.
4. Keep flow magnitude and removal impact in separate columns.
5. Apply the six standing refuters in `01` section 4. If one fires, withdraw the relevant finding and re-census from the residual line.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43 to :54 and Fig 1 :19 to :22. Four short passages.
2. Name a system you know where a first-order web missed a two- or three-step response. Does the scoping condition in C1 describe it?
3. Say whether material flow and removal impact rank species the same way in that system (C2). One counterexample refutes the single-currency reading.
4. Name one instrument that would measure the per-slot residual at a fixed step, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
