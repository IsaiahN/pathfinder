**Subject:** One checkable claim about indirect effects in food webs, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. A failure is more useful to me than an endorsement.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A direct-link model of a food web predicts the abundance response of each species from its direct edges. When an intervention on species A changes species C and the A to C edge is either absent or correctly modelled, the remaining gap is an indirect effect. The claim is not "indirect effects exist", which is settled. The claim is about **what carries the gap and when it dominates**.
>
> **The output to check (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the dominance condition, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The gap must be indexed per species and per contact distance, not pooled into a web-wide total. Pooling cancels opposing responses and erases the signal (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"; Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average").
> 2. Propagation is by **contact outward** through the habitat, enumerated until the cascade stops mattering. Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
> 3. Dominance has a **scoping condition**, not a universal threshold. Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." In other words, the indirect part outweighs the direct part when a first-order scope misses a residual that the ground shows sitting further out, and the relays do not damp it away.
> 4. The sign of a multi-step chain is relative to the residual being tracked, and the loop sign decides whether the effect closes or amplifies (Fig 12 :121). An intermediary that helps one species can harm another in the same web (Fig 11 :49).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that indirect effects always outweigh direct ones; that there is a universal percentage; that material flow along a link measures the impact of removing it; that an interaction type has a fixed valence; or that any of this is settled by an experiment I ran. It is a shape and a scoping condition, not a number.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. a pooled web-wide statistic that still identifies which species needs a mediator and at what step, with no per-slot indexing;
> 2. an indirect response confirmed by measurement across a gap in the contact habitat, with no intermediary at any step;
> 3. a web where, after a correct outward enumeration, the dominant per-slot residual is first order in every species under every perturbation tested;
> 4. a measured chain whose sign is not the product of its link signs relative to the tracked residual;
> 5. a scalar that ranks flow magnitude and removal impact exactly across several webs.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is the most useful reply. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would ask you to record first:** what you would expect to see if the scoping claim were true, and what result would make you reject it. I will note your verdict on this output before showing anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public material is below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in the project folder): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The formula and the eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a web you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
