## 0. The question in effect terms

> "Strange metals and whether their behavior hints at a holographic description."

Stated as an effect: a set of transport and spectroscopic readings (linear-in-T resistivity, an inferred relaxation time of order hbar/(kB T), broad rather than quasiparticle spectral functions, more than one apparent relaxation rate) fails to fit the ordinary quasiparticle account. The question asks whether that failure is a residual whose shape is predicted by a frame whose closure differs, namely a gauge/gravity (AdS/CFT or semi-holographic) description.

**Option A:** the behavior is evidence, or a hint, that the correct description is holographic.
**Option B:** the behavior is a real residual whose shape can be stated; the figures cannot name its source, several frames predict that shape, and whether a holographic import is owed depends on an open underivable-versus-unreachable question.

What each leads to: A hands an outside checker a name and a verdict before a discriminating measurement exists, spends their independence, and treats agreement among overlapping holographic models as corroboration. B hands them a shaped residual, a refuter written in advance, and the specific instrument that would close the question.

**What an answer would look like (bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, Formula "Direction").** An answer would be a claim of the form: "this measured pattern P is predicted by frame F at first order, no other available frame predicts P at the same cost, and the prediction was written before the measurement." Binding that shape lets it accrue a residual like anything else, so the preference for holography becomes refusable rather than invisible.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 — R is a union of conditions, not one anomaly (decisive for the framing)

Verbatim lines:

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

The question bundles T-linear resistivity, a Planckian timescale, absent quasiparticles and a two-lifetime transport pattern. These do not always co-occur. Hartnoll and Mackenzie (Rev. Mod. Phys. 94, 041002, 2022) write that "different mechanisms for T-linear resistivity, with the same slope, may plausibly be at work in a given unconventional metal" and that "quantum criticality alone is not sufficient to give Planckian transport. Neither is it necessary." Phillips, Hussey and Abbamonte (Science 377, 2022) tabulate candidate materials where rho proportional to T holds at high T but not as T goes to zero, and vice versa.

So "does the behavior hint at holography" is an over-bundled question. The honest unit is a slot: does *this* observed exponent, in *this* material, with *this* other constrained observable, require a frame whose closure differs?

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulation:** Fig 1 plus Fig 9 (low overlap: per-slot indexing versus union splitting) plus two world reviews. **Refuter:** a single mechanism measured to produce the whole constrained set from one parameter set, with the co-occurrence shown rather than asserted.

### F4 — The reading cannot comment on the source: multiple routes to one substance

Verbatim lines:

- Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

T-linear resistivity is a coarse reading. The opened adscft.org guide states plainly: "There is no unique holographic mechanism for rho_dc ~ T. That is not a bug. It is a warning that linear resistivity is a coarse observable." Hartnoll and Mackenzie independently list quantum criticality and electron-phonon scattering as "two very different mechanisms leading to a Planckian electronic lifetime," and warn that "perhaps it is best regarded as an empirical fact that a well-defined analysis procedure applied to a large range of materials consistently reveals a Planckian scattering rate." That is a reading, not a source. This is the metamerism point of *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source."

### F5 — Is an import owed? Underivable versus unreachable is open

Verbatim lines:

- Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom"
- Fig 8 :41-42 "Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."
- Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- Fig 6 :51-52 "importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed"

If the strange-metal residual is *underivable in principle* from the conventional many-body closure, OUTWARD import is licensed. If it is merely *unreachable at this budget* (the fermion sign problem, finite-size numerics, no controlled analytic handle), then the term may already be in the closure and the remedy is more search, not a new atom. Phillips, Hussey and Abbamonte write that "the Fermion-sign problem, however, prevents any definitive statement about the low-T behavior in the presence of Mott physics." That is a budget limit, not a proof of underivability. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The figures state the distinction; they cannot tell us which side a cuprate falls on. This is the solution's declared **gap**, of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind for this run, not a silence.

**Mode:** `specified` on the distinction, `gap` on the branch. **Triangulation:** Fig 8 plus Fig 6. **Refuter:** a proof of underivability from the conventional closure, or a controlled conventional derivation at accessible budget.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Seat Map field guide line 287, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is narrowed or withdrawn if any of the following fires:

1. **Single-source discriminator fires.** A measurement maps the strange-metal readings uniquely to one microscopic source, so that no second mechanism could produce the same constrained observable set. This kills F4.
2. **Conventional derivation fires.** A non-holographic derivation inside the CM closure (Fermi-liquid/Mott/quantum-critical/numerical) produces the residual's shape at accessible budget. This resolves F5 as unreachable-at-budget and removes the owed import; as in formula step 7, importing against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed.
3. **Forward holographic prediction fails or is shown to be retrospective only.** A dated forward prediction from a gravitational frame (a specific exponent in A(k,omega), a sign in the Hall response, a value of eta/s or a diffusion bound) is contradicted by experiment; or the ARPES k-dependence is shown to arise from ordinary band-structure or matrix-element effects. This kills F2's exception and F7's shadow test.
4. **Substrate fails.** One of the six terms is derived from the other five, or a direct state-state or structure-structure composition is admitted and cannot be refused. This breaks the framework's own composition rule (Fig 13 :41, :74-75).
5. **Overlap hidden.** Two apparently independent holographic models are shown to draw on one evidence pool, and their agreement is then offered as corroboration. This does not refute the shape but refutes any corroboration claim built on it.

Until one of these is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Reframe the question before answering it.** Ask which slot of the residual a given frame predicts, not whether "the behavior" in general hints at holography (F1, F2).
2. **Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** Write down that an answer must be a first-order prediction of a constrained observable set, dated before measurement, with no other available frame predicting it at the same cost (Formula "Direction").
3. **Run INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks.** Do not let failure in one hand a verdict to the other (F6).
4. **Demand a shadow test and provenance for any import** (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :51-52). A holographic model that fits rho~T but not sigma(omega), tan(theta_H) and A(k,omega) has not explained the compound.
5. **Send the shape and the refuter, not the name** (Fig 10 :52). Ask an outside checker what they would count as a failure before showing them any theory.
6. **Keep the declared gap visible.** Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).
