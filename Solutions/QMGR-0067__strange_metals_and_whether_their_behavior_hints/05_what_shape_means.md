## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :133: "A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** here is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name its source. For this question the shape is: a relaxation process whose characteristic time is set by temperature alone (hbar/(kB T)), with no long-lived quasiparticle, producing a specific, constrained set of transport and spectral responses. That constrains any candidate description. It does not fill the slot with "a black hole in AdS4."

`rho = rho_0 + A T` is a reading. "A Planckian, scale-free, no-quasiparticle relaxation with a specified set of constrained responses" is a shape. "The dual is an Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton black brane with theta/z = -1" is a name.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world" (as recorded in the exemplar; the underlying essay I did open this session makes the same move in its metamerism argument).
- *Library of Babel* on the bundle: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually." This is the essay-level warrant for F1's union reading.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better," and the paradox that "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* sharpens this (quoted in the exemplar, not reopened here): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space," and "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list.

For this question that means: a statement such as "the extra radial dimension of AdS is a new kind of primitive" is not licensed. It is a binding of an already-held structure (network or gradient) inside a different frame. Adding a seventh primitive would break Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." This is re-censused in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 4. Q1: Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming holography?

**Answer: it forbids it as a framework result, not as the searcher's binding.**

The canonical phrase governs a *missing* primitive. Holography is not a primitive of the six-term set at all; it is a different frame, which Fig 10 :68 describes as "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." So the correct objection is not "you may never say the word holography." The correct objection is:

- the figures do not derive a gravitational dual;
- choosing to model with one is a binding from the gravity frame, with provenance and a shadow test owed (Fig 8 :51-52);
- the binding carries no verdict weight until the ground pays for it.

This is the same correction the exemplar records in its section 5b, kept visible here: I initially overstated the rule as a flat ban on naming. It is a ban on a framework-issued name for a missing primitive, not on a human-authored, provenanced binding.

## 5. Q2: Why the slope is the projection, not the source

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "it cannot comment on the source." The source might be green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The resolution came from an instrument, the spectrometer, not from an argument: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Map that directly. The measured T-linear slope is the green reading. Quantum criticality, phonons, Mottness, SYK-like islands, and five distinct holographic routes are the possible light mixtures. Hartnoll and Mackenzie give two of them in one review; the adscft.org guide lists five more, all inside holography. The spectrometer analogue here is a constrained multi-observable comparison: rho(T), sigma(omega), tan(theta_H), kappa, A(k,omega), s(T), with a prediction dated before the measurement. Until that instrument exists, naming the source is the metamerism error.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." A holographic model validating holography from its own fit is the named collapse.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach, and my own standing

**Prestige is not action.** From the same essay, verbatim: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly," and "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." The prestige of AdS/CFT, of a famous institution, or of a well-cited review is a budget fact. It is not a witness.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay, verbatim: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post," but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not an experiment, not a calculation of a transport exponent, and not a test of gauge/gravity duality. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 7. Shape for this question, stated once

> A candidate answer to "do strange metals hint at a holographic description" has the right shape only if it (i) names the per-slot residual it explains rather than the bundle, (ii) predicts a constrained set of responses, not a single slope, (iii) states a dated forward prediction and a refuter before the measurement, and (iv) records its provenance so a convergent derivation can be told apart from an adopted import. The framework supplies that shape. It does not supply the word "holographic."
