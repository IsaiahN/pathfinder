# The Tether's answer: do strange metals hint at a holographic description?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a physics result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a set of anomalous readings in strange metals points to a holographic (gauge/gravity) description. The readings are: rho = rho_0 + A T over a wide range with no Mott-Ioffe-Regel saturation; an inferred relaxation time of order hbar/(kB T); broad, non-quasiparticle spectral functions; and in cuprates a two-lifetime transport pattern. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." So the residual is a **union compound**, not one hard question, and it has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape (one loop per domain, verdicts never averaged)

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- **Not** "strange metals are holographic." That would be a name where the framework returns a shape.
- **Not** "holography is the only known description, therefore it is right." "Only known" is a budget filter. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **Not** "quantum criticality, phonons, Mottness or a gauge principle are excluded." The opened reviews say several mechanisms produce the same readings.
- **Not** a derivation of one theory from the other, and no seventh primitive.
- **Not** grounded. No measurement settles `left(R,phi)` here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the metamerism. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Constrained multi-observable test.** Does one model reproduce rho(T), sigma(omega,T), tan(theta_H), kappa, A(k,omega) and s(T) for a given material from one parameter set, with a prediction dated before measurement? The adscft.org guide: "Agreement with rho~T is weak evidence; agreement among constrained response functions is strong evidence."
2. **Underivability test.** Is there a proof that the conventional closure cannot produce the residual's shape in principle? If yes, OUTWARD is licensed. If no, and a conventional derivation appears at accessible budget, the import is not owed (Fig 8 :40-43).
3. **Discriminating prediction.** AD/EMD models predict a fractional Aharonov-Bohm effect in Phillips, Hussey and Abbamonte's Table 2. Is it observed? A clean null or positive result would separate models that a slope cannot.
4. **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: a unique source discriminator; a conventional derivation; a failed or retrospective-only forward prediction; a broken six-term substrate.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The six terms are "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (framework essay, quoted in the exemplar); declaring them makes a choice visible, not neutral. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical README). The gap is named as a gap, because Fig 13 :134 says unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that strange metals are holographic, and it does not claim to settle the physics. It says that the strange-metal readings are a union of overlapping conditions, not one anomaly, so there is no single "behavior" that could point to a single frame. For any one slot, the shape is describable: a scale-free relaxation on the time hbar/(kB T), with no quasiparticle, producing a constrained set of responses. The framework returns that shape, not a name; several mechanisms, including several distinct holographic ones, reach the same reading, so the reading does not determine the source. A gauge/gravity dual is a legitimate candidate frame whose closure differs, but importing it needs provenance and a shadow test, and whether it is owed at all depends on an open question: is the conventional many-body closure underivable here, or merely unreachable at this budget? What the framework offers is the shaped residual, a refuter written in advance, and the discriminating multi-observable comparison that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground pays.

## Cost if wrong, undoable, reversed by

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, because the shape claim carries its own refuter and is not asserted as a physics result. The real cost is spending an outside checker's independence by sending a name and a literature list before asking what they would count as a failure (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable.
- **Undoable:** yes. No physical term is withdrawn by narrowing this claim; the bindings are removable without touching the derivation. A sent shape can be superseded by the corrected packet in `08`.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating multi-observable comparison, a proof of underivability, a controlled conventional derivation, or a dated forward prediction falsified by experiment would move the verdict. Prestige, citation count and the number of agreeing holographic models are not such instruments.
