# Outreach draft: check the shaped residual first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 4 are understood as a `specified` claim, not a physics result. The stage-1 object must survive on its own.

---

**Subject:** Check one shaped residual before you decide whether holography is indicated

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question about strange metals and I would like you to check one output against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The strange-metal phenomenology is not one anomaly but a union of overlapping conditions: rho = rho_0 + A T over a wide range with no saturation beyond the Mott-Ioffe-Regel limit, an inferred relaxation time of order hbar/(kB T), broad rather than quasiparticle spectral functions, and in cuprates a two-lifetime pattern (rho_xx about T, cot(theta_H) about T^2). These do not always co-occur. So the first output is a refusal of the bundled question: there is no single "the behavior" whose shape would point to one frame. There are per-slot residuals.
>
> The second output is the shape of one such slot: a scale-free relaxation with no quasiparticle description, reported across materials and probes. The framework I am using returns a shape, not a name. It can say what a successful frame must predict. It cannot say that the frame is a black hole in anti-de Sitter space. Naming that is my contribution, not the framework's, and it carries zero evidentiary weight until provenance is shown.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that a cuprate is dual to a black hole. I am not claiming that holography is the only description, even though a recent review says it is currently the only known one. That is a fact about the literature and the budget, not a measurement. I am not claiming that quantum criticality, phonons, Mottness or a gauge principle are excluded; several distinct mechanisms reach a T-linear reading. The reading does not determine the source.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a measurement that maps the strange-metal readings uniquely to one microscopic source, so a second mechanism could not produce the same constrained observable set;
> 2. a non-holographic derivation, inside the many-body closure and at accessible budget, that produces the residual's shape (then no import is owed);
> 3. a dated forward prediction from a gravitational frame that a discriminating measurement contradicts, or a demonstration that the k-dependent ARPES exponent is an ordinary band-structure or matrix-element effect.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are in `04_links_and_distribution.md`. The figures, not my summary, are the authority. The key primary literature is: Hartnoll and Mackenzie, *Planckian Dissipation in Metals*, Rev. Mod. Phys. 94, 041002 (2022); Phillips, Hussey and Abbamonte, *Stranger than Metals*, Science 377 (2022); Smit et al., Nature Communications 15 (2024); Cinti, De Haro, Golden, Gürsoy and Stoof, Foundations of Physics 56, 8 (2026).
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
