## Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

A partition would mean each anomaly has its own clean mechanism and the intersections are empty. The opened reviews say otherwise. Hartnoll and Mackenzie report T-linear resistivity in conventional metals at room temperature (their Table 1) and in unconventional metals at low temperature (their Tables 2 and 3), with different mechanisms: "two very different mechanisms leading to a Planckian electronic lifetime: quantum criticality and scattering by lattice vibrations." Phillips, Hussey and Abbamonte report materials where T-linearity holds only at high T (Sr2RuO4, Sr3Ru2O7) and others where it holds down to the lowest T (overdoped cuprates, FeSe1-xSx, MATBG).

So the observables overlap, and the overlap is itself a compound: a material that is both beyond the Mott-Ioffe-Regel limit and Planckian and quasiparticle-free is a specific object, not three puzzles stuck together. The correct move is unbundling per slot, then recomposing the compound.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single mechanism is measured to produce all the slots from one parameter set, with the co-occurrence shown, the union collapses to a partition and F1 of `01` is withdrawn.

## Q2 — Are the competing theories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
- Fig 8 :50 "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."

Two readings of the question, and they give different answers:

**(a) At the level of microscopic ingredients.** A quantum-critical spin-fluctuation model, a Mottness model and an AdS/EMD model do not share the same atoms. One has electrons and bosonic order-parameter fluctuations; one has Hubbard bands and spectral-weight transfer; one has a charged black brane and a dilaton. They are **not isomers** in Fig 12's strict sense, because the atom sets differ. They are different theories with different primitives.

**(b) At the level of the shared phenomenological atom set.** If we define the atoms as the constrained observable slots {rho(T) slope, tau/A, sigma(omega) shape, tan(theta_H) exponent, kappa, A(k,omega), s(T)}, then two models that reproduce the whole set but compose it through different mechanisms are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance (the substance being the theory, decisive in "the behaviour" only when an extra observable is added that one model predicts and the other does not). Fig 12 :97-98 gives the chemistry precedent: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties.

**(c) The convergent case.** If two models reproduce the *entire* constrained set with no residual between them, then by Fig 13 :121 they are different routes to one substance at the phenomenological level, and by Fig 8 :50 they count as corroboration only if their evidence pools are genuinely independent. Phillips, Hussey and Abbamonte's Table 2 shows this is not the case at present: different models satisfy different subsets of the constraints (AdS/CFT satisfies rho(T) and omega^(-2/3) but fails quadrature MR in their table; AD/EMD satisfies more, including extended criticality and a predicted fractional Aharonov-Bohm effect). The isomers are distinguishable, so the isomer claim is live.

**Conclusion on Q2.** The strict isomer claim holds only at a declared level (the shared observable atom set), and only while some constrained observable still separates the models. When one model predicts a novel observable (the fractional A-B effect in AD/EMD, per Table 2) and the others do not, that is the "decisive in the behaviour" part of Fig 12 :117. This is the falsifiable content, not a metaphor.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a model reproduces every constrained observable of a given material with no distinguishing prediction left, the isomer claim for that pair fails and the convergent-route reading (Fig 13 :121) stands instead. If the models share no observable atoms at all, the strict isomer claim fails immediately and only the weaker "different theories" statement remains.

## Q3 — Can the six-term substrate say anything about transport?

- Fig 13 :9-14: states are space, time, energy; spectrum is ordering, gradient is "a difference that can be spent," network is "that entities relate, and influence travels."
- Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
- Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."
- Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

A transport process reads as a composition over the held atoms: energy (state) with a structure (spectrum for the ordering of excitations, gradient for the spendable difference, network for the lattice/entanglement), extended over space and time (states). Fig 13 :76 is genuinely useful here: a gradient over space and one over time are *different terms*, which is exactly the structure of the two-lifetime phenomenology (rho_xx about T from one channel, cot(theta_H) about T^2 from another) reported in cuprates by Phillips, Hussey and Abbamonte. The substrate licenses the *shape* of "more than one relaxation channel," not the identity of the channels.

The extra spatial dimension of AdS must not be promoted to a seventh primitive. Re-census: it is a parameter of the network/gradient binding inside the gravity frame, not a new kind. Fig 13 :41 "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it" and :115 "The set is closed" forbid adding a member; composition only rearranges the six. This dissolves the apparent strain between "holography has an emergent dimension" and the six-term set. It is the same re-census the exemplar used for its Fig 13 versus Rule 2 strain: different objects, no contradiction.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** derive one of the six from the other five, or show that the extra dimension is a genuine new kind rather than a parameter of a held structure. Either would break the substrate and require withdrawal of this section.

## Q4 — What order is the comparison of frames?

- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"
- Fig 13 :110 "And each composition becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

A CM description settled against laboratory data becomes an atom at the next level. A gravitational description settled against the same ground becomes an atom too. Their comparison is then a **second-order composition**, legal for the same reason recursion is legal in Fig 12 :104. It needs no new primitive. But the operand type is strained: Fig 12 :87 defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities," while at second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. That is a type question requiring a check, not an argument. Recorded as a live strain, not hidden.

**Falsifier:** show that a settled molecule cannot serve as an atom, or that comparison must take bare quantities at every level. Then the second-order framing fails, and the two descriptions cannot be compared as Fig 12 permits.

## Q5 — Why the dual crossing is not a seventh member and not a recording

- Fig 10 :68 "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."
- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

A gauge/gravity mapping is a crossing between frames with a lossy transform, which Fig 10 :68 explicitly permits. What crosses is a *method* for computing constrained correlators, not a recording of one fit. The round trip (predict a boundary observable from bulk data, measure it, compare) has a gap R_T that must be measured. The Symbols table defines R_T as "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold." Cinti et al.'s distinction between semi-holography (an effective or quasi-duality in the IR) and full holography is precisely a report that this gap is nonzero and must be declared.

## CONCLUSION

- The strange-metal observables are a **union**, not a partition; unbundle per slot.
- Two theories are **isomers** only at a declared shared observable-atom set and only while some constrained observable still separates them; otherwise they are convergent routes to one substance (Fig 13 :121).
- The six-term substrate licenses the *shape* of multiple relaxation channels (Fig 13 :76) and forbids promoting the emergent bulk dimension to a seventh primitive (Fig 13 :115).
- The frame comparison is **second-order**, licensed by Fig 12 :104 but carrying a live operand-type strain.
- The dual crossing is a lossy generator crossing with a measurable R_T, not a new primitive and not a recording.
