## What the wider literature contributes, and where it overlaps

The literature splits cleanly across low-overlap fields, which is why agreement among them carries some weight:

1. **Climate physics / earth systems:** Loarie et al. and Ackerly define the moving band and show that the required speed is a habitat property, steep terrain slow and flat terrain fast. Climate velocity is a **condition** in the habitat, not the ground (Fig 11 :73).
2. **Community ecology / meta-analysis:** Chen et al. measure observed range shifts and show rapid averages built from widely varying individual responses. This is the ground for D2, and it also enforces per-slot reading (Fig 1 :19).
3. **Movement ecology / animal behaviour:** Nathan et al. split movement into internal state, motion capacity, navigation capacity and external factors. This corroborates F2: the external factors are load-bearing.
4. **Evolutionary biology:** Hoffmann and Sgrò, Gonzalez et al., and Buskirk et al. give the adaptive rate its currencies (breeder's equation, per-generation response, haldanes) and state the standing concern that environmental change can outpace adaptation. This is the ground for D3.
5. **Conservation policy:** Ricciardi and Simberloff supply the contingency and irreversibility that make assisted migration a habitat substitution with silent failure modes (Fig 11 :66-68).

**Overlap to declare.** Ackerly 2010, the 2024 Nature mountain paper and most later velocity work cite Loarie et al. 2009. They are not independent witnesses to the *existence* of climate velocity; they share a source. Their agreement corroborates the figure-derived shape (rate depends on habitat) but counts as one evidence pool for that specific claim. Chen et al. and the 2024 Nature paper draw on different occurrence databases (BioShifts versus the Chen meta-analysis), so their agreement about tracking is weaker-pooled and carries more weight. Hoffmann and Sgrò is cited by the other evolutionary sources, so the adaptation-rate pool is partly shared. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was not triggered.
