## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says when a climate band shifts and a species must follow. Option A: report one global rate, averaging across species and mechanisms. Option B: treat the question as a union of conditions, give a per-domain rate shape with separate grounds, and leave the number to provenanced measurement. What each leads to: A hides the per-species signal and spends the independence of outside readers on a filter masquerading as a verdict; B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open number until a ground settles it. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run: a per-species tracking capacity, decomposed into a range-shift rate and an in-place adaptive rate, compared against a local climate velocity, settled by observed persistence.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the rate of a moving climate band and the rate at which a species' realized niche can track it. Arity: at least three conditions (forcing, movement, in place change), so a union compound, not one hard question. Symmetry: movement changes location; adaptation changes the species-to-condition relation; they are not interchangeable and have different currencies and instruments. Scale: individual, population, range, community, recursive across strata.
- **Domain 1, climate forcing / habitat conditions.** Actors: isotherms, temperature and precipitation gradients, topography, season. Currency: climate velocity in km per year. Anchor: none that settles persistence; this is a condition enumerated from the world, not the ground. Not a domain that issues a verdict.
- **Domain 2, movement / range shift.** Actors: dispersing individuals, propagules, corridors, barriers, mutualists and partners. Currency: distance per time, colonization and extinction rates. Anchor: observed range boundaries through time, a measurement that does not update on our models. Ground-visible.
- **Domain 3, adaptation / plasticity.** Actors: individuals, heritable variation, generation time, selection gradient, phenotype. Currency: trait change per generation, plastic response magnitude. Anchor: measured trait change, heritability estimates, common-garden and selection experiments. Ground-visible.
- **Domain 4, verification and management.** Actors: researchers, managers, monitoring datasets, models. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: observed persistence and extinction, never agreement. Ground-visible.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Conditions overlap; a single lever clearing one leaves the intersections binding. Unbundle before search.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the shift, have terms for what the habitat affords (corridors, gradients, partners), state the objective (per-species persistence), plan the route (movement or adaptation, parallel branches), learn and carry (observed persistence settles which worked). The middle link is the usual break.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only. They cannot overrule a figure verdict. A conflict would send us back to the domain census; none did.

- **Earth systems / climate velocity.** Scott Loarie, Philip Duffy, Healy Hamilton, Gregory Asner, Christopher Field and David Ackerly, "The velocity of climate change" (Nature, 2009): "species survival may depend as much on keeping pace with moving climates as the climate's ultimate persistence". Predicts a moving band whose local speed depends on the spatial gradient. Corroborates the habitat-condition part of the shape. Overlap: high within the velocity literature, because later papers cite it.
- **Conservation biogeography.** David Ackerly, "The geography of climate change: implications for conservation biogeography" (Diversity and Distributions, 2010): "the velocity to keep pace with this same rate of climate change rises to 6 km year -1, rapidly outpacing dispersal rates for many organisms". Predicts that habitat structure changes the required speed by orders of magnitude. Corroborates F2. Overlap: cites Loarie 2009, so not independent on the existence of velocity.
- **Community ecology / meta-analysis.** I-Ching Chen, Jane Hill, Ralf Ohlemüller, David Roy and Chris Thomas, "Rapid range shifts of species associated with high levels of climate warming" (Science, 2011): "distributions of species have recently shifted to higher elevations at a median rate of 11.0 meters per decade, and to higher latitudes at a median rate of 16.9 kilometers per decade" and "individual species vary greatly in their rates of change". Predicts observed tracking and wide among-species variation. Corroborates the movement ground and F8. Overlap: distinct from the velocity papers on occurrence data.
- **Evolutionary biology.** Ary Hoffmann and Carla Sgrò, "Climate change and evolutionary adaptation" (Nature, 2011): "Evolutionary responses might only be sufficient to allow changes of a few per cent per generation". Predicts an adaptive rate limited by heritable variation and generation time. Corroborates F5. Overlap: cited by the other evolutionary sources.
- **Evolutionary rescue theory.** Andrew Gonzalez, Ophélie Ronce, Regis Ferriere and Michael Hochberg, "Evolutionary rescue" (Phil Trans R Soc B, 2013): "There is concern that the rate of environmental change is now exceeding the capacity of many populations to adapt." Predicts a rate comparison decides persistence. Corroborates the union verdict. Overlap: shares the Hoffmann evidence pool partly.
- **Movement ecology / animal behaviour.** Ran Nathan, Wayne Getz, Eloy Revilla, Marcel Holyoak, Ronen Kadmon, David Saltz and Peter Smouse, "A movement ecology paradigm for unifying organismal movement research" (PNAS, 2008): "four basic mechanistic components of organismal movement: the internal state (why move?), motion (how to move?), and navigation (when and where to move?) capacities of the individual and the external factors affecting movement." Predicts that external factors are load-bearing. Corroborates F2. Overlap: low with the evolutionary sources.
- **Phenotypic plasticity / ornithology.** Josh Buskirk, Robert Mulvihill and Robert Leberman, "Phenotypic plasticity alone cannot explain climate-induced change in avian migration timing" (Ecology and Evolution, 2012): "birds may be adjusting to climate change with a combination of phenotypic plasticity and rapid microevolution". Predicts two paths acting together. Corroborates the union reading and F5. Overlap: cites Hoffmann and Sgrò.
- **Conservation policy.** Anthony Ricciardi and Daniel Simberloff, "Assisted colonization is not a viable conservation strategy" (Trends in Ecology and Evolution, 2009): "Accurate risk assessment is impeded by contingency: the impacts of introduced species vary over time and space under the influence of local environmental variables, interspecific interactions and evolutionary change." Predicts silent failure modes in translocation. Corroborates F4. Overlap: low with the physiology and genetics sources.
- **Earth observation / ground measure.** "Climate velocities and species tracking in global mountain regions" (Nature, 2024): whether species track warming is assessed chiefly by comparing velocities of range shifts with velocities of climate change. Predicts that the verdict comes from a ground comparison, not model agreement. Corroborates F6. Overlap: cites Loarie 2009.

The agreement across climate physics, community ecology, movement ecology, evolutionary biology and conservation policy is broad, but several sources cite the same 2009 velocity paper or the same 2011 adaptation review, so parts of the agreement are one evidence pool talking. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the number stays open. The question is a union compound with separate grounds, not one rate. Tracking capacity belongs to species and habitat together, so a species-only speed is not a figure-licensed claim. A dispersule is a head start whose fate the landing conditions decide, and the binding partner can be three steps out in the contact cascade. Adaptation is selection running on a lineage, and only contact-changing responses count. The ground is observed persistence, read per species, never a global average or model agreement. The figures return this shape and no speed. Every specific magnitude is a provenanced import from empirical ecology with a source gate and shadow test. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The strongest checkable point is the ground rule: frames range against the ground, they do not negotiate with it.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording or a global average instead of a generator and a per-slot shape. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The global-rate framing and the treatment of climate velocity as an anchor are removable without touching the figure derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Per-species occurrence series showing range boundaries tracking or failing to track local isotherm velocity would settle the movement branch; common-garden and heritability studies giving a per-generation response rate would settle the adaptation branch; a documented case of a tracked band missing its required partner three steps out would confirm the cascade branch; a case of a species tracking with no partner dependence would refute it. The seven refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 would break the shape itself. A source-discriminating instrument that separates movement from adaptation in a persistence record, not another model, is what would move any of this to `grounded`.
