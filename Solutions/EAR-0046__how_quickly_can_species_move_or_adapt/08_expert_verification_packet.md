# Expert verification packet: the Tether on species tracking a shifting climate

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate-adaptation or species-migration researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the composition rules. Not `grounded`.** No species-specific speed is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How quickly can a species move or adapt as climate zones shift" is a union of conditions with separate grounds, not one number. Tracking capacity belongs to the species and its habitat together: a fast disperser with no corridor and no required partner tracks poorly, and a slow mover in a steep connected gradient may track with little movement. A dispersule is a head start whose fate the landing conditions decide. The binding partner can be three steps out in the contact cascade. "Adapt" is selection running on heritable variation in a lineage, and only a change that alters contact with the ground counts. The ground is observed persistence, read per species, never a global average or model agreement. The figures return this shape; the numeric rate is an import from empirical ecology with provenance and a shadow test.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 11

- **Statement:** realized capability is a property of agent and habitat, not of the species alone, and only changes that alter ground contact count.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Also Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." And Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."
- **What to check:** is this a fair reading of the figure text? Does the tracking literature treat habitat contact (corridors, gradients, partners) as load-bearing in the same way? Nathan et al. 2008 (PNAS) decomposes movement into internal state, motion capacity, navigation capacity and external factors; Ackerly 2010 (Diversity and Distributions) shows the required speed changes by two orders of magnitude between a mountainside and flat terrain.
- **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that changes realized tracking while habitat contact is held identical; or a plastic or genetic change that alters persistence without changing contact with the ground; or adaptation with no selection on heritable variation. Fig 11 :72 names the first as the standing refutation of the capability claim.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable by a researcher against observed range shifts and local habitat

- **Statement:** the rate that matters can be set by a relation three steps out, and the dispersule is a head start rather than a guaranteed adult.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 4 :34 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
- **What to check:** can you name a case where a species reached its newly suitable band and failed because a mutualist, prey or competitor was absent? Chen et al. 2011 (Science) report median poleward shifts of 16.9 km per decade with wide variation among species; Ricciardi and Simberloff 2009 (Trends in Ecology and Evolution) attribute translocation failure to local environmental variables, interspecific interactions and evolutionary change. Do these support the cascade shape, or do they show direct climate tracking dominates?
- **Refuter:** a species that tracks its climate band with no dependence on any other actor and no effect beyond first-order contact across the whole tracking interval; or a translocation that reproduces all required conditions and inherits no unchosen actors, with no silent failure mode.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about the verdict itself

- **Statement:** the ground for this question is observed persistence per species, not agreement among models, and the numeric rate is not derivable from the figures.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- **What to check:** is climate velocity correctly classified as a habitat condition rather than an anchor? Is the per-slot requirement consistent with the wide among-species variation in Chen et al. 2011? Do the 2024 Nature mountain-tracking study and Loarie et al. 2009 supply rates by import rather than by framework derivation?
- **Refuter:** two datasets drawn from one monitoring pool shown to be independent witnesses; a global mean shown to preserve the signal identifying which species is failing; or a species-specific tracking rate derived from the six-term set alone and matching observation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a single speed, a global average, or a number for any named species.
- Not that climate velocity decides persistence; it is a habitat condition, not the ground.
- Not that movement and adaptation are isomers or one mechanism; they are union conditions with different currencies (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not that assisted migration is a clean fix. Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"
- Not that the framework derived anyone's published rate. Loarie et al., Chen et al., Hoffmann and Sgrò, Nathan et al., Gonzalez et al. and Ricciardi and Simberloff are provenanced imports.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by this derivation.
- Not a test of the framework by this packet. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Movement ground:** per-species occurrence time series compared with local isotherm velocity, showing whether the range boundary tracks.
2. **Adaptation ground:** common-garden and heritability studies showing a per-generation trait response that closes the gap.
3. **Cascade ground:** enumeration of the required partners in the newly suitable band, showing whether the limiter is three steps out.
4. **The seven refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.** If one fires, the shape is withdrawn.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39, :40, :52 and :73, and Fig 1 :19. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against one case you know: did a species arrive in a suitable band and fail because a partner was missing, or did direct climate tracking dominate?
3. Name one existing instrument that discriminates movement from adaptation in a persistence record, or say why none does. "Another model" is not an instrument.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
