# Outreach draft: check one separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the per-slot residual and the anchor test in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 9 are stated for the case you are writing about. The offer collapses on contact if "disagreement" still means three different things.

---

**Subject:** What would count as a failure for this claim about disagreement?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the word "disagreement" does not name one thing. Under the framework being checked it names at least five different cases, and they do not have the same verdict.
>
> 1. Two groups that disagree in vocabulary but read one evidence pool. The union adds nothing; there are two names for one frame.
> 2. Two groups with genuinely different evidence whose surplus nobody aimed at a residual. The surplus exists but is mute.
> 3. Two groups with different evidence, a described residual, and an external anchor that does not update on what either group thinks. This is the one case where disagreement can move the wall.
> 4. A group with no such anchor, where members update on each other. They converge on whoever had the most influence, or keep disagreeing without progress.
> 5. A disagreement that does not shrink with effort while each standard works well on a different subset of cases. That is a signal to split the question, not to search harder.
>
> The separator is: disagreement improves collective reasoning only in cases 3 and 5, and case 5 is a diagnostic rather than a mechanism. The first thing to measure is not how much people disagree but whether the anchor moves when you push on it, and how much the two sides draw on the same evidence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any standing trait of a dissenter makes disagreement productive; that adding more disagreeing people helps; that shared standards of evidence are required for rationality; that this is a result about real crowds. A standard of evidence is a convention inside a frame, not the ground. The framework returns a shape and a route, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. A group with one shared evidence pool and no differing closure acquires a new settled atom purely by renaming.
> 2. A group with no non-updating anchor shows a per-slot residual that falls after disagreement and stays down under re-test.
> 3. Adding people who read the same evidence lowers the residual with provenance showing no new atom entered.
> 4. A group with no way to refuse a held claim carries a correction across a disagreement.
> 5. A ground is shown to move its answer because of what a frame thinks.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding case is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> - Figures and formula, SVG and text, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked multi-agent sketch, the closest essay to this question: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-a-society-of-agents-would-work
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a group you can actually measure. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, state whether your anchor updates, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — Isaiah
