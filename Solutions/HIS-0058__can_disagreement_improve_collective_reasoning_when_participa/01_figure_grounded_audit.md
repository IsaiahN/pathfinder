## 0. The question in effect terms, and the residual

The ordinary question asks for a yes or a no about "disagreement" under a condition called "not sharing standards of evidence." Neither phrase names a figure object, so both must be re-described in effect terms before any figure line is chosen (Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause").

**Participants are frames.** A participant is a bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model (Symbols table :111-112). Each frame holds a prediction and an observation, and the gap between them is the residual. Symbols table :36-40: "The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read." The residual is per slot, not a group average. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So the effect to be explained is not "the group got wiser" but "the per-slot residual of each frame moved toward zero, or did not."

**A standard of evidence is a frame-internal rule for what counts as settling.** It is how far a belief leans on private history versus inherited prior, which the Symbols table writes as `w`: Symbols table :24-26: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge." A standard is therefore part of a frame's own instrument, not the ground. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." This is the first decisive distinction: **not sharing a standard of evidence is not the same thing as not having a ground.** It means the frames do not have a shared convention for reading whatever ground is available.

**The anchor is a different object entirely.** Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." So the question resolves into two sub-questions that the ordinary wording conflates:

- **Q-a.** Is there an anchor that does not update on what any frame thinks, and that both frames can range against separately? Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."
- **Q-b.** How much do the frames draw on the same evidence? Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."

**Residual stated as an effect.** R is the group-level gap left after the frames have been in contact: does the collection reach a term that no single frame could compose (Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone"), and does that term pay for itself against the ground (Fig 5 :43-44: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before")? The question "can disagreement improve collective reasoning" is a question about whether the union of differing closures has a surplus that is aimed and settled. It is not a question about how much people disagree.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term.** The sought-for shape is a separator with five named cases (section 5), each with its own figure lines, mode and refuter. Binding it as a term follows Fig 5 :65: "The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else." That binding is what makes the shape refusable.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

**R (the residual).** Arity: the gap is indexed per object slot (Fig 1 :11), and the collective question is a relation between at least two frames, so R is at least binary in frames and unary in slots. Symmetry: disagreement itself is symmetric between frames (frame A differs from B as B differs from A), but the effect we ask about is ordered, "improve" means the residual went down, so the verdict cannot be read from a symmetric agreement score (compare Operators table :15, swap test for +, against :33, swap test for →: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."). Scale: first order inside one frame's closure, second order at the union of two closures (Fig 7 :26: "progress per handoff: R = h² × S").

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the participant frame** | each participant, its library Γ_i, its standard of evidence, its weighting w_i | predictions and per-slot residuals R_i | that frame's own instrument or local standard; by the question the standards are not shared | partition boundary: what each closure can compose (Fig 6 :21) |
| **D2, the inter-frame or collective domain** | the frames in contact, the room, the commons, the imported atom | disagreement, agreement, surplus atom, per-slot residual after contact | an external ground that does not update on any frame, if one is reachable (Fig 2 :29) | union of at least four overlapping conditions; not a partition (Fig 9 :54) |
| **D3, the verification domain** | this simulated primary seat, the simulated second seat, the expert reader | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :40) | union; in this run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero |
| **D4, the history-of-science habitat** | controversies, paradigms, standards of rationality, instruments, published records | settled results and the lineage that produced them | the experiment or instrument that settled past controversies (Fig 2 :29) | habitat, enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43) |

**Partition or union.** D2 is a union, not a partition. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A single lever, for example "add more disagreeing people," clears only the region where independence and an anchor already hold, and leaves the intersections standing. This is why the answer is a separator and not a number.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." The order here is: (1) name the anchor and check that it does not update (Fig 2 :33, :35); (2) describe the residual in effect terms before choosing any frame (Fig 9 :27-28); (3) measure independence at the evidence level (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :47); (4) refuse a false held term before minting a new one (Fig 5 :53); (5) select between rooms (Fig 7 :35); (6) settle against the ground (Fig 2 :45, Fig 5 :32). Most real disagreements about collective reasoning are argued at step 5 while step 1 was never measured. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 3. Findings

### F1 — "Disagreement" bundles three different objects, and only one moves the wall

Fig 8 names three cases in a single line of the diagram. Fig 8 :4-9: "collapse 4: the blend", "→ vocabulary, never an atom", "collapse 5: the undirected union", "→ interdisciplinarity, no mint", "grounded: triangulation", "→ the wall moves".

- **Blend.** Fig 8 :12-15: "high overlap / same atoms, different names / their union adds nothing / two names for one frame". Here the participants appear to disagree because they use different vocabularies, but they draw on one evidence pool. There is no surplus to improve anything.
- **Undirected union.** Fig 8 :19-21: "closures differ, so a surplus exists / but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute". Here the frames genuinely differ, so a surplus exists, but nobody described the residual first, so nothing is selected and the surplus is mute.
- **Triangulation.** Fig 8 :29: "low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence", and Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Here the surplus is aimed and the wall moves.

This is the central finding. A question that asks "can disagreement improve collective reasoning" without saying which of these three is meant has not described its residual. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 9 (low overlap between the union-surplus figure and the leave/arrive figure). Refuter: exhibit a case in which two frames with one shared evidence pool acquire a new atom purely by renaming, or a case in which an undirected union with no described residual reliably moves the wall. Either breaks the three-case census.

### F2 — A standard of evidence is a convention; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one

The ordinary view treats a shared standard of evidence as the thing that makes a group rational. The figures invert this. A standard is a declared rule for reading, and a declared rule is not the ground. Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Fig 10 :16-17: "the convention does not fix the bug / it makes the bug statable". Fig 10 :19: "→ install what can be violated."

And the named failure: Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." If a standard of evidence is used to decide which claim wins, it has become a verdict wearing a convention's clothes. Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."

This resolves the question's condition rather than bypassing it. Not sharing standards is a problem only if the group then tries to decide the outcome by negotiating a standard (Fig 2 :35, negotiation is not triangulation). If instead both frames range against a ground that does not update, differing standards are simply different instruments, and Fig 2 :31 applies: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 2. Refuter: show a standard that settles a question without any external anchor while remaining a convention, that is, a rule whose authority is neither grounded nor a verdict. Until such a rule is shown, the re-grounding move stands.

### F3 — With no anchor, disagreement degrades into coherent drift or mute motion

The question's phrase "do not share standards of evidence" is easily read as "have no common ground." If that is the intended condition, the figure answer is negative. Fig 2 :13: "no anchor; each updates on the others". Fig 2 :14-15: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

If the frames refuse to converge and simply persist in disagreement, they are a mirror chain. Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" with the factors at Fig 7 :38-39: "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and "the ground's pressure on the room". With no ground pressure, one factor is zero and the chain runs in place.

Fig 10 :14-15 adds a limit on how harshly to read this: "a disagreement nothing can state / is not yet a defect". Unstatable disagreement is not yet a failure of the group; it is a job for a convention that makes it statable, provided that convention does not decide the outcome (F2).

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7. Refuter: exhibit a collection with no non-updating anchor whose per-slot residual trends down after disagreement and stays down under re-test. That breaks the no-anchor verdict.

### F4 — Independence is the single parameter that decides both whether agreement means anything and whether the union has surplus

Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :48-49: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 6 :53-54: "And it inherits Figure 8's condition directly: searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names. Independence decides whether more of them buy more search or only more spelling, which makes it the parameter rather than a caveat."

This is why adding more disagreeing participants does not by itself improve collective reasoning. Fig 6 :49-50: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." and "Fails by spending budget on a region already swept. Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent." Fig 7 :12-13 again: "agreement compounds, evidence does not".

Fig 2 :42 prices the design: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :40 disqualifies the cheap version: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41 keeps the legitimate use: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 6 and Fig 2 (three figures, low overlap between the surplus figure and the closure figure). Refuter: a case in which adding frames that draw on the same evidence lowers the per-slot residual against a ground, with provenance showing no new atom entered. That breaks "independence is the parameter."

### F5 — Differing closures are necessary for surplus, and import is the only operator that moves the wall

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

So the condition under which disagreement can help is not "people disagree" but "their closures differ at the evidence level and the difference can be carried back." Fig 8 :45-46: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." and "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live." Fig 8 :50-52: "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

The caveat is priced. Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." A group that simply adopts the loudest frame's standard has imported a wrong atom and paid above the price of starting from its own primitives.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8. Refuter: show a frame adding an atom to its own closure with no contact with a differing closure, or show an import from a live peer that is not debited against independence. Either breaks the import account.

### F6 — Productive disagreement needs a refusal operator, not just more claims

A vocabulary without negation cannot carry a correction; it can only accumulate. Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Fig 5 :50-53: "AMENDMENT · the gap has a fifth kind, and the pipeline has no branch for it." and "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 5 :54-55: "A term that refuses another pays the same bargain. Stating the refusal, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."

This matters for the question because a group whose members can only add claims, never refuse a held term, will treat disagreement as more input to accumulate. The correction cannot be carried. Fig 12 :111-112: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." and "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

The reach falls when a refusal is recorded. Fig 6 :41-42: "AMENDMENT · the wall moves outward on import. It also moves inward, and this figure is silent on that." and "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." Productive disagreement can therefore lower a group's reachable set, and that fall is a feature, not a loss.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 12 and the Operators table. Refuter: show a group with no operator for negation that nonetheless carries a correction across a disagreement without adding a claim. That breaks the negation requirement.

### F7 — A non-shrinking disagreement is evidence that the question is a compound, not a harder question

Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Applied here: if two participants keep disagreeing and each standard keeps working well on a different subset of cases, the residual is a union of several well-formed questions with an unassignable shared fraction (Fig 9 :55-57). The disagreement has improved collective reasoning by surfacing that the question must be split. This is a "yes" of a different kind, and it must not be averaged with the yes in triangulation.

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 8 and Fig 13. Refuter: show a persistent disagreement in which each standard works on a disjoint subset that nevertheless behaves as one well-formed question under a single anchor, with no split required. That breaks the diagnostic case.

### F8 — No standing list of good or bad dissenters survives a change of residual

The ordinary search for "which kind of disagreement helps" looks for a property of the dissenter: contrarian, devil's advocate, outsider, minority. The figures refuse that standing list. Fig 11 :49-51: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." and "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." and "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

So the correct unit of analysis is the relation between a frame, a residual and an anchor, not the type of person. Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The habitat must be enumerated, not composed. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 10 :73-79 gives the honest selection mechanisms: "a lottery", "everyone who qualifies", "a set assembled before the question existed", "or the habitat itself, enumerated rather than composed. See Figure 11." Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 10. Refuter: exhibit a standing personal trait that predicts productive disagreement across two residuals with different anchors. That breaks the relational reading.

### F9 — Even a productive room can corrupt the check if the seat is authored or the verifier holds the interiors

D3 is not a side issue. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :59: "The condition is that nothing about which value the ground returns today serves the occupant: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :65-66: "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."

The group's own agreement is never the verdict. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote." Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

Dissent that is recruited by a participant is an authored room. Fig 10 :73: "The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second." Fig 10 :75: "A conscientious selection is still a selection, so unauthored needs a mechanism".

**Verdict:** `general` on the structural requirement, triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 7 and Fig 2. Refuter: show a group whose verifier authored the claim and whose ground was one of the parties, yet whose settled term survives blind re-test. That breaks the seat account.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the standing discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The separator is refuted, in whole or in the named part, if any of the following holds:

1. **Breaks F1.** Two frames with one shared evidence pool, and no differing closure, acquire a new settled atom purely by disagreement and renaming, with provenance showing no import.
2. **Breaks F3.** A collection with no non-updating anchor shows a per-slot residual that trends down after disagreement and stays down under re-test against an external measurement.
3. **Breaks the re-grounding move (F2).** A standard of evidence is exhibited that settles a question without any external anchor while remaining a convention rather than a verdict.
4. **Breaks F4.** Adding frames that draw on the same evidence lowers the per-slot residual against a ground, with provenance showing no new atom entered and no independence gained.
5. **Breaks F6.** A group with no operator for negation carries a correction across a disagreement without adding a claim, and the correction is settleable.
6. **Breaks F7.** A persistent disagreement in which each standard works on a disjoint subset behaves as one well-formed question under a single anchor with no split required.
7. **Breaks the anchor definition.** A ground is shown to move its answer because of what a frame thinks, so Fig 2 :29 fails.
8. **Breaks the gap.** A figure line is produced that returns a universal amount, ratio or standing rule for how much disagreement is optimal, which would overturn the `gap` row in F10.

If any fires, the affected claim is withdrawn, the census is redone from the residual line, and the correction is kept visible rather than silently substituted.

## 5. Conclusion: verdicts per domain, kept separate and never averaged

**D1, participant frame (`specified`, triangulated).** Each participant ranges against its own local instrument. Its residual stays indexed per slot; a standard is a frame-internal weighting of private history and inherited prior. A participant cannot score its own reasoning with a quantity it also produces. Disagreement is not a property of this domain; it is a relation between frames. No verdict about the group is returned here.

**D2, collective domain (`specified`, five cases, not averaged).**

1. **Blend, high overlap, no surplus.** No. There is one evidence pool and two vocabularies. Fig 8 :12-15.
2. **Undirected union, low overlap, no described residual.** Not yet. A surplus exists but is mute because nothing was aimed. Fig 8 :19-21, Fig 8 :53.
3. **Anchored triangulation, low overlap, described residual, non-updating anchor.** Yes. This is the only figure-licensed case in which disagreement improves collective reasoning: the wall moves. Fig 8 :29, :45, Fig 7 :35, Fig 2 :45.
4. **No anchor, mutual update or mirror chain.** No. Convergence lands on whoever had the most influence, or the chain runs in place. Fig 2 :13-15, :34, Fig 7 :12-13, :36-39.
5. **Non-shrinking disagreement with subset-specific standards.** Yes, diagnostically. The group learns that the question is a union and must be split before it is searched again. Fig 9 :4-5, :54-57.

These five verdicts are not points on a scale and are never averaged into "disagreement helps somewhat." A group can be in case 3 on one slot and case 1 on another; Fig 1 :19 requires the verdict to stay per slot.

**D3, verification domain (`general` structural).** This derivation and its second seat share one evidence pool, so their agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". No external expert has checked this packet. No verdict about the claim's truth is returned here.

**D4, history-of-science habitat (`specified` with world corroboration only).** Incommensurability, the historical name for "not sharing standards of evidence," does not entail the absence of a ground. The world source on commensurability records that Kuhn retained a common reference environment and that terms used in the same way provide a base for comparison (Wikipedia, "Commensurability (philosophy of science)", opened 2026-11-23). That corroborates the figure distinction between differing standards (F2) and no anchor (F3), but it does not decide it. The figures decide.

**Overall mode.** `specified` on D1, D2 and D4, `general` on D3's structural requirement, not `grounded`, with a declared `gap` on any universal amount of disagreement. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Re-describe the residual before choosing a frame or a method (Fig 9 :27-28). Ask which of the five cases the group is in, per slot.
2. Establish whether an anchor exists and whether it updates (Fig 2 :29, :33). If it moves when pushed, it is not an anchor and case 3 is unavailable.
3. Measure independence at the evidence level, not at the level of opinion (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :47). Buy it in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).
4. Give the group an operator for refusal (Operators :58). A group that can only accumulate cannot carry a correction.
5. Arrange selection between rooms rather than negotiation inside one (Fig 7 :35). Keep the member pool unauthored (Fig 10 :73-75).
6. Do not recruit dissenters by a standing trait list (Fig 11 :51). Enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43-44).
7. Record the lineage and the refuter before the run (Fig 12 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."). Send the generator, not a conclusion.

## 8. Strains, re-census and honest limits

- **Fig 8 :45 (union surplus) versus Fig 2 :40 (agreement worthless).** Apparent contradiction, dissolved by re-census. They govern different quantities: the surplus of two differing closures versus the verdict weight of two correlated frames. Fig 8 :47 says so explicitly: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Same parameter, different object. No strain after re-census.
- **Fig 7 :35 (selection between rooms) versus Fig 2 :45 (frames range against the ground).** Not strained. Selection between rooms is the mechanism by which frames range against the ground; the ground votes between rooms (Fig 7 :35), not inside them.
- **World source on group polarization versus the yes case.** Moscovici et al., as reported in the group-polarization source opened 2026-11-23, found that "interaction within a group and differences of opinion are necessary for group polarization to take place." This looks like evidence that disagreement harms reasoning. Re-census: polarization is the coherent-drift or mirror-chain collapse (Fig 2 :34, Fig 7 :36), which is case 4, not case 3. The source corroborates a case already in the census; it does not conflict with the figure verdict. Verdict not flipped.
- **Live strain, named, not hidden.** Whether a given "standard of evidence" functions as a convention (Fig 10 :47) or as a measuring instrument (Fig 2 :29) depends on whether it can be violated and whether it updates on what frames think. That cannot be decided by an argument; it needs an instrument on the actual group. It is left open.
- **Second-order type question.** Fig 7 :26 writes "progress per handoff: R = h² × S" with h² as the bracket's fidelity. Whether a social bracket's fidelity can be measured in the same units as a breeding heritability is not settled here. Marked as a type question, carried into `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `11_audit.md`.

## 9. What would settle it

Not more agreement. A ground reading on an actual collection:

1. A measurement of per-slot residual for each frame before and after contact, with provenance showing which atoms were imported and from which closure.
2. An anchor test: push on the proposed ground and check that its answer does not move (Fig 2 :29-30).
3. An independence measure at the evidence level, not the opinion level (Fig 2 :31), with the effective-sample-size correction named in Fig 2 :44.
4. A check that the group's vocabulary contains a refusal operator (Operators :58).
5. A re-test after the room closes, to see whether the residual reduction survives (Fig 4 :30).

Until these are run, the answer stays a `specified` shape with a written refuter, not a result about actual groups. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39), and "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).
