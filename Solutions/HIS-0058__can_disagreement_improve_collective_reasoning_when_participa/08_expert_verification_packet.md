# Expert verification packet: the Tether on disagreement without shared standards of evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on collective reasoning can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural requirement, not `grounded`.** No group was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Disagreement" does not name one effect. Over a fixed set of participants, standards, residual and anchor, it names three different arrangements from Fig 8 plus a no-anchor collapse from Fig 2 and a diagnostic split from Fig 9. The arrangements are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Only two carry a benefit. Anchored triangulation, with low evidence overlap, a described residual and a non-updating anchor, can move the wall. A non-shrinking disagreement whose rules work on disjoint subsets is a signal to split the question. A blend over one evidence pool adds nothing; an undirected union with no described residual is mute; a chain with no anchor converges on influence or runs in place. A standard of evidence is a frame-internal convention, not a ground, and the group needs an operator for refusal to carry any correction. No figure line returns an optimal amount of disagreement or a standing trait list of useful dissenters.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — The union framing and the unit of measurement

- **Statement:** the question is a union of at least five conditions, not one effect with a net value. The residual must be tracked per slot, not as a group average. A persistent disagreement with subset-specific standards is evidence for a split.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a persistent disagreement in which each standard works on a disjoint subset that nevertheless behaves as one well-formed question under a single anchor with no split required. Or show that a group-average residual fully predicts the per-slot residuals after contact.

### C2 — The two decisive conditions: a non-updating anchor and independence at the evidence level

- **Statement:** disagreement improves collective reasoning only when an external anchor exists that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks, and when the frames draw on different evidence. With one evidence pool the union adds nothing; with differing closures but no described residual the surplus is mute.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :19-21 "closures differ, so a surplus exists", "but no residual is described", "nothing was aimed; the union is mute". Fig 2 :14-15 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence".
- **Refuter:** a collection with no non-updating anchor whose per-slot residual falls after disagreement and stays down under re-test; or a case in which frames drawing on the same evidence acquire a new settled atom with provenance showing no import and no new independence.

### C3 — Refusal and the refusal of a trait list

- **Statement:** a group that can only add claims cannot carry a correction; it needs an operator for negation. A standard of evidence that decides an outcome is no longer a convention. No standing personal trait predicts which dissenter helps across residuals.
- **Source, verbatim:** Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **Refuter:** a group with no operator for negation that carries a correction across a disagreement without adding a claim, with the correction settleable against the ground; or a standing dissenter trait that predicts productivity across two residuals with different anchors.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that disagreement is always beneficial or always harmful.
- Not a universal amount, ratio or optimal level of disagreement. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that sharing standards of evidence is necessary for rational inquiry.
- Not that lacking shared standards means lacking any ground.
- Not that more participants help, or that a recruited panel of contrarians is an independent check.
- Not that the five cases have been observed. They are figure-derived bindings onto held figure objects, recorded with provenance.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement of a group.
- Not a claim that this simulated derivation can validate the figures. It cannot (see *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, essay).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading on an actual collection, not an argument and not further agreement:

1. Push on the proposed anchor and check that its answer does not move (Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor.").
2. Log each frame's per-slot residual before and after contact, with provenance for any imported atom.
3. Measure independence at the evidence level, not the opinion level (Fig 2 :31), with the effective-sample-size correction (Fig 2 :44).
4. Check whether the group holds an operator for negation (Operators :58).
5. Re-test after the room closes to distinguish a method from a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :12-21 and :45-49, plus Fig 2 :29-32 and :46. These are short passages.
2. Check C1 against one disagreement you know: can you assign each side to a case per slot, and does a group average hide the signal?
3. Name the anchor in that case, and say whether it moves when pushed. If there is none, say so. That alone decides case 3 versus case 4.
4. Say whether the two sides draw on the same evidence or on different evidence. If the same, the union adds nothing regardless of how strongly they disagree.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
