## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether introducing disagreement between participants whose standards of evidence differ lowers the per-slot gap between prediction and what an external ground returns. Option A: answer yes, disagreement improves collective reasoning. Option B: answer no, it does not. Option C, the one the figures return: the question is a union of conditions, and the answer is a five-case separator with separate verdicts. What each leads to: A and B each hide cases where the opposite holds; C returns the shape any answer must have, the instruments that discriminate the cases, and refuters written in advance. The five cases are blend (one evidence pool, nothing added), undirected union (surplus exists but is mute), anchored triangulation (wall moves), no anchor (influence convergence or mirror motion), and diagnostic split (the question is a compound).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: per-object-slot (Fig 1 :11), and the collective effect is a relation between at least two frames, so at least binary in frames and unary in slots. Symmetry: "disagree" is symmetric between frames, but "improve" is ordered, so a symmetric agreement score cannot carry the verdict. Scale: first order inside one closure, second order at the union. The ordinary phrase "standards of evidence" names a frame-internal convention, not the ground.
- **D1, participant frame.** Actors: a participant, its library, its standard, its weighting of private history versus inherited prior. Currency: predictions and per-slot residuals. Anchor: that frame's own instrument; by the question the standards are not shared. Type: partition boundary of what the closure can compose.
- **D2, collective domain.** Actors: the frames in contact, the room, the commons, the imported atom. Currency: agreement, disagreement, surplus atom, residual after contact. Anchor: an external ground that does not update on any frame, if one is reachable. Type: union of overlapping conditions, not a partition; five cases with separate verdicts.
- **D3, verification domain.** Actors: this simulated seat, the second seat, the expert reader. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. All seats in this run draw on one evidence pool, so independence here is zero.
- **D4, history-of-science habitat.** Actors: controversies, paradigms, standards of rationality, instruments, published records. Currency: settled results and lineage. Anchor: the experiment or instrument that settled past controversies. Type: habitat enumerated from the world, not composed.
- **Order by dependency.** Anchor and non-updating test first; residual described in effect terms before a frame is chosen; independence measured at the evidence level; refusal before mint; selection between rooms; settlement last. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." No strain after re-census: the apparent Fig 8 versus Fig 2 conflict is two quantities governed by the same independence parameter (Fig 8 :47).

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only; none overrules a figure. Each entry gives field, author or source, venue and year, a quote, a prediction here, and its status.

- **Mathematics and economics.** Robert Aumann, "Agreeing to Disagree", *The Annals of Statistics* 4(6), 1976, read via Wikipedia "Aumann's agreement theorem" (2026): "two Bayesian agents with the same prior beliefs cannot 'agree to disagree' about the probability of an event if their individual beliefs are common knowledge." Predicts case 1: one evidence pool plus common knowledge forces convergence, so persistent disagreement marks differing partitions or ambiguity. Corroborates Fig 2 and Fig 8. Low overlap.
- **Cognitive science and social epistemology.** Scott E. Page, diversity prediction theorem, reported in Wikipedia "Wisdom of the crowd" (2026): "The squared error of the collective prediction equals the average squared error minus the predictive diversity." The same source states that "the crowd needs to be independent but also diversified" and that averaging removes random but not systematic errors. Predicts case 3 and F4. Corroborates Fig 8 :45 and Fig 2 :31. Overlap with the other human-group sources: shared pool.
- **Social psychology.** Wikipedia "Group polarization" (2026), citing Moscovici et al. 1972 and Lord, Ross and Lepper 1979: "a disagreement becomes more extreme as the different parties consider evidence on the issue. It is one of the effects of confirmation bias." Predicts case 4, not case 3. Re-census dissolved the apparent conflict: polarization is the mirror-chain collapse, a case already in the census. Corroborates Fig 2 :34.
- **Social psychology and organizational studies.** Wikipedia "Groupthink" (2026), citing Janis 1972 and 1982 and Sunstein: concurrence-seeking "tends to override realistic appraisal of alternative courses of action"; an insincere devil's advocate is much less effective than genuine dissent. Predicts F2, F8 and F9. Corroborates Fig 10 :47 and Fig 11 :51. Same broad pool as the other human-group sources.
- **Animal behaviour.** Wikipedia "Collective animal behavior" (2026), citing Sumpter, Krause et al. 2008 and Biro et al. 2006: a fish quorum rule "generally resulted in the 'correct' decision but occasionally cascaded into the 'incorrect' decision"; pigeon groups showed "increased efficiency and decision making". Predicts the mechanism shape of quorum and cascade, not the human verdict. Corroborates Fig 7 :35 at the level of mechanism. Genuinely low overlap.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union, so the honest answer is a separator with five verdicts kept apart. Blend: no. Undirected union: not yet. Anchored triangulation: yes, the only mechanism case. No anchor: no. Diagnostic split: yes, diagnostically. Across all cases, a standard of evidence is a convention, not a ground; a group needs a refusal operator to carry a correction; no standing dissenter trait predicts productivity; and no figure line returns an optimal amount of disagreement. Mode `specified` on the separator, `general` on the structural requirement, not `grounded`. The absence of a universal setting is a declared `gap` reported as unreached, not a silence.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the refuters are used. The main cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording or a trait list instead of a separator. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the five cases and their failure conditions. A second cost is treating a convention as a ground, so a group's own standard decides an outcome and the drift becomes coherent and invisible.
- **undoable:** yes. The five cases are bindings onto held figure objects, so none requires withdrawing a primitive. A wrong assignment of a real group to a case is corrected by new provenance, a push test on the anchor, or a per-slot residual log. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one; the refuters stay visible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Measurement that shows per-slot residual falling and staying down in a group with no non-updating anchor would break case 4. Provenance showing a new settled atom entering a blend with one evidence pool would break case 1. A standard that settles a question without an external anchor while remaining a convention would break the re-grounding move. A figure re-census that returns a universal disagreement setting would break the declared `gap`. Until any of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
