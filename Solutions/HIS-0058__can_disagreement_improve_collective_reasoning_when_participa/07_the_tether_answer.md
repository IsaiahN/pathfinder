# The Tether's answer: can disagreement improve collective reasoning without shared standards of evidence?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a separator and a route, not a result about any real group. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The ordinary question bundles five different effects under one word. "Disagreement" can be a vocabulary difference over one evidence pool, a genuine difference of evidence with no aimed residual, an aimed difference with a non-updating anchor, a mutual-update chain with no anchor, or a persistent disagreement whose rules each work on a different subset. These do not have the same verdict, and averaging them would throw away the signal. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The residual is the per-slot gap between a frame's prediction and what the ground returns, before and after frames are put in contact. "Standards of evidence" are frame-internal conventions for reading that gap, not the gap itself and not the ground. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

## 2. The shape: five cases, separate verdicts

1. **Blend, high overlap, no surplus. No.** Two vocabularies, one evidence pool. Fig 8 :12-15: "their union adds nothing", "two names for one frame". Disagreement is cosmetic here.
2. **Undirected union, low overlap, no described residual. Not yet.** Fig 8 :19-21: "closures differ, so a surplus exists", "but no residual is described", "nothing was aimed; the union is mute". The surplus exists but cannot be selected. Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."
3. **Anchored triangulation. Yes, this is the only mechanism case.** Low overlap (Fig 8 :29), a described residual, a non-updating anchor that each frame ranges against separately (Fig 2 :46), and independence at the evidence level (Fig 2 :31). Then Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone", and Fig 6 :14 calls import "the only operator that moves the wall".
4. **No anchor, mutual update or mirror chain. No.** Fig 2 :13: "no anchor; each updates on the others"; Fig 2 :14-15: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"; Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". With no ground pressure, one factor of Fig 7 :26 is zero and the chain runs in place.
5. **Non-shrinking disagreement with subset-specific standards. Yes, diagnostically.** Fig 9 :4-5: such disagreements "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The gain is a corrected question, not a settled claim.

Two structural conditions cut across all five. First, the group needs an operator for refusal, because a vocabulary without one can only accumulate: Operators table :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Second, no standing trait of a dissenter predicts productivity: Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual". Valence is a relation to a residual, not a property of a person (Fig 11 :49-50).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "disagreement is always good" and not "disagreement is always bad". Both collapse the union.
- Not that sharing standards of evidence is required for rationality. A standard is a convention; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that not sharing standards means there is no ground. The ground is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29).
- Not that more participants help. Independence, not headcount, decides whether searchers buy more search or only more spelling (Fig 6 :54).
- Not that a cleverly chosen set of dissenters is a valid check. A participant-chosen room is an authored room (Fig 10 :73).
- Not a universal amount, ratio or optimal level of disagreement. No figure line returns one (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134). That is a declared `gap`, which is a verdict and not silence.
- Not a result about real groups. Nothing here was measured.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminator, not more agreement. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay) shows the pattern: a reading of green "cannot comment on the source", and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Here the corresponding instruments are:

1. **Anchor push test.** Push on the proposed ground and check that its answer does not move (Fig 2 :29-30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor.").
2. **Per-slot residual log.** Record each frame's residual before and after contact, indexed per slot, not as a group average (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. **Provenance of imports.** Show which atom crossed from which closure, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :24; Fig 8 :50-52).
4. **Independence measure at the evidence level.** Two people reading the same report are not two frames (Fig 2 :31); apply the effective-sample-size correction named in Fig 2 :44.
5. **Refusal check.** Does the group's vocabulary contain an operator for ¬, so a held term can be refused (Operators :58)?
6. **Re-test after the room closes.** Does the residual reduction survive, or was it a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25, :30)?

Standing refuters from `01` section 4 are written down and remain live. If any fires, the named case is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The separator is the shape the answer must have; which case a particular collection is in has not been measured. The canonical README says the framework returns "a shape, not a name", stops at the limit of the instruments, and records the lineage so the stop is honest. "Indiscernibility is not identity." A group that merely reports feeling more aligned after disagreement has not shown that its residual fell. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Simulated second seat in `11_audit.md` shares this derivation's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a verdict on whether disagreement is good. It returns a separator. Disagreement is one evidence pool wearing two vocabularies when the frames share a source, and it adds nothing; it is a genuine surplus with nobody aiming it when the closures differ but no residual was described, and it is mute; it can move the wall only when two independent frames range separately against an anchor that does not update on what either thinks and the residual was described first; it becomes influence-convergence or a mirror chain when there is no anchor at all; and when it does not shrink while each standard keeps working on a different subset, it is telling you the question is a compound and must be split. The framework refuses to rank dissenters by a standing trait and refuses to name an optimal amount of disagreement. What it offers is the five-case shape, the instruments that would tell you which case you are in, and refuters written in advance. The measurement stays open until the ground settles it.
