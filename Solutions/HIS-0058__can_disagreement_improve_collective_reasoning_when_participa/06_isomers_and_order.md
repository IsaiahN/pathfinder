# Structure and order: are the five cases isomers, and in what order do they run?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

`01` returns five cases with separate verdicts, not a single effect. Two questions are owed: (1) are those cases different substances over one atom set, or are they degrees of one quantity, and (2) what is the dependency order, so that a group works on the right link first?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the five cases isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Hold the atom set fixed: `{frame A, frame B, standard A, standard B, residual R_s, evidence, anchor}`. The cases differ only in which relation holds between the frames and the anchor:
  1. Blend: identity relation between the two standards, one evidence pool. Fig 8 :12-15: "two names for one frame".
  2. Undirected union: differing closures, no described residual, no aimed bond. Fig 8 :19-21: "nothing was aimed; the union is mute".
  3. Anchored triangulation: comparison relation between two independently ranged frames plus a non-updating anchor. Fig 8 :29, :45.
  4. Mirror chain: sequence relation of mutual updating with no anchor. Fig 2 :13: "no anchor; each updates on the others".
  5. Union split: comparison plus a decomposition of the residual into several well-formed questions. Fig 9 :4-5.
- Same atoms, different bond, different substance. On Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**. The difference is decisive in behaviour: case 3 moves the wall, case 2 is mute, case 1 adds nothing, case 4 drifts, case 5 splits.
- Weaker form: if the atom set is allowed to vary, all five share the functional group "two frames in contact over a residual". Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The bond is the qualifier.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the five outcomes can be generated by varying one continuous quantity, such as communication frequency or number of participants, while the anchor and the evidence overlap stay fixed and no new atom enters, then they are degrees, not isomers, and the isomer claim fails. The functional-group reading would stand instead.

### Q2 — What bond is each case?

- **Blend is identity.** Operators table :25: "two names, one referent". Two vocabularies, one evidence pool. The test at Operators :27: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." If substituting frame A's vocabulary for frame B's loses nothing, they are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :49).
- **Mirror chain is sequence.** Operators table :31: "A then B; B needs A to have happened". The test at Operators :33: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Mutual updating is two sequences, and the order of influence decides where they converge, which is Fig 2 :15: "is whoever had the most influence".
- **Triangulation is comparison plus an anchor.** Operators table :49: "a threshold relation between two quantities". Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities". The two quantities are the per-slot residuals of the two frames, each ranged separately against the non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :46). The threshold is whether the union's surplus pays under the acceptance bargain, Fig 5 :43-44: it "costs less than what was unexplained before".
- **The undirected union has no aimed bond.** Fig 8 :20-21: "but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute". This is not a sixth bond. It is the absence of a relation that would select the surplus. Fig 12 :90: "A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet." (This wording is from the Operators table at :68; the Fig 12 version is at :90-91: "A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet.") An undirected union is a claim about an arrangement not yet made.
- **Negation is not a bond.** Operators table :70-77: "Seven of the eight are bonds: they join two operands, and both the symmetry column and the tests are questions about how two things join. Negation joins nothing, so its symmetry entry is empty rather than no". A group that refuses a held claim is not relating two frames; it is acting on one term. Operators :73: "Negation is the refusal of a claim." This is why the refusal operator in F6 of the audit cannot be counted as another kind of disagreement between people.

### Q3 — In what order must the links run?

- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A list of participant types cannot say how a group gets from disagreement to a settled term.
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." So the group needs four different operators, not one: a route, a target, a measure of movement, and a refusal.
- Dependency order, from `01` section 1 and Fig 3 :20: anchor before method; residual description before frame selection (Fig 9 :27-28); independence before headcount (Fig 8 :47); refusal before mint (Fig 5 :53); selection between rooms before settlement (Fig 7 :35); settlement against the ground last (Fig 5 :32).
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." A group that already has a good instrument but no anchor is working above the break. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Most managed disagreement is at the selection link; the anchor link is unmeasured.
- Parallel is not the same as independent. Operators table :22: "not concurrency. Two things running in parallel both matter; remove one and it breaks, which is +". Two frames running side by side compose by conjunction only if both matter; remove one and the union breaks. Independence is about evidence, not about simultaneity (Fig 2 :31).

### Q4 — What is the analogue that carries over, and what does not?

The strongest low-overlap analogue is Page's diversity prediction theorem, recorded in the world source opened for `04`: "The squared error of the collective prediction equals the average squared error minus the predictive diversity." That is Fig 8 :45 in arithmetic form: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." It carries the shape of the surplus.

What does not carry over is the assumption that diversity is a quantity that can simply be increased. The same source says a crowd needs to be "independent but also diversified", and that averaging removes random but not systematic errors. Fig 8 :47 says the same in figure terms: "Independence is one quantity read twice." The analogue corroborates the surplus shape; it does not license a diversity target.

A second analogue is Aumann's agreement theorem (world source, `04`): with a common prior and common knowledge of posteriors, agents cannot agree to disagree. That is the formal version of the blend case: one evidence pool, two names. It does not describe the anchored case, because a non-updating anchor plus separate evidence breaks the common-posterior premise.

## CONCLUSION

The five cases are **isomers** over a fixed atom set: same frames, standards, residual, evidence and anchor, different bond and different substance. The weaker functional-group reading survives if the atom set is allowed to vary. Each case has a named bond: identity for blend, sequence for mirror drift, comparison for anchored triangulation, no aimed bond for the undirected union, and comparison plus decomposition for the split case. Negation is a unary operator outside this set and is required to carry corrections.

The dependency order is anchor, residual, independence, refusal, selection, settlement. Working on selection before the anchor is effort spent above a break that has not been diagnosed. The live falsifier is the single-continuous-lever test in Q1: if one quantity can generate all five outcomes with the anchor and overlap fixed, the isomer claim fails.
