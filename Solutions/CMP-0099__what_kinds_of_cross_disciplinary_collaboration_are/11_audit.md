## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 8 :7, :19-21, :45, :53; Fig 2 :31, :35; Fig 9 :54-57; Fig 12 :47, :111, :117; Fig 13 :74.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07`: unbundle the union, select low-overlap frames, import a bond, maintain a room, keep one ground, measure R_T.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete collaboration design: a named project team, a shared data schema, a weekly meeting, a reviewer, a publication.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **Union unbundling** (Fig 9 :54-57). A single project team clears the regions where one condition holds and leaves the institutional-ecological and ecological-behavioural intersections standing.
- **Source gate and shadow test** (Fig 8 :12-13). A shared data schema records agreement, not provenance; convergent derivation and adopted import remain indistinguishable in the contents.
- **Habitat enumeration** (Fig 11 :43). A weekly meeting composes a team but does not enumerate the actors, conditions and relations in contact with the residual.
- **Methods-only crossing** (Fig 4 :22). A shared repository of recordings crosses instances, not generators, and produces a team that repeats a past success.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a structure must be bonded to a process under a typed relation that the topology alone does not carry, and the frames must range against one ground. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
