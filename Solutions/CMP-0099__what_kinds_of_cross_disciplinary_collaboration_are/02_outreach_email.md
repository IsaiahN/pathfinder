> **The output, stated as a shape, mode `specified` and not `grounded`:** building a model that connects network structure to institutional, ecological, or behavioural processes needs a *kind of collaboration*, not a bigger team. The figure I am checking says that an undirected interdisciplinary mix with no described failure mints nothing. What it permits is the opposite: describe the failure in effect terms first, then find a frame whose closure already predicts its shape, keep the frames low in shared evidence, and let each be corrected by the same unmoving ground. What crosses between the frames is a typed relation, a bond, that the node and edge list alone does not carry. The figures give the shape of that bond and its kinds; they do not select one for a given process. I leave that slot open.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that collaboration is valuable because more people agree; that a shared vocabulary is the missing thing; that one team format works for institutions, ecosystems and behaviour alike; or that the bond can be named without a measurement. The question bundles three process conditions, and I treat them as separate loops with their own anchors.

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and its five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. Say explicitly that the figures are the authority, not the summary.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust, agreement, or endorsement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
