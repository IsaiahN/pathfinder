# Bonds, isomers and order in network-to-process models

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §8).

## RESIDUAL

A network model and a process model can share every observable node and edge and still be different models. This file derives what carries that difference, whether two such models are isomers under Fig 12, and what order the modelling chain must have. The residual is the part of the model not recoverable from the ingredient list.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. What is the missing thing: an atom or a bond?

- Fig 13 :3: "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."
- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
- Fig 12 :47: "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

So a topology plus a process variable is an ingredient list. The collaboration owes a **typed bond** between them. Network (structure) may compose with a state (time or energy) and yield a flow or a rule; it may not compose directly with another structure under Fig 13 :74. This is a `general` structural constraint, not a `specified` model of any one domain.

### Q2. Are two network-to-process models isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :100: "A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."
- Take one network `N` and one process variable `P`. Four legal models over the same atoms:
  1. `N + P` (conjunction): structure and process both hold at once; symmetric.
  2. `N → P` (sequence): structure first, process follows; ordered.
  3. `N ⇒ P` (production): the process did not exist before the structure fired; ordered.
  4. `N ⋛ P` (comparison): a threshold relation between two quantities; ordered.
- Under Fig 12 these are **four different substances**, not four spellings of one. Only the symmetric bond gives k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing").
- **Real-world test, low overlap:** complex contagion (Wikipedia, 2026, reporting Centola and Macy 2007) reports that information and disease spread as simple contagions with one contact, while "behaviors typically spread as 'complex contagions', requiring multiples sources of reinforcement to induce adoption." Same network, different process rule, different outcome. This is a `specified` instance, unmeasured by us, of Fig 12 :117.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two process domains over the same topology are shown to be related by identity (`≡`) with no loss of predictive information, that is, one is a mere relabelling of the other, the isomer claim fails and the flat-formula reading stands.

### Q3. What order does the modelling chain have?

Fig 3 names five links and fixes the order by dependency, not by importance.

| link | in this question | failure if skipped |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | observe the network and the process outcome separately | no residual can be read |
| 2 vocabulary | have terms for what the structure affords and what the process counts as progress | "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar" (Fig 12 :115); this is the middle link |
| 3 objective | say what would count as progress in each domain's own terms | Fig 3 :11, "what the search is for" |
| 4 planning | a route under incomplete information | Fig 3 :13 |
| 5 learn and carry | name what worked, verify it, carry it | Fig 3 :15 |

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The middle link (2 to 3) is where cross-disciplinary work usually has no instrument, because each discipline has terms for its own affordances but not for the joint objective. This is where a collaboration must be aimed.

Dependency order with the figures: refuse the generic union before minting (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle each first-order model before composing a cross-domain one (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"); then measure the round trip (Fig 4 :30).

### Q4. Two currencies, not one

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A cross-disciplinary collaboration therefore has two budgets that cannot be summed: the bits needed to state the joint model, and the actions needed to check it against institutional cases, field data, or experiments. A long chain of cheap parts may cost one measurement to refuse; a short arrangement over a costly operand may take a whole field season. Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

**Falsifier for Q4:** show a single scalar budget that predicts both the description cost and the settlement cost across the three process domains without losing information. If one exists, the two-currency claim fails.

### Q5. Negation and refusal

Fig 12 :139: "So the operators divide by arity, and this figure describes the binary ones. Without a unary operator a system can write what a situation contains, what follows what, and how far along it is - and it cannot write that any of those is false."

A collaboration vocabulary that can only accumulate shared findings cannot carry a correction. It needs negation: a way to record that a held cross-domain regularity is false, and to remove the standing of models built on it without deleting the record (Fig 6 :41-43). This is why `01` section 5 requires refuters before conclusions.

## CONCLUSION

The difference between a network model and a network-to-process model is a **bond**, not an extra node attribute. Under Fig 12 the same atoms under different bonds are different substances, and a real low-overlap analogue (simple versus complex contagion) shows the same network with different process dynamics. The modelling chain is ordered by dependency, and its weakest link is the joint vocabulary/objective pair where no instrument usually exists. Two currencies price the work and may not be added; a unary refusal is required to carry corrections. The isomer claim is `specified`, falsified by an identity relation that preserves all predictive information.
