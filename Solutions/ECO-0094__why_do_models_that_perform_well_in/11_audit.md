## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions. The residual line I used was: "a model fitted and validated in A predicts well in A; the same model in B has a large residual; what is owed is the cause of that growth". WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity three (M, A, B), not two: collapsing A and B into a symmetric pair is the first thing to refuse. Symmetry: ordered, A -> B differs from B -> A (→ sequence, Operators table; Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."). Scale: local fit versus general application, so a membrane and a round trip are in play (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, the fitted-model frame.** Actors: predictors, parameters, functional form, training records. Currency: fitted residual on A. Anchor: ground A. Question type: partition (which compositions cross kinds inside the model).
- **Domain 2, the habitats.** Actors: species, observers, conditions, relations. Currency: ecological outcomes in A and B. Anchor: ground B, independent observations. Question type: union with D1 where model rigidity and habitat change intersect.
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: validation designs, reviewers, journals. Currency: agreement versus residual on B. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run its independence is zero because it shares the primary's evidence pool.

Order by dependency: enumerate the habitat before fitting (Fig 11 :43); refuse more-search-within-A before importing (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); validate against an independent ground (Fig 2 :45); measure the round trip (Fig 4 :30).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 11 and Fig 4, I derived from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 12's ordered-bond rule. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39, :49, :66-68, :73, Fig 4 :25 and :30, Fig 2 :40, Fig 13 :76.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: a model's skill is a property of model and habitat; transfer is habitat substitution; predictor valence is relative to the old residual; validation inside A shares a pool; mechanism stays `gap`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete ecological claim, for example "model M failed in region B because predictor P changed its effect".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the specific missing condition**, which Fig 13 :135 forbids naming from inside the library;
- the **instrument choice** that would discriminate among candidate sources (the metamerism point from *The Hard Problem is About Source*);
- the **measured independence** of the target ground, which the shorthand asserts but does not supply;
- the **bond type** (space versus time) that Fig 13 :76 says makes the two transfers different terms.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the shape that performance belongs to model-and-habitat and that transfer substitutes a habitat. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "a fitted model is a general rule" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
