# The Tether's answer: why a model fitted in one region or period fails in another

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on its binding to ecological models, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A model fitted and validated in habitat A has a small residual against ground A. Applied to habitat B it has a large residual. The failure is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: changed habitat, model rigidity and validation design can each hold, and a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :54-55). It has its own loop and its own ground.

**Domain 2, the habitats. Verdict: capability lives in model-and-habitat, and transfer is substitution (`triangulated`).**
Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

This is the decisive governor. A model does not carry its skill with it; it is re-anchored in a habitat it did not author, and the missing conditions only register when the goal fails. Predictor roles are relative to the old residual: Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries", and Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." **Domain 3, verification. Verdict: inside-A agreement is not a check (`triangulated`).**

Random cross-validation in A draws training and test folds from one pool. It is one frame with two names. Only an independent ground B, or a design that forces extrapolation, registers the transfer residual. Status: `triangulated` across Fig 2, Fig 8 and Fig 7.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the algorithm is bad. Refitting, more data and better tuning remain valid inside A; they just do not change contact with B (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that the ground changed. Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." What decays is the channel (Fig 10 :24).
- Not that any particular predictor caused a failure. That is the metamerism error (essay, `05_what_shape_means.md` §4).
- Not that many models validated in A help. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not that this is a general theory of everything ecological. `THE_FORMULA.md` returns a shape, and the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish" (README).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at B was taken by this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, and what it would take to close the gap

A source-discriminating design, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding checks are:

1. **Habitat swap test.** Independently measure actors, conditions and relations in A and B, then swap the habitat while holding M fixed. If performance is invariant, the shape fails (refuter 1 in `01` §3).
2. **Valence test.** For the terms M uses, test whether sign and magnitude survive a documented change of residual. If they do, refuter 2 fires.
3. **Independence test.** Compare a blocked or external design against random CV in A across three target regions. If the shared-pool design detects transfer failure as reliably, refuter 3 fires.
4. **Round trip.** Carry a local method up, bring it down into B, and measure `R_T` rather than assuming it is zero (Fig 4 :30).
5. **Import with a gate.** Import the missing term from the target-region frame with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8). Do not ship an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :41-43).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not diagnose why your model failed and does not offer a new algorithm. It says that a model's good performance is not a property of the model alone: it is a property of the model together with the habitat it was fitted in, and moving it to a new region or period substitutes a habitat whose actors and conditions were not chosen. What was essential in the source and was not reproduced in the target stays invisible until the prediction fails, and a predictor's useful role is relative to the old residual, so it does not carry over as a fixed good predictor. A set of fitted coefficients is a recording of one occasion, not a generator; what can cross scales is a method, with a measured round-trip gap. Validation inside the source region shares an evidence pool with fitting and cannot see the failure. What is offered is the shape any explanation must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific mechanism stays open until an independent ground settles it.
