# Outreach draft: check one claim about your own validation before we discuss any theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter survive the recipient's own check. The claim below is a shape, not a result. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** A claim about why models fail when moved to a new region or period

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` in shape, `specified` in its ecological binding, not `grounded`):** a model that predicts well in region A is not a standalone capability. Its performance is a property of the model together with the habitat of A: the conditions, actors and relations that were present when it was fitted. Moving it to region B substitutes a habitat the modeller did not choose. What was essential in A and was not reproduced in B stays invisible until the prediction fails. A predictor's useful role is relative to the residual in the habitat where it was learned, so it does not carry over as a fixed good predictor. Random cross-validation inside A tests one evidence pool against itself and cannot see this.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular algorithm is bad, that more data never helps, that the world changed its answer, or that I can name the mechanism for a specific failure. Those need a ground reading I have not done. This is a shape any candidate explanation would have to have, not a diagnosis of your model.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. A documented habitat substitution (actors, conditions and relations independently measured in A and B) that leaves model performance unchanged, with the model unchanged.
> 2. For the terms the model uses, a predictor role (sign and magnitude) that is invariant across a documented change of habitat and residual.
> 3. A validation design that shares its evidence pool with training and detects transfer failure as reliably as independent external validation in three target regions.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Look at Fig 11 lines 43-51 and 66-73, Fig 4 lines 22-30, Fig 13 line 76.
> - The habitat figure in context: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - The two-stage method note (context, subtext, pretext and checking against the world): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
