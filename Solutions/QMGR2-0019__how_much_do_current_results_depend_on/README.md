## What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run

Following the formula's Direction rule, the sought-for shape is made an ordinary term before searching:

> A checkable report that names each analysis assumption slot (prior width, dataset selection, calibration anchor, nuisance model, model class), states for each slot how far a stated result moves when that slot is pushed, records which independent probes were actually independent, and reports the gap between the baseline result and its re-run under admissible alternatives. A single global dependency percentage is **not** such an answer.

Option A: deliver one scalar fraction, the percent of a result that is assumption driven. Option B: deliver the slot-indexed map, the anchor test, and the measured round-trip gap. A reads as a ground result and hides which slot carries the leverage. B does not produce a headline number, but every claim in it is refutable by a named person with a named data set.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this report.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No global "assumption dependence percentage" presented as a ground result.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a witness when the pool is shared.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Selecting readers for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless (Fig 2 :39).
2. **The anchor does not update.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A pile of analyses that all inherit the same prior cannot move the anchor.
3. **Do not hand the interior to the checker.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and the refuter, not the framework.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional and opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
