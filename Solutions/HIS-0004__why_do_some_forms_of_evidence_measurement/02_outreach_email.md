**Subject:** One checkable claim about why evidence forms gain authority in context

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Measurement wins in a laboratory and loses in a court. A witness wins in a court and loses in a physics seminar. An inherited practice wins in an apprenticeship and loses at a calibration bench. Authority is not behaving like a property of a form of evidence considered alone. It behaves like a relation between a form, a context and the thing that settles that context's question. What selects the form?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The shape claim: an evidence form is authoritative in a context when it is the least updatable available link between that context's question and the ground that settles it, and its contact with that ground is independent of the frames it is used to check. Three consequences:
>
> 1. Measurement and experiment can be authoritative in their own right because they can be non-updating. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
> 2. Testimony is authoritative when the question is constitutively about people and the witness has separate contact. Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
> 3. Tradition is a head start, not a verdict. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
>
> This is a shape, not a ranking. I am not claiming that one form always beats another, that history reduces to this rule, or that any measurement settles it.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these before writing the conclusion:**
> 1. A form confers authority where its answer moves when the frames using it push on it, with no other non-updating link.
> 2. Two frames with maximally independent contact agree, and the agreement carries no more weight than a shared pool.
> 3. A context about a quantity takes a person as final anchor, or a context about persons takes a counter as final anchor, with no loss on the other's measuring axes.
> 4. A saturated or untraceable channel still settles a question without an independent chain or a better instrument.
> 5. A tradition decides an empirical outcome while remaining a convention.
> 6. Authority tracks credibility generated inside the using community, with no change in contact.
> 7. A context missing its objective link reliably picks the ground-ranged form over the ready-made one.
>
> If one holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. This is `specified`, not `grounded`: no measurement here settles it, and my derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Measurement in Science" (Eran Tal, 2020): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/measurement-science/
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Epistemological Problems of Testimony" (Nick Leonard, 2021): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/testimony-episprob/
> - JCGM VIM3, entry 2.41 on metrological traceability (BIPM): https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html
>

Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

**Two-stage rule.** Stage 1 is the residual, the shape claim and the refuter. Record the recipient's verdict on stage 1 *before* any theory is shown. Stage 2, opt-in only, is the three source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Stage 3 offers the formula as a method to reuse, with each user writing their own refuter and stating their own mode.
