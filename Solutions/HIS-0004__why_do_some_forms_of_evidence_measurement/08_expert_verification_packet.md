# Expert verification packet: the Tether on evidence and authority (HIS-0004)

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of authority in a historical context is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Authority is not a property of a form of evidence considered alone. A form is authoritative in a context when it is the least updatable independent link between that context's question and the ground that settles it. Measurement and experiment can be anchors because they can be non-updating; testimony is the right anchor when the question is constitutively about persons and the witness had separate contact; tradition is a head start to be re-ranged, never a final arbiter. Agreement from a shared evidence pool, prestige and credibility accrual do not confer authority. The four forms are a union of contact relations, not a ranking.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The anchor test governs all four forms
- **Statement:** authority requires a reference that does not change its answer when the frames using it push on it, and the context selects which candidate is the right anchor by what the question is about.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
- **What to check:** does the anchor test separate the four cases in a context you know, or does it merely redescribe who won? A good test must predict a case before you look at who won.
- **Refuter:** a form confers authority where its answer moves when the frames using it push on it, with no other non-updating link; or a context about a quantity takes a person as final anchor, or a context about persons takes a counter as final anchor, with no loss on the other's measuring axes.

### C2. Independence, not agreement, is the currency of testimony
- **Statement:** separate contact is what makes two witnesses more than one; a shared pool makes their agreement worthless for a verdict. Tradition is the mirror-chain limit of the same mechanism.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31-32 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **What to check:** in a case where several witnesses agreed, did they touch the event separately or inherit one report? In a case where tradition was authoritative, was it re-ranged or merely repeated?
- **Refuter:** two frames with maximally independent contact agree and their agreement carries no more weight than a shared pool; or a tradition decides an empirical outcome while remaining a convention under Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

### C3. The channel can fail while the ground does not
- **Statement:** a measurement's authority is conditional on an unbroken, fit-for-purpose channel. Traceability and absence of saturation are what keep it ranged against ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 13 :16 "it names what is being measured" and Fig 13 :26 "it decides what the measurement would mean."
- **What to check:** find a historical case where an instrument kept returning numbers after it stopped ranging against the quantity. Did its authority persist because of the number or because of an unbroken chain?
- **Refuter:** a saturated or untraceable channel still settles a question without an independent chain or a better instrument. The engineering definition supports this test: JCGM VIM3 entry 2.41 defines traceability as "property of a measurement result whereby the result can be related to a reference through a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to the measurement uncertainty", and NOTE 5 warns that this "does not ensure that the measurement uncertainty is adequate for a given purpose or that there is an absence of mistakes."

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of evidence forms, and not "measurement always wins." Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment the right anchor for questions about people.
- Not a complete history of evidential standards. No society's records were read for this derivation.
- Not that agreement is worthless everywhere. Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."
- Not that tradition is useless. It is the cheapest carrier of earlier contact, legitimate as a head start.
- Not that this derivation is `grounded`, and not that it can validate itself. *Arrival of the Fittest*: "Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself."
- Not that the four forms exhaust the census. A fifth with a clear anchor requires a new census.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating historical or experimental case, not an endorsement:

1. A form's answer moves under pressure with no alternative anchor, yet authority persists (breaks F1).
2. Maximally independent frames agree and gain nothing over a shared pool (breaks F2).
3. The wrong kind of anchor is final for a question with no loss on the other axis (breaks F3).
4. A dead channel still settles (breaks F4).
5. A tradition decides an empirical outcome as a convention (breaks F5).
6. Authority tracks in-group credibility with no contact change (breaks F7).
7. A missing objective link reliably picks the ground-ranged form (breaks F8).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :36-38, :40 and Fig 4 :22. Four short passages.
2. Pick one historical context you know well and apply C1. Does the anchor test predict which form was authoritative before you check who won?
3. Apply C2 to the witnesses or instruments in that context. Were they independent or one pool?
4. Name one case where a channel decayed while the ground did not, or say why none exists in your period.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is not pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
