# The Tether's answer: why evidence forms gain authority in particular contexts

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a historical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The variation to explain: measurement is decisive in a laboratory and weak in a court; a witness is decisive in a court and weak in a physics seminar; tradition is decisive in an apprenticeship and weak at a calibration bench. Four forms, four contexts, no stable winner. The question is a **union**, not a partition: Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Each form is a distinct contact relation with its own loop; verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Authority attaches to the anchor, not to the form.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Measurement and experiment are named as possible anchors; testimony and tradition are not.
2. **The context selects the anchor by what the question is about.** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." So "context" is not atmosphere; it is the selection of a ground.
3. **Contact must be independent.** Fig 2 :31-32 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Ten witnesses who read the same report are one witness.
4. **A measurement is an anchor only while the channel holds.** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Traceability and fit-for-purpose are conditions, not decorations.
5. **Experiment's distinctive power is refusal.** Fig 5 :14 "mechanism broken: a new term is owed." Fig 9 :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides." A witness can report a refusal; only the intervention supplies it.
6. **Tradition is a head start, never a verdict.** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Inherited practice carries former contact cheaply; it goes wrong when it is promoted to the thing that settles.
7. **Prestige is disqualified.** Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Credibility accrual cannot confer authority.

The shape, in one line: **an evidence form is authoritative in a context when it is the least updatable independent link between that context's question and the ground that settles it, and tradition contributes only as a re-ranged head start.** This is `specified`: a checkable prediction about what would make authority legitimate, not a measurement of any society.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a ranking of forms. No form wins in all contexts; Fig 2 :37 blocks it directly.
- Not a theory of every historical shift. This derivation never read a society's records; it reports a shape, not a history.
- Not "authority is whatever the community accepts." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not that tradition is worthless. It is the cheapest carrier of former contact, which is why it is upstream in the dependency order (Fig 4 :22).
- Not that measurement is infallible. The channel decays and the instrument cannot comment on its own source.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument here measured authority in a context.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A discriminating case, not a further argument:

1. A context where a form's answer moves when frames push on it, and no other non-updating link exists (breaks F1).
2. Two maximally independent frames whose agreement adds no weight over a shared pool (breaks F2).
3. A question about a quantity settled finally by a person, or a question about persons settled finally by a counter, with no loss on the other's axes (breaks F3).
4. A saturated or untraceable channel that still settles without an independent chain (breaks F4).
5. A tradition that decides an empirical outcome while remaining a convention (breaks F5).
6. Authority tracking credibility generated by the using community with no contact change (breaks F7).
7. A missing objective link that reliably selects the ground-ranged form (breaks F8).

What would move the verdict from `specified` to `grounded` is a historical or experimental study that operationalises "non-updatable" and "independent contact" and measures the relation. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Until such a study, the verdict stands as `candidate`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four-form census is a visible guess at the vocabulary of evidence, not nature's own list. *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature*: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." A fifth form with a clear anchor would require a new census, not a strained fit. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The record stops at the limit of the available instruments and keeps the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The answer is not that one kind of evidence is stronger. Authority follows the ground, not the form. An instrument is authoritative when it is traceable to a reference that does not move when people push on it; a witness is authoritative when the question is about people and the witness had separate contact with the event; an experiment is authoritative when its outcome can refuse a held claim; tradition is authoritative only as a head start that gets re-checked, never as the final arbiter. In each case the context matters because it selects which ground the question is constitutively about. Agreement from a shared pool, prestige and accumulated credibility are all disqualified: they are quantities a frame produces about itself. What this offers is the shape any answer must have, seven refuters written in advance, and the cases that would settle it. It is `specified`, not `grounded`: no measurement here decided which form actually won in any society.
