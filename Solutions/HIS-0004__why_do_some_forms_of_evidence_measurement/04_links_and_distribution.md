## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Tal (2020) separates the privilege of measurement from the conditions that earn it.** The SEP entry calls measurement "a privileged source of knowledge" and immediately notes "little consensus among philosophers as to how to define measurement, what sorts of things are measurable, or which conditions make measurement possible." That is exactly the gap the anchor test in Fig 2 :29 fills: it does not say measurement is always right, it says what would make it an anchor.
2. **Leonard (2021) blocks the global rank.** The testimony entry's own structure disaggregates testimony by subject matter and by source. Combined with Fig 2 :37, it predicts that "testimony" cannot have one authority score across a court, a seminar and a clinical consultation.
3. **Lamon et al. (2017) show tradition working without a ground.** Chimpanzee moss-sponging persists through kin-based transmission. That is a real mechanism of authority inside a lineage and a proof that tradition's grip is social transmission, not ground ranging. It corroborates Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. Overlaps are declared in the ledger: the six low-overlap sources are from philosophy of science, social epistemology, political philosophy, metrology and animal behaviour; the five essays are by the framework author and carry high overlap, so they corroborate the internal reading only.
