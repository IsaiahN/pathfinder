## 0. The residual under audit

The source question is: why do measurement, testimony, experiment, or tradition gain authority in *particular contexts*?

Stated as an effect: authority A is not observed to be a property of a form E alone. The same instrument is decisive in a laboratory and irrelevant in a court; the same witness is decisive in a court and irrelevant in a physics seminar; the same inherited rule is decisive in an apprenticeship and irrelevant at a calibration bench. The residual is the *variation of authority across contexts*. An answer must say what selects the form. No list of forms answers it.

The question is a **union**, not a partition. The four items are not four species of one genus called evidence; they are four different contact relations between a question and whatever settles it. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing." So each form gets its own loop and its own verdict. Verdicts are not averaged.

**Bound shape (written before the run, per formula step "Direction").** E is authoritative in context C iff E is the least updatable available link between C's question and the ground that settles that question, and E's contact with that ground is independent of the frames E is used to check. This is a shape, not a name. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two overt terms (form E, context C) and one hidden deciding term (the ground G that the context's question is about). Authority is a three-place relation A(E, C, G) with G usually unstated. That is why the question looks underdetermined until the anchor is named.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric and relative to the residual. A witness is authoritative for what they witnessed, not for what they inferred; a meter is authoritative for the quantity it is calibrated on, not for the meaning of the result. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Swapping context changes the verdict, so no form carries authority as an intrinsic property.
- **Scale:** individual judgment through laboratory, court, discipline and civilization, recursively. Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." A settled reading becomes an atom at the next level: Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Measurement | instruments, standards, operators, the measured system | readings, uncertainties, calibrations | yes: the non-updating reference. Fig 2 :29 "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." | which reading settles the quantity |
| D2 Experiment | hypotheses, interventions, anomalies, predictions | refusals, bracketed residuals | yes: the outcome that can violate a prediction. Fig 9 :42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides" | which held term must be refused |
| D3 Testimony | speakers, hearers, experts, witnesses, communities | assurance, credibility, independent corroboration | yes, conditionally: human judgment "is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people" (Fig 2 :37) | is the claim about persons or about a quantity |
| D4 Tradition | lineages, apprentices, institutions, inherited priors (w in the Symbols table) | head starts, methods, conventions | no anchor of its own. It borrows one from past contact. Fig 4 :11 "a head start, never a recording" | is the inheritance being ranged or obeyed |

No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain. D4 has no anchor of its own, so it is admitted only as a *derivative* domain: its authority is borrowed from an earlier contact that must be re-ranged. If that re-ranging is refused, D4 collapses into D3's mirror failure or into a convention, not a ground.

**Partition or union:** union of D1 to D4, with overlaps (a court uses measurement and testimony together; a laboratory uses tradition as training). Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." So a court is a compound with its own loop, not a weighted average of laboratory and conversation.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain runs perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3 :4-15). The break that matters here is at "objective": a context must be able to "say what would count as progress here, in its own terms" (Fig 3 :10). Fig 3 :25-26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A context whose objective link is missing cannot use D1 or D2 authority even with instruments present; it falls back on D3 or D4. Ordering is by dependency, never by importance (Fig 3 :20).

## 3. Findings

### F1. Authority attaches to the anchor, not to the form (decisive)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The forms listed in the question are not peers in the eyes of the figure. Measurement and experiment are *named as anchors* in Fig 2 :29; testimony and tradition are not. That asymmetry is the beginning of the answer: measurement and experiment can be authoritative in their own right because they can be non-updating; testimony and tradition can only be authoritative when they are attached to a ground that does not move, or when the question is constitutively about persons (F3).

The historical evidence agrees from a different field. Eran Tal, "Measurement in Science" (SEP, 2020): "Measurement is often considered a hallmark of the scientific enterprise and a privileged source of knowledge relative to qualitative modes of inquiry." That is a *reported* privilege, not a proof of it, which is why the figure's anchor test is needed to decide when it is earned.

**Refuter for F1:** show a form conferring authority in a context where its answer changes when the frames using it push on it, and where no other non-updating link is available. That breaks Fig 2 :29-30.

### F2. Independence, not agreement, is what makes corroboration count

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."**

Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."**

Why testimony wins in some contexts and not others: a witness who saw the event has separate contact; ten witnesses who all read the same report are one witness with ten names. Why tradition fails as a verifier: a lineage handed along a chain of agreement is a mirror chain. Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Nick Leonard, "Epistemological Problems of Testimony" (SEP, 2021): "it is a mistake to treat testimony as a unified source of knowledge; that is, instead of thinking about testimony in general, we should think about the various categories of testimony in particular, e.g., categories differentiated by subject matter." That is the same domain-dependence from social epistemology, and it is why a single global rank of "which evidence form is strongest" is the wrong instrument.

**Refuter for F2:** show two frames with maximally independent contact whose agreement carries no more weight than frames sharing one pool, across the same question. That breaks Fig 2 :31-32.

### F3. The context selects the anchor by what the question is about

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

This is the load-bearing line for the word "contexts" in the question. The context matters not as a social atmosphere but as a *selection of ground*. A court asking "did this witness see the event" is constitutively about a person's experience, so testimony is the right anchor and a blood-pressure reading is only an instrument. A lab asking "what is the half-life" is constitutively about a quantity, so the counter is the anchor and the senior scientist's say-so is only an instrument. Seat Map :171: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

Leonard (2021), section 5: an epistemic authority is "someone who is both your epistemic superior and an expert about the domain in question." Domain is in the definition. Tom Christiano and Stefan Sciaraffa, "Authority" (SEP, 2025): "the duty of the subject is grounded not in the content of the directive itself but in the nature of the source issuing it." That is the source-vs-content distinction that makes testimony a different authority relation from measurement.

**Refuter for F3:** show a context whose question is constitutively about a measurable quantity where human judgment is the legitimate final anchor, or a context constitutively about persons where a counter is the legitimate final anchor, with no loss on the axes the other form measures. That breaks Fig 2 :36-38.

### F4. A measurement is only an anchor while its channel holds

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does."**

Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 13 :16: **"it names what is being measured"** and Fig 13 :26: **"it decides what the measurement would mean"**

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

So measurement authority is conditional, not absolute. A saturated metric has stopped ranging against the ground even though it still returns numbers. The instrument that "cannot comment on the source" is the metamerism case in Isaiah N's *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28): "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." What restores authority is a better instrument: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

The metrology source makes this operational. JCGM VIM3 entry 2.41 (BIPM, online): "property of a measurement result whereby the result can be related to a reference through a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to the measurement uncertainty". Its NOTE 5 adds: "Metrological traceability of a measurement result does not ensure that the measurement uncertainty is adequate for a given purpose or that there is an absence of mistakes." That is exactly the figure's channel-vs-ground distinction in engineering language: traceability is required, and fit-for-purpose is still owed.

Eran Tal (2020) on standardization: "constructing a quantity-concept and standardizing its measurement are co-dependent and iterative tasks", and "Each 'epistemic iteration' in the history of standardization respects existing traditions while at the same time correcting them." So measurement authority is *built*, and it carries tradition inside it in a corrected form. This resolves an apparent tension with F5 rather than creating one.

**Refuter for F4:** show a saturated or untraceable instrument whose readings still settle a question without any independent chain or better instrument. That breaks Fig 10 :24-26 and Fig 4 :30.

### F5. Tradition is legitimate only as a head start, never as the final arbiter

Fig 4 :11: **"a head start, never a recording"**

Fig 4 :22-23: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 2 :47: **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."**

Tradition's genuine authority is real but derivative: it is the compressed record of past contact, handed down so the next search is cheaper. It becomes illegitimate at the moment it is used to decide an empirical outcome, because then a convention has become a verdict. Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

The animal-behaviour source shows why tradition *works* locally without being a ground. Lamon, Neumann, Gruber and Zuberbühler, "Kin-based cultural transmission of tool use in wild chimpanzees" (Science Advances, 2017): tradition is "a distinctive behaviour pattern shared by two or more individuals in a social unit, which persists over time and that new practitioners acquire in part through socially aided learning." They find "the spread of cultural behavior in wild chimpanzees follows a sequential structure of initial proximity-based horizontal transmission followed by kin-based vertical transmission." That is social transmission, not ground ranging. It explains why tradition is authoritative *inside* a lineage and why it fails as a check *on* the lineage.

Isaiah N, *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (2026-11-10): "Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices." And: "Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."

**Refuter for F5:** show a tradition functioning as the final arbiter of an empirical question while still counting as a convention under Fig 10 :47, or a head start that survives re-ranging without correction. That breaks Fig 4 :22-25.

### F6. Experiment's distinctive power is refusal, not confirmation

Fig 5 :14: **"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"**

Fig 5 :53-55 (amendment): **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: "REFUTED a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Experiment outranks testimony in a laboratory not because it is louder but because it can *refuse* a held term. Testimony can report a refusal, but the report is one more frame; the experiment is the contact. Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Under this reading the court's experiment (a test, a measurement, a demonstrated outcome) is the ground, while the expert's interpretation is a frame.

Brian Hepburn and Hanne Andersen, "Scientific Method" (SEP, substantive revision 2026): "Science is risky. If observations showed the predictions from a theory to be wrong, the theory would be refuted." They also note "the effectiveness of any methodological prescription to be so context sensitive as to render it not explanatory on its own", and "Method may therefore be relative to discipline, time or place." The figure's claim is stronger and narrower: what is relative is the *anchor*, not the law.

**Refuter for F6:** show an experiment that cannot in principle refuse any held term yet still confers more authority than an independent witness on the same question. That breaks Fig 5 :14 and Fig 9 :42.

### F7. Prestige and credibility accrual cannot confer authority

Fig 1 :26: **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**; Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Seat Map :283: "A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."

A form of evidence that gains authority because reputable people endorse it is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it produces. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026-11-19): "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." In historical terms this is why "great man" accounts of evidential standards fail: they promote an authority to an anchor.

**Refuter for F7:** show a case where authority tracks a credibility measure generated by the same community that uses it, with no change in contact with the ground. That breaks Fig 1 :27.

### F8. The dependency order explains the context variation

Fig 3 :10: **"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"**

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed. Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."

A context with perception and vocabulary but no objective link cannot deploy D1 or D2 authority. It will accept testimony or tradition because those arrive with a ready-made objective ("this is what our people do"). That is an effect-level explanation of the historical variation, and it predicts where measurement will *not* win: at the uninstrumented middle link. Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

**Refuter for F8:** show a context with a missing objective link that reliably selects the ground-ranged form over the ready-made one. That breaks Fig 3 :20-30.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. Stating what failure looks like surfaces constraints the design does not, and the constraint arrives as a change to the experiment rather than as a note about it."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A form of evidence confers authority in a context where its answer moves when the frames using it push on it, and no other non-updating link is available (breaks Fig 2 :29-30).
2. Two frames with maximally independent contact agree, and their agreement carries no more weight than frames sharing one pool (breaks Fig 2 :31-32).
3. A context whose question is constitutively about a quantity takes human judgment as final anchor, or one constitutively about persons takes a counter as final anchor, with no loss on the other's measuring axes (breaks Fig 2 :36-38).
4. A saturated, untraceable or uncorrected channel still settles a question without an independent chain or a better instrument (breaks Fig 10 :24-26, Fig 4 :30).
5. A tradition decides an empirical outcome while still counting as a convention, or a head start survives re-ranging without correction (breaks Fig 10 :47, Fig 4 :22-25).
6. Authority tracks credibility accrual generated inside the using community with no change in contact (breaks Fig 1 :27).
7. A context with a missing objective link reliably selects the ground-ranged form over the ready-made one (breaks Fig 3 :20-30).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask what the anchor is in each disputed context before ranking evidence forms. If it moves when pushed, it is not an anchor (F1).
2. Test independence, not headcount. Ask whether the witnesses touched the event separately or read the same report (F2).
3. Classify the question before the form. If it is about persons, testimony is the right anchor; if about a quantity, the instrument is; if about a held term, the experiment is (F3, F6).
4. Audit the channel, not just the reading. Traceability, saturation and fit-for-purpose decide whether a measurement still ranges against ground (F4).
5. Treat tradition as a head start to be re-ranged, never as a verdict (F5).
6. Exclude prestige and credibility accrual from every authority calculation (F7).
7. When a context fails at the objective link, instrument *that link* rather than adding more evidence upstream (F8).
8. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the generator, the shape and the refuter, not a list of historical conclusions.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is authority intrinsic to a form?** No. Fig 11 :49 valence is relative to the residual; Fig 2 :36 selects the anchor by the question.
2. **Which forms can be anchors?** Measurement and experiment are named in Fig 2 :29; testimony conditionally in Fig 2 :37; tradition never in its own right.
3. **What makes two witnesses more than one?** Separate contact. Fig 2 :31-32, Fig 8 :48.
4. **What makes a measurement stop working?** Channel decay. Fig 10 :24-26, VIM3 NOTE 5.
5. **What does experiment add?** Refusal of a held term. Fig 5 :14, Formula step 2.
6. **What is tradition for?** A head start, re-ranged. Fig 4 :11, :22-25.
7. **Can a global rank be minted?** No. Fig 9 :54 union, Fig 2 :36 context selection. The sought-for shape was bound before the run and is refusable by the seven tests above.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: **authority follows non-updatable, independent contact with the ground that the context's question is constitutively about, and tradition contributes only as a re-ranged head start.** The global ranking of evidence forms does not survive, nor does any claim that one form is authoritative in all contexts. After re-census, strains: **one live and named**, the single-source status of the Fig 3 dependency fallback and the Fig 5 refusal operator. No strain was smoothed over.
