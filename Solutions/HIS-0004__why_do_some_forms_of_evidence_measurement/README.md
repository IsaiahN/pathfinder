## The residual in effect terms

Authority is not observed to be a property of a form of evidence considered alone. Measurement wins in a laboratory and loses in a court; a witness wins in a court and loses in a physics seminar; tradition wins in an apprenticeship and loses at a calibration bench. The effect to explain is the *context sensitivity* of authority. An answer is a rule that maps a context's ground to the form that may range against it. The residual is stated in effect terms because the theory's own rule says the frame "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32), and because "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28).

## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

Before the run, the sought-for shape is made an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else. Binding: an evidence form E is authoritative in context C iff E is the least updatable available link between C's question and the ground that settles that question, and E's contact with that ground is independent of the frames it is being used to check. This is a shape, not a list of winners. It is falsifiable by a moving anchor, by correlated frames that still corroborate, or by authority tracking prestige rather than contact.
