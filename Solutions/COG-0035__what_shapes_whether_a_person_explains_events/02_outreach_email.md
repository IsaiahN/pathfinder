# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live strain in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 (imported versus composed agent-atom) is stated in the first paragraph and the four refuters in §4 are attached. The output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how people choose explanations, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` on the individual case. Not `grounded`):** a person's explanation of an event is not the product of a stored preference or a culture label. It is selected by four conditions at the event slot: (1) which cause-terms are held in the person's library and reachable by composition, (2) which cause carries positive mass on this particular event, (3) which cause the surrounding habitat makes salient, and (4) whether a reference that does not move when people update on it is available to settle the attribution. The four candidates you named (individual choice, social relationship, institution, spiritual force) are not four exclusive boxes. They are overlapping conditions that can all carry mass for the same event. The framework returns the shape of that selector. It does not return which cause a given person will pick, and I record that as a gap rather than filling it.
>
> What I am explicitly not claiming: that any of the four causes is true or false; that spiritual forces do not exist; that institutions are illegitimate; that there is a universal cultural ranking; or that I have measured anything. The structural point about spiritual forces is only that an agent-shaped cause no instrument registers sits in the same position as an import from a different frame, and imports need their source and their gap stated. Whether that is the right reading is one of the things I want you to check.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before I ran this):**
> 1. A case where three of the four causes are ruled out by construction and the person still shows positive residual on the ruled-out slots, with a single-cause model predicting new events better.
> 2. A cause-term minted with no positive mass on any slot, or outside the person's library with no contact with a different frame and no new atom.
> 3. A standing ranking of the four causes that predicts attributions across unrelated events better than event structure plus available vocabulary.
> 4. A cross-cultural instrument that is shown to move when informants update on it and whose verdict is still counted.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures, verbatim with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this package.
> - The eight essays that ground the reading of "shape": https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - The architecture article with all thirteen figures: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
