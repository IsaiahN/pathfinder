# The Tether's answer: what shapes attribution choice

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which cause a given person selects. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A person faces an event and owes an explanation that closes R at that slot. Four candidate bindings are available: a person-scale agent, a bond between people, a convention over many agents, and an agent-shaped cause not available to the senses. The question asks what selects among them. The answer is a discriminator, not a name. The four candidates form a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55, :57), so more than one can carry positive mass for the same event and one lever cannot clear the intersections.

## 2. The shape: four discriminators

1. **Reachability of the cause-term.** Formula step 3 gates a mint with SUPPORT `|R⁺_s| > 0`, REACHABILITY `φ ∈ closure(Γ)` and NOVELTY `φ ∉ atoms(Γ)`. Fig 5 :33: "The three guards are a product, not a checklist." A person can only offer a cause that is reachable from the atoms their library holds. What differs across upbringings and institutions is the library and its closure, not a dial labelled individualism. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

2. **Positive mass at the slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The selected cause is the one that closes the gap for *this* event, not the one with the highest culture-level mean. Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

3. **Habitat salience.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The habitat is enumerated from contact (Fig 11 :43-44): actors, conditions, relations. What the surrounding coordination demands make salient decides which atom is read as the cause. The world source on relational mobility gives the low-overlap corroboration: coordination needs "make salient the influence of external forces, including powerful others in the environment."

4. **A non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A cross-cultural vignette or rating scale is an instrument, not a ground. If its scoring moves with informants, the comparison has become coherent drift and nobody notices (Fig 2 :34).

These four are the shape. Any candidate answer to the question must pass all four. They are held apart as two currencies would be held apart: reachability and mass decide what can be offered; salience and anchor decide what gets selected and settled.

## 3. The four options in that shape

- **Individual choice** is a gradient over one person's time slot, an ordered composition (Fig 12 :92 on ordered bonds). It is reachable when the person-slot and gradient atoms are held and the event leaves positive mass on the actor.
- **Social relationship** is a network bond between person-slots (Fig 13 :24, "that entities relate, and influence travels"). Under + it is symmetric: swapping the two people yields one term.
- **Institution** is an ordering over many agents composed with a convention. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." A convention is legitimate as shared meaning (Fig 2 :41) and fails when its own agreement is used as the verdict.
- **Spiritual force** is an agent-shaped cause at a slot where no instrument currently registers. Structurally it sits as an import from a closure that differs (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :53, "Residual first, frame second"). That is a claim about where the atom came from, not a claim that it is false. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The live strain is that it might instead be a composition of the held network and gradient atoms, and no instrument in this run discriminates the two.

Over the full shared atom set the four are isomers (Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance"). As the question writes them, with disjoint ingredients, they are a functional group (Fig 12 :123). Because three are ordered and one is either symmetric or an import, their verdicts cannot be averaged.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any of the four causes is true or false. The figures decide reachability, salience and settlement, not metaphysics.
- Not that spiritual forces do not exist. The import reading is structural and carries no ontological verdict.
- Not that institutions are illegitimate, relationships primary, or individual choice the real cause.
- Not that cultures can be ranked by explanatory style, or that a personality trait selects causes. Both are refuted as standing lists by Fig 11 :51.
- Not a derivation of which cause a given person will use. That is a declared `gap` (§6).
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken and no per-event instrument was run.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminator needs an instrument, not a further argument. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." The existing instruments already returning something are the attribution questionnaire, the vignette study, the reaction-time and eye-tracking measures of salience, and the cross-cultural survey. The instrument owed is built from one of those, not stipulated.

1. **Enumerate the habitat per event.** Fig 11 :43-44: list everything in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Record which cause-atoms are available in Γ for that person and event.
2. **Register positive mass per slot.** For each event, which cause has `|R⁺_s| > 0`? A person can hold several; the question is which one closes the gap.
3. **State the sufficiency criterion before collecting attributions.** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." This is the middle link where most studies break.
4. **Range two low-overlap frames against an anchor that does not update.** Fig 2 :31 and :46. A socioecological measure (relational mobility) and a cognitive measure (agent detection) draw on different evidence; their agreement means something only if neither can move the anchor.
5. **Discriminate source from reading.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the green reading. Here the equivalent is an instrument that distinguishes an imported agent-atom from a composed agent-shaped network, and one that distinguishes the cause from the projection on the questionnaire.
6. **Run the pre-registered refuter.** The seven failure conditions in `01` §4 were written before this conclusion. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The four candidate causes are likewise a visible guess supplied by the question, not nature's own list. Declaring them makes the choice visible (Fig 13 :129, "the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage"). The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (*How Intelligence Actually Works*).

**The gap.** No figure line maps a person, a culture, or an event type to one of the four causes. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." To name the cause for a given person would be a binding from the research frame, provenanced as such and still a candidate. Reporting `gap` is a verdict, not a silence.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that culture, personality or belief installs one of four explanations in a person. It says that an explanation is a term minted to close a gap at a specific event slot, and that four conditions decide which term can be offered and settle: whether the cause is reachable from the atoms the person's library holds, whether it carries positive mass at that event, which cause the surrounding habitat makes salient, and whether a reference that does not move when people update on it is available to decide. The four options in the question, individual choice, social relationship, institution and spiritual force, are overlapping conditions, not exclusive boxes; over a shared atom set they differ by arrangement rather than ingredients, so their verdicts must be kept apart. A spiritual attribution is treated structurally as a cause imported from a frame that differs, with provenance and a gap to explain, and this is not a claim that it is false. The framework returns the four-condition selector and the refuters written in advance. It does not name the cause for any particular person, and records that as a gap until a source-discriminating instrument, not an argument, closes it.
