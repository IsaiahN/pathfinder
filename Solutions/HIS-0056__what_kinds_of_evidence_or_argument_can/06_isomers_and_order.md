## Q1: One question or a union?

Fig 9 :54-58 gives the rule. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The residual is a union of four conditions, not a list:

- D1 refuses a term (individual event).
- D2 ranges frames against an anchor (community process).
- D3 keeps the channel to the anchor open (instrument condition).
- D4 selects between rooms (generational condition).

A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections. This is why "give them better evidence" fails as a general answer: it clears D3 in some cases and leaves D1, D2 and D4 standing.

**Falsifier written before the run:** if a single intervention is documented that closes all four conditions across independent cases, with before/after readings by low-overlap coders, the union verdict is false and the question should be re-searched as a partition.

6. **Carry the method, not the recording.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*

## Q3: Are the three historical analogues isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Write the three cases as they are usually told:

- **Continental drift:** two frames (fixist and mobilist), a residual (fit of continents without mechanism), a ground (magnetometer and seismograph readings), a refusal (fixism loses its standing), an import (magnetometry from wartime submarine detection, world source W3).
- **Eddington experiment:** two frames (Newtonian and Einsteinian deflection), a reading (eclipse plates), a persistent quantitative residual, a refusal of the half-deflection value, and a later better instrument (radio astronomy, W4).
- **Planck principle:** cohorts rather than individuals, a residual that survives within a cohort, no refusal within it, and selection between rooms (W5).

As written, they do **not** share all atoms. The Planck case has no within-cohort refusal; the Eddington case has no cohort turnover as its primary mechanism. By Fig 12's definition they are not isomers. They are three members of a **functional group**: something ranges against a ground and something gives way. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.

The stronger reading is available but is a claim, not a fact. If all three are rewritten over the full shared atom set {two frames, residual, ground, refusal, import, room}, then they differ only in which bond mediates which pair, and they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that the cases will trade features rather than simply share them.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a fourth case can be written over the full shared atom set using an atom that none of the three requires (for example a new measurement with no prior failing reading), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. This is the same test pattern the exemplar uses for GR and QM.

## Q4: What is the order of the union, and does it recurse?

Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom at the next level. Each of the three cases, once settled historically, can serve as an atom for a later review of "how science changes". That recursion is what makes a meta-history possible without a new primitive. But Fig 6 :21 warns: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* A review that only re-arranges the same three cases into a neater story adds no atom and moves no wall.

To move the wall at this level, the meta-review needs an import from a closure that differs, for example the empirical studies cited in W5 that contradict the simple generational story, or the incommensurability literature in W2. Those are the atoms that can change the shape of the answer, and they are recorded with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

**Falsifier:** if a meta-review is produced that settles a dispute about belief change purely by re-ordering the same three cases, with no import and no new reading, then the recursion claim here is empty and should be withdrawn.
