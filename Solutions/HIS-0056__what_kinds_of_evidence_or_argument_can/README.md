## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No list of "scientists who changed their minds" treated as corroboration.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A list of famous conversions is a vocabulary, not an anchor.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before a check spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The outside frame must see the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interior.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
