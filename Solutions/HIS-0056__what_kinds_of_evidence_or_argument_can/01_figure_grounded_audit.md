## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

R is not "how do scientists come to agree." Stated as an effect: two or more expert frames each hold a theory (a set of terms that have accrued residuals) and the disagreement persists under more of the same argument. We ask which input reduces the residual on a held term enough that the term is refused and a rival can be composed over the vacancy.

- **Arity.** At least three positions: two contending frames plus one anchor. This is not a two-body negotiation. Fig 2 :13-27 frames the third position as the anchor, and insists it is *"not a third party to the disagreement"* but the thing each frame ranges against separately.
- **Symmetry.** The contending frames are symmetric in the relevant respect: neither can score itself. Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Their agreement is therefore not evidence when they share a pool. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **Scale.** Three distinct scales carry the same effect: the individual mind, the community consensus, and the generational turnover of rooms. These are not one question at three resolutions. They are three domains with different actors, currency and anchor, and their verdicts must be kept separate (section 1).
- **What an answer would look like.** Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching. Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."* Here: a kind of intervention with (a) a ground that does not update on opinion, (b) a refusal of a held term, (c) an import from a closure that differs, and (d) at community scale, selection between rooms rather than negotiation within one. Anything that would not have these features is not the answer, by construction. That binding is the pre-registered refuter in section 4.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale was stated above. One line per domain, giving actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the individual expert frame** | the expert, held theory terms, the sought-for shape held as a preference | residual per term; settlement; refusal | the ground via the channel (instrument, reading) | partition: a held term is either settled, refused, or still accumulating |
| **D2, the community of peers** | contending schools, reviewers, journals, committees | agreement, prestige, standing | should be the ground; the common failure is that agreement becomes the anchor | union of evidence, social and institutional conditions (Fig 9 :54-58), so unbundle before searching |
| **D3, the ground and its channel** | experiment, measurement, instrument, dataset | a reading that does not move when pushed | the ground itself, which does not decay; the channel does | partition: is contact present or lost (Fig 10 :24-29) |
| **D4, the historical or generational chain** | successive cohorts of experts, rooms, habitats | residuals handed along; selection between rooms | the ground across time, via rooms that differ | chain, not partition (Fig 7 :4-6, :24-26) |

**No domain is admitted without an anchor.** D1 has the ground; D2 has the ground when it is working and a mirror when it is not (that is a verdict on D2, not a missing anchor); D3 is the anchor domain; D4 has the ground across rooms. If consensus were treated as its own anchor, D2 would fail the anchor test, because consensus moves when frames push on it. That is the named collapse, not a fifth domain.

**Partition or union.** D1 and D3 are partitions (a term is refused or not; contact is present or lost). D2 is a union: a controversy persisted after key facts were settled (question 59 in the same section) because more than one condition binds. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections. D4 is a chain whose progress per handoff depends on two factors (Fig 7 :37-39).

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** The chain is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Applied here: the residual must be perceived and described in effect terms (D3 to D1 vocabulary) before an objective ("which term do we refuse") can be set; refusal precedes mint (Formula step 2); settlement precedes composition over a term (Formula step 5); only then does community or generational selection have something to select. A reading taken below the break, for example measuring consensus before asking what residual it answered, is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

## 3. Findings

### F1: More of the same argument inside the committed frame cannot change the mind (decisive against one family of answers)

Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

An expert committed to a theory holds a frame that both generates its own supporting quantities (papers, citations, successful predictions inside the paradigm) and grades them. Fig 1 rules that out as a metric. So "a better argument in the same vocabulary" is not a mind changer at D1; it is the frame congratulating itself. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essay says the same in plain terms: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."*

**Refuter for F1:** a documented case where a committed expert changed and held the change solely because of an internal argument, with no new reading, no imported term and no refusal, and where a low-overlap checker confirms that the held terms did not change their predictions. If such a case exists, F1 is false.

### F2: What changes a held mind is a refusal, not an accumulation (decisive)

Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*
Fig 5 :59: *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"*
Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
Fig 6 :42: *"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."*

The fifth bin at ROUTE sorts the residual by what is wrong, not by what is missing. A mind does not change by stacking anomalies; it changes when a term it holds is refused. The persistent-and-flat residual tells the expert which term to point at (the same shrinking test the selector runs, read with the opposite sign). Negation is unary and is not one of the seven bonds, so a vocabulary without it can record that a claim stopped paying but cannot carry the correction.

This is also why "evidence" and "argument" are not two kinds of input. They are distinguished by their effect on a held term: evidence that merely accumulates is in the first four bins; an argument that carries a refusal is in the fifth. The kind that changes a mind is the fifth.

**Refuter for F2:** a case where a held term's residual persisted and did not trend down, no refusal was recorded, and the expert's theory was subsequently unmodified, yet the episode is claimed as genuine mind change by independent low-overlap coders. If that holds, F2 is false.

### F3: The trigger is a reading at an anchor the frame cannot move, reached through an instrument already returning something

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*
Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

So the evidence that changes a mind is a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a patient outcome, a reading. But a new instrument is not specified from a description; it is improved from a worse instrument already returning something that fails to resolve. This is the bridge to the history-of-science record: in the continental drift case, the argument was available for decades and did not move the community; what moved it was magnetometers and seismographs returning zebra stripes and ridge structure (world source W3). In the Eddington case, the eclipse plates were a reading; the dispute was not definitively settled until radio astronomy in the late 1960s (world source W4). The Hard Problem is About Source essay puts the general point: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

**Refuter for F3:** a case where a dispute between committed experts was settled by an instrument that had never returned any reading at all in that domain, built from a description alone, with no prior failing reading to sharpen. That would break Fig 6 :34-35. Also: a case where the supposed anchor moved when a frame pushed on it, which would break Fig 2 :29.

### F4: A mind moves on import from a frame whose closure differs, and only on that (single-source for the operator, triangulated with Fig 13)

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
Fig 13 :38-39: *"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything."*

Composition inside a closure is idempotent; it cannot add an atom. So if the term that would settle a dispute is not in the expert's closure, no amount of internal argument reaches it. The term arrives only by import from a frame whose closure differs, and import must be debited against that frame's independence. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* and Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* give the order: characterise the residual in effect terms, then find the frame. Otherwise any field picked will appear to fit (the apophenia the exemplar names in its own audit).

**Refuter for F4:** a case where a committed expert acquired a genuinely new atom by internal composition alone, with no contact with a differing closure and no new instrument, verified by independent coders. That would break Fig 6 :21 and :23.

### F5: Consensus is not mind change, and selecting for agreement destroys the only useful signal

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*
Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
Fig 7 :13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

D2 verdict: agreement among peers is a convention when it is about shared meaning and a mirror when it is about the world. A consensus that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention and has become an ungrounded verdict. What does work at D2 is triangulation: frames that draw on little common evidence, each ranging against the same unmovable anchor. Fig 8 :56 notes that the triangulation claim *"fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward"*, so this is a `specified` structural claim, not a measured law of committees.

The How Intelligence Actually Works essay makes the same point about rooms: *"the room session only produces an outcome that you agree on, having come from diverse frames with different vantage points of the problem. It does not mean that the output was objective truth."*

**Refuter for F5:** a documented case where a community of experts sharing one evidence pool reached a novel, later-ground-confirmed conclusion by agreement alone, with no low-overlap frame and no new reading, and where the conclusion was not already implicit in the shared pool. If that holds, the mirror verdict is too strong.

### F6: At generational scale the mind often does not change; the room changes (single-source for the chain mechanism in the figures)

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
Fig 7 :42: *"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."*
Fig 10 :61: *"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*
Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

D4 verdict: the standing of a senior expert is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on, so an interest in the reading is structurally present. The figures do not expect that to be argued away; they require it to be declared (Fig 10 :60). Where it cannot be removed, the change occurs by selection between cohorts, not by conversion within one. Planck's principle is the world-facing description: *"scientific change does not occur because individual scientists change their mind, but rather that successive generations of scientists have different views"* (world source W5). The same source records an empirical rebuttal, so this is not presented as settled fact: age was a minor factor in the acceptance of evolution, and a study of plate tectonics found older scientists adopted it sooner. That rebuttal is a strain, recorded in section 8, not a reason to flip the figure verdict.

**Refuter for F6:** a well-sampled historical case in which a committed cohort changed its collective reading without turnover, without a new instrument, and without an imported atom, with change measured before and after by low-overlap coders. The Planck/room-chain reading would be weakened for that case.

### F7: The answer is a shape and a route, not a catalogue of names

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

So the deliverable is the form of the intervention, not a list of famous cases and not a claim to have identified the one mechanism history obeyed. The three historical analogues in `06_isomers_and_order.md` are retrodictions that fit the shape, not results produced by it. Fig 8 :56: *"the triangulation claim fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward"* applies here too.

**Refuter for F7:** a proposed answer that names a single kind of evidence as sufficient in all four domains and survives a check against each domain's anchor. The shape claim would then be too weak to be worth sending.

### F8: A genuine conflict exists with incommensurability; it is a strain at a different scale, not a flip

World source W2 (SEP, Incommensurability) records Kuhn's claim that *"nature does not guide us to some uniquely correct theory"* and that there is *"no neutral algorithm for theory choice, no systematic decision procedure which, properly applied, must lead each individual in the group to the same decision."* Read literally at D1, that conflicts with the figure verdict that the ground settles.

**Re-census, not a flip.** The conflict dissolves when the scales are separated. At D1 and D3 the figures say an anchor that does not update settles a term; at D4 the "ground vote" (Fig 7 :35) is distributed across rooms that differ, and a single cohort's inability to compare does not remove the anchor. What the world literature calls a gestalt switch or conversion is, in figure terms, the next room being selected because it differs from the last and matches the residual (Fig 7 :36). Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The figures can accommodate the observation; they do not assert that any particular individual will convert. This remains a live strain and is named in section 8 and in `11_audit.md`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim in section 0 is refuted if any of the following holds, with independent low-overlap coders confirming the case:

1. A committed expert changed and held the change solely through internal argument, with no new reading, no import from a differing closure and no refused term (breaks F1 and F2).
2. A dispute was settled by an instrument that had never returned a failing reading in that domain, built from a description alone (breaks F3 and Fig 6 :34-35).
3. A community sharing one evidence pool reached a novel, later-ground-confirmed conclusion by agreement alone, with no low-overlap frame (breaks F5).
4. A supposed anchor moved when a frame pushed on it (breaks Fig 2 :29).
5. A full cohort changed its collective reading without turnover, new instrument or imported atom (weakens F6 for that case).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is revised. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. Note the double standard this avoids: an anecdote of a famous conversion is not a refuter, because it has no coder independence and no before/after reading.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer "what changes minds" with a list of persuasive arguments. The figures put internal argument in the frame-internal bucket (F1).
2. Lead with three features, not one: an unmovable anchor, a refusal of a held term, and an import from a differing closure (F2, F3, F4). Keep the community and generational verdicts separate (F5, F6).
3. Describe the residual in effect terms before naming any historical case or field (Fig 8 :53, Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise the case picked will seem to fit.
4. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The packet carries the shape and the refuter; the links come only after a verdict is recorded.
5. Declare the stake. The question is about experts whose standing survives the outcome (Fig 10 :59, :61). An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged.
6. Treat Kuhnian incommensurability as a scale strain, recorded, not as a reason to stop (F8).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :55 unbundles it into D1, D2, D3 and D4. A single lever clears one condition and leaves the intersections. Union, not partition.
2. **Which link is currently missing instruments?** The vocabulary/objective link in Fig 3: what would count as progress is rarely stated before the search. Hence the pre-registered refuter.
3. **What operator refuses a held term?** Negation, the unary eighth operator (Operators :58). Not one of the seven bonds, so a list of ingredients cannot carry it.
4. **Which term is wrong?** The one with a persistent, non-trending residual (Fig 5 :59). This is enumeration from what the frame already records, read with the opposite sign.
5. **Where does the new atom come from?** A frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence (Fig 8).
6. **What is settled?** A reading at an anchor that does not update (Fig 2 :29). No such reading is taken in this derivation, so status is `candidate`, not `grounded`.
7. **What is the direction of the question?** Bound as an ordinary term before the run: the four features in section 0. Fig 5 :65. Without this, a frame would aim the refusal at the outcome it did not want rather than at the belief it holds.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives with all four features attached: an unmovable ground, a refusal of a persistent-and-flat term, an import from a low-overlap closure, and at community scale selection between rooms rather than agreement within one. Internal argument, consensus and prestige are not mind changers at any of the four domains. What fails after re-census is the ambition to name one mechanism: the four domains have separate verdicts and are never averaged. Strains after re-census: one live scale strain (Kuhn/Feyerabend incommensurability at D4, F8) and one empirical strain (the rebuttal to Planck's principle in W5). Both are recorded in `11_audit.md`. No figure quote was found to have drifted.
