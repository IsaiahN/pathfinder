# Expert verification packet: the Tether on belief change in science

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on three scale predictions, not `grounded`.** No before/after reading with independent coders is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"What changes the mind of an expert committed to a competing theory" is a union of four conditions, not one question. A change occurs when (1) a reading arrives at an anchor that does not update on what any frame thinks, (2) a held term whose residual persists and does not trend down is refused rather than merely noted, (3) the term that does the work is imported from a frame whose closure differs, described first in effect terms, and (4) at community scale the room changes rather than the person inside it, because standing is a stake that survives the outcome. More argument inside the committed frame, consensus within a shared evidence pool, and prestige are not mind changers at any of the four domains. The three historical cases usually cited are retrodictions that fit the shape, not results it produced.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 5

- **Statement:** internal argument and accumulated anomalies do not change a committed mind; a refusal of a held term does.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27, *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 5 :53, *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."* Operators table :58, *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **Refuter:** a documented case in which a committed expert changed and held the change solely through internal argument, with no new reading, no imported term and no refused term, confirmed by low-overlap coders. Also: a case where a held term's residual persisted and did not trend down, no refusal was recorded, and the theory was unmodified, yet the episode is counted as mind change.

### C2: world-facing; checkable against the three historical cases

- **Statement:** in the cases usually cited, what did the work was a reading at a new or better instrument and an import from a different closure, not more argument in the original vocabulary. The cases are a functional group as usually told, and a real isomer claim only when written over a full shared atom set.
- **What to check:** continental drift was rejected for decades for want of a mechanism and accepted after magnetometry and seismology (world source W3). The 1919 eclipse produced a reading, but the quantitative dispute was settled only by later radio astronomy (W4). Planck's principle describes cohort turnover, with published empirical rebuttals (W5). Are those characterisations faithful, and is the "functional group, not isomers as written" verdict right under Fig 12?
- **Figure line governing the check:** Fig 6 :34-35, *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 8 :56, *"the triangulation claim fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward."*
- **Refuter:** a dispute settled by an instrument that had never returned a failing reading in that domain and was built from a description alone; or a fourth case written over the full shared atom set that needs an atom none of the three requires, which kills the isomer reading and leaves the functional-group reading standing.

### C3: framework-internal derivation at community scale

- **Statement:** consensus within one evidence pool is not a verdict; selection between rooms is where the ground votes. Senior standing is a structural stake and must be declared, not argued away.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 10 :61, *"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."*
- **Refuter:** a community sharing one evidence pool that reached a novel, later-ground-confirmed conclusion by agreement alone, with no low-overlap frame and no new reading; or a full cohort that changed its collective reading without turnover, new instrument or imported atom, measured before and after by independent coders. Also: a supposed anchor that moved when a frame pushed on it, breaking Fig 2 :29.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that there is one mechanism behind every conversion in the history of science.
- Not that continental drift, the 1919 eclipse or Planck's principle were produced by this framework. They fit the shape after the fact.
- Not that a single measurement always settles a dispute, or that readings are theory-free.
- Not that Planck's principle is settled history; W5 contains published criticism.
- Not that incommensurability is false. It is a live scale strain, recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F8.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement.
- Not a claim that any named expert should have changed their mind. The claim is about the form of the intervention.

## 5. What would settle it

A before/after reading with independent, low-overlap coders, registered before cases are selected. The five conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are the falsifiers. The decisive practical question for a checker: in a case you know well, did the *term held* change, or did only the cohort, the vocabulary or the headline change? Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :29 and :40, Fig 5 :53, Fig 7 :35 and Fig 10 :61, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of one of the three cases. Is the characterisation faithful? Name one reading that was actually new at the time.
3. Name one case you know where a committed expert changed without any of the four features, or say why you cannot.
4. Identify which of the five refuters you would try first, and what result would make you withdraw the corresponding feature.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
