# The Tether's answer: what changes the mind of a committed expert

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the three scale predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

"Evidence or argument" is a bundled phrase, not a kind. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The question splits into four domains with separate verdicts: the individual mind (D1), the community of peers (D2), the ground and its channel (D3), and the generational chain (D4). Their verdicts are kept apart and never averaged.

## 2. The shape: four features, kept separate

1. **A reading at an anchor that does not update.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Evidence that changes a mind is a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a patient outcome, an instrument reading. Argument generated inside the committed frame cannot score that frame (Fig 1 :27). No new instrument is designed from a description; it is sharpened from one already returning something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :34-35).

2. **A refusal of a held term, not an accumulation.** Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."* Fig 5 :59: *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at."* Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* This is the part of the question that "argument" can do, provided the argument carries a refusal. An anomaly that is merely noted changes nothing.

3. **An import from a closure that differs.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* The residual is described in effect terms first (Fig 8 :53, Fig 9 :27-28), then the frame is sought. The import carries a source gate and a shadow test and is debited against the importing frame's independence (Fig 2 :42). That is triangulation, not negotiation.

4. **At community scale, selection between rooms.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Consensus that shares one evidence pool is a mirror (Fig 2 :40). Senior standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on (Fig 10 :61), so where it cannot be removed, the room changes rather than the person.

**What does not change a mind, at any of the four domains:** more of the same argument in the same vocabulary; agreement selected for receptiveness; prestige or the authority of the reviewer; a consensus that has stopped being a convention and started deciding an outcome (Fig 10 :47).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that there is one mechanism. Four domains have four verdicts; they are not averaged.
- Not that any particular historical episode is a result of the framework. Continental drift, the Eddington experiment and Planck's principle are retrodictions that fit the shape (Fig 8 :56), not derivations it produced.
- Not that a single measurement always settles a dispute. The Eddington case needed a later, better instrument for the quantitative value; readings can be theory-laden. That is why feature 1 requires an anchor that does not move when pushed.
- Not that Planck's principle is settled history. World source W5 includes published empirical rebuttals.
- Not that incommensurability is false. It is a real strain at D1 and is absorbed only by moving to the D4 scale (01 F8).
- Not `grounded`. No before/after reading with independent coders was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it, and what could reverse it

A ground reading, not a further argument: a historical study with independent, low-overlap coders measuring which interventions precede a recorded change of held terms, with the four features registered before the cases are selected. The five conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are the falsifiers. Specifically:

1. Did the change occur through internal argument alone, with no new reading, no import and no refused term?
2. Was there a prior failing reading for the instrument that settled it?
3. Did the community share one evidence pool, or did low-overlap frames range separately?
4. Did the anchor move when a frame pushed on it?
5. At cohort scale, did the room change or did the people?

A reversal would require one of those to come back against the corresponding feature, or a case where a single lever closed all four conditions (breaking the union verdict in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1). Agreement by itself is not such a reading. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The shape is as narrow as the available instruments allow: four features, five refuters, separate domain verdicts, one live scale strain. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity. A world reading with independent coders could move this; this derivation cannot.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a list of persuasive arguments, because a committed expert's own frame produces the quantities it would use to score itself. It gives a shape with four features. A mind changes when a reading arrives at an anchor that does not move when pushed, when a term with a persistent, non-trending residual is refused rather than merely noted, when the term that does the work is imported from a frame whose closure differs, and, at community scale, when the room changes rather than the person inside it, because standing is a stake that survives the outcome. Consensus within one evidence pool, prestige and more argument in the same vocabulary are not mind changers; they are mirrors. The three cases usually cited (continental drift, the 1919 eclipse, Planck's principle) are retrodictions that fit the shape, not results it produced. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the reading that would close it. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
