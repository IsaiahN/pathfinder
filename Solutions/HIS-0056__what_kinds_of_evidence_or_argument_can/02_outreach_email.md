# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 have been read and the refuter is still standing. If the shape claim fails the refuter, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One check on a claim about what changes experts' minds

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in the history and philosophy of science: what kind of evidence or argument actually changes the mind of an expert committed to a competing theory. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on three scale predictions, not `grounded`):** the shape has four features, not one.
>
> 1. A reading at an anchor that does not update when a frame pushes on it: a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a patient outcome. Argument inside the committed frame cannot score that frame.
> 2. A refusal of a held term whose residual persists and does not trend down. An accumulation of anomalies is not the same thing as changing a mind; the correction has to be carried by a refusal.
> 3. An import from a frame whose closure differs, described first in effect terms. A frame cannot reach what its own closure lacks by composing harder.
> 4. At community scale, selection between rooms rather than agreement within a room. Consensus that shares one evidence pool is a mirror, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before running anything:
> 1. a committed expert who changed and held the change solely through internal argument, with no new reading, no imported term and no refused term;
> 2. a dispute settled by an instrument that had never returned a failing reading in that domain, built from a description alone;
> 3. a community sharing one evidence pool that reached a novel, later confirmed conclusion by agreement alone;
> 4. a supposed anchor that moved when a frame pushed on it;
> 5. a whole cohort that changed its collective reading without turnover, a new instrument or an imported atom.
>
> If any one holds under independent coding, I withdraw the corresponding feature. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is one mechanism behind every conversion in the history of science; that any particular historical episode proves the shape; that the three cases I looked at (continental drift, the 1919 eclipse, Planck's principle) were generated by this framework rather than fitted to it. They are retrodictions. I am also not claiming that a room's consensus is evidence, or that a well known reviewer's endorsement settles anything. Prestige can fund a test; it cannot decide one.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would you count as a failure of feature 2 or feature 4 in a case you know well? I will record your answer before showing you any framework material, and I will not treat later agreement as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Figures README (usage rule): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Local verbatim figure text used for every `Fig N :line` citation in this package: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim and its five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the answer before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Stage 3 sends a generator, which Fig 4 permits. Each stage still needs a ground per problem.
