## Wider literature that bears on this question

1. **Kuhn on crisis and conversion.** The SEP Scientific Revolutions entry states that persistent anomalies erode confidence and that Kuhn described a gestalt switch or conversion. This corroborates the figure-verdict at D4 (selection between rooms) but is a strain at D1 (no neutral algorithm), handled in `01` F8.
2. **Incommensurability and crucial experiments.** The SEP Incommensurability entry records Feyerabend's test-model, which is a real challenge to the claim that a single anchor can range against two disjoint vocabularies. The figures answer with triangulation across low-overlap frames (Fig 8), not with a denial of the problem.
3. **Continental drift.** A decades-long case in which more argument failed and new instrumentation succeeded. It is a retrodiction that fits feature 3, not a result produced by the framework (Fig 8 :56).
4. **Eddington experiment.** A case where a reading changed minds and a better instrument was needed to settle the numbers. It fits feature 1 and also warns that a single reading can be theory-laden, which is why F3 requires an anchor that does not move when pushed.
5. **Planck's principle.** A case for feature 4, with its own published rebuttal. The rebuttal is kept in view so the case is not over-read.

Agreement among these sources is not two witnesses where they draw on one evidence pool. W3 and W4 both discuss acceptance of new theories in geology and physics via instruments, but they are different cases with different records, so they are low overlap on the specific claim. W1 and W2 are both SEP entries on Kuhn and share an authorial and editorial pool, so they count as one witness for the incommensurability strain.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the whole framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and the refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :42** — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a wrong account of how scientific disagreement resolves, so independent checkers are worth more than a larger audience.
- **Fig 7 :13** — *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* A chain of agreeing historians adds no anchor.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

**Nothing is sent by this agent.** The user hosts and sends. Prefer an opt-in download or submission page over an attachment pushed to a list.
