# Outreach draft: check the shape before deciding about the molecule

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the stage 1 text below. Do not attach the framework, the source links or `07_the_tether_answer.md` until a verdict on stage 1 has been recorded. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

---

**Subject:** Would you count this as a failure? One shape claim about the first dual-role molecule

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Modern life uses two different molecular classes for two different jobs: DNA stores heritable information, and proteins catalyze reactions. Each depends on the other. The origin question asks whether one polymer could have done both, and whether it was first. Those are three separate conditions, not one. Showing that RNA catalyzes something clears only the catalysis condition. It leaves storage and priority standing.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figures I work from keep six primitive terms in two kinds. One kind carries ordering, another carries directedness, and *"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction"* (Fig 13 :36). A composition across the two kinds is legal; a composition within one kind is not (Fig 13 :74). So a single molecule that both stores an ordered sequence and catalyzes a directed transformation is structurally permitted. It is the kind of thing a first molecule would have to be. The framework also defines a catalyst as *"a part that survives its own use"* (Fig 12 :119) and a cycle as one where *"the output re-enters as input"* (Fig 12 :121). Those two definitions are what I would want checked against real chemistry.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that the framework names RNA. Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Naming a molecule is a binding from the chemistry frame, and I would record it as such. I am not claiming RNA was first. No figure line selects a substrate's chronological priority, and I report that as a gap, not as silence. I am not claiming the laboratory self-replicating systems reconstruct the historical route. A test harness is a substituted habitat, and *"a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability"* (Fig 11 :70).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these down before writing this email.**
> 1. Show that storing an ordered sequence and catalyzing a directed reaction are the same physical capacity, so the two conditions collapse into one.
> 2. Show that a catalyst in the sense of Fig 12 :119 is spent by its own use and still has to be counted as a catalyst.
> 3. Show that a first system needs a maintainer choosing which molecule wins, rather than selection running (Fig 11 :36).
> 4. Derive one of the six primitive terms from the other five.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want the method:** it is in the public figures link I will send on request. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
