## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are RNA, DNA and proteins isomers under Fig 12?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

The figure's own example is silver fulminate and silver cyanate: identical composition, different properties. Applied strictly, an isomer claim requires the same molecular formula. RNA, DNA and polypeptides have different elemental formulas and different backbones, so they are **not** strict isomers in that sense. That reading is retracted and preserved in `01` §6.

The weaker, defensible claim: over the fixed atom vocabulary {C, H, O, N, P, S}, different bond topology yields different substances with the difference carried by the arrangement, not by the inventory. Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :92-93 explains the price: under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges.

So RNA versus DNA is an **arrangement claim over a shared atom vocabulary**, with the qualifier that it is not the figure's strict molecular isomerism. Mode `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 12), with the strain named.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the difference between two candidate first polymers is shown to be only a difference in atom inventory (a new element or a new functional atom not shared by the other), the same-atom arrangement claim fails and the two are simply different atom sets, not arrangements of one.

### Q2 — Where do catalyst and cycle sit in the structure list?

Fig 12 :116-124 names four structures a compositional system exhibits. Two are directly on point:

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A ribozyme is a part that survives its own use. A self-replicating RNA is a cycle whose output is its own input as template. These are not analogies imported from chemistry into the figures; they are two of the figure's own named structures, and the chemistry is an instance of them. Status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 definitions plus Lincoln and Joyce 2009 at the ground), with the caveat that a lab instance is a harness (Fig 11 :70).

The other two structures also bear. Fig 12 :117 (isomer) is handled in Q1. Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The `state → structure → state` motif is a functional group that RNA, DNA and protein-containing systems all share; RNA, PNA and peptide-nucleic chimeras are members of it. That is why finding the motif does not identify the member.

### Q3 — What is the order of a first replicator relative to the lineage it starts?

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"** (the extract continues *"for the same reason Figure 4 says there is no top"*).

Order by dependency:

1. A candidate polymer must be **settled** at the ground before anything composes over it (Formula step 5: *"SETTLE — the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*).
2. Once settled, it becomes an **atom** at the next level. Darwinian evolution composes over it (Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*).
3. A second-order composition then appears: replication with variation and selection. This needs no new primitive; it is the recursion Fig 12 names.

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** Effort spent on claims about which substrate was first (P) arrives at a step whose input, a settled dual-role replicator, is still missing (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).

**Falsifier:** show a lineage composing over a molecule that was never settled at the ground, and the dependency order of this derivation fails.

### Q4 — What bond carries the priority claim?

Operators table :30-31 defines `→` (sequence) as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Priority is therefore an ordered claim, and cannot be settled by a symmetric bond. Fig 12 :23 states the symmetric case: *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term and nothing consults the bond to find that out"*.

Fig 13 :4: *"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."* This is **not** a claim about temporal order among chemical substrates. It governs rank among the six primitives. Reading it as "no substrate came first" is a strain, dissolved by re-census in `01` §7 S4. The honest position remains `gap`: the bond type of P is `→`, and no figure returns its operand.

**Falsifier:** derive a symmetric bond that decides which of two substrates came first, or show that the six-term set does encode chronological priority.

### Q5 — Analogues and where they are refused

Three mappings were considered and two were refused:

| analogue | status | reason |
|---|---|---|
| RNA is a catalyst that survives its own use (Fig 12 :119) | accepted as an instance | the definition names exactly this, and the world source demonstrates it |
| DNA is "stored RNA" (Bernhardt 2012: from a biosynthetic view DNA is a modified RNA) | accepted as a binding, not a derivation | provenance is visible; it does not select which came first |
| The ribosome as evidence that RNA came first | **refused as a priority witness** | a molecular fossil is a reading whose source is not uniquely determined; metamerism, `05` §4. It corroborates an early RNA world, not P. |

The refusal follows Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

## CONCLUSION

Over the fixed framework atom set, the two capacities are cross-kind and compose legally in one molecule (Fig 13 :74). Over the fixed chemical atom vocabulary, RNA, DNA and proteins are arrangements rather than strict isomers, with the difference carried by bond topology (Fig 12 :94, :117). A first replicator is a catalyst that survives its use and a cycle whose output re-enters as input (Fig 12 :119, :121); once settled it becomes an atom for evolution at the next level (Fig 12 :104). The dependency order runs settlement before composition and composition before priority (Fig 3 :20, Fig 11 :36). The priority bond is `→` and its operand is not returned by any figure, so P remains a `gap`.

This rescues what is defensible without claiming that the figures name RNA or reconstruct history. The strict isomer reading is retracted and preserved in `01` §6.
