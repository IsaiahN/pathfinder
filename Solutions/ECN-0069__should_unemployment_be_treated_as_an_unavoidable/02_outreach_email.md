**Subject:** One checkable claim about the unavoidable-versus-policy-failure question

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from unemployment data and policy evaluation. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`general` on the shape, `specified` on each domain).** A frictionless clearing prediction says everyone willing to work at the prevailing wage has a job. What we observe is persistent positive unemployment in some groups and regions, plus large cyclical swings. The signal is in the slots, not in the headline rate: the long-term unemployed, the young, the low-skilled, the places with vacancies and joblessness side by side. Averaging them into one number is how the signal disappears.
>
> **The claim I want checked.** The question as posed, unavoidable feature versus policy failure, is a bundled question. It is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question. A single lever clears the part where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So neither option survives as stated. What survives is a split: a matching-and-friction domain where the residual is unavoidable *relative to the present closure* but the wall moves when an institution is imported; a demand domain that policy instruments reach directly; and a skills-and-institutions domain whose reach depends on which lever and which group. The share that is reducible is not something the derivation returns. That is a gap owed to measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a single natural rate which is the truth and all other readings are noise. The natural rate is unobservable and model-dependent. The anchor I am pointing at is the measured flow between employment and unemployment, the vacancy series, and duration by group. I am also not claiming that "policy can fix it" in general. Two credible bodies of evidence disagree about the average effect of active labour market programmes, and I take that disagreement as evidence for the union split rather than against it. Nothing here is measured by me, so nothing here is `grounded`.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this run):**
> 1. Unemployment is one well-formed question whose disagreements shrink with effort, rather than a union of overlapping conditions.
> 2. The measured flows move when a researcher changes their mind but do not move when a policy instrument changes.
> 3. One lever clears all the conditions, including the intersections, consistently across countries.
> 4. The frictional residual trends down under more search inside an unchanged matching frame, or no import has ever shifted a Beveridge curve.
> 5. A source-discriminating measurement returns the reducible share per slot with credible identification, and it is far from the shape above.
>
> If any holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
