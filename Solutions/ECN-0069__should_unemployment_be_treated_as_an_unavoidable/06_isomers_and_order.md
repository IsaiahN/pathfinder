# Are the three conditions isomers, and what order is the policy chain?

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The union split in `01` identifies frictional, structural and cyclical unemployment as overlapping conditions. Two further questions are owed: (1) are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or only members of a functional group, and (2) in what **dependency order** does a policy lever have to be chosen before its effect can be read? A third, smaller question: what does the queue identity `L = lambda W` license, and what does it not?

## Q1 - Functional group or isomer?

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

As the field usually writes them, the three conditions share one substructure, a flow imbalance between job seekers and vacancies, with different qualifiers:

| condition | qualifier | shared substructure |
|---|---|---|
| frictional | time and information needed to find a match | flow imbalance |
| structural | skills or location do not match the vacancies | flow imbalance |
| cyclical | aggregate demand too low to post enough vacancies | flow imbalance |

By Fig 12 :124 this is a **functional group**: the group gives the kind, the qualifier gives the member. That is the conservative reading and it is the one this package uses in its headline verdict.

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

The stronger claim is available if all three are written over the full shared atom set `{workers, vacancies, wages, time, information, institutions}` and differ only in which bond joins which atoms:

- frictional: `worker - information` composed with `vacancy -> time` (sequence; a match needs prior contact)
- structural: `worker ⋛ vacancy` on the skill or location comparison
- cyclical: `wages ⇒ vacancies` (production; a vacancy did not exist before demand fired)

Same atoms, different bond, different observable behaviour. That is a real isomer claim under Fig 12, with one important caveat: the mapping of economic quantities onto figure atoms is a `specified` binding, not a derivation from the six-term substrate. The figures license the *definition* of isomer; the world licenses the *attribution*.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any condition requires an atom the others do not, for example if cyclical unemployment can be written with no information term while frictional cannot, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the F8 refuter in `01`.

**Verdict:** functional group = `triangulated` (Fig 12 :124 plus the CRS decomposition and Diamond's matching-function distinction). Isomer = `single-source` for the figure definition, `specified` and unmeasured for the mapping.

## Q2 - What order is the policy chain?

Fig 3 orders by dependency, not importance: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry.

| Fig 3 link | in this question | status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | measure the residual per slot: duration, job-finding rate, vacancy rate by group and region | instrument exists (flows, survey); use it |
| 2 vocabulary | terms for what a slot affords: matchable, trainable, movable, demand-sensitive | partly present; "discouraged worker" and "underemployment" are recent additions |
| 3 objective | what counts as progress in this slot: a shorter duration? a lower rate? a better match? | **the uninstrumented link** |
| 4 planning | choose a lever given the objective: information, training, mobility, demand support | cannot be diagnosed until link 3 is stated |
| 5 learn and carry | verify and carry the result to the next slot | evaluation literature exists but is endogeneity-prone |

Fig 3 :25-27: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* And: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Effort spent on lever selection (link 4) arrives at a step that cannot use it if the objective (link 3) is not stated. This is why the question "unavoidable or policy failure?" cannot be answered before the question "what would count as success, in which slot?": the second is link 3 and the first is link 4.

Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. A goal here is a `⋛` between a measured duration and a declared threshold. Until the threshold is written down, "substantially reduced" is a direction, not a goal. `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says the remedy is to bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term. Writing the threshold is exactly that.

**Falsifier:** if a field-wide, slot-specific measure of successful labour-market progress exists and is used before lever selection, the "uninstrumented middle link" finding is withdrawn.

## Q3 - What the queue identity licenses

Little's law, `L = lambda W`, proved by John Little in 1961 and verified this session: the long-term average number of items in a stationary system equals the effective arrival rate times the average time an item spends in the system. Applied to unemployment, the stock of unemployed people equals the inflow rate times the average duration.

The Fed's FEDS Note derives the same relation in labour vocabulary: *"U = s/(s+f)"*, where `s` is the separation rate and `f` the job-finding rate, and notes that *"Because flows into and out of unemployment are quite high, the actual unemployment rate converges to the steady-state unemployment rate quickly"*.

**What this licenses:** a positive unemployment stock at a steady state is not by itself evidence of failure. It is the arithmetic of flows with finite search time. That is the formal content of "unavoidable relative to the closure," and it is a low-overlap corroboration from mathematics and operations research.

**What it does not license:** it does not say the level is desirable, it does not name which flow to change, and it does not return a reducible share. Little's law is an identity, not a policy rule. The Wikipedia article notes the stationarity requirement and that in finite intervals the direct application needs care. Treating the identity as a verdict would be a filter handing you a verdict (Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**).

## Q4 - The cycle and the catalyst

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Employment separates into unemployment, unemployment finds work, work separates again. The loop closes. This is why Fig 11 :63 says a managed ecosystem is *"permanent work rather than a solved state"*: there is no terminal settlement of the flow. Option A's "unavoidable" is partly a recognition of this cycle; Option B's "policy failure" is a recognition that the parameters of the cycle can be changed. Both are right about a different part of the machine, which is why neither is right as an exhaustive answer.

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A job-search service, a vacancy database or a training provider is a catalyst in this sense: it acts on the matching rate and is not consumed by the match. A one-off cash payment is spent. The two look similar in a budget line and are different in the loop, and most evaluation datasets do not record which a programme is. That is a structural reason for the mixed findings in the ALMP literature and a concrete thing an outside reader can check.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are conservatively a **functional group** with a testable stronger isomer reading over a shared atom set. The policy chain breaks at its middle link: the objective is not instrumented, so lever selection is attempted before success is defined. Little's law corroborates the arithmetic of a persistent stock without handing a verdict. The cycle makes management permanent; the catalyst distinction explains why some interventions survive use and others do not. None of this requires a seventh atom or an import that moves the wall.
