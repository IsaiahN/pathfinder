# Outreach draft: check the separator before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. The Cognitive Science Society's 2027 submission page explicitly asks authors to refrain from *"broadly advertising any non-anonymized version of your paper via social media or other means (e.g., emails to large recipient lists announcing your paper)"* during the review window, so a mass send would also breach a venue rule.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shared-atom condition (S2 in `01`) is stated as a condition rather than a fact, and until the refuter below is written in the recipient's own terms. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about universals and learned categories

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on the question of whether societies categorize the world with fundamentally different mental structures, or mainly with different learned categories. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, not `grounded`):** the either/or is a union of two conditions, not one question with opposite answers. Across societies the *mechanism* of categorization is shared: a residual drives learning, prototype abstraction is used where it pays, a new term is admitted only when it costs less than the confusion it removes, and what crosses between people is a re-applicable method rather than a recording of one occasion. What varies is the *binding*: which items group together, where a boundary falls, which classifier or spatial frame is learned. Over a shared atom set those different bindings stand to one another as isomers, same atoms arranged differently, different substance in behaviour. The claim is that the structures are shared and the categories are learned, and that a cross-cultural difference in a sorting task cannot by itself tell you which changed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal feature list. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and cannot say which primitive is missing. I am not claiming a particular society is better at categorization, or that all apparent differences are measurement artifacts, or that language alone determines thought. I am not claiming this is settled: no ground reading was taken in producing it.
>
> **The measurement condition:** a cross-cultural comparison needs an anchor that does not change when the frame's own norms are pushed. If the baseline is the categories of industrialised societies, the reference updates on itself and the comparison is a mirror. A single evidence pool, shared instruments and shared theory, is one witness however many laboratories produce it.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written down before this email:**
> 1. A domain where the same task with a non-updating anchor shows the *mechanism* differing between societies while learned content is held fixed.
> 2. Two societies whose category systems cannot be written over any shared atom set, so the isomer mapping fails.
> 3. A non-updating anchor measurement showing that the apparent universal is an artifact of one evidence pool, or that no invariant survives with genuinely independent frames.
>
> If any one holds, the relevant part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a category domain you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it.
