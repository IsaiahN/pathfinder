# The Tether's answer on universals and learned categories

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Categorization behaviour differs across societies: colour partitions, classifier systems, spatial frames, folk-biological kinds. The question asks whether that difference is a change of mental structures or a change of learned categories. Stated as an effect: which part of the observed variation survives a common task with a non-updating anchor, and which part is a change of local binding? Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The offered either/or is a bundled union, not a partition (Fig 9 :54, :57).

## 2. The shape, with separate verdicts

1. **The mechanism is shared.** Across societies the actors (learners), the currency (what pays locally) and the anchor (the local discrimination) change, but the loop and the rule for naming what worked do not. Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked"* ... *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* A residual drives learning, prototype abstraction is used where it pays, and a new term is admitted only when *"stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before"* (Fig 5 :42-43). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 4 plus Smith 2014 and Gelman and Roberts 2017, the latter two low overlap with one another).

2. **The categories are learned and local.** Fig 5 :70: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Which items group together depends on the local ground and the current residual, not on a standing universal inventory. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 5, Fig 11, Gelman and Roberts 2017, Saalbach and Imai 2007).

3. **Different learned systems are isomers over a shared atom set.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Two societies can group the same perceptual atoms differently and thereby produce different category substances. The mapping is *specified*, triangulated across Fig 12, Fig 13 :121 *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* and Saalbach and Imai's finding that *"the global structure of everyday object concepts is strikingly similar across different cultures and languages."* Live strain S2: the shared-atom set is supported per domain but not proven for every domain.

4. **A difference in a sorting task cannot name its own source.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* Different bindings, different mechanisms, translation, sampling and different local grounds all produce the same reading. What is owed is a discriminator, not a vote. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Verdict: `general` structural, corroborated by Fig 2 and Fig 9.

5. **The feature list is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The framework fixes the shape (shared mechanism, variable binding, per-slot anchor) and cannot name a universal feature list or a different primitive set. This is a verdict, not an omission.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all societies have the same categories. It says the *mechanism* is shared.
- Not that all differences are learned. It says the observed differences so far are bindings, and a mechanism difference is a live, refutable possibility (refuter 1).
- Not that language determines thought. Saalbach and Imai (2007) found classifier effects *"miniscule compared with the importance of taxonomic and thematic relations for both language-culture groups"*; Kay and Regier (2006) found both naming universals and naming-driven cognitive differences.
- Not that WEIRD samples are a valid baseline. Kay and Regier (2003) documented the defect in the original evidence and fixed it with an anchor: a fixed 330-chip array and 110 unwritten languages.
- Not that the six-term substrate of Fig 13 is a list of mental features. It is the physical substrate any categorizer confronts; the invariant across societies is the loop and bond grammar (Fig 4 :19-20).
- Not `grounded`. No cross-cultural measurement was taken in producing this.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

An instrument per domain, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading that cannot distinguish its sources demonstrates its own inadequacy; the spectrometer resolved the green case. Here:

- **Mechanism test:** a shared task with a fixed stimulus array in two societies, with learned content held as far as possible constant, designed to detect whether prototype abstraction and MDL-priced minting operate the same way. This fires refuter 1.
- **Shared-atom test:** can both systems be written on one atom set of dimensions, relations and transmission devices? Failure fires refuter 4 and breaks the isomer claim.
- **Anchor test:** does the result survive with a non-updating anchor and a frame that does not share the first frame's evidence pool? Failure fires refuter 3.
- **Import test:** is there a provenanced atom with a shadow test that neither frame held? That would open F4 and require a new census.

Effect sizes and sample counts are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shared atom set is a visible guess at the compression axes of a categorizer's world, not nature's own list. *They're Scanning Books* (carried from the exemplar): *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The answer stops where the instruments stop discriminating and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose between "different mental structures" and "different learned categories," because the question bundles two conditions with different grounds. It says that the *mechanism* of categorization is shared across societies, a residual-driven and priced loop that human learners, and other animals, also use; that what varies is the *binding*, which items group together and where a boundary falls, selected by local ground; and that two societies' category systems stand to one another as isomers, the same atoms arranged differently, decisive in behaviour. It refuses to name a universal feature list, because the framework returns a shape and never an answer. It also refuses to treat a cross-cultural sorting difference as a verdict about its own source: a non-updating anchor and an independent frame are owed in each domain. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slots stay open until the ground pays.
