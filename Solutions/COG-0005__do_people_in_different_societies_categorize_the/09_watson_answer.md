## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, between two answers to the observed variation in categorization across societies. Option A: the variation is mainly because people use fundamentally different mental structures. Option B: the variation is mainly because people use different learned categories over a shared structure. What each leads to: A treats each society's categories as evidence of a different kind of mind and invites a search for a different primitive inventory that the framework cannot supply; B treats the mechanism as shared and the bindings as local, and still owes a discriminator, because a sorting difference cannot name its own source. Refusing the either/or as a partition yields a third result: two conditions with different anchors, kept separate and never averaged.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: indexed per domain and per society-slot, not one global score; Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symmetry: similarity grouping is symmetric, while membership, inference and threshold comparison are ordered, so a pooled difference measure mixes two bond types. Scale: individual learner, community or language, institution or ecology, with a lossy transform between them.
- **Domain 1, the categorization mechanism.** Actors: human learners, with other animals as low-overlap comparison cases. Currency: residual-driven learning, prototype abstraction where it pays, MDL-priced term minting. Anchor: task performance under a common instrument across societies and species. Question type: shared-kernel claim. Nameable anchor: yes.
- **Domain 2, the cultural and linguistic binding.** Actors: speakers, communities, languages, practices. Currency: labels, generics, learned boundaries, classifier systems, spatial frames. Anchor: the local ground each category discriminates. Question type: variable-binding claim. Nameable anchor: yes.
- **Domain 3, the measurement and verification frame.** Actors: cross-cultural researchers and instruments. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: a non-updating anchor (fixed array, behavioural task, re-test after rotation) if one is built; otherwise the frame updates on its own WEIRD norms. Question type: can the difference be believed? Nameable anchor: yes only if constructed.
- **Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Removing the binding condition leaves the shared mechanism standing; a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds.
- **Order by dependency.** Settle the shared mechanism (D1) before judging bindings (D2), because a reading below a broken chain link is a reading of nothing; describe R before choosing a frame; build the anchor before weighting agreement.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The offered either/or is refused as a partition. The mechanism of categorization is shared across societies, and the observed differences are bindings over a shared atom set, related as isomers; a cross-cultural sorting difference cannot name its own source, so a non-updating anchor and an independent frame are owed in each domain. The feature list and any different primitive inventory are a `gap`. This is `general` on the shape and `specified` per domain, ungrounded, candidate. The animal-cognition sources are the low-overlap corroboration for the shared-kernel half; the colour and object-concept sources are one evidence pool each, not several witnesses.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, keeps the three verdicts separate, names the shared-atom condition as a live strain and writes refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger avoidable cost is treating a WEIRD baseline as an anchor and calling the result a universal, or treating a sorting difference as a different kind of mind; Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is reduced by leading with the anchor test and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The bindings are revisable per domain without touching the shared-kernel claim, and the isomer mapping can be withdrawn in any domain whose shared-atom condition fails. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet; no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A shared task with a non-updating anchor showing that the mechanism changes while content is held fixed would break D1; two systems shown to share no atom set would break the isomer claim; a comparison with a genuine anchor and an independent frame showing no invariant would flip D1; a provenanced import with a shadow test supplying a new primitive would reopen F4. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
