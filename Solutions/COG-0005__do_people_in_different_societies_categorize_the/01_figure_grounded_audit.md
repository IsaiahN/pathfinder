## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question offers two answers: societies use **fundamentally different mental structures**, or they use **mainly different learned categories**. Stated as an effect, the residual R is this: categorization behaviour differs across societies (which colour chips get grouped, which objects share a classifier, which spatial frame is preferred, which folk-biological kinds are recognised), and we do not know which part of that difference is a change in the *mechanism* and which part is a change in the *content the mechanism binds*.

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** R is indexed per category domain and per society-slot, not one global "worldview" number. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Colour, artefacts, space, folk biology and kinship are different slots with different grounds. A single cross-cultural "difference score" would aggregate them and throw away the signal that says where to look.
- **Symmetry:** category judgements come in two logical kinds. Similarity grouping is symmetric (bond `+`, *"both hold at once"*, Operators :12-14). Membership, hierarchy, inference and comparison are ordered (bonds `→`, `⇒`, `⋛`; Operators :29-51). A pooled "difference" measure that mixes them cannot choose which structure changed.
- **Scale:** individual learner, community/language, institution/ecology. The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30), so the round trip gap R_T must be measured, not assumed zero.

**What the two options predict.** Option A (different structures): with the same task and a non-updating anchor, the invariants of categorization should differ between societies, no shared descriptive space should align them, and the difference should survive a change of local content. Option B (different learned categories): the mechanism and its invariants should be shared, and variation should live in the bindings, which are selected by local ground. The derivation below decides between these predictions per domain, and refuses the framing that they are mutually exclusive.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 — The either/or is a union compound, not a partition

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The world literature says the same thing in different vocabulary. Kay and Regier (2006, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, full text read via the Berkeley PDF text and the abstract at Cell Press): *"universal tendencies in color naming"* exist and *"naming differences across languages do cause differences in color cognition"*; they conclude *"nature proposes and nurture disposes."* Abbott, Griffiths and Regier (2016, PNAS, full text opened at PMC): *"categorization in the contested semantic domain of color may be governed by principles that apply more broadly in cognition"* and one model *"accounts for both universal tendencies and variation in focal colors across languages."*

So the answer to "structures or categories?" is not a point on a scale. It is: shared structure at D1, variable binding at D2, measured at D3. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.

**Refuter:** a single domain is found in which the same task with a non-updating anchor shows mechanism differences and binding differences are held constant, so that one lever flips the whole domain. That would restore a partition.

### F2 — The kernel is invariant; actors, currency and anchor change

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked"** ... **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Applied across societies: learners are the actors, what pays locally is the currency, and the local discriminations are the anchor. The loop itself (perceive a residual, route it, mint a term if the description length pays, settle against ground, carry methods up and head starts down) does not change with the society. Fig 4 :22 **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** What crosses between societies is a re-applicable method, not a recording of one society's categories.

World corroboration, genuinely low overlap with the framework: J. David Smith (2014, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, full text opened at PMC) writes that *"Diverse species share operating principles, default commitments, and processing weaknesses in categorization."* Gelman and Roberts (2017, PNAS, full text opened at PMC): *"human language is a technology that enhances and expands the categorization capacities that we share with other animals."* Both predict a shared kernel. The animal source is the strongest corroborator because it does not share the human cultural evidence pool.

**Status:** `specified` per D1, **triangulated** across Fig 4 and two low-overlap world fields (comparative cognition, developmental/cultural psychology).

**Refuter:** show a society whose categorization mechanism, under the same task and a non-updating anchor, lacks prototype abstraction or MDL-priced minting while local content is held fixed.

### F3 — Variation is binding, not a new kind of mind

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."** Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A colour partition, a classifier system and a spatial frame are different *arrangements* over a shared atom set: perceptual dimensions, similarity, taxonomy and thematic relations, a prototype mechanism, labels and generics as transmission devices. Over that set they are isomers, not different periodic tables. Saalbach and Imai (2007, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, abstract and reproduction opened): *"the global structure of everyday object concepts is strikingly similar across different cultures and languages,"* with classifier and thematic effects that are *"miniscule compared with the importance of taxonomic and thematic relations for both language-culture groups."*

**Status:** `specified` per D2, **triangulated** across Fig 12, Fig 13 and the Saalbach/Imai result.

**Refuter:** show two societies whose category systems cannot be written over a shared atom set at all (no common dimensions, mechanism or transmission device), i.e. genuinely incommensurable closures. Then the isomer claim fails.

### F4 — "Fundamentally different mental structures" at the primitive level is a `gap`, reported as unreachable rather than silence

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** and :135 **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."**

No figure line returns a different primitive inventory for a society, and no figure line returns a feature list at all. A genuinely different *structure*, in the framework's sense, would require a new atom: Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* That import would need a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8). None is available. The honest verdict is `gap`: the framework fixes the *shape* (invariant mechanism plus variable binding) and cannot name the missing primitive even if one existed. This is not a claim that variation is impossible. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**Status:** `general` structural, `gap` on the named feature list.

**Refuter:** a provenanced import from a frame whose closure genuinely differs, with a shadow test, that supplies a cognitive primitive not in the shared closure, plus a measurement showing a society's categories require it.

### F5 — Categories are settled terms, local and residual-relative; there is no standing universal list

Fig 5 :70:

> **"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."**

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A category that pays in one ecology (a fine-grained snow or colour vocabulary, a cardinal spatial frame) need not pay in another. This is not a claim that one society is better at categorization; valence is relative to the residual. Fig 1 :19 again: the effect is per slot. Gelman and Roberts (2017) put the binding side plainly: *"We are not born with a fixed set of categories"* and *"human categories have a cultural overlay."*

**Status:** `specified` per D2, **triangulated** across Fig 5, Fig 11 and Gelman & Roberts.

**Refuter:** a standing list of categories that is settled by the ground in every society with the same residual, demonstrated with a non-updating anchor in more than one domain.

### F6 — Most apparent "fundamentally different minds" are a measurement failure

Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does."**

If the baseline is the categories of industrialised societies, the reference updates on the frame's own norms (Fig 2 collapse 1, mutual update) or several samples share one evidence pool (collapse 2, hidden shared source). Kay and Regier (2003, PNAS, full text opened at PMC) document exactly this defect in the original Berlin and Kay evidence: *"17 of the 20 languages studied were written languages of industrialized societies,"* with *"no more than a single speaker was tested for most languages."* Their objective test over 110 unwritten languages found that *"strong universal tendencies in color naming exist across both sorts of language."* The fix is an anchor that does not move, not a larger chorus of agreeing frames. Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

**Status:** `general` structural for D3, `specified` for the Kay/Regier case. Triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 10 and the 2003 PNAS measurement.

**Refuter:** a cross-cultural comparison that uses an explicitly non-updating anchor and independent frames and still shows mechanism differences in D1, with D2 held fixed.

### F7 — The "shape, not a name" limit and the correction to my own first reading

**First reading (kept visible).** I initially wrote that the figures forbid naming any specific category or feature, since the framework returns *"a shape, not a name."* That overstates the rule.

**Correction.** The figures README phrase is *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a missing primitive, one not held in the library. Colour foci, taxonomic and thematic relations, labels and generics are not missing primitives; they are bindings onto capacities already held. Naming them is provenance from the psychology/anthropology frame, not the invention of a seventh term and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :22 *"IMPORT adds an atom"* adds none here). The genuine limit remains F4: no figure line names a new primitive set or a universal feature list. The overstatement is retracted as applied to held atoms and preserved here so the correction is visible.

### F8 — A reading of category behaviour cannot by itself name its source

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

A cross-cultural difference in a sorting task is a reading. It does not determine whether the source is a different mechanism, a different learned binding, a translation artifact, a sampling artifact or a different local ground. Metamerism is the named error: the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the green case. Here the corresponding instrument is a task design with a non-updating anchor and an independent second frame. Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* This finding governs D3 and is **single-source** on the world side (one essay by the framework author), corroborated structurally by Fig 2 and Fig 9.

**Refuter:** a measurement that discriminates among these sources in one domain, with provenance and a stated failure condition. Until one exists, the source is open and is recorded as such.

### F9 — Agreement and prestige are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict

Seat Map :163 *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Seat Map :171 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. A list of cross-cultural researchers selected because they are likely to agree with a universalist or a relativist answer produces a convention, not a verdict. The outreach artifacts therefore ask for a refutation, never an endorsement, and record the verdict on stage 1 before showing any theory.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by Seat Map :287 *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. In a category domain, with the same task and a non-updating anchor, the *mechanism* of categorization (prototype abstraction, MDL-priced minting, cross-kind composition) demonstrably differs between societies while learned content is held fixed.
2. A third term is shown necessary, neither shared structure nor learned binding, and it is a new primitive admitted with a source gate and a shadow test. That breaks the two-condition union and requires a new census.
3. A non-updating anchor measurement shows that the apparent universal is an artifact of one evidence pool, or that no invariant survives with genuinely independent frames. That flips D1.
4. Two societies' category systems cannot be written over a shared atom set at all, so the isomer mapping of F3 fails and the closures are incommensurable.
5. Fig 13 is shown to be a cognitive feature inventory rather than a physical substrate, which would make the re-census in section 1 wrong.

If any fires, the relevant verdict is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Refuse the either/or as a partition. Keep three verdicts: shared mechanism (`specified`), variable binding (`specified`), measurement condition (`general` structural). Ref 9 :54 and F1.
2. State the universal claim as a *shape*: an invariant mechanism plus a bond grammar, with the feature list left open (`gap`). F4, F7.
3. Treat cross-cultural differences as isomers over a shared atom set unless a shared-atom test fails. F3, refuter 4.
4. Before believing any difference, build a non-updating anchor and at least one independent frame. F6, F8.
5. Do not use WEIRD norms as the baseline; the Kay and Regier (2003) correction is the worked example of what goes wrong. F6.
6. Send the generator (claim, refuter, anchor test), not a recording of the conclusion. Fig 4 :22, Fig 10 :52.

**Round trip gap R_T.** Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Sent up (x): the verbatim figures on invariant kernel, union, isomer, settlement and non-updating anchor. Dropped constraints: (a) per-slot indexing of R; (b) the source gate and shadow test required before calling anything an import; (c) the non-updating anchor requirement; (d) the shared-atom condition on isomerism. R_T is therefore nonzero and is recorded qualitatively. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error.
