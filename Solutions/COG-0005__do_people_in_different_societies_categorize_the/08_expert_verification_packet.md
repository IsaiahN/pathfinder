# Expert verification packet: the Tether on universals and learned categories

**What this is:** a claim in a form a categorization or cross-cultural researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No cross-cultural measurement was taken. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The either/or is a union of two conditions, not one question with opposite answers. Across societies the mechanism of categorization is shared: a residual drives learning, prototype abstraction is used where it pays, and a new term is admitted only when stating it plus what remains unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes. What varies is the binding: which items group together, where a boundary falls, which classifier or spatial frame is learned. Over a shared atom set two such systems are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, different substance in behaviour. No universal feature list is claimed. A cross-cultural difference in a sorting task is a reading that cannot name its own source, so a non-updating anchor and an independent frame are owed in each domain before any difference is counted.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 5 and 11

- **Statement:** the invariant across societies is the mechanism, not the category inventory. A term is settled only where the local ground pays, and no standing list of categories survives a change of residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20 *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked"* and *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 5 :70 *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** does the figure's kernel apply to a categorization mechanism at all, or only to an agent's learning loop? The mapping is a binding and is declared as such.
- **Refuter:** show a society where the mechanism itself changes under the same task and a non-updating anchor, with learned content held fixed; or show that the kernel cannot be bound to categorization without straining Fig 4.

### C2 — the discriminating claim; checkable against the categorization literature

- **Statement:** societal differences in learned categories are isomers over a shared atom set, not different kinds of mind. The shared set includes perceptual dimensions, similarity, taxonomic and thematic relations, a prototype mechanism and labels/generics. Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 13 :121 *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*
- **What to check:** is the shared-atom condition actually met in a domain you know? Saalbach and Imai (2007, *JEP General*) found *"the global structure of everyday object concepts is strikingly similar across different cultures and languages"* with classifier effects *"miniscule compared with the importance of taxonomic and thematic relations."* Smith (2014, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*) found *"Diverse species share operating principles, default commitments, and processing weaknesses in categorization."* Abbott, Griffiths and Regier (2016, PNAS) found one model that *"accounts for both universal tendencies and variation in focal colors across languages."* Are those three findings about the same atom set, or three different sets wearing one name?
- **Refuter:** two category systems that cannot be written over any common atom set (falsifier 4), or a domain where the isomorphism fails while mechanism is held constant.

### C3 — measurement; checkable from Figures 2, 9 and the Hard Problem essay

- **Statement:** a cross-cultural difference is a projection and cannot name its source. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."*
- **What to check:** in your domain, is the baseline a non-updating anchor (a fixed array, a behavioural task, a re-test after rotation) or the category norms of the researchers? If it is the latter, the comparison is Fig 2's mutual-update collapse, and the observed "difference" or "universal" is not yet evidence.
- **Refuter:** a comparison with a genuine non-updating anchor and an independent frame that still shows a mechanism difference in C1, or shows no invariant survives.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal feature list. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
- Not that all societies have the same categories, or that all differences are learned. Both are open per slot.
- Not that language determines thought, or that WEIRD samples are a valid baseline. Kay and Regier (2003, PNAS) documented the original sample defect: *"17 of the 20 languages studied were written languages of industrialized societies."*
- Not that the six-term substrate of Fig 13 is a mental feature list. It is the physical substrate of the world a categorizer confronts.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this agent.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument per domain, not a larger chorus. Three concrete tests:

1. Can the mechanism be shown to differ with the same task and a non-updating anchor while content is held fixed?
2. Can both category systems be written over one shared atom set, with the shared-atom condition stated and checked?
3. Does the result survive an independent frame that does not share the first frame's stimulus set, observers or theory?

Agreement, prestige and effect size are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 5 :70, Fig 11 :51, Fig 12 :117 and Fig 2 :29, :35. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against a domain you know: are the atoms shared, or is the shared-atom condition doing more work than the evidence supports?
3. Name one instrument in your domain that would distinguish a mechanism change from a binding change, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
