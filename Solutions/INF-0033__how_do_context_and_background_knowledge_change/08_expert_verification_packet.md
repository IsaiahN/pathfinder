# Expert verification packet: context, background knowledge, and the same signal

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in context and information research can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` on the predictions, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A signal fixes a token sequence, not an information content. What a receiver obtains is a per-slot residual between its prediction, formed from its library and belief, and the tokens that arrived. Background knowledge is that library and belief; context is the operative selection of which part of the history a signal addresses. The difference between two receivers decoding the same tokens is carried by the arrangement, the bond, which is not recoverable from the tokens themselves. Two receivers who share a background will agree, and that agreement is a convention useful for coordination but worthless as a check on truth. A second receiver whose closure differs adds reach from the same signal. The framework does not name which bond a given context supplies; that is a recorded gap. What it offers is the shape, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 12 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** information is a per-slot residual relative to the receiving frame, and the difference between frames is carried by the bond, not the tokens.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Symbols table :20-21 *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."*
- **Refuter:** a receiver with a demonstrably different library and belief produces the same per-slot residual vector for the same tokens across all testable slots; or the same tokens under two different bonds yield identical residual reductions with no instrument that can discriminate them.

### C2: Field-facing; checkable by a cognitive scientist or pragmatics researcher

- **Statement:** prior history and context weighting change the interpretation of the same signal; shared background produces agreement that cannot serve as evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table :24-26 *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and :41 *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*
- **What to check:** is the per-slot prediction defensible against predictive-coding and schema data? When two people share a background and decode identically, is that ever itself a ground for the factual question, or always a convention? The candidate evidence pool is predictive coding, Bartlett-style schema theory, and symbol-grounding work, listed in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.
- **Refuter:** two frames sharing all relevant context settle a factual question by agreeing, which would collapse Fig 2's anchor into a frame; or identical histories and weightings are required to explain identical decodes, contradicting :24-26.

### C3: Framework-internal derivation with an empirical tail

- **Statement:** where the atom set is fixed and only the bond changes, two readings of the same signal are isomers; where the atom sets differ, it is a reach difference. Which bond a given context supplies is unbound.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
- **Refuter:** a context change always changes the atom set rather than the bond, which kills the isomer claim and leaves only the reach-difference reading; or two frames with provably disjoint closures add no new composable term, which kills the union-surplus prediction.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that meaning is subjective, that all interpretations are equal, or that truth is negotiated.
- Not that Shannon information is wrong. It is scoped to the code domain; this packet refuses it only as an answer to a question about interpretation.
- Not that context equals `Γ`. Background knowledge maps to `Γ` plus `b` plus `w`; context is the selector.
- Not that the figures identify the bond a given context supplies. That is a gap and is presented as one.
- Not that agreement among selected respondents is corroboration. Respondents are chosen against the residual, not for likely assent.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Isomer test:** fix the atom set, vary only the bond, look for divergent downstream behaviour.
2. **Reach test:** vary `Γ` and show novelty for the same token tracks the atoms each receiver holds.
3. **Source test:** find an instrument that discriminates which source produced the same reading. Without one, context narrows but does not name.
4. **Per-slot test:** show an aggregate bit count missing a live slot whose interpretation changed.

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
