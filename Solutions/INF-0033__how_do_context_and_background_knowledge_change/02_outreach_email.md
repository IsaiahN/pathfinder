# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding whether context changes information

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the finding being checked is stated as a per-slot residual and the refuter below is attached. If the output cannot survive the refuter, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Same tokens, different information. Check one prediction.

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow output I would like you to check against your own field. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` structure, `specified` prediction, not `grounded`):** a signal is a fixed token sequence. What a receiver gets from it is a per-slot residual, the gap between what that receiver's library and belief predicted and what actually arrived. The figures state this as *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19) and define the belief as *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation"* (Symbols table :20-21). So two receivers with different libraries, histories, or weightings get different information from the same tokens. That difference is not noise added on top of the signal. It is the signal being composed into a different arrangement: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording"* (Fig 12 :5).
>
> The prediction is specific: hold the tokens fixed, vary the receiver's library and belief, and the per-slot residual vector changes in a way that tracks which bonds the receiver can compose. A shared bit count will not show it. Fig 1 :22 calls that *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that meaning is purely subjective, that any interpretation is as good as another, or that the framework names the mechanism by which your field does this. The figures define the bonds and the residual; they do not select which bond a given social or cognitive context supplies. That part is a gap, and I am recording it as a gap. I am also not using shared context as evidence: if two people decode a message the same way because they share a background, that agreement is a coordination product, not a check on what the message was about.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. A receiver with a demonstrably different library and belief produces the same per-slot residual vector for the same tokens across all testable slots.
> 2. Novelty turns out to be absolute rather than relative to what a receiver already holds, that is, a token that is news for every possible library.
> 3. The same tokens under two different arrangements yield behaviourally identical residual reductions with no instrument that can tell the arrangements apart.
> 4. Two people who share all the relevant context can settle a factual question by agreeing.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line failure is more useful to me than endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the three public sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, public repo, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule for the figures: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The essay that maps context, subtext, and pretext onto receiver-side structure: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/decoding-tacit-knowledge-why-context
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a case you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output before theory | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the prediction without holding the framework's interior. |
| Per-slot residual, not an average | Fig 1 :19 and :22; an aggregate hides the only signal that says where the change happened. |
| Refuter before the conclusion | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. It is listed in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3. |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`, *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Shared context not used as corroboration | Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :41 keeps the same agreement as efficient for a convention. |
| The bond is not named | Fig 12 defines seven bonds; the Operators table gives tests; neither selects one for a given context. Marked gap, not filled in. |
| No request for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method, with a refuter required | Fig 4 :22-25, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A method is transferable; a recording is not. |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* |

## One-to-one and opt-in only

Use the public submission and contact points in `03`. Do not harvest addresses, do not rank recipients by how likely they are to agree, and do not send the same note to a list. Independently chosen frames are the only ones whose difference buys reach (Fig 8 :45-49).
