## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

A **shape** is therefore the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name the mechanism. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is not "context is the library" and it is not "meaning is subjective." It is a three-part receiver-side structure plus a ground that alone decides truth. That structure is narrow enough to check and open enough not to name a mechanism the figures do not supply.

## 2. How the shape is obtained for this question

*The Library of Babel* gives the search method: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*

Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*

Applied here, the effect terms are: same tokens; different per-slot residual; different bond; agreement is a convention; the ground decides. Those five constraints are the shape. Anything that goes beyond them, such as "the bond is discourse pragmatics" or "the bond is schema assimilation," is a name borrowed from another frame and must be recorded as such.

The stopping rule from *The Library of Babel*: you are done when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here that means the shape stops exactly where the original question was stated: the same signal, and what the receiver brings to it.

## 3. The essay that maps the receiver side

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge: Why Context, Subtext, and Pretext Matter* (2026-11-10) is directly on this question. Its opening problem is: *"Why can an expert say a few words that convey paragraphs' worth of meaning, while a beginner understands only the literal words?"*

Its answer: *"every message is understood through three things the listener brings: context (what’s going on around the words), subtext (what the words are for, though they don’t say it) and pretext (the history and assumptions the listener had before the words arrived)."* And: *"Without the right one, a compressed message can't be unpacked, however clearly it was said."*

The essay then maps each term onto framework vocabulary:

- *"Pretext is the history (“the library”): the experience and assumptions the listener brings before the words arrive."*
- *"Context points the way (“search”): it picks the part of that history this message is about."*
- *"Subtext is the meaning you arrive at (“the interpretation”): what the message says once you read it through that history, in its setting."*

And the decisive limit: *"Context, subtext and pretext decide what a message means to you. None of them decides whether it is true. Only the ground does that."* Plus the compact version: *"Meaning doesn’t live in the words. It lives in what the listener brings: the situation, the point and the history behind it."*

This is the shape for INF-0033 in the framework's own words: a receiver-side three-part structure that decides interpretation, and a ground that decides truth. Note the essay's own caveat: *"\*Note: AI assisted, but the thoughts are my own."* That is a provenance declaration, and this derivation repeats it in its own mode line.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism that stops a name being premature. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The essay's general claim: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can’t distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

Applied here: the same observed token pattern can have several sources and several bindings. Context and background knowledge select one; they do not prove it. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name. Fig 6 :30 and Fig 9 :32 govern the same limit from the figures side.
