# The Tether's answer: how context and background knowledge change the information conveyed by the same signal

**Mode: `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on the predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same token sequence arrives at receivers with different libraries, beliefs, weightings, and histories. What changes is not the tokens but the information obtained from them. The residual is the gap between what each receiver predicted per slot and what arrived: `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`, indexed by slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The question is a union compound of five conditions (channel, library and belief, bond, convention, and ground), not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54-56). Each domain keeps its own verdict.

## 2. The shape

1. **The receiver, not the signal, fixes the resolution.** The tokens are the observation `o′`; the prediction is `Γ(b,a)`. Fig 1 :19 and :22 bar aggregation: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The information conveyed is the per-slot residual reduction, so it differs with the receiver even when `o′` is identical.
2. **Background knowledge is the library and the belief.** Symbols table :9-10 defines `Γ`; :20-26 defines `b` as *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation"* and states *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Fig 6 :22: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."* The same token is news for one receiver and *"nothing new here"* for another (Fig 5 :30).
3. **Context supplies the bond, and the bond is the information.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Same tokens under `→` (order matters), `≡` (two names, one referent), `⋛` (which is larger), or `−` (the absence is the point) are different substances. Which bond a given context supplies is **not** named by the figures; that is a recorded gap.
4. **Shared context is a convention, not a verdict.** Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Two people who decode the same way because they share a background have produced coordination, not evidence.
5. **A second, different frame adds reach from the same signal.** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* The surplus exists only while the closures differ, and the frame must be chosen against the residual, not the hoped-for interpretation (Fig 8 :53, Fig 9 :27-28).
6. **The ground decides which interpretation was right.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Context decides meaning; only the ground decides truth.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that meaning is subjective or that any interpretation is as good as another. The ground ranges against each frame separately.
- Not that Shannon information is wrong. It is correct in its own domain. Refused only as an answer to a question constitutively about interpretation.
- Not that context is identical to `Γ`. The corrected mapping is `Γ` plus `b` plus `w` for background knowledge, and an operative selector for context (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1).
- Not that the figures name the bond a given context supplies. That is a gap. Naming one is a binding from another frame.
- Not that respondents selected for agreement corroborate anything. Fig 8 :48-49: two frames that overlap almost completely are *"one frame wearing two names."*
- Not `grounded`. No source-discriminating measurement was taken.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

- **Isomer test:** hold the atom set fixed, vary only the bond supplied by context, and check for different downstream behaviour. A failure of discrimination would falsify Fig 12's bond claim for that case.
- **Reach test:** give two receivers different `Γ` and show that novelty for the same token tracks the atoms each holds (Fig 6 :21-22).
- **Convention test:** show that two frames sharing all context still need an external anchor to settle a factual question (Fig 2 :35, :45).
- **Per-slot test:** show that an aggregate bit count misses a live slot whose interpretation changed (Fig 1 :13, :22).
- **Standing refuters from `01` section 3:** identical residual vectors for different libraries and beliefs; absolute novelty; indistinguishable bonds; agreement settling fact; a reading that uniquely determines its source.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops where the instruments stop. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The specific bond that context supplies for a given case is not in `Γ` and cannot be named from inside the frame. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say the signal is unimportant, and it does not say meaning is whatever you like. It says a signal fixes tokens, not information. What a receiver gets is a per-slot residual against its own library and belief, and the difference between two receivers is carried by the arrangement the context supplies, because the bond is the rest of the information and is not recoverable from the tokens. People who share a background will decode the same message the same way, but that agreement is a convention, not a check on truth; only a ground ranging against each frame separately decides which reading was right. A second, different frame can get more from the same signal than either alone, provided it is chosen against the residual rather than the answer you want. The framework does not name the bond a given context supplies. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
