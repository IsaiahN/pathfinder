## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Engineering boundary.** Information theory makes the receiver's prior knowledge part of the accounting even at the syntactic level: if the receiver already knows the bit values, *no information is transmitted*. That is the same dependency the figures express with `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`. It is a boundary case, not a semantic solution.
2. **Prediction error as residual.** Predictive coding makes the per-slot residual the engine of perception and explicitly weights prior expectation against incoming signal. This is the closest independent field analogue to Fig 1 plus the Symbols table, and it predicts that the same signal with different priors yields a different interpretation.
3. **Schema and background knowledge.** Bartlett's retellings show the same story being transformed into familiar forms by the receiver's existing schema. Schema theory predicts that conflicting schemata can be applied to the same information, which is the D3 bond claim in cognitive-scientific language.
4. **Grounding.** The symbol grounding problem states that manipulating token shapes is not enough for meaning; grounding requires sensorimotor contact. This corroborates Fig 10's *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one,"* but at low independence from the framework's own essays, which cite the same literature.
5. **Animal behaviour.** Vervet alarm calls are a clean example of "same signal, different downstream effect depending on receiver and context." It is a genuinely different evidence pool from the human cognition sources.

## Distribution: offer, not push

- **Fig 4 :22-25:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Lead with the residual and the refuter. The full framework is a recording until the recipient has asked for it.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them any bibliography.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 8 :45-49:** the extra reach from two frames exists only while their closures differ. Choosing recipients for likely agreement spends the reach before the check happens.

**Recommended order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual: same tokens, different per-slot residual, with the four refuters. Ask for a failure. Nothing else is attached.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the three source links. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded and is never revisited in light of the theory.

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own mode and writes their own refuter before running it. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 and 2 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The bond that a given context supplies is still a recorded gap, so that question must be presented as open, not answered.
