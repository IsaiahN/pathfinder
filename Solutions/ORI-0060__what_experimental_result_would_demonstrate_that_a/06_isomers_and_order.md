## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two campaign structures isomers? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST PASS ON "SHAPE, NOT A NAME"

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Abil et al. 2024 use two configurations: *"intermittent"*, where *"DNA is amplified by PCR between rounds of evolution"*, and *"semi-continuous"*, where *"amplified DNA from a fraction of liposomes would be recursively passed on to an excess of fresh liposomes via vesicle fusion and fission"*. Both use liposomes, a self-replicating DNA template, the PURE expression system and a selection round. Same atoms.
- The bonds differ. Intermittent composes `H ⇒ (extract) → (PCR) → (re-encapsulate) → H`: production (`⇒`) followed by a researcher-mediated sequence (`→`). Semi-continuous composes `H → (fusion) → (fission) → H`: the state re-enters via the compartment's own boundary operation (Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."*). Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- Abil et al. report the behavioural difference: the intermittent campaign accumulated *"4–8"* mutations per molecule by round 11 while the semi-continuous one *"plateauing at around 1 mutation/molecule after round 3"*. The difference is *"invisible in the formula"* (the ingredient list is the same) and decisive in the behaviour. That is the figure's isomer test, met by a real experiment. **Triangulated** (Fig 12 :117 plus the world source).
- **Correction kept visible.** My first pass said the framework's "shape, not a name" forbids naming a protocol. It does not: the phrase governs *missing primitives*, not bindings onto held atoms. Both campaign structures are bindings from the world frame; they are allowed to be named, but neither is figure-derived and neither is the only admissible structure. Retracted as applied to protocols; see `05` section 5 and `01` section 8 (R2).

### Q2 - Which bond carries the head start?

- Fig 4 :21: **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**

- In the intermittent case, what crosses the generation boundary is a PCR product: a head start prepared outside the compartment. In the semi-continuous case, what crosses is the compartment content redistributed by freeze-thaw. ### Q3 - Is the order heredity → variation → selection → cumulative change real?

- Operators table: `→` is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, and the test is *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* The order is ordered, not symmetric.
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** A variant population with no heredity cannot carry a selected state forward; differential fitness with no variation has nothing to select; cumulative change with no selection is drift.
- Fig 12 :112: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** The four slots are not an ingredient list; the bonds between them are what make the verdict claim possible.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single-round result with strong differential amplification but no measured parent-offspring correlation were accepted by specialists as "sustained Darwinian evolution", the order claim fails and the right structure is a flat conjunction `H + V + P + D`, not a sequence.
- Live type question, named and not hidden: variation can arise from replication errors, background reactions or compositional transitions (Ameta et al. 2021; Bunin and Rivoire 2024). The figures require a source of variation but do not select among these mechanisms. Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely"* is a guard on a term, not a claim about which physical mechanism supplies mutations. Do not convert it into one.

### Q4 - Are the two published results functional-group members or full isomers?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Kurihara et al. 2015 demonstrate recursive self-proliferation over three generations with a primitive cell cycle. They do not demonstrate differential fitness among heritable variants; their own text makes adaptation prospective. Abil et al. 2024 demonstrate mutation fixation and improved self-amplification over rounds. Both share the cell-cycle functional group (ingestion, replication, division) and differ in whether the selection slot is composed.
- Over the atom set `{C, H, S, V, P, L}`, Kurihara et al. instantiate `{C, H, S}`; Abil et al. instantiate `{C, H, S, V, P, L}`. They are therefore **not isomers over the full set**. They are two members of the same functional group with different atom occupancy. Calling them isomers would be the flat-formula error of Fig 12 :14-16: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*.
- A genuine isomer claim would compare two campaigns that both instantiate the full set and differ only in bond: intermittent versus semi-continuous (Q1), or the same compartments under a catalytic-function selection (Liu et al. 2025) versus a replication-rate selection (Abil et al. 2024). The former comparison holds the platform fixed and varies the bond; the latter varies the platform too, so it is not a clean isomer test.

## CONCLUSION

Intermittent and semi-continuous campaigns are isomers over a fixed atom set: same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour, with the difference recorded in the mutation-accumulation data. The order heredity → variation → selection → cumulative change is ordered by dependency, with a stated falsifier. Recursive self-proliferation without differential fitness and full Darwinian evolution are functional-group relatives, not isomers, because their atom occupancy differs. No figure line selects which campaign is the right experiment, and no world ground has settled that either; the choice is a `gap` and a provenanced binding, not a figure-derived verdict.
