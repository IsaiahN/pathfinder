## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the size of a bonus but what happens to the gap between what a pay scheme predicts and what the organization actually gets. R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`).

- **b** is the employee's belief about what the scheme rewards, formed from personal history and inherited priors.
- **a** is the allocation of effort across tasks: what the employee actually does.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the employee's library of routines predicts the metric will reward.
- **o'** is what actually happens: long-run value, quality, cooperation, customer outcomes, survival.
- **s** is the employee, the team, or the task. R is indexed per slot, never averaged into an engagement score (Fig 1 :19, :22).

Two options and what each leads to:

- Option A, "pay tied to a measured result improves results": leads to the prediction that effort tracks the true organizational outcome because the metric represents it.
- Option B, "pay tied to a measured result corrupts the measure": leads to the prediction that effort drains toward the measured atom while the unmeasured outcome degrades.

The honest answer from the figures is that this is a **union of three conditions**, not one question with two answers. Which condition fails decides which option obtains. A meter that decides pay stops being a meter (Fig 2 :43), so the same scheme can improve one slot and corrupt another.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a set of conditions whose conjunction keeps metric fidelity, effort direction, and long-run contact intact, plus instruments that would discriminate a failure of one from a failure of another. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` keeps the per-domain verdicts separate. It is never averaged into "incentives work" or "incentives fail." Mode `specified`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of incentives.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
