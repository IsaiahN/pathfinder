## Q1 - Are two pay schemes with the same ingredients isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Suppose two firms use the same atoms: base pay, a bonus pool, one KPI, a quarterly review. If one composes them as an individual threshold comparison against peers and the other composes them as a shared production chain toward a customer outcome, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the compensation handbook. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you pay, it is what the pay is bonded to", but stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the two schemes share only a pattern, for example `effort → measured result → reward`, while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124). The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A compensation audit that compares only ingredient lists cannot distinguish isomer from functional group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two schemes with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in long-run results with no difference in habitat or ground contact, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D4), which would move the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the organizational application. The world corroboration is the surrogation finding that multiple measures reduce the effect, but that is the same management-accounting pool.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a team facing a new bonus metric:

1. **Perception.** Can employees read both the metric and the outcome? Failure here has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the metric have terms for what the task actually affords, and can the employee say what progress toward the real outcome would count as? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can the scheme state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A metric with only ingredients cannot state a goal about the unmeasured task.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next review cycle.

The three conditions map onto this chain: C1 (metric fidelity) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (effort direction) is mostly a failure of link 3 and 5, because the scheme names the measured result but never imports a term for the unmeasured one; C3 (horizon contact) is a failure of link 5, because the cycle names what worked each quarter but never verifies it against the long run. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear all three (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The metric-facing link (1) has dashboards and reports; the carry link (5) has almost none. So budget piles up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2, 3 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a scheme whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F7 in the primary audit.

## Q5 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the goal-setting emphasis on specific objectives, as link 3 under a metric that still ranges against the ground;
- the crowding-out emphasis on contingency and initial interest, as C1 and Fig 11 :49-51;
- the monitoring emphasis on measurement cost, as the two currencies of Fig 12 :125-129;
- the surrogation emphasis on multiple measures, as C2 and Fig 6 :23;
- the culture emphasis on habitat, as D4.

It cannot generate the *name* of the scheme that will work next. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two schemes with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two schemes sharing only an `effort → result → reward` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the vocabulary and objective links and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1, C2 and C3 need different instruments. The standard management explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The low-overlap analogues corroborate mechanism shape and carry two recorded strains, both resolved by re-census. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
