# Expert verification packet: the Tether on seasonal ecological networks

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a real network is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An ecological network is a Structure in the six-term set, the one where entities relate and influence travels (Fig 13 :23-24). When interactions vary with season, life stage or environmental conditions, what changes is the habitat's field of contact. Fig 11 :73 says the habitat updates while the ground does not. The change is read per interaction slot (Fig 1 :19), not in one network average; the same species pair can change relation type without changing partners, because valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-50); season-to-season networks are isomers over a shared species set (Fig 12 :117); and effects carry beyond direct contact (Fig 11 :52-53). The framework returns this shape, not a name, and states in advance what would refute it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 12 (triangulated)

- **Statement:** interaction type is not a property carried by the two species. It is a relation re-valued against the current residual, and the bond is not recoverable from the operands. Mutualism and parasitism are relations, not kinds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Triangulated across low overlap by Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* and Fig 12 :95: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- **Refuter:** a measured system in which each species pair keeps identical sign and per-capita strength across all seasons, stages and conditions, and all network change is node turnover. Or a demonstration that bond type is recoverable from the operands alone. Either fires C1.

### C2: ecology-facing; checkable against a seasonal network dataset

- **Statement:** the live signal is per interaction, and networks built over a shared species set with different bond arrangements are Fig 12 isomers, not minor variants of one substance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** in a dataset with repeated seasonal sampling, do individual edges change sign or strength while the species list is largely stable? Does a single global metric (nestedness, modularity, H2') hide that per-edge signal? Are the modules that fix the outcome the same across seasons with different members?
- **Refuter:** one global metric carries all the live signal and no per-interaction slot does (breaks the Fig 1 part), or successive networks share no species set (breaks the isomer part and leaves the functional-group part standing).

### C3: cascade claim; framework-internal derivation with world corroboration

- **Statement:** a condition-driven interaction change cannot be accounted for by direct contacts alone. The residual that matters can be two or three steps out.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Also Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- **What to check:** in a manipulation of one interaction or one condition, does the measured effect propagate beyond the directly connected partners, and at what order does it become large?
- **Refuter:** all measured effects are strictly first order after the cascade has had time to run. World corroboration (secondary only): Strona and Lafferty 2016, *"networks were far more robust to historical conditions than new ones"*; their result is an instance, not a derivation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a seasonal time series for any real ecosystem. No field or simulation data were taken here.
- Not that any particular network is stable, resilient or fragile. Those are ground verdicts.
- Not that "rewiring" is a mechanism the framework discovered. It is a binding onto held relation terms; it adds no atom and does not move the wall (Fig 6 :14).
- Not that a network-level index is the ground. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Not that season, life stage and environment are independent questions. They are a union compound.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an interaction-level measurement.
- Not that this derivation can settle the ecological question it reports on. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-interaction, condition-resolved measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does the same species pair keep identical sign and per-capita strength across seasons, stages and conditions?
2. Does one global metric carry all the live signal, or is there a per-interaction slot with positive mass?
3. What is the highest order at which a measurable effect appears after one interaction is perturbed?
4. Do successive seasonal networks share a species set, so that the isomer claim has operands?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-50, :52-53, :73 and Fig 1 :19, :22, :27 and Fig 12 :117, :124. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 against their own system: do any two species change interaction sign between seasons or stages while remaining the same two species?
3. Check C2 against a dataset they know: is there a per-edge signal that the network-level index hides?
4. Name one measurement that would distinguish a first-order account from a cascade, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
