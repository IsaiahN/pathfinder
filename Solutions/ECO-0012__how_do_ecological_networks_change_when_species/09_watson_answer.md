## VERDICT (from the figures)

An ecological network is a Structure, and variation in season, life stage or conditions is an update of the habitat's contact field, read per interaction slot, expressed as a re-valencing of bonds over a shared species set, cascading beyond direct contact. Season-to-season networks are isomers; the checkable units are functional groups and persistent parts. The framework does not name which edge flips, in which season, or by how much. "Rewiring" is a provenanced binding from ecology onto the held bond vocabulary, not a new primitive. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest figure-grounded points are the per-slot rule (Fig 1 :19) and residual-relative valence triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 12.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a network-level summary as if it were the ground, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. Each finding is additive and no primitive is withdrawn. If a per-slot refuter fires, C1 is narrowed; if a species-set refuter fires, the isomer claim falls back to the functional-group claim. A sent packet can be superseded by the corrected one without touching the derivation.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An interaction-level measurement showing unchanged sign and per-capita strength across all conditions would break residual-relative valence. A system in which one global metric carries all live signal would break the per-slot rule. A strictly first-order cascade would break Fig 11 :52-53. A derivation of the network as a state from the other five terms, or a seventh substrate member needed for partner switching, would break the census. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
