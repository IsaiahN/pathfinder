**Mode:** `specified` on the shape of the change, `general` on the structural claim that a network is a Structure composed over states. Nothing here is `grounded`: no measurement settles the shape in this run.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this package.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. By the framework's own rule (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap: LLM output is *"simulacra"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"*), it cannot settle the ecological question it reports on. It returns a shape any answer must have, a pre-registered refuter, and the instruments that would close it. The empirical answer belongs to the ecologists measuring the system.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make agreement worthless (Seat Map :163, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention"*).
2. **Sending the source first spends independence.** Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework interior.
3. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* This package does not tell an ecologist which edge in their network will flip. It tells them what form the change must take and how to look for it.

Legally: public opt-in and institutional submission channels (file `03`) avoid the structural failure above and the GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style failure of unsolicited bulk mail.
