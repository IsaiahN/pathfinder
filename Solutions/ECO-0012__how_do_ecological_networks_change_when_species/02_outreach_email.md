**Subject:** Check one claim about seasonal network change before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about your system. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an ecological network is a Structure in the framework's six-term set, the one where entities relate and influence travels (Fig 13 :23-24). When interactions vary with season, life stage or conditions, what changes is the habitat, the field of contact. Fig 11 :73 says *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Fig 11 :49-50 says *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"* and *"mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* So the shape of the change is: edges are re-valued relative to a moving residual, the same species set can carry a different bond arrangement, the effect cascades beyond direct contacts, and the signal is live per interaction rather than in a single network mean.
>
> This is a **shape, not a result**. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* I am not telling you which edge in your network flips, in which month, or by how much. Only your data can do that.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that "rewiring" is a mechanism the framework discovered. It is a binding onto a relation the framework already holds. That the network-level index is the ground. It is a frame-internal summary. That agreeing papers corroborate the claim. Agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. In your system, each species pair keeps identical sign and per-capita strength across all seasons, stages and conditions, and all change is node turnover.
> 2. One global network metric carries all the live signal and no per-interaction slot does.
> 3. All change is strictly first order, with no measurable effect beyond direct contacts.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds in your data, the corresponding part of the shape is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line *this fails because...* is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Figures README (canonical usage rule): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author's essay warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and its three refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three source links. The figures are the authority, not the summary.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction to write a refuter and state a mode before running it.

A stage-2 or stage-3 reply is a conversation, never corroboration. Later agreement is not pooled as evidence (Fig 2 :40).
