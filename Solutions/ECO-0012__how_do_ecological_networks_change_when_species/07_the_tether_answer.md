# The Tether's answer: how ecological networks change when interactions vary

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A network observed under one set of conditions fails to predict the interactions observed under another. The failure is live at particular species pairs and conditions, not in one global number. Arity: three named axes (season, life stage, environment) that overlap, so a **union compound**, not three clean questions (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Scale: edge, network arrangement, and cascade several steps out.

## 2. The shape

1. **The varying thing is the habitat, and the network is a Structure composed over it.** Fig 13 :23-24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels."** Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."** Season, temperature, light and water are habitat conditions on the figure's own list (Fig 11 :46). So a network changing means the field of contact is re-read, not that one object drifting as a single thing.

2. **Change is per interaction, not one metric.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A network-level index is a frame-internal summary (Fig 1 :27), useful for description and disqualified as the anchor.

3. **The same pair can change kind without changing partners.** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** Triangulated by Fig 12 :46 and :95: the bond is not recoverable from the operands, so two networks with the same species list can be different substances. This is the figure's most direct answer to the question.

4. **The changed networks are isomers, with functional groups and catalysts as checkable units.** Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."** Fig 12 :124: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** A persistent pollinator or foundation species is a part that survives its own use (Fig 12 :119).

5. **The change cascades beyond direct contact.** Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** A first-order partner list is not an adequate account of how the network changed.

6. **Life stage changes the operand and the order of composition.** Fig 13 :12: **"the ordered succession of differences."** A stage-shifted organism is a different operand, and a settled stage-specific interaction can become an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104). The dependency order is conditions, relations, network, time, ground.

7. **The framework does not name the edge that flips.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Which pair, in which season, by how much, is owed from measurement. "Rewiring" is a binding onto held relation terms, not a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :14).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a seasonal time series for any real ecosystem. No data were taken in this run.
- Not that any particular network is stable or unstable, resilient or fragile. Those are ground verdicts.
- Not that the word "rewiring" is a mechanism discovered by the framework. It is an ecological binding with provenance.
- Not that a network-level metric is the ground. It is a frame-internal summary (Fig 1 :27).
- Not that season, stage and environment are independent questions. They are a union compound.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an interaction-level measurement.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A per-interaction, condition-resolved instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding checks are:

- **Sign and strength test:** does the same species pair keep identical sign and per-capita strength across seasons, stages and conditions? If yes, the valence-relativity claim (Fig 11 :49-50) is refuted for that system.
- **Per-slot test:** does one global network metric carry all the live signal, or is there a live per-interaction slot? (Fig 1 :19.)
- **Cascade test:** measure effects two and three steps out. If all change is first order, Fig 11 :52-53 is refuted.
- **Isomer test:** do successive seasonal networks share a species set? If not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands instead.
- **Standing falsifiers:** derive the network as a state from the other five terms, or show that partner switching needs a seventh substrate member.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is a visible guess at the compression axes, not nature's own list. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* This shape may be indiscernible from the ecological answer at the current resolution; that is not the same as being it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which edge in your network will flip this autumn. It says what any answer to that question must have: the network is a structure of relations over species and conditions, so what changes when season, stage or environment moves is the habitat's contact field; the change is read one interaction at a time, not in a single network average; the same pair can change from mutualism to parasitism without changing partners, because valence is relative to the residual; season-to-season networks are isomers over a shared species set; and the effect carries two or three steps beyond direct contact. The framework returns that shape, not a name, and it writes down in advance what would refute it: unchanged signs and strengths across all conditions, all signal in one global metric, no cascade, or no shared species set. Which edge actually flips is owed from your data, not from this derivation.
