**Subject:** Check one shape claim about how to distinguish Lambda from evolving dark energy

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the
> output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a cosmological constant and a changing dark
> energy component are currently metamers. They give the same integrated distance reading
> from different sources. The reading cannot comment on the source. So the observation that
> would most clearly distinguish them is not a tighter fit to the same curve. It is a
> measurement that makes the source register, and there are two structural routes:
>
> 1. **Differentiate the reading across epoch.** Measure the dark-energy density or equation
>    of state as a function of redshift, including the poorly constrained z greater than 0.5
>    window, so that the time variation itself is the signal. Report it as a free function
>    rho_DE(z) as well as in w0-wa, because the choice of representation changes the answer.
> 2. **Add a low-overlap second channel.** Geometry and growth are different responses to the
>    same source. Distance data carry one dominant information direction; growth measurements
>    (redshift-space distortions, weak lensing, clusters) can open a second one, but in
>    Euclid-like forecasts only once growth precision reaches the percent level.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a measurement, and not the name of a single mission. The
> current DESI DR2 hint of evolution (roughly 2.8 to 4.2 sigma depending on the supernova
> sample, and representation-dependent) is an edge to extend from, not a result.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any existing survey has settled the question; that a fixed
> significance threshold decides it; that a w not equal to -1 detection alone separates a
> changing fluid from modified gravity, interacting dark sectors, sound-horizon calibration,
> neutrino mass or systematics. Those are overlapping conditions, so one lever leaves the
> intersections standing. I am also not claiming that a specific telescope is "the" answer.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a systematics-free, single-epoch integrated distance measurement that conclusively
>    selects one hypothesis with no redshift differentiation and no second channel;
> 2. a proof that geometry and growth carry identical information about w(z) at every
>    achievable precision, so no second information direction can ever open;
> 3. a demonstration that the apparent signal is entirely a shared systematic of the SN,
>    BAO and CMB pipelines, with none left in an independent channel;
> 4. a figure re-census that actually names an instrument or threshold as the discriminator.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather
> not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of
> them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - DESI DR2 public results guide: https://www.desi.lbl.gov/2025/03/19/desi-dr2-results-march-19-guide/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a
> problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your
> result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
