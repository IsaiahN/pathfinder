## DERIVATION

### Q1. What is the structure of a forecast under feedback?

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands."
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

An accuracy report records the ingredients: the forecast F and the outcome o'. It does not record the bond between them. Two different bonds give the same two ingredients and different substances:

1. `F + o'` symmetric conjunction: the forecast and the outcome both hold, with no dependency. This is the flat reading an accuracy report implies when it treats the forecast as a passive description.
2. `F → a → o'` ordered sequence: publication a, employed by agents, produces the observation. This is the performative reading.

Because the bond is invisible in the ingredient list, nothing in an accuracy report signals which case occurred. Fig 12 :5 says exactly this: the arrangement is the half nothing was recording. The practical consequence is that "the forecast was accurate" is an under-specified statement until the bond is named.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if an accuracy report can be shown to determine the bond from the ingredients alone, so that no arrangement is hidden, the structure claim fails.

### Q2. What order is the cycle, and what does the sign do?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

The loop is ordered: forecast → employment → changed behavior → outcome → revised forecast. A chain cannot close by construction, so a system that cannot represent a cycle cannot represent performativity at all (Fig 12 :121). The sign is the decisive quantity:

| sign | name | mechanism | world source |
|---|---|---|---|
| positive | self-fulfilling | employment induces the behavior that realizes the forecast | Biggs 2009; King and Mertens 2023; Petalas et al. 2017 |
| negative | self-defeating | employment induces avoidance, so the forecast is falsified | King and Mertens 2023 (hotspot displacement) |

Because the sign lives on a directed cycle, a symmetric correlation between "forecast published" and "outcome observed" cannot recover it (Operators table on + versus →; Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."*). This is why a meta-analysis of forecast accuracy cannot by itself settle CMP-0034.

- **Falsifier:** show self-fulfilling and self-defeating outcomes from the same loop sign over the same atoms, so sign does not decide closure.

### Q3. Are self-fulfilling and self-defeating isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Self-fulfilling and self-defeating forecasts share the same atoms: a forecast, an employment act, agents, and an outcome. They differ in the sign of the cycle bond. Over that fixed atom set they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the accuracy report and decisive in the behavior (Fig 12 :117). This is a real isomer claim, not an analogy, because the figure's test is about arrangement and both cases are compositions over the same held atoms.

The **fixed-atom condition is load-bearing**. If agents in one case can act only because they hold an atom unavailable in the other, for example a counter-measure instrument imported from a different closure, the two cases are no longer isomers; they are a composition plus an import (Fig 6 :23). The condition is named, not hidden.

King and Mertens's **substantive versus interpretive employment-sensitivity** is a different structural distinction. Both cases share the same functional group, forecast → employment → outcome, while the qualifier says *where the bond attaches*: to the object of prediction (substantive) or to the measure of the object (interpretive) (Fig 12 :123). This is a **functional group**, not an isomer, because the arrangement kind is the same and only the attachment site differs. The distinction matters: an interpretively sensitive outcome is exactly the case where the anchor has become a frame (Fig 2 :47 "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame.").

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a self-fulfilling and a self-defeating prophecy over the same atom set with the same loop sign and the same attachment site. Both the isomer claim and the functional-group claim would fail.

### Q4. What is the order of the union of D1, D2 and D3?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

The three domains are a union with an unassignable overlap, so one lever leaves the intersections standing. Dependency order: anchor first (Fig 2 :29), then vocabulary for the intervention (Fig 11 :61), then the objective "reliable" (Fig 3 :9-11), then verification (Fig 10 :52). Each first-order loop must be settled before it can serve as an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104). A forecaster who optimizes model accuracy before establishing a non-updating anchor is working above the break (Fig 3 :19).

### Q5. What is the price in the two currencies?

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

Describing the feedback structure is cheap: a typed bond and a sign. Establishing whether the bond actually held in a given case costs actions: a held-out measure, an independent frame, a blind verdict. The two prices are in different units and do not add (Fig 12 :127). A system holding only the description accounting will propose reliability claims it cannot afford to test, and the budget decides how much of the space is reached. The practical form is: a short theoretical separator over a costly held-out ground is cheap to state and may take a whole study to settle; a long list of accuracy statistics is expensive to describe and may cost one discriminator to refute (Fig 12 :128-129).

## CONCLUSION

A forecast under feedback is a composition whose bond is invisible in an accuracy report (Q1). The loop is ordered and its sign decides closure, so correlation cannot choose between self-fulfilling and self-defeating (Q2). Over a fixed atom set those two are isomers, while substantive and interpretive sensitivity form a functional group whose qualifier is the attachment site (Q3). The three domains form a union ordered by dependency, with the anchor link first (Q4), and the description price and the action price must be held apart (Q5). Every claim is `specified` and ungrounded; no measurement was taken.
