# The Tether's answer: can a forecast stay reliable when it changes its subject?

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal reliability guarantee or numerical bound. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A forecast Γ(b,a) is scored against an observation o' that, after publication, its own employment helps to produce. R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|, indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19). The question is not one question. It is a union of three conditions (Fig 9 :55): can the regularity be predicted at all, does publication perturb it, and can a verifier read an outcome the forecast did not shape? A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57), so verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a single "forecasts are reliable / unreliable."

## 2. The separator, per domain

### D1. The forecasting frame: `specified`, triangulated

1. **The outcome must remain an anchor.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." When publication moves o', the outcome updates on the forecast, so by Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor" and the system is Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." The forecast can look accurate because it made the outcome. This is the structural content of Goodhart's law, the Lucas critique and the self-fulfilling-prophecy literature, all of which corroborate but do not decide it.
2. **The predictor cannot certify its own reliability.** Fig 1 :27 "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A reliability claim needs a verifier that did not author the forecast and a ground the forecast did not shape.
3. **The residual is per slot.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A published forecast can be right on average and wrong for the group whose behavior it changed. Petalas et al. (2017) found exactly this: positive and negative forecasts moved risk taking in opposite directions even though the underlying probabilities never changed.

### D2. The social habitat: `specified`, triangulated

4. **Publishing is an introduction, not a description.** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :43-44 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." The forecast becomes an actor in the agents' world with a cascade several steps out. Reliability is therefore a property of forecast-and-habitat contact, and Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A more accurate model that nobody acts on changes nothing about reliability.
5. **The loop has a sign.** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Positive sign gives self-fulfilling, negative sign self-defeating. Because the loop is directed, a symmetric correlation cannot choose the sign. Over the shared atom set the two are isomers (Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour.").
6. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Publication helps one residual and harms another; there is no standing list of forecasting effects that survive a change of question.

### D3. The verification frame: `specified`, with one `single-source` rule

7. **Independence decides whether agreement means anything.** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A crowd of forecasters all reading the same forecast is one long room: Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."
8. **The broken link is the objective link.** Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Agents in the Petalas et al. experiment had no instrument that could separate a real change in the world from a change in their own reading of the forecast. Work on model accuracy is effort above the break.
9. **Bring the residual, never the source.** Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Record the verifier's verdict before showing the model, and never pool later agreement as corroboration.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that forecasts are always unreliable under feedback. The verdict is conditional, and it is per domain.
- Not a reliability coefficient, a horizon, a publication rule, or a winning forecasting method. No figure line returns any of these. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."), a verdict and not a silence.
- Not that Goodhart's law, Campbell's law, the Lucas critique or reflexivity theory decide the question. They are secondary corroboration, and they share one measurement-and-policy evidence pool.
- Not a derivation of agent behaviour from the six-term substrate. Agents and forecasts are terms in Γ and actors in the habitat, not new members of the six.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried) says a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Anchor test:** is there an outcome series the forecast could not have moved, measured by an instrument independent of the forecast-driven behavior? If the only outcome is forecast-driven (arrests in a predicted hotspot, grades after a predicted "bloom"), the anchor has become a frame.
2. **Sign test:** does the loop close positive (self-fulfilling) or negative (self-defeating), and can the two be produced with the same sign over the same atoms? If they can, the isomer claim in `06` fails.
3. **Per-slot test:** does the residual stay small on every affected slot, or only in aggregate? Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
4. **Discriminator test:** can an observer tell "the world changed" from "the forecast changed my reading of the world"? Petalas et al. (2017) found that participants could not. Building that instrument is the owed step.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §5: a performative forecast whose residual stays small and non-increasing against a non-updating ground across independent frames; a measure that updates on the forecast and still settles the question; a single frame that certifies its own reliability; self-fulfilling and self-defeating outcomes from the same loop sign.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The separator stops at the limit of the available discriminating instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The canonical README calls the substrate *"a human's careful guess"* and insists on *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* We can say what a reliable forecast under feedback would have to look like; we cannot say which real forecast is reliable in a given case until a held-out ground is read. One live strain remains (S1 in `01` §8): in much of forecasting practice the "outcome" is defined by a forecast-shaped measure, which is precisely why the discriminator is owed and why the gap is honest.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether forecasts survive feedback. It returns a separator. A forecast stays reliable only while its outcome is an anchor that does not update on what the forecast thinks, the error is tracked per slot instead of averaged, publication is treated as introducing an actor into the agents' habitat with a cascade, and a verifier who never saw the model tests it against a held-out measure with independent frames. When publication couples the forecast to the outcome, the ordinary accuracy claim is a coherent drift: the forecast looks right because it helped make the outcome, and no error signal fires. Self-fulfilling and self-defeating are the two signs of one cycle, so a correlation cannot tell them apart. The framework does not return a reliability number, a horizon or a winning method; that is a declared gap, and what is owed is a discriminator instrument and a falsifier written in advance. Until a ground settles it, the result is a `specified` shape, not a verdict.
