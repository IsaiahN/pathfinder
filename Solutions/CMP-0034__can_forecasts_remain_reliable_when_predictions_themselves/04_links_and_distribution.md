## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on the claim

1. **Interpretive sensitivity is the anchor failure in practice.** King and Mertens (2023) show that a predictive-policing forecast can be "fulfilled" because more patrols record more of the same behavior, without any change in background crime. This is Fig 2 :47 ("A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame.") in a concrete institutional setting. It is the strongest single reason to ask what measure registers the outcome before calling a forecast reliable.
2. **No error signal fires under a self-fulfilling prophecy.** Same source: truth shrouds the mistake from humans and machines alike. Biggs (2009) says the actors assume the belief had been true at the outset. Together these are an independent corroboration of Fig 2 :34 coherent drift, and a warning that accuracy tracking alone cannot detect the failure.
3. **The failure is per slot and persisted.** Petalas et al. (2017) found opposite shifts in risk taking after positive and negative forecasts, lower earnings after negative forecasts, and no change in the true probabilities, and the effect persisted across the 60-trial block. This is the per-slot residual of Fig 1 :19 with the missing instrument of Fig 3 :27.

- IJF enforces mandatory data and code sharing and a reproducibility check on acceptance, and uses double-blind review. A simulated derivation with no measurement must say so on submission; it should not be presented as an empirical result.
