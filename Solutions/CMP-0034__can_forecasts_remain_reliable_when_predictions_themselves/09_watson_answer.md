## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a forecast can remain reliable when its publication alters behavior and thereby the outcome it is scored against. Option A: reliability is robust to behavioral response, so accuracy alone is the right test. Option B: reliability survives only under a separator that keeps the outcome an anchor the forecast does not move, keeps the residual per slot, treats the forecast as an introduced actor, and verifies blind against a held-out measure with independent frames. What each leads to: A delivers a coherence drift as an accuracy verdict and spends the independence of the people checking it; B delivers a checkable separator with a pre-registered refuter and an open gap where a discriminator instrument is owed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|, per slot s. Arity: three operands, the forecast frame, the agents' employment, and the ground. The question is a **union** of three conditions: is there a regularity to predict, does publication perturb it, and can the verifier read an outcome the forecast did not shape (Fig 9 :55). Symmetry: the coupling is a directed cycle, not a symmetric join; positive sign is self-fulfilling, negative sign self-defeating (Fig 12 :121). Scale: the forecast is a compact model at one scale, agents act at the system scale, and publication crosses the membrane between observation and intervention (Fig 4 :22).
- **Domain 1, the forecasting frame.** Actors: model Γ, belief b, forecast F, analyst. Currency: residual R. Anchor: the ground, which must not update on the forecast (Fig 2 :29-30). Under performativity this anchor fails, so the reliability verdict cannot be naive accuracy. Question type: partition boundary. Dependency: anchor first.
- **Domain 2, the social habitat.** Actors: agents, their actions, the forecast now an introduced actor. Currency: agents' payoffs and behavior. Anchor: real consequences measured without a forecast-driven measure, if such a measure exists. Publishing introduces an actor with a cascade and residual-relative valence (Fig 11 :43-44, :49, :61). Question type: enumerated habitat, union. Dependency: after the anchor.
- **Domain 3, the verification frame.** Actors: evaluator, claim, ground, second frame. Currency: evidence and reliability verdict. Anchor: a held-out outcome that does not share the predictor's evidence pool (Fig 2 :31-32). Question type: triangulation, not negotiation. Dependency: last.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures do not return a bare yes or no. They return a separator. A forecast remains reliable only while its outcome stays an anchor that does not update on the forecast (Fig 2 :29-30, :33-34), the residual is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), publication is treated as introducing an actor into the habitat with a cascade (Fig 11 :43-44, :61), and verification is blind to the model and uses a held-out measure with independent frames (Fig 10 :42, Fig 8 :48). When publication couples forecast and outcome, the ordinary accuracy claim is a coherent drift in which the forecast looks right because it helped make the outcome, and no error signal fires. Self-fulfilling and self-defeating are the two signs of one cycle, so a pooled correlation cannot distinguish them. No universal reliability guarantee, coefficient, horizon or winning method is returned. That is a declared `gap`, a verdict and not silence. Mode `specified` on the separator, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the guarantee, never `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The separator is mode-labeled, states five refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is treating a coherent drift as corroboration and telling a decision maker the forecast is reliable when it manufactured its own outcome. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the anchor test and the held-out ground.
- **undoable:** yes. If a case fails the anchor test, the reliability claim is withdrawn without touching the separator; no primitive has to be withdrawn. If a discriminator instrument is built, the gap narrows and the per-slot verdicts can be re-run on real grounds.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A held-out outcome series the forecast could not have moved, measured independently of the forecast-driven behavior, would settle the anchor question; an instrument that separates a real change in the world from a forecast-induced change in the reading would close the discriminator gap; a demonstration that a performative forecast keeps a small, non-increasing per-slot residual across independent frames would break F1 and reverse D1; a demonstration that self-fulfilling and self-defeating outcomes share a loop sign over the same atoms would break the isomer claim in D2. Prestige, a large forecaster panel and later agreement are not such instruments.
