# Solution CMP-0034: figure-grounded package

**Question:** Can forecasts remain reliable when predictions themselves alter people's behavior and thereby change the system?

**Source:** `Questions/complexity science.txt` line 43 (question 34 in section 4).

**Section:** 4. Prediction, uncertainty, and causal inference.

**Mode:** `specified` on the separator and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on any universal reliability guarantee or numerical bound. Nothing here is `grounded`. Every substantive file is labelled a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending (set by the registry pass, not by this folder).

**Field for public contact points:** forecasting and social systems research.

## What this package decides, in effect terms

A forecast is a prediction Γ(b,a) made from a library and a belief about a system. When the forecast is published, agents in the system employ it, change their actions, and thereby change the observation o' against which the forecast is scored. The residual R = |Γ(b,a) − o'| then contains a term the forecast itself produced. The question asks whether R can stay small and non-increasing under that coupling.

The figures do not return a bare yes or no. They return a **separator**: reliability survives only while the outcome stays an anchor that does not update on what the forecast thinks, the residual is indexed per slot, the forecast is treated as an actor introduced into the habitat with a cascading valence, and verification uses a held-out measure the forecast did not shape plus independent frames. Where publication couples forecast and outcome, the ordinary reliability claim is Fig 2's collapse 1 (coherent drift), and the forecast looks accurate precisely because it produced the outcome (no error signal, Fig 12's cycle). Self-fulfilling and self-defeating are the two signs of one cycle, and a pooled correlation cannot choose the sign. No figure line returns a universal guarantee, a reliability coefficient, a horizon, or a named winning method. That is a declared `gap`, which is a verdict and not a silence.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that forecasts fail under feedback.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons: Fig 2 :35 (*"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*), Fig 2 :40 (*"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*), and Fig 10 :42 (*"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless. Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions.
