**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survives the twenty-minute check. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about forecast accuracy when people act on the forecast

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a published forecast makes people change their behavior, the outcome used to score the forecast is no longer independent of it. The framework's ground rule says an anchor is whatever settles a question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks, and that the anchor must not update. If the outcome updates on the forecast, the forecast and the outcome drift together, and because they agree nobody notices. The forecast can look accurate because it helped make the outcome.
>
> What survives is a separator, not a verdict. A forecast stays reliable only while (1) the outcome is measured by something the forecast could not have moved, (2) the error is tracked per slot instead of averaged, (3) publication is treated as introducing an actor into the agents' habitat with a cascade, and (4) a verifier who never saw the model checks it against a held-out measure with independent frames.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that forecasts are always unreliable under feedback. That there is a universal reliability number, horizon or winning method. That Goodhart's law, Campbell's law, the Lucas critique or reflexivity theory settle the question; they corroborate the shape but share one evidence pool. I am also not claiming this derivation is a measurement. It is a simulation checked for internal consistency.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a forecast whose employment demonstrably changed the outcome, yet whose per-slot error against a ground that does not read the forecast stays small and non-increasing across two independent frames;
> 2. a measure that updates on the forecast and still settles the reliability question;
> 3. a single frame that certifies its own reliability against an outcome its employment produced;
> 4. self-fulfilling and self-defeating outcomes from the same loop sign over the same atoms.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Questions I would value your view on:** who employs the forecast in a case you know, what measure registers the outcome, and is that measure substantively sensitive (the agents really changed) or interpretively sensitive (the same behavior was counted differently)? If it is interpretive, the outcome has become part of the forecast's own frame.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README and canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures folder: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a forecasting problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
