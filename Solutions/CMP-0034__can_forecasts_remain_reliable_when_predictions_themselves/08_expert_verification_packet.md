# Expert verification packet: the Tether on performative forecasts

**What this is:** a claim in a form a forecaster, social-systems researcher or measurement methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a universal reliability guarantee. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A forecast does not stay reliable merely by being accurate. When publication makes agents act on it, the outcome it is scored against becomes coupled to the forecast. The structural condition for reliability is a separator with four parts: an outcome anchor that does not update on the forecast, a residual indexed per slot, the forecast treated as an actor introduced into the habitat with a cascade, and verification that is blind to the model and uses a held-out measure with independent frames. When that separator fails, the accuracy report is a coherent drift in which the forecast looks right because it helped make the outcome, and no error signal fires. Self-fulfilling and self-defeating are the two signs of one cycle, so a pooled correlation cannot distinguish them. No figure returns a reliability coefficient, horizon or winning method; that is a declared gap and what is owed is a discriminator instrument plus a falsifier written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** if a forecast moves the outcome used to score it, that outcome is not an anchor, and the resulting agreement is coherent drift rather than evidence of reliability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
- **Refuter:** show a forecast whose employment demonstrably moved the outcome, yet whose per-slot residual against a ground that does not read the forecast stays small and non-increasing across at least two independent evidence frames. If that holds, C1 fails.
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 collapse geometry plus Fig 10 channel office, low overlap). World corroboration from Goodhart 1975, Biggs 2009 and King and Mertens 2023, which share one measurement-and-prophecy pool.

### C2: world-facing, checkable by a forecaster or social-systems researcher

- **Statement:** the feedback is an ordered cycle with a sign; self-fulfilling and self-defeating forecasts are isomers over the same atom set and cannot be distinguished by a symmetric correlation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **What to check:** in a real case, can you name the employment step between forecast and outcome? Is the outcome measured by the behavior the forecast changed (interpretive sensitivity, King and Mertens 2023) or independently (substantive)? Petalas et al. (2017, PLOS ONE) found that positive and negative economic forecasts shifted risk taking in opposite directions even though the true probabilities never changed, and that participants did not notice the absence of change.
- **Refuter:** exhibit self-fulfilling and self-defeating outcomes from the same loop sign over the same atoms and the same attachment site, or show that publication changed no action and no measure. In the second case C2 simply does not apply.
- **Status:** `single-source` for the figure line (Fig 12), with low-overlap world corroboration from philosophy of prediction and experimental psychology. The two world streams share the Mertonian prophecy literature, so they count as one witness. The fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim is a live strain, named in `06` Q3.

### C3: measurement-facing, checkable by a verification researcher

- **Statement:** reliability must be indexed per slot, verified against a held-out ground the forecast could not shape, and checked by a verifier who did not see the model and does not share the predictor's evidence pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :27 "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 8 :48 "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."
- **What to check:** does the reported accuracy average across the very subpopulation whose behavior changed? Is the outcome series forecast-driven? Did the verifier see the model before recording the verdict? Were the two frames genuinely independent?
- **Refuter:** show that per-slot indexing changes no verdict in a published performative case, or that a verifier sharing the predictor's evidence pool carries the same weight as an independent held-out verifier.
- **Status:** `single-source` for verifier non-access (Fig 10), `triangulated` for independence (Fig 2 + Fig 8).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that forecasts are always unreliable under feedback. The verdict is conditional and per domain.
- Not a reliability coefficient, horizon, publication rule or winning method. That is a `gap`, reported as a verdict and not as silence.
- Not that Goodhart's law, Campbell's law, the Lucas critique or reflexivity theory decide the question. They are secondary corroboration from one evidence pool.
- Not a derivation of agent behavior from Fig 13's six-term substrate. Agents and forecasts are terms in Γ and actors in the habitat.
- Not `grounded`. This packet contains no measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument.

1. **Anchor test:** an outcome series the forecast could not have moved, measured independently of the forecast-driven behavior.
2. **Sign test:** does the loop close positive or negative, and can the two signs occur over the same atoms?
3. **Discriminator test:** can an observer tell "the world changed" from "the forecast changed the reading"? Petalas et al. (2017) found that participants could not. Building that instrument is the owed step.
4. **Per-slot test:** is the residual small on every affected slot or only in aggregate?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30 and :33-34, Fig 12 :121 and :117, Fig 1 :19 and :27, Fig 10 :42, Fig 11 :61. Eight short passages.
2. Name the employment step in a forecasting case you know: who acts, on what, and what measure registers the outcome?
3. Is that measure substantively or interpretively sensitive (King and Mertens 2023)? If interpretive, the anchor has become a frame.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a real change in the ground from a forecast-induced change in the reading, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement is not pooled as corroboration.
