## Q1: Is "understanding" one of the six? CORRECTION TO A FIRST PASS

A first pass tried to bind "understanding" to the network atom, because the network is "that entities relate, and influence travels" (Fig 13 :23-24). That mapping is **retracted**. Fig 13's six are a physical minimum set, load-bearing in the established laws (Fig 13 :48-71); they are not vocabulary slots for cognitive labels. Re-census: at the machine stratum the atoms are the contents of `Γ`, the bonds are the typed relations among them, and the anchor is the ground. "Understanding" is a label for a relation between a frame and that anchor (Fig 11 :36), not a member of the six. Fig 4 :20 governs the crossing: only actors, currency and anchor change. Kept visible here rather than erased.

**Mode:** `general`. **Falsifier:** derive a seventh physical term from the other five, or show that a cognitive label is load-bearing in an established physical law. Neither was done.

## Q2: Are U and P isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

Fix the atom set: the same weights, the same corpus, the same architecture, the same prompt. Then U differs from P only in the bond between the system's prediction and the observation: U has a live ordered bond to an observation from a non-updating ground, P has a bond to a substituted harness or only to the system's own previous output. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is a real isomer claim, and it is `specified` and unmeasured.

The weaker reading fails the definition. If "U" and "P" are allowed different weights, corpora or architectures, they share no fixed atom set and are two members of a **functional group**, not isomers. Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Most real comparisons between two different models are functional-group comparisons, not isomer comparisons.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two systems over a demonstrably identical atom set show identical behavior against a non-updating ground but differ in whether they carry a live import from a differing closure, the isomer claim fails; the difference must then be in the atoms or in the record, not the bond. Equally, if a bond to a ground can be recovered from the operands alone, F4 of `01` fails.

## Q3: What order does the chain have?

Order is not a property of the six; it enters with composition (Fig 12 :111-113). The relevant ordered bonds are:

- `→` sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :70-72).
- `⇒` production: "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :75-77).
- `−` subtraction: "A with B removed or absent" (Fig 12 :80-82).
- `⋛` comparison: "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :85-87).

The contact chain for a discriminator is ordered as follows. Refusal precedes mint (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 bins REFUTED as a term held that is false, where "what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"). Import moves the wall (Fig 6 :23), with provenance and shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52). Settlement follows acceptance, and promotion carries a round-trip gap `R_T` (Fig 4 :30). Reversing this order is precisely the failure the verification packet is designed to expose: a benchmark named first and a residual described afterwards is an authored room (Fig 10 :73-74).

Fig 3 adds the dependency order inside a single frame: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The middle link is the objective, and Fig 3 :25-26 says it "has neither" obvious failures nor obvious tests. A system that can name its inputs but cannot say what would count as progress is broken at link 3. That is a structural statement about P, not a claim about any named model.

**Falsifier:** show that a frame can settle a comparison against a ground before it has a term for what counts as progress, with the objective measured rather than assumed. That would break the Fig 3 dependency order for this case.

## Q4: What does a flat vocabulary fail to write?

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

This is the structural form of the F6 finding. Structure processing with only a `+` bond can record that ingredients are present. It cannot write a route (`→`), a threshold goal (`⋛`), progress (`−`), or a refusal (`¬`). So a system whose library has no negation operator can be corrected but cannot carry the correction: it can report that a claim stopped paying, not that it is wrong. A verifier used to check U must therefore hold the unary operator; otherwise its record can only accumulate. That is why the packet in `08` asks for a failure, not an endorsement.

## Q5: The two currencies do not add

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A description of a discriminator can be very cheap in bits (a paragraph of effect terms) and extremely expensive in actions (building the instrument that discriminates source). Conversely a short arrangement over a costly operand might be cheap to state and take a whole budget to settle. Fig 12 :128: "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."

The practical consequence for INF-0040: a paper that proposes a clever test has paid in bits only. The question asks what would count as evidence, and evidence requires the action bill to be paid. Summing the two into one "progress" number is the error Fig 12 :129 names, not the fix.

## CONCLUSION

Under a fixed atom set, U and P are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond to an observation source, different substance, with the difference invisible in the outputs and decisive in the contact behavior. Under the weaker comparison of two different systems they are a **functional group**, not isomers. The first-pass attempt to bind "understanding" to a physical substrate atom is retracted; the six remain a physical minimum set, and at the machine stratum only actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :20).

The contact chain is ordered: refuse before mint, describe the residual before naming a frame, import with provenance and shadow test, settle, then carry up with a measured `R_T`. A flat vocabulary can name the state and cannot name the route, the goal, the progress or the refusal. The two currencies of a discriminator, description bits and settling actions, do not add. Every one of these claims is structural or `specified`, none is `grounded`, and each carries the falsifier stated above.
