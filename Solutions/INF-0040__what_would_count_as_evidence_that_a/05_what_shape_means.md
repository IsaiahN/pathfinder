## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

A **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is the set of conditions any discriminator must satisfy: per-slot residual, a non-updating anchor, a reproduced habitat, independent frames, a bond that is not recoverable from the operands, and a verifier who cannot reconstruct the claim. The name of a specific benchmark, threshold, or architecture is not the shape.

*The Library of Babel* states the stopping rule: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." The same essay warns that the searcher's vocabulary is the real limit: "Your ignorance, of not knowing what you don't know, is the actual obstacle between you and the answers, not the vastness of the library." For INF-0040 the scarce vocabulary is not "understanding" but "source": the question asks for a source discrimination that current measures do not perform.

Fig 13 :135 gives the limit in the framework's own words: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The missing thing here is an instrument, not a primitive, but the same rule applies: characterize the effect, do not invent the name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on proximity: "use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of," then track "recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it." With each new term "you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Applied here: start from "residual" and "ground" rather than from "understanding," because the first two are measurable and the last is the term under audit.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So instead of asking "does it understand?", ask "what would we observe if contact were maintained versus absent, per slot?"
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is the exact rule for the outreach in `02`: send the expected observation, not the theory that produced it.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place," and the paradox is that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it." Here the shape and the residual bind to each other in section 0 of `01`: once D is written down as an ordinary term, the search is steered by it.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest." For this question, a name would be licensed by an instrument that actually separates source from output in a given case.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this (quote carried from the exemplar, not re-opened): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." So a proposed benchmark is a visible choice, not a discovery of nature's own discriminator. Naming it as *the* test turns a choice into an invisible one.

## 4. The source problem is this question's metamerism

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened directly: "What if you have metrics that do not discriminate about its inputs?" and "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The essay's green-light example maps exactly onto INF-0040: the reading "green" does not say whether the source was one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. Likewise a fluent answer does not say whether the source was ground contact or internal composition.

The decisive sentence: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." It was "proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." That is the shape of what is owed here.

*How Intelligence Actually Works*, opened directly, applies the same structure to artifacts: "The projection of green can't tell you whether it was RGB, CMYK or something else that composed it." And on provenance loss: "the frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which." A transcript of a machine's answer is such a frame: it carries the output, not the route that produced it.

The same essay supplies the question's own vocabulary: "The test that environment applies is what we call fitness." And: "the answer doesn't exist until the question is contacted." Contact, not fluency, resolves the distinction.

## 5. Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a discriminator?

**Answer: no, and the gap still stands.** The README's phrase governs "what a **missing** thing would have to look like." A specific discriminator, if it already existed in the verifier's library, could be named as a provenanced binding, just as the exemplar narrowed Gradient and Spectrum to bindings onto held atoms. What the framework forbids is inventing a seventh primitive or claiming a binding moves the wall when it adds no atom (Fig 6 :22).

Here the discriminator is not held in `Γ`. It is an instrument that does not yet exist in the reported practice, so naming one would be inventing the thing the framework says it can only characterize. Hence:

- A **shape** of the discriminator: allowed and derived (section 1).
- A **named existing benchmark** asserted to be the discriminator: a binding needing a source gate and a shadow test, and not supplied in `01`.
- A **new invented test** claimed to settle the question: forbidden by Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :135, and not done here.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the practical version: "Sounding right isn't the same as being checked against the world, which is why I call the Turing test a test of fluency, not of accuracy." So a named test can be admitted as a binding, but its agreement is a convention about meanings, not a verdict about the world (Fig 2 :41).

## 6. Correction to my own sections 3 and 4

I initially read "shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on naming any test. That overstates it. The ban governs a missing primitive, not a binding onto something already held. What remains wrong in any specific test claim is not that it is named, but that its source is named from a reading where several sources could give the same reading, with no instrument that discriminates among them. Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost. This correction matches the exemplar's own `05` section 5b, which narrowed the same rule for Gradient and Spectrum.

## 7. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened directly: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous endorsements for a machine-understanding claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets a ground, not a audience, settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any machine and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
