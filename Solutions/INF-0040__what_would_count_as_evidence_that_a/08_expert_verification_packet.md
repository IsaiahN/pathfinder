# Expert verification packet: the Tether on machine understanding

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. Send this one, one-to-one, after stage 1 of `02_outreach_email.md`.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the discriminator. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of any machine is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Understanding, if the word is to do work, is maintained per-slot contact between a system and a habitat whose ground does not update on the system's own output. It is a property of agent and habitat together, not of the agent alone. Over a fixed atom set, a system in such contact and one processing only its internal structure are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the outputs and decisive in the contact behavior. A pooled score cannot show this because a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state; a synthetic harness cannot show it because it substitutes a habitat; a self-report cannot show it because a checker that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it. The specific instrument that would separate the two is a declared `gap`, characterized in effect terms and not named. The observer sense of the question (is this a good answer for a person?) and the contact sense (does the system maintain ground contact?) have different anchors and are kept as two verdicts, never averaged.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 11

- **Statement:** the question's noun is a label applied after contact, and the answerable question is capability as agent-and-habitat contact, indexed per slot. A pooled score can hide a live slot.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."
  - Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
  - Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
  - Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"
  - Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **What to check:** are these four passages present and do they license the re-framing? Is there a figure line that makes understanding a one-place property of the machine? If so, C1 is false.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that capability is a property of the system alone, surviving a habitat substitution that removes every goal-required contact with no reproduction; or a pooled score shown to track per-slot contact with no hidden live slot.

### C2: bond claim, checkable by Figure 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** over a fixed atom set the two states are isomers, not an ingredient difference. The bond to an observation source is not recoverable from the operands, so no reading of the output or the internal structure alone can decide the question.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
  - Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
  - Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
  - Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"
- **What to check:** is the fixed-atom condition satisfied in the case being discussed? Two different models with different weights are a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), not isomers; the isomer claim applies only over the same atom set. Does a vocabulary limited to symmetric conjunction fail to write a goal, progress and refusal, as claimed?
- **Refuter:** two systems over a demonstrably identical atom set showing identical behavior against a non-updating ground while differing only in whether they carry a live import from a differing closure; or a bond to a ground recovered from the operands alone. Either breaks the isomer claim.

### C3: verification-arrangement claim, checkable by Figures 9, 10 and 11

- **Statement:** harnesses are substituted habitats, so synthetic solves prove wiring and never capability; the verifier must be outside the slice being graded and must hold a negation operator; maker evidence is denied to the verifier.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
  - Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
  - Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"
  - Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
  - Fig 9 :51: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"
  - Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** is there a benchmark result in the case at hand that fails one of the two silence tests? Can the proposed verifier write a refusal, or only a score?
- **Refuter:** a harness shown to reproduce every contact the goal required and inherit no actor the real habitat lacked, demonstrated by moving the goal and observing the same failures in the same order; or a self-report shown to be a non-updating anchor that does not track training or incentives.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular machine understands or does not understand. No machine was tested here.
- Not that fluent answers are worthless. Under the observer sense, human judgment is the legitimate anchor, because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38). That verdict is separate.
- Not that a benchmark score is evidence of capability. It is a budget filter (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a discriminator has been found. No figure line names one; the `gap` is declared (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not a theory of consciousness or of subjective experience.
- Not `grounded`. Status is `candidate` until an instrument pays.
- Not that this simulated derivation can settle the question it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." The requirements are: per-slot residual (Fig 1 :19); an anchor that does not move when pushed (Fig 2 :30); a reproduced habitat with its silent failures declared (Fig 11 :66-69); independent frames ranging separately (Fig 2 :31-32); a verifier able to refuse (Operators :58); and a preserved lineage (Fig 8 :50-52).

The five pre-registered refuters and the named-strain refuter are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5, written before the conclusion. Any one firing requires a retraction.
