**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold.

---

**Subject:** Check one condition set on instruments and objectivity before you decide about the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`):** the question "when do instruments increase objectivity, and when do they create new dependence?" is not one threshold. It is a union of at least six overlapping conditions with different anchors. The condition set I derived is:
>
> 1. **Anchor.** An instrument raises objectivity while it is ranged against something that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. It stops doing so the moment it becomes the reference itself. The relevant line is: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it... It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." So the same device can be the anchor for a question about its own scale and merely an instrument for a question about the world.
> 2. **Independence.** Two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames; two people reading the same report are one. Objectivity increases when readings are independent, and not when they share a calibration chain or a reduction pipeline.
> 3. **Perception.** An instrument adds no primitive to the world, it changes what can be represented. It helps when it makes a previously unread residual readable.
> 4. **Channel.** The ground does not decay; the channel does. Stale calibration, saturated metrics and pooled readings sever contact while the ground stays put. The output then is not a false verdict, it is "you have lost touch."
> 5. **Source.** A reading can underdetermine its source. Several causes can produce the same reading; an instrument that cannot discriminate among them demonstrates its own inadequacy. What is owed is a discriminator, not a stronger claim.
> 6. **Habitat.** A test harness is a substituted habitat. It proves wiring, not capability. What you failed to reproduce and what you brought with you are both invisible until the goal fails.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a single crossover point, a universal percentage of instrumentation, or that instruments are objectivity itself. Each condition has a per-slot verdict and its own anchor, and I keep them separate rather than averaging them. Where no figure line returns the threshold, I record a `gap`, not a guess.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an anchor that changes its answer when a frame pushes on it and still increases objectivity;
> 2. an objectivity gain from a reading that draws on exactly the same evidence as an existing reading, with no new ground contact;
> 3. a channel that has lost contact with the ground while its own output still settles a verdict on that ground;
> 4. a convention that decides an outcome and still functions as a convention the ground can refuse;
> 5. a substituted habitat that preserves all the contact the goal required and introduces no new actor.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding condition is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure extract with line numbers (local canonical file): `FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a measurement problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
