## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about inserting an instrument between a knower and a ground. Option A: instruments increase objectivity because they remove human judgment and add readings. Option B: instruments create new dependence because they become frames, channels and substituted habitats. What each leads to: A sends a count-based rule that cannot distinguish independence from shared evidence and would treat a decision-fixing convention as objectivity; B is too broad, because instruments also surface previously unread residuals. The residual selects a third answer: a six-condition union with per-slot verdicts, a declared gap on the universal threshold, and five refuters written in advance.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Uncertainty about the effect of a mediator between knower and ground. Arity: at least four, extended to six on census. Symmetry: "increases objectivity" is ordered and comparative; "creates dependence" is a different quality, not its negation. Scale: first order inside one measurement, second order across instruments and replications. Fig 9 names a bundle of overlapping conditions, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, perception extension.** Actors: observer, instrument, target. Currency: residual registered or unregistered. Anchor: the target as it returns a reading.
- **Domain 2, anchor relation.** Actors: knower, instrument, ground. Currency: which term settles the question. Anchor: whatever does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks.
- **Domain 3, independence of readings.** Actors: two instruments or observers. Currency: how little they draw on the same evidence. Anchor: the ground each ranges against separately.
- **Domain 4, channel and habitat.** Actors: calibration, metric, operator, test harness. Currency: contact with the ground over time. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay.
- **Domain 5, source discrimination.** Actors: a reading and several possible sources. Currency: which source produced the reading. Anchor: a source-discriminating instrument, when one exists.
- **Domain 6, import.** Actors: frame A, frame B, imported primitive. Currency: provenance and shadow test. Anchor: the frame whose closure differs.
- **Union, not partition.** The six overlap and can hold at once. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** D1 before D2 before D3; D4 is a standing precondition; D5 when the reading exists but its cause is multiple; D6 when the closure lacks a term. Refusal precedes mint; describe the residual before choosing a frame.

Strain test: an early reading mapped the six epistemic domains onto Fig 13's physical six-term set. Re-census assigns Fig 13 union and gap structure only, and leaves anchor and channel work to Figures 2, 3, 6, 10 and 11. No figure contradiction after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union, not a threshold. Reliance on instruments increases objectivity when the instrument stays ranged against a reference that does not update (Fig 2 :29, :33, :36-38), when the added readings are independent rather than one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31-32, :42; Fig 8 :48-49), and when the instrument surfaces a residual nobody could read before (Fig 6 :26, :32-35; Fig 3 :27). It creates new dependence when the channel decays while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24-28), when the instrument is promoted into the anchor or a convention decides the outcome (Fig 2 :34-35; Fig 10 :47), when a reading underdetermines its source with no discriminator (Fig 6 :27), or when a test habitat is substituted for the real one (Fig 11 :66-70). Over a fixed atom set the good and bad regimes are isomers (Fig 12 :117), and the condition that fixes the good branch is a functional group, a reading ranged against a ground, with the particular instrument left as a provenanced binding. The universal "when" is a declared `gap`: unreachable is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134). This is `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structure, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Each condition carries its own refuter and anchor, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording of one historical case instead of the generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the condition set and the five refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. A refuted condition is withdrawn without touching the other five, because the verdicts are per domain and never averaged. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. No primitive has to be withdrawn; a refusal adds a term and removes only its standing to be composed over.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor test showing a non-updating reference; an independence audit showing separate evidence; a discriminator that separates the sources of a reading; a habitat comparison naming what a bench result failed to reproduce; or a figure re-census that actually returns the universal threshold. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. A list of agreeing experts, however prestigious, is not such an instrument.
