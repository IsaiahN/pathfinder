# Expert verification packet: instruments and objectivity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science, a human-factors researcher or a metrologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the six conditions, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken. This is a shape, a route and a set of refuters, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "when do instruments increase objectivity, and when do they create new dependence?" is a union of at least six overlapping conditions with different anchors, not one threshold. Objectivity is a relation to a reference that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. An instrument raises objectivity while it stays ranged against such a ground, while its readings are independent of other readings, and while it makes an otherwise unread residual readable. It creates dependence when the channel decays while the ground does not, when it is promoted into the anchor or a convention decides an outcome, when its reading underdetermines its source, or when a test habitat is substituted for the real one. Over a fixed atom set the good and bad regimes are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The framework returns the shape and the refuters, not the winning instrument and not the universal threshold.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Anchor and instrument are roles, not devices (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2)

- **Statement:** the same device is the anchor for a question constitutively about its own scale and merely an instrument for a question about the world. The difference is the bond to the ground, not the device.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33 — *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :36-38 — *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* and *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 10 :47 — *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **What to check:** is the role distinction recoverable from the question asked rather than from the device? (Fig 12 :46: *"which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*)
- **Refuter:** exhibit a reference that changes its answer when a frame pushes on it and still increases objectivity; or a convention that decides an outcome and remains a convention the ground can refuse.

### C2. Independence, not count, is the currency of the gain (framework-internal and world-facing)

- **Statement:** two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames, but readings that share a calibration chain or reduction pipeline are one frame wearing two names. Adding a second reading that draws on the same evidence buys no objectivity gain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 — *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :31-32 — *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :42 — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 8 :48-49 — *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did the second instrument share the first's evidence? If so, the apparent gain is a pool talking, not triangulation.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate an objectivity gain from a reading that draws on exactly the same evidence as an existing reading, with no new ground contact.

### C3. The negative branch is a channel and habitat failure, not merely an error rate (world-facing)

- **Statement:** the ground does not decay, the channel does; a lost channel returns "you have lost touch", not a false verdict. A test harness substitutes a habitat and proves wiring, not capability. A reading that cannot discriminate among its possible sources owes a discriminator.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 — *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :25-26 — *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :27-28 — *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*; Fig 11 :66 — *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*; Fig 11 :70 — *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*; Fig 6 :27 — *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*
- **What to check:** name the discriminator that would distinguish the possible sources of a reading, or name what a bench result failed to reproduce and what it unintentionally introduced. If neither can be named, report `unreached`.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a channel that has lost ground contact while its output still settles a verdict; or a substituted habitat that preserves all required contact and introduces no new actor.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal crossover point, percentage or date for when instrumentation helps. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
- Not that objectivity is the absence of human judgment. Trained judgment is part of the practice (world source W1).
- Not that more instruments help. Independence, not count, is the variable (Fig 2 :31-32).
- Not that a stable or precise reading is a grounded one (Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success."*).
- Not a derivation of any specific instrument from the framework. Naming an available instrument is a binding with provenance, not a name the framework returns.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In practice:

1. An anchor test showing whether the reference for a measurement changes its answer when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :30).
2. An independence audit of a replication set, showing whether the instruments share evidence (Fig 2 :31-32).
3. A discriminator, a better instrument that distinguishes the sources that produce one reading, if one exists (framework essay, *The Hard Problem is About Source*).
4. A habitat comparison naming what a bench result failed to reproduce and what it introduced (Fig 11 :68-69).
5. A figure re-census that produces the universal threshold declared as a gap in `01` F11 would also settle it by closing the gap.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :31-32, :33, :36-38 and Fig 10 :24, :47. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one case you know: was the device the anchor for the question asked, or only an instrument ranged against one?
3. Check C2 against one replication you know: did the second instrument share the first's evidence?
4. Name one discriminator for a reading you work with, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not reply to say you agree; if you do reply later after reading the framework, that agreement is not counted as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
