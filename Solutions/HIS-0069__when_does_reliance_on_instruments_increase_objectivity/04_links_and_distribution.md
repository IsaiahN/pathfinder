## Verified world sources actually opened

| what | URL | verified content |
|---|---|---|
| Scientific objectivity (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) | https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-objectivity/ | Reiss and Sprenger, first published 2014, substantive revision 2020. Contains the experimenter's regress (Collins 1985): to know whether a result is correct you first need to know whether the apparatus producing it is reliable, and you do not know that unless you know the result is correct. Also Franklin's reasoned judgment criteria: experimental checks, calibration, elimination of error sources, well-corroborated theory. |
| Objectivity (science), Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objectivity_(science) | Cites Daston and Galison, *Objectivity* (Zone Books, 2007/2010). "'Let nature speak for itself' became the watchword of a new brand of scientific objectivity"; scientists used "self-registering instruments, cameras, wax molds, and other technological devices"; later "trained judgment supplemented mechanical objectivity." Directly about instruments as a historical source of objectivity and about their limits. |
| Automation bias, Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation_bias | Cummings 2004, Parasuraman and Riley 1997, Parasuraman and Manzey 2010, Lyell and Coiera 2016 and others. "Automation bias is the propensity for humans to favor suggestions from automated decision-making systems and to ignore contradictory information made without automation, even if it is correct." Commission and omission errors, learned carelessness, automation irony, and a reliability-dependent trade-off. Genuinely low overlap with the framework. |
| Ironies of Automation, Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironies_of_Automation | Bainbridge 1983 in *Automatica*, with Strauch 2018 retrospective in IEEE THMS. Automating most of the work leaves the operator responsible for the non-automated residue, without practiced skills and with exhausting monitoring; operators need more training, not less. Genuinely low overlap. |
| Measurement uncertainty, Wikipedia | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_uncertainty | GUM/JCGM framework. "All measurements are subject to uncertainty and a measurement result is complete only when it is accompanied by a statement of the associated uncertainty"; "the outcome depends on the measuring system, the measurement procedure, the skill of the operator, the environment, and other effects." The bathroom-scale offset example shows a stable bias invisible to repetition. Genuinely low overlap (metrology). |
| The Hard Problem is About Source | https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source | Isaiah N, 28 Aug 2026. Metamerism: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"; the case was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Framework author, maximal overlap with the figures, secondary only. |
| The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap | https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-recursive-self-improvement-trap | Isaiah N, 19 Nov 2026. "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." Also "Prestige can fund an action... But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." Framework author, secondary only. |

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the six-condition separator and its refuter before the framework.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 2 :35** — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- **Fig 2 :31-32** — two instruments are two frames, but only if they do not draw on the same evidence. Do not send the same packet to a list that shares one evidence pool and count the replies as independent.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the condition set were true before giving them the source.
- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted; it does not send an answer.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the condition set and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which condition they think is missing; (3) only then the figure extract and the canonical usage note, with the figures named as the authority.

## The two-stage offer, with the third stage

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. The recipient checks the six conditions and the refuter without holding the framework's interior. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the figure extract and the canonical usage note. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.

No stage works if stage 1 does not survive. That is why `01` records a pre-registered refuter and why the email in `02` refuses to ask for trust.
